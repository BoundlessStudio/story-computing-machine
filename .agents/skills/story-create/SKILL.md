# Story workshop

One writer owns the manuscript. The coordinator protects the request, finds independent readers and keeps the drafts. Follow [repository permissions](../../../AGENTS.md) for existing work and canon.

1. **Read the commission.** Save the request and later directions verbatim. Writer and coordinator read them and inspect the supplied originals. Check shared facts the story will use. Identify what the reader was promised and what the writer is free to discover.
2. **Try the scene.** Before outlining, write two short attempts at a moment you want to read. Change something that could alter the story, such as who hears the remark, when it comes, or what the listener has already done. If neither attempt comes alive, try a different premise within the commission. Keep the trials under `tmp/`. From the one that makes you curious, sketch a provisional route to the ending.
3. **Write to the end.** Let discoveries on the page change the outline. In a conversation, listen for the turn the other speaker could never have anticipated. Check what they could have heard or inferred when they answer. Commit the first complete manuscript.
4. **Read cold and rewrite.** Give a fresh reader the whole prose. Before showing the request, ask what stayed with them, where their attention moved and what changed between the people. Ask for the passage. Then check the promised experience against the request and originals. The writer and coordinator discuss what to protect and what needs another attempt. A weak reply may need one word changed or an entirely different encounter. Commit substantial rewrites.
5. **Edit and choose.** A line editor or the coordinator reads the settled draft for voice, rhythm, clarity and losses from revision. Read the decisive exchange aloud when possible. The writer revises. Let a fresh reader experience the whole result before learning its history. Return to the pages if a consequential question remains. The earlier draft can win.
6. **Hand off.** Record the reader’s experience, dissent and the writer’s choice in a short editorial note. Assign the [content rating](../../../universe/style-guide.md#audience-and-content-boundaries). Storytelling Machine imports the reviewed prose, rating and approved references at their Git revision. [Story package](../../../stories/README.md#visualization-handoff)

For a specific difficulty, consult [scene development](references/story-development.md), [craft observations](references/craft-standard.md) or the [reading briefs](references/reader-briefs.md). When testing a method, hold the prompt and writing budget close, give fresh readers neutral copies in opposite orders, and record the work each version received. Compare with published fiction in a separate reading. Model responses help locate an effect on the page. Independent human readings across several stories are needed to claim a place alongside published work.
