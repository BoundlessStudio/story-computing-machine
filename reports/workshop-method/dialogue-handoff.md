# Testing the dialogue handoff

The coordinator can give an editor a reader’s exact observation while leaving the editorial decision open. We tested this on archived manuscripts. The deliverable was a change to the workshop instructions. All the editorial responses described below came from models.

## The comparison

Two independent editors received the same four excerpts and the same broad complaint about dialogue losing momentum. One also received a proposed instruction to read across a pause with the intervening words lifted and then restored. They received a fifth historical excerpt under the same conditions. Both editors asked for an explanation of a pronoun whose setup had been left outside one excerpt. In the historical excerpt both focused on the same expression of desire further down the page, overlooking an earlier delay that a previous revision had removed.

We repeated the test on two complete manuscripts. Each reviewer had the existing editor role at `a90d48ca`, the two manuscripts in the order below and a budget of 70 words per note. One received a general complaint about the pace near the ending. A second received the two specific reactions below. A third received the specific reactions and the proposed extra instruction. The reactions were written for the test. Reviewers did not see the prior editorial decisions.

We kept the specific observation and the complete manuscript in the ordinary editor handoff. The extra diagnostic paragraph produced the same two decisions, so we dropped it. An additional reviewer using the installed workshop instructions kept the third-morning detail and proposed lifting the courtship summary. The test shows a difference in which passage the editor addressed. Claims about literary improvement need independent human readings across manuscripts.

The Wordcraft study examined how thirteen professional writers used an AI editor, including custom rewrites. Sudowrite documents how a writer can select a passage, request a particular change and inspect the result. Those sources suggest a way to give a writer control over an experiment. The comparison above tests the narrower question of what evidence our editor needs.
