## Detail balance established in the pilot

The user rejected the first `All Accounts Due` restyle because every tiny mechanism and pile of fragments was described under heavy brush texture. The second pass reduced the wheel to nearly empty pie sectors and lost too much identity. The third pass was accepted as the detail benchmark: six distinguishable chambers and a central pointer carry the cover, while broad dark and gold masses give the image room to breathe.

Carry that distinction into each cover. Keep the few details that make the scene or symbol recognizable; group repeated or peripheral detail into larger painted forms. Do not turn a busy source into a texture-coated tracing or simplify it to a generic emblem. Place the highest edge clarity and detail at the focal subject, important prop, and title. Let background and secondary forms be looser. Visible strokes should describe form and light, not cover every square inch with the same small marks.

For a crowd, preserve the important people or creatures and reduce the rest to readable groups. For intricate devices, keep the functionally and narratively meaningful parts and simplify hardware. For a quiet scene, preserve the relationship, gesture, and atmosphere while reducing incidental trim. Preserve the source's story identity and established visual facts. Add no unrelated figures or symbols.

## Candidate review

Inspect the saved pixels at full size and thumbnail scale. Check the exact title, subject identity, count and placement of important elements, anatomy and object coherence, focal hierarchy, and detail balance. Retry a candidate when its story subject changes, title is wrong, or the entire picture becomes uniformly intricate or too sparse. Existing story covers and locked packages remain untouched.
