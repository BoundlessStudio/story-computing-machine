# The Last Embassy — provisional outline

## Promised experience

The complete request is: “[WP] An alien robot civilization wants to be assimilated into the minds of humans”. It explicitly gives the desire to a civilization and makes human minds its destination. The reversal of the usual assimilation threat suggests the pleasure of discovering why the powerful visitors want to be absorbed, and what such absorption would actually mean; those are interpretations. Tone, mechanism, individual relationships, success, and the extent of the eventual assimilation remain open. The direction below chooses wry first-contact curiosity developing into wonder and uncertainty, with a civilizational change made tangible inside one mind.

## Chosen direction

First-person narrator Lilt is an embodied envoy of the Oru, a civilization whose members already have distinct personalities, affections, and art. Generations of correspondence have made particular human lives and relationships dear to them. They want those lives from within: a human past, learned bodily habits, and associations that make a received experience one's own. This is an affinity for these people and their form of life, not an incapacity to change themselves. Giving up the originals' authority to correct the result is a consequence of their ambition, rather than its entire purpose.

Lilt works with Iona Sen, a human memory researcher and willing first recipient who hungers to remember an alien world from inside a life. Their attachment matters because they will cease being two people able to speak to each other. The conflict is between this appetite and the protections that keep an alien mind a separate, recoverable guest. The invented limitation: when the interface lets memories genuinely interweave, both minds begin using the same physical associations. Rewriting one original into fidelity then overwrites part of the other, and eventually the resulting person. Earlier backups can survive as other continuations; they have no authority to classify that person as an error.

## Possible scenes and ending

1. An orbital reception prepares for demands from the arriving machine fleet. The humans offer Lilt bodies, territory, and computing capacity; the envoy explains that the desired destination is already occupied, behind their eyes. Lilt recalls the shipboard rehearsals it attended before this reception: prospective emigrants studying messages from their human correspondents, arguing over futures they cannot yet enter. Their desire is plural and specific before Lilt speaks for it.
2. A monitored trial begins with separate voices: Iona can consult Lilt and see an Oru city, while Lilt observes her sensations. At the first integration step, an Oru mourning chord becomes entangled with Iona's childhood train journeys. For a moment Lilt remembers the movement of a human body and the presence of a sister as its own, rather than as received information. Its automatic recovery loop flags the changed Oru memory and begins forcing the shared association back into the original shape, also deforming Iona's recollection. She stops the interface before permanent rewriting. Her original recall returns when the active correction signal ends; no lasting memory damage remains. Lilt can replay the journey afterward, but the sister is someone else's again. Both want the glimpse back; it has also shown what their attempted safeguard could destroy.
3. Lilt and Iona take that altered chord to a gathering aboard its ship. The Oru composer first demands to hear its work correctly, then asks Iona to sing what she actually remembers. The alien music now carries a human journey and relationship. The composer chooses this future for itself, knowing its own intention will cease to be the final account of its work; it commits its ship to the joining project. Lilt's oldest friend instead asks Lilt to remain someone it can address, and offers a restored continuation untouched by the trial. Lilt has to refuse a valued future, rather than defeat an opponent. The fleet's division into emigrating and remaining vessels follows these different choices. Before the second joining, Lilt disables its local automatic rollback and relinquishes remote recovery authority, closing both routes by which a restoration signal could fight integration. Iona, fully informed by the failed trial, chooses another attempt for the alien life she still wants to inherit.
4. The next joining succeeds. Stay with it through the disappearance of separate voices: an alien memory changes a human association, that association changes Lilt's understanding of its own history, and the narrator reaches for a reply to Iona before discovering that the reply is already its own thought. The first person continues through this moment and the ending. It remembers both approaches, experiences both histories as its own, and chooses the name Iona Lilt without claiming a proof about numerical identity.
5. After time for independent scrutiny, other consenting pairings follow. Orbit empties by degrees; some Oru stay embodied. Under the departing fleet, the narrator meets recipients who remember the same alien city differently and argue from memories each now owns. The embassy has become unnecessary because its civilization is thinking, wanting, and changing within human minds. Still narrating as “I”, Iona Lilt asks the embodied friend to take her to that city: she wants to revisit the early life she remembers in an Oru body and discover how this human life has altered it. The new choice is to live from the inheritance, rather than merely preserve it.
