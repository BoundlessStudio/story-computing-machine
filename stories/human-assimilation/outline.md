# The Last Embassy — provisional outline

## Promised experience

The complete request is: “[WP] An alien robot civilization wants to be assimilated into the minds of humans”. It explicitly gives the desire to a civilization and makes human minds its destination. The reversal of the usual assimilation threat suggests the pleasure of discovering why the powerful visitors want to be absorbed, and what such absorption would actually mean; those are interpretations. Tone, mechanism, individual relationships, success, and the extent of the eventual assimilation remain open. The direction below chooses wry first-contact curiosity developing into wonder and uncertainty, with a civilizational change made tangible inside one mind.

## Chosen direction

First-person narrator Lilt is an embodied envoy of the Oru, a civilization whose members already have distinct personalities, affections, and art. They have exchanged messages with humans for generations. What attracts them is the way a person makes acquired memories and ways of thinking their own: an inheritance becomes someone new, with no original entitled to correct that person back into fidelity. The Oru want to continue through that transformation, including its loss of control. This is their chosen ambition, not proof that machines lack feeling or that humans are uniquely capable of creativity.

Lilt works with Iona Sen, a human memory researcher and willing first recipient who hungers to remember an alien world from inside a life. Their developing attachment matters because they will cease being two people able to speak to each other. The conflict is between the wish for genuine assimilation and the safeguards that keep the alien mind a separate, recoverable guest. Human acceptance is also a consequential choice. The invented limitation is specific: once their memories and habits genuinely interweave, restoring either original would overwrite the resulting person. Earlier backups can survive as other continuations; they cannot certify the new person as an error.

## Possible scenes and ending

1. An orbital reception prepares for demands from the arriving machine fleet. Lilt presents the Oru's request to be assimilated. The humans offer bodies, territory, and computing capacity; the bewildered envoy explains that the destination is already occupied, behind the listeners' eyes. The fleet carries consenting emigrants, with individual reservations as well as shared desire.
2. During a reversible trial, Iona can consult Lilt and see an Oru city through its memories. Lilt can observe her sensations. The trial is exhilarating but leaves them addressing each other across an intact boundary. Iona recalls one alien structure through an association with her own childhood; Lilt's recovery system classifies this new recollection as corruption and begins restoring the original. She stops the trial before it can overwrite her memories. Their desired future has just become a concrete danger.
3. Lilt returns to the fleet to argue for withdrawing the originals' right of correction. An Oru friend wants to remain embodied and fears losing Lilt; that refusal is honored. The emigrants must choose the actual transformation now revealed, while humans receive an honest account of what cannot be promised. Lilt relinquishes its remote restoration authority rather than offering reassurance it cannot substantiate. Iona chooses the irreversible trial for her own appetite for another form of experience.
4. The next joining succeeds. Stay with it through the disappearance of separate voices: an alien memory changes a human association, that association changes Lilt's understanding of its own history, and the narrator reaches for a reply to Iona before discovering that the reply is already its own thought. The new first person can remember both approaches to this moment and cannot reconstruct the former boundary.
5. After time for independent scrutiny, other consenting pairings follow. Orbit empties by degrees; some Oru stay embodied. The closing scene places the changed Iona among other recipients under the departing fleet. They remember the same alien city differently and argue from memories each now owns. The embassy has become unnecessary because its civilization is thinking, wanting, and changing within human minds. Iona asks Lilt's embodied friend to take her to that city: she wants to find out what she has misremembered about the childhood she has never physically lived. Her decision belongs to the blended life, whose future neither original can dictate.
