# Outline

## Story

- Premise and central promise: Ylva sees her adult elder sister Marzena crossing north alone in an ember-scarred black gown while Cyrak burns behind her. Resolve why no bride returned before, whether Marzena caused the fire, and whether Iskal receives her.
- Focal pressure or attachment: Ylva wants her sister alive but fears Marzena bought survival with strangers' lives; she cannot ask whether Marzena returned for her. Marzena wants entry as a daughter, not pardon as a useful bride. Queen Tirza wants both daughters safe without facing her incuriosity.
- Counterforce or complication: Tirza keeps the gate shut while Marzena's damaged breath, fused cords, and refusal to sound innocent impede explanation. Residual heat thins the causeway. Tirza's evasion and Marzena's destructive escape prevent clean blame.
- POV, distance, and information limit: Close third past through Ylva, intimate with footing, family gestures, and misreadings. Cyrak exists only as distant blaze, physical evidence, a stolen tally, and Marzena's incomplete account. Casualties and army movements remain unknown.
- Governing movement and time shape: One dusk crossing: Marzena advances north; Ylva meets her and turns homeward while cutting the gown loose. Tirza must step onto failing ice. The open-causeway climax is private; sleep interrupts reckoning inside the gate.
- Speculative rule or ordinary-world constraint: Cyrak's nonliving cinderwork binds one northern bride and her frost-woven black-glass gown into the capital's heat channels for one winter. Renewal kills her. Early severance rebounds heat through connected furnaces; remnants shed finite heat locally. It cannot choose or pursue and establishes no universal property of women, northerners, vows, or marriage.
- Dialogue promise: An estranged family's homecoming where royal euphemism meets smoke-shortened answers, ugly literal questions, old hand-signs, mistimed sisterly humor, and silence that grants no absolution.
- Dialogic medium: Wind-broken speech, gesture, touch refusal, soot marks on ice, a charred tally, glass cords cut from the gown, the closed gate, and Tirza's eventual approach.
- Dialogue engine: Ylva needs truth before helping; Marzena cannot ask help from the family that dressed her; Tirza wants accident, certainty, or contrition. Each answer changes distance, assistance, blame, and risk.

## Voice

- Narrative texture: Follow foot pressure, thaw halos, wet hems, wind-stolen sound, and Ylva's intrusive domestic memories. Let distant orange fire stain an iron-blue, black-ice palette without making destruction spectacle.
- Conversational texture: Keep family talk interrupted, repetitive, petty, and limited by breath and distance. Childhood shorthand allows warmth and misreading; titles return when intimacy hurts.
- Rhetorical ownership: Ylva owns blunt questions, nervous humor, associative memory, and premature conclusions. Marzena owns concrete correction, withheld context, physical refusal, and attentive affection. Tirza owns conditional argument and social authority, evades through protocol, and mistakes silence for compliance.
- Pressure behavior: Ylva fills gaps before learning to wait. Marzena loses fluency yet becomes direct about culpability. Tirza grows ceremonial and accusatory, then loses controlled speech on the ice.
- Relationship movement: Ylva cannot ask whether she mattered; helping creates trust without erasing judgment. Marzena cannot ask reception from those who sent her; she gains entry, not forgiveness. Tirza cannot request absolution; leaving the gate makes her an implicated mother rather than evaluator.
- Anti-default: Avoid polished royals solving geopolitics through balanced debate. Keep exhaustion, faulty inference, resentment, awkward affection, and unanswered danger; private reception matters more than reform, crowd conversion, or a clean verdict on Cyrak.

## Beats

1. Ylva recognizes Marzena's dark hair and uneven stride from Iskal's tower: a solitary black figure against frozen mountains, storm-dark sky, and Cyrak's blaze. Tirza bars the gate and questions her from safety.
2. Ylva carries a rope and cloak onto the causeway. Welcome fails: Marzena cannot sustain speech, recoils when cloth catches fused cords, and returns an old sisterly gesture incorrectly. Meltwater makes every pause dangerous.
3. Walking north, Ylva cuts trailing glasswork. A charred tally and broken account reveal that renewal killed earlier brides while supervised letters preserved Iskal's fiction. Tirza's questions expose that she knew return was forbidden and avoided asking why.
4. Seeking an innocent version, Ylva learns Marzena tore free after finding the renewal chamber, knowing heat would rebound into Cyrak. She warned people within reach but cannot account for the spreading fire. She returned alone because no earlier bride survived.
5. At the weakened center, an inaccessible collar cord forces a choice before certainty. Marzena tries to send Ylva back. Their angry, embarrassed contact establishes that she returned to prevent either sister entering the apparatus, not that her escape was selfless.
6. Tirza leaves the gate alone, steadies Marzena, and hears her refuse both accident and apology while Ylva frees the cord. Its finite heat scorches water and exhausts itself. Tirza admits Marzena without demanding a usable story and stops an immediate replacement; inside, Marzena falls asleep before political reckoning resumes.
