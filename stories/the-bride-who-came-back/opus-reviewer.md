# Opus review: The Bride Who Came Back

Independent audit for a later editing pass. It is not a `review.md` verdict,
does not change canon state, and does not authorize edits by itself.

## Verdict

- Dialogue: REVISE — Tirza answers words she could not hear, a "No" answers a question Ylva never asks, and the middle of the story is a question-and-answer drill full of quips and corrections.
- Prose: REVISE — the physical prose is concrete and well paced, but one paragraph at the resolution states the story's meaning outright and narration keeps explaining lines just spoken (D9).
- Continuity and prompt: PASS — the prompt is delivered in full at about 3,000 words; the only internal wobbles are minor (sleep timing, the neck cords, when Ylva's procession would leave).
- Edit scope: targeted-dialogue
- Existing review.md said: Verdict PASS, "Dialogue: PASS"; disagree — it missed the overheard exchange, the interrupted-question mismatch, and the interrogation rhythm.

The dialogue has real strengths, so this is not a heavy rewrite. Tirza's contraction-free council voice turns into "Where may I hold you?", the sisters tease each other with the pear sign and the pity-sheets, and Tirza's confession is hard and plain. The failures are concentrated. There are two sense failures: Tirza replies from the gate to a quiet exchange far out on the ice, and Marzena says "No" to a question that is printed complete and does not take that answer. Between L133 and L237 Ylva does almost nothing but prompt while Marzena answers, one clipped turn at a time. Throughout, both sisters fall back on the collection's habit of echoing the last speaker's words and correcting them, or closing a beat with a dry quip, and the narration often follows a line with a neat sentence explaining it. The edit should fix the two sense failures first, then break up the interrogation so Ylva speaks for herself, let Marzena's exhaustion shape how she explains the mechanism, and cut the quips and corrections back to the few that are earned.

## Edit guardrails

- Prompt: Iskal (the frozen kingdom) sends one bride south each winter to keep Orrava (the fire kingdom) from invading. This year's bride, Marzena, comes back alone while Cyrak burns behind her. The story must stay complete and resolve that promise, keep to 2,500–4,000 words (it is about 3,000 now, so small additions are fine but the edit must not bloat it), stay broadly accessible, and stay in close third person on Ylva. Craft profile: prospective-2026-08-23.
- Keep these internal facts: Ylva is 24 and Marzena is the elder sister. Marzena left at first snow, twenty-three days ago. The treaty is "one bride, one winter, one border at peace." Each bride sends three letters: Marzena's first praised oranges, her second used Ylva's full title, and there was no third. There are six cords in all: the first one cut, four behind, and the collar loop. The copper tally carries Iskal seals, and every row except the newest is punched. Keep the Crown Hearth, the hearth hook, and Second Warmth. The fire ran back through the palace furnaces, the bathhouses, and the kiln street. Marzena warned some people and does not know whether they got out, and casualties must stay uncounted. Ylva's name was first on the list. Tirza's rope is looped around the postern hinge. "No bride leaves Iskal." Keep the cracked blue cup and the hairpins.
- The postern/gate distinction is structural: "This is not the gate." (L61) sets up "Open the gate." (L379) and the crosspieces lifting. Any rewrite of either line must keep that link.
- Universe: all names are local to this story, and nothing contradicts a LOCKED or CANON entry. The mechanism fits LOCKED "Living agency and magic" (rules.md): the living bride supplies the agency and the glass and cords only channel it, so the severed cord must stay non-agentic. Do not grow the heat network or the bathhouses into water-infrastructure texture (style guide, "No default water infrastructure").
- Do not flatten what works: Tirza's formal register (she never uses a contraction), the sisters' childish humor, Marzena's short, body-limited answers, and the final sideways permission at the bedroom door.

## Dialogue findings

### D1 · Blocking · Tirza hears a quiet exchange across the lake

- Where: L179–L211, the tally and letters exchange, while the sisters are still south of the causeway's center
- Text:
> At the gate, Tirza said, “You signed them.”
> “Written with three readers behind me.”
> “You have the arrivals.” Marzena coughed into her wrist. “This is the rest.”
- Problem: Ground/knowledge and staging. At L207 the sisters have not yet reached "the old pressure ridge at the center of the causeway" (L245). From the gate, Tirza looked like "a small white shape" (L97), and at L211 the wind swallows her next line. Marzena says "Written with three readers behind me" to Ylva beside her, in a voice that has "the old shape of command, but not the strength" (L119). Yet Tirza answers it with a plain "said", as if she stood next to them. Earlier (L183–L187), the coughing Marzena carries on a multi-line conversation with the parapet. Tirza is replying to words she could not have heard.
- Fix: Choose one. (a) Make the distance cost something: Tirza's lines are called and short, Ylva shouts or relays what Tirza needs to hear, and Marzena's replies to the parapet are brief enough to shout, with the effort shown. (b) Keep the letters exchange between the sisters and move "You signed them" and "I hate oranges" to the face-to-face scene after Tirza reaches them (around L329–L339), where they would sharpen her confession. Either way, Tirza may answer only what she could hear. Keep "I hate oranges"; it is the best accusation in the story.

### D2 · Blocking · "How far?" is complete, yet Marzena interrupts it

- Where: L221–L227, right after "I knew it would go back."
- Text:
> “How far?”
> “No.” Marzena’s answer came sharp, before Ylva had finished.
- Problem: Ground/uptake. The question on the page is finished, and "No" does not answer "How far?". It answers an unasked "Did you know how far…?", the question the author meant rather than the words Ylva said. The narration then claims Ylva had not finished, which contradicts the dialogue as printed. This lands on the story's moral hinge: whether Marzena knew the cost.
- Fix: Print Ylva's line as cut off, so the interruption is visible and "No" answers what she had started to ask. Illustrative only: “Did you know how far it would—” Or keep "How far?" and drop both "No." and "before Ylva had finished". Keep "They never told me how far. They told me enough to keep me still."

### D3 · Major · A seventh-step limp cannot come from one bad ankle

- Where: L11–L29, recognition from the watchtower
- Text:
> Ylva knew her sister by the way she lost every seventh step.
> She still cheats that foot when she’s tired.
> “Not at seven.”
- Problem: Ground/scene logic. Footfalls alternate between left and right, so a drag on every seventh step (steps 7, 14, 21) lands on the left foot, then the right, then the left. An old left-ankle injury cannot produce that pattern, so Ylva's sincere evidence contradicts the count the narration keeps ("Six, drag. Six, drag."). "Not at seven" also relies on a count Ylva never says to Tirza, and it closes the exchange as a clipped last word. Next to "when she was twelve" it can even be heard as "not at age seven".
- Fix: Make the period even so the same foot always drags (for example, seven even steps and a dragging eighth), or count strides instead, and change L11, L15, L27 and L29 together. Give Ylva a plain reply to "Many people limp" that shares the count with Tirza instead of the epigram. Illustrative only: “Count them. Seven good steps, then the left drags. Every time.”

### D4 · Major · Ylva's comebacks turn the other speaker's word into a quip

- Where: L43 (watchtower), L115 (call from the gate), L125 (first cord), L257 (collar); see also L27 in D3
- Text:
> “She carries your daughter.”
> “The gate is wood.”
> “I had gathered that.”
> “Very useful.”
- Problem: Punchline Patch and house-voice convergence. Each time, Ylva picks up the other speaker's noun or claim and turns it into a dry last word instead of answering or acting. "She carries your daughter" fails on a literal reading: Marzena is the daughter, she is not carrying one, and the line could even suggest pregnancy. It works only as a pun on Tirza's "what she carries". "I had gathered that" contradicts what Ylva has just done, which is reach for the glowing cord. "Very useful" is sardonic calm while the ice splits under them and her sister's collar is burning. The outline itself gives Ylva "blunt questions, nervous humor"; these lines are arch, not nervous.
- Fix: Replace them with blunt or physical responses from a frightened 24-year-old: a plain answer, an insult, a refusal, or no line and an action. The sisters' humor already has a home in the bed and pity-sheets exchange (L281–L295), which is play, not correction. Keep "This is not the gate." (L61), which is a real loophole with a payoff. Illustrative only (L43): “She's your daughter. Look at her.” At L125, Ylva can jerk her hand back and swear, or say nothing and go for snow. L127 will then need adjusting (see D5).

### D5 · Major · Marzena answers by echoing and correcting

- Where: L127, L151, L297; compare L187 and L287
- Text:
> “No. Hot enough to open your fingers.”
> “It is the part you asked for.”
> “I crossed the pass because south had nothing that was mine.”
- Problem: Correction ping-pong. Marzena's default reply takes the other speaker's phrasing and returns a more exact version of it. "I had gathered that" gets "No. Hot enough…". "That isn't the whole answer" gets "It is the part you asked for." "You crossed the pass to insult my bedding" gets "I crossed the pass because…". "We have the marriage record" gets "You have the arrivals." "You had six rooms" gets "I had one room with no latch." Five of these in one story make her sound like her mother's council; with the tags covered, "It is the part you asked for." could be Tirza's line. At L297 the echo turns the sisters' best exchange into a statement of the theme, and then the narration explains it (see D9). "Open your fingers" is also unclear: it could mean split the skin or force her grip open.
- Fix: Keep the two strongest echoes: "You have the arrivals." (L187) and "I had one room with no latch." (L287). Break the pattern elsewhere. At L151, Marzena can refuse or just keep walking (L153 already has her start walking). At L297, drop the mirrored "crossed the pass" and answer "You don't get to send me back now" with something smaller and incomplete, such as a concession, a complaint about the cold, or letting Ylva take her weight, so the reader infers why she came. At L127, cut "No." and give the warning in fewer, plainer words.

### D6 · Major · The central revelation is a question-and-answer drill

- Where: L133–L237, from "How many?" to "I don't know, Ylva."
- Text:
> “How did you get free?”
> “And the fire?”
> “Did you warn anyone?”
- Problem: Tennis Match and explanatory chorus. Ylva has seventeen lines in this stretch, and almost all are questions or one-word prompts: "How many?", "And if we leave them?", "Of what?", "Tell me.", "Renew.", "The letters," "And the fire?", "You knew?", "How far?", "Did they get out?". Marzena answers each in one clipped turn. Ylva's horror, anger and wish for "numbers that would make love simple" stay in the narration, so the relationship does not move in speech until "Was I next?" (L263). The information is needed, but it comes out like a deposition, one even beat after another, with nobody interrupting or taking control.
- Fix: Keep the order of discoveries and the short answers that land ("Us.", "The newest one.", "I don't know, Ylva."). Cut three or four bridging prompts ("Of what?", "Renew.", "And the fire?" are candidates) and let the copper squares, the cords hissing into the lake and the glimpses of fire carry those steps. Between L213 and L237, give Ylva at least one line that is not a question, such as an accusation, a wrong guess she has to take back, or a refusal to hear the rest, so Marzena has to answer her sister rather than a prompt. Let a cord or the ice cut across at least one turn. Do this pass together with D7.

### D7 · Major · The mechanism arrives as a fluent briefing

- Where: L191–L199, after "Tell me."
- Text:
> “The gown is a bridle for their heat channels,” she said.
> A living body inside Iskal glass keeps the winter fire from running wild.
- Problem: Exposition and uniform fluency under pressure. A moment earlier Marzena could manage only "Us." and "The newest one." She coughs through her other lines, yet here she delivers four complete technical sentences, like a clerk reading a summary. "Bridle for their heat channels" mixes a harness image with engineering jargon, and "the winter fire" is never defined: is it Cyrak's winter hearth, or something tied to Iskal? The clean delivery flattens the horror of what the gown does.
- Fix: Keep the facts: the cords go into the Crown Hearth, a living Iskal body holds the fire, and renewal drains the old bride. Have Marzena give them in broken pieces, one per breath or per cord, tied to things she saw or felt rather than to a system diagram. Define "winter fire" or replace it. Keep "They take everything left in the old one." exactly; it lands because it is plain.

### D8 · Major · Tirza's request for consent is staged as a correction

- Where: L351–L359, the collar cut
- Text:
> “Wait,” Tirza said.
> “I was not speaking to you.”
- Problem: Ground/uptake and Punchline Patch at the decisive moment. Ylva has just pushed the knife in, and Marzena is only standing still, so any listener takes "Wait" as an order to Ylva, which is how Ylva takes it. Tirza's claim that she was not speaking to Ylva turns "Wait" into an instruction to someone who is doing nothing. The correction exists only to set up "Marzena. Now?", which is strong and does not need it.
- Fix: Keep Tirza's steadier hand and "Marzena. Now?", and cut the correction. Tirza can stop Ylva with "Wait" or a hand and then turn straight to Marzena, or she can speak to Marzena with no "Wait" at all. Ylva's "There isn't time." can stay if Tirza simply ignores it. What matters is that Tirza asks.

### D9 · Major · Narration explains the line just spoken

- Where: L67, L141, L241, L271, L299, L313; see also L53, L201, L391, and L417 (P1)
- Text:
> That, at least, was an instruction he understood.
> Practical answers. Marzena had always given them when the other kind cost too much.
> It was the closest Marzena had come to asking.
> because titles returned when intimacy hurt
> She wanted numbers that would make love simple.
- Problem: Glossed subtext. After many key lines, a neat narrated sentence, often an aphorism, tells the reader what the speech meant or why it was said. The lines are clear without it: the switch to "Your Majesty" at L313 and Marzena's reply at L297 both read fine alone. The gloss takes away the reader's work and lays a second, wiser voice over the characters.
- Fix: Cut L67, the second sentence of L141, L299, and the "because…" clause at L313. Trim L241 and L271 to what Ylva does or thinks in the moment rather than a summary of her feelings ("The truth landed with less surprise than shame" names the emotion for the reader). L53, L201 and L391 can stay if the surrounding edits leave them room, since they read as Ylva's own perception rather than commentary. For L201, see also C4.

### D10 · Minor · Two loose referents

- Where: L269 (collar), L289 (bed exchange)
- Text:
> “Your name was first on the list they sent with me.”
> “You can have it back.”
- Problem: Ground/referent. All through the causeway scene, Marzena's "they" means her keepers in Cyrak ("They fix the cords", "They take everything", "They never told me how far"). So "the list they sent with me" first reads as a southern list, though only Iskal sent her. This is the line that implicates Tirza, so it should be unambiguous. At L289, "it" follows "I had one room with no latch" and so grammatically offers back the room in Cyrak; Ylva means the bed.
- Fix: At L269, make it clear the list came from Iskal, for example "the list that went south with me", or name the court. At L289, make the object "the bed" or "your room".

### D11 · Minor · "They neglected my instruction."

- Where: L315–L321, Tirza's first questions on the ice
- Text:
> “They neglected my instruction.”
- Problem: Arch court phrasing that can also be read as "they ignored an order I gave". With the collar flaring, Marzena answers her mother in her mother's register. It may be meant as mockery, but nothing signals that, and it adds to the D5 drift of Marzena's voice into Tirza's.
- Fix: Either make the mockery visible (a look from Marzena, or Tirza noticing it) or say it plainly and briefly.

## Prose findings

### P1 · Major · The resolution paragraph states its own meaning

- Where: L415–L417, right after "No bride leaves Iskal"
- Text:
> It was not a plan for the army Orrava might send. It was not justice for whoever had failed to outrun the fire. It was one door held shut.
- Problem: A thesis statement and a closing emblem. Tirza's order and the captain's news ("no southern troops at the pass, no messenger, no way to see beyond the smoke") already show what the order does and does not settle. This paragraph explains it again in a polished "It was not X. It was not Y. It was Z." pattern and hands the reader a symbolic door just before the real ending uses an actual one.
- Fix: Cut the paragraph, or keep only a concrete consequence (what someone does next). The bedroom door should be the only door the ending leans on.

### P2 · Minor · Negation and reversal habit

- Where: L69, L77, L365 (and L357)
- Text:
> The causeway was not a bridge but a winter road
> not sewn but hardened around her ribs
> It did not reach for them. It did not follow.
- Problem: "Not X but Y" and negated actions are a default way of describing things here. At L365, listing what the cord did not do suggests it might have had intentions, which the story is otherwise careful to avoid.
- Fix: At L69 and L77, state the positive fact directly. At L365, keep the point that the cord has no will, but say what it physically did (sank, dimmed, went black) rather than what it held back from doing. L357 ("not tighter but steadier") earns its contrast; leave it.

## Continuity and prompt findings

### C1 · Minor · Marzena falls asleep twice

- Where: L419–L429, the bedroom
- Text:
> Marzena was asleep before Ylva reached the apology.
> while Marzena slept before any of them could ask her for another thing
- Problem: Marzena is asleep at L419, speaks with her eyes shut at L427, and then the last sentence has her sleeping "before" anyone could ask, a tense tangle that reads like a third time falling asleep. The intended effect, a half-asleep woman giving permission sideways, is good, but the timeline is muddled.
- Fix: At L419, make her nearly asleep (eyes closed, not answering the apology). Let her fall asleep once, cleanly, in the last sentence. Leave "Shut the door" and "You're letting the cold in" untouched.

### C2 · Minor · The neck cords are counted inconsistently

- Where: L77, L135, L209, L251
- Text:
> Hair-thin cords ran from its collar into the skin below her ears.
> “Four more behind. One at the collar.”
> the cords at her neck flashed
- Problem: The first description gives several hair-thin cords entering her skin at the neck. Marzena counts one at the collar, and the cord Ylva cuts is "thicker than the rest" and comes out between her shoulder blades. The hair-thin neck cords are never removed or explained, unless the healers' "every cord" at L401 covers them.
- Fix: Either make the L77 neck cords part of the single collar loop, or have Marzena's count or the healers' scene account for them. A few words will do.

### C3 · Minor · Why Ylva's procession would leave tonight

- Where: L411–L415, the guardroom
- Text:
> “And the procession?” the captain asked. Everyone in the room knew he meant Ylva’s.
- Problem: The treaty sends one bride each winter, and Marzena left twenty-three days ago, so "Not tonight. Not at dawn." implies an immediate departure the story never sets up. The likely reasoning, that with the bride back Orrava will expect a replacement at once and Ylva has just been measured again, is left for the reader to supply.
- Fix: One clause in the captain's question or the narration should make it explicit that the court would send the next bride at once to keep the treaty.

### C4 · Minor · Ylva understands the punched squares twice

- Where: L171 and L201, the tally
- Text:
> “How many are alive?” Ylva asked.
> Ylva understood the punched squares. She wished she did not.
- Problem: Her question at L171 already reads the squares as deaths, and Marzena confirms that only one bride is alive. L201 then presents the same understanding as new. What is actually new at L201 is how they died.
- Fix: Make L201 about the renewal (each square marks a bride drained when her successor arrived), or cut it (see D9).
