# Outline

## Story

- Premise and central promise: Adult saint Sidonie arrives in bridal white to propose a marriage ending two kingdoms' war. Demon Queen Rhazelle kneels first with the ring she commissioned three hundred years ago. Gothic romantic comedy opens into the unequal experience of loving across mortal lives, with the queen's awareness of being narrated making her courtship inconveniently public.
- Focal pressure or attachment: Rhazelle wants to surprise Sidonie and be wanted extravagantly in return. She survived two marriages to Sidonie's earlier incarnations, bereavement, and long absences. Since recognizing the adult Sidonie at a truce meeting, she has cherished six weeks of increasingly personal correspondence while postponing her proposal.
- Counterforce or complication: Sidonie, twenty-nine and her kingdom's reigning sacred sovereign, means to settle rival territorial succession claims through marriage. She has also fallen for her supposedly monstrous correspondent. Prepared for threats, mockery, or imprisonment, she interprets Rhazelle's sudden familiarity as an intimate history she cannot compete with; Rhazelle mistakes political preparation for romantic indifference. Neither mistake needs a villain or a second war crisis.
- POV, distance, and information limit: Close third past through Rhazelle, with audible addresses to the actual reader. The narrator may expose her thoughts; Sidonie's thoughts require speech or observable conduct. Rhazelle hears her own scene being narrated, including its embarrassing disclosures, but cannot see forthcoming text, other stories, readers, or untold minds.
- Governing movement and time shape: One moonlit evening expands around the interrupted proposal, then compresses preparations into a brief dawn wedding. Three hundred years enter through two distinct marital memories and the unused ring. Target 2,500–4,000 words; keep the emotional climax between the women.
- Speculative rule or ordinary-world constraint: Rhazelle is a living, long-lived winged and horned woman. Her narrow soul-sight recognizes one returning soul; each mortal incarnation passes through the underlying afterlife before rebirth without autobiographical memories. Her awareness of stories grants contact with this account, never revision of events. Both sovereigns can bind their armies; their marriage compact resolves the competing succession claims, and transmitted joint orders actually end hostilities.
- Dialogue promise: A terrifying queen loses her rehearsed composure while a practical bride discovers that her enemy has been longing for her. Affection, jealousy, teasing, and genuine questions can share an exchange.
- Dialogic medium: Private spoken courtship, correspondence recalled or physically produced, the queen's one-way reader address, touch, and short public vows.
- Dialogue engine: Rhazelle's asides reveal evasions Sidonie can notice but not automatically understand. Sidonie's questions make her explain whom she is addressing; once she understands, Sidonie deliberately surprises someone who can hear the present account but cannot know her next words.

## Voice

- Narrative texture: Luxurious attention to velvet, moonlight, hands, and bodily proximity; centuries contract into what Rhazelle cannot discard. Narrative disclosures puncture her theatrical dignity without making grief a joke.
- Conversational texture: Sidonie starts ceremonially, grows inquisitive, and enjoys flirtation once she realizes it is welcome. Rhazelle alternates royal ease, overprepared romance, mundane answers, and embarrassed reader asides.
- Rhetorical ownership: Rhazelle owns historical comparison, dramatic gestures, and evasive humor. Sidonie owns political intention, impatient curiosity, jealous misreadings, and the willingness to risk sounding eager. Neither supplies the other's conclusions.
- Pressure behavior: Rhazelle becomes excessively helpful and loses track of simple questions. Sidonie's formal fluency fails when desire becomes mutual; she recovers through specific, sometimes mischievous requests.
- Relationship movement: Rhazelle wants delight but cannot comfortably ask whether Sidonie loves her. Sidonie wants to be uniquely desired but hates admitting jealousy of her own forgotten lives. Exchanging present preferences and private motives turns rehearsed return into unexpected courtship.
- Anti-default: Avoid a tribunal about consent, a lesson delivered to an immortal, or the reader supplying a rescue. Let an extravagant proposal become an even more extravagant answer.

## Beats

1. Open at the castle's moonlit threshold with the wedding dress disrupting Rhazelle's preparations. Sidonie visibly braces for an adversary. Before she can propose, Rhazelle kneels and offers the preserved ring: “You always ask first,” the queen whispers. “I wanted to beat you to it this time.” Keep both women unmistakably adult. Use the inspected reference's black/crimson queen, pale-haired standing bride, arched gothic opening, and exposed tenderness.
2. Sidonie asks the immediate literal questions. Rhazelle commissioned this still-unused proposal ring three hundred years earlier; both previous beloveds proposed before she found her moment. Those were real marriages with ordinary pleasures and eventual mortal deaths, not repeated failed rescues. The present war began generations after their last peace. Sidonie produces her compact, revealing both her readiness and the queen's mistaken assumption about tonight.
3. The narration starts revealing Rhazelle's humiliating quantity of proposal rehearsals. She interrupts it aloud and addresses the person reading; the interruption visibly changes the paragraph's course. Sidonie follows her gaze, hears only her side, and insists on understanding the distraction. Establish fourth-wall contact through the printed exchange itself. Her faculty offers no answer from the audience.
4. Let Sidonie's curiosity turn toward the previous marriages, then snag on a small remembered preference she does not share. Rhazelle answers without summoning memories into her. Sidonie reveals what in their recent correspondence attracted her; Rhazelle discovers an intention she had misread. Past happiness and grief remain present, but desire gains new, current particulars. Give them room for affectionate foolishness rather than a metaphysical hearing.
5. Sidonie takes the romantic initiative through an unforeseen counterproposal or personal addition to her acceptance. Rhazelle cannot consult the next paragraph. Let the writer discover its wording and the reciprocal gesture; the old ring finally reaches a living hand. A kiss and unmistakable mutual acceptance resolve the private proposal before the political ceremony.
6. At dawn, briefly show the wedding and military witnesses accepting joint cessation orders under the compact. Existing losses remain real. Sidonie interrupts another reader aside by claiming Rhazelle's attention for herself. End within their delighted intimacy, without a history lecture or a promise against future grief.
