# At the Back of the Picture

## Promised experience

The request puts an archivist at work restoring a photograph of a victorious royal family. The discovery is the silver-haired woman behind them, followed by her appearances in their coronation paintings, in sketches of their executions, and in security footage from the successor republic. It is the same woman in the same white armor, with more damage as the records advance. The changing media invite the reader to do the archivist's double take and then examine each new image for what time did to her. Victory, coronation, and execution suggest the span of a dynasty and an uncanny continuity beneath sharp changes of fortune. I expect the damage and her place in the pictures to acquire new meaning. The request leaves the woman's allegiance and nature, any relationship beyond the archivist's attention to her, and how fully the mystery resolves open. The attached portrait supplies her visual presence.

## Takes considered

- **One hall across a change of government (chosen).** The family's conquests and the republic's founding keep bringing people to the same place. The archivist discovers what the woman has been doing there and joins her the night she decides to stop. The live encounter makes the old pictures sources of immediate, physical knowledge.
- **The last sitter.** A visit from the woman sets the archive search in motion. She wants a portrait with her own name, while the archivist discovers she helped bring some of the rulers to power and walked some to their deaths. The sitting with the pictures laid out could give the two women a charged negotiation. It would put their relationship ahead of the surprise of finding her.
- **A record out of order.** Two archivists at the public showing realize the damage puts the footage before some of the paintings in the woman's personal chronology. They attempt to get a message into her next appearance through the pose of a sitter. That set piece has room for wonder and would make a different story of the linear accumulation.

## Provisional direction

In Tarse, the Republic is reopening Hundred Steps Hall on the anniversary of its first public count. Archivist Ireta is restoring the royal family's victory photograph for the projection. The crowned used to turn and face the east gallery when the people acclaimed them. The revolutionaries turned the condemned family toward those same benches so everyone could see who died. In the photo, a royal child twists to look up at a silver-haired woman. In one of two coronation oils, the woman stands behind the same child grown up and crowned. What the painter makes look like a hand raised to bless the crown is one of the woman's gauntlets hooked through an old iron eye at the back of the dais.

The executions were drawn in sequence. On the first sheet the queen, last of the family to die, looks up toward a gallery full of faces. In the next she turns away from them to reach for the woman's free hand, which the woman gives her. The other gauntlet stays closed. Ireta has been reading the hand at the eye as a gesture of personal allegiance, but footage of the republic's first count shows the woman catch the same eye as it slips and beckon a spectator, who has stumbled up on the swaying gallery, toward the steadier seats while the count continues. That angle and the sound make the eye intelligible. It is the loose end of the tie taking the east gallery's weight. The gallery stands when empty. The woman has been coming when crowds fill it and taking the load herself. She watches the people these ceremonies put in the same room. The choice to hold the queen's hand has helped Ireta see where the woman has had room to choose.

When the current feed shows one of the fingers break, Ireta goes upstairs to stop the reopening. The woman has already returned to bear the weight of a full gallery, and wants the people out so she can let it go. Movement on the public stair below the east gallery puts the crowd in the path of the eventual fall. The old photograph gives Ireta a view of the side exit from a time when the opening was in plain sight. She and the woman get the crowd across the solid part of the dais and out onto the brewery stair. An older woman and her granddaughter, whom Ireta has seen earlier, are last to leave. The older woman came back because she had watched the executions as a girl and wanted to show her granddaughter what she could see from her seat, which the public images cropped away. The woman in white puts her plated shoulder against the eye's iron housing to keep the tie in its seat while she offers her good hand to the last spectator. Then she releases herself from the damaged gauntlet, leaves it in the eye, and follows them. Ireta knows her by the things she has done with her hands, while her origins and loyalties remain open.

## Craft plan

- **Teller.** Try Ireta in close third, against the clock and impatient with imprecise claims, and Ireta in retrospective first, trying to account for which details she previously helped to crop. The person who will spend an hour looking for one eye in a print still wants her work to be seen at full size. She has cooperated with the people who give her the wall and select what goes on it.
- **Cast and speech.** Ireta gets exact and talks too long when someone cuts her off before she's shown her evidence. In her head she habitually distinguishes what she sees from how she knows it. The woman measures her words by what the person beside her can do. She gives directions with the quick, intimate confidence of someone getting strangers across dangerous rooms. Small talk comes back to her at odd times when her hands are busy. The unnamed steward speaks in broad, placating injunctions and will eventually have to direct people out in words they can use. The grandmother and girl stay a familiar face in the crowd through most of the story.
- **The woman in the original.** She has a level, assessing look from inside a ragged white hood, long silver hair, and a half-open articulated hand. Red shows in the recessed joints of chipped white plates. She plants herself, lets pressure settle, and absorbs the cost with a reserve that reads as self-possession. Keep the hood and fitted mechanical profile with attention to face, hands, and weight. The hood shelters a face Ireta has to work to see in the print. A loose strand in the footage makes the first deliberate change of posture apparent. The red-lit joints make the rising load legible and go dark partway through the rescue. She decides to turn her shoulder into the load in full view, then crosses with her face up. Injury stays off the page.
- **Place.** Hundred Steps Hall, a former covered assembly ground halfway up Tarse's brewery hill, ties all four kinds of records to one sightline. I considered the court archive in the old Varo mint and a traveling projection in Rusk Square. The Hall puts Ireta inside the composition she's been scrutinizing. It's a cold autumn afternoon into dusk. Wet coats steam in the stairwell, the old wool acoustic hangings have a sweet stale smell, a fan with a bent blade knocks in the former ticket room, and footfalls on the gallery send a faint second note up through the bench legs. A white stripe runs along the old wall toward the shrouded side door. The lower public stair runs beneath the gallery, while the raised stone dais and the side door have solid hill behind them. Press staff are testing projection and a brewer from uphill wheels out cups while families save seats with their gloves.

## Scenes

1. **The print, midafternoon (25%).** In the former ticket room, Ireta is restoring the victory print for a steward who wants a crop to suit the projection. Under oblique light she distinguishes a nick depicted on white plate from damage to the print. The nick has a crack running from it in the first coronation oil. When the girl seen in the photo takes the throne, the next oil shows a longer crack and a red joint exposed. The paintings make the grip look ceremonial. Ireta glimpses the present-day girl and her grandmother as the old woman tries to show her where the witnesses sat. Ireta looks for someone who drew the gesture from another angle.
2. **The witness seats, late afternoon (25%).** The execution artist leaves a suggestion of faces above the condemned on one sheet. The queen reaches back on the following sheet. Ireta stays with the drawn hands long enough to see whom the woman can steady and what the other hand is still doing. In security footage of the republic's first count, a footfall dips the gallery in real time, the woman catches the eye, and she directs a spectator off the sag with her other hand. The radio log calls her an unauthorized guest. Ireta switches to the current feed and sees the woman again as an armored finger drops. The public is already climbing and she keeps the old photograph open as she goes to stop them.
3. **The crossing, dusk (long scene, 50%).** The stair beneath the east gallery is already full. The way out has to be across the dais. Ireta locates the side exit by lining up the photo on her tablet with the stripe and the mason's mark still visible beside the hanging. An old woman gets stuck crossing the gap where the wood pulls away from the dais, and her granddaughter balks at going first. The woman in white talks the girl onto stone while Ireta holds the spring-loaded door open and sends the last of the crowd down the stair. Give the woman the moment when she feels the floor move farther than she has ever let it and decides she can spend what remains of the armor. She rolls her shoulder under the housing so she can keep the tie in place and give the grandmother a hand through the crossing. She could still stand there after the last person is safely out. Ireta holds the door and has to wait for the woman to decide. The woman opens the cuff of her spent gauntlet, pulls her arm free, and deliberately steps her shoulder out from under the load. Keep the mechanics visible at the point of load, and leave the armor's maker and the place's wider political history to incidental traces.

**Ending.** The republic's prepared projection loses its surface when the gallery comes down. The outer security camera sees the woman step out with the grandmother and granddaughter instead of staying behind the important figures. She tucks her silver hair back with the hand that still closes and waits for the old woman to find her feet on the brewery stair. The other gauntlet is still in the iron eye. The record of its loss can also be the record of what she chose to do with it.
