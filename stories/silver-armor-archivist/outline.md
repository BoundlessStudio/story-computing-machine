# At the Back of the Picture

## Promised experience

The request puts an archivist at work restoring a photograph of a victorious royal family. The discovery is the silver-haired woman behind them, followed by her appearances in their coronation paintings, in sketches of their executions, and in security footage from the successor republic. It is the same woman in the same white armor, with more damage as the records advance. The changing media invite the reader to do the archivist's double take and then examine each new image for what time did to her. Victory, coronation, and execution suggest the span of a dynasty and an uncanny continuity beneath sharp changes of fortune. I expect the damage and her place in the pictures to acquire new meaning. The request leaves the woman's allegiance and nature, any relationship beyond the archivist's attention to her, and how fully the mystery resolves open. The attached portrait supplies her visual presence.

## Takes considered

- **One hall, three governments’ pictures (chosen).** The family's conquests and the republic's founding keep bringing people to the same place. The archivist discovers what the woman has been doing there all along and joins her the night she decides to stop. The live encounter makes the old pictures sources of immediate, physical knowledge.
- **The last sitter.** A visit from the woman sets the archive search in motion. She wants a portrait with her own name, while the archivist discovers she helped bring some of the rulers to power and walked some to their deaths. The sitting with the pictures laid out could give the two women a charged negotiation. It would put their relationship ahead of the surprise of finding her.
- **A record out of order.** Two archivists at the public showing realize the damage puts the footage before some of the paintings in the woman's personal chronology. They attempt to get a message into her next appearance through the pose of a sitter. That set piece has room for wonder and would make a different story of the linear accumulation.

## Provisional direction

In Tarse, the Republic is reopening the Hundred Steps Hall seven years after the executions. Archivist Ireta is preparing the restored victory photograph for the inaugural projection. Once she notices the woman, she recognizes her in two coronation oils and in several execution sketches. Different artists misrepresent the gesture, but the woman always has her armored hand closed through the same iron ring at the back of the dais. The ring is the exposed end of the old tie that holds the east gallery up. She has been supplying the purchase its ruined anchorage lost in the conquest. Each fresh break in the armor follows the line of force through her body. Security footage lets Ireta see her catch the ring when it slips, something the still pictures made look like a heraldic pose. The soldier's longevity and the armor's workings stay strange. What she is doing here can be seen.

Ireta goes to stop the reopening and finds the gallery has begun to fill while the last armored fingers are giving. The woman wants the public out of the hall so she can let it fall. She has held while others made consequential use of the place, and she has her own limit on what she will spend herself to preserve. Together they get the east gallery's old side exit open. Ireta reads the way out in the frame of the victory picture and gets the audience to use it. The woman takes the entire gallery's weight on the plate of her shoulder while people cross the sag at its head, then lets the load go with enough time to step clear herself. Her final choice changes which parts of the building survive and where she appears in this republic's record.

## Craft plan

- **Teller.** Try Ireta in close third, against the clock and impatient with imprecise claims, and Ireta in retrospective first, trying to account for which details she previously helped to crop. The person who can spend an hour looking for one eye in a print wants the officials to see her work at full size and has cooperated with their idea of what belongs in frame.
- **Cast and speech.** Ireta gets exact and talks too long when someone cuts her off before she's shown her evidence. In her head she habitually distinguishes what she sees from how she knows it. The woman measures her words by what the person beside her can do. She gives directions with the quick, intimate confidence of someone who has had to get strangers across dangerous rooms. Small talk comes back to her at odd times when her hands are busy. She wants the living out and off her hands. The unnamed hall steward speaks in accommodating circulars early, then eventually has to give ordinary, comprehensible instructions.
- **The woman in the original.** She has a level, assessing look from inside a ragged white hood, long silver hair, and a half-open, articulated hand. Red shows in the recessed joints of the chipped white plates. She plants herself, lets pressure settle, and absorbs the cost with a reserve that reads as self-possession. Keep the hood and fitted mechanical profile, with attention to face, hands, and weight. The edge of the hood shelters a face Ireta has to work to see in the print. A loose strand of hair in the footage makes the first deliberate change of posture apparent. The illuminated joints make the rising load legible and then go dark along the failed gauntlet. In the hall she decides to turn her whole shoulder into the load when her hand won't close, then crosses the room with her face up. No close depiction of injury.
- **Place.** The Hundred Steps Hall, originally a covered indoor assembly ground halfway up Tarse's brewery hill, ties all four kinds of records to one sightline. I considered the court archive in the old Varo mint and a traveling projection in Rusk Square. Neither puts Ireta in the actual composition she has been scrutinizing. It is a cold autumn afternoon into dusk. Wet-brown coats breathe in the heated stairwell, the old wool acoustic hangings have a sweet stale smell, the table fan in the borrowed conservation room has a bent blade, and a footfall on the east gallery sends a faint second note up through the bench legs. A white stripe in the old wall paint leads through the now-shrouded side passage. While press staff test projection and a brewer from uphill wheels out cups, families try to save seats with their gloves. The bowed gallery and the blind turn of the public stairs make the official exit dangerous once the floor moves. The shrouded audience exit offers a short crossing onto the brewery stair and can be recognized by the stripe and the stonemason's mark in the old photograph.

## Scenes

1. **The print, midafternoon (25%).** In the Hall's former ticket room, Ireta is restoring the victory print for the steward, who wants a framed crop that suits the projection. Under oblique light she distinguishes a nick depicted on the woman's white plate from damage to the dark print itself. The first coronation oil puts the same nick by her hand with a crack running from it. In a later oil the crack is longer and one red joint shows through. The painter has flattened the hand into a gesture of allegiance. Ireta goes to see if anyone drew it differently.
2. **What is left out, late afternoon (25%).** In the ticket room Ireta opens the execution sketches, whose independent purpose was to record the condemned one by one. The artist drew a royal hand reaching for the woman's and the woman with fingers still closed through the ring. Her cape is torn and a plate edge bent. Frames from the republic's security archive, then the current gallery feed, give Ireta the auditory and moving evidence the other records couldn't: the tie contracts with a dull report, she adjusts under it, and a piece of gauntlet drops to the floor. Ireta can point out a fresh failure to the steward and sees that the public is already climbing the stairs. She keeps the old photograph open on her tablet.
3. **The gallery, dusk (long scene, 50%).** Ireta tries to call audiences back down before she can see how the public stairs have shifted. The woman shows her the span she can still hold and asks for another way off it. Ireta finds the shrouded side exit by putting the photo up against the actual wall and uncovers its means of opening. Let the practical and emotional argument happen during the crossing. Ireta wants to get the woman to move before the metal goes. The woman makes them look at the one family still on the gallery and changes her grip, knowing precisely which part of the armor she will be unable to take with her. Let Ireta stay to hold the door despite the urge to pull her away. The woman chooses the moment to release, and they get out onto the brewery stair together. Keep the founding politics, the inventor of the armor, and accession dates to incidental detail.

**Ending.** The republic's prepared projection loses its surface when the gallery comes down. One security camera covering the outer stair records the woman after the fall. Ireta is close enough to see her face when she tucks her silver hair back with the hand that still closes. The other gauntlet is lodged at the iron ring. The living woman steps down into the moving public where the next record can show both the new damage and, for the first time, her walking out of the back of the picture.
