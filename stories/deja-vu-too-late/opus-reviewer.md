# Opus review: The Warning Comes Second

Independent audit for a later editing pass. It is not a `review.md` verdict,
does not change canon state, and does not authorize edits by itself.

## Verdict

- Dialogue: REVISE — Dalsen reports a control input he has said he never received, one mid-story run turns into correction ping-pong, and Kessa states the lesson just before the release.
- Prose: PASS — clean, concrete and well paced; only minor glossing fragments, one muddled staging beat and a few word slips.
- Continuity and prompt: PASS — the verbatim line, the complete resolution and the 2,880-word length all meet the prompt, and the range and clock arithmetic hold; the remaining issues are small scale and lag nits.
- Edit scope: targeted-dialogue
- Existing review.md said: Verdict PASS; "Dialogue: PASS". I partly disagree: the voices are differentiated as it claims, but it missed the Dalsen evidence contradiction and the L129–L141 run.

This story's dialogue is better grounded than most in the collection. The knock code, the "four side" mishearing, the overlapping transmissions and the missed receipt that forces Kessa to hold all come from the broken channels and do real plot work. Radio discipline also earns much of the terseness. The failures are concentrated in three places. At L165 Dalsen states what Kessa chose during the gap, which breaks the evidence limit the story has set up. At L129–L141 the cast stops working the problem and debates what counts as evidence, trading corrections and ending on a quip. At L247 Kessa gives a one-line moral that the hold scene has just shown to be wrong. The edit pass should fix those three places, cut the narration that explains lines after they are spoken, and otherwise leave the procedural texture and the understated ending alone.

## Edit guardrails

- The prompt's line must stay verbatim, delivered by Dalsen's recorded voice at L21: "If you've ever had déjà vu, then it's too late." Keep the close third-person past on Kessa and a broadly accessible rating. Keep the length inside 2,500–4,000 words (it is about 2,880 now). The active craft profile is prospective-2026-08-21.
- The 96 lost seconds must stay unrecovered. No source may tell Kessa outright what she chose, and her body's anticipation plus physical evidence is the only route back. Fixes to D1 must strengthen this limit, not loosen it.
- Keep the device rules: one stored baseline and one charge; it restores working and episodic memory only; motor prediction, stress chemistry and sensory association remain; the fused bridge proves firing and timing, not choices.
- Keep the arithmetic as it stands: closure point two five, ranges 184 → 156 → 112 → 96 → 72 → 58 → 49, clocks `00:07`, `09:41`, `11:32`, `12:04`, and "seven and a half minutes" at 112. Do not add dialogue that shifts these times.
- Keep the physical facts: one knock means yes (and later "high"), two means no; Orivan's right leg is trapped; the lever is in his right-hip pouch; he has forty-three minutes of air; the hatch is thirty degrees above him; the tug rolls starboard; all six drums are lost.
- Universe: no LOCKED or CANON conflicts found. Personhood protection is honored because Kessa keeps her consent and responsibility and the cost is logged. No water infrastructure appears, and none should be added.
- Do not add a conversation that explains the gap, the déjà vu or the theme. The ending should stay on first aid and the ledger.

## Dialogue findings

### D1 · Blocking · Dalsen reports a control input he said he never received

- Where: L67 and L165, the recorder check and "Cargo rail state"
- Text:
> I received the loss event and harness firing time. I did not receive your controls.
> Last frame shows latches three and four released
> Release command began before my exterior feed failed.
- Problem: Ground/knowledge. At L67 Dalsen lists what he received and rules out her control inputs, and L131 adds that the bridge does not record "what you chose." At L165 he reports as fact that a release command began, which is one of her lost choices. He can only have inferred it from latch states, so an inference is being presented as an observation. It also comes at the story's pivot, where Kessa learns what her hand was reaching for, and it lets a remote source hand her a piece of the lost interval that the story says no source can supply.
- Fix: Keep "Last frame shows latches three and four released. One and two unknown." and cut "Release command began before my exterior feed failed." Leave the inference to Kessa and the reader; L167 already makes it ("An unfinished action."). Then make L67's inventory admit an exterior camera feed so L165 isn't a surprise (for example, add "some exterior frames" to his list), and keep "I did not receive your controls." L245 can keep Dalsen's question as a question.

### D2 · Major · The evidence debate turns into correction ping-pong and ends on a quip

- Where: L129–L141, just after Kessa kills the green overlay
- Text:
> I don't—my control choices aren't there.
> They won't be in the fused bridge
> Exterior recorder shows the port truss twist
> “Consistent,” Dalsen said, “not complete.”
> Orivan's trapped. Those aren't missing.
> Glad we settled that.
- Problem: This is the collection's house pattern in one run. Kessa says her memory has a hole. Dalsen answers a question about the bridge that she didn't ask (Ground/uptake), and he restates device limits already given at L59 and L67. Pavax summarizes evidence the reader has just seen (L65, L103, L125–L127) and cites Kessa's exterior recorder, which only she has looked at (Ground/knowledge; nothing establishes a downlink to the ring). Dalsen then corrects Pavax's wording, Kessa corrects Dalsen, and Orivan closes the debate with a sardonic last word. Nothing changes: Kessa had already reached the conclusion at L127. For six lines at 156 meters the characters argue about what counts as evidence, with Dalsen and Pavax as an explanatory chorus.
- Fix: Cut Pavax's L133 and Dalsen's L135 entirely. Either cut Dalsen's L131 or make it respond to what she said (her memory), not the bridge. Illustrative only: "They won't come back. I'm sorry." Keep Kessa's "The cluster is sheared" with the cut-lip beat and "Orivan's trapped", and drop "Those aren't missing". Put her "control choices" into plainer words. Cut "Glad we settled that" and the L141 gloss, and go straight to her thruster check at L143, so her move to present facts shows in what she does.

### D3 · Major · Thesis line before the release

- Where: L245–L247, the fluent-hands paragraph after the missed receipt
- Text:
> When Dalsen asked whether she remembered beginning the cargo release, no answer came.
> “I know where my hand goes now,” she said. “That's enough.”
- Problem: Philosopher. "That's enough" states the story's lesson aloud at its key moment, and the lesson is wrong for this story. At L235 her hand's memory would have released at the wrong crossing, and she survives because she held for Pavax's boundary and Orivan's count. The line flattens the story's real point, which is that body memory has to be checked against present evidence.
- Fix: Cut "That's enough." If she answers at all, let the answer shut down Dalsen's data-gathering in the middle of the crisis, which gives their relationship a real beat. Illustrative only: "No. Ask me after." Keep "I know where my hand goes now" only if it reads as a plain fact about the switch, not a principle; cutting it is also fine.

### D4 · Minor · Narration explains lines just spoken

- Where: L141, L151, L157, L273
- Text:
> Pain shortened him to the useful edge of a sentence.
> Pavax's voice stayed in numbers because the numbers were his work.
> No defense. No apology.
> The exchange was so ordinary that Kessa trusted it more than the green had ever deserved.
- Problem: Each sentence tells the reader how to take the line or exchange before it. L151 is also inaccurate, since that speech contains no numbers. L141 misdescribes a joke as "the useful edge of a sentence". L273 comments on a triage exchange that already makes its point.
- Fix: Cut L141 (it goes with D2), cut "because the numbers were his work" and "No defense. No apology.", and cut or shorten L273 to a physical beat. Keep L331's "not a signal, just pain": in a scene built on signals it tells the reader how to read a gesture, and it doesn't repeat what a line already said.

### D5 · Minor · Unclear referents in the radio overlap

- Where: L93–L105 ("Don't—"), L231–L233 (the missed receipt)
- Text:
> “—right leg. Collar's bent. Don't—”
> I have a latch transition—possibly one, possibly noise—
> “Both of you clear.” Kessa kept her thumb off the switch. “Pavax, repeat.”
- Problem: Ground/referent. "Both of you clear" has no workable referent. The last two voices were Orivan and Dalsen, and clearing Orivan would cut off the roll count she needs. The earlier pair was Pavax and Dalsen, and she immediately asks Pavax to talk. "Possibly one" could mean latch one or a single transition. Orivan's "Don't—" is answered by a position report, so the warning inside it stays unresolved until L185. That delay is acceptable, but readers stumble at L105.
- Fix: Name who is to clear (Dalsen). Make it "possibly latch one". Optionally let Orivan's clarified line at L103 or Kessa's reply register that "Don't—" was a warning about rolling further to port. Keep the static and the interruptions.

### D6 · Minor · Stiff procedural lines

- Where: L205, L237–L239, L303
- Text:
> Count the truss vibration as the bent collar comes under load.
> You were not heard acknowledging.
> No acknowledgment because I didn't receive it.
> No thrust available worth naming.
- Problem: L205 is manual prose addressed to a trapped, injured partner, and "count" doesn't fit "vibration": he is being asked to feel the roll, not count anything. At the tensest point, L237–L239 turns into a short argument about who failed to acknowledge. "No thrust available worth naming" is a dry last word in the same register as D2.
- Fix: Put L205 in words Kessa would actually say to Orivan: tell him the high, level, low feel she wants, and keep "Forget the display" only if his suit has one. Cut "You were not heard acknowledging" or reduce Kessa's reply to "Didn't get it. Holding." Reduce L303 to a plain acknowledgment.

## Prose findings

### P1 · Minor · Fragment paragraphs that interpret the beat

- Where: L123, L167, L243
- Text:
> Old green. Not a lie, not guidance. A loop that could still prove itself correct.
> An unfinished action. Her body's next movement had not come from nowhere.
> One option gone. The hull rolled on.
- Problem: These are verbless fragment runs and "not X, not Y" reversals that tell the reader what a beat means right after it lands. They repeat the story's own ideas (L113, L127), which weakens those lines.
- Fix: Keep L127, which counts the evidence, and L113. Cut L123's middle fragment, or all of L123. Shorten L167 to its first fragment or merge it into L169. Fold L243 into the next paragraph.

### P2 · Minor · First near-release: the wrong part kicks, and it's unclear whether she pressed

- Where: L195–L199, first attempt at the manual release
- Text:
> Press with the thumb, because the old switch kicked.
> The guard kicked against her thumb exactly as her hand had promised.
> Her thumb pressed. The hull struck.
- Problem: At L15 her hand promised that the switch would kick, and that payoff arrives at L289. Here the guard kicks, which muddles the prediction and borrows from the payoff. "Her thumb pressed" is flashback, but it reads at first as present action, so the reader can't be sure she didn't press the switch before "She lifted her thumb."
- Fix: At L195 have the guard lift, not kick. Mark the flash clearly as remembered sensation (for example, "Her thumb had pressed. The hull had struck.") so "She lifted her thumb" is plainly her taking her thumb off the guard.

### P3 · Minor · Word slips and a collar that changes name

- Where: L19, L23, L91, L291
- Text:
> A red numeral on the dead-center display
> The consented harness had stored one neural baseline before approach
> A contact microphone in the exterior work collar caught a torn piece of his voice.
> Kessa's harness caught her cut lip against her teeth.
- Problem: A readout of `00:07 AFTER REPRISE` is not "a numeral", and a harness cannot be "consented". The collar is called "exterior work collar", "collar", "bent collar" and "service collar". At L93, "Collar's bent" from a suited man can be read as his suit's neck ring, which would be an air emergency. A harness cannot catch a lip.
- Fix: Change to "red readout", "consent-keyed harness" or similar, and "the jolt drove her cut lip against her teeth". At L91 name the part once as the tug's service collar, and use the same name afterwards.

## Continuity and prompt findings

### C1 · Minor · The harness's purpose rests on one jargon phrase

- Where: L23 and L111, the harness inventory and the training memory
- Text:
> baseline sealed, outboard loss latch confirmed, cognitive lock recorded, working and episodic restore complete, charge exhausted
> She had considered that an untidy footnote when she signed consent.
- Problem: The reader is never told why a pilot would consent to a device that deletes the worst ninety-six seconds of a crisis. The only clue is "cognitive lock", which the outline uses to mean a pilot frozen by shock, buried in a machine list. Without it the premise can look like a device built to hurt its user.
- Fix: Add one clause to the L111 training memory saying what the harness is for, as Dalsen sold it: returning a pilot locked up by shock to a working mind. Keep it to a clause, not a lecture, and don't touch the L21 line.

### C2 · Minor · Scale, signal lag and mass

- Where: L25, L121, L175, L157 and L171
- Text:
> She remembered the ring no larger than a coin beyond the glass.
> A pause traveled out to the ring and back.
> His answer came after the radio delay.
> pointing eighteen loaded tonnes at their wall
> She placed the eighteen tonnes into the manual impulse model.
- Problem: At point two five closure, ninety-six seconds covers about 24 meters, so the ring cannot go from coin-sized to half the window. At 100–200 meters radio delay is effectively zero, so the pauses and the "one point eight seconds old" solutions need a different cause. Eighteen tonnes is the whole loaded tug at L157 but reads as the cargo mass at L171.
- Fix: Make her last memory of the ring larger (for example, filling a third of the glass), or leave out the size. Put the lag on the ring's radar processing or a relay, not on travel time; the "one point eight seconds" lines can stay. Make L171 say "the loaded mass" or give the cargo its own figure.
