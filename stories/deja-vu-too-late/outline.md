# Outline

## Story

- Reader-facing title: *The Warning Comes Second*.
- Premise and central promise: An unnamed traveler, already unable to find the edge of an apparently endless wheat field, reaches a seamless white-marble block whose four square faces are each a little taller and wider than a person. Every straight walk away has returned to it without a visible turn. When the traveler rounds its first vertical corner, noon jumps toward evening and an exact childhood moment overlays the wheat with the bodily certainty of déjà vu. The second face bears a shallow hand-cut sentence: “If you've ever had déja vu, then it's too late.” Fulfill the line literally and centrally: each corner joins the present encounter to one of four earlier moments when the traveler felt unexplained familiarity. Those past sensations were caused by sensory spill from this future circuit, so once any one of them occurred, entering the field and completing the first circuit were already part of causal history. It is too late to avoid the stone, recover the untouched past, or turn déjà vu into a warning that could have changed it. After closing the four-corner circuit, however, the traveler can leave; the unresolved choice is whether to take the newly linear path home or round the first corner again for another sight of a dead brother.
- Intended reader experience: Pastoral cosmic unease under an enormous bright sky: wind combs wheat to every horizon, the modestly human-scaled marble block dominates because nothing else interrupts the world, and sun, shadow, distance, and sequence cease agreeing at its corners. Move from awe and physical disorientation to the intimate dread of recognizing where four lifelong flashes of familiarity came from. Keep the mechanism exact enough to follow without explaining the stone's makers or purpose. The ending should release the spatial trap but not repair grief: the traveler escapes one compulsory circuit by refusing a voluntary second one.
- Generating force:
  - Mode: Plot-led four-face causal circuit. Each corner supplies one missing half of an earlier déjà vu and one stronger temptation to keep moving: orientation, explanation, the brother's last call, then the chance to repeat proximity. Progress is completion rather than escalation; the story returns to the first face with its meaning changed.
  - Focal attachment or contradiction: Years earlier, during an estrangement, the traveler silenced a younger brother's final ordinary call after a jolt of déjà vu, then learned of his sudden death before they spoke again. The traveler has treated that sensation as advance notice missed through pride. The stone proves it was an aftereffect arriving early, not usable foreknowledge—removing one kind of guilt while denying the fantasy of correction.
  - Plot-led wants and useful limit, if applicable: The traveler immediately wants shade, a boundary, and a route out before thirst and the accelerated sky become dangerous. Competing with escape is the desire to reach the corner containing the missed call and force a different answer. The useful flaw is retroactive control: the traveler believes that recognizing an event should confer power over it, so every failed attempt to leave becomes a reason to inspect the next corner rather than stop supplying the circuit.
- Counterforce or complication:
  - Source: Within the affected field, walking straight away from an uncompleted circuit returns to another face without a felt turn; climbing fails because the top edge recedes by the same handspan each time while the square face remains unchanged from the ground. A corner carries a living traveler's sight, sound, pressure, and emotion into one already-lived moment of familiarity, but no body, object, or new information can enter that past. Turning back crosses another temporal join rather than undoing the last one. The traveler's thirst, cuts, carried objects, and fatigue continue in one body and one material sequence.
  - Credible inside logic, if a person: There is no antagonist and no consciousness inside the monument. The dead brother appears only in already-lived sensory overlaps and retains the words and choices he made then; he is not a spirit, reconstruction, trapped mind, or guide waiting to forgive the traveler.
  - Pressure or change created: The traveler cannot leave before the four existing echoes are closed, cannot convert recognition into intervention, and cannot know whether a second circuit would ever reopen a path. The only clear outward route appears after the first circuit, at the same time the first corner begins offering the brother's voice again.
- Narrative design:
  - POV, tense, distance, and limitation: Close first-person present in the field, with brief past-tense sensory overlays for the four earlier moments. The narrator is exact about walking pace, sun angle, thirst, wheat movement, marble temperature, and remembered details, but cannot see around a corner without crossing it or distinguish explanation from hope until the circuit closes. Keep the brother exterior and historically fixed.
  - Time shape and compression: One clockwise circuit in nine movements. The traveler experiences roughly two hours, while the sky advances from high noon through sunset, night, dawn, and back to a pale morning. Render each corner and the missed-call overlap in scene; compress only repeated straight walks that return to the stone. The ordinary world advances by one night, not by years, and no flash-forward or later public coda follows.
  - Information and reveal strategy: Begin with spatial recurrence before naming time distortion. Let the first corner produce déjà vu; place the exact warning on the second face, after the symptom. Prove causal continuity through unchanged injuries and supplies, then match specific present sensations—a marble-cold palm, wheat rasp at the wrist, the narrator's own shouted syllable—to details remembered from four earlier episodes. At the last join, show that shouting “answer” into the missed-call moment causes only the exact incomprehensible jolt the past self already felt; nothing is rewritten. The return of a genuine horizon path after the fourth corner establishes the bounded exit. Do not reveal a prophecy, alternate timeline, overwrite, duplicate person, dream, universal cause of déjà vu, or explanatory civilization.
  - Governing movement: Walk straight; return to white marble; mark the wheat; return again; touch the first face; round the corner; lose an afternoon; relive childhood familiarity; read the late warning; test retreat; fail to climb; cross the second corner; identify a second past echo; cross the third for the silenced call; try to intervene; discover that the attempted warning was always the sensation; reach the fourth face through night and dawn; complete the last echo; return to the first face; see an ordinary hedgerow at last; hear the brother beginning again beyond the next corner; leave without completing another turn.
- Speculative surplus: Food and grain carried around a corner slip among adjacent stages of ripeness without changing quantity: bread becomes briefly oven-warm, then hard enough to scrape the mouth, while plucked wheat turns milk-soft and later sprouts in the traveler's pocket. This makes provisions unpleasant and materially strange but cannot date a face, identify an exit, carry information to the past, or affect the corner sequence.
- Structural distinction: A solitary circuit around one small, immovable center in a landscape with no apparent edge. Its four movements deepen causal recognition rather than enlarge spectacle, add pursuers, or accumulate copies. The decisive event is a private refusal at a sunlit corner, not technical proof, rescue action, artifact destruction, crowd conversion, institutional rewrite, or symbolic correction of the past.
- Decisive turn, deepening, or recognition: At the third corner, the traveler is again holding the old phone while the brother's name brightens its screen. From the field-present, the traveler shouts for the past self to answer. The past hand pauses with the same déjà vu remembered for years, then silences the call exactly as before; the brother's death and the traveler's choice do not change. At the fourth corner, the traveler recognizes that the stone did not send a warning before the call—the later cry caused a sensation without meaning. Completing the circuit opens a straight path to a hedgerow and road. The brother's voice also starts behind the first corner, clear enough to promise another pass. The traveler chooses the road while it exists.
- Aftereffect or live uncertainty: The traveler reaches the ordinary field edge after one missing night, dehydrated and carrying bread that has gone hard again. The marble block is no longer visible from the road, but its origin, purpose, duration, other visitors, and whether a second circuit would trap someone permanently remain unknown. The brother stays dead, the unanswered call stays unanswered, and the traveler receives no absolution. What changes is narrower: déjà vu was not an instruction deliberately ignored, and one encounter with the past does not obligate an endless return.
- Opening / ending relation: Open with the traveler walking toward the marble because every attempted departure has placed it ahead again, treating the monument as the only remaining source of direction. End with the monument finally behind an ordinary wall of wheat while the traveler follows a drainage rut toward engine noise; no turn is required, and the first mundane corner in the road remains simply a corner.

## Beats

1. Alone in apparently endless wheat under a huge blue sky, the traveler uses steps, sun, and bent stalks to walk a straight escape line, only to see the square-faced white marble block rise ahead for the third time.
2. The traveler measures and tests the seamless block, finds each face slightly taller and wider than a person, cannot gain the receding top edge, and rounds the first vertical corner.
3. Sunlight jumps toward evening. A childhood room and the field coexist for several breaths; cool marble under the present palm completes the first remembered déjà vu. The second face carries the exact carved warning.
4. Retreat and another straight walk both return to the stone. Persistent thirst, a cut, and missing water rule out bodily reset, while bread and loose grain change ripeness at the next corner.
5. The second corner overlays a later youthful episode. Wheat brushing the present wrist supplies the unexplained touch remembered from that day, establishing that current sensations leak into fixed past moments.
6. The third corner opens the estranged brother's final call. The traveler shouts for the past self to answer, but the cry arrives only as the same meaningless familiarity; the phone is silenced and history remains intact.
7. Through night and dawn, the traveler crosses the fourth corner and completes the last known episode. The first face returns under pale morning light, now with a hedgerow and road visible in one straight direction.
8. The brother's voice begins again beyond the next corner. The traveler recognizes that another circuit could offer repetition without correction and might close the only demonstrated exit.
9. The traveler walks toward the hedgerow without turning, reaches an ordinary drainage rut and road, and stops where the story's first normal corner asks for no memory, sacrifice, or explanation.

## Continuity

- Canon used: One coherent world permits independent local systems and partially recoverable truth; memory-like phenomena require story-specific mechanisms; living attention may supply an effect that nonliving material stores or shapes; material consequence and personhood persist.
- Boundaries and unknowns: The white marble block is a nonliving, non-agentic, story-local focus shaped by the living traveler's attention and memory. It is not a god, mind, judge, prophecy engine, or autonomous magic user. The episode does not reset or overwrite time, branch history, copy a person, revive the brother, alter a record, or make all déjà vu supernatural. Only four exact episodes in this traveler's life are established as sensory spill from this one circuit. Establish no relationship to the Glass Gate, a temporal anchor, the Fenwick watch, the Covenant of Hours, another crossing system, standing stone, field, marble artifact, or civilization. The monument's maker, original function, energy or magic source beyond the observed living participation, geographic era, and fate of other visitors remain unresolved.
