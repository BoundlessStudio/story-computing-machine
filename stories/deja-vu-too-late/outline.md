# Outline

## Story

- Reader-facing title: *The Warning Comes Second*.
- Premise and central promise: Locksmith and building-clearance contractor Halen Corren enters a condemned apartment block after her adult younger brother, Joss Corren, calls from inside while retrieving their late mother's cassette tin. On a stair landing Halen knows she has not seen before, she suffers a violent flash of familiarity; on the far side of its fire door someone has written, “If you've ever had déja vu, then it's too late.” The line proves locally precise rather than universally prophetic: whenever a person inside completes a route they recognize, the affected structure folds that route into another material copy before conscious recognition catches up. Déjà vu is the delayed sensation of having crossed, and by then the entrance in that copy no longer leads outside. Resolve why the warning must come second and whether Halen can reach Joss without multiplying the routes between them: she abandons retracing as rescue, cuts one unfamiliar lateral path through the building's service fabric, finds him, and escapes with him through a neighboring cellar. It is too late to turn back by the known way, not too late to make a way neither of them has rehearsed.
- Intended reader experience: Clear, tactile spatial horror rather than dream logic: familiar squeaks arrive on the wrong floor, chalk arrows disagree without changing, windows open onto light wells at impossible depths, and a sibling's hammer sounds close through a wall but distant around the hall. Let competence initially soothe the reader, then make Halen's wedges, numbered doors, and measured retracing produce the trap's worst growth. The release should be breathless and provisional rather than triumphant: the siblings get out by accepting an ugly one-use route, while the copied interiors, the unknown warning writer, and the cassette tin remain behind.
- Generating force:
  - Mode: Plot-led spatial fan. Every attempt to verify, backtrack, or approach a sound along a recognized route produces another adjacent version of that route. Halen's orderly inward search therefore widens the distance she is trying to close; movement reverses only when she treats walls and service voids, rather than doors and corridors, as the route.
  - Focal attachment or contradiction: Halen loves Joss by making danger legible and solvable; Joss resists being managed, especially after Halen dismissed the recordings he wants as estate clutter. Her rescue depends on trusting his present observations even when they contradict her map. He must decide whether the last physical cache of their mother's voice is worth repeating the passage that has already trapped them.
  - Plot-led wants and useful limit, if applicable: Halen immediately wants to locate Joss, get them both outside before the morning demolition crew arrives, and prevent him from pushing deeper for the tin. She also wants her professional account of the building to remain true: one entrance, six floors, two stairs, every lock and threshold recoverable. Her useful limit is route confidence. She verifies uncertainty by going back to the last known point, marks doors as if marks share one continuous space, and initially treats Joss's conflicting floor count as panic. Joss immediately wants the cassette tin and then wants Halen to stop following the corridors toward his voice; grief and embarrassment make him underreport how many times he has already doubled back.
- Counterforce or complication:
  - Source: A bounded, non-agentic spatial effect within the old block reflexively retains a route after a living person traverses it and folds a physical copy when that person completes a recognizable retracing. Time continues once; carried objects, fatigue, damage, calls, and injuries persist, while marks left in one layer do not appear in its neighbors unless they were already present on the surface copied. Windows and reopened exterior doors remain inside the affected footprint once a crossing has occurred. A freshly breached, one-way path can cross the footprint before it too is retraced. The structure does not lure, hunt, read motives, or choose victims.
  - Credible inside logic, if a person: No antagonist is required. Joss is a resistant countervoice with something worth protecting: the tin holds their mother's ordinary home recordings, which Halen slated for disposal without listening. He believes one more short return is different from Halen's repeated systematic passes, but after seeing the corridor beside the tin reproduce, he retains agency and chooses to leave it. Halen is right that a recording is not worth his life; Joss is right that calling it junk let her avoid both the recordings and his grief.
  - Pressure or change created: The siblings cannot maximize certainty, retrieve the tin, and leave together. Following phones or voices by hallway is unreliable because copied rooms remain physically adjacent through walls while their corridors diverge. The coming demolition provides an ordinary deadline but never becomes a public rescue set piece: the immediate danger is that Halen's preferred method keeps generating more building than they can cross.
- Narrative design:
  - POV, tense, distance, and limitation: Close third-person past through Halen. Keep the camera intimate with lock feedback, stair counts, wall thickness, breath, sound transmission, and her reflex to diagnose a threshold before admitting fear. She cannot see Joss's layers or know whether a voice comes through a doorway, phone, duct, or party wall. Joss's objective is carried in terse calls, interrupted knocks, and what he does after they meet; do not enter the phenomenon or grant it intention.
  - Time shape and compression: Approximately ninety continuous minutes from Halen opening the street entrance to both siblings reaching the neighboring cellar, with no flashback scenes and no later institutional coda. Render the first retracing and déjà vu, the failed return to the entrance, the conflicting phone call, the wall-sound discovery, the breach, reunion, tin choice, and crawl to the service hatch in full. Compress only repeated checks through a short accumulation of changed floor numbers, doors, and marks so the reader feels multiplication without losing the governing rule.
  - Information and reveal strategy: Let Halen initially dismiss the sentence as frightened nonsense because almost everyone has experienced ordinary déjà vu somewhere. Establish the local rule by discrepancies that preserve causality: continuous phone time, a wedge and chalk mark remaining in the stair copy where she placed them, a second unmarked version beside it, Joss's carried pry bar changing location only with him, and fresh damage persisting. Partial scratches from an earlier trapped person can add “DON'T TAKE THE WAY YOU KNOW,” but no notebook, expert, late cosmology, or complete manual explains the effect. Halen infers that the sensation follows rather than causes the crossing and that fresh lateral damage has not yet been folded. Do not reveal a time reset, prophecy, alternate-life memory, sentient building, or relationship to any established gate, watch, predictive system, or memory mechanism.
  - Governing movement: Pick; wedge; number; climb; answer Joss; backtrack; recognize; read the warning; turn toward the presumed entrance; open onto an interior laundry instead of the street; mark harder; retrace to verify; multiply; compare conflicting floor counts; stop trusting the hall; test continuity through a drilled wall; hear Joss's current hammer blows; cut laterally through an unrecognized partition; meet in the service cavity; see the tin down a familiar copied corridor; let Joss choose against returning; descend once through pipe chases and a disused coal hatch; force the neighboring cellar grille; emerge; refuse re-entry.
- Speculative surplus: In copied layers, synthetic cloth accumulates aggressive static charge. Halen's jacket crackles, loose screws cling briefly to Joss's sleeve, and both phones fur over with lint, creating minor tool-handling irritation and small comic shocks. The charge cannot identify an original, map layers, transmit voices, predict a fold, disable the effect, or open the escape route.
- Structural distinction: A lateral rescue that begins as a clean line through a six-floor plan, fans into mutually adjacent copies, and ends as a single unrepeated cut across walls. The decisive work happens inside a cramped service cavity and pipe chase, not at the entrance, on a stage, in a laboratory, before a crowd, or through public technical proof. There is no loop-reset escalation, pursuing villain, collective turn, converted witness, institutional rewrite, or polished symbolic restoration of what the siblings lose.
- Decisive turn, deepening, or recognition: Halen drills a thumb-width hole toward Joss's hammering and sees his light less than an arm's length away although her map places him two floors above. Rather than return to the stair to “correct” either account, she accepts that both observations are materially true in different copies and dismantles the thin partition between them. Once reunited, Joss spots the red cassette tin through an open doorway along a corridor both recognize. Halen starts to order him into the service chase; instead she stops and tells him the choice is his. He takes the single cassette already in his pocket, leaves the tin visible, and enters the fresh breach. Halen follows rather than going first, letting his direct observation govern the next turns. Their changed relation does not solve the structure; it prevents them from supplying it another repeated route.
- Aftereffect or live uncertainty: Halen and Joss reach the cellar next door bruised, dusty, and missing most of the recordings. The ordinary street lies one flight above, but neither assumes the copied rooms have vanished or that the earlier warning writer escaped. Halen can call to delay demolition and report people possibly inside, yet she has no safe route back, no decisive public evidence, and no basis for promising a rescue method will work twice. Joss still resents her handling of their mother's effects; one salvaged cassette offers something to hear later, not reconciliation or an answer from the dead.
- Opening / ending relation: Open with Halen feeling the street-door lock turn cleanly and telling herself that any door she opens can be opened again. End in the neighboring cellar when an ordinary swollen door refuses her key; Joss shoulders it while she lifts the dragging bottom edge, and they open it together without retracing the route that got them there. The first threshold confirms solitary professional certainty; the last requires two incomplete kinds of leverage and leads only to the next real space.

## Beats

1. Halen reaches the condemned block after Joss's broken call, picks the street entrance, wedges it, and starts a professional route log while the demolition notice fixes the morning deadline. Joss says he has the red cassette tin but cannot find the stairs he used.
2. She climbs toward his reported floor, then backtracks one landing to verify a room number. On returning upward she experiences a full-body flash of familiarity before seeing the scratched warning on the far side of a fire door: “If you've ever had déja vu, then it's too late.”
3. Halen dismisses the line and follows her chalk arrows down. The expected street door opens into a laundry room; her wedge and first arrow exist beyond an adjacent fire door she does not remember crossing, while her phone shows uninterrupted time.
4. She repeats the route to prove she took a wrong turn. A second laundry, an unmarked stair, and two incompatible versions of the same numbered landing establish accumulation rather than erased or rewritten events.
5. Joss calls from what he insists is the fourth floor while Halen hears his voice faintly through a sixth-floor wall. He admits he has passed the cassette tin three times and kept returning to it whenever the stairs changed.
6. More marks increase rather than reduce ambiguity. Halen finds the earlier writer's partial instruction not to take the known way and notices that newly drilled plaster remains unique while reopened doors lead into copies.
7. Halen stops navigating by corridor. She has Joss remain still and strike a pipe, tests successive walls without circling back, and follows the immediate vibration laterally through kitchens, closets, and a laundry partition.
8. A drilled sight hole reveals Joss close across a thin wall despite the conflicting floor counts. Halen cuts the lath and he pries from his side; they meet inside the service cavity rather than at any mapped doorway.
9. Through the opening, Joss sees the cassette tin down the route that already trapped him. Halen returns the decision to him. He pockets the one cassette he had carried, leaves the tin, and chooses the unrepeated service chase.
10. They descend once through pipes and old laundry ducts, managing static-clung tools and one failing grille without reversing. Halen follows Joss's direct view when her measurements cease to fit.
11. They force a disused hatch into the neighboring cellar and close no explanatory circle. Safe on the other side of the affected footprint, Halen calls for a demolition delay while the siblings face the ordinary locked cellar door together.
