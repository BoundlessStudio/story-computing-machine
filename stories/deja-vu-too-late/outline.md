# Outline

## Story

- Premise and central promise: On approach to an orbital service ring, pilot Kessa sees the docking display repeat a green alignment sequence her hands already distrust. Her emergency harness erased ninety-six seconds of explicit memory, not time. Its post-restore audio carries designer Dalsen's warning: “If you've ever had déjà vu, then it's too late.” Kessa must establish the lost consequences and keep the damaged tug from striking the ring.
- Focal pressure or attachment: Kessa wants to recover exterior mechanic Orivan and complete the delivery keeping their crew solvent. Training favors certified visuals; retained motor prediction says those visuals led her wrong. Calling this a second chance risks repetition, but accepting the loss means acting without remembered justification.
- Counterforce or complication: The collision froze the receiver on an old green loop, sheared a thruster cluster, and bent Orivan's collar outside the harness. Injured Orivan remembers but is intermittently heard. Dalsen has partial exterior logs; controller Pavax has delayed radar and must protect the occupied ring. Cargo preservation conflicts with deflection.
- POV, distance, and information limit: Close third-person past through Kessa. Keep the interval missing; reveal it through the spent harness bridge, timestamps, Orivan's report, geometry, and involuntary anticipation. No single source supplies an objective replay.
- Governing movement and time shape: Twelve continuous minutes after restoration: apparent repetition, reconstruction under closing distance, then a turn from what happened to what remains possible. Resolve collision and isolation; end with first aid and damage accounting, before blame or reform.
- Speculative rule or ordinary-world constraint: With consent, the reprise harness stores one neural baseline. After an outboard latch records irreversible structural loss and the pilot enters cognitive lock, it can once restore working and episodic memory to that baseline. Time, body, ship, transmissions, and exterior consequences continue. Motor prediction, stress chemistry, and sensory association remain; matching inputs produce its distinctive déjà vu. Since the loss latch must fire first, the initiating event is already past. A fused bridge and exterior recorder verify use and timing but not Kessa's choices. The nonliving harness has no agency, memory recovery, or second charge.

## Voice

- Narrative texture: Track repeated light, anticipated hand motion, hull sound, and discrepancy. Each familiar detail gains a new practical meaning; recurrence tightens without fragmenting the prose.
- Conversational texture: Use radio overlap, latency, missed receipts, one corrected mishearing, broken suit audio, and ordinary checks about air, pain, latches, and tools. Not every transmission diagnoses or emotes.
- Rhetorical ownership: Kessa owns timing and choice; Orivan describes bodily load, tilt, and injury; Dalsen reasons conditionally from device states; Pavax speaks in priorities, ranges, and authorized options.
- Pressure behavior: Kessa's fluency holes appear when memory is requested but close during action. Pain shortens Orivan. Dalsen qualifies conflicting logs. Pavax repeats protocol until geometry narrows it.
- Anti-default: Avoid a physicist lecture, time jokes, panicked chorus, or aphorism contest. The warning is polished once; the rest depends on noisy evidence and work.

## Beats

1. Kessa's hand reaches for cargo release before she knows why. Green guide arrows recur while physical drift disagrees. The harness plays Dalsen's warning and a reset identifier, not an explanation.
2. Orivan cuts in from the bent exterior collar, reporting a prior impact, injury, and missing structure. His air reading and hull knocks provide current facts, not complete proof.
3. Dalsen and Pavax compare exterior recorder, delayed radar, and tug telemetry. The fused bridge and clock gap verify restoration; damaged channels show impact, an unfinished release, and thruster loss without sequence or intent.
4. Kessa tests green guidance against a mechanical sightline, radar brackets, and Orivan's felt rotation. The loop repeats an old checksum. She accepts that his injury and the damaged ship belong to the erased interval and remain real.
5. Pavax's authorized option protects the ring by abandoning the tug, stranding Orivan. Kessa finds a narrower option: eject valuable cargo down its spring rail for recoil that clears the ring and rotates the rescue hatch toward him. It costs the delivery and return margin; mistiming worsens impact.
6. Pavax supplies delayed range, Orivan counts present hull vibration, and Dalsen marks uncertainty. A missed receipt makes Kessa hold. At the next physical alignment, she ignores green and completes the release.
7. The tug clears the ring. Kessa reaches Orivan and follows his concrete directions to free the collar without worsening injury. End on ordinary first aid and the lost cargo's accounting, not an explanatory exchange.
