# Opus review: What the Wind Wanted

Independent audit for a later editing pass. It is not a `review.md` verdict,
does not change canon state, and does not authorize edits by itself.

## Verdict

- Dialogue: REVISE — every line holds together, but the basin current speaks in everyone's clipped register at the climax, and Zerune's own wants never reach her dialogue.
- Prose: PASS — concrete and varied, no em dashes and no reversal tic; only a closing gloss and a few awkward staging phrases.
- Continuity and prompt: PASS — delivers the whispering-wind promise and resolves it at 3,400 words; one time reference is off.
- Edit scope: targeted-dialogue
- Existing review.md said: Verdict PASS, "Dialogue: PASS"; I partly disagree. The lines are coherent, but its claim that the basin current's voice keeps the final exchanges distinct does not hold.

The dialogue makes sense beat by beat. Setups are grounded, replies follow what
the listener heard, and the breeze has a real voice: literal, self-absorbed,
never grateful ("Don't tell it. I'm telling it."). The problems are who gets to
speak and how. Zerune's direct speech is mostly prompting questions and short
acknowledgements. What only she would say (the drowned temples, the insult book,
her fascination with the three lifts) stays in narration, so the story's central
friction, her appetite for revelation against the breeze's appetite for passage,
is told more than heard. At the climax the basin current is described as vast
and slow-phrased, yet every line it speaks is as short and snapped as the
breeze's. The edit pass should make the basin current sound large and give
Zerune a few lines of her own appetite. It should leave the story's spare
register alone everywhere else.

## Dialogue findings

### D1 · Major · Basin current sounds like everyone else at the climax

- Where: L307–L333, the courtship over the pond (narrated voice at L291 and L343)
- Text:
> continuing far beyond what she would have been able to say on one breath
> One hurried; one extended the turning long after Zerune expected it to end.
> "They're what I have."
> "Come through again."
> "Show me."
> "That one," said the basin current.
- Problem: Identical Voices at the decisive exchange. The narration says the larger current's phrases pass from tree to tree past the length of a human breath, and that it "extended the turning long after Zerune expected it to end". Its first overheard phrase (*here, this way through me*, L217) speaks of itself as a place. Yet every line actually written for it (L307, L313, L317, L323, L329, L333) is a short statement or command in the same clipped register as the breeze and Zerune. With the tags covered, only function (who invites, who performs) tells the two winds apart. "They're what I have." also lands as a neat last word. The scale that bewilders Zerune at L339 reaches the reader only through narration, never through the voice.
- Fix: Rewrite only the basin current's lines and keep what each one does:
  - the offer of the three bands;
  - the invitation to come through again;
  - the question about the folded turn;
  - "That one" catching the breeze as it slides back into its wave turn.

  Give the lines the length and grammar of something that is a place: longer joined clauses, speaking of itself as terrain, repeating or trailing off instead of snapping back, taking longer to respond. Keep the breeze's replies clipped and defensive so the contrast can be heard. Do not make the current wise, explanatory, or a guide (the outline's anti-default). Illustrative only, for L307: "There is a way through me here, and here, and here, low where the reeds are, if you come in under."

### D2 · Major · Tunnel planning plays as interview and terms; Zerune's appetite stays in narration

- Where: L163–L209 (working out the tunnel), L237–L261 (the terms of the crossing), and her wants reported at L91, L129 and L235
- Text:
> "Why haven't you gone through?"
> "And over the top?"
> "You can't go around the ridge?"
> "And then?"
> "Can you even fit?"
> She had been about to ask what it remembered of the drowned temples along the shore.
> She asked whether the breeze had ever entered a ruined temple.
- Problem: The protagonist works as an explanatory chorus, and the decision plays out as a procedure routine.
  - In L167–L201, six of Zerune's seven lines are prompting questions. Each one draws a clean mechanical answer from the breeze: the cold air, the flow over the top, going around, warmth, fitting.
  - In L237–L261 the choice settles into a list of conditions ("if I need to breathe", "Could you get back", "And in the middle?", "Will I hurt you?").
  - Across the story, 18 of her 42 lines are questions, five begin "And" (L91, L133, L173, L197, L245), and most of the rest are short acknowledgements or instructions.
  - What only Zerune would say is kept in narration or reported speech (L91, L129, L235): the drowned temples, the insult book, her fascination with the other wind's three lifts. Apart from two dry quips (L127, L257), her direct speech could belong to any sensible adult.
  - The result is that the story's central friction (she wants revelation, it wants passage) is narrated rather than heard.
  - The breeze pressing against her lips (L185–L187) is the one messy moment in the tunnel scene, and it shows what the rest of the scene lacks.
- Fix: Make small, targeted changes, not a rewrite:
  1. Move one of her own wants from reported speech into direct speech. L129 is the best place. Let the breeze answer beside the point, as it already does with the thyme.
  2. In L167–L201, turn two of the prompting questions into moves of her own. She could make a guess the breeze has to correct. She could half-speak the refusal she "drew breath to say" at L183 before the breeze cuts in. Or she could object to the mouth idea with more than "Don't do that." Let the mechanics come out of that friction.
  3. Drop at least two of the "And ___?" openers.
  4. In L237–L257, let one term carry feeling, such as fear or an eagerness she is embarrassed by, instead of every line being a condition.

  Keep her humor dry and aimed at herself. Add no aphorisms, quotations or thesis lines. Illustrative only, as speech at L129: "The old temples on the shore, the drowned ones. Have you been inside those?"

### D3 · Minor · She understood the phrase perfectly, then reports it vaguely

- Where: L217–L235, Zerune tells the breeze what the basin current said
- Text:
> She understood it perfectly and could not have followed its instruction
> "Something about a way through it. I can't make the sound."
> before she remembered what they were discussing
- Problem: Ground/knowledge.
  - L217–L219 give her the exact meaning (*here, this way through me*) and say she understood the second phrase "perfectly". Asked "What did it say?", she hedges with "Something about". Nothing shows why: she can speak her own language, and the breeze understands it.
  - At L235 her summarized talk about "the three separate lifts" is said to have strayed from "what they were discussing". But the lifts are part of what the other wind said, and that is the topic. The reader can't tell what she is pulled back to.
- Fix: Have her give the meaning plainly while failing at the sound, which is the established rule. Or give the hedge a reason the scene shows. At L235, name the topic she comes back to (the crossing) so the digression reads as one. Keep "But it's there." as the breeze's reply.

### D4 · Minor · Clipped references the reader must reconstruct

- Where: L179 (at the tunnel), L257 (just before the crossing), L307 (at the pond)
- Text:
> The other wind will change when the sun leaves that basin.
> "I won't want you to."
> "There's another way," the basin current said.
- Problem: Ground/referent.
  - "that basin" assumes a place neither Zerune nor the reader has seen or heard of. The basin first appears at L217.
  - "I won't want you to" leaves out "stay folded like that". For a beat it reads as a quip rather than reassurance, at the moment she commits.
  - What "another way" refers to becomes clear only from the next paragraph and "Those are not waves."

  None of these is severe, but together they add friction at three key beats.
- Fix: At L179, name the place from the breeze's side, for example the hollow past the ridge. At L257, write a plain line tied to her own limit. Resolve L307 in the D1 rewrite so the basin current's line says what it is offering.

## Prose findings

### P1 · Minor · Closing sentence explains the exclusion

- Where: L347, final paragraph
- Text:
> They had no need of her answer.
- Problem: Emotional commentary. No one has asked Zerune anything; the winds are asking each other whether to rise (L343). The sentence explains that she is shut out, which the paragraph already shows: she pulls her feet in, the book stays unopened, and the voices leave up the slope.
- Fix: Cut the sentence. Keep the rest of the last paragraph as it is.

### P2 · Minor · Staging phrases that stop the reader

- Where: L159 (arriving at the tunnel), L181 (sitting on the rock), L315 (at the pond, two phrases)
- Text:
> was carrying it with both hands behind her neck
> at the height to which she had brought it
> Above the pond the water quieted
> tucked the softer bag behind her back
- Problem: Unclear staging.
  - A scarf "carried with both hands behind her neck" is hard to picture. Is it draped, with the ends held?
  - "to which she had brought it" is a stiff inversion.
  - "Above the pond the water quieted" puts water above the pond.
  - "the softer bag" has nothing to compare against; presumably it is softer because the book is out.
- Fix: Reword each plainly. Keep the scarf in her hands, because it is used at L175, L187 and L193, and keep the book in her lap.

## Continuity and prompt findings

### C1 · Minor · "For an hour" undercounts the climb

- Where: L235, after she comes back through the tunnel
- Text:
> She had wanted its whole attention for an hour.
- Problem: Internal continuity (time). She meets the breeze after noon in the market (L11, L23). She then climbs past the orchards and the almond tree to a tunnel below the snow line, arriving with "afternoon sunlight" on the western face (L159). "An hour" is the story's only stated duration, and it is too short for that climb.
- Fix: Change it to "all afternoon" or "since the market".

## Recurring patterns

- Zerune's prompting questions: 18 of her 42 lines. "And ___?" openers at L91, L133, L173, L197, L245. Covered by D2.
- The breeze re-scoping Zerune's word in one line: L53, L63, L89, L119, L151, L195, and L311 to the basin current. This is deliberate and mostly good. Keep "Some of it.", "I'm looking for that wind.", "This is the wave one." and "Farther. Where you keep your breath." Add no more, and let at least one of Zerune's questions go unanswered instead (D2).
- Dry last-word replies from all three speakers: L43, L127, L215, L257, L313. Each is fine on its own; add no more. L313 is handled in D1 and L257 in D4.
- A short, clipped register shared by every speaker, throughout. Fix it through D1 (basin current) and D2 (Zerune), not by loosening the whole story.

> "You're the wind."
> "Some of it."
- The lover misreading, then the temple question she swallows before "And you want directions." (L87–L95): the moment the breeze's desire displaces hers.
> "This is the wave one."
- Her "Oh." and the breeze's "There was more room between the waves." (L153–L155): the gap between hearing a pleasure and sharing it.

- The flutter inside her during the crossing and its later callback in "There was warmth on every side." (L273, L337).

- The basin current's question at L323 about the turn the breeze keeps folding away. Keep what it asks even if D1 changes its voice.

- The final image: the book unopened and the two voices carried up the sunlit slope.
