# Opus review: A Busy Year for Dying

Independent audit for a later editing pass. It is not a `review.md` verdict,
does not change canon state, and does not authorize edits by itself.

## Verdict

- Dialogue: REVISE — the voices are distinct and the exchanges hold together, but Ruldo's account of the burned hut never says what the morning search found, and his grief, the wake and his speech to the crowd all depend on it.
- Prose: PASS — a wry, concrete close third on Kelro; two passages where narration explains a beat already shown, and a few prop-staging slips.
- Continuity and prompt: PASS — the prompt is delivered in about 3,190 words with a complete comic resolution; the one causal gap is filed as D1 because the fix belongs in Ruldo's line.
- Edit scope: targeted-dialogue
- Existing review.md said: Verdict PASS, "Dialogue: PASS"; I largely agree about voice and structure, but it missed the hut-search gap and several small anchoring slips.

This is one of the healthier dialogue stories in the collection. Kelro speaks in over-formal courtesy and dodges with jokes and technicalities, then drops to a bare "Yes" when cornered. Glessa talks in prices, objects and repairs. Ruldo uses plain, subject-dropping sentences about the order of events. The man in the yellow scarf is a cheerful, literal witness. Most exchanges turn on corrections, but here that is the premise at work: disputed eyewitness accounts and a re-enactment heckled by the people who were there. It is not the collection's reflexive ping-pong, and the edit pass should not add more retorts. The real problem is causal. Ruldo says "By morning we could get near enough to lift the roof" and never says what was under it, so the reader cannot see why he held a wake, and his "I can see that now" presupposes something the text never supplies. The rest is anchoring work: a pivotal line with no clear speaker, a witness report whose source and timing are unclear, an "it" that is about 220 lines from its noun, Ruldo's silence when the paint story contradicts evidence he holds, and a hat the dummy never wears.

## Edit guardrails

- Prompt: an immortal forced to fake his death more often in the wasteland than before; a complete story that resolves that promise; darkly comic and non-graphic; 2,500–4,000 words (now about 3,190, so fixes should add sentences, not scenes); craft profile prospective-2026-08-23. Keep close third on Kelro, with the narration calling him "Kelro" through every alias.
- Story facts the fixes must keep: he used the name Kelro with the quarry salvagers ("He was with us three weeks"), was Jalmot with Ruldo for a fortnight and is now Orvett. The coat went from the quarry to the salvagers to Glessa, who mended it twice. The hut fire was an accident, and Kelro heard Ruldo shout and stayed silent. Ruldo found the spoon outside the hut. Kelro cannot heal anyone else (the sister). Magic is common and badly paid (the woman with the sparks). "This was his fifth death that year." No places are named.
- Timeline: three weeks with the quarry men; days 9, 12 and 14 of the Jalmot fortnight; eighteen days south; Ruldo's "five weeks ago." A new sentence must not break this arithmetic.
- The outline's intent, which the prose honours: exposure redistributes leverage without friendship or general forgiveness. Ruldo must not forgive Kelro or become foolish, and Kelro must not confess immortality or deliver a lesson.
- What already works and must not be flattened: the escalating slips (horn buttons, "A different Ruldo, then."), the spoon, Kelro's one-word "Yes," the heckled re-enactment, and the ending, which stops on an action instead of a thesis.

## Dialogue findings

### D1 · Major · Hut search has no outcome

- Where: L177–L181, Ruldo and the spoon at the gathering; echoed at L187 (the wake) and L305 (Ruldo's speech to the crowd)
- Text:
> "I sat out there all night. Thought you might call from underneath. By morning we could get near enough to lift the roof."
> "I can see that now."
> "He let me sit beside his burnt hut until morning," Ruldo said. "Thought he was in there."
- Problem: Ground/causality at the story's emotional centre. Ruldo describes the search and stops before its result. Either the morning showed no body, in which case he already knew Kelro wasn't underneath, the wake has no basis, and his sarcastic "I can see that now" (as though he were only now finding out) doesn't follow; or it showed something he took for remains, and the text never says so. A small signal hut gives the reader no reason to assume a body would vanish. The narration at L105 establishes that Kelro simply left and staged nothing, so the only evidence of his death is whatever that search found. The outline also warns against making Ruldo's mourning look stupid, and as the text stands it risks exactly that.
- Fix: Add one clause to L177 saying what the morning showed, in a way that makes a reasonable man believe in a death: the roof came down on ash and burnt bedding, nothing could be told apart, there was nothing to bury. Keep "I sat out there all night," "Thought you might call from underneath," "I wasn't underneath" and "I can see that now." Keep it non-graphic and in Ruldo's plain register, not as a punchline. Illustrative only: "By morning we could lift the roof. There wasn't enough left of anything to bury." Check that L305 still reads true after the change.

### D2 · Minor · The brother catch has no clear speaker

- Where: L191–L195, after Kelro says "You asked me to look at her leg"
- Text:
> Ruldo regarded him.
> "So we're finished with the brother?"
- Problem: Attribution. The catch sits in its own paragraph, apart from Ruldo's beat, and Glessa's next line opens with "And the wound on your head?", which reads like the same questioner continuing. The line could be Ruldo's or Glessa's. It is the moment Kelro's brother story officially dies, and it matters who does the catching.
- Fix: Give it to Ruldo, since he was lied to about the brother. Join it to his beat paragraph ("Ruldo regarded him. 'So we're finished with the brother?'") or add a tag. Do not reword it.

### D3 · Minor · Two witness references lack an anchor

- Where: L197, Glessa turns to the head wound; L249, the scarf man after the salve demonstration
- Text:
> "And the wound on your head? Yellow scarf here told me there was blood all over you, then nothing."
> "You made a noise when it hit you."
- Problem: Ground/knowledge and Ground/referent. (1) The scarf man never mentions the blood on the page, so the reader can't tell when he told Glessa. Was it when she bought the coat from the salvagers, or quietly during Ruldo's exchange? "Here" suggests she has only just met him. (2) "it hit you" follows the salve demonstration, so the first reading attaches "it" to the salve. The noun it needs, the fragment of metal at L27, is about 220 lines back.
- Fix: (1) Tie Glessa's knowledge to a moment the reader can place. If she bought the coat from the salvagers, add "when he sold me the coat," which also accounts for her owning it. Otherwise, give the blood detail to the scarf man on the page before she asks. (2) Name the object: "when that metal hit you" or similar. Keep both lines short.

### D4 · Minor · Ruldo's silence when the paint story contradicts his evidence

- Where: L201–L237, the wrist, the paint explanation and Ruldo's turn to the hut
- Text:
> "His wrist, too," said Ruldo. "Wouldn't let me look at it until later."
> Ruldo put the spoon away. "You did it on purpose. The hut. You meant us to think you were dead."
- Problem: Relationship movement and uptake. Ruldo is the one witness who can refute "paint": he held the hand, saw the soaked sleeve (L85) and staked his sister's leg on the healing (L91, L187). The crowd adopts the stage-paint explanation and Ruldo gives no sign whether he believes it. The reader can't tell whether he has let it pass because the night matters more to him, or whether his hope for his sister has just died. His next line coherently applies the performer theory to the hut, so this is not a Ground failure. It is a missing beat for his central want.
- Fix: Add one narrated beat, not a speech, between the paint nod (L217) and L229: Ruldo looking at the wrist he examined, or not asking what he would have asked, so that his letting it pass reads as his choice. Do not have him expose the healing, since that would break the plot. Do not add a spoken line about the sister.

### D5 · Minor · "I did used to perform"

- Where: L211, Kelro's answer to "Were you begging?"
- Text:
> "No," he said carefully. "I did used to perform."
- Problem: The slip reads as a typo in the mouth of a man whose English is otherwise exact. He says "I said I used to perform" at L251.
- Fix: "I used to perform." (Or "I did use to perform." if the emphasis is wanted.)

### D6 · Minor · The patter names a hat the dummy never wears

- Where: L311–L335, the quarry re-enactment and the patter that follows
- Text:
> He went behind the canvas, put on a battered hat, and reappeared with the solemn gait of a man approaching unstable rocks.
> A red sleeve flopped out at the foot of the canvas.
> He held up the sack. "You recognize a hat and a coat. You think you've seen a man. By the time you can look properly, he's somewhere else."
- Problem: Ground/staging. Kelro puts the hat on himself, the fall shows only the sleeve, and then the patter says the audience recognized "a hat and a coat." The line describes a stage the reader never saw. The patter is otherwise earned: it pays off Kelro's "I'm going to explain the illusion" at L293, and the scarf man's "Only the sleeve." gives it uptake. It is already the story's neatest statement of Kelro's method, so it should not be polished further.
- Fix: Either move the hat onto the dummy in the L321 action (the hat rolls out beside the sleeve) or drop "a hat" from the patter. Leave the rest of the patter and the scarf man's reply as they are.

## Prose findings

### P1 · Minor · Narration explains beats already shown

- Where: L237, after Kelro's "Yes"; L333, the second run of the quarry re-enactment
- Text:
> It was preferable to being kept as a remedy. He could feel himself choosing that preference again.
> They knew where he had gone. He was giving them the pleasure of catching him at it.
- Problem: At L237 the silence and the spoon's outline already show the choice. "It" has no clear antecedent: the silence, fleeing, or being thought dead? The second sentence names the moral decision in abstract terms. At L333 the second sentence explains why the audience claps. The wry narrator is a strength elsewhere (L185, L275, L339), and these two are the places where it turns into a gloss.
- Fix: At L237, keep the concrete spoon beat. If the "remedy" thought stays, give it a clear subject and cut the "choosing that preference" sentence. Illustrative only: "Letting Ruldo mourn him had been preferable to being kept as a remedy." At L333, keep one of the two sentences, not both.

### P2 · Minor · Prop staging: two sacks and trousers from nowhere

- Where: L379, Ruldo at the crate; L275, the dummy is dressed
- Text:
> Ruldo had come to collect his sack.
> He drew trousers over the bottom and reached for his coat.
- Problem: For the last several paragraphs "the sack" has meant the juggler's straw dummy (L335, L347), so a reader at L379 may think Ruldo is taking the dummy. At L275 the trousers have no source.
- Fix: "his own sack" or a word about what is in it. "a pair of Glessa's trousers" or similar. One or two words each.

## Suggested edit order

1. D1: the causal ground of Ruldo's grief, which the confrontation, L305 and the ending all rest on.
2. D4: in the same scene, and best done with D1 so that Ruldo's side reads as one arc.
3. D2: a tag or paragraph join at the pivot where the brother story dies.
4. D3: anchor Glessa's source and the scarf man's "it."
5. D6: put the hat on the dummy, or take it out of the patter.
6. D5, P1, P2: grammar, glosses and prop words, as a final polish.
