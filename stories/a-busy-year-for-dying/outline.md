# Outline

## Story

- Premise and central promise: **A Busy Year for Dying.** An immortal expected the collapse of civilization to simplify disappearing. Small survivor communities, conspicuous recoveries, and the salvage of everything he abandons now make him retire identities every few weeks. When two former lives collide at a trading gathering, he must explain his repeated survival without admitting immortality.
- Focal pressure or attachment: Kelro wants one comfortable winter, particularly his excellent red coat, which he keeps losing with his identities. Centuries of successful concealment have made him complacent about ordinary people's memories. He also dislikes letting anyone else describe his supposed last moments badly.
- Counterforce or complication: Trade routes now join the few habitable camps. Glessa sells salvaged clothes and remembers customers; Ruldo leads a wagon and resents having mourned a passenger who appears alive under another name.
- POV, distance, and information limit: Close third past through Kelro; retain that narrative name through his aliases. The reader knows his condition immediately. Others' deductions emerge through words and conduct, sometimes surprising him.
- Governing movement and time shape: Three increasingly short lives across six weeks, following compressed earlier failures. Expand the last gathering into continuous farce. Target 2,500–4,000 words. Acquaintances and possessions accumulate and converge.
- Speculative rule or ordinary-world constraint: His body never ages or wholly dies; wounds restore within minutes, severe injuries more slowly. Pain, hunger, confinement, and humiliation remain real. He cannot change his face, regenerate clothes, or heal others. His staged deaths depend on incomplete observation. Past identities once lasted decades.
- Dialogue promise: An accomplished liar faces people whose inconvenient memories, curiosity, wounded pride, and commercial interests outlast his disguises.
- Dialogic medium: Camp introductions, sales talk, disputed eyewitness accounts, crowd interruptions, and an improvised public demonstration.
- Dialogue engine: Each answer protects one identity while contradicting another. Glessa mistakes supernatural survival for theatrical fraud; Kelro eventually encourages that useful mistake, at a price he keeps trying to reduce.

## Voice

- Narrative texture: Practical, vain, bodily comedy. Notice the discomfort of concealment and the quality of lost belongings. Compress centuries briskly; give an ill-timed question room.
- Conversational texture: Uneven camp talk, bargaining, misplaced condolences, unfinished explanations, and interruptions for ordinary needs. Permit an unfunny answer and a question that genuinely seeks information.
- Rhetorical ownership: Kelro owns elaborate distinctions and defensive historical comparisons. Glessa enjoys exposing salesmanship while practicing it; her jokes seek advantage. Ruldo values accurate sequences and recognition of his trouble, and can refuse a joke without becoming humorless.
- Pressure behavior: Kelro supplies unnecessary details and corrects stories he should not know. Glessa becomes more attentive as he becomes more fluent. Ruldo repeats the unanswered practical question.
- Relationship movement: Kelro wants their forgetfulness but cannot request it. Glessa wants a sale and then a profitable spectacle; Ruldo wants his experience acknowledged. Exposure redistributes leverage without producing friendship or general forgiveness.
- Anti-default: Avoid a wise immortal confessing to a sympathetic community. The survivors need neither reform nor an ethical lecture; their competing interests generate the comedy.

## Beats

1. Open during Kelro's latest staged disappearance at an unstable quarry. People saw him enter a fall of rubble; he survives concealed and leaves his distinctive coat caught beneath debris. He hears someone claim its recoverable sleeve before anyone finishes discussing his death. Establish that a witnessed wound closing had made this identity untenable. Briefly contrast thirty-year retirements before the collapse with this year's humiliating tally.
2. Two weeks as Jalmot bring ordinary meals and a sleeping place beside Ruldo's wagon. Glessa arrives with the repaired red coat. His precise complaints about its mending nearly expose him; he cannot afford her price. A separate visible injury heals too fast. He exploits an accidental fire in his own isolated sleeping shelter to establish another apparent death, leaving before witnesses can find him alive. Keep cause, sightlines, and others' safety clear.
3. As Orvett, shaven and differently dressed, he reaches the next trading gathering expecting anonymity and passage farther away. The narrowing routes have brought both earlier groups here. Glessa still has the coat. Establish the gathering's appetite for entertainment through an unsuccessful ordinary performer before it becomes an escape route.
4. Ruldo recognizes his former passenger. Kelro's attempted explanation attracts someone who knew the quarry identity. Their accounts collide in a public argument. He claims family resemblance, then spoils it by correcting a detail from his own memorial. Glessa concludes he is a practiced death faker. Ruldo's anger includes having carried the supposed dead man's few belongings and arranged a wake; do not turn that effort into mere stupidity.
5. Kelro seizes on theatrical fraud as an explanation for the recoveries. He knows modest stage tricks from an earlier life and offers a demonstration. Glessa turns his defensive offer into an attraction before he can control its terms. Ruldo insists on introducing it with the real names and his own account. Kelro must accept public exposure as a liar to protect the larger secret.
6. Make the demonstration an extended physical and conversational scene: borrowed screens, a clothed dummy, competing accounts of where he stood, an interruption for the borrowed coat, and spectators demanding both advertised deaths. Show credible mundane substitutions; never have spectators watch unexplained regeneration and simply ignore it. Kelro's pride in improving the performance helps trap him in it. The ordinary trickster explanation takes hold
7. Close with a concrete new livelihood and its comic cost. Glessa offers passage, food, and the coat against performances at subsequent stops. Kelro bargains for fewer appearances; she has already advertised separate turns for both deceased men. He accepts a limited engagement because it offers the comfortable winter he wanted.  Leave Ruldo's distrust intact and let the final exchange concern an immediate performance demand, without scripting its wording.
