# Outline

## Story

- Premise and central promise: **A Reasonable Declaration.** A retired Runner must identify and pay tax on his accumulated artifacts. His own expedition memoirs have supplied the preliminary assessment with nineteen apparently different treasures; he has brought seven objects. Deliver fantastical inventory, embarrassing demonstrations, and a completed filing.
- Focal pressure or attachment: Barnaby Greave wants a reclining chair and strangers' admiration. He survived daring expeditions through the Realms, renaming dependable equipment whenever its next appearance deserved a better chapter title.
- Counterforce or complication: Assessment clerk Fulvia owns his books, knows their illustrations, and is disappointed by his trolley. She wants defensible identifications before the deadline and confirmation of several treasured passages. Her fan questions interrupt her official ones; Barnaby cannot tell which answer will cost him money.
- POV, distance, and information limit: Close third past through Barnaby's self-importance. Let readers notice contradictions he manages. Fulvia's expectations emerge through questions, book annotations, and mishandling an unfamiliar artifact.
- Governing movement and time shape: One afternoon at a local tax office. Entries accumulate; physical evidence triggers brief recollections. Target prose: 2,500–4,000 words. Finish after identification, assessment, and payment.
- Speculative rule or ordinary-world constraint: This locality's retention levy uses present materials and demonstrated function, not literary fame. Owners can correct its preliminary catalog before today's late charge. These nonliving artifacts shape power supplied by living users; stored charge is finite. Identification counts physical objects, retaining aliases and uncertain provenance honestly.
- Dialogue promise: Boasting to an admirer and answering an assessor require dangerously different precision, especially when both listeners are the same woman.
- Dialogic medium: Spoken questions, cramped printed fields, memoir illustrations, handwritten corrections, and demonstrations whose results contradict attractive descriptions.
- Dialogue engine: Fulvia converts claims into actionable questions. Barnaby's clarifications add apparent acquisitions until he connects aliases to objects. Her enthusiasm creates detours; his irritated practical explanations make the collection countable.

## Voice

- Narrative texture: Worn equipment, uncomfortable furniture, Barnaby's calculations, and the indignities of transporting former glory. Recollections contract when he notices their financial implications.
- Conversational texture: Courtesy, overlapping questions, disappointing answers, attempted anecdotes, and requests for room or another sheet. Laughter inconveniences its owner.
- Rhetorical ownership: Barnaby owns peril, theatrical scale, and opportunistic distinctions. Fulvia owns page references and assessments but becomes expansive about favorite exploits. Neither supplies the other's polished conclusions; her admiration includes possessive opinions about his books.
- Pressure behavior: Barnaby becomes terse about real danger and elaborate about prices. Fulvia loses her question order when excited, then rereads the actual form. An incorrect assumption receives visible correction before anyone builds on it.
- Relationship movement: Barnaby wants Fulvia's admiration and a smaller bill, but cannot request both gracefully. She wants accurate answers and a personal inscription without soliciting a favor during assessment. Their exchanges shift control of his public account and establish which questions he must answer.
- Anti-default: Avoid an expert clerk teaching a chastened adventurer humility or friendship. Preserve petty competition, readerly disappointment, and Barnaby's appetite for official recognition after the paperwork succeeds.

## Beats

1. Barnaby arrives with his trolley and chair-money envelope. Ignored notices have become a preliminary bill compiled from his books. Establish the deadline and correction opportunity. Fulvia recognizes him, retrieves her annotated volume, and expects more crates.
2. The sword's nick and replaced grip recur beneath several grand names in the illustrations. Barnaby resists collapsing its identities, then discovers what separate entries cost. Ground genuine adventure in how that damaged grip kept him attached to the blade over a lethal drop: real peril beneath changing titles.
3. The cloak and voice-carrying circlet complicate valuation through demonstration. The worn cloak blurs its powered wearer's upper outline but leaves his knees conspicuous. Fulvia tries the circlet before appreciating how far it carries her own voice; Barnaby must explain and stop the effect. Keep the mistake embarrassing and contained. Its intact function earns a real assessment despite his newly vigorous depreciation arguments.
4. Handle the warming cup, lifting buckle, dark-reading spectacles, and closed exit token in compression. The cup is his daily favorite; the spectacles have mismatched repairs. The token recalls Queen Street's white seam, upward black rain, and an Exit Witness closing the return. No active route survives. Seven entries cover everything retained, including the token's nil assessment.
5. Reconcile nineteen memoir names against seven numbered entries. Fulvia accepts aliases supported by wear marks and illustrations; she cannot discard unexplained treasures. Barnaby supplies unglamorous packing facts. Questions about favorite passages expose resourcefulness and exaggeration without a confession or his admirer's conversion.
6. Complete descriptions, signatures, assessment, and payment. The corrected bill is payable, but functioning equipment consumes some chair money. Barnaby chooses which comfort to sacrifice. Fulvia requests her personal inscription after settlement. Leave the final exchange to prose: vanity finds opportunity in an accurate record, with retirement intact.
