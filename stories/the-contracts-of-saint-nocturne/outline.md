# Provisional direction

The request promises a public breach of an apparently religious secret that becomes a more unsettling discovery: humanity was a bargaining party, and abandoning belief did not erase what it signed. The Vatican Archives, eight generations-long guardians, international expedition, and worldwide revelation are explicit. Tactical suspense, intelligible and disturbing terms, and consequential agency for the sisters are my interpretations of the pleasure to protect. The prompt leaves viewpoint, the bargains' morality, and humanity's eventual answer open.

**Chosen story:** *The Contracts of Saint Nocturne*, about 4,000–5,000 words, close third person through Sister Ione, the youngest of the current eight. A tense expedition story turns into supernatural contractual horror, with non-graphic action. Mother Sabine, the Black woman in white headwear from the original, leads the sisters; Dr. Ada Nasse leads the expedition's historical commission and insists on a live public record. Neither is secretly a fool or a villain. Ione has been trained to die before the vault opens; she has never been allowed to read what her death would protect.

- **The breach:** In the palm courtyard, all eight sisters fight a losing, disciplined defence against an international force. They disable machines and block passages; the expedition uses overwhelming equipment and captures them alive. Ada believes a hereditary armed order has no right to keep humanity's past hostage. Sabine warns her not to bring the commission's seal below ground. Ada takes the warning as another claim of religious jurisdiction. A breaching charge opens the final door on the live feed.
- **The originals:** No relic or divine body waits inside. There are agreements with many different human hands and eight immense counterpart signatures. The expedition's mandate makes its commission a human assembly capable of receiving a demand. Living former gods, still powerful despite losing worship and godhood, have waited for such an assembly to present the originals. They begin to appear in the vault and on the broadcast. The order's guns were also intended for these living bodies.
- **One bargain made concrete:** The central agreement keeps its named predators out of human sleep. In exchange, people owed prescribed observance: kneeling and a spoken service one night in seven. Its human signers also purported to bind their descendants. When worship ended, a later settlement suspended enforcement while the eight originals remained sealed under eight mortal custodians. Opening does not create consent; it ends that suspension. The creditor now demands accumulated service and renewal, or the surrender of the old protection. The other originals make clear this was a system of bargains, not one eccentric cult. The world's audience sees actual clauses, human marks, and the later settlement.
- **The choice the revelation creates:** Sabine wants to reseal the vault and offer another generation of eight lives as custodians. The creditor will accept that delay only if the originals return to secrecy. Ada refuses to promise humanity's worship, but realizes that public knowledge has ended the old practical escape. Ione reads the original's termination clause aloud: a receiving human assembly can repudiate future hereditary service, including the arrears, by surrendering its contracted protection. The price is real. The things in the document will again be able to enter dreams; no guarantee replaces the bargain. The commission has authority to receive a claim and reject inherited obligations, but cannot honestly call that a harmless act.
- **The climax and stopping point:** As a creditor tries to take the originals back and suppress the clause, Ione fights to keep the live camera and the page together. Sabine must choose between helping her or securing the old silence. She joins Ione; the other sisters hold the living intruder at gunpoint while Ada records repudiation and transmits the complete originals. They surrender a protection, not defeat eight powers through a phrase. The signatures release their old claims, the presence retreats to the new freedom it has gained, and reports of impossible sleepers reach the feed. The story ends with the eight sisters leaving the open vault alongside the expedition, under a sky now full of public arguments, as the first unprotected night begins. Ione must sleep in the world she helped choose; the final image should carry that dread rather than explain a lesson.

The moral risk to test is whether the commission's right to reject inherited terms merely becomes another small group's decision for everyone. The story should keep that dispute alive instead of blessing its own procedure. The physical risk is that the climax becomes a legal trick: the creditor must remain dangerous, the loss must be legible, and the sisters' armed action must actually preserve the choice and the world's access to it.
