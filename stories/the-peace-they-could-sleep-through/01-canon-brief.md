# Canon brief

## Hard constraints

- If the polity is placed in the established main continuity, it belongs to the
  one physical world that persists through deep time. Earth, Ravel, and the
  Glass-Sea world cannot be treated as separate planets or planes; an
  unspecified era and region of that world is the safest placement.
  (`universe/premise.md` — `## The deep-time shared world`;
  `universe/locations.md` — `## The world across eras`)
- The dragon monarchy must remain a local government, not a universal hidden
  authority regulating the impossible or steering every civilization. Any
  recurring institution created here would have to remain consistent in later
  canon. (`universe/premise.md` — `## Knowledge and connection`)
- Magic, technology, and divinity are cultural labels for underlying systems,
  not objective substances. Draconic disguise, long sleep, or other powers
  should be one narrow mechanism belonging to this group rather than a rule for
  all dragons or all magic. (`universe/rules.md` —
  `## System categories`; `universe/glossary.md` —
  `## Magic, technology, and divinity`)
- Only life can use magic. Living dragons can supply magical agency; a
  nonliving crown, palace, decree, seal, or machine may only store, focus,
  transmit, or shape that magic unless the story establishes that it is alive.
  (`universe/rules.md` — `## Living agency and magic`)
- Canon defines godhood through the continuing conjunction of worship, power,
  and authority. The story must track which entity receives royal worship,
  which possesses power, and which exercises authority. If the same dragon,
  persona, collective, or Crown satisfies all three, canon's taxonomy treats
  that entity as a god. Worship alone neither creates godhood nor generally
  rewrites draconic nature; losing all worship ends godhood.
  (`universe/rules.md` — `## Divinity`)
- Consent has no universal metaphysical effect. Royal dependence, paternalism,
  coerced deference, and the dragons' surrender of control must have political
  and ethical consequences, not automatic magical consequences unless a local
  system is explicitly established. (`universe/rules.md` —
  `## Costs, desire, and local mechanisms`)
- False genealogy, official precedent, sacred histories, and records may become
  propaganda, dogma, or oppression, but no contradiction is automatically
  proof. The discovery and public dispute need causally credible evidence,
  witnesses, or material context. (`universe/premise.md` —
  `## Memory, myth, and recoverable truth`; `universe/rules.md` —
  `## Records, falsehood, and historical traces`)
- Regnal names and titles are normally symbolic and socially powerful, not
  universally magical. A royal office that literally binds identity or power
  would require an explicit local mechanism. (`universe/rules.md` —
  `## Names and identity binding`)
- The draconic explanation cannot erase human identity, consent, relationships,
  choices, or the moral consequences of dependency and deception. Threatened
  civil conflict and historical violence must remain non-graphic and focused
  on people and consequence. (`universe/style-guide.md` —
  `## Personhood and moral consequence`; `universe/style-guide.md` —
  `## Audience and content boundaries`)

## Useful established context

- Canon explicitly allows later cultures to mistake rulers for gods and
  survival measures for sacred law. That supports royal worship growing from
  long peace without pre-establishing this monarchy, religion, or species.
  (`universe/premise.md` — `## Memory, myth, and recoverable truth`)
- Truth does not automatically produce justice. Provenance, competing evidence,
  material context, and witnesses matter, which supports a resolution based on
  human negotiation and revisable institutions rather than revelation alone.
  (`universe/premise.md` — `## Memory, myth, and recoverable truth`)
- Cultures and characters may hold incomplete or incorrect models of reality.
  The adjudicator may understand royal law intimately while misunderstanding
  the rulers, and the dragons' long view need not give them perfect political
  knowledge. (`universe/premise.md` — `## Knowledge and connection`)
- Civilizational collapse recurs but is not a law or synchronized cycle.
  Approaching civil conflict can be a contingent failure caused by human and
  institutional choices, and it can be averted by different choices.
  (`universe/premise.md` — `## The deep-time shared world`)
- Canon permits independent audience entry points and a wide tonal and genre
  range. Wry institutional fantasy need not advertise its relation to named
  eras or the larger cosmology. (`universe/style-guide.md` —
  `## Tonal range, scale, and connection`)

## Conflicts or ambiguity

- No conflict was found among the relevant `LOCKED` or `CANON` sources. No
  canon ruling blocks planning.
- `stories/INDEX.md` marks every listed story canon `no`.
  `a-crown-of-quiet-hours` is therefore non-canon and, under this prompt's
  explicit revision directive, supplies no plot, setting, cast, worship
  structure, institution, settlement, or world fact. Its registered names
  remain production reservations only because `stories/NAMES.md` retains
  non-canon names until released.
- The object of “royal worship” is ambiguous. Worship may attach to an abstract
  Crown, a supposed dynasty, one public monarch, successive personas, the
  hidden collective, or individual dragons. Power and authority may attach
  differently. This omission matters because canon's godhood taxonomy applies
  to the entity that actually holds the conjunction. (`universe/rules.md` —
  `## Divinity`)
- Canon does not define whether an office's worship or authority transfers to a
  new wearer, whether a disguised collective counts as one entity, or whether
  sleeping rulers retain active authority. Those are unresolved local
  identity questions, not permission to assume a universal rule.
  (`universe/rules.md` — `## Divinity`; `universe/rules.md` —
  `## Memory, transformation, copies, and branches`)
- The story's era is unspecified. Canon fixes the modern-like era before the
  Glass-Sea era and leaves Ravel's position unresolved, but the polity need not
  be assigned to any named setting. This is an omission, not a chronology
  conflict. (`universe/timeline.md` — `## Established era order`)

## Unknowns

- Dragons are not established in authoritative canon. Beyond the prompt's
  required collective of roughly twelve disguised rulers, species population,
  origin, longevity, reproduction, transformation, vulnerability, power, and
  sleep biology are all `Unknown`.
- The disguise mechanism is `Unknown`, including whether each dragon has one or
  several public identities, whether personas are shared, what happens during
  sleep, and what evidence a transformation leaves.
- The thresholds and identity accounting for worship, power, and authority are
  `Unknown`: canon does not specify whether worship of an office attaches to
  its occupant, how authority divides across a collective, or whether public
  deference continues to sustain a sleeping ruler.
- The polity's name, geography, era, neighbors, economy, technology, magical
  literacy, laws, courts, succession, bureaucracy, religion, and conflict
  history are `Unknown`.
- The adjudicator's exact office, jurisdiction, loyalties, authority, and role
  in creating royal dependency are `Unknown`.
- The immediate political dispute, the rival parties, their legitimate needs,
  their leverage, the pathway toward civil conflict, and the terms of a
  revisable settlement are `Unknown`.
- The duration and benefits of royal peace, the pre-monarchical crises, the
  harms hidden by benevolent paternalism, and the human participation in both
  success and dependency are `Unknown`.
- The conditions under which the dragons can sleep for centuries, whether all
  twelve sleep simultaneously, and what risk or uncertainty they must accept
  are `Unknown`.
- The polity's relationship to Earth, Ravel, the Glass-Sea era, the Glass Gate,
  known gods, recurring characters, or other candidate stories is `Unknown`.
  Similar titles, motifs, institutions, or political problems do not establish
  a connection. (`universe/rules.md` —
  `## Artifacts, motifs, and folklore`)

## Safe invention space

- Invent a new local polity, court system, adjudicator's office, civic
  factions, public religion, dispute, and human settlement mechanism that are
  distinct from the rejected reimagining and no broader than this story needs.
- Invent roughly twelve dragons, their disagreements, public disguises,
  rotation pattern, finite abilities, and competing tolerances for political
  uncertainty. Keep all biology and history specific to this group or lineage.
- Establish one narrow mechanism for disguise and multi-century sleep, with
  observable limits and costs. Do not present it as universal dragon lore.
- Define locally who receives worship and who holds power and authority. The
  story may distinguish Crown, persona, dynasty, collective, and individuals;
  if it joins all three divine conditions in one entity, it should accept and
  consistently dramatize the canon consequence.
- Invent a political crisis driven by incompatible interests, brittle
  procedure, precedent, or learned dependence. Wars, coups, famine, collapse,
  and “not on fire” may remain idiomatic risks; no literal conflagration is
  required or desired by the prompt.
- Invent an imperfect, revisable human agreement and institutions capable of
  changing it. A local transfer, limitation, or dispersal of royal authority is
  safe if it does not become a universal rule about consent, sovereignty, or
  divinity.
- Use ordinary records, testimony, legal inconsistencies, bodily evidence, or
  witnessed transformation for discovery. No supernatural truth detector or
  universal property of contradictory records is needed.
- Leave the wider era unstated. A named era, gate, deity, artifact, or recurring
  institution would require a separate evidence-backed continuity reason.

## Name constraints

- The new prompt contains no character-facing personal name or alias, so it
  creates no exact registry collision. `Crown`, `monarchy`, `adjudicator`,
  `dragon`, and ordinary court or religious offices are common nouns unless the
  prose turns one into a proper identity label.
- The rejected predecessor still reserves `Istra Noll`, `Istra`, `Noll`,
  `Kharost`, `Queen Orvessa IV`, `Queen Orvessa`, `Orvessa`,
  `Vicar Estrel`, `Estrel`, `Crowned Ancestors`, and
  `the Crowned Ancestors`. Do not reuse them; registry reservation does not
  import their characters or lore. (`stories/NAMES.md` —
  `## Character name registry`)
- The revision directive also bars the predecessor's institutions and
  settlement. `Founding Vigil` and `Charter of Waking Hours` appear only in the
  predecessor's non-canon index note and must not be reused as story facts or
  labels. (`stories/INDEX.md` — `# Story index`)
- Avoid other reserved royal or divine forms, including `King Othran`,
  `Othran`, `Queen Aurelia`, `Aurelia`, `Sun Queen`, `Burning Queen`,
  `Goddess`, `Mother`, `Solstice Evening Bell`, `Solstice`, `Evening Bell`,
  `First Monarch`, `the Goddess of Time`, `Time`, `the Hollow Regent`, and
  `Regent`, unless reuse is narratively meaningful and fully documented.
- Avoid title-like forms when used as names, including `Captain`, `Court
  Ghost`, `Daughter`, `Keeper Below`, and `Mother-fragment`. Ordinary role
  nouns are not aliases, but titles functioning as character labels must be
  checked and registered.
- Do not add to the unresolved collision sets `Lena`, `Mara`, `Nisha`, `Pell`,
  or `Voss`. (`stories/NAMES.md` —
  `## Known unresolved name collisions`)
- A cast involving roughly twelve dragons, human disguises, regnal identities,
  and political factions creates high alias risk. Check exact forms, given
  names, surnames used alone, close spellings, reversals, epithets, and offices
  used as names. Avoid crowded sound clusters such as `Ilya` / `Irix` / `Ivo`
  and `Siva` / `Sula` / `Solstice` / `Seris` / `Sabine` / `Sorel`.
- If multiple dragons share one royal identity, or one dragon wears multiple
  regnal identities, the plan and registry must state which forms belong to
  the same or distinct persons, why any reuse matters, and how readers will
  distinguish them.
- `stories/NAMES.md` is production memory rather than canon. The primary
  coordinator must register planned names and run the scoped validator; the
  canon researcher must not edit the registry.

## Required checks after drafting

- Confirm no central literal fire, arson, cistern, firefighting, or
  burning-building crisis has replaced the prompt's idiomatic political
  meaning of “not on fire.”
- Compare the draft only against the revision directive and current canon, not
  the predecessor's story. Search for and remove any reused predecessor plot,
  cast, names, worship structure, civic institution, or constitutional
  settlement, including the reserved labels identified above.
- Identify each instance of worship, power, and political or religious
  authority. Verify that the draft applies godhood consistently to the entity
  that holds all three, and that any reform, sleep, or transfer of authority
  has coherent effects on that conjunction.
- Verify that worship alone does not create, transform, humanize, or empower
  the dragons. If public worship diminishes while the dragons sleep, remember
  that loss of all worship ends godhood but does not erase draconic personhood.
- Check the count and continuity of the dragons, all public disguises, shared or
  sequential royal identities, who knows each secret, and which dragons can act
  while others sleep. Ensure every power and limitation established before the
  climax remains binding.
- Confirm any magical disguise, adjudication device, or royal artifact depends
  on living magical agency unless the supposed user is explicitly alive.
- Trace discovery through credible evidence and causality. Do not make a
  contradiction, sacred story, dream, prophecy, repeated image, or apparent
  precedent automatically decisive.
- Verify that the civil-conflict pathway follows from intelligible interests
  and institutional dependence, and that humans avert it through negotiation,
  responsibility, and a revisable agreement rather than a decree, dragon
  intervention, coincidence, or newly revealed power.
- Confirm the ending leaves material responsibility with humans and requires
  the dragons to accept genuine uncertainty. The real good of their peace,
  their deception, and the harm of dependency-producing paternalism must all
  retain moral weight.
- If the polity is placed in established main continuity, ensure it remains
  local to the one shared world and is not described as a universal empire,
  divine government, or regulator of all magic and history.
- Search final prose for every personal name, disguise, alias, title used as a
  name, and person-like entity; reconcile them with `stories/NAMES.md`,
  document intentional identity sharing, and rerun the scoped name validator.
- Record every reusable new fact about the polity, dragon collective, disguise
  and sleep systems, religion, conflict, chronology, or settlement in
  `06-canon-delta.md`. The delta remains proposed, not canon, without explicit
  promotion.
- Confirm threatened civil conflict, coercive religion, and historical
  violence remain within the prompt's Teen / PG-13 framing and the universe's
  non-graphic content ceiling.
