# Proposed canon delta

This file proposes reusable shared-universe facts. It is not canon until the
user explicitly approves promotion and the facts are added to `universe/`.

Every entry below is evidenced by
`stories/the-peace-they-could-sleep-through/05-story.md`. The proposed facts
remain local to this unnamed polity, era, and twelve-dragon collective. They
must not be generalized to all dragons, gods, transformations, governments, or
periods of the shared world. This file records proposals only and performs no
canon promotion.

## New characters or character facts

- **Lumen Mott** is a human royal adjudicator and the first-person narrator.
  For eleven years, Lumen has turned petitions and disputed interests into
  polished judgments for the Sleepless Majesty, removing doubt and bargaining
  so twelve different contributors sound like one perfect mind. Lumen
  sincerely worships the Majesty and values the peace, but recognizes that
  this work has helped make human disagreement dependent on royal answers.
  During the grain crisis, Lumen declines to select between two incompatible
  judgments, refuses a third formula, discloses the twelve wearers and Lumen's
  own editorial role, invokes the existing common-plea procedure, elicits costs
  without drafting the bargain, and signs only as witness. Lumen's royal title
  ends with the dragons' authority; afterward Lumen serves as one of several
  recallable chairs on a temporary human docket.
- **Ludo Dask** represents the terrace districts after two failed harvests.
  His communities risk losing clean planting seed and tenancy land pledged
  against royal loans. He has promised supporters full divine vindication and
  initially treats repayment as an admission that seizure is lawful. In the
  common plea, he accepts capped repayment from future surplus, drafts one of
  the agreement's central clauses, signs for the terrace districts, and sends
  his own order telling terrace companies to stop short of the provincial
  storehouses.
- **Gelda Parn** represents the city milling and importing houses. Their credit
  financed seed and three grain shipments during the failed harvests, and they
  fear that canceled principal will stop future imports and harm city workers.
  Gelda initially treats any collection pause as a violation of divine
  contract. In the common plea, she accepts temporary protections and audited
  margins, drafts one of the agreement's central clauses, signs for the city
  houses, and sends her own order reopening city depots and releasing detained
  wagons.
- **Varkesh** is a deep-blue dragon with pale markings around the eyes, one of
  exactly twelve distinct dragons, and the current public wearer of the shared
  Sleepless Majesty guise. Varkesh explains the collective's history, motive,
  rotation ledger, guise, and division over the grain case. Varkesh offers a
  final compulsory formula but accepts Lumen's refusal, joins the other eleven
  in choosing silence while humans negotiate, waits without compelling either
  armed group, renounces authority with the full collective, and departs with
  all eleven other dragons. Varkesh is a member of the former collective
  sovereign, not individually identical to it and not individually identified
  as the object of worship.
- **The other eleven dragons** are distinct, unnamed persons rather than
  copies, branches, extensions, or aliases of Varkesh. Their bodies visibly
  differ in size, color, scales, fur, ridges, plates, frills, spines, and wings.
  Together with Varkesh they make the exact total of twelve, rotate through one
  shared public identity, disagree over policy, choose silence during the
  common plea, renounce authority, and leave together. Their physical
  descriptions are not names or stable epithets.
- **The Sleepless Majesty** is the single continuous living collective
  identity deliberately constituted by all twelve dragons across two
  centuries. It appears publicly through one androgynous human guise with a
  smooth brown face, close silver curls, and gray robes. Only one dragon
  ordinarily wears the guise in public, although all twelve assume it privately
  while comparing grain judgments. The guise has no independent mind and is
  not a thirteenth person. Before the authority transfer, the collective acts
  as the polity's sovereign and god; afterward the shared guise is abandoned
  and does not appear again.

## New locations

- An **unnamed local polity in an unspecified era and region** experiences two
  centuries of peace under the Sleepless Majesty. The story establishes no
  link to Earth, Ravel, the Glass-Sea era, the Glass Gate, a named deity,
  another story, or a wider governing institution.
- The polity includes the **Hall of Petitions**, local hearing offices, an
  unusually vaulted private consultation room, terrace districts, provincial
  grain storehouses, city depots, inhabited roads, and an old remote dragon
  refuge. None is given a personal or geographic proper name.
- The old refuge lies beyond the last inhabited road and is far enough from
  ordinary settlement for this collective's deep sleep, although major war,
  trade collapse, mass hunger, or disputed succession can still wake them.

## New factions or cultural facts

- The Sleepless Majesty's two-century rule ends three cycles of conquest,
  removes disputed royal succession as a recurring crisis, establishes grain
  reserves, makes judgments more consistent across districts, and builds roads
  that reduce bad harvests from mortal catastrophes to costly years. These
  benefits and the population's gratitude are real.
- The rule also creates a paternalistic dependence on final answers. Human
  clerks perform ordinary administration—arranging deliveries, auditing
  measures, and maintaining roads, loans, and grain accounts—but disputed
  interests are escalated in blue-covered files to the supposedly infallible
  monarch. Petitioners conceal concessions and perform certainty upward
  because compromise appears less legitimate than divine vindication. Lumen's
  professional editing reinforces the same dependence.
- Two centuries of records remain **ordinary accumulated court files**:
  clerk-tied grain releases, loan schedules, road accounts, and district
  disputes, most carrying human initials. They are not an exhaustive royal
  codex, do not prescribe every answer for a sleep era, and do not claim to
  govern automatically while the dragons rest. The rotation ledger is a
  separate mundane record of appearances, illnesses, journeys, hearings, and
  twelve recurring private marks.
- Public worship is directed to the living collective identity called the
  Sleepless Majesty. The responsive prayer is “We ask; you answer.” Revelation
  of the twelve bodies does not immediately end worship: some people kneel
  lower and ask the revealed collective to command negotiation.
- After the common plea, all twelve dragons publicly disclose the false royal
  continuity and renounce royal and religious authority. Human clerks remove
  the royal-answer line from every open docket, and district delegates stop
  recognizing any dragon's word as judgment. Some private worship continues,
  but the former collective no longer governs or adjudicates.
- The temporary post-royal docket has several recallable human chairs. Every
  petition must include a proposed concession. After one year, district
  institutions may retain, replace, or discard that procedure. This is a
  provisional local practice, not a permanent constitution or universally
  applicable settlement.

## New rules, capabilities, or costs

- Each of these twelve living dragons can make the same human bodily shape
  while awake. During the private-room reveal, all twelve release that shape on
  an exhalation. Each dragon remains a distinct person through the
  transformation; the shared guise has no mind of its own. No title, name,
  robe, seal, file, ledger, court, or other nonliving object supplies agency.
  These observations apply only to this collective and do not establish
  universal dragon or transformation rules.
- This collective requires centuries of deep, uninterrupted sleep in its old
  refuge. War drums, collapsing trade, mass hunger, and disputed successions
  can disturb or wake these particular dragons. They leave intending roughly
  three centuries of sleep, but neither the duration nor civic stability is
  guaranteed. These are local needs and risks, not general dragon biology.
- **Godhood before the transfer:** people directly worship the living
  Sleepless Majesty; the twelve dragons jointly supply the living power behind
  its continuous guise; and the same collective exercises final royal and
  religious authority. Because worship, power, and authority are conjoined in
  one deliberately constituted collective identity, the Sleepless Majesty
  qualifies as a god under the established taxonomy. Varkesh and the other
  dragons remain twelve distinct persons within that collective identity.
- **Godhood after the transfer:** revelation alone does not end godhood. The
  conjunction breaks only after all twelve renounce royal and religious
  authority and human institutions materially cease treating dragon speech as
  judgment. The dragons retain living power, and some private worship
  continues, but without authority the former Sleepless Majesty no longer
  qualifies as a god. The change is political and relational rather than an
  automatic magical effect of consent, and it does not erase any dragon's
  personhood.
- The common plea, rotation ledger, sealed judgments, ordinary court files,
  grain ledgers, procedure book, messages, signatures, and temporary docket
  are legal, archival, or administrative instruments. None acts
  independently, compels consent, or confers supernatural authority.

## Timeline events

- Across two centuries, the twelve dragons rotate through one continuous
  public guise and collective sovereign identity. Their rule ends conquests
  and succession crises, supports roads, reserves, and courts, and gradually
  teaches petitioners to seek perfect royal answers instead of owning
  revisable compromises.
- After two failed harvests, royal loans, seed debt, tenancy pledges, three
  imported grain shipments, and contested guarantees create a grain dispute.
  Terrace districts face loss of next year's seed and pledged land; importing
  houses face broken credit and future supply risk. Two incompatible sealed
  judgments reach Lumen on the same morning, followed by a third compulsory
  formula offered at noon.
- The immediate mobilization has **two coherent sites**. Terrace companies
  carrying farm tools advance toward provincial storehouses, while house
  guards close city depots, turn back wagons, and detain terrace shipments.
  Both groups act in expectation of total royal vindication, creating an
  imminent path to wider civil conflict without any literal fire, arson,
  burning structure, or firefighting crisis.
- Lumen investigates the contradictory drafts through an ordinary service door
  and finds twelve identical Majesties arguing over twelve annotated rulings.
  The witnessed release of twelve living disguises, twelve distinct bodies,
  rotation ledger, twelve recurring private marks, twelve differently
  annotated drafts, and Varkesh's admission jointly establish the secret; the
  contradiction alone does not prove it.
- At noon, Lumen refuses the third royal judgment and publicly reveals the
  collective and Lumen's editorial role. When the crowd asks the dragons to
  command negotiation, Lumen refuses that substitute as well. All twelve
  dragons choose silence.
- Ludo and Gelda author and sign a **ninety-day common plea** in their own
  hands. It protects seed stores and pledged tenancy land from seizure until
  the next harvest; preserves principal but caps repayment and draws it only
  from future surplus; opens district grain inventories, import costs, and
  depot margins to inspection; resumes city-depot release at an audited
  margin; requires terrace withdrawal from the provincial storehouses;
  releases detained shipments; and expires after ninety days unless open stock
  counts support amendment or renewal. Lumen witnesses and enters the plea but
  writes none of its terms. No dragon speaks a clause, and no royal seal,
  judgment, or ratification authorizes it.
- The plea alone does not halt the armed actions. Ludo writes and dispatches
  the terrace stand-down, ordering the companies to hold the provincial road
  and stay out of the storehouses. Gelda writes and dispatches the city
  stand-down, ordering depots opened, wagons released, and detentions stopped.
  Their own runners report that both groups comply at their respective sites;
  protected seed, resumed grain movement, auditable terms, copied clauses, and
  representative human authority avert the immediate crisis.
- The articles raise the city price of flour and lower several debt payments.
  On the seventeenth day, open counts reveal that one district holds less grain
  than either side assumed. Ludo and Gelda amend the release schedule during a
  fourteen-hour human meeting, demonstrating that the settlement is costly,
  imperfect, and revisable.
- In their final public act, all twelve dragons renounce royal and religious
  authority. They retain no veto, appoint no hidden successor, and keep no
  monitor, messenger, escort, or human office charged with watching for their
  waking. They receive no guarantee that the grain articles or new docket will
  succeed. All twelve arrive at the Hall in their own bodies, abandon the
  shared guise, and depart together for the old refuge without circling back;
  no last dragon remains to request assurance.
- Three days after the collective departure, travelers from the last inhabited
  road report twelve rumbling voices debating the refuge's acoustics after
  dusk, followed by silence before dawn. That indirect report, not a monitor or
  formal guarantee, is the story's confirmation that the collective has
  settled into sleep. No office is established to listen for a second night.
- The temporary docket begins after the transfer. At Lumen's third hearing,
  two millers and six villages argue over transport fees, revise a proposal,
  and continue untidily without a royal answer while the twelve dragons remain
  asleep.

## New glossary terms or aliases

- **Common plea** — A pre-existing six-line court procedure allowing parties
  to end a case by filing their own agreement before royal judgment. The grain
  parties use it without dragon authorship or royal ratification.
- **Hall of Petitions** — The public royal hearing hall in which the
  Sleepless Majesty is exposed, the grain bargain is negotiated, and the
  twelve later make their collective renunciation.
- **Ninety-day articles** — The temporary, human-authored grain agreement filed
  by Ludo Dask and Gelda Parn under the common-plea rule.
- **Sleepless Majesty / the Sleepless Majesty** — The one continuous living
  collective sovereign identity jointly enacted by exactly twelve dragons
  through a shared human guise. `Majesty` and `Your Majesty` are its shorter
  address forms. The guise is not a separate person.
- **Temporary docket** — The provisional post-royal hearing process run by
  several recallable chairs, requiring proposed concessions and subject to
  district reconsideration after one year.
