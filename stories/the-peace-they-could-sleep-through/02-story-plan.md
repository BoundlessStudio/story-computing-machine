# Story plan

## Premise and promise

- **Premise:** When royal adjudicator Lumen Mott discovers that the infallible
  monarch she serves is one public identity shared by twelve exhausted
  dragons, she must stop two grain factions from becoming armies without
  giving them the divine verdict that taught them never to compromise.
- **Story promise:** A wry palace revelation becomes a humane political
  pressure-cooker: the dragons' peace has saved lives, their paternalism has
  made the state brittle, and the climax turns on humans accepting a temporary
  bargain while their former gods accept that no settlement can guarantee
  centuries without failure.
- **Central dramatic question:** Can Lumen refuse the perfect royal answer she
  has spent her life supplying and make frightened adversaries own an
  imperfect one before their supporters begin a civil conflict?
- **Meaningful change:** Lumen moves from treating good judgment as something
  experts deliver downward to treating it as a process in which affected
  humans disclose costs, concede something, and retain the power to revise.

## Story controls

- **POV / tense:** First person, Lumen; past tense.
- **Tone / genre:** Wry, humane political fantasy with institutional comedy,
  moral pressure, and earned hope.
- **Length:** Approximately 3,500 words, within the 2,500–4,000-word contract.
- **Content:** Teen / PG-13. Armed standoffs, threatened famine, religious
  pressure, past non-graphic violence, and deception; no on-page battle and no
  bleak or punitive ending.
- **Scope:** One unnamed local polity in an unspecified era and region of the
  shared physical world. No wider-universe names or links.
- **Interpretive boundary:** “Not on fire” stays wholly idiomatic. There is no
  literal fire, arson, water-system failure, firefighting, or burning
  structure.
- **Compression:** Three named humans, one named dragon, one shared royal
  identity, and eleven individually unnamed dragons. Faction supporters and
  clerks remain functional background, not subplots.

## Character engine

- **Lumen Mott — protagonist:** The first-person royal adjudicator who turns
  testimony into draft judgments for the Sleepless Majesty. Lumen's immediate
  desire is to deliver a ruling on the grain case before noon, when armed
  supporters plan to take opposing storehouses. The larger personal desire is
  to remain useful to the benevolent order Lumen sincerely reveres. Lumen's
  blind spot is polished paternalism: by translating messy human claims into
  clean questions for an allegedly perfect ruler, Lumen has helped strip
  litigants of practice in bargaining with one another. Internal movement:
  loyal interpreter of authority → horrified keeper of its secret → facilitator
  who refuses to supply legitimacy and accepts public responsibility for past
  dependence.
- **Ludo Dask — terrace delegate:** Represents farming districts after two
  failed harvests. Royal grain quotas and loan terms leave communities facing
  seizure of seed stores and tenancy land. Ludo needs an immediate moratorium
  and local control of seed, but has promised supporters the Majesty will
  vindicate them completely; accepting repayment obligations risks being called
  faithless. Ludo has legitimate survival stakes and dangerous all-or-nothing
  leverage: terrace companies are ready to occupy provincial granaries.
- **Gelda Parn — city delegate:** Represents importing and milling houses that
  financed seed and brought in grain during the failures. Voiding their claims
  may destroy credit, stop future imports, and leave urban workers hungry.
  Gelda needs enforceable repayment and transparent inventories, but has also
  promised that divine law will protect every contract; compromise threatens
  both her coalition and the worship that legitimizes it. House guards are
  ready to close depots and detain terrace shipments.
- **Varkesh — dragon interlocutor:** One of exactly twelve distinct dragons and
  the current wearer of the Sleepless Majesty's human guise. Varkesh wants one
  final comprehensive judgment so all twelve can abandon disguise and sleep
  for several centuries. Varkesh is kind, exhausted, administratively funny,
  and genuinely proud that conquest, famine, coups, and succession wars have
  receded under their rule. The blind spot is a long-lived demand for
  guarantee: Varkesh would rather impose a brittle answer than trust humans to
  revise one. The earned concession is not solving the case or commanding
  compromise, but surrendering the last word and going to sleep knowing the
  agreement may fail.
- **The other eleven dragons:** Eleven distinct persons, not copies, branches,
  extensions, or aliases of Varkesh. Together with Varkesh they debate policy,
  maintain records, and rotate through the same royal guise. Their split over
  the grain case shows finite judgment: plausible royal answers conflict.
  They remain unnamed and receive no stable nicknames or epithets.
- **The Sleepless Majesty:** The single continuous public identity under which
  all twelve dragons have jointly ruled for two centuries. Every wearer assumes
  the same androgynous human appearance, voice, and title; only one appears in
  public at a time. The guise has no independent consciousness. The underlying
  dragons are distinct people who deliberately act as one collective sovereign.

## Causal arc

- **Beginning state:** Lumen has reduced a dangerous grain dispute to a draft
  question for the Sleepless Majesty. Terrace farmers cannot survive seizure of
  next year's seed; city houses cannot maintain imports if royal guarantees
  become meaningless. Both sides have armed supporters, each expects total
  divine vindication, and neither has supplied a concession. A rarely used
  **common plea** rule allows parties to close a case by filing their own
  agreement before judgment, but Lumen dismisses it as unsuitable for a crisis
  this important.
- **Inciting disruption:** Two mutually incompatible royal draft rulings arrive
  in the same sealed file. The contradiction proves only clerical trouble, so
  Lumen seeks an ordinary clarification through a service door. Inside the
  private consultation room are twelve identical human Majesties arguing from
  twelve annotated copies. When discovered, all twelve release their living
  disguises; Lumen witnesses twelve physically distinct dragons, the mundane
  rotation ledger, and Varkesh's admission. The revelation is supported by
  bodies, records, and testimony rather than inferred from contradictory text.
- **Escalating turn 1 — the motive and offer:** The dragons explain that they
  ended recurring dynastic crises and built courts, grain reserves, roads, and
  predictable succession so wars and famines would stop waking them. Each
  successful answer increased petitions and worship until the state could no
  longer choose without them. They have compiled a nonmagical book of settled
  questions and intend this to be their last case before several centuries of
  sleep. Their council is divided because every permanent ruling saves one
  necessary system by endangering another. Varkesh asks Lumen to select and
  polish one answer under the Majesty's name. The secret makes Lumen, not the
  dragons, the effective chooser.
- **Escalating turn 2 — managed consultation fails:** Lumen privately asks Ludo
  and Gelda what concessions they might tolerate but still presents the meeting
  as fact-gathering for the divine verdict. Each performs certainty for the
  unseen monarch and refuses to bargain with the other. News arrives that the
  terrace companies and house guards are moving toward the same granaries.
  Lumen recognizes the causal trap: another royal answer may stop today's
  confrontation, but it will leave the losing side with no legitimate human
  method to contest or revise it, making rebellion the only remaining appeal.
- **Crisis:** The Hall of Petitions fills for the noon judgment. The factions
  will obey the Sleepless Majesty, but the losing coalition is likely to split
  afterward or mobilize elsewhere. Varkesh offers Lumen a sealed permanent
  formula and promises that the dragons will remain awake long enough to force
  compliance. Lumen must choose between immediate order through the authority
  she worships and a public truth that may destroy her office before a bargain
  exists.
- **Climax:** Lumen does not read the royal ruling. Instead, Lumen confesses to
  drafting much of the supposedly perfect voice, summons all twelve wearers,
  and exposes the shared identity with the rotation ledger and their witnessed
  transformation. The crowd's demand merely changes from “judge us” to “command
  us to negotiate.” Varkesh offers that command; Lumen refuses it too. Using
  only the existing common-plea procedure, Lumen makes Ludo and Gelda state in
  public what their people cannot survive and what loss they can bear. Their
  leverage forces reciprocal concessions:
  - seed stores and tenancy land are protected from seizure until the next
    harvest;
  - importing houses receive published inventories, capped repayment from
    future surplus, and no blanket cancellation of principal;
  - city depots release grain at an audited margin while terrace districts
    withdraw from the granaries;
  - the terms expire in ninety days unless district delegates amend and renew
    them after open stock counts.
  Ludo and Gelda sign as accountable human representatives and dispatch their
  own stand-down orders. Lumen witnesses the plea, but neither the dragon
  collective nor a royal seal ratifies it. Varkesh and the other eleven choose
  silence, accepting that the agreement is incomplete and could be revised
  badly. The imminent clash is averted by human interests, concessions, and
  authority, not revelation alone.
- **Resonant aftermath:** The grain articles produce complaints, price rises,
  and one early amendment, proving they are not disguised perfection. The
  dragons publicly cease adjudication, disclose the false continuity of the
  Sleepless Majesty, abandon the shared guise, and depart for their old remote
  sleeping place without creating a human monitoring system or retaining an
  emergency veto. Existing clerks open a temporary docket in which every
  petition must include a proposed concession; district meetings can replace
  the procedure after one year. Lumen loses the royal title and becomes one of
  several recallable chairs. End with an untidy human hearing continuing while
  all twelve dragons remain asleep: peace is now work people can revise, not a
  perfect answer guaranteed from above.

## Setup and payoff

- The common-plea rule appears in scene 1 as a dusty option Lumen considers too
  small for a national crisis; it becomes the lawful container for the climax
  precisely because it requires party ownership and no royal ratification.
- The two incompatible draft rulings initially resemble clerical error. Their
  twelve sets of annotations, rotation ledger, physical wearers, witnessed
  transformation, and public admission later establish the deception through
  converging evidence.
- Lumen routinely removes “undignified bargaining positions” from petitions.
  In the climax, asking what each side can afford to lose restores the material
  information the royal process trained them to conceal.
- The dragons' book of settled questions is visibly nonmagical and already
  crowded with exceptions. Its failure on the grain case seeds why no final
  codex can safely govern several centuries.
- Ludo's seed sack and Gelda's import ledger each appear during testimony:
  concrete proof that one side needs biological continuity and the other needs
  financial continuity. Both stay on the negotiating table when concessions
  are written.
- Courtiers praise Lumen for making judgments sound as if they came from one
  timeless mind. That professional triumph pays off as Lumen's public admission
  that the divine voice was partly editorial labor.
- The hall's responsive prayer—“We ask; you answer”—interrupts the first
  attempt at negotiation. Its failure to receive an answer in the climax makes
  silence a risk the dragons and worshippers must bear, not a new command.
- Varkesh repeatedly asks for a guarantee that nobody can give. The first
  ninety-day amendment in the aftermath proves that accepting revisability,
  rather than discovering perfection, is what permits the dragons to sleep.

## Canon constraints

- Keep the polity local, the era unstated, and the world singular. Do not imply
  a separate plane, universal dragon dynasty, or control over all magic and
  history. (`universe/premise.md` — `The deep-time shared world`; `Knowledge
  and connection`)
- Transformation is a narrow action of each living dragon: while awake, a
  dragon reshapes visible body and voice into the memorized royal guise. No
  seal, throne, book, title, or archive acts independently. Do not generalize
  the mechanism to all dragons. (`universe/rules.md` — `System categories`;
  `Living agency and magic`)
- **Worship / power / authority before the climax:** worship is intentionally
  directed to the living collective acting as **the Sleepless Majesty**, not to
  a nonliving crown, dead dynasty, abstract ideal, separate mask, or any named
  individual dragon. The same coordinated collective supplies living power to
  the shared guise and exercises final royal and religious authority. It
  therefore satisfies canon's conjunction and is a **god**. The story must not
  deny this merely because twelve persons compose it or because their motive is
  sleep.
- The shared human guise has no separate consciousness. The twelve dragons
  remain distinct persons who intentionally constitute one public collective
  identity; Varkesh is one member and is not individually worshipped. Regnal
  language is socially binding only, not true-name magic or identity fusion.
  (`universe/rules.md` — `Divinity`; `Memory, transformation, copies, and
  branches`; `Names and identity binding`)
- **After the human plea:** the dragons retain living power and some people may
  continue private worship, but the collective renounces and ceases exercising
  political and religious authority; humans materially stop treating its word
  as law. The three-part conjunction is broken, so the Sleepless Majesty no
  longer meets canon's godhood definition. This change is institutional and
  relational, not an automatic magical effect of consent. Sleeping or loss of
  office does not erase any dragon's personhood.
- Prove the secret through witnessed bodies, transformation, rotation records,
  twelve marked drafts, and admission. Neither contradiction nor sacred
  history proves it alone. (`universe/premise.md` — `Memory, myth, and
  recoverable truth`; `universe/rules.md` — `Records, falsehood, and historical
  traces`)
- Preserve the moral balance: peace, courts, reserves, and predictable
  succession saved real lives; false identity and worship-backed dependence
  inflicted real political harm; human factions remain responsible for their
  threats and choices. (`universe/style-guide.md` — `Personhood and moral
  consequence`)
- The conflict must remain a causal contest over seed, debt, imports,
  legitimacy, and learned procedure. No literal conflagration, prophecy,
  coincidence, dragon compulsion, or effortless intervention may resolve it.

## Proposed inventions

- An unnamed local polity with two centuries of peace, predictable succession,
  royal courts, grain administration, and direct worship of an apparently
  immortal monarch.
- Exactly twelve long-lived dragons who intentionally act as one collective
  sovereign identity because civilization-scale crises disturb their
  centuries-long sleep.
- A narrow living disguise by which each awake dragon can assume the same
  androgynous human body and voice. Maintaining it prevents deep sleep; no
  object or title supplies agency.
- The Sleepless Majesty's direct cult, Hall of Petitions, royal adjudicator
  practice, common-plea procedure, and nonmagical book of settled questions.
- A grain crisis caused by failed harvests, royal quotas, seed loans, import
  credit, threatened seizures, and rival armed mobilization.
- The ninety-day grain articles: temporary seed and land protection, audited
  imports and margins, capped future-surplus repayment, open stock counts,
  stand-down orders, district revision, and automatic expiry without renewal.
- A provisional post-royal docket whose facilitators are multiple and
  recallable, whose petitions must propose a concession, and whose procedure
  expires for human reconsideration after one year.
- This group's ability to sleep for several centuries in a remote old lair,
  along with the practical risk that war or collapse could wake them. They
  receive no guarantee, veto, human-built sleep institution, or monitoring
  arrangement.

All inventions remain local proposals unless separately reviewed and promoted.

## Failure modes to watch

- Do not import any cast, names, worship structure, institution, ceremony,
  physical crisis, or settlement from `a-crown-of-quiet-hours`; specifically
  avoid its reserved names and the labels `Founding Vigil` and `Charter of
  Waking Hours`.
- Do not literalize “not on fire.” No fire, arson, cistern, firefighting,
  burning structure, or fire-suppression metaphor should become the engine.
- Reveal the twelve dragons in scene 2. Do not save them as a twist or replace
  physical evidence with suspicious paperwork.
- Keep the count and identities exact: twelve distinct dragons; one shared
  royal identity and human guise; only Varkesh individually named; no hive
  mind, copies, separate mask-person, extra hidden dragon, or drifting aliases.
- Do not avoid the divinity consequence. Before authority is surrendered, the
  worshipped, powerful, authoritative collective is a god under canon. After
  authority materially transfers, it no longer meets the three-part test even
  if affection or private worship persists.
- Do not make either faction foolish or villainous. Seed continuity and import
  credit are both necessary; their absolutism is dangerous, but royal
  procedure and worship trained it.
- Do not let Lumen merely substitute a clever personal verdict. The common plea
  contains a bargain authored and signed by Ludo and Gelda; Lumen elicits and
  witnesses rather than ratifies it.
- Do not let revelation, a dragon speech, religious renunciation, or crowd
  applause stop armed groups. The delegates' material concessions and their
  own stand-down messages must do that causal work.
- Do not make the ninety-day articles secretly perfect. Show costs, complaints,
  an amendment, a sunset, and unresolved debt after the next harvest.
- Do not let the dragons retain an emergency veto, supervisory office, hidden
  regnal successor, magical enforcement, or guaranteed wake-up arrangement.
  Their final choice must include genuine political uncertainty.
- Do not erase the monarchy's real good or Lumen's complicity. The ending
  should preserve saved lives, assign consequences for deception and armed
  threats, and cost Lumen the revered office.
- Do not add personal names, guise names, aliases, capitalized epithets,
  personified artifacts, or office-titles used as names without checking
  `stories/NAMES.md` and updating this plan before drafting.
