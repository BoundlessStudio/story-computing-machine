# Prompt contract

## Verbatim writing prompt

> **[WP] The benevolent monarchy people have long since come to worship is really just a dozen or so dragons in disguise, hoping to set up a state that can keep everything not on fire for a few hundred years so that they can finally get some actual quality slumber.**

## Story controls

- Working title: The Peace They Could Sleep Through
- Target length: 2,500–4,000 words
- POV: First person, from a human royal adjudicator whose professional faith in
  benevolent monarchy has helped make the state dependent on royal answers.
- Tense: Past.
- Tone and genre: Wry, humane political fantasy with institutional comedy,
  moral tension, and an earned hopeful ending.
- Audience/content rating: Teen / PG-13. Threatened civil conflict, religious
  pressure, deception, and non-graphic historical violence are acceptable.
- Required elements:
  - The revered monarchy is genuinely a disguised collective of roughly twelve
    dragons, not merely advised or controlled by them.
  - Their sincere practical motive is to build a state stable enough to avoid
    wars, coups, famine, collapse, and other civilization-scale emergencies
    while they sleep for several centuries.
  - Their rule has produced substantial real good even though their identities,
    genealogy, and ultimate motive are concealed.
  - Royal worship has become part of the problem: people have learned to await
    perfect judgments from above rather than own difficult compromises.
  - The secret must be revealed early enough to create the protagonist's main
    choice and must directly shape the climax.
  - Humans must avert an imminent political breakdown through negotiation,
    responsibility, and revisable institutions rather than a final royal
    decree; the dragons must accept uncertainty in order to sleep.
- Prohibited elements:
  - No literal fire, arson, dry cistern, firefighting, or burning-building
    crisis as the story's central engine.
  - Do not reuse the plot, cast, names, worship structure, civic institutions,
    or constitutional settlement from `a-crown-of-quiet-hours`.
  - No effortless dragon intervention, perfect ruler, simple “monarchy bad”
    conclusion, or nihilistic collapse.
  - No contradiction of authoritative shared-universe canon and no canon
    promotion without explicit user approval.

## Assumptions

- “Keep everything not on fire” is entirely idiomatic in this version: maintain
  a low-crisis, non-warring, materially functional society.
- The immediate threat is a human political dispute nearing civil conflict,
  not a physical conflagration.
- The dragons' greatest design failure is not cruelty but paternalism: by
  making the Crown the trusted answer to every dispute, they have prevented
  the state from becoming safely self-correcting without them.
- The climax will test whether the protagonist can decline an authoritative
  royal answer and make adversaries participate in a compromise they can later
  revise.
- This is an independent reimagining. The previous candidate remains evidence
  of a rejected literal reading and supplies no canon or story facts here.
- Dragon biology, disguise, the polity, its faith, and its political crisis
  remain local inventions unless separately promoted.

## Completion tests

- The result is a complete short story, not a synopsis or chapter fragment.
- The central promise of the prompt is dramatized and resolved.
- No literal fire or fire-suppression plot substitutes for the idiomatic
  meaning of “not on fire.”
- A human protagonist discovers the draconic monarchy early enough for the
  revelation to transform their loyalties and choices.
- The approaching civil conflict is averted through a human-owned, imperfect,
  revisable agreement rather than a dragon's command or surprise power.
- The ending credibly lets the dragons begin centuries of quality sleep while
  leaving humans responsible for keeping their own state functional.
- The monarchy's benevolence, deception, and dependency-producing paternalism
  all remain morally real.
- The story passes canon and continuity review.
- Every character-facing name and alias is checked against `stories/NAMES.md`;
  any deliberate reuse has a documented narrative reason.
