## 2026-09-28 — outline conversation and repair

The writer first proposed bringing Iona's father into the crossing. Both readers saw the gain: someone who remembers the full court could measure its absence, and his handling of the offering might close the story through action. Both also found that this would remove Iona's distinctive task of reporting to someone who could not check her account. The writer reconsidered and kept him at home.

The revised direction gives Iona two promises: report precisely and leave the marked bell she made, which her father sent through an older temple rite before his goddess vanished. Barnaby takes a metal fruit from another deserted pantheon's court, then reaches for the marked bell because it would identify their crossing publicly. Iona stops him and leaves it. At the inhabited garden doorway, Barnaby asks for bearings and Iona declines to sign a public map of a home whose occupant shut the door. Her private report still acknowledges that she saw inhabitants. The father challenges her unsupported line, “She never heard us”; she crosses it out, leaving their disagreement and his hope intact.

Codex reader found these changes repaired the principal outline risk; it asked that Barnaby's reach and Iona's intervention be physically enacted, and that the father correct an actual line rather than a hypothetical overclaim. Claude likewise accepted the witness-centered direction, then identified a remaining causal question: the familiar bell phrase that leads Iona to the seam needs a credible relation to the wind-rung bells she later describes. The writer is resolving that before prose. Coordinator judgment: the public map can be withheld without falsifying the private report, and Barnaby's separate account may remain outside Iona's control; these limits are part of the story's moral consequence, not a universal crossing rule.
