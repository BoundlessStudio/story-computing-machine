# Provisional direction: Where the Gods Left Their Doors

The existence and departure of several pantheons are documented history in Iona's city: people kept treaties, witnessed their departures, and still possess objects they made. Iona's father was a bell keeper for one of them. Before his goddess vanished, he sent her a small bell Iona had made and marked with a crooked blue ribbon. When a few notes from the court's old peal sound below Queen Street, Iona and a young Barnaby Greave follow them to upward-falling black rain and a living white seam in an abandoned tunnel. It is a clue, not proof that a god is calling. Her father asks her to tell him precisely what she sees if she reaches the bell court. Separately, he asks her to leave his gift there if she finds it: it was given, not lent. She promises both. Barnaby comes for the first crossing because he is brave enough to enter and eager to be first to name what lies beyond.

The seam opens onto red grass, a white road, and a sky crowded with doors. One route takes them through the deserted harvest court of a different vanished pantheon, where metal fruit has fallen into drifts around untended festival tables. Its emblems match old public records. Barnaby pockets a fruit as evidence of a strange place. A passage once used between the two pantheons brings them to the bell court. Gusts move loose ropes and open shutters before the bells sound in incomplete phrases; Iona can observe that much without claiming it explains every note heard below Queen Street. She finds her childhood knot intact on the little bell among old offerings. Barnaby already has his hand on its fastening when she stops him. Unlike his fruit, the marked bell could prove exactly which historical domain they entered. He argues that she is choosing a private promise over their joint discovery; she leaves the gift in place anyway and records the ringing without calling it an answer from anyone.

Returning to the red-grass approach, they reach a door onto Mavrix's realm: the spiked flowers closing at dusk and a blue-clawed inhabitant who sees them and shuts her own door. This one bounded route reaches the inhabited place in *A Pet Human*; Mavrix's later temple-wall call remains her distinct living act. Barnaby wants Iona's bearings to chart this door as further proof of their route. She refuses to help publish the position of a home whose occupant turned them away; he can write his own account, but she will not sign its map. This second refusal strains their partnership. Barnaby starts using “the Realms” for places they cannot identify, language he will later admit was shorthand in *A Reasonable Declaration*.

Back home, Iona tells her father the whole observed account, including the inhabited door and why she withheld its location from a public map. She describes the gusts, ropes, and bells she watched, and the gift still in place; then, out of a wish to end his waiting, adds, “She never heard us.” He asks how she could know that from one visit to an empty court. Iona crosses out that sentence in her notes. He keeps the ordinary bell by his window: the sound can still matter to him without being an answer. The ending should live in that exchange and his concrete response, not in a forecast of future Runner lore.
