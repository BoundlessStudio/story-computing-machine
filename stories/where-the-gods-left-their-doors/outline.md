# Provisional direction: Where the Gods Left Their Doors

Iona grew up with the departure of several pantheons as documented history, not legend. Her father tended the bells of one deserted court until its last public temple closed. He asks her, if anyone ever finds a way inside, to tell him what she actually sees there. Iona wants to bring back something firmer than another theory. She and a young Barnaby Greave make an early crossing through the living white seam beneath Queen Street, before its later Runner routines are settled. The black rain falls upward; beyond the seam are red grass, a white road, and doors moving against the sky. Barnaby wants to name and claim what they find. Iona needs his nerve and his company, but she has promised her father an honest account rather than a consoling one.

Their route passes through two absences. In the realm of a vanished harvest pantheon, metal fruit has fallen and gathered in drifts around untended festival tables; its carved names and emblems are the same ones Iona knows from public records. An old passage between that realm and the bell court of her father's pantheon gives the search a direction rather than a tour. In the bell court, ropes sway and bells sound when a crosswind reaches them, but the thrones and listening galleries are empty. Her father's small offering is still where he left it. Iona is tempted to carry it away as proof, or to call the ringing an answer; either would change the account he asked her to bring home. Barnaby finds their route back beginning to fail, forcing her to decide what evidence she can take and what she must leave.

On the return approach, a reachable door briefly shows a *different*, inhabited realm: Mavrix's spiked flowers closing at dusk, a child calling inside, and a blue-clawed adult who shuts that particular doorway on the strangers. This is a bounded route to the place seen in *A Pet Human*, not the living temple-wall call Mavrix later answers there. Iona and Barnaby cannot turn two empty courts into a claim about every door. Barnaby begins using “the Realms” as a useful name for places they cannot yet identify, a usage that will outlive his certainty in *A Reasonable Declaration*. Iona goes home to tell her father where his offering remains and that she did not see the gods. What he makes of that answer, and what it costs her to give it without softening, should carry the ending.
