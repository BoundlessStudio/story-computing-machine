# Editorial notes

## Request readings, November 19, 2026

The Codex keeper identified the technician’s duty to the exhibited people as the only specified relationship. Its inferred pleasure was “the peculiar intimacy of being the person who services an artwork that can experience the service.” It read the foreground woman’s busy thumbs as privacy in full view and the lifted chin beside her as ambiguous because of the neck fitting. It expected the price of time and the experience of being exhibited to affect each other. Consent to display and the outcome remained open.

Claude independently read the technician as someone who spends their days “caring for other people’s eternities.” It understood the overhead cord as livelihood and confinement and wanted the two economies to meet in a choice. It read everyday dress, warm chests, and candid poses against cool concrete as ordinary people caught in private moments. Its preference for a close, melancholy story and its suspicion about the exhibits’ class are inferences. Clothing supplies no birth or bank record.

## Outline

The keeper saw Dolly’s move from plinth to dark wall as the strongest link between servicing a transparent person and making a portrait that person can refuse. It wanted a cost “already exacted by the stairs” so four years wouldn’t buy a mere amenity. Claude praised “the transfer as a sitting” and worried a portraiture story could take over if Iria experienced a live sitting the same way she experienced public display. It asked how her purchased future is sustained, what her neighbours see, and whether the transfer itself would get enough space. Both wanted Dolly’s pleasure in her own picture protected.

In the exchange, the writer gave Dolly a missed callout and a night sleeping at work. Iria now offers a composed picture in advance. Dolly asks to choose the instant herself. The keeper found a genuine surrender of control in that counteroffer. Claude agreed, wanted evidence of the price through the last exposure, and withdrew a claim that the raised palm in the image necessarily touches a wall. The revised sitting gives the transfer the last third, lets another soul choose whom to watch, and keeps Dolly’s body changing as the two women reach for the picture.

My November 19 comparison rests on who needs the sitting and who can afford it. Dolly’s work makes a clear face photographable against a dark wall while the lead keeps it alive. Her wages and stairs have already made sleep at work the affordable choice. She is entitled to sell and to ask for her own exposure. Iria can buy four years now and more whenever she likes. Only a living work could make Iria’s counteroffer, and only an audience with money could have brought it about. The private portraits deepen the class bargain through Dolly’s appetite for a good picture. The revised closing moment holds both that appetite and the cost in her hands.

## First draft

The writer chose Dolly and worked in three scenes that read as one appointment. The prose profile prompted an ear-check of Iria’s long account and Dolly’s work with hot dust, cuff and reel. Cold, Codex said the collector touching his teeth “made me do it too,” and liked the interval between Dolly returning the pin and having to hold the resulting picture farther away. Its main question was who could hire Iria out. Claude felt “both halves” of Dolly’s pleasure and loved “There are always ten,” but had to reconstruct the badge and collar pictures’ chronology. It found the rent notice in Dolly’s pocket a mind working and the yellow dress making brown dots in Iria’s eyes a distraction. The two readers disagreed about the toothache image and the story’s pace. The keeper felt the money waiting until Dolly can stand and the weight under her elbow preserved the real cost.

In discussion both cold readers identified the same unintended inference that Thursday’s photograph was the badge sitting. Claude withdrew its request to give Loane a last word after rereading the coat, cuff marks and focal distance. Codex wanted Iria’s ability to turn down a household tested in Dolly’s work. Claude preferred a borrowed freedom, suspecting a veto would undercut the phone. I read a meaningful difference between choosing the house in which you work and controlling what the people there can ask of you.
