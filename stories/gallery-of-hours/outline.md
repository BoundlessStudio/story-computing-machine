# House Time

## The experience promised

The request puts a struggling technician at work among human beings whom collectors can buy and look at. A person shines through the clear body on its overhead lead. Someone who needs money has given up actual years for that light. I read a promise of beauty, unease and intimacy with the material cost of immortality. The later directions explicitly ask for Mature and insist that the technician stay external, including at the end. “They are tech.” Which souls we meet, who tells, what happens at the gallery and whether anyone prevails are open.

## Three possible stories

- At a deinstallation an independent rigging mechanic has to part the hands of two immortal people being sold to different houses. Their last few yards together make a scene, though the sellers of years remain at a distance.
- A collector buys a minute in which an immortal artist lives a year. When she signals from inside that year, a technician paid to watch a switch has to answer. This brings all three times into one room, the purchaser's, the seller's and the worker's. It gives the most to see and the most to lose.
- After a gallery closes, old year-sellers try to recognize where their lives went by the ordinary habits of the bodies in storage. One mechanic can bring a row awake for them. The recognition would hold the story, with less room for the collectors to act.

## Provisional direction

Odette Foy runs a one-van service business. She maintains the seven ceiling drops in the members' room at the North Lamp Works, among lifts, signs and other customers elsewhere. The transparent woman bent over her phone is Coralie Hecht, who made enough money to have her consciousness taken out of her dying body. She herself conceived the work for which a collector has hired the room tonight. For one minute her mind will run through a whole purchased year while her clear body holds its ordinary pose. Her thumb can signal to return to the speed at which everybody else is living. The seller, Jan Pavo, comes on the single guest ticket allowed her by Coralie. She has sold a year outright. She would consider selling another at this rate, to move from a room rented by the week to a flat with a stove.

Odette repairs the return contact that fails when Coralie bends her head. The collector wants Odette to supervise the signal manually tonight, owing to earlier false stops. The hire fee would get Odette's van out of arrears. During the viewing Odette takes one second to make sure a flicker is real. In Coralie's life that costs six days. On house current again, Coralie can speak. During the months inside those seconds she made a creature out of a fold of Jan's coat and waited for it to lift its head. She had known exactly how many days a second was before she began. She hadn't known what she might want in six days.

The buyer asks them to finish the minute. Jan would like a second sale. Odette would like that fee. Coralie asks to finish living the balance of this first year at the rate of a woman in a room. Odette takes the fast supply off the overhead line, leaving Coralie warm and mobile on the house feed. It costs her the full-performance fee. Coralie can spend the rest of the year here in public, and anybody who has paid to see it is welcome to stay. Jan keeps her full payment for the first year, which has gone out of her body for good. No second year is bought here. The story stays with what that ordinary first afternoon looks like. Odette works from the service aisle throughout. Her body, mind and years never enter the collection, and she has no part in buying or selling a life.

## People, voice and room

**Odette** has a little vanity about how quickly she can find a bad connection blind. She knows the machinery and takes the customers' word for what a false signal feels like to a sitter. She prices her time aloud, makes expansive promises on the future tense, and gets precise when frightened. She wants Coralie to trust her hands. Later she has to tell Coralie exactly how long one of those hands took. Try Odette telling it after the unpaid invoice comes back, then close third in the minutes when she still expects to collect.

**Coralie** has had more practice at being looked at than at being overruled. She can let a long silence stand, make an extravagant claim and amend it out loud. She wants Jan to see what she has bought and an outside hand she can trust with her signal. At the break she wants Jan to show her the inside of an ordinary coat. **Jan** starts from prices, gets sidetracked in affectionate detail when something is worth owning and can come back very sharply to an offer. Earlier sales have made her older than her older sister. She wants to see what someone does with a year she sold and to sound out the chance of a second sale. She has reason to be annoyed with both women. The collector's few interventions carry purchasing power. The three women have the room.

In the original the foreground woman gives her warm light and nearly all her attention to her clear phone. Her hair is screwed up with loose ends falling toward the screen. Her bowed nape changes the angle of her upper-back plug. Her thumb can reach the return in the cast handset. The collectors have come to examine that absorbed, self-possessed posture. Let her keep her appetite and powers of observation after the year turns ugly. Behind her the people on separate black plinths have their own preoccupations. The upturned face and clear backpack, a seated texter, a hand held up against the light give the mechanic different head angles to test. Drawing one woman out of her pose changes a whole row.

Choose the old lamp works' north inspection room over a penthouse salon or a subterranean showroom. The tall cold end window and polished floor once let people see flaws in glass. Now collectors take the best sightlines and Odette has to use a reflection to see a thumb from the service aisle. A late winter afternoon, wet wool drying, grit under a ladder foot, low hum in the ceiling, rough concrete, warm bodies mirrored in grey light. The real light outside can move across the room at only one speed.

## Scenes and ending

1. **Before the doors open, about a quarter.** Odette chases the intermittent signal up Coralie's line and down again by asking her to assume her actual pose. They establish what the thumb asks. Jan arrives in her good winter coat to see what she sold. Odette accepts the manual watch and its fee. Other repair visits and the extraction stay in summary.
2. **The private viewing and the interruption, the long scene, about half.** Stay with the room through the running seconds. The rich have bought the chance to watch somebody have a year they themselves can barely see passing. Restore ordinary current on the first true call. The conversation owed is between the three women at this break. What was that second like inside, what did Jan give up to make it possible, and will Odette put the woman back on fast current? Coralie gives her account in sequence, right down to the last six days spent with a fold of wool. Jan can want the money and have a retort of her own to being asked to supply an experience worth having.
3. **That same room after the buyer asks for the rest, about a quarter.** Give Coralie's request and Odette's physical change of supply room to happen in front of people who have paid. The exhibit remains alive. The next year Jan might have sold here is still hers when she takes her coat. Coralie has been waiting months for the creature in the sleeve to move. Let her see what a woman taking down her coat actually looks like when two people have time in common.
