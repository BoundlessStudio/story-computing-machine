# House Time

## The experience promised

The request puts a struggling technician at work among human beings whom collectors can buy and look at. A person shines through the clear body on its overhead lead. Someone who needs money has given up actual years for that light. I read a promise of beauty, unease and intimacy with the material cost of immortality. The later directions ask for Mature and keep the technician outside the display and the trade in years through the ending. “They are tech.” By choosing “Option 1,” the user explicitly permitted Odette to operate the emergency stop from her service bench during a showing. Which souls we meet, who tells, what happens at the gallery and whether anyone prevails are open.

## Three possible stories

- At a deinstallation an independent rigging mechanic has to part the hands of two immortal people being sold to different houses. Their last few yards together make a scene, though the sellers of years remain at a distance.
- A collector buys a minute in which an immortal artist lives a year. When she signals from inside that year, a technician paid to watch a switch has to answer. This brings all three times into one room, the purchaser's, the seller's and the worker's. It gives the most to see and the most to lose.
- After a gallery closes, old year-sellers try to recognize where their lives went by the ordinary habits of the bodies in storage. One mechanic can bring a row awake for them. The recognition would hold the story, with less room for the collectors to act.

## Provisional direction

Odette Foy runs a one-van service business. Her regular calls include the seven ceiling drops in the members' room at the North Lamp Works. The transparent woman bent over her phone is Coralie Hecht, who made enough money to have her consciousness taken out of her dying body. The collector who owns the Works, Gil Parran, wants a pair of portraits taken a year apart without even a shaft of light having moved across that clear face. Coralie liked the idea enough to invent the sitting. The commission bought her a further 365 waking days from Jan Pavo. Jan has aged the year and banked her money before she comes as Coralie's guest. The overhead feed sustains the shell. The removable fast drive can run that purchased year through the mind in one minute while the shell holds still.

Odette repairs the thumb contact that fails when Coralie bends her neck. Because a false stop spoils his pair of photographs, Parran asks her to watch the call lamp and pause the fast drive herself. She accepts an ordinary hourly call, paid whether the viewing lasts a minute or never starts. Four mornings a month at the Works cover her van instalment. During the viewing she takes one second to make sure the flicker is real before she drops the lever at her service bench. In Coralie's life that costs six days.

On ordinary current again Coralie can speak. At first she cultivated a dog out of a fold in Jan's coat for company. In the days after she signalled and nobody came, she tried to piss herself in the hope that someone would see a pool spreading across the nice polished floor. Her immortal body cannot give her even that. When she can first move again she tries to cover her trousers. Parran takes an extra exposure of that involuntary gesture. He can keep it, and he still wants the commissioned portrait at the end of the year. He offers Jan the money for another sitting if they finish this one and do a second. She could get out of her weekly room. A previous sale took her through menopause in days and cost her a deposit on a mattress she bled through. She wants this choice and says so.

Coralie asks to spend the unused part of her 365 days at ordinary speed. It will still be hers, and it will still be on show. At the bench the pause can be reversed. Parran demands a restart and tells Odette another firm can have her regular rounds if she declines. Odette goes up the service ladder and takes the additional fast head off the live house drop. The sitter remains warm and able to move. It would take another service call to run her fast again. Parran takes Odette's standing service key back when she gets down. He keeps the exposure he got of Coralie. Jan keeps her first payment. Without the offered second payment she has to release the flat she had on hold. From her aisle Odette hears Jan make that call in her coat, a sight Coralie has waited months for. The technician's skill, hands and independence decide what the collector can do tonight. She stays in her trade throughout.

## People, voice and room

**Odette** has a little vanity about how quickly she can find a bad connection blind. She talks a customer through motions as if the customer were doing the repair. She guarantees too much in the future tense and gets precise when frightened. She wants Coralie to trust her hands. Later she must tell her exactly how long one hand took. Try Odette telling it in first person, reluctant to admit an error of professional judgment, then close third in the afternoon when she still expects to do a good job for everybody. Let neither opening give away the loss of the rounds.

**Coralie** likes to look as well as to be looked at. She can let a long silence stand, make an extravagant claim and amend it out loud. She wants Jan to see what she bought and an outside hand she can trust. At the break she needs Jan to believe her about the dog and to show her the inside of the coat. **Jan** starts from prices, gets sidetracked in affectionate detail when something is worth owning and comes back sharply to an offer. She wants a flat with four stove burners, another year at Parran's price, and the right to set a value on what she has left. **Parran** directs portraits by asking people to look at something specific. He's an exact observer and considers his own capacity for attention a form of generosity. When it fails to persuade he can give an order.

In the original the first woman gives her warm light and nearly all her attention to her phone. Loose ends fall from her bun toward the screen. The bowed nape changes the angle of the upper-back plug. Her thumb can reach the call in the cast handset. Her wealthy shell preserves the clothes of a woman out on an ordinary errand. Behind her the people on separate black plinths have their own preoccupations. The upturned man can watch the room reflected in the polished ceiling track. When Coralie tries to cover herself he shifts his clear backpack into the photographer's line of sight, too late to spare her that first exposure. The seated texter and the raised hand belong to the patient company these two keep. Jan is a dark visitor by the window.

Choose the old lamp works' north inspection room over a penthouse salon or a subterranean showroom. The tall cold end window and polished floor once let people see flaws in glass. A dozen paying collectors stand with Parran's camera on the window side. Odette's service bench backs into the opposite aisle. She has to see the thumb in the floor while she watches her lamp. A late winter afternoon, wet wool drying, grit under a ladder foot, low hum in the ceiling, rough concrete, warm bodies mirrored in grey light.

## Scenes and ending

1. **Before the doors open, about a quarter.** Odette chases the intermittent signal through Coralie's actual pose and establishes what her thumb asks and what the fast head does. Jan comes to see what she has sold. Parran shows Odette the immovable camera and asks her to take the watch. Previous calls and the extraction stay in summary.
2. **The private viewing and its interruption, about half.** Stay through the seconds and the return to ordinary time. At that break the women have the conversation the story owes. What did six days waiting for Odette feel like, and what did an actual sale do to Jan's body and her prospects? Coralie tells the waiting in order, to the two people in front of her who can disagree about what the experience was worth. The new photograph exists before she can get her clothes straight.
3. **The dispute beside the camera, about a quarter.** Parran makes the second offer to Jan and demands that Odette release the pause. One woman asks for slower time, another wants a stove. Odette climbs up while Parran is still waiting for her to resume the minute. Let the removed head, the surrendered key and the photograph carry the consequences. Odette is putting her service ladder away when Jan takes down the coat. The waiting glass woman gets to see what the creature in the sleeve actually does when somebody moves an arm.
