# House Time

## The experience promised

The request puts a struggling technician at work among human beings whom collectors can buy and look at. Someone who needs money has given up actual years for the person shining through a clear body on its overhead lead. I read a promise of beauty, unease and intimacy with the material cost of immortality. The later directions ask for Mature and keep the technician outside the display and the trade in years. “They are tech.” The user permitted Odette to operate the emergency stop from her service bench during a showing. They then chose the ending in which she leaves the accelerator installed, puts its working restart control in Coralie's hand and gives Coralie the decision about whether and when to resume the collector's sitting. Coralie's answer, Jan's response and the effect on Odette's livelihood remain choices for the story.

## Three possible stories

- At a deinstallation an independent rigging mechanic has to part the hands of two immortal people being sold to different houses. Their last few yards together make a scene, though the sellers of years remain at a distance.
- A collector buys a minute in which an immortal artist lives a year. A technician answers the artist's call with the emergency stop, then puts the working restart in her hand. This brings the purchaser's, the seller's and the worker's time into one room.
- After a gallery closes, old year-sellers try to recognize where their lives went by the ordinary habits of the bodies in storage. One mechanic can bring a row awake for them. The recognition would hold the story, with less room for the collectors to act.

## Provisional direction

Odette Foy runs a one-van service business. Her regular calls include the seven ceiling drops in the members' room at the North Lamp Works. The transparent woman bent over her phone is Coralie Hecht, who made enough money to have her consciousness taken out of her dying body. The collector who owns the Works, Gil Parran, wants a pair of portraits taken a year apart without even a shaft of light having moved across that clear face. Coralie liked the idea enough to invent the sitting. The commission bought her a further 365 waking days from Jan Pavo. Jan has aged the year and banked her money before she comes as Coralie's guest. The overhead feed sustains the shell. Its accelerator can run that purchased year through the mind in one minute while the shell holds still.

Odette repairs the signal that fails when Coralie bends her neck. A press of the thumb against her clear handset calls Odette at the service bench. It has no command over the drive. Because a false stop spoils his pair of photographs, Parran asks Odette to take the watch. She accepts an ordinary hourly call. Four mornings a month at the Works cover her van instalment. When the signal comes she takes one second to make sure the flicker is real before she uses the emergency stop at her bench. In Coralie's life that costs six days. The house feed stays on. Even as the accelerator falls quiet Odette sees the light run through Coralie's fingers as she moves again.

On ordinary current again Coralie can speak. At first she cultivated a dog out of a fold in Jan's coat for company. In the days after she signalled and nobody came, she tried to piss herself in the hope that someone would see a pool spreading across the nice polished floor. Her immortal body cannot give her even that. When she can first move again she tries to cover her trousers. Parran takes an extra exposure of that involuntary gesture. He can keep it, and he still wants the commissioned portrait at the end of the year.

Parran offers Jan the money for another year if Coralie finishes this sitting and will do a second. Jan needs the deposit on a flat. She knows what it is to age overnight. A previous sale took her through menopause in days and cost her a deposit on a mattress she bled through. She makes her own brief offer to a prospective buyer. If Coralie wants another year to spend how she likes, she can pay Parran's price herself. They arrange to meet at the exchange in the morning. Jan must still go and let them take those days out of her. That bargain settles who pays for her year. It cannot settle what happens to the one Coralie is already living.

Coralie asks to live the unused months at ordinary speed. Parran turns to Odette and demands the restart. Odette leaves the accelerator installed. She brings its corded restart pendant the two paces from her bench, checks that it is live and puts it in Coralie's hand. All it takes now is a squeeze from the woman who must live the time. The accelerator can run tonight if Coralie chooses. Parran tells Odette to leave next Thursday's service round out of her book. The others can wait for his call. Odette goes back to her bench, still on duty for the showing.

Parran reaches for the pendant and Coralie closes her fingers on it. One of the guests moves out of the way so another can see what he's going to do. To take it he'd have to prise her hand open in front of the people who bought tickets to see her work. He lets his hand fall and asks her himself. They both wanted the two pictures taken in the same shaft of light. Let them speak about what they hoped to see in the second one. Coralie faces him and decides to leave the restart untouched. She will live those months at ordinary speed. If he wants the second portrait, he can bring back his camera on the day her purchased year is up and take whatever light there is. She keeps the control in her hand as Jan puts on the coat.

## People, voice and room

**Odette** has a little vanity about how quickly she can find a bad connection blind. She talks a customer through motions as if the customer were doing the repair. She guarantees too much in the future tense and gets precise when frightened. She wants Coralie to trust her hands. Later she must tell her exactly how long one hand took. Try Odette telling it in first person, reluctant to admit an error of professional judgment, then close third in the afternoon when she still expects to do a good job for everybody.

**Coralie** likes to look as well as to be looked at. She can let a long silence stand, make an extravagant claim and amend it out loud. She wants Jan to see what she bought and an outside hand she can trust. At the break she needs Jan to believe her about the dog. Holding the restart, she still wants years and is prepared to pay for them. **Jan** starts from prices, gets sidetracked in affectionate detail when something is worth owning and comes back sharply to an offer. She wants a flat with four stove burners and the right to name a price to someone who can pay it. **Parran** directs portraits by asking people to look at something specific. He's an exact observer and considers his own capacity for attention a form of generosity. When it fails to persuade he tries an order, and finally has to address the woman in his viewfinder.

In the original the first woman gives her warm light and nearly all her attention to her phone. Loose ends fall from her bun toward the screen. The bowed nape changes the angle of the upper-back plug. Her thumb can reach the call in the cast handset. Her wealthy shell preserves the clothes of a woman out on an ordinary errand. Behind her the people on separate black plinths have their own preoccupations. The upturned man can watch the room reflected in the polished ceiling track. When Coralie tries to cover herself he shifts his clear backpack into the photographer's line of sight, too late to spare her that first exposure. The seated texter and the raised hand belong to the patient company these two keep. Jan is a dark visitor by the window.

Choose the old lamp works' north inspection room over a penthouse salon or a subterranean showroom. The tall cold end window and polished floor once let people see flaws in glass. A dozen paying collectors stand with Parran's camera on the window side. On the opposite side a narrow service strip comes within two paces of Coralie's left elbow. Odette's bench there has a hood over the lamp. From underneath the hood she can see the thumb in the polished floor. The pendant lead uncoils down the service strip as far as the plinth. A late winter afternoon, wet wool drying, grit under a ladder foot, low hum in the ceiling, rough concrete, warm bodies mirrored in grey light.

## Scenes and ending

1. **Before the doors open, about a fifth.** Odette chases the intermittent signal through Coralie's actual pose and establishes what her thumb asks and what the accelerator does. Locate the portable start pendant beside the emergency stop. Jan comes to see what she sold. Parran shows Odette the immovable camera. Previous calls and the extraction stay in summary.
2. **The private viewing and its interruption, a little over two fifths and the long scene.** Stay through the seconds and the return to ordinary time. At that break Coralie has to tell the waiting in order, including those last six days, to the two women who have different claims on it. Odette has to tell her what she was doing at the bench. The new photograph exists before Coralie can get her clothes straight.
3. **The dispute beside the camera, the remaining third or more.** Jan names her bodily cost and her price in answer to Parran's second offer. Settle the meeting at the exchange before the handover. Once Odette gives Coralie the working control, stay through Parran's curtailed exchange with Odette, his hand held out before the witnesses, and the confrontation the ruling promises. Coralie offers him a date months away and abandons her own ambition for two pictures taken in the same light. From her bench Odette sees her still holding the restart when the creature in the sleeve becomes a woman putting on her coat.
