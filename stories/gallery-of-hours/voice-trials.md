# Two openings

## 1

The girl is worth seeing at four in the afternoon when the light from the end window gets under the coil of her hair and one can make out the wire pin inside. Who would put a pin in glass hair? Dolly Soper, whose fingers are there now, knows how soft the hair is, and how heavy once you have gathered it in one hand to get at the live socket in the nape. The girl is ticklish on one side. She lifts that shoulder when Dolly gets near it, though ninety years have passed since she had the body she learned to do that in.

“Leave my hair up a minute,” Iria says. “Would you give me a scratch where my thumb is? A shade higher. Yes, there. Twenty gentlemen spent the evening admiring the back of my neck through all those curls, and you were the one person I was longing to see.”

The young fellow with the backpack, two plinths down, scratches his neck in sympathy. Iria catches him in her phone. His two brown-gold hands and his grin are on her screen by the time she lets her shoulder drop. For years Dolly took all that steadiness over the screen for the attention of somebody reading. It took being called over to see her own picture before she understood which way the little lens was pointing.

She shuts the collar on the plug. A white crumb from the bedding she slept in here last night falls off her cuff onto Iria's clear shoulder. It catches there, afloat in a living woman, for an instant more closely observed than anything in the Sample Room. Dolly longs to be past all this. One room on the ground floor where she can shut a door and stay the night behind it. She picks the crumb off, meaning to slip it in her pocket.

“Keep your hand just there,” Iria says, already composing what she sees in the long window. “Look at the lovely shape your fingers make.”

## 2

Three times that November I had to give Iria Senne her teeth back. There was a plane tree outside the end window of the Sill Street Sample Room, and when the leaves fell, a black twig showed through the glass of her two front teeth. A collector would come to the rope and put his tongue in the gap in his own mouth where he thought he saw one in hers. I could give Iria another six inches of lead and get her to step along her plinth, and there was the lovely mouth she'd paid for, down to the little chip where she'd bitten a cherry stone as a girl. She'd paid to keep that too.

It was my job to see that the current got to every person on that floor. The teeth were a favour. When I needed a new photograph for my work badge, she did one for me.

“Dolly,” she said on the afternoon I'm speaking of, “you can't possibly remember the one with your good collar, because I never let you see it. Come and admire yourself. If I'd had that face I would have kept it and lived on bread.”

“You'd have wanted something on the bread.”

It was a pretty picture. I ought to say so at the outset, considering the trouble it made. I was looking over my collar at a person outside the frame who had just paid me for five years of my life, and the look I was giving him would have curled the paper off a wall. What I remembered of that dress was how often I'd had to put it on sitting down because of the sticking zip. I had never known I could look like that in it.

She enlarged my face between two glass fingers. There was my own mouth, big as a peach, and coming through it I could see the grey wool of the blanket I had tried to hide behind her stand. I'd slept the night there. Iria shifted one foot to hide the blanket from the door and let me go on looking.
