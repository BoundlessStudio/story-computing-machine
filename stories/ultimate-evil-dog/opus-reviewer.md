# Opus review: A Very Good Dog

Independent audit for a later editing pass. It is not a `review.md` verdict,
does not change canon state, and does not authorize edits by itself.

## Verdict

- Dialogue: REVISE — the alert-scene fetch exchange contradicts itself, and three other beats have broken staging, a misreadable speaker, or a summary line where a reaction should be.
- Prose: PASS — clean, concrete comic prose with good timing; only a stray pronoun and one wrong verb.
- Continuity and prompt: PASS — every prompt constraint is met; one small object contradiction (the text tablet).
- Edit scope: targeted-dialogue
- Existing review.md said: Verdict PASS, "Dialogue: PASS" (credited distinct rhetorical ownership); I agree the voices are distinct but disagree with the PASS, because it missed a self-contradicting exchange at the key-theft attempt and broken staging in the lab.

Voice is this story's strength, and the edit pass should protect it. Brightguard's owner talk ("White foot first, sweetheart", "Close enough, baby"), the butler's courteous formality, and Maledict's all-caps proclamations shrinking to WITH BED? all sound like different people. The prompt's "hey, you don't normally do X" engine works in most exchanges. The failures are local Ground problems, not the collection's usual house-voice convergence: a fetch ritual whose rules change from line to line, a line the reader first hears as the dog speaking, a harness Pickle tugs from across the room, and an owner speech that sums up the comedy instead of reacting to it. The legalistic house register leaks in only once, when Brightguard offers to take Maledict in and starts talking like the butler. Fix those beats with small edits and leave the rest of the dialogue and its comic timing alone.

## Edit guardrails

- Freaky Friday swap at 7:00 a.m.: Maledict is in Pickle's dog body; Pickle is alive, unharmed and present in Maledict's human body throughout. Pickle never speaks words (prompt safety line; outline). No fix may give Pickle a spoken line.
- The butler expects fourteen hours (dial "13:49 TO RETURN"; "automatic return at nine this evening"). The swap fails to end at nine because Maledict engages the hold by choice (L305–L315). That beat is the prompt's "isn't ending" and must survive.
- Brightguard is sixty-one and contemporary (impact mesh, wrist display, field harness) and already owns and pampers Pickle. Setting is the Tri-City Area; Maledict's base is a skyscraper. Pickle's white forepaw is natural fur.
- The prompt's comic engine is Brightguard spotting wrong behavior: white foot first, the peas, no victory lap, red boots vs. sneaker, the blue harness, spinning twice, the shoe/slipper test on video. Fixes may clarify these beats but must not cut them.
- Recast needs Maledict's continuous confirmation after he is human again (L343, L389, L409–L419). Keep it; it keeps the ending inside the LOCKED "Personhood and moral consequence" entry in `universe/style-guide.md`.
- Ending state: Pickle in his own body with his bed, bowls, blanket and sofa place; Maledict a distinct long-legged black dog, publicly "missing". The close is behavior (breakfast, waiting), with no moral line; keep it that way.
- "Brightguard" is user-supplied in `prompt.md`. LOCKED "Brightward College" (`universe/locations.md`) is a deliberately distinct later name. Do not rename either one or connect them.
- Broadly accessible rating. Length band 2,500–4,000 words (currently about 3,580), so keep fixes roughly net-neutral.

## Dialogue findings

### D1 · Blocking · Fetch-the-guard exchange contradicts itself

- Where: L43 and L95–L115, the emergency alarm in the office doorway
- Text:
> Pickle normally fetched her left wrist guard at the emergency tone.
> “Guard, Pickle.”
> “You hate this floor.”
> “That’s more like you.” She pointed at the guard. “Can you bring it?”
- Problem: Ground/uptake. L43 makes fetching the guard, which sits in the office, Pickle's normal alarm job, and "Guard, Pickle." reads as that command. Brightguard then objects to him entering the office, praises him for backing out, and at once asks him to go back in. The reader gets: fetch it / don't go in there / good, you're out / now fetch it. The likely intent is that Pickle always balks at the humming threshold and has to be asked, but that is never established. Maledict's own inference ("The correct behavior, apparently, was not to enter the room containing the thing he needed.") pushes the reader toward the wrong reading. This is the plot's one direct move on the key, so the confusion lands on a key beat.
- Fix: Establish the balk in L43 with one clause (Pickle fetches the guard, but only after refusing the humming floor until he is asked). Change L111 so Maledict concludes that he should have hesitated, not that he should stay out. Brightguard's lines can then stay almost as written: her surprise is that he charged in, not that he entered. Leave "Not the key." and the tax-fraud expression alone.

### D2 · Major · The butler's answer reads as Pickle speaking

- Where: L193–L199, the butler finding Pickle at the secured elevator
- Text:
> “Home?” the butler asked.
> Pickle looked at him, then at the door.
> “Yes,” he said. “I believe so.”
- Problem: Attribution. The sentence before has Pickle as its subject, so "he said" first reads as the dog-minded body answering in English. That would break the story's rule that Pickle has no words. The reader has to back up and give the line to the butler, and this is the beat where the butler decides to act. Even once it's reassigned, "I believe so" is slightly off: nobody asked him a question. He is drawing a conclusion from Pickle's look.
- Fix: Tag the butler by name or role and make the line his conclusion rather than a reply. Illustrative only: “Yes,” the butler said, mostly to himself. “I thought so.” Give Pickle no words.

### D3 · Major · Owner's inventory closes on a summary line

- Where: L225, noon kitchen, just before the butler's call
- Text:
> “And you’ve never forgiven the mail carrier, but you let her scratch your ears this morning. You slept through the blender. You went into my office.”
> “You know commands you shouldn’t and forget things you love.”
- Problem: Philosopher/summary at a key moment, plus an unsupported claim. Everywhere else the prompt's comic engine runs on one specific reaction to something on the page. Here Brightguard lists two offstage incidents and ends on a tidy chiasmus that hands the story's premise back to the reader. "Commands you shouldn't" has nothing behind it. No scene shows the dog obeying a command Pickle doesn't know, and the salmon-crisp sit is one Pickle does know ("So we remember that one."). The speech also makes her sound composed and diagnostic at the point where she should be most unsettled.
- Fix: Cut the last sentence. Keep at most one offstage item (the mail carrier is the funniest). Let her react instead of concluding: a plain question, a hand checking his ears or gums, or a pause before "Maledict pressed OUT with emphasis." Illustrative only: “What is going on with you?”

### D4 · Major · Pickle tugs a harness across the room; "Green tomorrow" has no clear addressee

- Where: L295–L301, the lab at 8:53 p.m.; set up at L221
- Text:
> Pickle tugged at the padded blue harness Brightguard had put on the dog body across the room.
> “Green tomorrow,” Brightguard said without turning. “I heard you.”
> “You’ve refused blue since it pinched you at the lake.”
- Problem: Ground/staging and referent. Pickle is on the mat with his forehead on Brightguard's shoulder. The dog body is under the worktable "across the room," so he can't tug its harness. Because "without turning" comes right after Maledict bares his teeth, the reader first takes "Green tomorrow. I heard you." as said to Maledict, and to him it means nothing. It only works as a promise to Pickle about the harness on his body. There's also a continuity snag. At noon Brightguard reminded the dog that blue pinches, yet she has put blue on Pickle's body for the evening, and nothing says why.
- Fix: Swap the impossible tug for something Pickle can do from the mat, such as staring and whining at the blue harness or pulling Brightguard's sleeve toward it. Make it clear she is answering Pickle, with a tag or her hand on him. Add one clause somewhere in L281–L299 explaining the blue harness (for example, Maledict insisted, or the green one is at home). Keep "I heard you." Once its addressee is clear, it's a good owner line.

### D5 · Major · Brightguard's offer to take Maledict in switches to the butler's legal register

- Where: L359, lab negotiation after SNACKS ALSO.
- Text:
> “If you recast yourself, the butler remains responsible for you and your operation gets dismantled. I’ll supervise temporary containment because I have the space and because he
> comes first there. Separate bed. Separate bowls. No office.
- Problem: Negotiation routine and voice convergence in the decisive exchange. Everywhere else Brightguard sounds like a dog owner or a field commander. Here she talks in clauses ("remains responsible," "temporary containment," "operation gets dismantled"), matching the butler's register one line before he gives his own list at L361. The motive for the story's key outcome, the hero taking her nemesis into her own home, is also garbled. "Because I have the space and because he … comes first there" offers floor space as a reason, plus half a sentence about Pickle's priority, and neither explains why she would want Maledict at all. The house rules that follow (separate bed, separate bowls, no office, across the room) work because they are dog-owner rules applied to a supervillain; they should stay.
- Fix: Keep "Pickle's bed is Pickle's…" and the house rules. Replace the two legal sentences with one plain line of motive in her own voice, and leave the dismantling of the operation to the butler, who already covers it at L361. Illustrative only: “You'll sleep in my living room, where I can see you.” Do not trim the butler's readouts at L335–L343 or L389; that register is his and the consent mechanics depend on it.

### D6 · Minor · The tablet handoff and "Find that choice" don't fit the objects

- Where: L329, L345 and L381, lab after I HAVE CONQUERED IT
- Text:
> She handed the tablet to the butler. “Find that choice.”
> Maledict took the tablet.
> At last Maledict pressed DOG.
- Problem: Ground/object fit and knowledge. What she hands over is the dog's typing tablet, but the options are in the console instructions, so the action and the order don't match. "That choice" also assumes an option nobody has found yet, which reads as the author steering toward the recast. The tablet then turns up with Maledict again without the butler putting it down. And "pressed DOG" points at no established button: DOG was a word he typed, not a console option.
- Fix: Have her take the tablet to stop him typing and give the butler a plain order about the console. Illustrative only: “Read me the rest of it.” Show the butler setting the tablet down before Maledict takes it. Make DOG a visible console template (the list appears when the butler scrolls), or have Maledict nose the console field instead.

### D7 · Minor · Two replies use knowledge the speaker doesn't have yet

- Where: L259 (noon video call) and L315 (lab, after the rivet)
- Text:
> “How long?”
> “You’ve engaged the occupant hold, sir.”
- Problem: Ground/knowledge. At L259 nobody has told Brightguard the swap is on a timer. "How long?" assumes a return she hasn't heard about, and the butler answers that reading. At L315 the butler names the "occupant hold" before the violet light reveals the text describing it (L333). The heat-revealed lines he read at L303 only confirmed the automatic return.
- Fix: Let Brightguard ask what she can know to ask (Illustrative only: “Can it be undone?”), or have the butler mention the dial before she asks. For L315, give the butler a visible source: the dial prints HOLD, or he recognizes the rivet from the planning files he searched at L191.

### D8 · Minor · The reply to the final question has no tag

- Where: L413–L417, Maledict with his hand in the confirmation field
- Text:
> “Will the heated bed be of equivalent quality?”
> “It will be appropriate to your size.”
- Problem: Attribution in the final meaningful exchange. The reply is untagged, so the reader can't be sure whether this is the butler being tactful or Brightguard already planning the cooler bed of L435. The joke is sharper when we know. The diction, and Maledict's glance at "his butler" just before, point to the butler.
- Fix: Add "the butler said" (or a beat that shows him answering). Do not change the words.

## Prose findings

### P1 · Minor · A stray pronoun and a wrong verb in the butler's thread

- Where: L171 (service garden) and L389 (lab plates)
- Text:
> The suit was impossible. He found a black tracksuit in the private gym.
> The butler placed Pickle on the human plate and Maledict on the canine one.
- Problem: In L171, "He" follows a sentence about Pickle and could mean either Pickle or the butler. In L389, "placed" treats a grown man's body like the dog.
- Fix: Name the butler in L171. Use "guided" or "led" for Pickle in L389.

## Continuity and prompt findings

### C1 · Minor · The text tablet belongs to two owners

- Where: L263, noon kitchen
- Text:
> Maledict found Pickle’s text tablet docked beneath the communication mat. Its large keys were meant for Brightguard’s gloved hands.
- Problem: A tablet can't be Pickle's and also built for Brightguard's gloves. A dog who already has a six-button mat has no clear use for a text keyboard either.
- Fix: Make it Brightguard's glove-friendly field keypad that happens to dock by the mat, with keys big enough for a paw. One clause.

## Recurring patterns
