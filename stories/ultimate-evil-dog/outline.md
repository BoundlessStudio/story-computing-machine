# Outline

## Story

- Proposed reader-facing title: *The Ultimate Evil Has a Nice Lie-Down*.
- Premise and central promise: Maledict, the era's most feared living villain, changes himself into a convincing dog to bypass Brightguard's defenses, enter her home, poison her, and steal the counterseal that has frustrated his next mass-casualty scheme. Brightguard takes the apparent stray in for one night and calls him Pickle. The infiltration works perfectly; the failure is that food, warmth, play, touch, sleep, and freedom from being the ultimate anything are better than Maledict allowed for. Fulfill the promise when he reaches the sleeping hero and every objective is available, then knowingly leaves the poison unused, closes the hiding place over the counterseal, and gets into bed. Weeks later the world still searches for Maledict while he remains Brightguard's cheerfully demanding pet. His choice prevents harm but need not redeem, expose, punish, or morally educate him.
- Intended reader experience: Villain-side dramatic irony and steadily deflating menace. The reader should enjoy Maledict translating baths, fetch, dinner, and belly rubs into grand strategic language until even he stops believing the euphemisms. Keep the evil plan real enough that the midnight opportunity has weight, but let the dominant release be shameless comfort: the worst man alive discovers that being looked after is dope and simply opts out.
- Generating force:
  - Mode: Plot-led inward infiltration followed by voluntary dereliction. Each domestic barrier opens in a straight line—yard, threshold, washroom, hall, bedroom—while each success makes the mission less attractive. Do not build repeated problem-and-remedy loops or a redemption ladder.
  - Focal attachment or contradiction: Maledict built an identity around command, dread, and never being vulnerable to appetite. Dog form gives him anonymity and a body in which wanting dinner, company, motion, and sleep is immediate but not compulsory. He wants the privileges of surrender without admitting he is surrendering.
  - Plot-led wants and useful limit, if applicable: Immediately, Maledict wants one unobserved night inside, Brightguard dead, and the counterseal in his possession; he also wants his legendary disappearance to remain frightening rather than ridiculous. His competing want becomes to preserve the warm, responsibility-free life that only continued concealment allows. His useful flaw is untested contempt: he perfected the body's biological credibility but refused a full trial because he assumed pleasure could not divert a superior will. The form preserves his memory, judgment, and moral agency, but gives him canine senses, speechlessness, paws, fatigue, social ease, and appetites he must actually experience rather than remotely command.
- Counterforce or complication:
  - Source: Brightguard's reasonable stray-animal precautions slow access—bath, inspection, closed doors, a temporary sleeping mat—while the credible dog's needs keep interrupting Maledict's rehearsed timetable. The deeper complication is success: Brightguard brings him closer to the objective whenever she trusts him, so Maledict cannot blame an opposing force for his delay.
  - Credible inside logic, if a person: Brightguard is exhausted from hunting Maledict, cautious about possible bait, and unwilling to leave an animal outside. She intends only overnight shelter until ordinary help opens, checks for tracking magic, removes the concealed collar he planted, and keeps restricted rooms shut. Her practical, dry care is not a test designed to reform him. She remains capable and agentic, but Maledict's fully living transformed body carries neither his known human shape nor an active external spell for her wards to identify.
  - Pressure or change created: To keep his cover, Maledict must accept care, respond convincingly to the name Pickle, and defer any act that requires hands or human speech. Every postponement produces more evidence that he can revert and attack but does not want to. Brightguard's ongoing search reports also make his anonymity increasingly pleasurable while reminding the reader what he has chosen not to do.
- Narrative design:
  - POV, tense, distance, and limitation: Close third past through Maledict, with elastic free-indirect distance. His inner vocabulary begins operatic and tactical; concrete dog sensations steadily shorten it. Keep Brightguard exterior through weary speech, careful hands, security habits, and choices. Maledict can infer her routine but cannot read her mind, speak in dog form, or know whether one odd reaction has made her suspicious.
  - Time shape and compression: One continuous late afternoon through the first night, then a brief compression of several weeks at home. Give the staged discovery, first meal, play, household access, and midnight opportunity full scene space. Compress the later search through reports Brightguard hears or brings home; use no flashback plot, public showdown, or off-site view of Maledict's followers.
  - Information and reveal strategy: Identify Maledict in the opening and disclose the plan before Brightguard finds him; there is no dog-identity twist. Establish the transformation's limits through his first attempts to manipulate a latch, resist smell, and maintain grand attention. Let readers recognize his enjoyment before he names it. At midnight remove every practical excuse: he can reach the poison, counterseal, reversal method, and sleeping hero. His retreat must therefore read as a choice, not failed mechanics, brain overwrite, accidental mercy, or secret countermeasure.
  - Governing movement: Transform; stage helplessness; be found; pass private precautions; enter; endure washing; discover food; inspect the route; be distracted by play; gain the hall; hear the hunt for himself; intend to wait only until midnight; sleep too deeply; wake; reach the bedroom; expose the counterseal; prepare the poison; receive an unconscious invitation onto the bed; postpone; recognize the postponement as desertion; choose it; remain missing; settle into the household.
- Speculative surplus: The change leaves Maledict's coat prone to dumping harmless static into nearby brass. After petting him, Brightguard gets snapped by a door handle and begins grounding him with the wooden back of a brush before touching metal. The nuisance neither reveals identity, expresses emotion, powers the transformation, nor affects the midnight choice.
- Structural distinction: A linear comic heist whose target becomes less desirable with every successful step. The architecture narrows from the villain's immense intended consequences to one dog's bodily field of attention, then skips forward only after the crime has been privately abandoned. The decisive action occurs beside a sleeping person with no witness or proof; there is no crowd conversion, technical demonstration, institutional rewrite, confession, punishment scene, or closing emblem.
- Decisive turn, deepening, or recognition: After midnight, Maledict noses open the loose bedroom floorboard exactly where surveillance said it would be. The counterseal is within his teeth; the poison bead is still secured behind one molar; his reversal method remains available. Brightguard stirs, mistakes his movement for a bad dream, and lifts the blanket without waking. He tells himself he will wait until her hand stops resting on his shoulder. When it does, he deliberately pushes the floorboard shut, leaves the water untouched, and climbs farther under the blanket. The recognition is comic and unsentimental: he is not sparing her because she deserves mercy, though she does; he is declining victory because victory would end being Pickle.
- Aftereffect or live uncertainty: Over the next several weeks, Maledict hears that his command structure is splintering, rivals are raiding his abandoned holdings, and heroes are searching increasingly implausible refuges. Brightguard keeps Pickle after the shelter appointment falls through because both have already organized their routines around him. Maledict remains selfish, steals food, monopolizes the good chair, and retains the ability to resume human form; the story makes no promise that he has become safe in every circumstance. For now, continued disappearance is a repeated choice, and no one—including Brightguard—knows where he went.
- Opening / ending relation: Open with Maledict treating doghood as one humiliating afternoon purchased for a flawless night of evil. End weeks later on an ordinary afternoon when Brightguard, discussing the fruitless search within his hearing, says Maledict cannot hide forever. Pickle considers this, hears his dinner being prepared, and lets the thought go in favor of remaining exactly where he is. The opening's temporary disguise becomes an indefinitely preferred life without a revelation or symbolic renaming.

## Beats

1. In a concealed preparation room, Maledict reviews the concrete crimes he will commit, changes bodily into a dog, confirms that the poison bead and reversal method remain accessible, and stages himself as a muddy stray on Brightguard's route home. He treats every dog sensation as irrelevant telemetry.
2. Brightguard finds him, suspects bait, and conducts private precautions before bringing him inside for the night. Her wards reject neither a known body nor an externally sustained spell because Maledict has made himself a living dog. She removes his planted collar and names him Pickle from his enthusiastic theft of a dropped pickle slice.
3. Bathing and drying become the first comic defeat of scale: he cannot operate fixtures, speech is unavailable, wet scent overwhelms strategic attention, and being rubbed dry feels excellent. He maps exits and restricted doors anyway.
4. At dinner, Maledict intends to fake ordinary appetite but experiences it. Brightguard eats while listening to reports of his own disappearance; he enjoys the fear in the reports, then discovers he enjoys lying under the table without being responsible for any of it more.
5. A short play sequence carries him through the house. He follows a thrown object only to preserve cover, returns it because pursuit feels good, and accidentally earns access to the upstairs hall. Keep the progression linear and let his tactical euphemisms fail faster each time.
6. Brightguard sets a mat outside her bedroom and resumes the search from home. Maledict waits for midnight, misses his intended minute because he sleeps, wakes offended, and proceeds through the now-quiet house. Nothing external prevents the crime.
7. In the bedroom he opens the floorboard, reaches the counterseal and poison, and receives the half-conscious invitation onto the bed. After one last self-deceiving delay, he closes the board and chooses sleep. State the selfish truth cleanly once; do not add a moral speech or dream.
8. Compress several weeks through household changes and search reports. Maledict repeatedly declines available chances to revert, accepts permanent-pet logistics, and lounges within earshot as Brightguard wonders where the ultimate evil has gone. End on dinner and continued concealment, not discovery.

## Continuity

- Canon used: The episode occurs somewhere in the one persistent deep-time world, in an otherwise unattached high-magic heroic culture. Multiple local systems may exist; only living Maledict performs this transformation. The mechanism-specific transformation preserves his identity, memory, agency, and moral responsibility.
- Boundaries and unknowns: Treat Maledict's bodily change, Brightguard's household defenses, and the counterseal as new local mechanisms with no link to established transformation, concealment, identity, animal-affinity, or ward systems. Establish no universal rule that dog bodies overwrite minds, pleasure fuels magic, evil has a detectable signature, or kindness mechanically reforms anyone. Keep the era, city, country, Maledict's wider organization, exact past chronology, and future return unresolved. Brightguard does not learn Pickle's identity, the public receives no proof, and this non-canon story does not alter an established faction or event.
