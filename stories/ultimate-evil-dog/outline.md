# Outline

## Story

- Proposed reader-facing title: *A Very Good Dog*.
- Premise and central promise: Pickle has been Brightguard's dog for years and already enjoys a heated memory-foam bed, favorite snacks, practiced routines, and the undivided affection of an older modern superhero. Maledict swaps minds with him for fourteen hours so Pickle's recognized body can carry poison and a lock override into Brightguard's townhouse. Pickle wakes unharmed in Maledict's human body at the villain's skyscraper, where Maledict's unnamed butler must contain eighty pounds of cheerful dog instinct inside the most feared man in the `Tri-City Area`. At home, Maledict's ignorance of Pickle's established habits makes Brightguard repeatedly say versions of “you love this” and “you never do that.” The infiltration succeeds anyway. Maledict reaches the sleeping hero and her counterseal, but deliberately misses the reversal window because Pickle's existing life of luxury is better than ruling. The public never discovers where Maledict went; only his increasingly exhausted butler knows why the arrangement has not ended.
- Intended reader experience: Immediate visual cartoon comedy with a real evil objective underneath it. Open on Pickle-in-Maledict's-body doing something unmistakably canine while the butler realizes his fourteen-hour assignment has begun. Cut between the two mismatched households. Let Brightguard's affectionate familiarity, rather than generic dog sensations alone, supply the funniest pressure.
- Generating force:
  - Mode: Two-track body-swap farce narrowing into a private choice. Maledict must imitate one particular beloved dog while the butler must pass one particular dog off as an absent supervillain.
  - Wants and useful flaw: Maledict wants Brightguard dead, her counterseal stolen, and his body restored after fourteen hours. He also wants to believe any pet routine can be learned from surveillance. His useful flaw is contempt for intimate knowledge: he catalogued security systems but dismissed favorite flavors, commands, rituals, fears, and small household rules as beneath him.
  - Competing want: Every correction exposes a comfort he did not know Pickle possessed. Maledict increasingly wants the identity he is failing to perform because its owner already loves it.
- Counterforce or complication:
  - Brightguard knows Pickle's baseline. Wrong reactions first read as mood, then illness, then possible magical interference. She is observant without guessing the impossible truth.
  - At the skyscraper, dog-minded Pickle makes Maledict's human body physically dangerous but emotionally transparent. The butler must prevent injury, conceal the situation from lieutenants and video calls, and prepare the paired return apparatus.
  - The reversal is safe and repeatable but requires Maledict-in-Pickle to bite and hold the activation plate inside Pickle's collar while Pickle-in-Maledict stands in the paired penthouse circle. The butler cannot trigger Maledict's half remotely. Missing fourteen hours postpones the next window until the following night, making continued absence a repeated choice.
- Narrative design:
  - POV, tense, distance: Past tense, close third primarily through Maledict, with short scene-break cutaways close to the butler. Pickle remains legibly dog-minded through action, expression, appetite, and the butler's responses; do not give him humanized dialogue.
  - Time shape: Begin at the instant after the swap, follow one day and night to the missed fourteen-hour reversal, then compress several days of repeated missed windows.
  - Information strategy: State the swap, identities, existing-pet relationship, and fourteen-hour limit immediately. There is no identity mystery. The dramatic irony is whether Maledict can pass as Pickle and whether the butler can keep Pickle's borrowed human body from exposing the truth.
  - Visual/comic rhythm: Alternate compact domestic set pieces rather than explaining the premise at length. At least four Brightguard corrections must escalate: Maledict rejects a beloved snack; fails a known command or greeting ritual; embraces something Pickle normally hates; and claims a forbidden sleeping place or behaves with eerie human purpose. Mirror these with Pickle-in-Maledict wrecking villainous dignity at the skyscraper.
  - Governing movement: Swap; butler loses a shoe to dog-minded Maledict's body; Maledict returns through Pickle's biometric access; fails the greeting ritual; gets corrected through food, toy, bath, and furniture habits; enjoys the pre-existing bed and snacks; hears the public search for himself; butler survives a command call and prepares reversal; Maledict uses bedtime access to reach poison and counterseal; Brightguard's final “you never do this” becomes permission rather than exposure; Maledict refuses the fourteen-hour signal; repeat missed windows; both households adapt.
- Speculative surplus: The swap carries motor memory but not autobiographical memory. Maledict's dog body can perform a familiar trick a fraction before he consciously decides, while Pickle's borrowed human hands sometimes complete a practiced villainous gesture before returning to pawing or chasing. This creates physical gags but never erases either mind or decides Maledict's choice.
- Structural distinction: A matched-body farce: the hero's cozy townhouse and the villain's severe penthouse repeatedly rhyme. One contains a villain learning to be a known dog; the other contains a dog forcing a butler to treat ultimate evil like an oversized pet. No new-stray discovery, adoption, shelter, erased consciousness, redemption speech, public showdown, or late identity twist.
- Decisive turn: At midnight Maledict has the counterseal between his teeth, poison ready, and the collar plate against his tongue as the fourteen-hour return signal pulses. Brightguard wakes enough to notice Pickle in a place he never chooses and says, “You never sleep up here.” Then she makes room because she thinks her old dog needs her. Maledict reseals the counterseal, leaves the poison unused, and lifts his tongue from the plate until the window closes.
- Aftereffect: Each next evening the butler arranges Pickle-in-Maledict inside the return circle and sends increasingly restrained messages. Each evening Maledict declines. The butler secretly commissions reinforced furniture, childproofs the weapons console, and feeds Pickle safely while telling the organization its master remains in seclusion. Brightguard remains puzzled by Pickle's new tastes and habits but accepts that her dog has changed. The wider `Tri-City Area` cannot find Maledict.
- Opening / ending relation: Open with the ultimate villain's human body joyfully stealing the butler's shoe while Maledict, already inside the real Pickle, insists the indignity lasts fourteen hours. End after another missed window: the butler watches Pickle curl Maledict's body onto an oversized custom dog bed while, across the city, Maledict settles into Pickle's original bed after snacks. Both bodies are alive and cared for; only one mind wants the old arrangement back.

## Beats

1. In the sealed penthouse laboratory of Maledict's fortified glass-and-steel skyscraper, the paired circles flash. Pickle wakes in Maledict's body, drops to all fours, steals the butler's shoe, and chases a hovering security drone. Maledict wakes in the real Pickle's rough brown body with its natural white-fur forepaw. Establish the fourteen-hour collar trigger, the butler's instructions, and Maledict's contempt for pet trivia in the opening page.
2. A trusted automated pet shuttle returns “Pickle” to Brightguard's townhouse exactly as surveillance says it should. The door recognizes Pickle. Brightguard, older and silver-templed in a contemporary rescue-tech suit, greets him with an established ritual Maledict does wrong. Her first “You never do that” lands before he is fully inside.
3. Food and toy mismatches compound. He refuses Pickle's adored liver bite, devours a cucumber Pickle normally spits out, fails the name of a favorite red ball, and voluntarily enters the washroom although Pickle hates baths. Brightguard begins checking his eyes, paws, tracker, and health app. Maledict must use motor-memory leaks and increasingly desperate imitation to avoid a vet scan that might discover the swap.
4. Cutaways show the butler coping with dog-minded Pickle in Maledict's powerful human body: preventing him from eating unsafe food, faking Maledict's presence on a muted command call, retrieving a forbidden artifact used as a chew toy, and discovering that a belly rub is the only reliable way to keep the ultimate body still.
5. Maledict discovers the luxury already waiting for Pickle: heated bed, orthopedic blanket, cabinet of labeled snacks, brushing, scheduled play, and a household organized around his comfort. Brightguard's corrections become less accusatory and more worried. He resents being known incorrectly and then realizes being known at all feels dangerously good.
6. Brightguard monitors the public search for Maledict while playing the specific game Pickle loves. Maledict initially performs it badly, then motor memory and genuine enjoyment align. At the skyscraper, the butler prepares Pickle-in-Maledict for the return and sends the first countdown.
7. Maledict exploits Pickle's nighttime access to enter Brightguard's bedroom, open the counterseal compartment, and prepare the poison. The collar plate begins the fourteen-hour pulse. Brightguard wakes, notes that Pickle never sleeps upstairs, and invites him beside her because his strange day suggests he needs reassurance. Maledict abandons the crime and deliberately misses the return.
8. Compress several repeated windows. Brightguard adapts to “new” Pickle while still occasionally naming old habits. The butler adapts the skyscraper for dog-minded Maledict's body and conceals the truth from the organization. End with parallel luxurious dog beds, the public search unresolved, and another return signal ignored.

## Continuity

- Canon used: The non-canon episode remains a local modern-superhero story within the persistent deep-time world. The body swap is a story-specific paired mechanism, not a universal rule and not linked to established transformation, identity, animal-affinity, concealment, or magical-girl systems.
- Boundaries and unknowns: Both Maledict and Pickle remain continuously alive, distinct, conscious, and morally separate. Motor memory does not alter identity or force choices. The butler is deliberately unnamed and is the only accomplice who knows Maledict's location; the public, heroes, and villain organization do not. Brightguard never discovers the swap. The story establishes no death, erasure, permanent possession, canon faction, named additional place, or inevitable duration beyond Maledict's repeated refusal to activate his half of the safe reversal.
