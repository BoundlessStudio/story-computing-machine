# Provisional direction: Break My Bones

## Promised experience

The request explicitly supplies an alarmed observer's “Dude, you must have broke like ALL your bones?”, the reply “Oh, I break my bones all the time,” and the speaker resetting them correctly without flinching. I infer a pleasure in the startling mismatch between ordinary alarm and extraordinary bodily familiarity, followed by discovering what makes this so routine. The casual diction suggests room for humor; neither comedy nor a particular explanation is mandatory. Genre, scale, viewpoint, the observer's relationship to the speaker, and the ending are unspecified.

## Chosen story, open to change

A first-person comic fantasy adventure, approximately 2,500–3,000 words. The narrator is a bounty hunter who has finally located a wanted sorcerer whose signature defense is breaking the bones of anyone who approaches him. The narrator can deliberately restore their own broken skeleton without pain; this is an individual living ability, not a universal rule. Both people are exactly as powerful as advertised. The contest is over what that power lets them do, and the narrator's practiced composure remains the story's center.

The observer is the sorcerer's young adult lookout, Tavi, an opportunist who thought he had signed on to watch a door and wants out. Their temporary alliance supplies an ordinary person's evolving astonishment, rather than making Tavi's trust or approval the narrator's goal. The narrator wants the bounty and is also unwilling to leave a witness locked in with the man who has just demonstrated why nobody quits. The sorcerer, provisionally Vard, wants to escape with his money and retain his reputation as someone nobody can lay a hand on. Names remain subject to the collection check.

The narrator's power repairs the bones they still have; it does not teleport them, create strength, heal anyone else, or automatically put the body upright. A badly broken body remains awkward until actively set. Vard can break a skeleton he sees by closing his hand, but cannot move its owner or damage already-broken bones a second time. His magic comes from him. These are story choices, not prompt requirements. Injuries stay fantastic, non-graphic, and non-instructional.

## Concrete movement

1. The narrator enters Vard's abandoned hillside casino to collect him. Vard makes a casual fist. The narrator folds and falls through a decorative paper screen onto the lower card floor, in plain sight of Tavi. Tavi gives the prompt's exact question. The narrator replies while setting the wrong angles right, entirely without flinching. Vard has already turned away, assuming the problem is finished. The narrator is irritated about a crushed sandwich, not frightened of another fracture.
2. Tavi shows the barred service exit and reveals that Vard intends to depart by the upper cable carriage, leaving his lookout locked inside. The narrator chooses to get them both out while collecting the prisoner. Vard's defense makes ordinary approach impossible: each time the narrator stands within sight, he breaks them again. The first attempted rush therefore fails despite painless recovery. Tavi's earlier warning proves useful; the narrator's body is recoverable, but the departure time is not.
3. Rather than keep rebuilding into the same target, the narrator uses the awkward interval deliberately. They descend a sloping gaming table while still folded, pass beneath the overturned furniture Vard uses as cover, and work one section of their body at a time. The action remains plainly fantastical, without anatomical procedure. Tavi opens the barred public exit with a key the narrator has already taken during the first approach; this gives him a meaningful exit rather than requiring him to solve the capture.
4. Vard retreats onto the open cable-car platform. He breaks the narrator once more and steps over the apparently helpless body. The narrator has left an arm folded around the platform rail and Vard's ankle; its deliberate reset closes that loop and trips him before he reaches the carriage. The narrator uses the moment of the fall to pull the prisoner hood brought for the job over Vard's head, then closes the ordinary restraints. His spell needs sight of its target. Breaking an arm again cannot release the mechanical cuff. The decisive move belongs to the person who understands being broken as a temporary bodily state, not defeat.
5. On the downward ride, Tavi keeps expecting the narrator's composure to crack. The narrator resets one last overlooked finger while checking the bounty description. Vard is uninjured and furious, the promised reward is now tangible, and Tavi begins negotiating a cut for being an exceptionally good witness. End on the narrator's cheerful insistence that the broken bones are the easy part, as Tavi produces the surviving half of the sandwich he thought they were mourning. Keep the final joke concrete; no explanatory moral or recovery sermon.

## Questions for the outline discussion

Does the repeated contest extend the prompt's casual bodily impossibility, or does the sorcerer's rule turn it into a mechanical puzzle? The principal drafting risk is letting the capture choreography demand a medical diagram. Preserve the narrator's physical agency and Tavi's alarm while simplifying any movement that cannot be understood in a sentence. The first line of the prompt and its unflinching self-reset must be enacted, not merely reported.
