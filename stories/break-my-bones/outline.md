# Provisional direction: Break My Bones

## Promised experience

The request explicitly supplies an alarmed observer's “Dude, you must have broke like ALL your bones?”, the reply “Oh, I break my bones all the time,” and the speaker resetting them correctly without flinching. I infer a pleasure in the startling mismatch between ordinary alarm and extraordinary bodily familiarity, followed by discovering what makes this so routine. The casual diction suggests room for humor; neither comedy nor a particular explanation is mandatory. Genre, scale, viewpoint, the observer's relationship to the speaker, and the ending are unspecified.

## Chosen story, open to change

A first-person comic fantasy adventure, approximately 2,500–3,000 words. The narrator is a bounty hunter who has finally located a wanted sorcerer whose signature defense is breaking the bones of anyone who approaches him. The narrator can deliberately restore their own broken skeleton without pain; this is an individual living ability, not a universal rule. Both people are exactly as powerful as advertised. The contest is over what that power lets them do, and the narrator's practiced composure remains the story's center.

The observer is the sorcerer's young adult lookout, Tavi, hired that morning to watch the door. This is his first sight of Vard's violence. He wants to quit and has the sense to stay out of the sorcerer's sight while doing so. Their temporary alliance supplies an ordinary person's evolving astonishment, rather than making Tavi's trust or approval the narrator's goal. The narrator wants the bounty. Vard wants to escape with his money and retain his reputation as someone nobody can lay a hand on. Tavi and Vard have no exact match in the checked name memory, universe entries, or current story prose.

The narrator can consciously draw their broken bones into their correct positions and fuse them there. Setting a limb moves it, with ordinary muscular force; it grants no extra strength, distance, or speed. A full-body reset takes roughly ten seconds; a single limb can be restored in a beat, but the narrator initially works in their familiar full-body order. Vard can break a skeleton he sees by closing his hand, but cannot push or pull its owner. Broken bones do not cancel existing momentum. Both abilities arise from their living users. No additional rule about already-broken bones is needed. These are story choices, not prompt requirements. Injuries stay fantastic, non-graphic, and non-instructional.

## Concrete movement

1. The narrator finds Vard packing cash in the raised private box of an abandoned hillside casino. Vard makes a casual fist. The narrator folds and tumbles down the short steps onto the gaming floor, in plain sight of Tavi. Tavi gives the prompt's exact question. The narrator replies while setting the wrong angles right, entirely without flinching. Vard has already turned away, assuming the problem finished. The narrator is irritated about a crushed sandwich. A brief lived-history detail makes this habitual outside today's duel: the narrator's coat is reinforced at the knees because landing is quicker than taking stairs, and this is already its third lining.
2. The gaming floor, the rear doorway, and the cable landing are on one level; the box is only a few steps higher. Vard can reach his arriving downhill carriage by crossing the gaming floor and passing through that one rear doorway. On the narrator's first recovered rush, he breaks them again. Their slide across the polished floor visibly continues after the spell, but stops well short. Vard watches the whole ten-second rebuilding while packing the last money. Both characters learn something: he now budgets that recovery interval, and the narrator sees that being broken does not stop a running start. A departure bell creates a deadline; the next carriage would leave him with an opponent he cannot permanently put down.
3. The narrator tells Tavi to stay behind the card counter, outside Vard's sight. There is no locked-exit or stolen-key puzzle. Tavi's choice is to wait safely, then take the offered witness fee and downhill ride after the capture. The narrator approaches along the far side of the gaming tables, regaining speed before coming into view. They deliberately aim to slide sideways across the narrow boarding doorway, ahead of Vard. He breaks them; their momentum carries the folded body into position. The hood and cuffs brought for the bounty remain secured on their belt and are established early.
4. Vard has seen full recovery take ten seconds. The departing carriage, his heavy cash case, and the body across the sole boarding route force a real choice: wait for another recovery or step over now. He steps, expecting time to spare. The narrator sets only one leg, which straightens across his next footfall as an ordinary rigid obstacle. He trips through the doorway onto the landing; no loop around a rail, lifting force, or long-distance reach is involved. With his back to the narrator, they reset their hands and arm first, pull the prepared hood over his eyes, and close the cuffs before bothering to stand. His mistake is assuming every reset must begin and end in the same order. The hood then prevents another sight-based spell. The breaking, selective reset, and capture are one continuous reversal.
5. Tavi comes from behind the counter and joins the downward ride with the captured sorcerer. The narrator completes the deferred resets, then checks the bounty description; Vard is furious, and the reward is now tangible. Tavi negotiates a cut for being an exceptionally good witness and produces the surviving half of the sandwich. End with a concrete comic response from the narrator rather than an explanation of the story's lesson.

## Questions for the outline discussion

The revised climax depends on three visible facts: momentum persists after a break, full rebuilding takes time, and a selected limb can be set first. The prose must make the first two obvious through failed action, then reveal the third through the successful action. Its risk is excessive explanation; the intended immediate image is simply a crooked leg becoming a straight obstacle just as Vard steps over it. Tavi's alarm and the narrator's bodily familiarity should remain stronger than the machinery of the contest.
