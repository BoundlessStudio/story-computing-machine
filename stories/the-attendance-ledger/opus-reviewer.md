# Opus review: The Attendance Ledger

Independent audit for a later editing pass. It is not a `review.md` verdict,
does not change canon state, and does not authorize edits by itself.

## Verdict

- Dialogue: REVISE — The classroom dialogue works. The relay scenes break the anonymity boundary twice: Night Clerk uses school facts nobody gave him, and he speaks the crisis transmission aloud in the girls' room. Those scenes also close on thesis lines and a protocol punchline.
- Prose: REVISE — The prose is concrete and well paced. The ending restates the theme four times, and narration keeps commenting on what the action has already shown.
- Continuity and prompt: REVISE — The climax is placed in the wrong room (L420). The two relay beats also undercut the prompt's "girls remain unaware" requirement. Length is about 3,590 words, inside the 3,200–3,800 band.
- Edit scope: targeted-dialogue
- Existing review.md said: Verdict PASS (pass 5, 0 findings), no Dialogue line recorded; disagree. Pass 1 flagged the relay/visible-action risk and Pass 2 called it resolved, but the crisis transmission is still spoken within the girls' hearing. No pass audited dialogue.

The classroom scenes are the story's strength. Roll call, the ledger drop and the evacuation orders are quick and specific. Mr. Tolland's deadpan quips also sound nothing like Night Clerk's procedural menus of options, and that is the best voice decision in the piece. Every material failure happens on the relay. There the secret the prompt depends on leaks: Night Clerk talks about a showcase and a building map the girls never gave him (D1), and at the crisis he speaks his transmission out loud a few metres from them (D2). The relay conversations then end on clauses and thesis lines ("you are not in charge of our bodies", "Especially then.") that state the consent theme instead of letting the blackout show it. Apart from Mothlight's jokes, the girls fall into the handler's clipped, formal register, so the trio never sound like friends. The edit should make the secret credible through knowledge and staging, cut the thesis and punchline lines, and keep the protocol spine that the CANON entries record. Each girl should also get one unguarded line in her own voice.

## Dialogue findings

### D1 · Blocking · Night Clerk uses the showcase and building map before anyone gives them to him

- Where: L127–L149 and L170–L194, lunch relay in the equipment room
- Text:
> three rough status lights, no names, no map points.
> Gideon let her account stand without adding anything she had not told him.
> "Agreed. My recommendation is evacuation and cancellation.
> or a contained trap using the building map. You decide whether you engage at all."
> Kiteglass said. "And we built that presentation."
> Pulsewire requested the building's camera positions and traced two exits on the map Gideon sent
> "I'll give you camera coverage and evacuation timing.
- Problem: Ground/knowledge. The scene first establishes that the channel carries "no names, no map points". It then promises that Gideon adds nothing Kiteglass hasn't told him. One beat later, Night Clerk recommends "cancellation" of an event nobody on the relay has mentioned and offers a trap "using the building map" of a school nobody has named. Kiteglass answers with "that presentation", so both sides now share knowledge the relay never carried. Pulsewire, the team's skeptic, then asks him for the building's camera positions without surprise, and he sends a map and promises "camera coverage". A reader primed by the careful anonymity setup will ask at once how Night Clerk has their school's floor plan and camera layout. It also contradicts the CANON note (characters.md, "Gideon Tolland") that his lunch advice used only Kiteglass's account. A smaller slip in the same run: at L141 Pulsewire says "The ledger", a word nobody on the relay has used (Kiteglass said "a book", Night Clerk said "register"). It is the narrator's word in her mouth.
- Fix: Have the girls put the facts on the relay before Night Clerk uses them. Kiteglass or Pulsewire mentions the afternoon showcase before his recommendation, and he recommends in their terms. For the map, Pulsewire sends a photo of the building's posted evacuation plan and the girls mark the cameras they know. Night Clerk works only from that image: evacuation timing, not "camera coverage". Change "the map Gideon sent" (L172) and "camera coverage" (L193) to match. Change Pulsewire's "The ledger" to "the register" or "the names". Keep "Could it see the register?"; "attendance" makes a register an obvious inference. Illustrative only: "I've got a photo of the fire plan by the stairs. Sending it."

### D2 · Blocking · The crisis transmission is spoken aloud in the room where the girls are standing

- Where: L301–L347, auditorium after the copied "Transform now", before the breaker
- Text:
> "Everyone away from the screen," Mr. Tolland said.
> "The wiring is one route," Pulsewire said through the relay.
> Instead he pressed the transmit switch. "Facts once. The network is the exposure path.
> The local power can be killed, giving you darkness but no telemetry or handler contact. Engagement remains your choice."
> Mr. Tolland opened the designated service exit, as the evacuation plan required.
- Problem: Ground/staging. This breaks the prompt's central secret. At L324 Gideon speaks four sentences into the relay while the whole class, the three girls included, is still in the auditorium: he doesn't open the service exit until L347, and the girls stay in that room. The girls hear Night Clerk's scrambled words in their earpieces at the moment their teacher, a few metres away, is visibly speaking the same words aloud. Nothing places him out of their sight or hearing. The old review's Pass 1 flagged this risk, that Night Clerk's actions would visibly match Mr. Tolland's. The fix changed what he says and left the simultaneous speech, so the ending's claim that the girls remain unaware (L552–L553) is no longer credible. Two smaller problems sit in the same exchange. Pulsewire's request and the three "Dark." replies are also spoken in a crowded room, next to classmates who have just seen those aliases on the screen. And the transmission reads as two options (cut the network, or kill local power) when a single breaker does both.
- Fix: Add one staging sentence before he transmits that puts him out of the girls' sight and hearing. The breaker panel is already "near the service door" (L225), so have him at the panel, turned into the doorway, speaking low under the scraping chairs and the crooning speakers. Add a clause covering the girls' own replies (heads down, under the noise). Cut the transmission to one option, its cost and the choice. Keep "Facts once", because the later protocol and the CANON note depend on it, and still have him not say he will pull the breaker himself. Illustrative only: "Facts once. The breaker kills the stream, the cameras and this relay. You'd have darkness and no contact from me. Your call."

### D3 · Major · Protocol check ends in an unprompted thesis rebuke, glossed by the narration

- Where: L176–L194, end of the lunch relay
- Text:
> "And if you order a transformation?"
> It was the first rule they had made together.
> "Good," Pulsewire said. "Because you are useful, but you are not in charge of our bodies."
> The sentence pricked because it was true and because he had lately behaved as though noticing everything entitled him to arrange everything.
> The plan remains yours."
- Problem: The Philosopher, plus a reply with no trigger. Pulsewire's contingency check suits her and plants the climax. But "And if you order a transformation?" asks about a rule she co-wrote, and her reply to his answer states the story's consent theme outright. Nothing Night Clerk has done in this conversation is controlling: he offered options and waited out Mothlight's count. So "you are not in charge of our bodies" answers the author's theme, not anything he said. The narration then explains why the line stung, and Gideon closes on a third statement of the same point, "The plan remains yours." That makes three beats in a row saying one thing.
- Fix: Keep "If the network is compromised?" / "Disconnect." and the transformation rule. Frame the rule as the checklist Pulsewire runs every time, so it doesn't read as a question she needs answered. Cut the second sentence of her L185–L186 reply, cut the gloss "The sentence pricked… arrange everything", and cut "The plan remains yours." Keep "He looked at the school map, at the three status lights, at every variable he wanted to close his hand around." It shows his urge to control without explaining it. If you want an edge between them here, attach it to something Night Clerk actually does on the page, not to the theme.

### D4 · Major · Final relay exchange runs as a protocol clause and a punchline, and re-reports what the reader already knows

- Where: L510–L542, evening relay
- Text:
> "New protocol," Pulsewire said. "When we request darkness, you give facts once and disconnect."
> "Even if I dislike it?"
> "Especially then."
> They offered those facts. Gideon received them. He did not ask for more.
- Problem: A negotiation routine and a Punchline Patch at the final meaningful exchange between the handler and the team. "Even if I dislike it?" exists only to set up "Especially then." Night Clerk has just shown that he accepts their terms, so he has no reason to ask except to hand Pulsewire the last word. The protocol itself only codifies what already happened at L324–L343, so the exchange moves little. Around it, the girls' last words to him are summarized as reports (L526–L531) that repeat what the narration concluded at L465–L471. The trio have just survived the worst day of their partnership, yet they sound like staff filing incident reports. Mothlight's "Not a schedule?" is the only relief, irritation, pride or teasing that reaches the page.
- Fix: Keep the protocol itself. It is recorded as CANON (rules.md "Northbridge trio transformation and support practice"): Pulsewire states it and Night Clerk accepts ("Agreed." can stay). Cut "Even if I dislike it?" and "Especially then." Turn one of the three summaries into direct speech with some feeling in it. Kiteglass's wrist explanation is the best candidate, since she has guarded against looking fragile all day. Cut L533. Keep "Not a schedule?" and the recovery plan turned face down, and if the question reads as a setup for the gesture, make it plainly a tease. Illustrative only: "New protocol. We ask for dark, you give us the facts once and hang up."
