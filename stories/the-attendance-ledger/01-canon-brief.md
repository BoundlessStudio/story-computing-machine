# Canon brief

## Hard constraints

- **Canon authority:** Only `LOCKED` and `CANON` universe entries can establish
  facts for this story. `stories/INDEX.md` currently contains no row with canon
  `yes`, so no finalized story contributes binding lore. Candidate, abandoned,
  in-progress, archive record, or proposed material must not be imported as canon.
  (`universe/README.md` — `## Authority order`; `stories/INDEX.md` — story
  table)
- **One deep-time world:** If the story is placed in the modern-like era, that
  era is part of the same physical world as Ravel and the later Glass-Sea era,
  but it is not necessarily literal real-world Earth. The story must not turn
  those era labels into separate planets or planes. (`universe/premise.md` —
  `## The deep-time shared world`; `universe/locations.md` — `## The world
  across eras`)
- **Modern-like era boundary:** The only established contemporary-seeming era
  is a low-magic trough in which ordinary life treats magic as myth or legend,
  although isolated relic anomalies can survive. If the school is located
  there, magical-girl activity must remain sufficiently scarce, concealed, or
  locally misunderstood to preserve that baseline. (`universe/timeline.md` —
  `## Established era order`; `universe/glossary.md` — `## Modern-like era`)
- **Local rather than universal magic:** “Magic,” “technology,” and “divinity”
  are cultural descriptions of multiple real underlying systems; they are not
  objective universal categories and do not share one origin. Any magical-girl
  mechanism introduced here must be presented as a particular local system,
  not the explanation for every anomaly in the setting. (`universe/rules.md` —
  `## System categories`; `universe/glossary.md` — `## Magic, technology, and
  divinity`)
- **Living magical agency:** Only life can use magic. A nonliving wand,
  transformation token, phone, headset, monitoring platform, weapon, or school
  record may store, focus, transmit, or shape magic supplied through living
  agency, but it cannot itself be a magic user unless the story establishes it
  as alive. (`universe/rules.md` — `## Living agency and magic`)
- **System-specific costs and consent:** Cost, desire, sacrifice, and consent
  have no universal metaphysical behavior. The story may define local costs
  and consent rules, but it must apply them consistently and take their ethical
  consequences seriously. (`universe/rules.md` — `## Costs, desire, and local
  mechanisms`)
- **No automatic true-name rule:** Names are normally socially and symbolically
  important, not innately magical. Civilian names, magical aliases, attendance
  records, or spoken names cannot automatically expose, bind, or empower a
  person unless this story clearly defines a narrow local mechanism that does
  so. (`universe/rules.md` — `## Names and identity binding`)
- **No automatic truth in records:** An attendance ledger, administrative
  database, altered file, battle log, or contradictory record is not inherently
  truthful or magical. Any unusual evidentiary or self-altering behavior needs
  an explicit, causally coherent local mechanism. (`universe/rules.md` —
  `## Records, falsehood, and historical traces`)
- **No universal hidden authority:** Canon establishes no global organization
  that regulates magic across every era. Any handler service, school compact,
  safeguarding network, magical-girl lineage, or enemy organization must be
  local to this story unless later canon explicitly connects it elsewhere.
  (`universe/premise.md` — `## Knowledge and connection`;
  `universe/factions.md` — `# Factions and cultures`)
- **Personhood, agency, and content ceiling:** An explanation for
  transformations or magical identity must not erase a girl's personhood,
  relationships, consent, or moral choices. Minors may never be sexualized, and
  violence or injury must remain non-graphic and consequence-focused.
  (`universe/style-guide.md` — `## Personhood and moral consequence`;
  `universe/style-guide.md` — `## Audience and content boundaries`)

## Useful established context

- The shared universe permits an urban-fantasy school story at intimate scale;
  stories do not need to advertise the larger cosmology, and characters may
  understand only their local anomaly. This supports a teacher whose practical
  care matters more than a cosmic explanation. (`universe/premise.md` —
  `## Memory, myth, and recoverable truth`; `universe/premise.md` —
  `## Knowledge and connection`)
- The prompt's secret identities fit naturally with the modern-like era's
  ordinary disbelief in magic, provided the story does not suggest that public,
  routine magical combat is common knowledge. Surviving local anomalies are
  compatible with the low-magic trough. (`universe/timeline.md` —
  `## Established era order`)
- Different magical systems may interact with or imitate technology. This
  leaves room for a teacher to use ordinary school procedures and
  system-specific magical communications together, while preserving the
  living-agency rule. (`universe/rules.md` — `## System categories`;
  `universe/rules.md` — `## Living agency and magic`)
- The setting's broader theme of partial knowledge can enrich the double-life
  structure: the girls, teacher, school, and public may each possess different
  but causally consistent fragments of the truth. Partial knowledge must not be
  used as a shortcut around point-of-view or identity logic.
  (`universe/premise.md` — `## Memory, myth, and recoverable truth`;
  `universe/style-guide.md` — `## Continuity and evidence discipline`)
- Canon does not require a crossover, recurring faction, portal, deity,
  apocalyptic scale, or shared artifact. A self-contained local entry point is
  fully compatible with the universe. (`universe/style-guide.md` —
  `## Tonal range, scale, and connection`; `universe/rules.md` —
  `## Artifacts, motifs, and folklore`)

## Conflicts or ambiguity

- **No direct canon contradiction found:** The prompt can proceed under the
  established framework. There is no authoritative magical-girl, handler,
  school, or secret-identity entry with which it must reconcile.
  (`universe/characters.md` — `# Recurring characters`;
  `universe/factions.md` — `# Factions and cultures`; `stories/INDEX.md` —
  story table)
- **Era ambiguity:** “Urban fantasy” and the school setting suggest, but do not
  canonically require, the modern-like era. Choosing that era invokes its
  low-magic/public-disbelief constraints; choosing an unnamed urban era avoids
  asserting that this is the Nell-and-Bash period but must not invent a new
  global chronology. The plan should state which narrow option it uses.
  (`universe/glossary.md` — `## Modern-like era`; `universe/timeline.md` —
  `## Universal chronology`; `universe/timeline.md` — `## Established era
  order`)
- **Production-memory overlap, not canon:** `Solstice Evening Bell` is a
  non-canon candidate identity with the registered titles `First Monarch` and
  `queen of all magical girls`. The present prompt's use of magical girls does
  not establish a crossover, shared system, monarchy, or historical
  relationship. Shared genre language or imagery is insufficient evidence of
  connection. (`stories/NAMES.md` — `# Character name registry`, row `Solstice Evening
  Bell`; `stories/INDEX.md` — row `solstice-evening-bell`;
  `universe/style-guide.md` — `## Continuity and evidence discipline`)
- **Handler meaning:** Canon defines no handler role. The prompt contract's
  support/logistics/safeguarding interpretation is therefore a story
  requirement, not an established universe office. Do not generalize it into a
  universal chain of command. (`stories/the-attendance-ledger/00-prompt.md` —
  `## Assumptions`; `universe/premise.md` — `## Knowledge and connection`)

## Unknowns

- **Unknown:** The story's exact era, city, country, school, calendar,
  technology level, school policies, and relationship to literal Earth.
- **Unknown:** Whether magical girls have existed before this group, how they
  are selected, how transformations work, whether powers have costs, and what
  ethical or consent rules govern participation.
- **Unknown:** The girls' number, ages, civilian identities, magical aliases,
  abilities, injuries, families or guardians, and prior combat experience.
- **Unknown:** The threat's nature, origin, objectives, awareness of civilian
  identities, and relationship—if any—to the girls' power system.
- **Unknown:** How the teacher became a handler, who (if anyone) authorized the
  role, how the teacher identified the girls, and why the girls do not know the
  teacher has identified them.
- **Unknown:** Whether the handler operates alone or through a local
  organization; who can access battle intelligence; and what safeguarding,
  medical, communications, or emergency protocols exist.
- **Unknown:** Whether secret identities are protected by ordinary discretion,
  a glamour, information security, institutional concealment, or some
  combination. Canon supplies no universal masquerade or memory-erasure rule.
- **Unknown:** The public visibility of magical incidents and how ordinary
  witnesses, cameras, school records, emergency responders, and families
  interpret them.
- **Unknown:** Whether transformation changes memory, body, apparent age,
  personality, or continuity of self. None of those effects should be assumed
  from genre convention.
- **Unknown:** Any larger connection to existing eras, artifacts, deities,
  factions, characters, or candidate stories. Absence of evidence is not
  evidence of such a link.

## Safe invention space

- Invent one local school, its staff structure, timetable, attendance and
  welfare procedures, extracurricular pressures, and a nearby urban district.
  Keep the geography story-scale and avoid claiming it represents the whole
  modern-like era.
- Invent a small, self-contained magical-girl team and one local threat. Define
  only the transformation, powers, limits, recovery needs, and combat
  consequences necessary for this plot; do not state that these rules govern
  all magic.
- Invent a local handler practice centered on logistics, communications,
  first aid, emotional support, academic accommodations, identity protection,
  and informed choice. It may be informal or locally institutional, but should
  not become a universal magical authority.
- Invent a narrow secrecy method: mundane misdirection, careful scheduling,
  locally bounded glamour, restricted communications, or a combination. If it
  alters perception, memory, names, or records, define its scope and limits and
  preserve causal consequences.
- Invent nonliving support tools that channel or organize magic supplied by
  living users, plus ordinary technology that performs ordinary monitoring and
  communications. Alternatively, establish a person-like tool as alive and
  treat its personhood seriously; this is a larger reusable addition and should
  be used only if the plot needs it.
- Invent a local reason the teacher knows the girls' identities while the girls
  remain unaware of that knowledge, provided discovery follows concrete
  evidence and does not rely on universal true-name power or implausible
  omniscience.
- Invent magical and civilian aliases with an explicit one-identity mapping.
  Aliases may protect identity socially or through a defined local system, but
  must not imply that names are universally magical.
- Record every reusable addition—especially a recurring magical-girl system,
  handler organization, transformation rule, secrecy effect, living artifact,
  or named location—in `06-canon-delta.md`; keeping inventions local reduces
  promotion risk.

## Name constraints

- The prompt supplies no character-facing name. `The Attendance Ledger` is a
  work title, not a registered character identity. If `Ledger` or any similar
  term becomes a handler codename, magical alias, mascot, construct, or
  person-like system name, it must be checked and registered.
- Every name and alias in `stories/NAMES.md` remains reserved across canon,
  candidates, abandoned stories, and released reservations. Choose unique,
  readily distinguishable civilian names, magical identities, nicknames,
  usernames, honorific-plus-surname forms, villains, mascots, named weapons or
  constructs, and person-like entities. A magical and civilian alias belonging
  to one girl should be documented as one continuing identity.
- Especially relevant exact reservations are `Solstice Evening Bell`,
  `Solstice`, `Evening Bell`, `First Monarch`, and `queen of all magical
  girls`. Do not reuse them or imply that this team belongs to that candidate
  character's system without a deliberate, documented crossover rationale.
- Other potentially genre-adjacent reserved aliases include `Green Coat`,
  `Red Scarf`, `Tea Girl`, `Queen Aurelia`, `the Goddess`, `Goddess of the
  Sun`, `Sun Queen`, `Burning Queen`, `the Goddess of Time`, `Time`,
  `Mother`, `Daughter`, `Captain`, and `Saint`. Avoid exact reuse for magical
  identities, team roles, or person-like entities.
- School-staff forms already reserved include `Instructor Bexel`, `Bexel`,
  `Mrs. Halpern`, `Halpern`, `Dr. Kan`, and `Doctor Kan`. A title such as
  “teacher,” “principal,” “nurse,” or “coach” remains an ordinary role noun
  unless prose uses it as a character's proper name; any title used as a name
  must be registered.
- Avoid the registry's unresolved collisions entirely: `Lena` (Lena Mercer /
  Lena Venn), `Mara` (Mara from Hinge / Mara Osset), `Nisha` (archive record S02 /
  S04), `Pell` (Orrin Pell / Pell), and `Voss` (M. Voss / Rhea Voss).
  Repeating one would compound an existing unresolved defect.
- Check close forms, not only exact matches. Relevant crowded clusters include
  `Bell`/`Evening Bell`/the Bellweather names; `Leth`/`Letha`; `Ilya`/`Irix`/
  `Ivo`; `Rana`/`Rhea`/`Mara`/`Yara`; `Seris`/`Siva`/`Sorel`/`Solstice`/
  `Sula`; and `Tair`/`Talvi`/`Tamsin`/`Tavra`/`Tess`/`Tilda`. Distinctiveness
  should be clear in spelling, sound, role, and context.
- Any deliberate reuse requires the plan and registry to state whether the
  identities are the same or distinct, why the reuse matters, and how readers
  can distinguish them. No current prompt element requires reuse.
  (`stories/NAMES.md` — `## Rules`, registry table under
  `# Character name registry`, and `## Known unresolved
  released-reservation collisions`; `universe/style-guide.md` — `## Prose defaults`)

## Required checks after drafting

- Verify that the chosen era is explicit enough for continuity and that a
  modern-like placement preserves the low-magic/public-disbelief baseline.
- Verify every magical act has living agency. Flag any autonomous nonliving
  wand, device, platform, ledger, recording system, weapon, or enemy construct
  that appears to use magic without being established as alive.
- Verify the story defines its local transformation, power, cost, recovery, and
  consent rules only as far as needed and applies them consistently.
- Verify that any identity glamour, memory effect, name-binding, altered
  record, or truth-bearing ledger has a bounded mechanism and does not become a
  universal rule by implication.
- Trace the teacher's evidence and decisions: how the identities were learned,
  what the teacher knows in each scene, how interventions avoid revealing that
  knowledge, and why the girls plausibly remain unaware through the ending.
- Trace each girl's agency. The teacher may safeguard, coordinate, and support,
  but must not reduce the girls to weapons, coerce their participation, replace
  their battle decisions, or erase meaningful choices affecting their lives.
- Trace information security across classroom and battle: attendance,
  injuries, absences, communications, witnesses, cameras, parents or guardians,
  colleagues, administrators, emergency responders, and the antagonist should
  reveal only what the plot causally permits.
- Check that public magical visibility remains compatible with the chosen era
  and that the immediate identity threat is contained without inventing an
  unexplained global masquerade.
- Check minors' treatment against the non-sexualization and non-graphic
  violence ceiling; keep adult/student boundaries unambiguous.
- Search every character-facing form against `stories/NAMES.md`, including
  civilian and magical identities, nicknames, surnames used alone, titles used
  as names, usernames, named animals, companions, constructs, and person-like
  systems. Map aliases to identities, document any intentional reuse, update
  the registry through the primary coordinator, and run the story-scoped name
  validation.
- Check that imagery, titles, or genre language do not imply an unsupported
  connection to `Solstice Evening Bell`, another candidate, a archive record,
  the Glass Gate, Ravel, or any deity.
- Ensure `06-canon-delta.md` captures every reusable new fact and labels
  story-only details appropriately; none becomes authoritative until explicit
  promotion.
