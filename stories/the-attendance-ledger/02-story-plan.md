# Story plan

## Premise and promise

**Premise:** When a living mimic follows three exhausted magical girls into
their school and uses an ordinary attendance ledger to narrow down their
civilian identities, their teacher—who is also their anonymous handler—must
protect a classroom, disable the mimic's planned livestream, and trust the
girls to finish the fight without ever revealing that he knows who they are.

**Story promise:** A tender, sly school-day thriller in which mundane care
(deadlines, snacks, attendance, evacuation practice, and listening) matters as
much as magic, the teacher's two duties collide in one building, and the girls'
own choices—not an adult rescue—defeat the threat.

**Central dramatic question:** Can Gideon safeguard both the girls and their
chosen boundary of anonymity when doing so requires him to surrender the
monitoring and control that make him feel useful?

**Meaningful change:** Gideon begins by treating good care as the ability to
notice everything and quietly arrange the right outcome. He ends by giving the
girls complete information, honoring their requested blackout, and trusting
them to decide and act beyond his sight.

## Story controls

- **Era:** The modern-like low-magic trough. Northbridge Secondary has ordinary
  phones, cameras, a school network, livestreamed student presentations, fire
  doors, and paper backup attendance. Magic is a rare local anomaly; the
  public does not know magical girls exist. Nothing establishes literal
  present-day Earth or a connection to another named era.
- **POV and tense:** Close third person through Gideon Tolland, past tense.
  Battle information is limited to what he can observe or what the girls
  voluntarily share over the relay; after he cuts that relay, the narration
  must preserve his uncertainty.
- **Tone and genre:** Tender, sly, and suspenseful magical-girl urban fantasy,
  with dry humor drawn from school logistics rather than mockery of the girls.
- **Length:** 3,500-word target; acceptable range 3,200–3,800 words.
- **Shape:** Six scenes over one school day, beginning during morning
  attendance and ending with the next morning's quieter roll call.
- **Audience and boundaries:** Teen / PG-13. Non-graphic bruising, fatigue,
  anxiety, a minor adult injury, threatened exposure, and school evacuation.
  No sexualization, romance, graphic injury, punitive disclosure to guardians,
  or framing the students as weapons.
- **Magic scope:** Define only this trio's voluntary transformations, three
  complementary abilities, their immediate recovery costs, their bounded
  identity veil, and the Rollcaller's local mimicry. Do not explain magic in
  general.
- **Handler boundary:** “Handler” is the team's own joking label for Gideon's
  anonymous support role, not a rank or command position. He supplies
  information, logistics, first aid, recovery checks, and options. The girls
  decide whether to transform, fight, retreat, or disconnect.

## Character engine

### Protagonist

- **Gideon Tolland / Night Clerk:** An attentive secondary-school teacher and
  volunteer emergency-radio operator who answered the trio's anonymous request
  for practical help six months earlier. All four agreed to keep civilian
  identities off the encrypted relay; ordinary voice-scrambling hides his
  voice. Gideon subsequently inferred the girls' identities through cumulative
  mundane evidence: he saw the transformed team vanish into an alley moments
  before Drita, Bryn, and Cassia emerged; later, their absences and specific
  injuries repeatedly matched the team's reports; and their speech habits and
  hand signals completed the pattern. No magical name knowledge, private-file
  search, or single implausible clue is involved.
- **Concrete desire:** Get every student through the afternoon community
  showcase safely while keeping Drita, Bryn, and Cassia academically intact,
  physically functional, and unexposed.
- **Pressure:** The Rollcaller has entered the school, a guardian-facing
  livestream begins in hours, the girls are scheduled to present, and removing
  all three from class would itself strengthen the identity pattern.
- **External stakes:** If the Rollcaller records or publicly stages a
  transformation, it can expose three minors, endanger their families and
  classmates, and feed on the resulting crowd recognition. If Gideon neglects
  his classroom to handle the battle, other students remain in danger.
- **Internal stakes:** Calling the girls by civilian name on the relay, ordering
  them away from the fight, or revealing that he has tracked them would violate
  the anonymity they made a condition of accepting adult help.
- **Blind spot:** Gideon equates safeguarding with privately noticing,
  anticipating, and controlling every variable. His covert accommodations are
  kind but paternalistic at the edge: because he knows more than the girls know
  he knows, he can confuse good outcomes with informed consent.
- **Movement:** From “I keep them safe by staying connected and arranging
  around them” to “I keep faith with them by stating the danger, offering
  support, and letting them choose even when I cannot monitor the result.”

### Key cast and agency

- **Drita Ademi / Kiteglass (fifteen):** The trio's tactical planner. Her
  transformed power makes angled, translucent barriers and redirects force;
  sustained use worsens a bruised wrist and drains body heat. She wants to
  complete the showcase instead of being treated as fragile, and she proposes
  drawing the Rollcaller away from the audience. In the climax she chooses
  protection over pursuit, holding the evacuation route rather than chasing a
  quick victory.
- **Bryn Ahn / Pulsewire (sixteen):** A skeptical systems thinker who senses
  and severs active magical connections at close range. Each severance causes
  tremor, headache, and brief sensory overload. She identifies that the
  Rollcaller is riding both the school wiring and the team's relay, requests a
  complete communications blackout, and makes the decisive cut only after her
  teammates consent to losing their shared channel.
- **Cassia Dominguez / Mothlight (fifteen):** A quick-witted illusionist whose
  wearer-powered light can produce moving silhouettes and displaced voice
  echoes. Numerous decoys consume glucose and concentration. She is anxious
  after the previous night's fight but refuses to have that anxiety mistaken
  for incapacity. She asks for a grounding pause, then designs the decoy field
  that exposes the Rollcaller's living core.
- **The Rollcaller:** A living, sheet-thin local anomaly with ink-dark
  filaments. It imitates voices and feeds on the attention surge created when a
  crowd recognizes a concealed identity. It cannot learn truth from names or
  records: it must observe, infer, coax reactions, and use ordinary cameras and
  networking to spread evidence. It extends its own living tissue into speakers
  and cables, so all magical action remains its living agency. It opposes
  Gideon by turning his tools of care—attendance, patterns, and communication—
  into surveillance.

### Local support and secrecy rules

- Each girl voluntarily transforms through a personal, nonliving focus token
  powered by her own living agency. A girl can stop or refuse a transformation;
  neither teammate nor handler can compel it.
- Transformation provides a bounded refraction veil: while the girl sustains
  it, unaided faces and voices blur in live sight and ordinary recordings. It
  does not erase memory, alter records, conceal the visible transition, or
  defeat deliberate correlation between a civilian and an alias.
- Powers consume calories, body heat, and concentration. The practical
  protocol is water, food, warmth, injury checks, rest, and the girl's own
  report—not a game-like numerical meter.
- The encrypted handler relay and its voice scrambler are ordinary technology.
  Health and location updates are opt-in. The Rollcaller can physically bridge
  the relay only by placing living filament on connected school equipment.
- The team established a consent protocol: Night Clerk may state risks and
  recommend retreat, but never orders transformation. Any such order means the
  channel is compromised. This rule lets the girls detect the mimic rather
  than requiring Gideon to save them.

## Causal arc

1. **Beginning state:** After a difficult night battle, Gideon quietly supports
   the same three girls in morning class through universal policies: a
   classwide revision token, rotating lab roles, snacks available to anyone,
   and a brief grounding routine. As Night Clerk he has already recommended a
   recovery day, but the girls chose to attend and give their shared afternoon
   presentation. He respects the choice while privately tracking every tremor.
2. **Inciting disruption:** During spoken attendance, a voice in the
   ventilation grate whispers “Kiteglass?” in the cadence heard during the
   previous battle. Drita nearly reacts. Gideon drops his papers, turns the
   interruption into dry classroom business, and switches everyone to silent
   check-in. A strip of living filament withdraws with a view of the ledger.
   The Rollcaller survived and followed the team to school.
3. **Escalating turn one:** On the anonymous lunch relay, Gideon gives the team
   the facts and offers evacuation, cancellation, or a school-map-assisted
   trap. The girls reject being removed without consultation: three sudden
   absences would advertise a pattern and cost their project. Drita proposes
   using the showcase's empty rehearsal corridor to draw the creature away;
   Bryn adds a hard abort signal; Cassia explicitly chooses to participate
   after requesting a grounding break. Gideon provides routes and camera
   locations, then accepts their plan.
4. **Escalating turn two:** The Rollcaller uses the names it physically saw in
   the ordinary ledger and Gideon's conspicuous switch to silent attendance to
   narrow its suspects. Before the showcase, the PA mimics an administrator and
   summons Drita, Bryn, and Cassia together. Gideon blocks the false summons
   using normal teacher procedure, but that protection confirms to the predator
   that those three matter. It steals the ledger page, threads itself into the
   auditorium A/V rack, and starts the guardian livestream early.
5. **Crisis:** In front of classmates, the Rollcaller projects the three
   civilian names beside blurred battle images and, in Night Clerk's copied
   voice, orders the girls to transform. They recognize the order as false and
   remain civilian. Gideon can preserve his live telemetry and try to direct
   them, or honor Bryn's request to kill the network and handler relay
   together—leaving him unable to monitor or command the fight. Calling their
   civilian names on the relay would warn them precisely but reveal his covert
   knowledge.
6. **Climactic choice:** Gideon does not use their names. He gives the same
   factual choice Night Clerk always owes them: the network is the exposure
   path; cutting it will also cut him off; he can create ninety seconds of
   darkness and evacuation cover. The girls choose “dark.” Gideon throws the
   local breaker, closes the fire partition, shepherds the class through the
   blind service corridor, and accepts that care now means losing sight of the
   team.
7. **Climax:** Under blackout cover, the girls transform unseen and execute
   their own revised plan. Kiteglass protects the evacuation route rather than
   overextending; Pulsewire severs the Rollcaller's living bridges from the
   cameras, PA, and relay after both teammates consent; Mothlight asks for and
   takes a breath, then fills the rehearsal corridor with false alias-bearing
   silhouettes. The predator commits its filaments to the decoys, revealing
   the one living body. Kiteglass folds a barrier around that body while
   Pulsewire makes the final severance. Gideon's only direct aid is mundane:
   he holds the heavy acoustic partition despite a strained shoulder, keeping
   witnesses out until the girls contain the creature. Their coordinated
   decisions win.
8. **Resonant aftermath:** With cameras and upload disabled before any
   transformation, the incident remains a local electrical failure and
   evacuation scare; witnesses saw names and fuzzy costume images, not a
   confirmed identity link. The girls rejoin the muster in civilian form, so
   Gideon records their real presence without falsifying attendance. On the
   restored anonymous relay, he offers a menu of food, medical checks, deadline
   help, and rest rather than prescribing a recovery plan. The girls choose two
   mission-free nights and add a new protocol: when they request darkness, the
   handler gives facts once and disconnects. The next morning Gideon replaces
   verbal roll call with silent self-check-in for everyone. He still keeps
   their secret, but the ledger's three marks now signify students safely
   returned, not pieces he successfully controlled.

## Setup and payoff

- **The paper attendance ledger:** Set up as a dull backup after the online
  system glitches. The Rollcaller physically reads and steals one page; it
  never becomes magical or inherently truthful. Paid off when Gideon rebuilds
  attendance from the class's silent check-in and records the girls honestly
  after they return.
- **Universal revision token and rotating classroom roles:** Set up as Gideon's
  covert way to give exhausted students room without targeting them. Paid off
  when the girls preserve their grade and agency without a suspicious private
  exemption.
- **Drita's bruised wrist and cold hands:** Set up in class and acknowledged
  only as an ordinary student injury. Paid off when she rejects a flashy chase
  and chooses a stationary protective barrier, demonstrating judgment rather
  than adult-imposed restraint.
- **Bryn's “two routes out” habit:** One of the mundane clues by which Gideon
  previously recognized Pulsewire. Set up again when she maps both the
  rehearsal corridor and service exit. Paid off when her second route becomes
  the class evacuation path and she identifies the relay as a second route for
  the Rollcaller.
- **Cassia's grounding count:** Gideon leads a brief whole-class grounding
  exercise rather than singling her out. Night Clerk later offers the same
  option without implying weakness. Paid off when Cassia asks for one breath
  during the climax and then creates the decisive decoy field.
- **“Night Clerk never orders a transformation”:** State during the lunch
  check-in as an established consent rule. Paid off when all three girls
  recognize the mimic's order and refuse it.
- **Acoustic partition and blind service corridor:** Introduced during
  showcase setup as boring safety infrastructure. Paid off as the mundane
  witness barrier and evacuation route after the blackout.
- **Opt-in telemetry:** Gideon checks that each girl has chosen what to share.
  Paid off when he honors their request to sever it and discovers that losing
  data need not mean abandoning care.
- **The Rollcaller's copied voices:** It first whispers an alias, then forges an
  administrator, then copies Night Clerk. The escalation establishes its
  ability and limitation before the climax; it can mimic speech but cannot
  counterfeit the team's shared consent rules.
- **The livestream's early-start indicator:** Seed the small camera light and
  guardian-view counter during setup. Paid off when Gideon knows exactly what
  the local breaker will stop; containment does not depend on a vague or
  omnipotent “internet wipe.”

## Canon constraints

- Treat the modern-like era as one period of the same deep-time world, not
  literal Earth, a separate plane, or a new global chronology.
- Magical-girl activity is a rare, concealed local anomaly. The incident has a
  small witness pool, loses its recording path before transformation, and
  produces no public proof. Do not imply routine public battles or a global
  magical society.
- Every magical act must come from a living agent: Drita, Bryn, Cassia, or the
  living Rollcaller. Tokens focus wearer-supplied power; radios, cameras,
  breakers, the ledger, barriers, and containment surfaces do not use magic by
  themselves.
- The trio's transformations, veil, powers, costs, and consent practices are
  one local system. Never describe them as rules governing all magic,
  technology, divinity, names, or identity in the universe.
- Names and records carry only ordinary social and evidentiary power here. The
  Rollcaller reads the ledger, observes reactions, exploits networked cameras,
  and makes inferences. It cannot identify truth because a name is spoken or
  written.
- The refraction veil is bounded and living-user-powered. It blurs transformed
  features but does not erase memory, rewrite files, conceal the transition,
  or guarantee secrecy.
- The handler role is an informal agreement local to these four people. It
  confers no command authority, no right to identities, and no access to a
  universal hidden organization.
- Gideon's knowledge must remain traceable to cumulative mundane evidence. He
  must not address a girl by civilian name on the handler relay, reveal a
  student-specific fact only her teacher would know, or let narration imply the
  girls discover his dual role.
- Gideon may arrange classwide accommodations and provide operational cover,
  but the girls decide to remain, set the trap, request the blackout, consent
  to severing their link, choose their combat roles, and decide recovery terms.
- Keep injuries non-graphic and consequences real. Maintain unambiguous
  adult/student boundaries and no romantic framing.
- The story has no crossover, lineage, monarchy, shared iconography, or system
  connection with Solstice Evening Bell, the First Monarch, any “queen of all
  magical girls,” any deity, Ravel, the Glass Gate, or another candidate or
  historical story.

## Proposed inventions

All items below are candidate story inventions only. None is authoritative
unless separately approved and promoted.

- **Era placement:** This event occurs in an unspecified city during the
  modern-like low-magic trough.
- **Northbridge Secondary:** A local secondary school with a paper attendance
  backup, a streamed community showcase, a rehearsal corridor, an acoustic fire
  partition, and a blind service exit. These details are story-scale and make
  no claim about schools elsewhere.
- **The trio's local system:** Drita, Bryn, and Cassia use voluntary,
  wearer-powered focus tokens to transform. Their particular powers are
  barriers/force redirection, connection sensing/severance, and luminous
  decoys/displaced echoes. Use costs calories, body heat, and concentration.
- **The refraction veil:** The transformed girls can sustain a local blur of
  face and voice. It protects live identity but has explicit limits: no memory
  alteration, no record rewriting, no concealed transition, and no immunity to
  correlation.
- **The informal handler arrangement:** Six months before the story, the trio
  anonymously asked for logistical help through a local emergency-radio
  contact. Gideon answered as Night Clerk. Their ordinary encrypted relay,
  opt-in telemetry, mutual anonymity, and no-orders consent rule belong only to
  this arrangement.
- **The Rollcaller:** A single living local anomaly that imitates voices,
  extends living filaments through connected equipment, and feeds on the social
  attention created by public recognition of a concealed identity. It learns
  through observation and ordinary evidence, not true names or truthful
  records.
- **Immediate containment:** The trio can isolate the Rollcaller's living body
  inside a sustained Kiteglass barrier after Pulsewire cuts its external
  filaments. The story need only establish safe short-term containment pending
  an off-page team decision; do not invent a larger authority that collects it.
- **Post-crisis protocol:** The team adds “facts once, then disconnect” when
  they request operational darkness, and chooses two mission-free recovery
  nights. This is a negotiated local practice, not a universal rule.

## Failure modes to watch

- **Teacher rescues the girls:** Do not let Gideon design or execute the magical
  victory. His blackout and evacuation create safe conditions; the girls
  identify the compromised channel, request darkness, revise the plan, manage
  their limits, and contain the antagonist.
- **Agency reduced to nominal consent:** Each girl must make a consequential
  decision visible in action. Drita changes from pursuit to defense, Bryn
  requests and performs the severance with consent, and Cassia requests a pause
  before choosing the decoy maneuver.
- **Covert care becomes creepy omniscience:** Keep Gideon's identity inference
  cumulative, observable, and fallible. Do not add private-record snooping,
  tracking without opt-in, magical recognition, body scrutiny, or a revelatory
  single clue.
- **Secret accidentally revealed:** Night Clerk never uses civilian names,
  school-specific private facts, or Gideon's unscrambled voice on the relay.
  Mr. Tolland never speaks handler-only phrases in a way the girls can connect.
  The ending must explicitly leave them unaware of both his handler identity
  and his knowledge.
- **Paternalism rewarded:** Gideon's instinct to monitor everything should
  create part of the problem when his protective attendance change narrows the
  Rollcaller's suspects. His growth is demonstrated by honoring the blackout
  and offering recovery choices, not by being proven right to overrule them.
- **Magic treated as universal:** Avoid lore exposition, cosmic origin claims,
  magical-girl history, global organizations, or statements that all magic,
  names, records, or transformations work this way.
- **Nonliving magic user:** Do not make the ledger, token, radio, school
  network, camera, livestream, barrier surface, or containment cabinet act
  magically on its own. Explicitly locate agency in a living girl or the living
  Rollcaller.
- **Inherent name or record truth:** The antagonist must steal/read the ledger,
  observe Gideon's behavior, and use cameras and reactions. Merely hearing or
  writing a name cannot bind, expose, or verify anyone.
- **Implausible public secrecy:** Cut the stream and cameras before the
  transformations, evacuate witnesses through the blind corridor, keep magic
  out of clear public view, and leave only an electrical-fault-scale local
  explanation. Do not invoke unexplained memory erasure or a global cover-up.
- **Unseeded climax tool:** Establish the breaker, network indicator, service
  corridor, fire partition, consent rule, team costs, and each power before the
  crisis.
- **POV leak after blackout:** Once Gideon cuts the relay, he cannot know the
  girls' thoughts or exact tactics. Convey the fight through sound, glimpses
  through the partition window, and later volunteered facts.
- **Care checklist without drama:** Physical, emotional, academic, and
  operational support must alter choices and consequences rather than appear as
  exposition.
- **Overstuffing:** Keep the class, administrator, school nurse, guardians, and
  other students unnamed and functional. Do not add subplots, families, a
  mascot, a named weapon, a larger faction, or a second antagonist.
- **Tone failure:** Dry humor should target forms, scheduling, and Gideon's own
  fussiness—not injury, anxiety, or adolescent judgment. Keep danger genuine
  and the aftermath warm rather than glib.
- **Name drift:** Use only the forms inventoried above. Do not shorten Night
  Clerk to `Clerk`, use surnames alone, invent a team name, or introduce a
  person-like title without repeating registry validation. Do not use bells,
  solstice imagery, monarchic aliases, royal language, or “queen of magical
  girls” phrasing that could suggest an unsupported Solstice Evening Bell
  connection.
