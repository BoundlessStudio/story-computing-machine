# Outline

## Story

- Proposed reader-facing title: *The First Call* (retain).
- Premise and central promise: At an unnamed kingdom's first muster for a long border war, an armored rooster arrives before the levy horn and falls in. Tebri, a young campaign armorer, tightens his absurdly old harness while the soldiers laugh; the captain keeps him as a mascot because that is the only role the army can imagine. Across years, Dawnwatch repeatedly chooses sentry posts, hazards, wounded stragglers, and fights of his own, while the company turns each deliberate act into a lucky-rooster tale. When the rearguard is destroyed on a burning battlefield, Tebri releases the hidden structure of his armor at his insistence. At dawn, the last survivors see him return in his enormous war body with arrows in his back and understand that the small rooster did not become a warrior for them: he marched to enlist as one.
- Intended reader experience: Affectionate martial comedy slowly acquiring an ache, then mythic visual awe grounded in smoke, damaged metal, feathers, and exhaustion. Readers should recognize Dawnwatch's agency before Tebri names it; the release comes when the survivors respond to the same judgment they had long explained away as luck.
- Generating force:
  - Mode: Plot-led accumulation through an armor biography. Each campaign movement adds a repair or altered fit while removing one plausible way to call Dawnwatch's conduct instinct; the final night forces Tebri either to honor or obstruct his decision.
  - Focal attachment or contradiction: Tebri's pride in keeping Dawnwatch safe also preserves the fiction that he belongs to the company. He accepts care and companionship while showing that his duty is self-chosen, not ownership, omen, or reward.
  - Plot-led wants and useful limit, if applicable: Tebri immediately wants to get the last soldiers through the smoke and keep Dawnwatch from returning to the rearguard; she also wants to respect the military judgment he has shown for years. Her useful flaw is protective classification: calling him mascot lets her choose which risks are his. Dawnwatch wants the survivors moving before archers close the withdrawal and wants the black release pin in his harness cut. He cannot explain in human speech that this will release his full body, and the change appears irreversible with the knowledge and tools at hand.
- Counterforce or complication:
  - Source: A broken withdrawal, fire set by both armies, and pursuing archers leave a handful unable to hold the field or carry another badly wounded body. The beloved mascot stories have also taught the soldiers to read warning, refusal, and tactical initiative as entertaining luck.
  - Credible inside logic, if a person: No individual antagonist. The unseen pursuers are tired soldiers trying to prevent an enemy remnant from reforming on the supply road; their volleys follow ordinary battlefield aims, not special cruelty toward Dawnwatch. The captain values his warnings, but the mascot label made cooperation possible without recognizing a nonhuman soldier.
  - Pressure or change created: Tebri can “save” Dawnwatch only by denying the choice he is plainly making. Cutting the pin may ruin the armor she has maintained, expose an unknown power, and make his body too large to hide, feed, or evacuate; refusing leaves the survivors trapped.
- Narrative design:
  - POV, tense, distance, and limitation: Close third past through Tebri. She reads rivets, leather, weight, wounds, and the history recorded by armor precisely, but is slow to grant intention without words. Keep Dawnwatch exterior; his mind is legible only through consistent preparation, refusals, remembered routes, and costly choices. Readers share Tebri's ignorance of the harness and full body, not her denial of his agency.
  - Time shape and compression: Four selected movements across several years—enlistment, a costly warning, recovery of a winter straggler, discovery of an older repair—then one continuous final evening through sunrise. Bridge time through changed materials, missing faces, altered unit slang, and fewer hands at Tebri's bench rather than campaign summary.
  - Information and reveal strategy: Establish comprehension early when Dawnwatch presents a loose buckle, rejects a baggage cart, and distinguishes commands no trained animal was taught. Let the legends preserve facts but change their grammar: he “brought luck” instead of choosing a watch post. Foreshadow the full body with inward wear, nested plates too broad for a cock's chest, repairs in an unknown hand, and a black pin Tebri cannot remove. Leave his origin, kind, former army, and armor maker unknown; establish only that the release is voluntary and costly.
  - Governing movement: Arrive; laugh; fit; permit a mascot; receive a warning; explain it as luck; repair damage earned without a handler; follow his route; turn agency into legend; find older workmanship; avoid the question; lose the company; order him safe; be refused; read his request; cut what Tebri meant to preserve; retreat; witness the war body; tend the warrior; take his route.
- Speculative surplus: Dawnwatch's shed feathers repel grain beetles but slowly bleach red dye orange. Quartermasters tuck them into flour bins and complain when they mottle uniform cuffs; the effect neither proves intelligence nor predicts, powers, triggers, or explains his change.
- Structural distinction: A longitudinal accretion story recorded in one repeatedly altered suit of armor, narrowing halfway into a single retreat. The required climax remains on the burning battlefield, but recognition belongs only to the exhausted survivors; no royal audience, enemy conversion, public proof, corrected chronicle, institutional rewrite, victory ceremony, or memorial follows.
- Decisive turn, deepening, or recognition: Near midnight, after Dawnwatch twice leads survivors away from killing ground, he turns toward the burning rearguard. Tebri catches him and begins stripping off the harness for a baggage sling. He resists only that removal, then strikes the black pin and the road in deliberate sequence. Tebri finally reads a request rather than an animal body and cuts the jammed pin. Small plates spring into long nested arcs bearing full-sized wear; Dawnwatch enters the smoke while the survivors obey his route signal. At sunrise he emerges enormous, the opened armor fitted to him and arrows standing from his back. Recognition comes through continuity: the same injured gait, head count, refusal of aid until the rear is checked, and exact signal he has used all war. Size reveals nothing his choices had not already said.
- Aftereffect or live uncertainty: Dawnwatch has bought a retreat, not won or morally settled the war. He is mobile but badly wounded; his expanded body makes hiding, food, transport, and repair immediate problems. Whether he can fold small again, where he learned war, whether others like him exist, and whether anyone will believe the survivors remain open. No speech, oath, rank, ownership, public recognition, or promise of future service is required; the handful simply accept his safer route before pursuit resumes.
- Opening / ending relation: Open with Dawnwatch placing himself behind the column while laughter rolls backward and Tebri steps into his path to decide whether he may follow. End with the wounded giant taking the old supply road at a pace the survivors can manage and Tebri forming the remaining soldiers behind him without issuing an order. The reversal stays practical and in motion rather than becoming a proclamation or symbol.

## Beats

1. Before the levy horn, Dawnwatch marches onto the muster road in battered, ill-fitting armor. Tebri finds the harness genuine, tightens the strap he presents, and laughs when the captain records him as mascot; the rooster joins only after inspecting the column.
2. In the first wet campaign, he leaves the dry baggage cart for an exposed watch rise, crows before hidden movement reaches human sight, and pecks a withdrawal line in mud. The company escapes an ambush, calls it luck, and gives Tebri a breastplate cut he earned at the most dangerous useful post.
3. In winter, he refuses feed and warmth until Tebri follows him back to a wounded straggler omitted from the head count; he accepts treatment only after the soldier. The retelling makes the act cute possession—he “found what belonged to him”—while Tebri adds another plate.
4. Years later, with the company thinned, Tebri opens a seam and finds nested metal, full-sized inward wear, an unknown hand's repairs, and the immovable black pin. Dawnwatch stops her forcing it, then chooses the next day's forward march. She suspects prior service but keeps using the safer mascot word.
5. On the final evening, the line breaks around burning wagons. Dawnwatch signals an old side road and gets a handful out while most of the rearguard disappears behind smoke and volleys. Tebri prepares to sling him with the wounded.
6. He refuses, returns to the road mouth, and indicates pin, pursuers, and escape route. Tebri tries to remove the harness instead; his precise resistance makes her distinguish saving a companion from overruling a soldier. She cuts the requested pin.
7. The nested armor opens beyond the small body. Dawnwatch enters the smoke before the change is visible. Tebri leads the survivors through a drainage cut while impacts and wingbeats give way to silence; keep the fight non-graphic and mostly occluded.
8. Dawn reveals Dawnwatch emerging in full war shape, arrows in his back. The survivors recognize his familiar gait, count, refusal, and route signal rather than debating a thesis. They shorten the obstructing shafts, brace him without assuming command, and form behind him on the supply road before safety or official belief can turn recognition into another legend.
