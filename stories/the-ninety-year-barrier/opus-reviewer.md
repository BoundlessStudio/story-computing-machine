# Opus review: The Ninety-Year Barrier

Independent audit for a later editing pass. It is not a `review.md` verdict,
does not change canon state, and does not authorize edits by itself.

## Verdict

- Dialogue: REVISE — the two workbench hinges don't hold together (the Witch's "contradiction" and the model test), the choice to help rests on an offer she never made, and a correction-and-comeback reflex runs through the walk and several scene endings.
- Prose: PASS — clean and concrete; only minor tics ("not X, Y" reversals, narration that explains an exchange after it lands, a one-line-paragraph run near the end).
- Continuity and prompt: REVISE — the prompt promise is delivered, but the story never keeps track of which side of the barrier anyone is on: it shows four inward crossings and no outward one.
- Edit scope: structural
- Existing review.md said: Verdict PASS; "Dialogue: PASS". Disagree: it certified the "coherent causal sequence" without checking the model test, the interrogation logic or the barrier geography.

This story is better than the collection norm on the thing the owner complains about most. The two speakers really do sound different. The Witch never uses contractions and talks about load, tension and crossings. The youngster babbles when frightened, thinks in basketry (waling row, lay, rim, snag), and grows short and bossy only once their hands know more than hers. The failures are elsewhere. At the two workbench hinges the reader is asked to accept a deduction and a demonstration that don't work on the page. The Witch also slides into the house habit of correcting a word and closing on a dry last line, which the outline's own anti-default ("Avoid quip-trading prodigy") ruled out. The failed first repair, the reset, and the final exchange are the best dialogue in the story, so leave them almost untouched. The "structural" work is a bounded staging repair. Decide which side of the stakes each person is on and put every crossing on the page. The plot, beat order and ending stay.

## Edit guardrails

- Second-person past tense throughout. The youngster stays unnamed and ungendered, and the Witch stays "the Witch of the Dark Forest" / "the Witch".
- The opening must keep the prompt's speech beats: ninety years to cast it, a dragon struck it and failed, the youngster opened a hole (L21), "I am not angry with you" said ahead of the screaming (L25), and the starving-at-a-feast gaze (L17, L27, later L281 and L421). The prompt's "Would you kindly inform me how…" is paraphrased, not required word for word. An edit may make the question explicit but must not drop these beats.
- Length band 2,500–4,000 words (now about 3,190). Craft profile prospective-2026-08-21. Broadly accessible.
- Universe: `universe/rules.md` "Living agency and magic" (LOCKED) says only life can use magic. The Witch supplies all force. Stakes, cords, the hook and the barrier only store, carry or shape it, and the youngster supplies physical pattern knowledge. Keep "The slat did nothing by itself" and "The barrier did nothing on its own" true, and don't let any re-staging make the barrier recognize, choose or answer anyone.
- Mechanism that must survive: outside force divides and tightens the ring. The buried inner release is slow lift, reverse turn, neighboring strand. The youngster's hook did that from outside. The repair adds a decoy, so a tug from outside now tightens instead of releasing, and the full sequence still opens from inside.
- Keep the dragon, village and geography unnamed and local, as the outline requires. Don't bring in water infrastructure while re-staging the verge (style guide, "No default water infrastructure"). The river bank, rain and rain barrel are fine as they are.
- Keep the voice split described in the voice map. Don't give the Witch contractions or the youngster her vocabulary.

## Dialogue findings

### D1 · Major · The interrogation's contradiction can't be followed

- Where: L47–L83, workbench, first questioning
- Text:
> “Your basket was on your village side?”
> “Then the hook entered from the forest side.”
> “All three answers cannot be true.”
> “Feeling is useful. Agreeing is not.”
- Problem: Ground/uptake and Ground/referent. "Then the hook entered from the forest side" is framed as following from where the basket was, but where a basket sat says nothing about where a hook went in, so the "Then" doesn't follow. The youngster has given five spoken answers ("The village.", three "Yes"es, "Very hard."), so the reader can't tell which "three" conflict. The reason (outside force tightens the ring) only arrives at L107 and L291, and even then "pulled toward yourself" is an outward pull, not the dragon's inward blow. The reader has to take the contradiction on the Witch's authority. The basket answer is later quietly contradicted ("the bite of the basket rim against your back", L259) and nobody notices, so it reads as an error rather than a payoff. "Which hand?" (L51), asked right after she watched the mime, works as appeasement only if the reader supplies that. The scene closes on an aphorism instead of a demand.
- Fix: Keep the emotional beat: she catches the youngster agreeing to please her. Make the contradiction something the reader can see. The best lever is already here, because she is watching the mimed hand. Have the youngster say "Very hard" while the mimed hand only turns at the wrist. That also plants the wrist motion that pays off at L279. Keep "Were you facing the forest or the village?" / "The village." as the line that puts the youngster outside the stakes (see C1). Cut the basket question and the "Then…" inference, or keep the basket and let the youngster correct it aloud at L259. Replace the closing aphorism with a plain order. Illustrative only: "You said very hard. Your hand hardly moved." / "You keep saying yes. Show me again, and don't talk."

### D2 · Blocking · The model test pulls the crossing the youngster already fixed

- Where: L133–L149, workbench, cord-and-rim model
- Text:
> You tucked the cord beneath its neighbor, lifted the crossed part on your thumb, and drew the loose end through.
> The split rim pulled nearly round.
> “It did not before.”
> “It would when you pulled that side.”
> She pulled. The rim bent exactly where you had indicated.
- Problem: Ground failure at the hinge where the Witch's hunger locks onto the youngster's hands. The fix is already in when she pulls, so pulling now tests the corrected crossing. A bend "exactly where you had indicated" would then mean the fix failed, and the reverted version isn't restored until L159. "It did not before" denies what both of them have just seen: the rim only "pulled nearly round" after the change, so the strain was on the break before. "Where you had indicated" has no antecedent either, because the youngster pointed at nothing and only said "that side". The reader gets "the kid was right" from tone alone.
- Fix: Put the demonstration in causal order. She doubts the change, reverses the youngster's tuck herself (or pulls her own second loop), and the split gapes at a spot the youngster has actually pointed to. Then the youngster's version holds under the same pull. Cut "It did not before" or turn it into a doubt that doesn't contradict what's visible (she hasn't seen the difference yet). Keep "Why?" / "Because otherwise the strain sits on the break." and "The Witch's attention became worse."

### D3 · Major · The youngster's freedom to leave was never offered

- Where: L299–L307, at the tear, the Witch's offer
- Text:
> “You may direct me from back there,” she said. “Or you may show me with your hands. I will not require either after today.”
> You could walk away. She had said it plainly, and the hook was within reach.
- Problem: Ground/uptake at the decisive choice. She offers two ways to help and says only that she won't require either *after today*. Nothing on the page says the youngster may leave now, yet the narration claims "She had said it plainly." The choice of "Hands" is framed as free, and it's free only on the strength of a line that doesn't exist.
- Fix: Either give her the third option outright and keep the narration, or cut "She had said it plainly" and make the freedom the youngster's own reading of the moment (the hook held out handle first, the roofs in sight, nobody blocking the path). Keep "Hands," and the terms that follow, which are excellent. Illustrative only: "Or you may take your basket home, and I will manage it alone."

### D4 · Major · Correction-and-comeback routine, heaviest on the walk

- Where: L195–L217, the path down; also L83, L95, L291–L297 and L399–L401
- Text:
> The Witch caught the back of your coat before you put a foot on a shining root.
> “You stepped on it.”
> “I was stepping over.”
> For several minutes, the only talk was useful.
> “The way you came now contains a hole.”
> “Then I have discovered a second problem.”
> “Chance had a hook.”
> “I have said it now.”
- Problem: Tennis Match and Punchline Patch. The walk runs two back-to-back rounds of clipped warning, correction and a dry last word. "You stepped on it" contradicts L195, where she catches the youngster before the foot lands. That makes the exchange a precision duel, not a warning. The narration then calls this talk "useful". "The way you came now contains a hole" gives no usable reason, because the hole is where they are going. It also depends on a route to her house the story never shows (see C1). The same shape closes other exchanges: "Feeling is useful. Agreeing is not." (L83), "Then I have discovered a second problem." (L95, whose meaning is also unclear: is something else to blame, or is the youngster's not knowing the problem?), the "chance would not complete it" / "Chance had a hook." / "Yes… That is the difficulty." unit (L291–L297), and "You could have said that first." / "I have said it now." (L399–L401). No single one is fatal. Together they make the relationship a quip exchange, which the outline's anti-default forbade.
- Fix: On the walk, make the talk as useful as the narration says: one plain warning per hazard and short or absent replies. Drop "You stepped on it" or make it true to L195. Replace the hole line with a real reason tied to the settled geography, or cut the fork exchange. Keep "Low branch." / "I know." At L95, let her answer plainly or not at all (she believes the wall, not the youngster, and goes to the chest). At L293, give the youngster a reaction that comes from fear of blame instead of wit. Illustrative only: "I didn't know it was there to find." At L399–L401 the youngster's complaint is fair (she sent them through before explaining), so it can stay. Let her answer by closing the passage instead of with a retort. Keep "It has not improved while we talked." and "Now we find out whether we repaired it or made a prettier hole."; they carry information in her voice. As a rule, one dry closer per scene at most.

### D5 · Minor · "From outside." is a clip the youngster can't decode

- Where: L287–L291, at the tear, the recognition
- Text:
> “From outside.” She went to the tear and held her fingers near its sparking edge without touching.
- Problem: Ground/referent. Coming straight after "I found something in my way," the two words only make sense once her speech has explained that the release is meant to work from inside. The youngster (and reader) can't tell whether she is correcting, marvelling or accusing. The speech that follows also runs close to a design briefing for the reader.
- Fix: Make it a whole thought the youngster can take in. Trim the speech to what they need: force from outside tightens, a way out from inside exists, and she hid it. Keep "My death." with "as flatly as rain". Illustrative only: "You did it from outside. That should not be possible."

### D6 · Minor · Beats and tags that name the emotion

- Where: L317, after "Hold it. Don't help."; L431, the farewell question
- Text:
> That cost her something. You saw it in the set of her jaw.
> “Should I come tomorrow?” you asked, hating yourself for asking and needing to know.
- Problem: A stock beat ("set of her jaw") plus a gloss, and a tag that labels what the question already carries.
- Fix: At L317, give her one specific physical act (the fingers that flexed at L313 folding still, say) and cut the gloss. At L431, cut the participle clause or reduce it to an action.

## Prose findings

### P1 · Minor · "Not X. Y." reversals as a habit

- Where: L17, L97, L131, L151, L227, L281, L319, L377
- Text:
> The stories had been right about her eyes. They were not red, or bottomless, or aflame.
> It was not the gray you had expected.
> But now you could see what it followed: not your throat, not your fear. Your wrist.
- Problem: Eight denial-then-reveal constructions in about 3,200 words. L17 also trips on its own logic: it says the stories were "right", then lists what the eyes were not.
- Fix: Keep two or three that earn it (L131's "Not wrong for what she wanted, perhaps"; L281 as the prompt's payoff, possibly simplified). Rewrite the rest as plain statements. At L17, decide whether the stories were right or wrong.

### P2 · Minor · Narration explains exchanges and theme after they land

- Where: L171, after the lay/load exchange; L377, the repair summary; L405, the outside test
- Text:
> You were both speaking about the same cord. It did not yet feel as though you were speaking about the same thing.
> In your hands it was tension; in hers, force; between you, for a little while, it became work that could be finished.
> She put one living hand against the outer span.
- Problem: L171 glosses the translation gap the dialogue just showed ("Against the load." / "If you like."). L377 ends the repair with a thesis sentence. "One living hand" displays the magic rule rather than depicting anything.
- Fix: Cut L171 or keep only a physical beat. End L377 on the sweat and the stakes, not the summary. Drop "living" at L405.

### P3 · Minor · One-line paragraphs as the pulse of the ending

- Where: L403–L441, the two tests and the farewell (L405, L409, L415, L419, L423, L429, L439, L441)
- Text:
> Outside, the Witch lowered her hand.
> Then she came through.
- Problem: Earlier, single-line beats earn their weight (L331–L337). Here nearly every beat stands alone and the rhythm goes flat.
- Fix: Join related beats into paragraphs (hand lowered and "Again?"; waiting and turning away). Keep the isolated line only where a turn needs it.

## Continuity and prompt findings

### C1 · Blocking · Which side of the barrier anyone is on

- Where: L219–L255, the verge and tear; L307; L391–L393; L417–L423; L449
- Text:
> Only when the Witch stepped through did you understand that she had opened it.
> The tear was forty paces north, hidden behind a stand of willow.
> “Hands,” you said. “But you stand on that side.
> Then she led you back to the deliberate passage, opened it, and sent you through to the village side.
> But she waited at the threshold until you stepped back.
> When she opened the village passage, you took the basket through first.
- Problem: Continuity contradiction. Coming down from the forest, both of them go through the Witch's passage toward the village (L221–L243), which puts them inside. The story's own logic puts the youngster outside when the hook caught ("facing… The village", L57; "the hook entered from the forest side", L63; "You struck from outside", L121; "From outside.", L291), yet nobody crosses back out before "Stand there". At L391 she sends the youngster "through to the village side" and says "If I press from out here", which needs both of them outside, though no outward crossing has happened. At L423 the Witch comes inside. At L449 the youngster crosses into the village yet again. That makes four inward crossings and no outward one. "That side" (L307) and the bundle that "lay inside" (L245) can't be placed. How the youngster reached the Witch's house is never said, and L215–L217 depends on it.
- Fix: Pick one map and put every crossing on the page. Recommended map, with the fewest edits and matching L63, L121, L291 and the ending: the willow bank, the youngster's standing place and the repair are all on the forest side. (1) At L219–L243, don't cross inward. Either the youngster only reaches a hand through for the "Did you feel that?" test, or move that test to L391, where the youngster really does cross in. (2) At L245, say which side the bundle fell to. (3) At L307, name the side ("that side" of what). (4) L391–L393 then work as written. (5) At L417–L423, have the youngster step back out through their own opening while she waits without reaching through (this keeps her restraint), or add one clause taking both back out before L425. That makes L449 the youngster's real crossing home. (6) Add one clause early (L11–L35) or at L215 on how the youngster came to the house, or cut the fork exchange (D4). The other map (cross in at L227, then step out through the tear at L253) is fine too if every crossing is stated.

### C2 · Minor · Gaps in the repair staging

- Where: L337–L357, the failed attempt and the retry; L417, the inside test; L425, the decoy
- Text:
> Pain shot through your wrist. You let go. The hook stayed trapped, humming.
> This time, when you lifted, she did not move.
> At the western tear, you had added one more crossing than before
- Problem: The hook is left trapped in the cinched crossing and then used again with no retrieval. The inside-release test (L417) doesn't say whether it happens at the passage or at the repaired tear. The decoy, the youngster's own design contribution and a change to her ninety-year work, appears only in retrospect, and the Witch's acceptance of it is never shown.
- Fix: One clause freeing the hook after L351. Place the L417 test. Put one line in the L377 repair paragraph where the youngster adds the extra crossing and she lets them. L425 can then stay as the confirmation.

### C3 · Minor · "Reed" and "willow" name the same stuff

- Where: L11, L245, L259, L275, L285, L447, L455 ("reed"); L35, L245, L437, L455 ("willow")
- Text:
> The Witch of the Dark Forest laid your reed hook on the workbench between you.
> One of your cut bundles lay inside, reeds scattered where you had dropped them.
- Problem: The youngster cuts willow, but the tool, the bundle and the snag are "reeds". A reader can think there are two materials.
- Fix: Establish once, early, that the village calls cut willow rods "reeds", or standardize. Keep the final line's pairing, which works once the usage is set.
