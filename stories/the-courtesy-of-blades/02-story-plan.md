# Story plan

## Premise and promise

- **Premise:** On the evening before she leaves Lacebridge to lead another
  town's fencing yard, Fiora Halwick insists on a nonlethal rematch with the
  rival she wants to date, because only a cleanly witnessed contest can settle
  whether his disputed earlier concession was honesty or pity.
- **Story promise:** A warm, brisk romantic comedy in which mutual attraction
  is obvious, the duelists' refusal to skip the duel becomes emotionally
  sensible, five chalk-bright touches carry a real professional and intimate
  argument, and the final self-called point earns an honest invitation rather
  than possession of another person.
- **Central dramatic question:** Can Fiora risk losing the public vindication
  she thinks she needs in order to trust Jasper's respect, tell the truth about
  the deciding touch, and choose a relationship that does not cost her
  independence?
- **Meaningful change:** Fiora begins believing that accepting Jasper's care
  would let him define her as someone he spared; she ends able to distinguish
  care from condescension, stake her reputation on mutual honesty, and accept
  affection without surrendering either her new post or her competitive self.

## Story controls

- **POV and tense:** Close third person through Fiora only; past tense. No POV
  shifts are needed because Jasper's choices can be made legible through his
  fencing, direct statements, and acceptance of Fiora's final call.
- **Tone and genre:** Warm fantasy romantic comedy; clipped competitive banter,
  affectionate exasperation from their friend, sincere professional stakes,
  and a quick but spatially clear action climax.
- **Length:** 3,300 words targeted within the required 2,500–4,000 range.
- **Setting scale:** Lacebridge, a compact riverside fantasy town in an unnamed
  era of the one continuous world. The story remains inside its public civic
  blade yard and nearby evening food stalls; it makes no era-placement or
  cosmological claims.
- **Content:** Teen. Blunted practice blades, padded target panels, exertion,
  and harmless chalk marks; no blood, graphic injury, sexual content, coercion,
  or humiliation presented as romance.
- **Duel operation:** A wholly mundane, local courtesy bout. First to three
  clear chalk-edge touches on padded torso or upper-arm panels wins. Flexible
  blunted blades, eye-and-throat guards, a rail separating onlookers, pre-bout
  equipment checks, surrender and halt calls, and Quade's power to stop the
  match keep it nonlethal. Quade judges clear touches; the fencers must
  truthfully self-call order when a double touch is too close for him to see.
- **Consent boundary:** The mutually negotiated personal stake determines who
  gets the first invitation and chooses the date's place. It cannot require an
  acceptance, continued courtship, physical contact, a career change, or any
  later date. Quade states this local limit before blades are crossed, and both
  adults affirm it.

## Character engine

- **Fiora Halwick — protagonist:** An adult woman, assistant instructor, and
  current holder of Lacebridge's trial badge. She leaves at dawn to become head
  instructor at the neighboring East Weir yard, a post she earned and will not
  abandon for romance. She concretely wants a completed, witnessed rematch
  before she goes, ideally a win she can trust. Delay costs her the only
  official rematch possible for months and leaves a rumor attached to her
  badge and new appointment. Her external stakes are professional legitimacy
  and clean closure; her internal stake is whether she can believe admiration
  that arrives alongside protectiveness. Her blind spot is treating any act of
  care from Jasper as evidence that he does not regard her as an equal. Her
  decisive agency is to call his final touch first even though silence could
  preserve her chance to win.
- **Jasper Renlow — rival and romantic counterpart:** An adult river-patrol
  fencer and Fiora's longtime training rival. He wants to win the rematch and
  make the first plain invitation before she leaves. In their previous trial,
  he self-called Fiora's touch a fraction before his own, giving her the third
  point; subsequent gossip recast his honesty as gallantry and his refusal to
  argue publicly allowed the insult to fester. He fights hard, adapts to her
  new technique, states that neither victory nor affection gives him a claim
  on her future, and accepts her losing call instead of patronizingly handing
  the point back. His secondary movement is from assuming actions should speak
  for him to saying clearly what he wants and what he does not presume.
- **Quade — friend and bout official:** An adult mutual friend and the yard's
  qualified referee, dryly frustrated by two people whose attraction is
  obvious. He understands the safety and consent rules but initially mistakes
  the duel as needless romantic indirection. His logical objection delivers
  the prompt's joke; witnessing the rematch teaches him that the contest is
  doing necessary work about truth, reputation, and equality. He stops unsafe
  behavior, refuses crowd pressure, and records the result, but does not solve
  either the match or the romance.
- **Opposition:** Fiora and Jasper are each other's active tactical opposition.
  Their pride, the public misreading of the prior result, Fiora's imminent
  departure, and a scoring system that depends on the honesty they have doubted
  provide pressure without a villain or coercive authority.
- **Relationship engine:** They know each other's habits well enough to fence
  intimately but have used competition as a safe substitute for direct speech.
  Every point must alter Fiora's interpretation of Jasper: studied opponent,
  not indulgent admirer; demanding equal, not rescuer; fallible man willing to
  risk a clear answer. Attraction supplies comic heat, while the rematch tests
  whether trust can support it.

## Causal arc

1. **Beginning state:** Fiora arrives at the Lacebridge yard with her travel
   case already packed for dawn. She possesses the old trial badge but not
   confidence in what it means, because spectators still say Jasper let her
   win. She and Jasper have negotiated an official rematch and, separately but
   symmetrically, offered a date as the personal stake.
2. **Inciting disruption — the complete prompt exchange:** During the consent
   and terms check, Quade says, in the prompt's exact sequence, “So if you win
   the duel, you get to take her on a date.” Both nod. “And if she wins the
   duel, she gets to take you on a date.” Both nod. “So why don't you both
   just... skip that and go on a date?” They both glare. Quade then clarifies
   that “take” means the first freely answerable invitation, not custody or an
   obligation. The joke exposes the surface absurdity while the glare commits
   them to resolving the deeper dispute on the strip.
3. **First escalation:** Jasper reads Fiora's familiar high feint and scores the
   first clean chalk mark. Because he has studied rather than softened against
   her, the loss is also evidence she wanted. Fiora immediately unveils a new
   low pivot developed while preparing to teach at East Weir and evens the
   score, establishing that she has grown beyond the fighter he remembers.
4. **Pressure turn:** Fiora drives Jasper hard and takes the third point for a
   2–1 lead. A spectator's mutter that Jasper is still easing against her makes
   her overextend on the next pass. Jasper refuses to answer the crowd for her,
   asks whether she wants an argument or his best fencing, and—when she chooses
   the latter—punishes the overreach for 2–2. Her demand for equality has a
   consequence, and he honors it.
5. **Truth surfaces:** During the mandatory re-chalk and safety pause, they
   finally name the old wound. Jasper says Fiora's earlier touch truly landed
   first; his mistake was expecting a self-call to explain itself after gossip
   turned it into pity. Fiora says simply dating him would make the town's false
   version look true and leave her unable to know which version he believed.
   He confirms that winning would buy neither her continued presence in
   Lacebridge nor a second date. With the deciding point still unclaimed, each
   affirmatively chooses to continue.
6. **Crisis:** At match point, Fiora can still defend the old badge by winning,
   or preserve herself from a clean loss by treating any close exchange as
   unknowable. Doing either would leave the central question unanswered. She
   must decide whether courtesy means being spared or being trusted to report a
   costly truth.
7. **Climax:** The final exchange combines the bout's seeded techniques. Jasper
   prepares for Fiora's new low pivot; she turns it back into the high attack he
   read at the opening. Both chalked blades land in what looks simultaneous
   from Quade's angle, hers on Jasper's upper arm and his on her rib panel.
   Fiora feels his flex and sees his chalk bloom half a beat first. Before
   Jasper can speak or Quade can invite debate, she calls Jasper's touch. He
   accepts her judgment rather than yielding the point, and Quade declares him
   the 3–2 winner. Her mirrored act confirms that Jasper's old self-call could
   have been honest respect; the public ledger now contains both costly calls.
8. **Resonant aftermath:** Jasper explicitly separates his victory from her
   answer and asks for one riverside supper before her departure. Fiora says
   yes because she wants him, not because he won. They begin the date at the
   evening stalls still marked with each other's chalk. Fiora keeps her dawn
   departure and new post; Jasper asks when he may visit East Weir, and she
   answers with a teasing condition for a future rematch rather than a promise
   to shrink her life.

## Canon constraints

- Keep Lacebridge and East Weir in an unnamed fantasy era of the one continuous
  physical world. Do not call the setting another plane or planet, name Earth,
  Ravel, the Glass-Sea world, a Gate, or a Galactic Cycle date.
- The bout is mundane. Blades, chalk, pads, rails, and the ledger have no
  agency, make no choices, and create no magical effects. This avoids implying
  that nonliving objects use magic. (`universe/rules.md`, **Living agency and
  magic**)
- Present the courtesy bout, self-call rule, yard office, and personal-stake
  limit as practices of Lacebridge's civic blade yard only. Do not call them
  ancient, universal, sacred, or evidence of how all magic, law, courtship, or
  names work. (`universe/rules.md`, **System categories**, **Costs, desire, and
  local mechanisms**, and **Names and identity binding**)
- Neither score nor custom can compel a date. Both adults retain the ability to
  halt, surrender, decline, leave town, or refuse later contact. The conclusion
  needs Jasper's unforced invitation and Fiora's affirmative answer on page.
  (`universe/style-guide.md`, **Personhood and moral consequence**)
- Fiora must remain the most causally important character even though she loses
  the bout: she earns two points, brings a new technique and career goal, calls
  the deciding touch, keeps her post, and chooses the date. Do not let Quade,
  Jasper, the crowd, or the wager decide for her.
- Keep action non-graphic and consequence-aware. Quade conducts checks, honors
  halt and surrender, controls spectators, and calls the exchange before either
  fencer follows through. No target is the head, throat, hands, or joints.
  (`universe/style-guide.md`, **Audience and content boundaries**)
- Do not import people, institutions, artifacts, imagery, or facts from
  candidate, abandoned, or historical stories. Similarity to another weapon or
  duel creates no continuity. (`universe/style-guide.md`, **Continuity and
  evidence discipline**)
- Everything under **Proposed inventions** remains a story proposal until the
  normal final review and explicit canon-promotion workflow; none is
  authoritative because it appears in this plan.

## Proposed inventions

All items below are local story proposals, not established canon:

- **Lacebridge and East Weir:** Neighboring river towns in an unnamed era.
  Lacebridge contains the story's civic blade yard; East Weir has appointed
  Fiora to lead its own yard. No relationship to an established era, place,
  faction, or story is implied.
- **Lacebridge civic blade yard:** A small public training and competition
  venue with a fenced strip, spectator rail, safety gear, an official result
  ledger, and a qualified yardkeeper/referee. This is a municipal-scale
  practice, not a world-spanning order.
- **Courtesy bout protocol:** A local, mundane, nonlethal first-to-three contest
  using flexible blunted blades dusted with chalk and dark padded target panels
  on torso and upper arms. Eye and throat guards are mandatory. Quade inspects
  equipment, can call halt or no-point, accepts surrender, and keeps onlookers
  behind the rail. Clear marks score; out-of-view double touches rely on the
  fencers' honest self-call of order.
- **Personal-stake limit:** Lacebridge's yard permits mutually negotiated
  wagers, but a wager involving company or courtship can award only the first
  invitation and choice of venue. Acceptance and every later interaction stay
  voluntary; the rule has legal/social force only inside this local practice
  and no magical force.
- **Professional history:** Fiora and Jasper are longtime training rivals. A
  prior public trial ended 3–2 for Fiora after Jasper self-called her touch
  first; romantic gossip misrepresented his call as pity. Fiora holds the
  resulting local badge, Jasper works with the river patrol, Fiora has earned a
  head-instructor appointment at East Weir, and Quade is their friend and the
  rematch official.
- **Ordinary local color:** Dark practice coats with replaceable padded scoring
  panels, an evening crowd of trainees and neighbors, riverside supper stalls,
  and routine travel between the towns. These details have no magical or
  cosmological significance.

## Failure modes to watch

- **Possessive prize language:** The required “take her/him on a date” wording
  may appear in Quade's quoted summary, but narration and subsequent dialogue
  must immediately preserve the local rule that it grants an invitation, not
  ownership or compelled attendance.
- **A passive woman because she loses:** Fiora must drive the challenge, score
  twice through demonstrated skill, protect her career choice, make the costly
  final call, and give an explicit yes. Do not frame honesty as feminine
  surrender or Jasper's win as authority over her.
- **Jasper secretly throws the bout:** Every point must show full effort. He
  wins the last point by a seeded tactical read, accepts Fiora's truthful call,
  and does not “give” her the match or claim that restraint proves love.
- **Only a one-note joke:** Deliver the prompt exchange early and let it be
  funny, then reveal through action why the rematch is necessary for trust and
  reputation. Do not merely repeat the skip-the-duel logic at the ending.
- **Exposition between every touch:** Seed the old result before the first
  salute, reveal its emotional interpretations in short breath/re-chalk pauses,
  and let scores change the argument. Avoid a lore speech about local customs.
- **An unseeded ambiguous climax:** State the self-call rule and prior self-call
  before match point. Quade's obstructed angle must follow naturally from the
  speed and crossing bodies, not sudden incompetence.
- **Safety theater:** Keep target zones, gear, official control, surrender, and
  stoppage consistent. No sharp blade, severe wound, or external emergency is
  needed to create stakes.
- **Romance erases Fiora's goal:** She does not decline or postpone East Weir,
  and Jasper never asks. The first date starts tonight; any future visit is a
  separate, mutual possibility.
- **Quade solves the story:** He may enforce rules and record the score, but he
  cannot call the unseen final order, broker consent, or tell Fiora what the old
  match meant. Her choice and the duelists' direct speech resolve both arcs.
- **Magic or global-law drift:** Do not animate the equipment, make truth or
  attraction a power source, or describe the courtesy bout as a universal
  custom. It is a mundane practice in one yard.
- **Name drift:** Use only `Fiora Halwick`/`Fiora`, `Jasper Renlow`/`Jasper`, and
  `Quade`. Do not introduce surname-only labels, capitalized role aliases,
  nicknames, named spectators, animals, person-like weapons, or businesses
  without another complete registry check and plan update.
- **Soft ending:** Show Quade's recorded 3–2 result, Jasper's nonbinding spoken
  invitation, Fiora's affirmative answer, and the pair physically beginning
  supper together. Do not end before both the contest and date are resolved.
