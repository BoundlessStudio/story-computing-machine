# Canon brief

## Hard constraints

- **The story must occur somewhere in the one continuous physical world, even
  if neither story nor characters know its wider history.** A new fantasy
  locale may be left in an unnamed era, but it must not be presented as a
  separate planet, plane, or disconnected fantasy cosmos. Earth, Ravel, and the
  Glass-Sea world are era-bound labels for that same world, not interchangeable
  names for generic settings. (`universe/premise.md`, **The deep-time shared
  world**; `universe/locations.md`, **The world across eras**)
- **If magic appears, only life may use it.** A nonliving blade, ward, scoring
  device, or arena fixture may store, focus, transmit, or shape magic supplied
  through living agency; it may not independently cast or choose unless the
  story establishes that it is alive and treats that personhood seriously.
  (`universe/rules.md`, **Living agency and magic**; `universe/style-guide.md`,
  **Personhood and moral consequence**)
- **“Magic,” “technology,” and “divinity” are cultural descriptions, not
  objective cosmic categories.** A viewpoint culture may call a duel mechanism
  magic, but the story should give it a concrete local operation rather than
  claiming that all power works that way. (`universe/rules.md`, **System
  categories**; `universe/glossary.md`, **Magic, technology, and divinity**)
- **Neither desire, sacrifice, pain, nor consent is a universal fuel or magical
  law.** If attraction, honest intention, ritual stakes, or willing
  participation affects the duel mechanically, that must be a defined local
  rule used consistently. Consent's ethical weight remains mandatory even when
  it has no metaphysical effect. (`universe/rules.md`, **Costs, desire, and
  local mechanisms**)
- **Names are not universally magical.** Any oath, challenge-name, title,
  true-name clause, or identity-bound weapon must be established as a rare
  local legal, cultural, magical, or technological mechanism, not an automatic
  property of names. (`universe/rules.md`, **Names and identity binding**)
- **The romance must preserve personhood, agency, and affirmative choice.** The
  prompt's “right to take” language cannot make either duelist property or make
  the date an enforceable transfer of a person. Both leads are adults; the
  woman must remain an active, skilled participant with an independent goal;
  the contest is nonlethal; and the ending must be a freely chosen date or its
  unmistakable beginning. (`universe/style-guide.md`, **Personhood and moral
  consequence** and **Audience and content boundaries**;
  `stories/the-courtesy-of-blades/00-prompt.md`, **Story controls** and
  **Assumptions**)
- **Violence must remain non-graphic and consequence-aware.** The story may
  stage a brisk fantasy duel, but cannot glamorize coercion or linger on
  instructional injury detail. (`universe/style-guide.md`, **Audience and
  content boundaries**)
- **Do not create continuity merely through resemblance.** Shared imagery,
  artifacts, titles, portals, names, or weapon forms do not prove a link to an
  existing system or identity. (`universe/rules.md`, **Artifacts, motifs, and
  folklore**; `universe/style-guide.md`, **Continuity and evidence discipline**)

## Useful established context

- The universe deliberately permits warm romantic comedy, fantasy action, and
  small ordinary lives. This story does not need a cosmological reveal, a
  crossover, a catastrophe, or a supernatural hint beyond what its local
  premise needs. (`universe/style-guide.md`, **Tonal range, scale, and
  connection**; `universe/premise.md`, **Memory, myth, and recoverable truth**)
- Cultures may understand the world incompletely and may disagree about their
  own institutions. That supports a local courtship custom whose participants
  and exasperated observer understand its meaning differently, provided the
  narrative does not elevate one culture's explanation into a universal law.
  (`universe/premise.md`, **Knowledge and connection**)
- Many distinct systems can coexist, interact, imitate one another, or remain
  inactive in a given era. The duel may therefore be mundane, magical, or a
  local hybrid without requiring a universal magic system. (`universe/rules.md`,
  **System categories**)
- The modern-like era is specifically a low-magic trough; the Glass-Sea era is
  much later; Ravel's placement relative to either remains unresolved. An
  unnamed fantasy-era pocket avoids unnecessary chronology commitments. If a
  named era is selected later, its established limits must be honored.
  (`universe/timeline.md`, **Established era order**;
  `universe/glossary.md`, **Modern-like era**, **Ravel**, and **Glass-Sea
  world**)
- The authoritative character and faction files currently establish no
  recurring person, organization, courtship culture, dueling school, noble
  house, or adjudicating body for this premise. This is an omission, not proof
  that such things do or do not exist. (`universe/characters.md`, **Recurring
  characters**; `universe/factions.md`, **Factions and cultures**)

## Conflicts or ambiguity

- **No canon-source contradiction was found. Canon status: READY; no canon
  ruling is required before planning.** `stories/INDEX.md` contains no row with
  canon `yes`, so no finalized story prose has authority at the story tier.
  Candidate, in-progress, abandoned, and historical works must not be mined as
  established setting facts. (`universe/README.md`, **Authority order**;
  `stories/INDEX.md`, **Story index**)
- **Prompt-language tension, not a canon conflict:** “the winner gets to take
  the other on a date” can sound possessive. The prompt itself prohibits
  treating either person as property and requires affirmative consent. Planning
  should make the prize a mutually recognized permission, invitation,
  face-saving form, or comparable local meaning whose romantic outcome each
  adult can still decline. (`stories/the-courtesy-of-blades/00-prompt.md`,
  **Story controls** and **Completion tests**;
  `universe/style-guide.md`, **Personhood and moral consequence**)
- **Terminology ambiguity:** a local character may confidently call a practice
  “magic,” an ancestral law, or sacred custom, but that belief is not an
  omniscient cosmological ruling. The mechanism and its local reach should be
  legible on the page. (`universe/premise.md`, **Knowledge and connection**;
  `universe/glossary.md`, **Magic, technology, and divinity**)
- **Provisional-material check:** no relevant `PROVISIONAL` entry appears in the
  authoritative universe notes. The name registry is non-canon production
  memory, and every indexed story is presently non-canon; none can settle the
  setting, ritual, or cast. (`universe/README.md`, **Authority order**;
  `stories/NAMES.md`, **Rules**; `stories/INDEX.md`, **Story index**)

## Unknowns

Each item below is absent from authoritative canon. It must not be described as
already established for the universe; a narrow local answer appears safe.

- **Unknown:** the exact era and chronology of this story. Safe to leave the era
  unnamed and avoid Galactic Cycle coordinates.
- **Unknown:** the locale, political authority, social class structure,
  profession of either lead, and identity of the frustrated speaker. No canon
  faction or geography answers these questions.
- **Unknown:** whether formal nonlethal duels exist anywhere, who may issue or
  accept one, what counts as victory, who officiates, how surrender works, and
  what safety equipment or healing is available.
- **Unknown:** whether any culture uses a duel as courtship, why these two
  adults need that ritual, whether its meaning is shared by the wider culture,
  and what social consequences attach to refusing or losing.
- **Unknown:** the weapons, fighting styles, arena, magical effects, injury
  limits, and interruption or complication. “Blades” in the working title does
  not establish a canon weapon system.
- **Unknown:** whether the duel is entirely mundane or uses a local magical or
  technological system. No cost, power source, consent behavior, scoring
  system, or healing rule can be assumed.
- **Unknown:** naming conventions, gender customs beyond the prompt's specified
  woman duelist, attitudes toward romance, and dating etiquette in the chosen
  pocket.
- **Unknown:** all character-facing names. The prompt supplies roles and
  genders, not names; collision evaluation must be repeated after the plan
  chooses them.

## Safe invention space

- Create one clearly local settlement, estate, academy, regiment, guildhall,
  festival, courtyard, training yard, or comparable contained venue without
  attaching it to Earth, Ravel, or the Glass-Sea era.
- Create a local, nonlethal challenge protocol with visible safeguards: limited
  target zones, blunted or warded weapons, first-touch or point scoring,
  surrender and stoppage rules, an empowered official, and credible injury
  response. Keep it a practice of this community or pair, not “the ancient law
  of all duels.”
- Create a local courtship convention that makes the duel emotionally coherent
  for both leads while preserving a genuine right to refuse. It may concern
  equality, being taken seriously, earned vulnerability, face-saving, family
  expectations, professional rivalry, or mutual proof, so long as the story
  dramatizes rather than universalizes it.
- Invent a small cast of unrelated adults and a frustrated friend, second, or
  official. No canon recurring characters need to appear and no crossover is
  implied.
- Invent one bounded complication that interrupts or complicates the duel and
  forces cooperation, provided it does not nullify the contest, choose the
  romantic outcome for them, or require new global cosmology.
- If magic is useful, invent one narrow arena, weapon, ward, oath, or scoring
  mechanism with a stated living user, operation, limit, and consequence. A
  living construct is possible but expands the personhood burden and is not
  needed by the prompt; a passive tool directed by a living practitioner is
  simpler and safer. (`universe/rules.md`, **Living agency and magic**)
- Invent ordinary local calendars, foods, clothing, buildings, etiquette, and
  date activities. Avoid formal Galactic Cycle placement unless the story
  actually needs it. (`universe/timeline.md`, **Universal chronology**)
- Record every setting fact that could recur beyond this story as a proposal in
  `06-canon-delta.md`; invention here is permission to propose, not canon
  promotion. (`universe/README.md`, **Authority order**;
  `universe/style-guide.md`, **Continuity and evidence discipline**)

## Name constraints

- The prompt introduces **no proper character-facing name**, so there is no
  prompt-required reuse and no exact collision to preserve. Every planned full
  name, given name, mononym, nickname, alias, username, title used as a name,
  or named person-like entity must be new and checked case-insensitively against
  the complete registry. Existing entries remain reserved even when their
  source is candidate, abandoned, or archive record; no registry row is marked
  released. (`stories/NAMES.md`, **Rules** and registry table)
- Avoid the registry's unresolved collision forms entirely: **Lena**, **Mara**,
  **Nisha**, **Pell**, and **Voss**. Those forms already refer to distinct archive record
  identities and are warnings, not available names. (`stories/NAMES.md`,
  **Known unresolved name collisions**)
- Watch the registry's dense close-match clusters when proposing fantasy names:
  **Leth / Letha / Lumen**; **Ilya / Irix / Ivo**; **Olyra / Oren / Orrin /
  Osric / Osket / Othran**; **Mara / Marcus / Mateo / Maude / Mae / Merrit /
  Miriam**; **Rana / Rae / Rhea**; **Sabine / Sebastian / Seris / Siva /
  Solstice / Sorel / Sula**; **Tair / Talvi / Tamsin / Tavra / Tess / Tilda**;
  and the surname-like forms **Vale / Varen / Velez / Venn / Vey / Voss**.
  A new spelling that is only one sound or letter away may still be an easily
  confused form and should be rejected or explicitly justified.
- Existing aliases must be treated as reserved names too. Particularly relevant
  to a fantasy story are **Bash**, **Court Ghost**, **Daughter**, **Goddess**,
  **Mother**, **Nyr**, **the Grief-Clad**, **Princess Olyra**, **Queen Olyra**,
  **Regent**, **Saint**, **Sleepless Majesty**, **First Monarch**, **the Keeper
  Below**, and magical identities **Kiteglass**, **Mothlight**, and
  **Pulsewire**. Do not assume that using only an alias avoids a collision.
  (`stories/NAMES.md`, registry table)
- Role nouns may stay lowercase and generic, but a role used as a stable
  character label becomes a character-facing name and needs validation. This is
  especially important for the mediator or official: **Captain**, **Commander
  Kest**, **Adjudicator Mott**, **Assessor Havil**, **Examiner Dovek**,
  **Instructor Bexel**, **Night Clerk**, **Rollcaller**, and **Vicar Estrel**
  already have reserved character-facing forms or components. Prefer an
  ordinary role description plus a fresh personal name.
- Any deliberate reuse would need the plan to identify whether the identity is
  the same or distinct, explain why the reuse materially matters, and state how
  readers can distinguish the characters. This prompt supplies no such reason,
  so the default is no reuse. (`stories/NAMES.md`, **Rules**)

## Required checks after drafting

- Verify that all three beats appear in substance: the two reciprocal duel/date
  conditions, the suggestion to skip the duel and simply date, and both
  duelists' glare; then verify that the duel and the freely chosen date both
  receive a real resolution. (`stories/the-courtesy-of-blades/00-prompt.md`,
  **Story controls** and **Completion tests**)
- Audit every description of the duel's stakes. No line should imply ownership,
  compulsory romance, loss of refusal rights, or that victory by itself creates
  consent. Both adults must retain agency before, during, and after the contest.
- Confirm that the woman has her own concrete goal, demonstrates skill and
  choice on page, and is not merely the viewpoint character's prize. Confirm
  that both leads' insistence on the ritual becomes emotionally and culturally
  intelligible rather than only a punch line.
- Check that the duel remains nonlethal, that safety and stoppage rules operate
  consistently, and that any injury or threat is non-graphic and carries
  believable consequences.
- If magic appears, trace every effect to living agency; check that nonliving
  blades or arena devices never act as independent magic users. If an object is
  alive, verify that the story establishes this and respects its personhood.
- Check that any effect powered by attraction, truth, pain, sacrifice, names,
  oaths, or consent is explicitly local, mechanically consistent, and not
  narrated as a universal metaphysical rule.
- Search for Earth, Ravel, Glass-Sea, Gate, portal, dimension, plane, and
  Galactic Cycle claims. Any use must agree with the established one-world and
  era-order constraints; otherwise keep the setting unplaced.
- Search for language that turns the invented courtship ritual, duel law,
  faction, venue, or magic into a global or ancient universal fact. Narrow such
  claims to the local community and record reusable additions as proposed canon
  in `06-canon-delta.md`.
- Extract every final character-facing name and alias, including titles used as
  names and named person-like entities. Recheck exact forms, aliases, close
  spellings, reversals, and sound-alikes against `stories/NAMES.md`; document
  any intentional reuse. The primary coordinator must register planned names,
  reconcile the registry after final review, and run
  `.agents/skills/story-name-validation/scripts/check-story-names.ps1 -Story
  the-courtesy-of-blades` at the required workflow gates.
- Confirm that no candidate, abandoned, or historical story detail has been treated
  as canon and that no resemblance alone creates a crossover, shared artifact,
  or shared institution.
