# Proposed canon delta

This file records reusable facts proposed by *The Courtesy of Blades*. Every
item below remains non-canon unless the user explicitly approves promotion and
the approved facts are added to `universe/`.

## New characters or character facts

- **Fiora Halwick** (`Fiora Halwick`; `Fiora`) is an adult woman, an
  accomplished Lacebridge fencer, an assistant instructor at its civic blade
  yard, and the holder of a trial badge. She earned appointment as head
  instructor of East Weir's yard and developed a low pivot while planning
  lessons for its constrained strip. On the evening before leaving upriver,
  she drives the rematch, scores two points, truthfully self-calls Jasper's
  earlier deciding touch, keeps her East Weir post, and freely accepts one
  supper with him.
- **Jasper Renlow** (`Jasper Renlow`; `Jasper`) is an adult river-patrol
  fencer,
  Fiora's longtime training rival, and her mutual romantic interest. In the
  earlier trial he self-called Fiora's winning touch; in the rematch he fences
  at full effort, wins three to two, accepts Fiora's costly call without
  yielding the point back, and makes a nonbinding invitation that leaves her
  career and departure untouched.
- **Quade** (`Quade`) is an adult mutual friend and qualified Lacebridge bout
  official. He inspects equipment, controls the strip and spectators, conducts
  safety and consent checks, adjudicates visible touches, records results, and
  does not decide an unseen touch order or either duelist's romantic choice.
- Fiora and Jasper end the story as acknowledged mutual romantic interests
  whose first riverside supper has begun. A future East Weir visit is invited
  only as a possibility tied to further fencing practice, not as a settled
  promise.

## New locations

- **Lacebridge** is a riverside town in an otherwise unnamed era of the shared
  physical world. It has a public civic blade yard and evening supper stalls
  along the river.
- **Lacebridge civic blade yard** has a marked fencing strip, a spectator rail,
  equipment racks and safety gear, an official result ledger, and space for
  trainees and neighbors to watch public bouts.
- **East Weir** is a neighboring upriver town reachable from Lacebridge by
  hired cart. Its blade yard has at least one short practice strip constrained
  by two pillars, and it has appointed Fiora Halwick as head instructor.

## New factions or cultural facts

- Lacebridge supports a municipal-scale blade-yard institution with assistant
  instructors, qualified officials, pupils, public trials, recorded match
  results, and a badge awarded from a trial bout.
- A local river patrol employs trained fencers such as Jasper Renlow.
- Lacebridge's yard permits a **courtesy bout** with a mutually negotiated
  personal stake. For a stake involving courtship or company, victory grants
  only the right to make the first invitation and choose its venue; the other
  person may decline, and no later company, contact, career choice, travel
  choice, second date, or dessert is owed.
- Ordinary local color includes dark practice coats with padded scoring panels,
  evening crowds of trainees and neighbors, hired-cart travel between the
  towns, and riverside stalls serving folded warm breads with vegetables,
  browned onions, and sharp cheese.

## New rules, capabilities, or costs

- A Lacebridge courtesy bout is a mundane, nonlethal, first-to-three contest
  fought with flexible blunted blades whose edges are dusted with chalk.
- Valid targets are dark padded torso and upper-arm panels. Head, throat, hands,
  and joints are out of bounds, and eye and throat guards are required.
- The official inspects blade flex and damage, refreshes chalk, checks the
  fencers' hands and vision, may call halt or no-point, accepts surrender, and
  may stop the bout regardless of a participant's preference.
- The official judges clear visible touches. When both fencers land and body
  position hides the order, the fencers must truthfully self-call which touch
  landed first; the official records a decisive self-call in the yard ledger.
- Courtesy-bout stakes and truthful self-calls have local social and procedural
  force only. They are not magical, do not bind identity, and cannot create
  romantic consent.

## Timeline events

- Six months before the story's rematch, a public Lacebridge trial ended Fiora
  three, Jasper two after Jasper self-called Fiora's touch as first. Fiora
  received the trial badge. Later gossip misrepresented Jasper's honest call as
  romantic pity, and his failure to challenge that account allowed the insult
  to persist.
- On the evening before Fiora's dawn departure for East Weir, Fiora and Jasper
  fought an official rematch. Fiora led two to one; Jasper tied the score; in
  the deciding exchange his touch landed half a beat before hers, and Fiora
  self-called the order. Jasper won three to two, and Quade recorded both the
  result and Fiora's call beside the earlier trial entry.
- After the rematch, Jasper invited Fiora to one riverside supper, Fiora
  accepted because she wanted to, and their date began. Her departure and
  head-instructor appointment remained unchanged.

## New glossary terms or aliases

- **Courtesy bout:** Lacebridge blade-yard term for the local nonlethal bout and
  its limited, mutually negotiated stake.
- **Self-call:** A fencer's truthful declaration of touch order when the
  official cannot see which member of a double touch landed first.
- **Trial badge:** A Lacebridge blade-yard award associated with a recorded
  public trial result.
- New aliases: none.
