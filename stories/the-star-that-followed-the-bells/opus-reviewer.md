# Opus review: The Star That Followed the Bells

Independent audit for a later editing pass. It is not a `review.md` verdict,
does not change canon state, and does not authorize edits by itself.

## Verdict

- Dialogue: REVISE — two Ground failures (a rebuttal based on something the page never shows, and a bell count that contradicts the flock) plus a final exchange that ends on a tidy antithesis.
- Prose: PASS — concrete, controlled, no em dashes. Minor issues: a negation/fragment tic, heavy use of one-line paragraphs, and two muddled staging beats.
- Continuity and prompt: PASS — delivers every prompt element at about 2,880 words, and the ship never gets its home route back. Only small naming and object slips.
- Edit scope: targeted-dialogue
- Existing review.md said: PASS; "Dialogue: PASS". I disagree: it missed the unsupported claim at L179 and the wrong count at L109.

The story's most important conversation is non-verbal: Nali's bells against the ship's bands, floods and harmonics. That thread is coherent and holds up well: strike, pause, repeat, interrupt, hold. The ship then talks over its own satellites and has to relearn the silence it was taught. The human dialogue is radio coordination between two working shepherds. Its terseness is mostly earned by the emergency, and the two are told apart by role: Nali gives orders and runs tests, while Mavek reads back, counts, objects and jokes. The failures are local. Nali rebuts Mavek with evidence the reader never saw. Mavek's bell count breaks the story's own arithmetic. Mavek's readback habit spreads to Nali and into the climax. The final exchange swaps plain speech for a cryptic line and a neat "For them. Not for us." The edit pass should fix the two Ground failures, thin the readbacks, and end on plain work and plain attention. It should not add feeling-speeches or wonder the two haven't earned.

## Edit guardrails

- Prompt elements that must survive: a shepherd; an injured living starship grazing on an aurora above summer pastures; the ship cannot remember its destination; it has lost the small satellites it treats as its flock; it takes the bells as an offer to guide it home. The story must stay complete and broadly accessible, within 2,500–4,000 words (currently about 2,880). The active profile is `prospective-2026-08-21`.
- POV: close third, past tense, through Nali. The narration never enters the ship or the satellites. The ship does not recover its white route. Nali offers only tested, local guidance ("no promise beyond sight").
- Story arithmetic the edits must keep: 83 sheep in all (82 counted plus the lame ewe); 76 inside and 7 out, the lame ewe among the 7; the lame ewe's left forefoot; altitude readings 8.1 → 6.9 (lost) → 7.2, then the climb at 8 and 10 km.
- Bell convention: a completed pair, a pause and a repeat mean "clear here". An interruption or incomplete pair means "hold". The ship later uses the same spacing to call its satellites. The satellites join by choice and do not dock; this is where the personhood and consent point lives (style guide, Personhood protection, LOCKED).
- No universe entries are touched. Ruskala, the ship's biology and the bell practice are all story-local. No water infrastructure is present: the spring is natural and the trough is for salt.
- Keep the radio register as the shepherds' working speech. Do not turn Nali or Mavek into talkers or philosophers. Keep Mavek's pauses (L43, L269) as her way of reacting.

## Dialogue findings

### D1 · Blocking · Rebuttal cites a copy the page never shows

- Where: L169–L181, salt-call test and the debate that follows
- Text:
> The ship did not turn away from Mavek. Instead the broken route blazed across its projection again.
> Place mattered. Their meanings did not.
> "Or it can't hear the calls are different."
> "It copied the difference."
- Problem: Ground/knowledge and uptake. Nali answers Mavek's rival hypothesis with an observation that is absent from the page. The salt call produced no copy of anything, only the broken route blazing, the same response as before. The only copying shown is the two-band answer to the counting pair at L153, which proves the ship hears spacing, not that it "copied the difference" between two calls. The reader cannot recover when this happened, and this is the key deduction that justifies the whole spacing method. There is also a double tell: L173 states the conclusion in narration before Nali says it aloud at L175. Her "go there" has a soft referent too: toward the bell, or along the route?
- Fix: Smallest edit: at L171, show the ship answering the salt call in its own uneven rhythm before the route blazes, so Nali's rebuttal points at something the reader saw. Illustrative only: Its underside flickered the salt call back, uneven, before the broken route blazed again. The alternative is to leave L171 alone and have Nali cite the evidence she actually has, the two bands at L153 that kept the gap between her notes. Cut L173 so the rule is reached in the exchange instead of announced before it. Anchor "go there" with a gesture at the blazing route. Keep Mavek's rival hypothesis, her "Then stop changing calls," and "Nali almost argued."

### D2 · Blocking · "That's thirty bells" contradicts the belled flock

- Where: L107–L113, muffling after the stampede
- Text:
> "That's thirty bells."
> Instead the eighty-third bell knocked twice from the sedge beyond the upper spring
> They passed her downhill, seven bells battering without pattern.
> "I have seventy-six inside."
- Problem: Ground/continuity. The story counts its sheep by their bells. There is an "eighty-third bell"; seven loose sheep carry seven bells; "Every bell on the range rang at once"; "the last clear bell stopped"; and at dawn "each clapper" goes back to its bell. With 76 sheep in the pen, Mavek's figure is simply wrong. Mavek is the story's counter and the story's texture is counting, so the slip lands on exactly the character who shouldn't make it. The objection itself is good and in character. Only the number is broken.
- Fix: Make the number match the pen ("seventy-six") or make it non-numeric (every bell in the pen). Keep "Start with the leaders." and "Mavek did not repeat the number." Do not switch to bellwethers-only belling, which would force changes at L13, L81, L99, L115 and L325.

### D3 · Major · Final exchange closes on a cryptic line and a neat antithesis

- Where: L319–L323, dawn by the pen
- Text:
> "Weather's clearing east," Mavek said.
> "It cleared early."
> "For them. Not for us. We're moving off this strip."
- Problem: Ground/referent, plus a Punchline Patch that leans toward the Philosopher. Read literally, "It cleared early" contradicts Mavek's report that the weather is clearing now. The reader decodes it only through Mavek's reply ("For them"), so the reply exists to explain Nali's subtext. "For them. Not for us." is a balanced last word that states the story's contrast (their clear sky, our work) instead of letting the scorched strip and the restored bells show it. It is the collection's default closing move. The practical pivot ("We're moving off this strip") is good and is earned by L325.
- Fix: Keep Mavek's weather report and the move off the scorched strip. Ground Nali's line in something she saw last night or is looking at now, so it neither contradicts "clearing" nor needs decoding. Cut "For them. Not for us." and let Mavek answer with the work, or with one of her silences, instead of a gloss. Add no thematic line. Illustrative only: Nali, still facing east: "East was clear last night." Mavek let that stand. "We're moving off this strip."

### D4 · Minor · The "Confirm" readback spreads past its useful places

- Where: L49, L89, L127, L147, L209, L259, L289–L293, radio exchanges throughout
- Text:
> "Confirm no bell. Closing west."
> Nali watched the blank points. "Confirm eighty-three."
> "Not joined yet."
> "Confirm three located. Not joined."
- Problem: Mavek's readbacks are a deliberate trait and pay off at L209–L213. Seven instances, though, make the talk sound like dispatch procedure. At L259 Nali adopts the habit, blurring the one verbal marker that belongs to Mavek. At the reunion (L289–L293), the exchange becomes count, correction and readback, a procedure routine at the moment of wonder.
- Fix: Keep L89 and L127 (working radio under pressure), L147 (it sets up Mavek's joke; see D5) and L209, which the "Confirm silence." joke needs. Cut Nali's readback at L259; while she watches the points, a plain acknowledgment or silence will do. At L289 keep Mavek's "Three," because counting the ship's flock like sheep is the right instinct, but drop the readback at L293. Nali's qualifier can stay, or become something other than a correction. Consider trimming L49 to the action.

### D5 · Minor · Bell-placement exchange runs as a tennis match with a capper

- Where: L137–L149, sending Mavek to the north cairn
- Text:
> Nali glanced at the ship's pinched wound. "Drop the bell. Don't run with it."
> "Confirm drop, don't run. That's poor general advice."
> "For tonight."
- Problem: Seven evenly weighted one-liners alternate, with one weigh and one glance as the only beats. Mavek's joke is in character: she is the one being sent to stand under the wounded ship. Nali's "For tonight." is a scope-correcting comeback that gives her the last word. That is the collection's quip-and-correct rhythm, and it borrows Mavek's humor.
- Fix: Keep Mavek's joke. Cut Nali's capper, or replace it with an action (she's already turning back to the spring) or a plain reply that doesn't correct. One physical beat inside the run would break the alternation.

### D6 · Minor · The ewe rings on cue for "Apparently we can."

- Where: L227–L235, the interruption test
- Text:
> "We can't make that sheep ring on order."
> The bell sounded again as the ewe scratched its neck against the wall.
> "Apparently we can."
- Problem: A mild Punchline Patch. The accidental second ring is realistic and useful, because it supplies the repeat test. The untagged comeback, though, exists only to land the joke. By alternation it is Nali's, although it reads as Mavek's humor (Attribution/Identical Voices at the joke).
- Fix: Keep the second ring. Cut "Apparently we can." and go straight to "Nali completed nothing." If a line is wanted there, tag it and make it Mavek's.

### D7 · Minor · "It talked over them" repeats what the narration just showed

- Where: L275–L279, the satellites' first answers; staging at L51
- Text:
> It sent its ring request over both replies. The nearer tone withdrew.
> "It talked over them," Nali said.
> "I saw the first leave."
> This time cold light spread over the grass in front of Nali
- Problem: An inert exchange. Narration and dialogue report the same event back to back, and Mavek's reply adds nothing. Her "saw", like her "Three" at L289, depends on her seeing the projection from the cairn. The story only ever places the projection "in front of Nali", so this is "could have seen" promoted to "saw".
- Fix: Keep Nali's line; it is her recognition that the ship is repeating the mistake she broke. Trim L275 to what she perceives (the ship brightening, a tone dropping out) so her line isn't a replay. Establish once, for example at L243 when the ship projects the local slope, that the projection is now large enough to be seen from the cairn. Give Mavek something her position gives her. Illustrative only: "Low one's dropped off my set."

## Prose findings

### P1 · Minor · Negation and fragment triplets used for emphasis

- Where: L29, L117–L119, L131, L173, L247, L311
- Text:
> Not rose. Not turned. Held.
> Not home. Not destination. Only this way was clear as far as Nali could see.
> It had not merely lost a direction. It was calling others with the wrong one.
- Problem: The same emphatic device ("Not X. Not Y. Z." / "not merely X. It was Y.") recurs at most turning points. "Not rose" is ungrammatical. L119 repeats L117, and L247 restates the bell convention the scene has just demonstrated.
- Fix: Cut L119 (L117 already says it) or make it grammatical. Turn L247 into one plain sentence or cut it. Keep at most one or two of these across the story.

### P2 · Minor · Staging muddles at two signal beats

- Where: L199–L201, the first spaced pairs; L243, the ridge trials
- Text:
> Then she put both hands flat on the bell and did nothing.
> The ship began to answer over her second strike.
> the tear easing open rather than pinching shut
- Problem: L199 narrates the whole two-pair sequence, ending with her hands flat. L201 then rewinds: the ship spoke over her second strike and she slapped the rim. The reader can't tell whether she completed the pairs. At L243, "easing open" reads as the wound getting worse, the opposite of what's meant (higher air relieves it, per L245).
- Fix: Put the overlap inside the sequence (second pair, the ship comes in over it, rim slap, stop) and drop or move "hands flat". Replace "easing open" with a relief image that matches L103 and L245, such as the hide slackening around the split.

### P3 · Minor · One-sentence narration paragraphs as the default pulse

- Where: about 30 instances, including L19–L21, L55, L63, L101, L155–L157, L203, L215, L249, L301, L307
- Text:
> The ship's route line brightened.
> Above them, the starship turned too.
- Problem: They are earned in the stampede (L75–L83) and the waiting run (L191–L195). Elsewhere, isolated lines chop connected action into beats, against the style guide's paragraph default.
- Fix: Merge the non-climactic ones into adjacent paragraphs (L55 into L53, L63 into L65, L157 with L159, L301 with L303). Keep the stampede and the waiting runs as they are.

## Continuity and prompt findings

### C1 · Minor · Small naming and object slips

- Where: L137 and L243 (cairn); L77 and L241 (radio display); L105 and L325 (clappers)
- Text:
> "Stand by the north cairn."
> while Mavek remained at the lower cairn
> The storm set recovered its echo.

- Problem: "North cairn" becomes "the lower cairn", which reads like a second cairn. The radio display becomes the "storm set" without being introduced by that name. The clappers were wrapped with felt, wool and a scarf, never removed, so they cannot be "restored".
- Fix: Use one name for the cairn and one for the display. At L325, say the wraps came off the clappers.
