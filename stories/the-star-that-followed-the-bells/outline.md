# Outline

## Story

- Premise and central promise: Bringing sheep off the exposed Ruskala summer range, Nali Sern finds a wounded living starship feeding an auroral filament through its vanes. Its route memory and three autonomous satellites are missing; it mistakes the flock's moving bells for an offer to lead it home. Resolve that dangerous error, account for the satellites, stabilize the ship, and establish a truthful next destination without recovering its interstellar route.
- Focal pressure or attachment: Nali must pen the sheep, treat a lame ewe, and beat incoming weather. She will help a distressed being but not abandon her animals or pretend to knowledge she lacks. The ship needs lift and reunion; damaged memory makes correlation feel like recognition.
- Counterforce or complication: A routine gathering movement draws the ship into air that compresses its wound. Auroral charge rings every livestock bell and panics sheep toward a broken slope. Cloud, animal noise, and delayed orbital replies make each test incomplete; Mavek Tural can assist by radio, but neither shepherd is an expert translator.
- POV, distance, and information limit: Close third-person past through Nali, who reads hoof traffic, bell spacing, weather, and response better than projected diagrams. The narration never enters ship or satellites; intention remains provisional until signals and choices repeat under changed conditions.
- Governing movement and time shape: One dusk-to-dawn emergency in three communication cycles: mistaken following, controlled comparison, consent-based rendezvous. Herd work and overhead signals repeatedly interrupt each other. Keep the turn private and procedural; end with Nali resuming altered summer work, not presenting proof.
- Speculative rule or ordinary-world constraint: The organism feeds by drawing charged auroral particles across living field vanes. A torn lift lobe and adjacent route-memory tissue trap it between limits: below seven kilometres pressure worsens the tear; above sixteen it cannot hold attitude alone. Three satellites normally choose separated positions that brace its field, map hazards, and relay routes; they may refuse a corrupted summons. Auroral color shows load and attention, magnetic pulses vibrate metal and copy rhythm, radio harmonics carry identity and timing, and projected symbols expose damaged associations without reliable labels. No mode translates intention. A complete request, deliberate pause, witnessed repeat, and second pause becomes a local consent convention; overlap or interruption means hold.

## Voice

- Narrative texture: Practical close narration notices animal distribution, slope, wind, hands, and maintenance before interpreting light. Tighten during herd danger; lengthen only for pattern comparison. Keep the aurora material, not mystical.
- Conversational texture: Nali and Mavek use brief radio calls, counts, location checks, and unfinished corrections amid weather and livestock noise. Ship contact stays layered, repeated, and pause-dependent, never fluent speech.
- Rhetorical ownership: Nali reasons through spacing and animal response; Mavek through thresholds, missing bodies, and logistics. The ship offers correlations and damaged diagrams; satellites express delay, approach, or refusal through distinct harmonics.
- Pressure behavior: Nali becomes clipped but keeps listening; Mavek repeats confirmations. The ship floods channels, then learns to pause. Satellites withhold synchronization until its request remains consistent.
- Anti-default: Avoid a lyrical shepherd-philosopher decoding a humanlike alien, cute one-to-one sheep machinery, polished dialogue metaphors, or local skill inflated into cosmic expertise.

## Beats

1. During the late count, Nali finds a lame ewe and starts toward the stone pen. A low auroral strand reveals the starship. Its pulse rings scattered bells while a projection shows one central body, three absent points, and an unfinished route. Nali's gathering movement brightens the route symbol.
2. Treating converging bells as a corridor, the ship descends. Its wound darkens; charge rings every clapper and panics sheep toward the broken slope. Nali saves animals and footing while Mavek secures the lower gate. Another correction is misread before Nali muffles nearby bells and the ship holds.
3. With sheep contained and Mavek treating the ewe, they place two removable bells at controlled distances. Repeating one rhythm from opposite positions, then changing rhythm without moving, shows that the ship follows geometry while retaining the same destination association. The bells locate animals; they do not contain a route.
4. Nali stops incomplete sequences rather than answering the implied destination. The shepherds build the narrow pause-and-repeat convention, marking only lower air as unsafe, sky above the ridge as locally clear, and the distant route as unknown. Weather and incidental bells interrupt a trial; the ship honors the missing completion and waits.
5. The ship changes its corrupted home summons to a stable-orbit request with the following route blank. Three delayed harmonics answer: one satellite probes nearby, one holds high and relays, and the far-side member waits for repetition. Distinct bearings account for all three and reveal deliberate separation from the unsafe call.
6. The satellites choose staggered positions and brace the lift lobe without docking. Nali confirms only the local ascent corridor. The ship chooses their intact high rendezvous orbit, climbs from the pressure band, and leaves deeper memory unresolved.
7. At dawn, Nali and Mavek recount the sheep, adjust the ewe's pace, move grazing away from a discharge-scorched strip, and reset the working bells.
