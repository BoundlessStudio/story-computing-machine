# Outline

## Story

- Premise and central promise: During an evening count on unnamed summer
  pastures, an unnamed shepherd hears an extra answer among the sheep bells and
  finds an immense injured living starship feeding from the aurora. Its torn
  sensory fringe has cost it both its destination and the seven small living
  satellites it calls its flock. Because its own route-guides signal through
  moving harmonics, it takes her bells for a deliberate offer and follows.
  Fulfill the promise by making her retract the destination she never knowingly
  offered, then turn the bells from a false road into a call the satellites can
  answer. Leave the vessel reunited, able to recover its route, and choosing its
  own departure; its memory injury never makes its trust or grief unreal.
- Intended reader experience: Begin in tactile pastoral quiet, let an ordinary
  act of counting open into sky-scale wonder, and preserve a gentle vein of
  comedy in two expert herders making incompatible assumptions. Add unease as
  the ship follows too low. Let each returning answer bring intimate relief,
  then leave dawn with awe and unfinished ordinary work rather than triumph.
- Generating force:
  - Mode: Plot-led cumulative flock-count. Every movement begins with the
    shepherd interpreting an absence or extra answer at a larger scale. A
    straight route fails; a widening field of sound lets separated voices find
    one another.
  - Focal attachment or contradiction: The shepherd is attached to being
    someone lost creatures can follow, yet care here requires admitting she
    cannot name the destination. The starship retains the social procedure for
    accepting a guide but not the context that would distinguish its call from
    her bells. Each recognizes real flock-work in the other without making the
    two flocks interchangeable.
  - Plot-led wants and useful limit, if applicable: The shepherd immediately
    wants to settle her sheep, reach the lower fold, and stop the vessel from
    descending after them; she also refuses to abandon an injured intelligence.
    Her useful flaw is practiced certainty: she says “follow” before learning
    that the ship hears a promise of home. The starship wants its seven
    satellites and destination back while feeding and protecting its wound;
    injury limits memory, altitude, and signal discrimination.
- Counterforce or complication:
  - Source: There is no antagonist. Bells needed to keep sheep out of dark
    gullies draw the ship into a low, rigid line; muffling them makes both flocks
    feel lost. Its wounded magnetic sense smears satellite replies into the
    sounds below, and its auroral feed thins toward dawn.
  - Credible inside logic, if a person: The starship is capable of asking,
    revising, refusing, and choosing. It accepts because mobile chimes genuinely
    signal guides in its experience, not because a contract compels it. The
    shepherd's answer is careless but compassionate, and she corrects it.
  - Pressure or change created: A straight drive lowers the ship, reopens its
    wound, and scatters sheep; silence protects neither flock. The shepherd must
    replace false certainty with a bounded offer before the aurora fades.
- Narrative design:
  - POV, tense, distance, and limitation: Close third-person past through the
    shepherd, intimate with her auditory map, weather sense, sore hands, and
    practical misreadings. The vessel first appears as an impossible change in
    the count and speaks by resonating iron bells into layered words. She can
    infer only from action and speech; do not enter alien minds or make her an
    instant expert.
  - Time shape and compression: One continuous auroral night from the last
    evening count to morning. Render contact, the mistaken “follow,” dangerous
    descent, correction, and change from leading to gathering in scene. Treat
    the first and last satellite returns fully and compress the middle as a
    distinct accumulation. Do not leave the pasture for backstory.
  - Information and reveal strategy: Show the wound and auroral feeding before
    naming the vessel; let the extra answer precede speech. Reveal both losses
    early. Each satellite carries a refreshed part of a shared route, not part
    of the vessel's self. Delay only the practical distinction between a route
    and a gathering call. Do not reveal builders, species history, mission, or
    links to existing systems.
  - Governing movement: Count the sheep; hear an impossible answer; climb to the
    vessel; recognize person and wound; say “follow” too quickly; drive toward
    the fold; descend dangerously; muffle and restore the bells; retract the
    false promise; offer a search; spread sheep into a grazing crescent; let the
    ship hold still and call; count seven returning replies; let the satellites
    brace the wound and rebuild their route; release the vessel; count again.
- Speculative surplus: The living vessel feeds by combing charged particles
  through translucent cilia, couples those fields to iron bells for speech,
  and shares refreshed navigation fragments across its flock. Unrelated to the
  search, feeding condenses cold green dew that tastes of salt; sheep gorge on
  it and refuse water until the sun warms the grass. The dew neither reveals
  nor repairs satellites.
- Structural distinction: A lateral, open-air nocturne shaped by alternating
  counts and widening sound. An apparent journey is abandoned as the pasture
  becomes a listening field; corrected terms and accumulating replies replace a
  fixed-venue climax. No public proof, battle, crowd turn, institution, expert,
  or symbolic new home appears.
- Decisive turn, deepening, or recognition: After muffled bells cause the ship
  to sink, the shepherd restores every bell and admits she cannot name a star
  she has never seen. The promised destination was false; the offer to help can
  still be real. Sheep dividing around a stony rise turn their bells from an
  arrow into a field, and she realizes the ship must listen rather than follow.
  It explicitly accepts her limited instruction to hold still and call. The
  first satellite answer validates the new terms, not a road home.
- Aftereffect or live uncertainty: All seven satellites return with different
  degrees of damage. Their route fragments restore a destination and their
  formation supports the wound, but arrival and the collision remain unknown.
  The sheep are safe but late, thirsty after the dew, and one warped bell needs
  repair.
- Opening / ending relation: Open with the shepherd counting by sound and
  walking uphill because one answer is extra. End after the visitors depart,
  when one ewe is truly missing and a familiar bell sends her downhill before
  breakfast: listening opens the sky, then returns her to work.

## Beats

1. At evening count, the shepherd identifies every sheep by its bell and hears
   one cadence beyond the count answering from above the upper slope. An out-of-season
   aurora hangs low enough to color the grass.
2. She climbs expecting a strayed animal and sees a ridge-sized living vessel
   combing light through translucent cilia. One side is torn; seven empty
   satellite paths pulse around it. When it vibrates the flock's iron bells,
   the extra cadence resolves into halting words.
3. The starship reports only what it knows: injury, no destination, seven lost
   satellites, failing feed. It identifies the moving bells as a guide offer.
   Trying to calm sheep and vessel at once, the shepherd says “follow me.”
4. She starts for the lower fold. The ship follows the bell-line literally,
   descending as the path bends; cold charge lifts wool, tips stones, and makes
   the flock bunch toward a gully. Its torn side opens further when it corrects
   too sharply.
5. She blankets several bells to slow it. The sheep become harder to locate,
   while the starship sinks and repeats that the guide has vanished like its
   flock. She restores the bells before either group scatters.
6. In the quiet after the recovery, the shepherd admits that she cannot guide
   it home and that “follow” exceeded what she knew. The starship challenges
   the false offer, listens to her narrower proposal to help find its flock,
   and consciously accepts those terms.
7. A stony rise forces the sheep to fan around both sides rather than travel in
   a line. Hearing their bells become a field, the shepherd recognizes why the
   vessel keeps mistaking direction for gathering. She turns the flock into a
   broad, slow crescent across the upper pasture and asks the ship to hold still.
8. The starship sends one call through every bell and the aurora. A faint
   satellite answers beyond the crescent; its return clarifies another sector.
   Subsequent replies accumulate unevenly, each small body choosing its own
   approach and taking a place around the wound.
9. Near dawn, six satellites have returned. Their route fragments give bearings
   but not a destination; the seventh answer is almost buried in the shepherd's
   ordinary bell-count. She separates the two rhythms by listening for the one
   note that does not move with a sheep, enabling the vessel to call toward it
   without chasing the flock below.
10. The last satellite arrives damaged but alive. The seven exchange a chorus
    that restores the destination to the starship and brace its torn side in a
    travel formation. The shepherd makes the bells still long enough for the
    vessel to confirm it is leaving by its own recovered route, not continuing
    to follow her.
11. The ship departs as the aurora thins. The shepherd handles the sheep's
    refusal of water, notes the warped bell for later repair, counts again, and
    turns downhill when one familiar ewe answers from the wrong side of the
    path.

## Continuity

- Canon used: The story occupies the one deep-time physical world in an
  otherwise unspecified era and setting. It follows the established plurality
  of independent systems, the rule that a being's origin does not negate life
  or agency, the personhood protection for memory injury, and the distinction
  between recurring imagery and shared mechanism.
- Boundaries and unknowns: Do not identify the era as modern-like Earth, Ravel,
  or the Glass-Sea era; do not name a country, range, star, or Galactic Cycle
  coordinate. The vessel, auroral feeding, harmonic guide practice, satellites,
  distributed route memory, and injury are new local facts for this non-canon
  story. They establish no relationship to Glass-Sea living solar fire, the
  western-bay invasion frames or mesh, Solstice Evening Bell's chimes, the
  Glass Gate, another crossing system, or any other bell, sun, flock, memory,
  or navigation mechanism. Destination loss is bounded damage, not proof that
  the starship's identity or relationships are errors; the satellites remain
  distinct living participants rather than replaceable memory storage. The
  cause of injury, builders, species, prior route, destination society, and
  success after departure remain unresolved.
