# Outline

## Story

- Proposed reader-facing title: *The Cat Who Had Enough*.
- Premise and central promise: Eight months after the unnamed adult narrator inherits responsibility for Tallow, the uncanny cat formerly cared for by their late Aunt Noma, they have turned his dry advice into a substitute for acting. Tallow can speak human words when a complaint is addressed to him, but his counsel comes from observation, not prophecy. On the evening a paid apprenticeship application is due, the narrator converts every practical instruction into a fresh grievance, then blames Tallow for failing to improve their life. He snaps and delivers the promised long, furious lecture: the narrator is the source of many problems they blame on others, wasting opportunities, other people's patience, and their own genuine ability by rehearsing reasons not to risk a definite answer. The deadline passes during the reckoning. Resolve the promise without making Tallow infallible or restoring the lost chance: the narrator contests his contempt and one harmful assumption, admits the choices that were theirs, and takes one accountable next step without asking him to certify it.
- Intended reader experience: Domestic comedy tightening into the guilty pleasure and discomfort of an earned rant. The lecture should be funny through precise evidence, painful where it reaches grief and fear, and clarifying without becoming a total verdict on the narrator's worth. End with chastened affection, an unrepaired consequence, and cautious relief.
- Generating force:
  - Mode: Plot-led recursive complaint/advice ratchet. Each loop begins with a concrete obstacle, produces one usable instruction, and becomes a more elaborate reason that instruction cannot be attempted; the shrinking deadline makes repetition change rather than reset.
  - Focal attachment or contradiction: The narrator seeks Tallow's attention because his bluntness makes them feel seen, but uses that attention to outsource judgment while rejecting every judgment. Tallow wants them to stop disappearing into avoidance, yet hides attachment inside maxims and has begun to enjoy always being the diagnostician.
  - Plot-led wants and useful limit, if applicable: The narrator wants the apprenticeship and steadier work, but also wants to avoid submitting unfinished work that could earn an unambiguous rejection. Their useful limit is agile self-explanation: they notice real complications quickly, then turn that intelligence toward exceptions and low-risk busywork. Tallow wants either a submitted application or an honest decision not to apply, and release from being used as an inexhaustible conscience. His limit is observational certainty: he knows repeated behavior but cannot read minds, predict outcomes, or weigh facts withheld from him.
- Counterforce or complication:
  - Source: A midnight deadline, an incomplete but viable portfolio, eight months of deferred messages and small neglected tasks, the narrator's fear of a verdict, and Tallow's accumulated frustration. None is a villain; the apprenticeship remains ordinary and does not bend around the domestic crisis.
  - Credible inside logic, if a person: Tallow is a confronting person, not an antagonist or magical truth engine. Noma treated his advice as one perspective and rarely solicited it; the narrator has made it a household service. He stayed by choice because he cares, but care does not perfect his judgment, excuse contempt, or obligate endless emotional labor.
  - Pressure or change created: Each complaint consumes time, shifts responsibility toward Tallow, and gives the narrator another reason to resent him. His lecture is both warranted boundary-setting and a breach in their comic ritual; because it lasts through midnight, insight cannot substitute for submission.
- Narrative design:
  - POV, tense, distance, and limitation: Close first-person past through the unnamed narrator. Their funny, self-protective narration makes each excuse briefly plausible while concrete objects—the unread reminder, finished pieces, unmailed form, and clock—let readers detect the pattern sooner. Tallow's posture, departures, and exact recall disclose irritation and care without granting access to his thoughts.
  - Time shape and compression: One continuous evening from about six until shortly after midnight, then a brief next-morning coda. Fold the eight months since Noma's death into present objects and two short memories. Compress successive complaint loops, then let the lecture occupy roughly a quarter of the story before slowing for the smaller exchange after it.
  - Information and reveal strategy: Establish Tallow's speech and its trigger immediately; the impossible fact is not a mystery. Show that his advice is practical, never oracular, and sometimes wrong. Gradually reveal that the narrator complains partly to obtain contact, while withholding the cost to Tallow until he names it. Move the lecture from comic evidence to repeated interpersonal choices and finally the narrator's use of genuine grief as a reason no task can begin. A specific correction of Tallow's bad judgment proves that responsibility does not require accepting his total verdict.
  - Governing movement: Open the application; complain; receive a usable instruction; substitute a safer task; complain about the detour; seek comfort disguised as counsel; ignore a reminder; watch the clock narrow; demand a life-changing answer; blame the adviser; trigger the lecture; parry; recognize repetitions; contest an overreach; miss the deadline; separate cause from contribution; set a boundary; act without requesting permission.
- Speculative surplus: Human speech from Tallow rapidly overproofs yeast dough nearby. Noma kept rising dough in a closed pantry; tonight terse advice lifts the bowl's towel and the long lecture sends dough down the cabinet. The effect creates cleanup and limits where he talks around baking; it does not measure truth, emotion, or blame.
- Structural distinction: A closed-room verbal ratchet whose recurring unit—complaint, answer, evasion—shortens until it breaks into one sustained monologue. The decisive confrontation occurs at an untidy kitchen table and produces no external emergency, public proof, collective turn, restored opportunity, institutional response, or symbolic destruction.
- Decisive turn, deepening, or recognition: After the narrator accuses Tallow of enjoying their failure, he climbs onto the application papers and lectures from months of evidence: messages opened too late, requested help punished with argument, work polished where no one can reject it, money spent to soothe delays, and Noma's death invoked whenever a living obligation becomes frightening. He calls the narrator a waste because they keep wasting what is available, not because their life has no value. Their practiced rebuttals fail when he repeats contradictory complaints in order. They finally name a real counterexample—advice he gave without enough facts that damaged a friendship, followed by his refusal to hear the outcome. Midnight passes. Tallow concedes that seeing a pattern is not knowing a whole life; the narrator concedes that grief, bad luck, and his mistake do not explain the choices he correctly named. The narrator owns the missed application; Tallow owns the limits and cruelty of his delivery.
- Aftereffect or live uncertainty: The deadline remains missed, the friendship and finances remain strained, and no answer from the former tutor is guaranteed. The narrator nevertheless sends a truthful next-morning message with the five finished pieces and asks for feedback, not an exception. They agree that a complaint is no longer automatic permission for advice: Tallow will ask whether counsel, sympathy, or silence is wanted. Sustaining that boundary remains uncertain.
- Opening / ending relation: Open with the upload page ready and Tallow telling the complaining narrator to send the five usable pieces. End the next morning when they send those same pieces in a smaller, honest message, close the laptop, and feed him without asking whether the action was correct. The task returns diminished but real; one email does not repair a life.

## Beats

1. At six, open at the kitchen table with the apprenticeship portal, five finished pieces, several half-finished files, and dough rising for dinner. The narrator complains that the selectors demand a false, coherent life; Tallow advises sending the five. His words swell the dough. The narrator rewrites captions instead.
2. Run two shortening loops. Tallow recommends opening the former tutor's reminder and choosing the simplest caption; the narrator cleans a mug, checks a bill, adjusts colors, and calls each safer task progress. Objects show that several “external” obstacles await one small action.
3. While feeding Tallow, compress the inheritance: Noma died eight months ago; her will provided care funds, but Tallow stayed only after choosing the apartment. Her note called him a companion with opinions, not property or oracle. She asked his advice rarely and could ignore it without making refusal another complaint.
4. The reminder remains unread. The narrator complains that the tutor has given up; Tallow tells them to read it. They seek a more profound answer and improve an irrelevant piece. Recall one useful and one wrong piece of Tallow advice so experience, not magic, explains his usual accuracy.
5. Near eleven-thirty, a file-size error appears although the five pieces remain unselected. Complaints widen from the portal to money, exhaustion, the tutor, and Tallow. He offers specific remedies until the narrator accuses him of enjoying superiority and never making anything better.
6. Tallow snaps. Sustain the lecture through comic domestic evidence, a chronology of avoidance, the way the narrator makes helpers defend requested help, grief named as real but not all-explanatory, and waste grounded in time, ability, food, favors, and chances. Dough escapes the bowl while neither stops.
7. The narrator's familiar defenses collapse against two incompatible complaints Tallow quotes in order. They recover enough agency to name his harmful, underinformed advice about the unnamed friend. His attempt to generalize it away proves their point. The clock reaches midnight.
8. In the quiet aftermath, Tallow admits he performed certainty; the narrator rejects “source of everything” but names the missed submission and avoidable harms that were theirs. They set the advice/comfort boundary without demanding instant forgiveness.
9. Next morning, preserve the lost opportunity. The narrator cleans hardened dough, sends the tutor a concise truthful message with the five pieces, and asks for feedback. Tallow watches but supplies no verdict; the narrator feeds him and begins with the outcome open.

## Continuity

- Canon used: An otherwise ordinary modern-like low-magic era where anomalies may survive while everyday culture treats magic as legend. Tallow is alive and supplies his own local magical agency, consistent with the rule that only life uses magic. No awareness of the deeper chronology is required.
- Boundaries and unknowns: Establish only Tallow's individual complaint-triggered speech and nearby dough effect. Do not create a universal talking-cat species, familiar tradition, advice compulsion, truth power, prophecy, mind-reading, inheritance system, or link to another anomaly. Legal inheritance transfers care responsibility and funds, not ownership; Tallow chose to stay and may refuse counsel. Leave his origin, lifespan, and possible peers unresolved. Keep the apartment and city unnamed, all other people generic, and the apprenticeship ordinary.
