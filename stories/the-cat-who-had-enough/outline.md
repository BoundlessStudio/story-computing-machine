# Outline

## Story

- Proposed reader-facing title: *The Cat Who Had Enough*.
- Central promise: The unnamed adult narrator inherits Tallow, the magical cat formerly cared for by their late aunt Beryl, and establishes a comic household routine: whenever the narrator aims a complaint at him, he can answer in human speech with one dry, shrewd piece of life advice. Across six low-stakes weeks, the narrator repeatedly ignores the obvious remedy, inflates it into an impossible philosophy, or buys an elaborate substitute for doing it; the predictable consequence then returns as proof that life or the apartment is against them. On an uneventful Sunday, the narrator complains that the flat has somehow eaten every clean teaspoon. Tallow finally snaps. He inventories the spoons, tours the accumulated evidence of every ignored answer, and delivers a long, escalating, caustic lecture naming the narrator as a waste of time, food, money, good intentions, and advice—and as the chief manufacturer of the petty problems they keep presenting as fate. The roast, not reconciliation, is the dominant release.
- Intended reader experience: Bright recognition comedy with a mounting sense of “surely the cat is about to say it.” Each excuse may amuse for a sentence, but physical evidence should put the reader ahead of the narrator by the second returning problem. The lecture delivers the anticipated guilty pleasure: specific, disproportionate in duration, ruthless, and overwhelmingly earned. Do not rebut or ethically soften the indictment into a balanced dispute; leave a chastened comic aftershock, not therapeutic repair.
- Target scale: A 2,500–4,000-word story, with roughly the first half in short accumulating loops, roughly one third in Tallow's sustained lecture, and the remainder in its immediate aftereffect.
- Generating force:
  - Mode: Comic recurrence and accumulation. Each unit is a causal boomerang: complain; receive one usable answer; convert it into abstraction, shopping, research, or wounded self-description; avoid the act; meet the forecast consequence; complain again. Earlier remedies remain physically present, turning the apartment into an involuntary inventory of authorship.
  - Movement-bearing attachment and contradiction: The narrator wants relief but also enjoys Tallow's acid attention, the prestige of having a magical adviser, and the innocence gained by treating choices as diagnoses. Tallow initially enjoys applying long observation to solvable problems; he increasingly wants an uninterrupted nap, an ordinary meal, and evidence that one answer has become action.
  - Useful limit: The narrator is verbally agile and attracted to preparation as a substitute for completion. They can turn “put the keys in the dish” into a debate about whether life should require systems, then spend longer selecting a brass hook. Tallow observes repeated conduct exceptionally well but has no prophecy, truth sense, mind-reading, or power to compel obedience. His advice on the chosen petty problems is consistently sound; introduce no harmful counterexample or concealed hardship that vindicates the evasions.
- Counterforce or complication:
  - Source: The narrator's habits reproduce faster than Tallow can close them. Every ignored answer creates a nuisance and a fresh defense; purchased planners, organizers, gadgets, and unopened fixes take up space and make later action slightly harder. There is no deadline, antagonist, serious deprivation, bereavement crisis, or institution pressing on the apartment.
  - Tallow's pressure: He is a confronting companion, not the source of the problems or a morally matched opponent. His polished maxims shrink to one-word reminders while the narrator's explanations lengthen. He silently counts how often the keys are “lost,” laundry sours, vegetables rot, and spoons are abandoned. The narrator mistakes his silence for renewed availability.
- Narrative design:
  - POV, tense, distance, and limitation: Close first-person past through the unnamed narrator. Their voice is charming, categorical, and self-exonerating; every workaround sounds briefly discerning until an object contradicts it. Keep Tallow exterior. Ear angles, interrupted grooming, abandoned sunbeams, exact pauses, and glances toward the already available remedy disclose his draining patience.
  - Time shape and compression: Six selected complaint loops across about six weeks, followed by one continuous Sunday breakfast and a short same-day aftereffect. Bridge time through recurring objects and Tallow's remembered counts. Avoid flashback scenes, a clock-driven night, an application or career crisis, and grief-centered recollection; Beryl's death belongs only to the inheritance setup.
  - Information strategy: Establish the magic and its limits in the opening paragraph; it is never a mystery. By the second returning problem, let readers understand that the narrator is building the next complaint. Later loops can omit intermediate steps because abandoned products make the causes inferable. Seed every spoon casually before the snap so Tallow's exact inventory reads as observation, not omniscience. Reveal no trauma, opposing case, or secret error that alters the climax's moral arithmetic.
  - Governing movement: Inherit a cat and an adequate blue key dish; complain; receive the short route; purchase a longer route; leave it packaged; suffer the forecast result; promote result into fate; repeat with food, laundry, bills, charging, and dishes; enjoy the adviser's wit; miss his patience; scatter teaspoons through the evidence; accuse the flat; trigger the snap; be marched through one's own causality; attempt familiar deflections; hear them quoted back in advance; lose the argument; make one unglamorous correction without praise; almost begin again; stop.
- Non-thematic speculative effect: Keep the magic entirely inside the advice premise. A complaint directly addressed to Tallow opens human speech only on that grievance; if he chooses to answer, his first response engages it as advice, and he may continue while it remains the subject, but unrelated requests emerge as ordinary cat sounds. The narrator therefore phrases bids for conversation as complaints, and Tallow can turn the teaspoon grievance into one uninterrupted lecture. The rule grants voice, not correctness or compulsion, and creates no prophecy, truth mechanism, species lore, emotional meter, or unrelated household effect.
- Distinct shape: A longitudinal domestic boomerang made of comic returns, widening at the climax into a moving inventory of the apartment. Unlike the rejected version, it has no one-night deadline, valuable opportunity, therapeutic dispute, grief reckoning, mutual confession, negotiated advice boundary, apology, or reconciliation. The climax travels from kitchen to hall, desk, laundry cupboard, and back; there is no public proof, crowd response, collective turn, institutional change, or symbolic closure.
- Decisive turn and deepening: During a quiet Sunday breakfast, the narrator finds the teaspoon compartment empty. Several spoons are already visible to the reader: in a dead herb pot, in cups at the desk, in an open yogurt carton beside the full bin, crusted onto saucers, and submerged in the sink. The narrator tells Tallow that the apartment “eats teaspoons” and that a functional home should not require constant vigilance. He advises retrieving and washing the twelve spoons the narrator used. When they insist he has missed the larger point, he snaps and supplies that point at punishing length.
  - Begin with forensic comedy: exact location and recent use of every spoon, defeating the attempt to rename abandonment as disappearance.
  - Tour the earlier loops: the blue dish is empty while the brass hook remains packaged; meal tubs are pristine beside spoiled aspirational greens; scent beads stand beside laundry left wet after dismissed alarms; the unused budgeting app has become a second recurring fee; the premium charging dock is unplugged.
  - Escalate by imitating the narrator's distortions: a dish becomes an oppressive system, buying edible groceries means abandoning health, a timer mechanizes the soul, and washing a mug proves adulthood is a scam. Quote paired complaints that cannot coexist and note that advice changed only when the narrator demanded novelty.
  - Turn “waste” into an exhaustive catalogue without cushioning it: spinach, detergent, subscription fees, packaging, batteries, mornings, Beryl's dish, Tallow's naps, and the narrator's intelligence spent manufacturing loopholes. Tallow calls the narrator a waste and plainly identifies the common source of these recurring problems as the person before him.
  - Peak when the narrator argues that all lives require systems and therefore no particular failure belongs to anyone. Tallow distinguishes unavoidable chores from deliberately recreating the same avoidable chore. He need not blame the narrator for every sorrow in existence; he establishes their complete, visible authorship of the problems in this story without softening the insult.
  - Give the eventual lecture approximately 900–1,200 words. Permit only brief failed interjections that Tallow anticipates and absorbs. No equal counter-speech, evidence of his culpability, interruption, tears that redirect his anger, or conciliatory concession should break its momentum.
- Aftereffect: Tallow stops without apologizing, negotiating, or offering reassurance. The narrator checks the spoon locations to prove exaggeration and finds all twelve. They start to complain that collecting them will take all morning, recognize the next loop forming, and wash enough for breakfast without announcing reform. Tallow gives no praise. Most gadgets remain packaged and most habits remain available, so one acted-on answer shows impact rather than redemption.
- Opening / ending relation: Open with the narrator explaining that Tallow's first advice after the inheritance was to put their keys in Beryl's blue dish already beside the door—and that they responded by buying a better hook. End later that Sunday when the narrator nearly asks why the keys are never where they belong, sees the empty dish and the keys in yesterday's coat, and retrieves them without completing the complaint. Tallow opens one eye but does not need to speak. An ignored obvious answer becomes a temporarily acted-on one; the roast, not reform, remains the final authority.

## Beats

1. **The blue dish:** Establish the inheritance, Beryl's concise practical note, and Tallow's speech rule. He answers the lost-key complaint by pointing to Beryl's dish. The narrator buys a handsomer hook, never installs it, and keeps dropping keys into pockets and parcels.
2. **Aspirational groceries:** Tallow advises buying three familiar ingredients rather than food for an imaginary disciplined self. The narrator builds a nutrition project, buys stackable tubs, and lets worthy ingredients spoil while the tubs remain wrapped.
3. **The wet load:** Tallow advises removing laundry when the machine stops and setting one alarm. The narrator downloads a layered habit system, buys scent beads, dismisses every notification while comparing settings, and rewashes the same load. Tallow's later answer is simply “Alarm.”
4. **The charge for solving charges:** Tallow advises opening a statement and canceling one petty recurring fee. The narrator subscribes to a budgeting app, postpones setup until a “fresh morning,” and later complains about two fees. Keep the sums comic, not precarious.
5. **The uncharged solution:** Tallow advises plugging headphones into the cable already beside the desk. The narrator buys a premium dock, cannot choose its permanent location, and leaves dock and headphones uncharged while researching cable management. Readers should now supply Tallow's answer before he does.
6. **The spoon trail:** Across compressed days, the narrator carries tea, yogurt, and instant coffee into different rooms while beginning new systems. Seed all twelve teaspoons without declaring a puzzle. Tallow looks from each cup to the drawer; the narrator interprets this as interest in beverages.
7. **The last complaint:** At consequence-free Sunday breakfast, the empty drawer prompts the claim that the apartment eats teaspoons. Tallow gives the literal remedy. The narrator demands a larger explanation for life's hostile little frictions, exhausting him.
8. **The tour and roast:** Tallow counts spoons, revisits each exhibit, predicts the narrator's evasions, expands from particular waste to the narrator's wastefulness, and identifies them as the source. Keep his attention feline and concrete: smells, repeated routes, spoiled food, interrupted sleep, and objects occupying warm surfaces.
9. **The aftershock:** The narrator verifies the inventory and collects the spoons. No apology scene follows. The key problem later tries to recur; the narrator interrupts the complaint, retrieves the keys from the coat, and uses the old dish while Tallow stays silent.

## Continuity

- Canon used: The story can sit in the modern-like low-magic era without claiming literal contemporary Earth. Tallow is alive and supplies his own local magical agency, consistent with the locked rule that only life can use magic. No recurring person, place, faction, event, or larger chronology is required.
- Noun history: Tallow and Beryl have no exact or alias collision in universe authority, `stories/NAMES.md`, or passing current review inventories. Tallow remains distinguishable from Gideon Tolland, Talvi, and Talune. Both nouns are new, not recurring.
- Boundaries and unknowns: Establish only Tallow's individual complaint-triggered advisory speech. Do not create a universal magical-cat species, familiar office, inherited ownership rule, advice compulsion, truth power, prophecy, mind-reading, or relationship to another anomaly. The inheritance transfers responsibility for Tallow's care and Beryl's ordinary effects; Tallow remains a living companion, not property. Leave his origin, age, lifespan, possible peers, and the faculty's cause unresolved. Keep the city, building, and all incidental people unnamed.
