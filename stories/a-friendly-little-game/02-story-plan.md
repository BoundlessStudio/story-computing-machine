## Story promise and controlling movement

In an ordinary dockside bar in an unnamed era and region, an unnamed expedition planner waits with the one iron key that can open a spillway gate at the night's low water. Their three companions are late because the viewpoint character left them dealing with an overturned cart at a narrow bridge rather than lose the schedule. A living hooded stranger, having observed the separation and the four waiting cups, offers a game in which the viewpoint character must predict each companion's first meaningful choice on arrival. The stranger's three nonliving ceramic pieces can retain the player's declared expectations only while the stranger actively supplies magic; they cannot predict, judge, influence, or bind anyone.

The viewpoint character accepts because they need the low-water job, want the stranger's offered purse for expenses, and are privately desperate to prove that assigning each companion a reliable role is the same as knowing them. Each arrival disproves that belief through an independent choice. On losing, the player discovers the stranger has designed the forfeit language to invite them to treat one companion as a tool that can be “spent.” The viewpoint character refuses to volunteer anyone or anyone else's property and instead gives up the valuable gate key they personally own. The stranger wins the material game and leaves with the key; the expedition and its payment are lost. The emotional turn is that the companions remain by choice, while the viewpoint character stops arranging them into positions and accepts the practical and relational cost of their own decision.

The resolution must condemn the stranger's manipulated consent without pretending the game magically authorized a claim over people. The player loses something they had authority to surrender. No rescue, reset, hidden-rule victory, or sequel hook reverses that loss.

## Narrative contract

- **POV:** Close second person, limited entirely to the unnamed viewpoint character. Never reveal or imply a personal name, nickname, title-as-name, persistent epithet, initials, or pronouns for the viewpoint character. Companions can address them without a name.
- **Tense:** Past throughout.
- **Tone/genre:** Tight social suspense with a tactile, locally bounded magical display; unease grows from polite wording, observed habits, and the viewpoint character's dawning recognition that the stranger's categories resemble their own. Warmth arrives only after the loss and remains practical rather than sentimental.
- **Audience/content rating:** Teen. Threatening proximity, one checked reach for a knife, and a sharp ceramic edge are acceptable; no graphic injury, gore, sexual content, or cruelty as spectacle.
- **Required prompt beat:** The hooded stranger must physically sit on the stool beside the viewpoint character at the bar and say, verbatim: **“While we wait for the rest of your party to arrive, would you like to partake in a friendly little game?”** Do not correct the invitation's grammar or split it with action.
- **Setting scope:** One unnamed bar beside an unnamed river settlement, plus brief references to the nearby bridge and spillway. No named era, country, faction, bar, expedition company, game, magical technique, or universal order of strangers.
- **Completion:** Dramatize all three arrivals, finish the game on the page, transfer the key to the stranger, establish that the low-water expedition and expected payment are lost, and end on the party's changed seating arrangement and the viewpoint character's unclosed hand.

## Character engine

### Unnamed viewpoint character

- **Practical role:** Adult route planner and holder/owner of the one iron gate key; assembled this four-person recovery expedition and is responsible for the timetable and client deposit.
- **Immediate want:** Get all four people to the spillway before low water, complete the paid recovery, and avoid admitting that leaving the bridge was a selfish choice.
- **Deeper want:** Receive proof that the companions still accept their leadership.
- **Need:** Separate familiarity from authority. A person can be known without being reduced to a reliable function, and another person's predictable care does not make that care available on demand.
- **Pressure:** The low-water window; money already advanced and partly spent; the bridge accident; the stranger's uncanny attentiveness; growing embarrassment as every confident forecast fails in public.
- **Blind spot/lie:** “I keep everyone safe because I know exactly what each of them will do.” In practice, the viewpoint character has been treating Gwen as foresight, Alba as care, and Felix as force.
- **Arc:** Defensive certainty → doubles down on role-based predictions → recognizes that the stranger's proposed human forfeit merely makes the viewpoint character's own habits explicit → assumes the cost personally → sits without directing what the others must do next.

### Gwen

- **Role/relationship:** Adult scout and surveyor; longest-working companion, sharpest critic of the viewpoint character's schedule-first habits.
- **Independent goal:** Make sure the cart driver and blocked bridge are safely managed, then learn whether the recovery can still be attempted without gambling the group on the tide.
- **Arrival choice:** Instead of reporting delay and taking her customary right-hand stool, she uses her first breaths to ask the bartender to send hot water and food back to the bridge, then remains standing. She prioritizes a stranger and retains control of where she sits.
- **Consequential later choice:** Pulls her folded survey out of the stranger's reach and states that it is hers; she refuses to let the viewpoint character or the game stake it.

### Alba

- **Role/relationship:** Adult healer and climber; has repeatedly allowed concern to turn them into the group's automatic caretaker.
- **Independent goal:** Confirm the cart driver has help, check the strange game before touching anyone, and preserve their own boundaries after an exhausting roadside treatment.
- **Arrival choice:** Notices the viewpoint character's scraped knuckles but asks whether they want examination. When the viewpoint character reflexively says “not now,” Alba accepts the answer and turns to inspect the ceramic pieces. This defeats the prediction that they will treat first and ask later.
- **Consequential later choice:** Refuses to press a piece, touch the stranger, or contribute their medical bag as forfeit; identifies aloud only what they can observe—that the pieces cool when the stranger's fingers lift—not an encyclopedic explanation of the mechanism.

### Felix

- **Role/relationship:** Adult porter and former caravan guard; physically imposing, ashamed of a past bar fight the viewpoint character still treats as proof that he is the group's inevitable weapon.
- **Independent goal:** Finish getting the cart upright without worsening anyone's injury, then prevent another fight even if that makes him look afraid.
- **Arrival choice:** When he sees the hood, tense table, and viewpoint character's alarm, he deliberately unhooks his sheathed knife and lays it on the far end of the bar before asking what the viewpoint character wants. He refuses the predicted reach for violence.
- **Consequential later choice:** Refuses the stranger's attempt to classify him as a usable “strong arm” and leaves the knife out of reach even when the stranger takes the key.

### The hooded stranger

- **Status/function:** An original living adult person and practiced social predator. Their personal name, origin, species taxonomy, and any institutional affiliation remain unrevealed. `the hooded stranger` and `the stranger` stay lowercase descriptions, never proper titles.
- **Goal:** Acquire the viewpoint character's rare gate key without a noisy theft. They saw the four companions split at the bridge, followed the one carrying the key, and use the viewpoint character's visible impatience and arranged cups to construct the game. They do not know the companions through prophecy, names, mind-reading, or universal game law.
- **Method:** Exact terms, selective disclosure, flattery, calm reframing, and a small magical recording display that makes ordinary observation look omniscient. Their wrongdoing is the engineered ambiguity that encourages the player to confuse “a useful thing at the table” with a person or another person's property.
- **Limit:** Their magic records only a consenting speaker's declared expectation in a tile. They must remain alive, awake, within twelve paces, and in fingertip contact with the wooden rack or a tile. It cannot enforce the wager or affect a companion's behavior.

### The bartender

- **Minor role:** Unnamed adult witness who keeps serving other patrons, notices the thermal writing, and will not enforce the stranger's claim. The bartender establishes that the transaction is happening in an ordinary public room, not a supernatural court. Keep dialogue to one or two practical lines; use lowercase `the bartender`, never a proper identity.

## Bounded game specification

### Physical components and agency

- Three thumb-sized salt-glazed ceramic pieces, each blank and nonliving; one shallow wooden rack with three slots; four ordinary cups already set by the viewpoint character; the stranger's small purse as the offered prize.
- The pieces and rack are non-agentic focuses. They never move, choose, speak, judge, cast, predict, or enforce. Heat-lines appear only because the living stranger actively works magic through their own fingers.
- The stranger is the sole living magical user and supplier. Charging all three pieces leaves their fingertips visibly pale and trembling, establishing effort and a practical ceiling rather than unlimited power.

### Access and information

- The player must verbally accept, press a thumb to a blank piece, and speak one concrete forecast about one expected companion's first meaningful choice within ten breaths of entering the bar. The stranger touches the opposite edge and actively fixes a shorthand heat-pattern of the player's words.
- The viewpoint character and reader know all declared forecasts. The companion is identified by an ordinary personal name only for conversational clarity; names have no mechanical force.
- The stranger cannot see the future or read anyone's mind. They personally observe what each arrival does, then touch the relevant piece to replay the stored heat-pattern. The player and witnesses compare forecast to action. Ambiguity must be decided against the stranger's claim; this prevents an arbitrary hidden-rule score.

### Disclosed turn structure

1. Before each companion arrives, the player commits one specific forecast to one blank piece.
2. The companion enters and acts without instruction from the viewpoint character during the first ten breaths.
3. The stranger replays the declared forecast. A clear match scores for the player; a clear mismatch scores for the stranger.
4. Two matches win the stranger's purse and the party's drinks. Two mismatches require the player to surrender “one useful thing brought to this table for tonight's work, chosen by you.” The forfeit wording is stated before the first tile, so the final turn recontextualizes an existing rule rather than inventing a new one.
5. A third arrival completes the game even after the majority is known because it determines whether the stranger will press for the most damaging interpretation and gives every companion causal participation.

### Legal moves, refusal, and stopping

- Before charging the first piece, the viewpoint character may refuse with no effect or debt.
- After a forecast is fixed, it cannot be revised, but the player may stop. Stopping concedes the game and activates only the disclosed personal forfeit; the magic still cannot compel payment.
- Companions may act however they choose, speak, refuse, leave, or touch nothing. No rule prohibits them from noticing earlier arrivals or responding to the room.
- A companion may volunteer property or service only through a separate informed choice, but no one does. The viewpoint character has no move that can make such a contribution valid.
- Lifting the stranger's hands makes the heat-writing fade within breaths. Moving the pieces beyond twelve paces, breaking them, or exhausting the stranger also ends the display. None of these actions changes the already witnessed social score.

### Apparent and actual objectives

- **Apparent objective:** A light test of how well the player knows their friends, with a useful purse against one tool from the night's job.
- **Stranger's actual objective:** Exploit the player’s role-based thinking until they either surrender the gate key or fracture trust by designating a companion, their labor, or their gear as the useful “thing.” The stranger has inferred the key's value from seeing it handled at the bridge and its guarded pocket, not from magic.
- **Story's actual test:** Whether the viewpoint character will treat companions as spendable functions once that habit has a visible price.

### Outcome and costs

- All three forecasts fail. The stranger wins the stated game.
- The stranger first points to Gwen's map, Alba's medical bag, and Felix's strength as if they were the player's options. Those attempted claims are invalid; each owner refuses, and the pieces provide no leverage over them.
- The viewpoint character chooses the iron gate key, which they personally own and brought for the night's work. The stranger accepts it and leaves. The player is not magically forced, but the choice occurs under a manipulative bargain the narrative does not bless as fair.
- Immediate cost: the expedition cannot enter the spillway at low water; the paid recovery is cancelled, the expected fee is lost, and the viewpoint character remains responsible for the spent deposit. Relational cost: apologizing will not instantly restore confidence, and the companions do not reinstate the viewpoint character as unquestioned leader.
- Earned gain: no companion is traded, coerced, or reduced to a tool; all three remain in the bar by independent choice. This does not erase the key, money, or trust lost.

## Style profile

- **Dominant voice qualities:** Watchful, practical, and quietly self-justifying. The second-person narration should sort the room into routes, tools, distances, timing, and likely reactions because that is how the viewpoint character thinks. As certainty cracks, sentences admit competing observations instead of becoming generically lyrical.
- **Narrative distance:** Very close second person with free-indirect pressure: `you` notices door swing, cup positions, wet hems, hands, and time before recognizing shame. Move even closer for each failed prediction and the final placement of the key. Pull back only to compress the minutes between arrivals and the stranger's departure.
- **Dialogue pressure:** Every exchange is a contest over who defines a person or an agreement. Politeness is a weapon for the stranger, clipped logistics protect the viewpoint character, and companions answer the assumptions beneath the stated forecast rather than delivering ethical lectures.
- **Image sources/motifs:** Four cups arranged like assigned places; bridge grit and river damp; tools put down or drawn back; pale fingertips warming dark ceramic; the iron key's weight; hands that ask, refuse, offer, or remain open. Use literal material detail before metaphor. Do not make the room “close in,” silence become a substance, or objects look accusatory.
- **Rhythm:** Start with measured, schedule-conscious clauses. Shorten at each mismatch, but reserve one-line emphasis for the key choice. Let aftermath breathe in plainer sentences.
- **Wit/lyricism:** The stranger may have one dry, unsettling observation; Gwen may be bluntly funny about schedules. Do not turn fear into polished banter or give every speaker a clever line.
- **Intentional departure:** The protagonist's name and pronouns remain absent even in intimate close narration. Maintain clarity through body position, possession, and relationship, without replacing the missing name with a capitalized role or repeated epithet.

## Major-speaker dialogue profiles

- **Unnamed viewpoint character — dialogue profile:** Wants compliance with the timetable and reassurance that their reading of the group is accurate; defaults to clipped practical statements, numbered options, and confident predictions; keeps guilt about leaving the bridge vague; after resistance becomes overprecise about rules; with companions, issues suggestions that sound like settled plans; would not naturally deliver a polished speech about autonomy. At the climax, reduce their language to a concrete correction and “Mine” while moving the key.
- **The hooded stranger — dialogue profile:** Wants acceptance, continued play, and the key; uses complete courteous clauses, exact repetitions, and apparently neutral questions; refuses to state how they identified the target; assumes people can be described by uses; after resistance shifts from flattery to literal parsing of `thing`, `useful`, and `chosen`; tolerates silence and lets others indict themselves; never rants, claims objective divinity, or explains a universal law.
- **Gwen — dialogue profile:** Wants accurate situational control and acknowledgment of consequences; speaks in terse route facts and concrete imperatives, with dry humor only when safe; refuses to say directly that she no longer trusts the viewpoint character's leadership; after being categorized, stops arguing about the schedule and physically retrieves her map; with the viewpoint character she interrupts planned explanations; would not use therapeutic or governance vocabulary.
- **Alba — dialogue profile:** Wants enough information to choose safe contact; uses measured questions, bodily specifics, and conditional offers; refuses the assumption that being a healer makes access automatic; after evasion becomes quieter and waits for an answer rather than filling silence; with injured strangers they are gentle, with the viewpoint character more exact; would not diagnose an unfamiliar magical system from one glance or turn care into a moral lecture.
- **Felix — dialogue profile:** Wants to prevent escalation and be regarded as more than force; speaks slowly in short concrete clauses, often placing an object before speaking; avoids mentioning the old fight; after provocation gets more formal rather than louder; with the viewpoint character asks what they want instead of accepting an order; humor fails under pressure and he lets it die; would not produce a slick threat or celebrate violence.

## Causal scene sequence

### Scene 1 — Four cups, one early arrival (approximately 400 words)

Open already under pressure: the viewpoint character has arranged four cups at the bar, keeps checking the door, and feels the iron key through their pocket while calculating the low-water window. Give only the necessary bridge history: an overloaded cart tipped at the narrow crossing; the viewpoint character squeezed past with the key after the others said they would handle it and follow. Their rationalization is that someone had to protect the schedule. River grit on the floor and their scraped knuckles keep the human cost present.

The hooded stranger enters from elsewhere in the room or has been sitting in shadow; no supernatural arrival. They take the stool immediately beside the viewpoint character. They glance at the four cups, the guarded pocket, and the door, then deliver the exact invitation verbatim. The viewpoint character initially refuses, but the stranger shows a purse heavy enough to cover tonight's supplies and flatters their confidence: surely a planner knows what their own party will do. End with the viewpoint character turning one blank ceramic piece under their thumb.

- **Causal turn:** Financial/timing pressure plus wounded pride causes acceptance; acceptance permits the stranger to charge the first piece.
- **Incompatible conversational aims:** The viewpoint character wants the stranger gone without revealing anxiety; the stranger wants an explicit yes and the first forecast.
- **Tactic change:** When polite dismissal fails to move the stranger, the viewpoint character challenges the rules; the stranger shifts from genial invitation to a concrete prize and lets the player supply the boast.

### Scene 2 — Terms and the first category (approximately 450 words)

Lay out the complete disclosed rules before play. The stranger places the three pieces and rack but does not pretend they act alone. As their fingertips meet the first piece, warmth crawls into lines that preserve a compact version of the player's words. Show the stranger's fingertip blanching so the power has a living source and cost.

The viewpoint character selects Gwen and forecasts that she will enter, report the delay before anything else, and take the right-hand stool from which she always studies the exits. This exposes both familiarity and ownership of place. The stranger asks whether Gwen ever chooses a different seat. The viewpoint character turns that emotional question into route logic. Establish the forfeit exactly: two misses mean one useful thing brought to the table for tonight's work, chosen by the player. The viewpoint character thinks of a replaceable tool, not the key, and accepts because two correct predictions seem certain.

- **Causal turn:** The fixed first forecast cannot be revised, so Gwen's arrival will publicly test the protagonist's claimed knowledge.
- **Incompatible conversational aims:** The stranger wants the forecast to define Gwen as a function; the viewpoint character wants a narrow, winnable behavior without discussing the relationship.
- **Tactic change:** When the stranger's personal question lands, the viewpoint character retreats into timing and seat geometry; the stranger stops probing and records the exact reduction.

### Scene 3 — Gwen chooses the bridge (approximately 500 words)

Gwen arrives damp and late because she stayed until ropes secured the cart and then took a longer alley around the blocked crossing. She does not start with a report to the viewpoint character. She goes to the bartender, pays for hot water and food to be carried to the driver, and asks whether anyone has already gone back. She remains standing rather than taking the assigned stool. Her actions are understandable consequences of the bridge, not behavior engineered merely to beat the game.

The stranger replays the first heat-pattern. Its visible words/marks match what the reader heard; the failure is indisputable. The viewpoint character argues that Gwen did report through the request, then realizes this is exactly the stranger's tactic—turning people into uses and parsing them afterward. Gwen notices her name in the pattern and demands context. The viewpoint character minimizes the wager. Gwen moves the right-hand cup away from “her” stool and asks what else has been promised. End when the second piece warms while Gwen watches, making continued play a conscious doubling down.

- **Arrival's causal contribution:** Gwen's contrary priority produces the first loss, exposes the game to the party, and physically disrupts the seating arrangement that encoded the viewpoint character's control.
- **Incompatible conversational aims:** Gwen wants a truthful account and attention returned to the injured driver; the viewpoint character wants her to validate the forecast after the fact; the stranger wants play to continue before Gwen can end it socially.
- **Tactic change:** Gwen moves from logistical report to direct interruption when she sees her recorded name; the viewpoint character shifts from technical appeal to minimization; the stranger invokes the already agreed score and silently offers the second piece.

### Scene 4 — Alba asks first (approximately 550 words)

Before Alba arrives, the viewpoint character predicts that they will see the scraped knuckles, seize the hand, and begin treatment before asking what happened. Gwen objects to `seize`; the viewpoint character softens the wording but keeps the expectation that care will be automatic. Fix that exact wording so the later mismatch is fair.

Alba arrives after staying until another local caretaker reached the cart driver. They register the tense people and the glowing rack before noticing the scraped hand. They ask, “May I?” or another plain request. The viewpoint character, intent on proving the prediction, nearly offers the hand, then sees Alba waiting rather than taking it and says not now. Alba accepts, turns to the stranger, and asks what powers the marks. Their choice is doubly independent: asking instead of assuming, and honoring the refusal even though it costs the player the round.

The stranger replays the second forecast and scores the decisive second miss. Alba briefly tests observation by asking the stranger to lift one hand; when they do, the heat-lines fade. Alba concludes only that the stranger is doing the work and the pieces are holding it, not that the pieces know anything. The game is already lost, but the stranger insists the third turn completes the agreed proof and “clarifies the available forfeit.” The viewpoint character stays because leaving now would let the stranger define the group through two failed categories and because they still think the third result can limit the cost.

- **Arrival's causal contribution:** Alba's consent practice defeats the second forecast, proves companions are not magically controlled, and reveals the living-source/nonliving-focus limit that will govern the rest of the scene.
- **Incompatible conversational aims:** Alba wants informed distance and a clear answer about touch; the viewpoint character wants Alba to perform care quickly enough to count; the stranger wants the second score accepted without explaining the magic.
- **Tactic change:** Alba shifts from a care offer to controlled experiment when refused; the viewpoint character moves from invitation to silence rather than ordering care; the stranger gives the smallest true answer—“The marks are mine”—and presses to finish.

### Scene 5 — Felix puts the knife down (approximately 650 words)

For the final forecast, the viewpoint character says Felix will see the threatening stranger, put a hand to his knife, and wait for an order. This is the ugliest category because it treats a past failure as a permanent job. Gwen names the old bar fight only obliquely; Alba asks whether Felix knows he has been assigned this reaction. The viewpoint character cannot answer cleanly. The stranger records the forecast, their hand now visibly trembling from maintaining all three pieces.

Felix arrives last because he fetched a lifting jack, helped right the cart, and walked the shaken driver to a safe doorway. He reads the bar: Gwen standing, Alba keeping distance, the viewpoint character rigid, the stranger hooded. He starts to reach toward his belt, sees the viewpoint character watching for it, and deliberately unhooks the sheathed knife. He sets it on the far end of the bar and asks the viewpoint character what they want him to do. The act is not passivity; it is a chosen refusal of the violent role. The third forecast fails.

The tiles prove only what was declared, not who Felix is. The stranger reveals the pressure beneath the forfeit. They point in turn to Gwen's map, Alba's bag, and Felix himself as useful things brought to tonight's work. Phrase the stranger's offer as a social proposition, not a magically valid claim: choose one companion's tool, or let one companion perform a small job for the stranger, and the key can remain. The living pieces do nothing to the companions. Gwen removes her map and refuses. Alba will not place their bag or hand near the rack. Felix leaves his knife down and says he did not enter the game.

The stranger then mirrors the viewpoint character's own categories—route, care, force—without a thematic monologue. This is the central reversal: the stranger's dehumanizing interpretation is recognizable because the viewpoint character supplied its vocabulary. The viewpoint character must not defeat that recognition with a technical loophole or ask which friend is willing. They take out the iron key, place it beside the pieces, and say only enough to establish ownership: “Mine.” They choose a qualifying tool they actually brought and own.

- **Arrival's causal contribution:** Felix's nonviolent choice supplies the final contradiction, prevents an easy brawl, and makes the player's decision possible in a room where no companion has been coerced into rescue or payment.
- **Incompatible conversational aims:** Felix wants to learn the viewpoint character's actual wish without becoming a weapon; the viewpoint character initially wants him to fulfill the prediction, then wants to keep him outside the forfeit; the stranger wants the group to ratify person-as-tool language.
- **Tactic change:** Felix changes a near-reach into visible disarmament; the stranger moves from scoring to divide-and-bargain pressure; the viewpoint character abandons rule-lawyering and answers through ownership plus action.

### Scene 6 — The cost remains (approximately 400 words)

The stranger takes the key by hand. The tile-writing fades as they release the rack, confirming that the components never held independent power. The bartender declines to validate any claim over the companions but does not reverse the player's surrendered property. Gwen could block the exit; Felix could threaten; Alba could call the bargain void. Instead, the viewpoint character tells them to let the stranger go because taking the key was the protagonist's own costly settlement, not the companions' debt. Keep this morally mixed: the con was manipulated, but the loss is real and the group will not solve it through retaliatory violence.

The stranger leaves with the gate key and their purse. State the practical consequence plainly: low water will pass, the gate will stay shut, the recovery fee is gone, and the spent deposit belongs to the viewpoint character's account. No spare key, sudden locksmith, secret route, or promise to chase the stranger appears.

The viewpoint character apologizes specifically for leaving the bridge and predicting the companions as roles, but no one grants instant forgiveness or votes them leader again. Gwen says tomorrow's route will be discussed tomorrow. Alba finally asks about the scraped hand; this time the viewpoint character offers it rather than assuming care, and Alba chooses to clean it. Felix retrieves his knife only after the stranger is gone, then chooses a stool for himself.

End as the companions pull the four cups into an uneven ring rather than the ordered row with a head place. The viewpoint character rests an open, newly bandaged hand where the heavy key used to be. The image resolves the change without stating a moral: the key and job are gone, the hand has stopped assigning positions, and the three companions remain because each decided to.

- **Incompatible conversational aims:** The viewpoint character wants to accept consequence without converting apology into a demand for reassurance; Gwen wants accountability and time; Alba wants permission before care; Felix wants the room to become ordinary again.
- **Tactic change:** The viewpoint character starts to issue an order about the next plan, stops, and asks instead; companions answer with bounded choices rather than a collective absolution.

## Reversals and escalation ladder

1. A stranger who appears uncannily informed has used ordinary observation of four cups, the bridge separation, and the guarded key pocket.
2. The magical pieces appear to judge predictions but only preserve the player's own words under the living stranger's active power.
3. Gwen's first mismatch can be dismissed as an exception; Alba's consent-based mismatch makes the game a loss; Felix's mismatch exposes the protagonist's most damaging category.
4. The apparent prize game becomes a pressure campaign to classify a companion or their property as a useful thing.
5. The protagonist cannot win by knowing friends better or exploiting a hidden rule. They resolve the game by paying with their own key.
6. Losing the expedition does not dissolve the party. Remaining together becomes three independent choices, not proof of the protagonist's control.

## Genuine facts versus assumptions

- **Genuine:** The bridge accident delayed the companions; the viewpoint character owns the gate key; low water is the job window; the stranger saw enough at the bridge and bar to construct the invitation; the stranger actively powers the pieces; each companion makes the observed choice; the key is surrendered and the job is lost.
- **Initially assumed by the viewpoint character:** The stranger knows the future; long familiarity guarantees behavior; each companion's role is available on demand; a majority win can protect the key; finishing the game will preserve leadership.
- **Claimed by the stranger but rejected:** Being part of a `party` makes companions or their gear part of the player's stake; `useful thing` can ethically include a person; magical display supplies moral authorization.

## Canon constraints and collision differentiation

- Keep all events in the one shared physical world without naming an era or claiming a disconnected universe.
- Only the living stranger uses magic. The ceramic pieces and rack are nonliving, non-agentic storage/focus objects. They cannot cast, predict, choose, bind, or enforce.
- The mechanism is local and completely demonstrated: consent-to-record access, living fingertip supply, spoken expectation, three-piece maximum, twelve-pace/contact limit, rapid fade on release, and no behavioral effect.
- No universal power attaches to games, wagers, truth, desire, pain, sacrifice, names, promises, or winning. Property changes hands through a social choice, not metaphysical ownership law.
- Names merely distinguish the companions in speech and have no magical access function.
- The stranger's manipulation compromises the spirit of consent; the narrative treats it as predatory even though the protagonist chooses a personally owned forfeit.
- No absent or present companion is bound, owned, recruited, touched, or made to serve by the protagonist's statements.
- **Fenwick distinction:** No card game, watch, witnessed napkin, time effect, bloodline ownership, or binding contract.
- **Lacebridge distinction:** No regulated duel, qualified official, surrender protocol, courtship stake, or invitation as prize.
- **Briar Hill distinction:** No route-host test, copied messages or dead loved ones, three request stops, verification call, glamour, sunrise return, or promise-completion mechanism. The three arrivals are people making present choices in one public room.
- **Deepmarket distinction:** No desire pricing, shame gate, relational-memory bargain, memory erasure, or market organism.
- **Rollcaller distinction:** No identity hunt, copied voices, records, concealment exposure, filaments, or name-powered containment.
- **Wrong Side distinction:** No personality inversion, amplification of a denied self, or truth-triggered release.
- Preserve Teen limits and consequence-centered threat. The stranger may be frightening without graphic violence, sexual danger, objective godhood, or a monstrous species reveal.
