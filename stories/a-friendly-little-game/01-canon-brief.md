## Safe local invention space

- Create a new bar or tavern in an unnamed locality and era of the shared physical world. It may be mundane except for one bounded speculative participant or mechanism.
- Keep the viewpoint character unnamed while giving them a clear party role, emotional fault line, and reason to be alone early.
- Create an original living hooded stranger. The hood may conceal an ordinary face, a culturally unfamiliar person, a transformed body, a constructed living person, or another locally defined identity; none of those options is established canon.
- Create an original group of companions with distinct relationships, capabilities, boundaries, and reasons for arriving. They need not form a recurring institution.
- Use cards, dice, stones, cups, coins, marked coasters, questions, gestures, stories, or another compact bar-table format. Nonliving components may display or focus living-supplied power without becoming independent magic users.
- Make the deception legible in retrospect. It may exploit equivocal wording, unequal information, social assumptions, or a concealed opponent, but the resolution should not depend on an arbitrary rule invented after the decisive move.
- Let the viewpoint character risk only something they can actually choose—time, money, information about themself, a promise they can personally fulfill, temporary pain, public embarrassment, or another bounded personal cost. If the stranger attempts to claim companions as stakes, frame that as coercion or fraud rather than legitimate ownership.
- Make the party’s arrival part of the game’s causal structure: each arrival may invalidate an assumption, reveal withheld context, refuse a claimed stake, make a new choice, or force the viewpoint character to choose between winning and protecting trust.
- The party may help, but the viewpoint character should make the decisive choice that resolves the invitation they accepted. This avoids an unearned rescue while preserving the party’s significance.
- A mundane con with one speculative consequence is viable. The game itself need not be magical if the stranger, bar, party, or cost supplies the speculative element.
- If memory, identity, prediction, binding, transformation, or copied perception is used, define a new local mechanism and differentiate it from Deepmarket, the Fenwick watch, Ravel, the Rollcaller, Briar Hill, and the truth-trigger wig.
- Keep any proposed canon addition narrow: these observed people, this bar, this specific game, and its demonstrated limits. Do not generalize one incident into a universal class of hooded strangers or supernatural games.

## Concrete canon recommendation

Proceed with an original standalone bar in an unnamed era, an unnamed viewpoint character who arrived before their companions, and a living stranger running one bounded social game through non-agentic table pieces.

The safest central design is a game that appears to test prediction or loyalty but actually exposes what authority the viewpoint character assumes over the absent party. The stranger may exploit ambiguity or attempt to convert statements about companions into stakes, while the arriving companions independently reject, confirm, or complicate those statements. The viewpoint character should resolve the game through a costly choice of their own rather than by successfully wagering someone else.

Keep the mechanism distinct from existing precedents: no bloodline timepiece or napkin contract, no desire-priced memory erasure, no three-stop glamour trial, no copied dead loved one, no truth-trigger personality inversion, and no date-as-prize courtesy bout.
