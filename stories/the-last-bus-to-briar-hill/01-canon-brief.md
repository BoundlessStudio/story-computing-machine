# Canon brief

## Hard constraints

- Place the story in the **modern-like era**: a low-magic period that resembles
  contemporary Earth but is not established as literal real-world Earth. An
  invented modern city is compatible with canon, but it must not be described
  as another planet or plane; all established era labels refer to one physical
  world. (`universe/premise.md` — “The deep-time shared world”;
  `universe/locations.md` — “The world across eras”; `universe/glossary.md` —
  “Modern-like era”; `universe/timeline.md` — “Established era order”)
- Ordinary life in this era generally treats magic as myth or legend. A real
  fairytale anomaly may survive the low-magic trough, but the prose should not
  imply that overt enchantment is routine across contemporary civic life unless
  this story establishes a bounded local exception. (`universe/timeline.md` —
  “Established era order”; `universe/premise.md` — “Knowledge and connection”)
- Magic, technology, and divinity are cultural labels for real systems, not
  objective cosmic categories, and no single origin explains every anomaly.
  Phones, ticket readers, buses, and other devices may interact with a magical
  system, but the story must define that local interaction rather than assume a
  universal magic-versus-technology rule. (`universe/rules.md` — “System
  categories”; `universe/glossary.md` — “Magic, technology, and divinity”)
- Only life can use magic. A nonliving bus, phone, route display, ticket, or
  other device may store, focus, transmit, or shape magic supplied through
  living agency, but it cannot itself be a magic user. If the bus or another
  construct independently uses magic, the story must establish that it is
  alive rather than merely call it enchanted. (`universe/rules.md` — “Living
  agency and magic”)
- Any enchanted route, threshold, or impossible bus journey must be a defined
  **local crossing system**. Its access, direction, limits, and consequences
  belong to that system; it must not imply a universal portal network or a
  connection to other eras. It is not the Glass Gate merely because it crosses
  an impossible boundary. (`universe/rules.md` — “Portals and crossing
  systems”; `universe/locations.md` — “The Glass Gate”)
- A bargain's price, the protagonist's desire, and consent may matter within
  the story's local fairytale mechanism, but none is a universal fuel or law.
  Once established, the mechanism must apply its rules and its treatment of
  consent consistently, with ethical consequences taken seriously.
  (`universe/rules.md` — “Costs, desire, and local mechanisms”)
- Names are normally symbolic and socially consequential, not universally
  magical. Any true-name test, name theft, or name-bound ticket must be
  established as a rare local technological, magical, legal, or cultural
  system. (`universe/rules.md` — “Names and identity binding”)
- Resemblance to folklore, recurring imagery, another portal, or an artifact
  does not prove a shared origin. The story may use recognizable fairytale
  motifs, but it must not turn thematic resemblance into hidden continuity
  without evidence. (`universe/rules.md` — “Artifacts, motifs, and folklore”;
  `universe/style-guide.md` — “Continuity and evidence discipline”)
- The protagonist's choice, transformation, grief, consent, and relationships
  must remain morally real. A cosmic or technical explanation cannot reduce
  the adventure to a meaningless error or erase its consequences.
  (`universe/style-guide.md` — “Personhood and moral consequence”)
- Keep the treatment broadly accessible and non-explicit. Danger, horror, and
  death may occur if relevant, but should remain non-graphic and emphasize
  character and consequence. (`universe/style-guide.md` — “Audience and
  content boundaries”)

## Useful established context

- The low-magic trough supports the prompt's contrast: recognizable buses,
  phones, gig-work schedules, street lighting, and civic services can remain
  ordinary while one relic anomaly or local living magical system becomes
  genuinely impossible. (`universe/timeline.md` — “Established era order”)
- Reality is coherent even when the protagonist, transit workers, city
  authorities, and fairytale beings have incomplete or mistaken explanations.
  The story does not have to reveal the wider cosmology. (`universe/premise.md`
  — “Knowledge and connection”)
- Magic-like and technology-like systems may interact, hybridize, imitate one
  another, or share ancestry, so modern tools can causally matter to the
  adventure. Any such relationship remains specific to this story's mechanism.
  (`universe/rules.md` — “System categories”)
- Folklore may combine buried systems, surviving beings, invention,
  distortion, and coincidence. That allows fairytale language and tests to
  coexist with a modern viewpoint without canon requiring the characters'
  folklore explanation to be literally correct. (`universe/rules.md` —
  “Artifacts, motifs, and folklore”)
- Memory versus myth is a central universe tension, and evidence may be partial
  or distorted while individual choices still matter. This can enrich the
  adventure if useful, but the prompt does not require it. (`universe/premise.md`
  — “Memory, myth, and recoverable truth”)
- No recurring faction or recurring character is currently established in the
  authoritative topical notes. No row in `stories/INDEX.md` is marked canon
  `yes`, so no finalized story supplies additional binding facts for this
  brief. (`universe/factions.md` — “Factions and cultures”;
  `universe/characters.md` — “Recurring characters”; `stories/INDEX.md` —
  “Story index”)

## Conflicts or ambiguity

- **No authoritative contradiction found.** The prompt can proceed without a
  canon ruling.
- “Earth” is an era-bound cultural label for a contemporary-Earth-like period,
  not confirmation that the setting is our literal Earth. The prompt's
  assumption of an invented compatible city is safe, but specific real-world
  national geography would be a new story choice rather than an established
  fact. (`universe/premise.md` — “The deep-time shared world”;
  `universe/locations.md` — “The world across eras”)
- The modern-like era's exact date and Galactic Cycle coordinate are unresolved.
  Ravel's position relative to it is also unresolved. The story should not
  identify Briar Hill with Ravel or place the night on the master chronology
  unless a later canon decision supplies evidence. (`universe/glossary.md` —
  “Modern-like era”; `universe/timeline.md` — “Universal chronology” and
  “Established era order”)
- The Glass Gate can link eras of the same world, but its access points,
  operation, limits, builders, and paradox behavior are unresolved. Those gaps
  are not permission to identify the bus route as the Glass Gate.
  (`universe/locations.md` — “The Glass Gate”)
- `The Station Between` is a certified candidate involving supernatural train
  travel, but its index row is canon `no`. It creates no lore constraint or
  shared transit system for this story; only its character names remain
  production reservations. (`stories/INDEX.md` — “Story index”;
  `stories/NAMES.md` — “Rules”)

## Unknowns

- **Unknown:** Briar Hill, the city containing it, the transit authority, bus
  route, stops, schedules, fares, street network, phone network, gig-work
  platform, and civic institutions are not established in authoritative notes.
- **Unknown:** Whether “Briar Hill” is an ordinary destination, an enchanted
  place, a threshold, a moving destination, or only the route's advertised
  name.
- **Unknown:** The origin, age, operator, purpose, power source, access rules,
  price, and recurrence of the last bus and any associated fairytale system.
- **Unknown:** Whether the bus, route, ticketing system, helper, or antagonist is
  alive; apparent agency alone does not establish life.
- **Unknown:** Whether the anomaly is a relic of an earlier era, a new local
  system, folklore made concrete through a defined mechanism, or something
  else. No connection to the Glass Gate, Ravel, the Glass-Sea era, or a wider
  portal network is established.
- **Unknown:** The protagonist's identity, family, occupation, obligation,
  knowledge of magic, and reason for leaving ordinary life.
- **Unknown:** The exact mechanics and ethical terms of any bargain, three-part
  test, transformation, gift, or return. The prompt requires some of these as
  story elements, but canon does not predefine them.
- **Unknown:** Whether gods, ghosts, death, copies, memory alteration, or
  identity binding are involved. If the story introduces any of them, the
  relevant established framework must be honored; none should be inferred from
  the fairytale or transit imagery alone. (`universe/rules.md` — “Divinity”,
  “Afterlife and continuation”, “Memory, transformation, copies, and branches”,
  and “Names and identity binding”)

## Safe invention space

- Invent a contemporary city, neighborhood or hill called Briar Hill, municipal
  or private bus service, route number, stops, fare system, businesses, and
  street-scale geography. Keep these local and avoid claiming they define the
  modern-like era globally.
- Invent one bounded fairytale crossing layered over ordinary transit. Define
  who can board, what activates it, where it goes, what the rider must do, how
  return works, what failure costs, and whether it can recur. Do not identify it
  with the Glass Gate or use it to settle era chronology.
- Let phones, messages, maps, batteries, cameras, contactless fare cards,
  vehicle telemetry, or scheduling systems reveal, constrain, or transmit the
  anomaly. Ordinary technology can act according to technology; if a device
  channels or independently wields magic, supply living agency or establish
  that the device qualifies as alive.
- Invent a living driver, conductor, passenger, animal, plant, fairy, or local
  person-like construct as the magical agent. If a constructed or transformed
  being is involved, treat its personhood, consent, and relationships as real.
- Invent a local bargain, three-part test, taboo, gift, price, or transformation
  whose rules are consistent and materially consequential. State or dramatize
  enough of the rule that success and failure are causal rather than convenient.
- Use fairy kings, saints, godmothers, or similar words as cultural or
  story-facing descriptions if desired, but do not classify a being as a
  universe-taxonomy god unless it has the continuing conjunction of worship,
  power, and authority. (`universe/rules.md` — “Divinity”)
- Invent local folklore about the route and allow characters to misinterpret
  it. The story may leave cosmological provenance unresolved so long as its
  immediate mechanism and consequences are coherent.
- Keep the cast independent. No crossover, recurring institution, global rule,
  or deep-time explanation is needed for the prompt.

## Name constraints

- The prompt assigns no character-facing name. `Briar Hill` has no exact match
  in `stories/NAMES.md`; as currently used it is a place name, not a registered
  character alias. No authoritative universe entry establishes it as a
  location. `Baird`, `Bracken`, and `Larkspur` are the nearest
  spelling/thematic character-name reservations, but none is a readily
  confusable match for the place name. Do not silently reuse `Briar` as a
  person-like being's name without running the full character-name check.
- Avoid the transport-adjacent reservations from non-canon
  `the-station-between`: `Ruth`, `Asha`, `Hester Pike`, `Hester`, `Sabine
  Kroll`, and `Sabine`. That candidate creates no canon link, but its names
  remain reserved production memory.
- Avoid `Tair Ked` / `Tair` for a traveler or route surveyor, `Kest` /
  `Commander Kest` and `Oren` for road names, and `Highway` for a traveler,
  driver, or route-linked alias. These forms already identify distinct
  production-memory characters.
- If a driver, clerk, conductor, named vehicle, animal, magical helper, or
  person-like entity receives a title as its name, do not reuse `Captain`,
  `Night Clerk`, `Saint`, `the Rollcaller` / `Rollcaller`, `the Hollow Regent`
  / `Hollow Regent` / `Regent`, or `the Keeper Below` / `Keeper Below`.
  Ordinary lowercase role nouns remain usable when they are not functioning as
  character-facing names.
- Avoid the registry's unresolved collisions entirely: `Lena`, `Mara`, `Nisha`,
  `Pell`, and `Voss`. Adding another identity would compound an existing
  unresolved production defect. (`stories/NAMES.md` — “Known unresolved legacy
  collisions”)
- No deliberate name reuse is required by the prompt. Default to unique,
  readily distinguishable full names, given names, mononyms, nicknames,
  usernames, titles, named animals, constructs, and person-like entities.
  Register planned forms after planning; if a reuse becomes narratively
  essential, document whether the identities are the same or distinct, why the
  reuse matters, and how readers will distinguish them. (`stories/NAMES.md` —
  “Rules”; `universe/style-guide.md` — “Prose defaults”)

## Required checks after drafting

- Confirm the final story remains in the modern-like low-magic era and does not
  accidentally establish literal real-world Earth, another plane, a global
  magical society, or an era date that canon leaves unresolved.
- Identify every apparently magical act and verify that a living agent performs
  it. For each bus, phone, card, sign, ticket, camera, or other device involved,
  distinguish ordinary automation from stored/focused/transmitted magic and
  from independent magical agency.
- Verify the enchanted route's access, direction, limits, price, failure state,
  return condition, and causal role remain internally consistent. Search for
  language that accidentally calls it the Glass Gate, a universal portal
  network, or a proven route to another established era.
- Verify every bargain, test, gift, and transformation follows the local rules
  introduced for it. Check that desire, payment, names, and consent are not
  generalized into universal metaphysical laws and that magic does not erase
  the protagonist's choices or their cost.
- Check that folklore, repeated imagery, and fairytale archetypes are not used
  as proof of shared origin, crossover, or cosmological fact.
- If the story introduces a god, ghost, afterlife passage, sentient construct,
  copy, memory change, or name-binding effect, review it against the matching
  sections of `universe/rules.md` and the personhood protections in
  `universe/style-guide.md`.
- Confirm public knowledge and reactions fit a low-magic trough: any witnesses,
  recordings, institutional responses, or lack of wider awareness need a
  causal explanation at the story's scale, not a convenient universal cover-up.
- Search the final prose, title, metadata, and canon delta for every
  character-facing full name, given name, surname used alone, mononym,
  nickname, alias, username, title used as a name, named animal, vehicle,
  construct, or person-like entity. Reconcile all forms with
  `stories/NAMES.md`, resolve exact/alias/close collisions, and rerun the
  story-scoped name checker with
  `.agents/skills/story-name-validation/scripts/check-story-names.ps1 -Story the-last-bus-to-briar-hill`.
- Ensure any reusable new facts—especially the city, Briar Hill, route,
  crossing mechanism, living magical agent, and consequences—are reported as
  proposed additions in `06-canon-delta.md`, not treated as already established
  universe canon.
- Verify the treatment remains within the prompt's general/teen target and the
  universe's non-explicit content ceiling, and that consequence rather than
  spectacle governs any danger or violence.
