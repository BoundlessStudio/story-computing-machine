# Prompt contract

## Verbatim writing prompt

> **[WP] A fairytale about going on an adventure but set in modern day.**

## Story controls

- Working title: The Last Bus to Briar Hill
- Target length: 3,000–3,800 words; acceptable range 2,500–4,000 words
- POV: Close third person, centered on one protagonist
- Tense: Past
- Tone and genre: Contemporary fairytale adventure; warm, uncanny, suspenseful,
  and ultimately hopeful
- Audience/content rating: General/teen audience; approximately PG
- Required elements: A recognizable modern-day setting and infrastructure; a
  protagonist who deliberately leaves ordinary life for an adventure; fairytale
  logic, imagery, tests, and transformation; modern tools that matter to the
  plot without trivializing the magic; a complete beginning, middle, climax,
  and emotionally resolved ending
- Prohibited elements: A purely medieval or faux-medieval setting; “it was all
  a dream”; a synopsis or sequel hook in place of resolution; gratuitous gore;
  magic that conveniently erases the protagonist's choices or their cost;
  contradiction of authoritative shared-universe facts

## Assumptions

- “Modern day” means a contemporary urban environment recognizable through
  buses, phones, messaging, gig-work schedules, street lighting, and ordinary
  civic services, even if the story uses an invented city compatible with the
  shared universe.
- “Fairytale” means the story may openly use enchanted thresholds, bargains,
  impossible helpers, a three-part test, and a materially meaningful gift, but
  those elements should feel lived-in rather than parodic.
- The adventure will occur over one night so the story can travel meaningfully
  while remaining complete at short-story scale.
- The protagonist will begin with a concrete modern obligation and a credible
  reason not to leave it; choosing the adventure must reveal character rather
  than happen by accident alone.
- No romance, comedy, or specific folkloric retelling is required. Any such
  element will remain secondary to the adventure and transformation.

## Completion tests

- The result is a complete short story, not a synopsis or chapter fragment.
- The central promise of the prompt is dramatized and resolved: the protagonist
  knowingly crosses from an ordinary modern routine into a dangerous fairytale
  journey and returns changed.
- Modern systems and objects affect at least three causal turns rather than
  serving as decorative references.
- The fairytale structure includes a threshold, a rule-bound bargain or test,
  escalating trials, and a final choice whose consequences cannot be undone by
  convenience.
- The ending resolves both the external adventure and the protagonist's
  internal reason for undertaking it.
- Final prose remains within the agreed 2,500–4,000-word tolerance.
- The story passes canon and continuity review.
- Every character-facing name and alias is checked against `stories/NAMES.md`;
  any deliberate reuse has a documented narrative reason.
