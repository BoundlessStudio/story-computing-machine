# Outline

## Story

- Premise: On the fifteenth anniversary of the battle that killed King Veradan, fifteen-year-old Rielle enters the throne hall carrying the royal sword and claims to be the child of Veradan and Demon Queen Sarthene, forcing a kingdom governed through a long interregnum to choose between inherited fear and a living heir.
- Character want and pressure: Rielle wants the court to hear both parents' account and renew the peace their fatal duel began, but the lord protector needs the vacancy to preserve his authority, the court treats demon blood as proof of conquest, and Rielle can secure herself quickly only by using the sword as a threat.
- Turn: When the lord protector orders the sword seized and the divided guards near violence, Rielle gives up her only protection and invokes the sword's sealed testament; her refusal to win the throne by bloodshed moves the battle's surviving standard-bearer to confirm that the dying king freely entrusted the sword and his unborn heir to Sarthene.
- Ending: The opened testament proves Rielle's descent without declaring blood alone sufficient, so she accepts a constrained crown under a human-demon council and orders the true account of the battle published; the vacancy ends with the sword resting beside the throne rather than raised over it.

## Beats

1. During the public anniversary observance, Rielle walks alone through the throne hall doors with the wrapped royal sword; its recognizable battle damage stops the ceremony, while the visibly half-demon child and the empty throne give the court incompatible facts it cannot ignore.
2. Under guard, Rielle names Veradan and Sarthene as her parents and gives Sarthene's account: the rivals genuinely fought, Veradan received his mortal wound, and both chose to end the duel before their armies destroyed one another; with his last strength, he entrusted the sword and their unborn child to Sarthene until the child was old enough to choose whether to return.
3. The lord protector calls the account a theft designed to finish the demon conquest. Rielle draws the sword when challenged, activating its local dynastic mark and proving royal descent, but the court's fear deepens because the same proof gives demon blood a claim.
4. The protector orders the weapon seized as guards and spectators divide. Rielle could cut her way free, but instead lays the sword on the vacant throne's step and demands that the council open the testament sealed inside its scabbard, accepting arrest if the document fails.
5. That surrender breaks the protector's framing of her as an invader. The aged standard-bearer who survived the battle admits seeing Veradan give Sarthene the sword, and the authenticated testament names their unborn child as heir while requiring the future court to judge the child's choices rather than ancestry alone.
6. The protector offers recognition only if Rielle denounces Sarthene and rules as wholly human. Rielle refuses the false half-identity and instead proposes a limited first settlement: a mixed council, public testimony about the battle, and renewed negotiations with Sarthene, with the crown answerable if either people is treated as inherited enemy.
7. The council and guard captains withdraw support from the protector's continued interregnum and recognize Rielle. She takes the throne without erasing either parent, places the royal sword point-down beside it, and uses her first order to publish the truth and summon peace talks rather than punish her opponents.

## Continuity

- Canon used: No recurring canon is required; the kingdom, royal line, battle, interregnum, sword law, and settlement are local to this story.
- Boundaries and unknowns: Existing `archdemon` usage is explicitly local, so Rielle and Sarthene establish no universal demon biology, hierarchy, magic, or relation to Belphegor or Irix. The era, geography, wider human-demon history, Sarthene's later political response, and the new council's durability remain unresolved; no connection to another monarchy or succession crisis is implied.
