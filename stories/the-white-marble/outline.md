# The White Marble

## Promised experience

For three hundred years even the kingdom’s great sculptors have been forbidden to touch a white block said to contain something the gods feared. A young, violet-eyed apprentice strikes it by accident and hears three faint answering knocks. A whispering voice claims to be a prisoner and offers her the power to change history if she finishes carving the woman free. “Three faint knocks” suggests a physically intimate first contact, with “even the gods feared” and “change history” raising the possible cost and reach of the encounter. The apprentice and the woman owe us a bargain worth making and a choice with consequences. The terms, the woman’s nature and the ending are open.

## Takes considered

- **The lost gate.** An apprentice frees a woman who helped carve a divine victory into the world. Chosen for the way a hand emerging from the marble changes the bargain and puts the kingdom’s founding act within the apprentice’s reach.
- **The last apprentice.** A master testifies at her trial years later. The first knock took her back to the day of the prisoner’s sentencing, where the woman argued for a future she might never see. The trial would compete with the original encounter.
- **A face for a god.** A captive in an open-air carving yard offers to make the apprentice’s first independent work into the kingdom’s patron. The crucial decision is what face the prisoner will have to wear in order to be free.

## Provisional direction

The gods commissioned their living sculptor Sevra to carve Vennor’s founding on one end of an enormous white slab. Her chisel made things happen outside the slab. When she carved a divine hand shutting a district gate, the entire district dropped out of the world mid-evening. She had thought she was closing a real gate. The people were still alive behind it, including her daughter. The gods locked Sevra in the unworked end to keep her from opening the relief again. The king’s story is that the district surrendered.

Ketta, the apprentice, is secretly roughing out a royal likeness. Her hammer skids off a wedge and hits the unworked marble. Three faint taps answer under her palm. Sevra whispers that she’s been imprisoned and offers to teach Ketta to change what a deed carved on the block still does in the world. The relief includes the founding grant that restricts the royal guild to six families. Ketta’s mother can carve a face as well as anyone, but only performs unsigned work. With Sevra's art Ketta could incise her mother’s family sign beside the six and give them a rightful place. Once Ketta frees a wrist, Sevra guides her hand and a chip taken off the god’s finger opens a thin strip of the old gate right through the back wall. They hear someone shutting up a shop for the evening. The proof makes both cuts possible for Ketta.

The guild master who has expressly forbidden Ketta to work alone comes back with the men sent to collect the likeness. They can expose any cut, and once they see Sevra or the new doorway they will take the hammer away. Sevra would leave with her by the hoist stair once her body is free. Ketta has time to change the guild grant or finish severing the god’s hand. She opens the gate. The lost district and its people rejoin present Vennor, with the intervening centuries still lived. Ketta unbolts the front doors to give the returnees their way out into the kingdom. When the master takes them for Sevra’s doing, Ketta claims the cut in his hearing and in theirs. Sevra must speak to her daughter about what she did before she can ask to take her hand.

## Craft plan

**Teller.** Try close third past with Ketta’s absorbed, irritable attention against Ketta in first person years later, taking some relish in how much she wanted to get away with. Third lets the first knock disturb her reasoning. First might give the act of finally taking credit a voice.

**Ketta.** The original shows a young apprentice, grave and intent, leaning chest and weight into the stone with an open hand spread against it and her hammer arm extended. Bare feet, violet eyes, fair hair with a braid, loose red sleeves and a black apron gray with use. She braces, leans in, tests and holds her ground. Her palm establishes the wall’s sound, a loose sleeve catches where she reaches after the first deliberate cut, and bare feet feel the gate move through the shop floor. Her violet eyes belong in her human face, when a visitor is close enough to look. She knows her mother’s good work and the guild’s story of the conquest. She resents working under another’s name and thinks the best revenge would be a place in the guild herself. She asks specific practical questions, then runs on when ashamed of how badly she wants something.

**Sevra.** An easy talker who used to persuade people with considerable authority to stand still for their own likenesses. She manages listeners with digressions and the occasional unearned familiarity. She knows which cuts on the relief can restore what she took. She assumes Ketta will want to flee afterward and that her daughter's best chance is to leave the gate as it is rather than try to face her.

**The conversation owed.** In the long scene each woman says what she wants changed and asks whether the other will help. While she has a hand out and could withdraw it, Sevra tells in order how she took the gods’ commission, shut the gate and lost her daughter. Ketta is close enough to help her leave and will be able to use the art regardless of Sevra’s wishes.

**Place.** Vennor’s Carving House, over the exposed Ash Quarry and the public White Steps shrine. The shop gives illicit work a few hours of privacy and makes it obvious later who was left inside. In spring, low sun stripes the block, grit lodges between teeth, tallow in the hoist rags goes rancid, timber skids groan, and the threshold’s wheel ruts are cold to the sole. The long slab of white marble runs against the back wall, its relief carved on one end; the tall unfinished mass on its timber bed is the prison. The wall south of it has a worn wagon threshold with nowhere to go. During regular hours a master draws a chalk line across the rear bay and makes his own apprentices walk around it to reach the hoist stair. Rough work uses a point chisel, finer work a claw or flat edge. ## Scene briefs

1. **First knock, afternoon (20%).** In a stolen interval Ketta works on the likeness the court men are coming for before supper. Show her reaching dangerously around the chalk boundary to keep the heavy work steady. The stray hammer catches the taboo block. Ketta presses her hand over the spot, wanting to know how much damage she’s done. Sevra wants her to land a deliberate blow. She names her prison and offers a power Ketta can imagine putting to use.
2. **The deal, dusk, long scene (55%).** Ketta starts with a wrist; once the seam is struck the figure stands just beneath a skin of stone, and as Sevra pushes out Ketta works toward the shoulder and feet. She wants proof that she can change the six guild signs. Sevra wants out and tries to keep her bargain to teaching one test cut before they leave. The god’s finger chips, the shop wall passes evening air, and Ketta hears shutters. Let them stop long enough for Sevra’s account and for Ketta to ask her to stay and tell the district the truth. As Ketta frees Sevra, the master knocks and calls for her to unbolt the outer doors, and she hears the shoes of one of the court men on the hollow yard stair leading to the hoist door. She has time for one cut.
3. **The opening, dusk (25%).** Ketta chooses and frees the gate. Hold on the way one street and then the whole hillside comes back into sight from the shop. Its nearest people come through on foot. Keep the encounter to the guild master at the outer doors and Sevra’s daughter in the old gateway. Ketta brings the master in with her own hand on the bolt and gives her own name as the one who opened the gate. Sevra tells her daughter, who has never seen Vennor like this and wonders what happened while she was turning for home, that she originally shut it.

Summarize the shop’s long obedience and the official version through Ketta’s working knowledge. Leave palace and divine countermeasures outside the story. End at the old wheel ruts when Sevra's daughter takes her mother’s hand after hearing what she did. Her mother’s hand began as three knocks and became the means by which Ketta learned her power. Ketta now has her name on a work no one can take for somebody else’s.
