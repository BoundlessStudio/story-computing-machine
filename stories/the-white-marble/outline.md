# The White Marble

## Promised experience

For three hundred years even the kingdom’s great sculptors have been forbidden to touch a white block said to contain something the gods feared. A young, violet-eyed apprentice strikes it by accident and hears three faint knocks. A whispering voice claims to be a prisoner and offers her the power to change history if she finishes carving the woman free. “Three faint knocks” suggests small, unnerving first contact. “Even the gods feared” and “change history” suggest that this encounter between two people can reach far beyond the bench. The bargain deserves an exchange in which it becomes tempting, credible and dangerous. The terms of the power, the woman’s nature, and whether either gets what she first asks for are open.

## Takes considered

- **The lost gate.** In a sculptor’s shop built on the site of a divine victory, an apprentice frees a woman who helped carve that victory into the world. The long scene is their bargaining in cuts, as the prisoner risks showing the apprentice what she did. Chosen for the way a hand emerging from marble changes what each can ask of the other and what the apprentice can do to the kingdom’s founding act.
- **The last apprentice.** A master testifies at her own trial many years later. The first knock took her into the day of the prisoner's sentencing, where the woman showed her how to protect her future self. The central scene would be their meeting in the past as the prisoner tries to argue for a future she may never see. The trial would compete with the workshop for attention.
- **A face for a god.** In an open-air carving yard, the voice offers to make the apprentice’s first independent work into the kingdom’s new patron. In the long scene the apprentice discovers she can free the woman in any likeness she can make and has to decide whether to give her a face anyone can worship. Who gets to make whose face would become the chief tension.

## Provisional direction

Ketta is alone in the royal carving house of Vennor when her hammer glances off a wedge into the forbidden block. Sevra, the living woman inside, was the gods’ sculptor at the founding conquest. At their command she carved their hand closing a gate into a marble relief. The gate, the district beyond it and everyone on the far side disappeared from the world mid-evening. The court celebrates the district’s supposed surrender. Sevra had thought she was closing a real gate, and by the time she understood what she’d done the gods had put her in the unworked end of the same marble. They feared she could carve the event open again.

Sevra offers to teach Ketta how to recut a deed fixed in this marble and change what survives of that deed in the world. Ketta initially wants to change the decree that put the district’s sculptors and their children out of the royal guild. Her mother could carve a likeness, yet kingdom and guild treated her as a polisher. When Ketta cuts enough for Sevra to get a hand out, Sevra shows her how to nick away the tip of one carved finger over the gate. The corresponding edge of an open doorway appears in the solid wall behind them. A living street sounds through the sliver, with the noise of shutters being closed for the evening. From this point Ketta wants to know who would come through if she opened the gate the whole way. Sevra would sooner leave with Ketta by the ordinary door. Her own daughter was on the other side and is still the age Sevra remembers. To face any of them, Sevra must admit whose hand held the hammer.

Ketta finishes freeing Sevra. On the founding relief she undercuts the last finger across the gate rather than bargaining for an honored past for her mother. The lost district rejoins present Vennor, bringing the founding generation within walking distance of its official monument. The intervening three centuries have still happened. The apprentice changes which part of the founding night the kingdom has to live with now.

## Craft plan

**Teller.** Try close third past with Ketta’s absorbed, irritable attention to workmanship against Ketta in first person, years later, admitting with some relish what she wanted for herself. Third keeps the first knock unsafe. First might find a more particular way to tell the story of a woman whose name finally stuck to her work.

**Ketta.** The supplied image shows a young apprentice leaning her weight into a block taller than her waist, poised and intent, one hand spread on the stone and the hammer far out in the other. Her violet eyes and fair hair, part of it braided, are striking against loose red sleeves and a dark, dusted apron. Read her as physically confident and nosy enough to get nearer to a danger. She checks the accidental blow with her open palm and keeps that contact to hear the voice. A loose red sleeve gives away her trembling elbow after the first deliberate cut. Her bare feet feel the lost gate moving through the shared stone floor. The violet eyes matter when the earliest witness tells her to look through the slit for herself. Keep the braid, sleeves and working silhouette in natural prose. Ketta knows her mother’s work, the guild’s disdain, and the story of a surrender. She wants a royal piece with her own name on it and would hate to need someone else to secure it. She talks in probes and specific objections, then runs on when she's ashamed of how badly she wants something.

**Sevra.** An easy talker who spent years persuading powerful people to stand still long enough to have their likenesses made. Her pitches have room for asides and too much attention to her own ingenuity. She initially treats Ketta as someone she can hurry into finishing a job and has to learn to wait for a direct answer. She knows the relief, her part in making it and the act it can reverse. She assumes the only reunion she can bear is with someone who never knew what she did.

**The conversation owed.** Ketta and Sevra must tell each other what each would change if she held the hammer, and whether they can bear to give the other that choice. Let Sevra give the full account of her part in the founding while she already has a hand out and could withdraw it. Ketta is the first person in three centuries close enough to help her leave and the first who can hold the promise to account.

**Place.** Choose Vennor’s Carving House over the exposed Ash Quarry, where the exchange would have little chance of privacy, and the White Steps public shrine, where worshippers would be actors too early. In early spring, from afternoon into dusk, the workhouse makes official likenesses in its front bays and keeps the old relief and unworked white mass under a common back wall. Its oddity is a wagon-width threshold in front of solid marble, worn into shallow wheel-grooves older than the shop. Canted sunlight stripes the block, wool rags hold the smell of tallow from the hoist, fine grit catches between Ketta’s teeth, timber skids groan when weight shifts, and the back bay goes cold as daylight slips off it. The return of moving air through a blind wall is proof Ketta can trust over the tale. It also means she’ll have to move the work off the old threshold if anyone is to cross it. Point chisel for rough marks and finer cuts with a claw or flat edge. ## Scene briefs

1. **First knock, afternoon (20%).** Ketta is risking a few unauthorized minutes on the back of a royal commission, where her own work would be hard to dispute. Her glancing blow lands on the white block. She wants to check for a mark and pin down the sound before anyone comes back. Sevra wants one more blow right where it hit. She names herself as a prisoner and makes the offer. The moment to stay in is the apprentice with her palm flat, waiting for the stone to finish answering.
2. **The deal, dusk, long scene (55%).** Ketta wants a demonstration and insists on knowing where the relief’s maker got her particular skill. Sevra wants her body back and tries to bargain away the questions about the people beyond the gate. Working from the opened seam, Ketta exposes a wrist and then the chest and legs over the course of the argument. A first mark on the relief opens a strip of the old gate. The outside sound lets Ketta test the account against what Sevra can and cannot see. The last stretch is cutting the place where the prisoner’s shoulder meets the block. Let the carved woman ask Ketta to stop long enough to speak about the daughter and what she wants to say to her. Ketta finishes the release with a request of her own. She wants Sevra to stay for the opening and tell the returning people who carved the gate shut.
3. **The opening, dusk (25%).** Ketta makes the decisive cut herself. The room rearranges around a great open gateway. A street three centuries out of time makes its way to the doorway, with the kingdom beyond in a different dusk. Sevra wants to be allowed to speak for herself, particularly to her daughter. Ketta has to put down the hammer and make space by clearing the trestle as the arrival turns public. The long-silent first witness crosses the same ruts filled with marble dust in the first scene.

Keep the three centuries of guild practice and the celebration of conquest in working detail and Sevra’s account. Leave palace and divine countermeasures outside this story. End on the woman who taught Ketta to cut history taking the hand of the daughter she last saw crossing the threshold, in the same open passage whose first slit Ketta could barely get a fingertip into. Something enormous has come back into the world, and the two women now have someone before whom they can answer for their work.
