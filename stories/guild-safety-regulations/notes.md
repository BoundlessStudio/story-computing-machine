# Editorial memory — 2026-09-28

The prompt's surprise is a guild rule rewritten for those harmed by an adventurer's approved protection. The provisional outline uses a homeward clasp that pulls the person who most wants its wearer home into danger. This is a standalone guild; the prose should not claim a connection to Salken's dungeon inhabitants or other established groups.

## Outline first readings

**Codex reader, independent first read:** “The homeward clasp is a strong, memorable engine.” Iona's love is “both the evidence against the policy and the thing the device exploits.” The largest risk is that Tav “must remove his clasp” without a physical or magical reason. The reader also questioned what compels the guild to change and warned that “consent and a return plan” can become a pre-solved ending.

**Claude Opus, independent first read:** “The mechanism is the joke and the wound at once.” The ledger counting only the wearer supplies the reversal. The key concern: “The decisive choice belongs to the wrong person, or to no one”; Iona's role in the rescue is passive in the outline. Claude also asked where the stranded woman's husband is, what forces the rule change, and whether the woman might have chosen to come voluntarily while still rejecting the clasp's presumption.

The writer rejected the invented requirement to remove Tav's clasp and made Iona the knotwright who knowingly certified a wearer-only test. Codex agreed this gave her material choice, while warning that a replacement certifier could defeat a mere refusal. Claude withdrew its proposed crowded-ledge rule, agreeing the clasp's established operation already supplied the danger. The revised outline gives Iona a slower two-person rescue, Tav the choice to remove his existing clasp, and the safety officer a witnessed record that can support suspension. Both outline readers found this ready to draft. Codex wants Iona's complicity and the officer's authority made concrete. Claude's strongest remaining concern is that Iona's safer method delays rescue for the stranded wife; the wife must be allowed her own response to that cost.
