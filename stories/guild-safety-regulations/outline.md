# Provisional direction

The guild's approved *homeward clasp* saves an adventurer at the instant of a fatal fall by drawing the person who most wants them home into arm's reach. It offers that person no protection and no journey back. The guild's injury ledger counts only the wearer.

Iona has been snatched from her own life several times to haul her younger brother Tav out of danger. She loves him and wants him alive; she also wants the right to decide when and how to help. She brings a complaint to the guild just as another clasp leaves an adventurer's wife stranded on a perilous mountain route. Iona and Tav join the retrieval, partly because their argument cannot remain theoretical while someone else is still out there.

On the route, Tav's fear of dying without the clasp and Iona's fear of being made responsible for every risk he takes both have force. To reach the stranded woman, Tav must take off his clasp; the rescuers arrange an ordinary rope and an actual, willing second hand. The attempt should make the difference between being loved and being available visible through a choice under pressure, with a cost the characters feel. Afterward, the guild must rewrite its rules around the people the old rules omitted: no involuntary summons, and a plan to bring every helper home. The ending belongs to Iona and Tav rather than a triumphant regulation announcement; their care for each other remains, but its terms change.

Close third through Iona, with the guild's brisk official language providing occasional humor against the physical inconvenience and fear it conceals. The rescue and the relationship should carry the reversal beyond the initial joke.
