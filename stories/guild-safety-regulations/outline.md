# Provisional direction

The guild's approved *homeward clasp* saves an adventurer at the instant of a fatal fall by drawing the person who most wants them home into arm's reach. It neither protects that person nor carries them back. The guild counts injuries to wearers, not to the people the clasps summon.

Iona ties and certifies the clasps. She has been summoned more than once to save her younger brother Tav. She loves him; the clasp has kept him alive. She also knows what it has taken from her. She has complained, then kept putting her maker's mark on knots that pass the guild's narrow test of whether the *wearer* returns. She told herself she could bear an occasional frightening detour. Then another adventurer's wife is pulled from home to a high mountain shelf, helps her husband survive a fall, and is cut off by falling stone. His party brings the injured husband back; the guild records him as safe but classifies her as a witness rather than a casualty, delaying a search past its safest departure hour. He is in the infirmary, asking after her. The wife would have chosen to help him, but she had no chance to prepare and no way home.

Iona insists on a retrieval and brings the guild's safety officer along to witness it. A quick expedition with more clasps would risk leaving more loved ones on the mountain. Iona refuses to certify the new clasps offered for the rescue, an immediate choice that delays departure; she takes time to tie a line long enough to bring *two* people back and packs a coat for the wife. This does not disable Tav's existing clasp. He chooses to take it off when he understands that a slip would pull Iona into danger without her consent. Iona chooses to cross to the stranded woman herself, using the ordinary rope and her knowledge of knots. The slower rescue should cost daylight and put pressure on both of them; Tav is frightened and still has reason to be. The wife comes back as a participant, not an exhibit in Iona's case.

The safety officer must enter what happened to the wife in the guild's incident record, with her own account, while Iona withdraws her certification from the next batch. Another knotwright could eventually replace her, so a refusal alone cannot make a lasting rule. The documented harm and the rescue the guild had failed to mount make the old definition of a safe expedition untenable. The guild suspends the clasps and begins regulations that count every person a device puts in danger and require that person's advance consent and a practicable return route. No new magical device solves this on the spot. The ending should stay close to Iona and Tav: the pause in his protection is real, as is their care for one another, and neither can decide the other's part in the next adventure.

Close third through Iona; brisk guild language can provide humor where it rubs against actual cold, distance, and affection. The outline leaves room for the prose to discover a less tidy course or ending.
