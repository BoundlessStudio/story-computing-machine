# Outline

## Story

- Premise and central promise: At the sixtieth centennial dinner after three heroes attempted regicide, their descendants entertain the surviving queen with increasingly inflated family legends. Merovel brings the supposedly sacred wedding ring inherited from her mother. Opening its little gold hand reveals a name; the queen's worn companion hoop completes the clasped-hands marriage ring. Let the public discovery expose an ordinary, imperfect marriage inside six thousand years of myth.
- Focal pressure or attachment: Merovel, an adult widow, wants to tell her mother's favorite story well and restore dignity to their ancestor Ildren, whose kneeling features embarrassingly in the other families' accounts. She has brought the forbidden ring as evidence, enjoying the possibility of producing a relic when the others can produce only speeches. Her mother's recent death makes that borrowed authority tender.
- Counterforce or complication: The queen knows the dead people, but knows neither every intervening descendant nor what this guest wants from her. She has learned that rebuttals become further proof of her supposed enchantments. Her apparent indulgence conceals both affection and a taste for flattering lies. Other guests protect different pleasures: Osmer likes performing, while Beldan likes winning an argument and recognizes when someone is distressed.
- POV, distance, and information limit: Close third past through Merovel. Notice food, clothes, social placement, remembered maternal delivery, and the queen's hands. No access to the queen's memories except her spoken account; physical evidence establishes the paired ring, while motives and anecdotes remain witness testimony.
- Governing movement and time shape: One dinner, arrival through dessert. Expand the performances, ring opening, and untidy conversation following recognition. The past enters through present conversational pressure. Target 2,500–4,000 words.
- Speculative rule or ordinary-world constraint: The living queen is ageless, with red eyes and fangs; she cannot confer longevity. The ring has no power: two complementary gold hoops bear clasping hands; Ildren's gold hand hinges open over his spouse's engraved name. Durable gold and sheltered engraving need no magic. Genealogies are family claims maintained through changing names and adoptions.
- Dialogue promise: An extravagant dinner permits boastfulness, pleasure, embarrassment, and startling intimacy between a practiced hostess and guests who have inherited very different relationships with her dead husband.
- Dialogic medium: Spoken performances interrupted by table business, spontaneous questions, the queen's withheld corrections, and an object physically opened and paired in front of witnesses.
- Dialogue engine: Claims solicit polite assent until Merovel needs the queen to endorse a particular account. A refusal makes her challenge the family's taboo. The revealed name and matching ring alter who can tell the next story; disagreement about remembered people survives the factual discovery.

## Voice

- Narrative texture: Socially observant, materially sumptuous, lightly impatient with ceremonial fuss; move closer as Merovel hears her mother's cherished delivery fail in her own mouth. Use full paragraphs around attention and changing interpretation.
- Conversational texture: Osmer performs rolling legends and tolerates interruption badly. Beldan asks brisk questions, competes, and eats. Merovel begins with rehearsed grandeur, then loses her prepared order. The queen speaks with hospitable economy until a particular memory makes her digress.
- Rhetorical ownership: Public wit belongs chiefly to Osmer; Beldan punctures numerical claims. Merovel owns family loyalties and embarrassed doubt. The queen may enjoy being misremembered, evade an unwelcome truth, and tell a poor anecdote without becoming a solemn oracle.
- Pressure behavior: Merovel repeats a question when the answer disappoints her. The queen occupies her hands with service and initially supplies dates where feelings would be harder. Beldan can stop teasing without announcing why.
- Relationship movement: Merovel wants royal confirmation of her mother's story; the queen wants to hear something her husband left in his family. Neither can request that honestly at first. Recognition permits mutual curiosity without granting forgiveness for every historical act.
- Anti-default: Do not turn the table into a historical tribunal, a coordinated chorus learning one lesson, or a promise of future visits that substitutes for this evening's encounter.

## Beats

1. Arrive with the forbidden ring concealed in a glove. Establish adult Merovel's recent maternal loss, inherited performance, and pride in representing Ildren. Observe the queen's red hair, ivory lace, ring, appetite, and practiced attention. Image #1 supplies dinner grandeur, #2 regal scale, and #3 amused intimacy; avoid importing every prop.
2. Let the other descendants compete over the heroes' numbers, feats, weapons, and motives. The queen gives concrete evidence of century-by-century inflation without humiliating them. Meals, clothing, and the rebuilt room make elapsed time tangible. Preserve pleasure in storytelling even when accuracy fails.
3. Merovel tries the tale in which Ildren kneels only to recover his weapon and never begs. The queen's reluctance touches her pride. Merovel produces the ring, still closed, and explains the family prohibition. The queen recognizes it without taking possession.
4. Through questions and the queen's recognition of the small mechanism, establish that the taboo is inherited interpretation rather than operative danger. Merovel decides to open it herself. Reveal the sheltered name, then the queen's previously visible matching hoop and the physical fit of both hands.
5. Establish that Ildren's children predated his marriage to the queen; no new blood relationship or universal inheritance claim follows. His earlier attempt to kill her was real. Allow awkward questions about the intervening courtship, ordinary aging, marital irritation, and his eventual death. Explain why royal correction and the original household's hostility failed to preserve their marriage in family memory.
6. Bring Merovel's mother back through a specific inherited domestic anecdote or habit whose surviving truth matters differently now. Let Merovel and the queen disagree about one remembered characterization. The guests need not all believe, apologize, or become historians. Return both rings to their holders.
7. End within the dinner after the queen begins telling a concrete, imperfect marital memory and the guests respond as listeners. Let her pleasure and difficulty coexist. No historical reform, public revelation campaign, required future reunion, or explanatory moral follows.
