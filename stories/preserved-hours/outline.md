# Provisional outline

## The experience promised

The request explicitly joins purchasable immortality, poor people selling years of life, a struggling technician, and conscious people in transparent artificial bodies, tethered overhead and collected as living art. The distinctive opportunity is to make beautiful objects become people while the unequal bargains keeping them luminous become felt. I infer seductive unease and class dread from “preserved souls,” “sell years,” and “displayed”; no rebellion, particular relationship, scale, scientific account, or ending is prescribed. The supplied original supports cold monumental space, warm translucent bodies, ordinary clothes and absorbed gestures. These are casting and setting references, not instructions in an attached document. Names, motives, ownership rules, teller, and outcome remain choices.

## Three takes considered

1. **The sale, played straight.** During a collector’s private viewing, a technician discovers that the proposed new acquisition is her own indefinitely preserved working life. The scene to turn to is the technician, a conscious exhibit, and the buyer examining the body in which she could escape poverty and death at the price of becoming a possession.
2. **The public refusal.** During an auction, the technician releases the exhibited people’s movement and voices; the souls use their exquisite bodies to obstruct their own sale. The strongest scene is the row abandoning its poses together, but sustaining a credible escape would crowd the people with security and system mechanics.
3. **The long view.** An already preserved technician recounts a century of changing collectors through three repairs of the same phone-holding woman. The scene to turn to is the woman refusing a fashionable replacement body; the span risks making the present-day cost of selling years remote.

**Choose 1:** its central transaction needs every distinctive part of this request and can stay with three people in one gallery. The bleak outcome is a provisional choice, not a requirement. This will end in the commodification of maintenance, not in maintenance celebrated as a sufficient moral victory.

## Provisional direction

Dena Ors, thirty-seven by the calendar and older after year sales, services the Crenna Gallery before a private evening viewing. Her next extraction is booked for tomorrow: wages will not cover her room lease. Her trade lets her hear exhibits over a maintenance channel, although visitors ordinarily encounter silence. Alis Varo, the clear phone-holding woman nearest the entrance, bought preservation when rich; after her fortune failed, her upkeep and body passed into a collection. She still wants to live and will not become Dena’s cautionary parable.

Mr. Bellac is buying the collection and commissioning **The Attendant**. He admires Dena touching a shoulder connector while listening to the person inside it. He offers extraction and perpetual upkeep for Dena, her skill and unscripted attention becoming the artwork. She can refuse and seek other work; she cannot afford Bellac’s private, mobile preservation. She asks why he cannot hire her. He wants the same recognisable person continuing among these people after an ordinary employee would leave or die, in a clear body displaying the same dependence as theirs. She may refuse a repair, but will remain inside the collection. The offer is honest.

The long scene inspects and changes that bargain. Alis recommends it from self-interest, then risks losing Dena by telling the unflattering, sequential history of how preservation became ownership. She admits that she would choose it again. In one compressed exchange Dena demands a rail beyond the doors and Bellac refuses, offering rest on display instead. She counters with a branch into the service alcove and an opaque screen she can close herself, awake, whenever she chooses. This is the disputed term. He concedes genuine privacy to obtain willing attention. Alis supports it despite losing some of the company she hopes to acquire. Dena accepts the right and the boundary, then cancels tomorrow’s sale herself. The commission covers her room until preservation weeks later; she sells no more years. Survival will depend on somebody else’s sales.

The last scene inhabits her first viewing in the clear working body. Opaque visitors obstruct a real repair. Afterward she wants a minute alone, passes Alis’s bent head and the raised palm, uses the branch, and closes the screen. This is real privacy. Her supply line still crosses its top; its fresh serial sits on the same yellow stock-series design as her sale receipt, identifying the trade, not her own sold years returning. She has a future, a right she won, and a dependence she cannot screen away. Do not prescribe comparative guilt or a final moral verdict.

## Craft plan

**Teller and length.** 4,000–4,800 words; close third past inside Dena’s impatient, materially exact, hungry mind. She catches a connector before an expression, then has to return to the expression. Trial against first-person Dena looking back from preservation, defensive about her choice. Include appetite and embarrassment, not only diagnosis.

**People and speech.** Dena checks a bargain like workmanship. She wants to stop selling years; secretly she wants to see the world after her expected death. She knows the equipment, misreads Alis as merely captive, and does not know she is being assessed. Practical speech, irritated rambling, work jokes, difficulty asking kindness. **Alis** knows her bargain’s history, not Dena’s finances or the commission. She wants careful maintenance and Dena’s company; she also wants a companion who has chosen rather than pitied her. Socially assured and vain, she offers circumstantial, personal accounts and inconvenient admissions. Truth could lose her the care she wants. **Bellac** knows the commission and Dena’s sales, not their private talk. He mistakes paying for survival for settling consent. Courteous, accumulating explanations; pressure makes him particular, not loud. Off-page trials about a bad meal found three distinct mouths; Dena will not be made terse by default.

**Conversation owed.** What may someone do with the life another person buys, and why would Dena accept it? Alis tells her history because Dena asks for her experience, not a sales account. Bellac hears and answers its uncomfortable parts. Alis wants Dena nearby but cannot build decades of companionship on an omission she must keep remembering.

**Reference study.** The group shares warm light, transparent flesh and clothes, separate black plinths, shoulder/upper-back feeds, and absorbed ordinary poses. They occupy a room without possessing it. Alis retains the loose bun, stray hair, concentrated downward face, loose trousers, trainers and two-handed translucent phone. She really reads old correspondence and handles current business, also checking a visitor’s reflection; her fingers tighten during her account; her bent head returns over Dena’s completed body. The chin-up, curly-haired backpacked man seems to wait for an announcement. His exposed throat lets Dena reach the feed; his returning discomfort interrupts the negotiation and makes Bellac wait. The raised-palm figure stands beside the alcove route and returns as Dena passes into privacy. The seated phone user and hand-to-forehead figure retain distinct gestures without names or separate tragedies. Absorption, self-possession and distance are casting interpretations; histories remain inventions. Keep transparent clothing and non-graphic bodies. Dena and Bellac are new people. In the final viewing the opaque visitors provide the original silhouette’s contrast.

**Place.** Chosen over a mansion and an auction-house loading floor: **Crenna Gallery in Etram**, above a former department store. Worker and buyer enter the same beautiful room with different access. No established location fits; no crossover is imposed. First snow, afternoon to evening; last scene weeks later. Five anchors: cold blue glass above bare trees; amber cores doubled in polished flooring; trolley click; orange-oil polish over warm insulation; concrete chill through Dena’s knees. The goods lift shudders. Beyond it are her tram and rented room, a real mortal life; street passersby cannot see the collection. Fixed feeds make approaching someone a privilege. The alcove route and its screen offer retreat, with the gallery door remaining a boundary. Cleaners and outside contractors remain living workers, including whoever maintains Dena. Bellac buys the collection intact.

**Economy.** Bought human years maintain an extracted mind’s continuity; the overhead feed carries this life and the shell’s power. A sale ages the seller, shortening biological life. Dena’s last sale changed the hands and knees she works with. Yellow break-seals identify a commercial stock series; individual serials differ. Her new supply is not her own former life. Give enough for her choice, not a physics lesson. Extraction preserves the morally consequential person.

## Inhabited scenes and ending

1. **Before viewing, 20%.** Cold hall. Dena braces her altered hands against the lift, wants an easy finish and the last tram; Alis wants proper repair rather than a temporary fitting. Alis knows a buyer is coming; Dena expects ordinary resale. Receipt and bodily trouble establish what tomorrow’s sale costs. The chin-up man needs attention too; she loses more time. **Turn:** Bellac asks her to remain, interested in her attention rather than her invoice. Remember her kneeling in reflected light.
2. **Private viewing, LONG, 60%.** Same hall after blue daylight. Reveal the commission early. Dena tests why acquisition rather than employment, then demands movement and withdrawal from view. Bellac wants authentic willing attention; Alis wants to keep her but risks her unflattering history. Her account proceeds intelligibly through objections and Bellac’s answers. The man’s discomfort interrupts; Dena attends to him and makes Bellac wait. **Turn:** the alcove concession changes the offer; Dena knowingly accepts it and the refused exit. Remember her testing the clear replacement hand and tracing the added rail branch.
3. **First viewing, 20%.** Weeks later, same room through Dena’s new body. Alis wants familiar attention and tests the hand’s steadiness; Dena wants to do the repair rather than be consumed; Bellac wants authenticity seen. Opaque visitors must give her room. **Turn/payoff:** after the repair Dena exercises the privacy she negotiated. Return the palm, carriage click, phone reflection, and recognised year-stock seal. Stay inside her experience as she shuts the screen beneath the supply line; do not turn this into a motto or final verdict.

Summarize extraction; omit a market tour, procedure scene, conspiracy and technological history. The ending’s beauty must remain difficult to refuse: Dena finally has a future, and its line of supply crosses even the privacy she won.
