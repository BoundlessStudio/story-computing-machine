# The Year on the Wall — provisional outline

## Promised experience

The request explicitly puts a **struggling technician** among continuing human consciousnesses in transparent bodies, tethered overhead and exhibited to wealthy collectors, in an economy where the rich buy immortality and the poor sell years to survive. The later correction requires the technician to remain a living, unextracted human throughout. I infer an attraction to the beautiful, disquieting proximity of these two kinds of life: people treated as art, and a worker whose finite future makes their continuation possible. The image supplies cool monumental concrete, warm transparent bodies in ordinary young clothing and poses, black plinths, cables and phones. It establishes no fixed feelings or histories. Tone, scale, relationships, mechanism and outcome remain open; neither revolution nor quiet reconciliation is required.

## Three takes considered

1. **Sale night, played straight:** a living technician discovers the extravagant price collectors will pay for a year whose seller they can meet. With help from an exhibited woman, she sells directly at a private viewing. The central scene is an apparently successful auction in which everybody must say what, and whom, the money buys. **Chosen:** it brings the whole economy into the room without making the inhabitants mute evidence or requiring a transfer-industry conspiracy.
2. **Unpaid wages:** at the gallery's liquidation, the technician is offered wages in years siphoned from the inhabitants' reserves. The central scene pits her lost future against three conscious creditors in bodies she can switch off. Strong reversal, but its legal machinery and manufactured emergency threaten to occupy the story.
3. **The closing exhibition:** six immortals learn their collector will leave them awake only for viewings. They plan a last exhibition of freely chosen actions, while the technician decides whether to help them leave. The central scene is the contested departure at the limit of the cables. Strong ensemble image, but escape equipment would easily become the main problem and the sale of human years a background injustice.

## Provisional direction

Nera maintains the six residents of the Haldane Gallery. She is flesh, tired and technically good; previous lifespan sales have made her older than her birth date. She has an appointment to sell another year for the ordinary wholesale price because her wages no longer cover her room. A simple local rule joins the premises: a purchased human year sustains a preserved consciousness for a year; ordinary electricity operates the shell. The seller's body ages by the sold duration, and the sold year cannot later be restored. Transparent bodies permit indefinite continuation for those who can keep buying.

The gallery is selling a year's exclusive display of Clary, the foreground woman with the phone. Clary bought preservation when wealthy, spent her fortune, and now leases her presence to pay for continuation. Her phone shows Nera the collector's price for the year included in the sale: **12,000 marks**, against the **600** Nera's wholesale appointment offers. Clary proposes a direct, named sale in the room. She wants Nera's success, her own renewal, and the pleasure of making the dealer need her. Damil, the backpack wearer, discloses an earlier donor's history and his own participation: after buying her years, he paid for birthday visits and treated her eventual absence as a personal betrayal. He would rather Nera reject this plan than watch another donor cease to like a recipient. This is new knowledge about him, not a lesson about lifespan for an experienced technician.

The dealer welcomes their proposition as a new work. During the preliminary cuff check, Clary steps off her plinth to reach Nera's free hand without making her get up from the low stool. Seeing this, the buyer offers Nera **36,000 marks** if she also agrees to appear beside Clary whenever summoned on twenty-four hours' notice during the purchased year, with no limit on appearances. She would repeat that hand-to-hand pose: a mortal visibly changing beside an unchanged immortal. The money tempts Nera herself: she could leave the gallery job, not just secure her room. Clary encourages it. Nera attempts to bound the appearances, then refuses the unlimited term and risks the sale. Clary backs her, keeping her hand and cutting **6,000 from her own 18,000-mark exhibition fee**. The final purchase is **Nera's single year for 12,000, and twelve months of Clary's display for 12,000**, at the Haldane Gallery. The buyer acquires exhibition rights, the right to show Clary to his guests at booked viewings, and the year sustaining her. He acquires no right to summon, pose or exhibit Nera. Nera remains the gallery's technician, maintaining Clary and the others during her ordinary shifts; the buyer can incidentally see this work, but cannot commission or compel it as a paired performance. Four years' rent gives her material room to refuse without pretending she has left wage work. Clary still profits and the dealer sees a repeatable product. The lost year is not restored.

Nera performs the finite transfer using the gallery's standard donor cuff and service lead. She first climbs to the overhead feeder, identifies Clary's line, checks continuity and connects its service port; her competence permits direct intake without the wholesaler. On her low stool within Clary's reach, she fits the cuff and takes the baseline. Only after the bargain is settled does she confirm one year and begin. It passes through the feeder and down Clary's shoulder cable. Clary steadies her free hand while Nera monitors intake. The cuff disconnects on completion. No standing donor link, second transfer or threatened blackout: Nera remains flesh and conscious throughout. Her apprehension, waiting and altered bodily feeling get time. After checking Clary she can put the equipment away and leave, giving the buyer's later request to repeat the pose a distinct meaning.

## Craft plan

**Teller/voice:** close third through Nera, past tense, attentive to touch, wear and the absurd expense of things she must handle without owning. Warmly particular rather than arch or uniformly bitter. Trial against Nera's first-person account, told later with the defensiveness of someone explaining why she accepted the money. Approximate length 4,000–4,800 words; three scenes, one continuous central viewing.

**People and speech:** Nera explains practical things generously and understates need; pressure makes her specific about money. She wants Clary's help without a new obligation. She knows equipment and wholesale rates, not retail, and wrongly assumes preserved wealth lasts. Clary fills space, names luxuries and revises offers while speaking, often still studying her phone. She knows this buyer and the retail rate; wrongly thinks enough money will make the extra conditions acceptable. She wants Nera's trust, renewal and a successful placement, then forgiveness without earning it cheaply. Damil speaks slowly in concrete anecdotes; he wants to avoid a repetition of his own donor's rejection and resents being called a killjoy. He knows the retail trade, not Nera's appointment. The dealer is briskly encouraging. The collector speaks in patient, hospitable proposals: genuinely moved by the mortal/immortal pairing, he assumes concessions can always be priced. His failure is interested entitlement, not snarling cruelty. Neither needs an additional biography. No invented family rescue obligation.

**Conversation owed:** What is surrendered, what does the preserved person owe the living seller, and can payment buy later access to the seller? Scene two lets Nera, Clary and the buyer tempt, misunderstand and refuse. In scene one Clary's claim to generosity prompts Damil's tellable account, not cryptic facts or redundant instruction.

**Source casting:** ordinary contemporary young figures, not abstract statues: bun, phone and loose clear shirt/trousers in front; short curls, jacket, backpack and raised chin behind; seated phone use, a raised palm, bent/seated figures; an opaque visitor beyond. These show individual attention, not fixed boredom, grief or obedience. Clary's phone reveals the price; her lit chest changes at intake; her clear trainers step off the plinth so Nera need not rise during the baseline check. She need not take another ceremonial stride to back Nera; she keeps her hand while refusing the added term. Her bun and downturned profile recur over the bid she must reject; a lifted face can become action without proving a formerly evasive temperament. Damil hooks a thumb under his backpack strap during his account, follows the overhead work with his raised face, draws his cable taut moving toward the sale. His scrutiny includes an unwelcome personal stake. The other four keep physical and ordinary social presence, without each acquiring a diagnostic speech. A hand to a face need not be diagnosed to matter. Nera may be the distant opaque worker; her identity is invented. No established character is borrowed or renamed.

**Place:** the **Haldane Gallery, Bellfoundry Street, Merrow**, rather than a collector's hilltop house (hides the retail transaction) or airport exhibition lounge (dilutes private pressure); no shared-world connection. First cold week of November, afternoon to evening. Stay in the long hall and its overhead access. Staff prepare condition reports, surfaces and lighting. Five anchors: amber sternums against the blue tree-lined window; reflected cables on polished dark flooring; feeder whine; hot panel dust; citrus polish in Nera's throat. The silent passenger lift stops below the narrow service ladder and open service level. Guests can watch Nera climb and work directly above the plinths; ordinary labor becomes part of this viewing before she agrees to sell anything. Above each plinth is a metal feeder box with a meter and service port. Each resident can step roughly one stride beyond their plinth, not reach the window or exit; Clary uses it to reach the stool. Nera can walk past the window and will return for her next ordinary shift. Names locate; architecture, work and bodies supply particularity.

## Scenes and ending

1. **Before the viewing, 30%.** Nera's shoulder-socket service and Clary's phone-price revelation remain one interrupted encounter. Clary wants trust in a direct sale; Nera wants a safe price. Damil's account discloses his own entitlement and fear of rejection; it complicates both generosity and objection. Turn: Nera agrees to ask the dealer. Remember the screen through clear fingers. Earlier sales and arrears enter briefly through Nera's present work; omit separate clinic/home scenes.
2. **Private viewing, 50%, the long scene.** Begin with the dealer's eager approval. Food Nera normally clears, cold windows, climb and feeder connection visible to guests. Buyer and exhibit begin turning her care into an acquisition. The tempting offer must work on Nera; Damil's account cannot have decided her answer. Her attempted limit, refusal and Clary's contested support follow. Damil's later part can stay small and interested. Nera conducts the single-year transfer as staged above; stay for apprehension and bodily experience. Other residents can shift, disagree or fall silent without forming a panel. Turn: buyer acquires the named year and Clary's display at Haldane, not Nera's appearances. The dealer's interest in repeating the sale belongs here. No applause or converted crowd.
3. **Afterward, 20%.** Verify payment once, then Nera cancels the wholesale appointment and checks Clary's intake and shoulder. Their conversation addresses Clary's attempt to include her; affection, resentment and an imperfect reply, not “I know.” They remain technician and resident; this is no farewell. The buyer asks them to resume the transfer pose, rather than merely watching the actual condition check. Nera refuses the performance, puts away her kit and leaves at the end of her shift. Last image: her opaque, living body beyond the reflected cords, money received and chosen food in hand, with ordinary work to return to tomorrow. Let the sold year be present in how she gets there and how familiar food tastes, without prescribing injury or guilt. Load the exit with the service ladder's restriction, the refused summons and Clary's food invitation. Material relief matters alongside permanent cost.
