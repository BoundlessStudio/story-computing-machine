# The Year on the Wall — provisional outline

## Promised experience

The request explicitly puts a **struggling technician** among continuing human consciousnesses in transparent bodies, tethered overhead and exhibited to wealthy collectors, in an economy where the rich buy immortality and the poor sell years to survive. The later correction requires the technician to remain a living, unextracted human throughout. I infer an attraction to the beautiful, disquieting proximity of these two kinds of life: people treated as art, and a worker whose finite future makes their continuation possible. The image supplies cool monumental concrete, warm transparent bodies in ordinary young clothing and poses, black plinths, cables and phones. It establishes no fixed feelings or histories. Tone, scale, relationships, mechanism and outcome remain open; neither revolution nor quiet reconciliation is required.

## Three takes considered

1. **Sale night, played straight:** a living technician discovers the extravagant price collectors will pay for a year whose seller they can meet. With help from an exhibited woman, she sells directly at a private viewing. The central scene is an apparently successful auction in which everybody must say what, and whom, the money buys. **Chosen:** it brings the whole economy into the room without making the inhabitants mute evidence or requiring a transfer-industry conspiracy.
2. **Unpaid wages:** at the gallery's liquidation, the technician is offered wages in years siphoned from the inhabitants' reserves. The central scene pits her lost future against three conscious creditors in bodies she can switch off. Strong reversal, but its legal machinery and manufactured emergency threaten to occupy the story.
3. **The closing exhibition:** six immortals learn their collector will leave them awake only for viewings. They plan a last exhibition of freely chosen actions, while the technician decides whether to help them leave. The central scene is the contested departure at the limit of the cables. Strong ensemble image, but escape equipment would easily become the main problem and the sale of human years a background injustice.

## Provisional direction

Nera maintains the six residents of the Haldane Gallery. She is flesh, tired and technically good; previous lifespan sales have made her older than her birth date. She has an appointment to sell another year for the ordinary wholesale price because her wages no longer cover her room. A simple local rule joins the premises: a purchased human year sustains a preserved consciousness for a year; ordinary electricity operates the shell. The seller's body ages by the sold duration, and the sold year cannot later be restored. Transparent bodies permit indefinite continuation for those who can keep buying.

The gallery is selling a year's exclusive display of Clary, the foreground woman with the phone. Clary bought preservation when she was wealthy, spent her fortune, and now leases her presence to pay for continuation. She is neither secretly insensible nor innocently outside this economy. On her phone she shows Nera the collector's price for the year included in the sale: many times what Nera will receive. Clary proposes naming Nera as the year's source and having her sell it in the room, with the proceeds paid directly to her. She wants Nera's success, her own renewal, and the pleasure of making the dealer need her. Damil, the standing figure with the backpack, challenges the kindness: a better price does not make Nera lose less life.

The dealer welcomes the proposition as a new work rather than suppressing it. In the long private-viewing scene, Nera attempts to sell **one year**, while the collector tries to acquire the technician's continuing availability and the intimacy between donor and exhibit as part of the art. Clary initially encourages this because the additional price is enormous. Nera must refuse those additional terms plainly, even at the risk of losing the sale; Clary must choose between completing her prestigious placement and backing the limit Nera set. She backs it, at a lower commission to herself. The collector buys the named year anyway. Nera makes a materially better bargain and can stop selling years for the foreseeable future, but her year is still gone, Clary has profited too, and the dealer is already thinking about the next seller. The ending gives this compromised success its full human weight rather than converting the room or solving the trade.

## Craft plan

**Teller/voice:** close third through Nera, past tense, attentive to touch, wear and the absurd expense of things she must handle without owning. Warmly particular rather than arch or uniformly bitter. Trial against Nera's first-person account, told later with the defensiveness of someone explaining why she accepted the money. Approximate length 4,000–4,800 words; three scenes, one continuous central viewing.

**People and speech:** Nera explains practical things generously but understates her own need; when pressed she gets specific about money rather than eloquent about class. She wants Clary to help her get paid without turning gratitude into another obligation. She knows the equipment and wholesale rate, wrongly supposes rich bodies have finished needing money, and does not know the retail price. Clary fills space, names people and luxuries, revises offers while speaking, and is charming enough to make a bad bargain sound like a shared adventure. She knows the collector and retail price; wrongly supposes a sufficiently large payment will make the extra conditions acceptable. She wants Nera's trust and participation, and later her forgiveness without having earned it cheaply. Damil speaks slowly and concretely, with anecdotes rather than maxims; he wants the women to admit what their attractive plan costs and resents being treated as a killjoy. He knows renewal has made him complicit too, but not Nera's appointment. The dealer and one collector are functional supporting speakers, not additional life stories. No invented family rescue obligation.

**Conversation owed:** What is the technician actually surrendering, what does the preserved person owe the living seller, and can payment buy access to the seller afterward? Scene two gives Nera, Clary and the buyer time to negotiate, misunderstand, tempt and refuse. Damil's account of an earlier donor belongs in scene one: Clary has called their proposal generous, and he has a particular reason to question that word. It will be a tellable incident in his own idiom, not four cryptic facts.

**Source casting:** the group comprises ordinary contemporary young figures, not abstract statues: a woman with a high bun, loose clear shirt/trousers and a phone; a short-curled standing man in a jacket, backpack and trainers, chin raised; a seated, forward-leaning phone user; a leaning figure with a palm to the wall; two further bent/seated figures; an opaque visitor at the far end. The poses suggest absorbed individual attention and shared proximity, not an established diagnosis of boredom, grief or obedience. Clary is the first woman: her phone makes the price revelation possible; her lit chest changes when Nera's year enters her supply; her clear trainers leave the prescribed position when she backs Nera. Her bun and downturned profile recur as she studies the bid she now has to reject. Damil is the backpack wearer: thumb under his strap when he begins his account, raised face following Nera's work overhead, cable drawing taut when he steps toward the sale. Their expansive/scrupulous temperaments are inventions, not claims about the image. The other four retain their distinct poses and contribute ordinary comments and interruptions as people sharing a room. Nera can be the distant opaque worker, but her identity is wholly invented. No reference character is renamed from established canon; no shared-world character is borrowed.

**Place:** choose the **Haldane Gallery on Bellfoundry Street, in the independent city of Merrow**, rather than a collector's hilltop house (would hide the retail transaction) or an airport exhibition lounge (would dilute the private pressure). No connection to an established universe location is implied. First cold week of November, late afternoon through evening. All action stays in its long display hall, including the service access above the plinths. Staff make condition reports, clean fingerprints, adjust lights and prepare the private viewing regardless of Nera's problem. Five anchors: warm amber sternums against blue window light; cables reflected in the polished dark floor; the high, insect-like feeder whine; hot dust at the service panel; citrus polish caught at the back of Nera's throat. A passenger lift stops short of the service level, so Nera carries her kit up a narrow ladder while collectors use the silent lift below. The aligned plinths and short cables keep residents visible to buyers and make solidarity a physical action: they cannot simply gather around Nera. By the end the hall will contain a profitable new kind of exhibit, but Nera can walk beyond the reach of every cord.

## Scenes and ending

1. **Before the viewing, 30%.** Nera services Clary's shoulder socket above the first plinth; the phone's prices undo her assumptions. Clary wants her to cancel the wholesale appointment and trust a direct sale. Nera wants a safe price, not a grand gesture. Damil interrupts from his plinth, gives his donor account and forces Nera to state that the year is lost even if the price improves. Clary brings the dealer their proposal. Turn: the dealer approves eagerly, revealing that the rebellion can itself be sold. Remember the phone screen visible through Clary's fingers. Summarize Nera's earlier sales and room arrears within this work, not in separate clinic/home scenes.
2. **Private viewing, 50%, the long scene.** Warm bodies, cold windows, food Nera normally clears after the guests. Nera must sell in front of the six people she services; Clary must negotiate as both artwork and interested party. The buyer's attractive larger offer gradually includes Nera's future visits and continuing personal availability. Nera says the actual boundary aloud; Clary first tries to persuade her, then rejects the added terms and sacrifices her own commission. Damil refuses to let the room euphemize the bodily cost. The other inhabitants interrupt, disagree or make room within the lengths of their cables. Turn: one year is sold at a fairer price, the buyer gets a year of Clary, and Nera has refused becoming part of what is owned. No applause or mass change of heart. Give the actual transfer, the wait afterward, Nera's physical apprehension and her receipt time on the page.
3. **Afterward, 20%.** The dealer offers to repeat the performance with new sellers. Nera does not pretend she has ended the trade. She checks her payment, cancels the next wholesale appointment and finishes the necessary check that Clary is well. Their final conversation addresses Clary's attempt to include her in the deal; affection and resentment can coexist, and neither says a closing “I know.” Clary's new buyer asks them to resume the touching, useful pose in which the year was transferred. Nera is allowed to refuse and leaves the plinth. Last image: Nera's opaque, still-living body passes out of the reach of the warm reflected cables, carrying the money and the ordinary food she chose from the viewing. Load this with her restricted movements while working above the residents, the proposed continuing-access condition, and Clary's earlier invitation to share the guests' food. The year remains irrecoverable; what she bought with it is room to live some of the rest.
