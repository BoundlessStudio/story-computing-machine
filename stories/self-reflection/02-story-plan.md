## Premise and promise

- **One-sentence premise:** After an anxious teenager's one unguarded wish
  branches a second, fully autonomous version of her into being, Nina has four
  days to stop treating Anin as a safer substitute for human connection and
  honor her as a person before the anomaly that made her comes apart.
- **Story promise:** A close, funny, painful speculative story about seeing
  oneself from the outside, discovering that perfect mutual understanding does
  not erase another person's independence, and choosing honest connection even
  when it cannot prevent grief. Anin is not a symptom, imaginary companion, or
  disposable rehearsal; she has her own post-branch memories, preferences,
  relationships, choices, fear, and loss.
- **Central dramatic question:** When the only person who understands Nina
  perfectly begins to disappear, can Nina love Anin as a separate person and
  then let imperfect friends know something true about her, or will she keep
  using Anin as a proxy and retreat behind social performance?
- **Meaningful change:** Nina begins convinced that she is intrinsically hard
  to like and that connection requires a flawless performance. She initially
  treats Anin as evidence about herself and then as a substitute who can perform
  Nina's life better. She ends unable to save Anin and unwilling to reduce her
  to a cure: Nina names the loss, speaks honestly to her friends, and accepts
  their imperfect answers as the beginning of a relationship rather than a
  verdict on her worth.

## Story controls

- **Form and length:** Complete short story planned at approximately 3,600
  words, within the required 3,000–4,000-word range.
- **POV and tense:** Close third person past, restricted to Nina. This preserves
  the creative input's dominant and most effective viewpoint. Anin's interior
  is never narrated as if shared; her personhood is shown through what she says,
  chooses, remembers, refuses, and makes.
- **Narrative distance:** Intimate enough to catch Nina's rapid self-monitoring
  and dry associative humor, but clear about bodies, speakers, locations, and
  post-branch knowledge. Once Anin chooses her name, narration uses `Anin`, not
  “the other Nina,” except in a quoted moment that is explicitly challenged.
- **Tone and genre:** Character-centered contemporary speculative fiction in
  the modern-like era; warm, wry, psychologically precise, and grief-aware.
  The impossible event is concrete, while its widest cosmological explanation
  remains bounded and uncertain.
- **Audience and content:** Teen. Social anxiety, bereavement, fear of erasure,
  and a non-graphic disappearance are permitted. No self-harm, explicit sexual
  material, body horror, or gratuitous distress.
- **Setting scope:** An undated school, home, and neighborhood in a
  contemporary-looking part of the modern-like era. Do not name a real-world
  country, city, brand, or calendar year and do not assert literal Earth.
- **Opening target:** Begin with Nina waking to a second breath and recognizing
  her own scar, freckles, and face on the girl beside her. Within the first 500
  words, establish the prior night's lonely statement, the bodily sensation
  that located the anomalous act in Nina rather than the mirror, and the two
  girls' shared memories only up to that moment.
- **Ending target:** After Anin's irreversible disappearance, Nina chooses to
  tell her friends one unpolished truth. Their response is imperfect but real.
  At home, Nina preserves Anin's words and moves her surviving bracelet to the
  lamp as a memorial; nothing restores Anin, certifies her fate, or turns her
  death into the price of Nina's improvement.

## Character engine

- **Nina — protagonist and close-third viewpoint**
  - **Concrete desire:** Keep the impossible duplication secret long enough to
    restore her ordinary life without exposing herself to her mother,
    classmates, or anyone who might judge her.
  - **Immediate tactics:** Hide Anin, test mirrors and bracelets, preserve their
    apparent interchangeability, and eventually let Anin attend school in her
    place so Nina can learn how a less frightened version of herself behaves.
  - **External stakes:** Discovery could make both girls unsafe; substitution
    could damage Nina's friendships; and Anin's growing transparency signals a
    deadline Nina cannot bargain away.
  - **Emotional stakes:** Nina may lose the first person who understands every
    context behind her fear. Worse, she may spend Anin's entire short life
    treating her as a function of Nina's loneliness instead of someone worth
    knowing in her own right.
  - **Blind spot:** Nina thinks perfect understanding is the prerequisite for
    love, and interprets every social pause as proof that others reject her.
    This makes Anin seem both ideal and threatening: safe because she shares
    Nina's past, intolerable because she can choose differently afterward.
  - **Internal movement:** Self-disgust and performance, then possessiveness
    and envy, then recognition of Anin's independent personhood, grief without
    metaphysical consolation, and finally one voluntary act of honest contact.

- **Anin — autonomous branch and co-lead**
  - She begins with Nina's body, clothing, bracelet, and memories through the
    previous night's anomalous moment. From her first waking breath onward,
    sensations and memories diverge; neither girl receives the other's new
    knowledge.
  - **Concrete desire:** Have the time available to her recognized as her own,
    including a chosen name, choices Nina would not make, and relationships in
    which she is not merely acting as Nina's substitute.
  - She chooses `Anin`, Nina reversed, to acknowledge where she came from while
    drawing a boundary: continuity is not sameness. The name has no magical
    force and does not decide her ontology.
  - Anin initially agrees to one day at school because she wants a day outside
    and believes her shared memories make the risk manageable. She later
    refuses any further substitution after seeing that impersonation harms
    both identities.
  - Her choices should be specific and modest: she tells their mother the truth
    rather than remain hidden, chooses the green hoodie, likes strawberry jam
    Nina avoids, asks about their father, writes a page in her own emerging
    voice, and decides how to spend her final hours.
  - Her fading follows the anomaly's fixed local instability, not Nina's need,
    affection, honesty, or recovery. Anin explicitly rejects the idea that she
    is disappearing because her “job” is complete.

- **Their unnamed mother — witness, parent, and bounded adult ally**
  - A hospital night worker grieving her late husband and trying to parent
    while exhausted. Keep her unnamed and use only ordinary relational
    descriptions such as `her mother` or `their mother`, never a capitalized
    kinship title as a proper alias.
  - She is frightened and practical but recognizes both girls as people. She
    checks breathing and temperature, calls in sick during the final night,
    listens to their competing accounts, and does not hand the story over to
    an institution or solve the anomaly.
  - Her admission that she once wished for two versions of herself after her
    husband's death echoes Nina's loneliness without activating magic. That
    failed comparison helps establish that wishing alone is not a general law.
  - Her independent relationship with Anin matters: Anin learns a story about
    their father while Nina is at school, so Anin possesses a memory Nina does
    not. Their mother later mourns Anin as a daughter she knew briefly, not as
    an extension of Nina.

- **Jocelyn Park — birthday host and closest school friend**
  - Jocelyn welcomed Nina at dinner but reads Nina's guardedness as dislike.
    Her final admission, “I thought you hated us,” reveals a reciprocal
    misreading rather than instantly curing Nina's anxiety.
  - She responds to Nina's final honesty with a concrete invitation and a
    boundary—Nina can say when she needs quiet, but Jocelyn cannot guess—which
    offers a relationship practice rather than absolution.

- **Ben — classmate and comic pressure release**
  - His driving-instructor anecdote gives Nina a low-stakes social scene and
    lets an accidentally true statement survive a brief silence. His joking
    response keeps the story humane without dismissing Nina's distress.

- **Daphne — observant school friend**
  - Daphne replaces the colliding input form `Nisha` before prose. Her gentle
    “Are you okay?” feels like a spotlight to Nina but is a sincere attempt to
    connect. In the ending she offers Nina a pudding cup because emotional
    honesty makes her awkward too, showing that Nina is not the only person
    improvising.

- **Their unnamed father — remembered family member**
  - He died years earlier. Use only lowercase relational phrases such as `her
    father` or `their father`; give him no personal name or title-as-name.
  - The mother's remembered detail that he sang fake opera while cleaning is
    an ordinary, affectionate fact Anin learns after the branch. It provides a
    clean knowledge-divergence test and connects the family's present strain to
    grief without making the father a supernatural presence.

- **Opposition:** No villain is needed. Opposition comes from Nina's social
  anxiety and need for control, the practical danger of two visible Ninas, the
  ethical damage of substitution, Anin's independent desires, family grief,
  and an irreversible local anomaly whose deadline no character can command.

## Causal arc

1. **Beginning state:** At Jocelyn's birthday dinner, Nina performs every
   smile and sentence until ordinary kindness feels like an exam. Home alone
   with a mirror, she says that perhaps the only friend she can make is
   herself. As she says it, she experiences a second inhalation pulling from
   behind her sternum toward the reflection. She laughs it off and sleeps.
2. **Inciting disruption:** Nina wakes beside a bodily identical girl who has
   the same memories only through that second inhalation. Mirror tests show two
   ordinary reflections; covering the mirror and removing a bracelet do
   nothing. The new girl chooses `Anin`, rejecting Nina's attempt to call her a
   copy and immediately creating a relationship Nina cannot fully control.
3. **Escalating turn one:** Nina leaves Anin hidden while she goes to school.
   Anin refuses to spend her first day in a closet and tells their mother the
   truth instead of impersonating Nina. When Nina returns to find them sharing
   toast, secrecy inside the family is over. Their mother's frightened but
   explicit recognition of both girls prevents Nina from dismissing Anin as a
   hallucination.
4. **Escalating turn two:** Nina's accidental admission at lunch—she is “bad at
   being in rooms”—produces a pause but not rejection. At home, however, she
   focuses on Anin's apparently easier conversation with their mother. The
   girls agree to one school-day substitution: Nina wants evidence about what
   she does wrong, while Anin wants a day outside as a person, even under the
   wrong name. Their incompatible motives plant the coming break.
5. **Midpoint / causal hinge:** Anin spends the day in Nina's face, using only
   memories available at the branch point. She must improvise when Jocelyn
   refers to Nina's later lunch admission, proving that knowledge no longer
   transfers. When Anin returns, she says Jocelyn thinks Nina is angry and that
   Nina's constant performance gives friends nothing reliable to answer. Nina
   hears only that Anin performed Nina better and accuses her of enjoying a
   stolen life. Anin refuses another substitution. As she raises her hand to
   end the argument, lamplight shows through her fingertips.
6. **Escalating turn three:** The family tests every plausible object and
   emotion. Repeating the sentence, reversing it, covering the mirror, changing
   Nina's mood, separating the bracelets, and their mother's wishing all fail.
   Anin's copied bracelet fades at the same rate as her body while Nina's
   remains solid; the borrowed green hoodie remains ordinary. The evidence
   constrains the cause: Nina's one-time living act produced a temporary
   branch, but no nonliving focus controls it and emotional “need” does not set
   its duration.
7. **Crisis:** Near the fourth dawn, Nina insists they keep testing or try to
   create another Anin. Anin refuses to spend her remaining time as an
   experiment or to endorse making a replacement. She says plainly that Nina's
   need is not killing her and Nina's improvement will not save her. She asks
   Nina to stop asking what Anin is *for*, listen to the memory and preferences
   that now belong only to her, and promise one true sentence to someone else
   tomorrow—not as payment, but as Nina's own choice.
8. **Climactic choice:** Nina stops the tests although doing so means surrendering
   the fantasy of control. She apologizes without asking Anin to reassure her,
   reads the page Anin wrote in her own voice, and asks how Anin wants to spend
   the remaining hour. The three sit together; Anin writes `I AM REAL` on her
   own arm, Nina writes `SAME`, and Anin adds `YOU TOO` to Nina's forearm. When
   Anin disappears, the words on her body and her duplicated clothes vanish;
   `YOU TOO`, the borrowed green hoodie, and her saved page remain. Her fate
   after disappearance is unresolved.
9. **Resonant aftermath:** At lunch, Nina nearly repeats her old evasions. She
   chooses instead to say she left Jocelyn's party because she was afraid of
   being seen failing, that she has lost someone, and that she needs her friends
   to stop guessing while she practices speaking. This causes Jocelyn to admit
   her own misreading, Ben to lower the stakes with a joke, and Daphne to offer
   clumsy kindness. Their responses do not prove future ease, but they create a
   truthful next interaction. At home, Nina finds her mother's note—`Love you.
   Both of you, somehow.`—preserves Anin's page, and ties her surviving bracelet
   around the lamp. She then begins an English response in her own words.

## Setup and payoff

- **The second inhalation:** In the recovered memory of Nina's mirror statement,
  a pull behind her sternum and a doubled breath locate the anomaly in a living
  person. Later object-only and mother-wish tests produce nothing, paying off
  that distinction without granting Nina a general power she can reuse.
- **Two reflections:** The bathroom mirror shows two ordinary reflections and
  behaves the same when covered. Reflection remains a thematic image of
  self-scrutiny, not an independently magical artifact or evidence of another
  known system.
- **Shared-memory cutoff:** Early, both remember the lonely sentence, but only
  Anin knows what she ate with their mother and only Nina knows what she said at
  the first school lunch. The school substitution forces Anin to improvise
  around that missing knowledge; no later scene silently restores sharing.
- **The reversed name:** `Anin` initially sounds like a clever rearrangement to
  Nina. It pays off as a statement of related but separate identity: Anin can
  honor continuity without accepting the label of copy. It never triggers,
  stabilizes, or explains magic.
- **Strawberry jam:** Nina avoids it; Anin tries and likes it while eating with
  their mother. The tiny difference prevents personality divergence from
  becoming a grand destiny and gives Nina something specific to remember that
  was never hers.
- **The green hoodie:** Anin selects an existing hoodie rather than duplicated
  sleepwear. It remains collapsed on the bed after her body disappears. Nina
  wears it at the final lunch as a chosen memorial, not as identity fusion.
- **The bracelets:** Both girls wake with an identical museum bracelet. Anin's
  bracelet becomes transparent with her; Nina's does not. Separating them,
  swapping wrists, and taking them away from the mirror changes nothing. Nina
  later ties her surviving bracelet around the desk lamp in remembrance.
- **Written words:** Ink on Anin's fading body vanishes with her, while words
  Anin puts on Nina's skin and in an ordinary digital document remain. This
  tracks matter consistently and leaves testimony without implying that the
  writing contains a soul or magical residue.
- **Their mother's old wish:** Her statement that she once wanted two selves
  creates an apparent parallel. Its complete lack of effect, then and during
  the tests, pays off the canon constraint that desire alone is not a universal
  manifestation mechanism.
- **“Bad at being in rooms”:** Nina's accidental first truth receives an
  awkward but survivable response. At the final lunch, she chooses a fuller
  truth and remains present through the silence that follows.
- **The father's fake opera:** Anin learns the anecdote while Nina is absent.
  Later Nina must ask Anin to tell it, demonstrating that Anin accumulated a
  life not available through shared memory.
- **Structural-integrity joke:** Nina's strange observation about fries lands
  awkwardly at the birthday dinner but is recalled affectionately by her
  friends at the ending. The same trait can be misunderstood or liked depending
  on whether Nina lets it be genuinely hers.
- **The mother's note:** Her routine `Love you` appears before the duplication.
  The final `Love you. Both of you, somehow.` confirms Anin's relationship and
  loss without explaining where she went.

## Canon constraints

- Place the story in one physical world during an undated modern-like era. Its
  school, hospital work, phones, and ordinary home life may feel contemporary,
  but the prose must not call the setting real-world Earth or pin it to a
  real-world city or year (`universe/premise.md` — “The deep-time shared
  world”; `universe/locations.md` — “The world across eras”).
- Every operative magical act must come from living agency. Nina's embodied,
  one-time act at the mirror creates the branch. The mirror, bracelet, sentence,
  ink, and digital page are nonliving context or evidence and never initiate,
  maintain, reverse, or redirect magic (`universe/rules.md` — “Living agency
  and magic”).
- Do not state that wishing, loneliness, jokes, self-knowledge, or emotional
  need generally manifests people. This is one bounded anomaly with local
  behavior; the mother's failed wish and the failed repetitions should make the
  limitation legible (`universe/rules.md` — “Costs, desire, and local
  mechanisms”).
- Treat Anin as a person at every stage. She consents to one substitution,
  revokes that consent, makes independent choices, develops memories and
  preferences, fears disappearance, leaves testimony, and is mourned by two
  witnesses. Do not call her a cure, practice run, therapeutic tool, imaginary
  friend, empty reflection, or meaningless error except as a character's
  harmful idea that the story corrects (`universe/style-guide.md` — “Personhood
  and moral consequence”).
- Keep the ontology local and modest. Nina and Anin may call the event a branch
  or duplication as provisional language, but the narration must not establish
  universal rules for copies, souls, memory, or identity (`universe/rules.md` —
  “Memory, transformation, copies, and branches”).
- Do not make Anin's fading respond to Nina's reduced need, increased honesty,
  love, apology, or promise. The observed deterioration begins before Nina's
  change, proceeds through every emotional state, and ends on its own fixed
  course.
- Do not assert that disappearance equals absorption, annihilation, survival,
  reincarnation, or passage to the afterlife. Nina and her mother can mourn a
  death-like loss while admitting they do not know Anin's ultimate fate
  (`universe/rules.md` — “Afterlife and continuation”).
- Reflection, reversed spelling, paired bracelets, and doubled imagery are
  motifs within this story only. They establish no link to another artifact,
  crossing, deity, or anomaly (`universe/rules.md` — “Artifacts, motifs, and
  folklore”).
- No anomaly authority, magical organization, crossover character, or global
  lore explanation is required. Keep all inventions household- and
  school-scale (`universe/premise.md` — “Knowledge and connection”;
  `universe/style-guide.md` — “Tonal range, scale, and connection”).
- Preserve the Teen ceiling. Render fading through translucency, light, touch,
  and absence without gore or spectacle (`universe/style-guide.md` — “Audience
  and content boundaries”).

## Proposed inventions

All of these are **story-local proposals**, not established facts and not rules
for other anomalies:

1. Nina lives with her mother and attends an unnamed school in a
   contemporary-looking part of the modern-like era; her father died years
   before the story.
2. During one unguarded self-directed statement, Nina's living agency produces
   a single embodied branch. A bodily doubled-breath sensation is the only
   positive indication of the operative locus. The mirror and bracelet do not
   act independently.
3. Nina and Anin begin with matching bodies, worn clothes, bracelets, and
   autobiographical memories through the branch point. They are immediately
   separate conscious people; all later sensations, choices, learning, and
   memories diverge.
4. Anin is warm, tangible, visible to other people, capable of eating and
   writing, and physically ordinary until progressive translucency begins.
   These observations establish her bodily presence without settling whether
   `branch`, `copy`, `projection`, or another term is metaphysically correct.
5. The branch is locally unstable and lasts through four dawns. Anin and matter
   duplicated with her gradually lose opacity and then disappear. Existing
   objects she borrows and ordinary records she creates remain. No character
   learns why the duration has this limit.
6. Repeating or reversing Nina's statement, making a similar wish, changing
   emotional need, covering the mirror, and moving or exchanging the bracelets
   cannot create, preserve, or dismiss a person.
7. Anin's post-disappearance destination or continuation is unknown. Her
   mother's witness, Nina's memory, and Anin's surviving written page establish
   the relational fact that she lived, not a cosmological answer.

## Failure modes to watch

- **Reducing Anin to treatment:** Do not frame her purpose as fixing Nina,
  teaching a lesson, or completing a therapeutic assignment. Nina changes
  because she responds to a relationship and loss; Anin's life remains valuable
  regardless of that result.
- **Need-powered fading:** Never make Nina's reduced need, honesty, affection,
  acceptance, or promise cause Anin to fade. Include the failed emotional tests
  and Anin's explicit correction. The deadline continues independently.
- **Nonliving magical agency:** Do not let the mirror answer, the bracelet
  pulse, written words cast a spell, or a repeated sentence control the event.
  Nina's living agency is the only operative locus; the precise capacity remains
  a one-time local unknown.
- **Universal manifestation claims:** Avoid formulations such as “wishes come
  true,” “the universe heard,” or “mirrors make doubles.” Characters may joke
  or speculate, but observed failures and narration must not endorse a global
  rule.
- **Ontology overclaim:** Do not settle whether Anin is a branch, copy,
  projection, duplicated soul, or transformed future. Use those words only as
  provisional character language and keep the canon delta mechanism-specific.
- **Personhood without agency:** Give Anin consequential choices: her name,
  telling their mother, consenting to and ending substitution, her new
  preferences, her written page, rejection of replacement, and the form of her
  final hour. Do not make every action a response to Nina's instruction.
- **Knowledge leakage:** After waking, Nina and Anin share no new memories.
  Track who attended each lunch, heard the father anecdote, chose jam, learned a
  friend's concern, wrote the page, and witnessed each test.
- **Identity confusion at school:** State clearly which girl attends each day.
  During substitution, Anin answers to Nina's public name but remains `Anin` in
  narration. She cannot know Nina's post-branch conversation until told.
- **Casual ethical substitution:** Treat impersonation as a mutually agreed,
  flawed experiment with consequences, not a clever convenience. Anin revokes
  consent and Nina cannot send her again.
- **Inconsistent matter:** Both begin in duplicated sleep clothes and with
  duplicated bracelets. Anin later borrows the pre-existing green hoodie. Her
  duplicated items fade with her; the hoodie, Nina's bracelet, text Anin types,
  and ink Anin puts on Nina remain.
- **Grief erased by uplift:** The lunch scene must not resurrect Anin, reveal
  that she merged into Nina, prove an afterlife, or imply the loss was worth it.
  Nina can experience connection and grief simultaneously.
- **Instant anxiety cure:** Nina makes one honest choice and receives mixed,
  awkward kindness. She remains anxious and must practice; friends gain no
  magical ability to read her perfectly.
- **Mother as solution or expositor:** Their mother can witness, care, test
  ordinary observations, and grieve. She cannot diagnose the magic, contact a
  universal authority, or supply cosmological truth.
- **Motif crossover:** Reflection, reversal, paired objects, and doubling must
  not imply relation to another story, artifact, deity, gate, or branch system.
- **Name regression:** The school friend is `Daphne`, never `Nisha`. Use only
  the checked forms in the Name check and introduce no named teacher, clinician,
  relative, school, app, animal, or incidental speaker without a fresh registry
  check.
- **Overlong setup:** The duplicate must appear immediately. Compress school
  routines into scene transitions and one controlled substitution; do not turn
  the four-day span into a diary or lore investigation.
- **Content escalation:** Keep the disappearance tactile and devastating but
  non-graphic. No decomposition, physical agony, institutional captivity, or
  spectacle is needed.
