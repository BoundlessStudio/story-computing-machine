# Outline

## Story

- Premise and central promise: **The Red Dot.** Jovie enters an antiques fence's warehouse to reclaim her stolen motorcycle. His assistants laugh at her cheap laser pointer; a fifteen-foot-tall cat made of shadows unfolds behind her and displays obsidian claws. Her apparent advantage becomes a contest over who is actually entertaining whom.
- Focal pressure or attachment: Jovie wants her motorcycle, its ridiculous hand-painted flames, and the pleasure of making Harcott afraid. She barely knows the creature whose obedience she implies. Getting away mattered until revenge became available.
- Counterforce or complication: Harcott bought the motorcycle knowing its provenance. He wants to preserve valuable stock and his habit of buying his way out of embarrassment. He correctly notices that the cat follows interesting movement, not Jovie's commands; she resists this inconvenient observation. The cat wants an absorbing chase, not either human's victory.
- POV, distance, and information limit: Close third past through adult Jovie. Expose her shaky knowledge before her bluff. Read feline attention through ears, pupils, weight, pauses, and choices; no translated feline thoughts.
- Governing movement and time shape: Twenty continuous minutes, from doorway through display floor to loading yard. Briefly recall discovering the cat earlier that evening. Expand reversals of leverage. Target 2,500–4,000 words; finish with exhilaration and the bike recovered, Jovie's composure less intact.
- Speculative rule or ordinary-world constraint: The cat is independently alive and supplies its own magic. Standing on four paws, its ears reach fifteen feet. It can flatten into connected shade and crawl along surfaces, but cannot teleport, pass closed barriers, or carry objects while flattened. It must emerge bodily to cross bright gaps or handle matter. Manifested paws have weight; obsidian claws cut. Ordinary illumination does not dissolve it. The battery-powered pointer supplies only visible light: attraction is voluntary, never obedience. All observed capacities remain local to this individual.
- Dialogue promise: A dealer and a vindictive owner misread a formidable animal; successful intimidation becomes mutual embarrassment.
- Dialogic medium: Unequal face-to-face bargaining, assistants' laughter and urgent interruptions, laser trajectories, and the cat's refusals, stalking, and bodily demands.
- Dialogue engine: Jovie's implied control prevents her expulsion. Harcott's demonstration exposes her bluff. The cat makes holding the pointer an obligation to continue playing; recovering it requires a changed human bargain.

## Voice

- Narrative texture: Material, quick to notice vulgar decoration and expensive fragility; close enough that Jovie's imagined triumph keeps colliding with visible feline indifference. Let stalking slow the prose before a burst of movement.
- Conversational texture: Awkward intimidation, salesmanship, interruptions about immediate danger, and ordinary unanswered questions. Physical exchanges can outrun speech. Assistants need distinct reactions, not matching jokes.
- Rhetorical ownership: Jovie owns petty comparisons and rehearsed menace but improvises poorly. Harcott can price anything and avoid admitting theft; his confidence is socially practiced. The cat owns timing, refusal, and the funniest interruptions without becoming a human wit.
- Pressure behavior: Jovie talks too much when her hand shakes. Harcott becomes terse when stock breaks and eventually asks directly. Neither diagnoses the other's character. The cat's prolonged stillness makes both overexplain.
- Relationship movement: Jovie wants restitution and fear but cannot admit needing Harcott's cooperation. He wants her dangerous advantage cheaply and cannot comfortably request rescue. The cat solicits continued play from either, then makes stopping difficult. Exchanges convert the attempted purchase into an exit bargain without reconciliation.
- Anti-default: Avoid the competent animal handler correcting a foolish crowd and receiving recognition. Preserve Jovie's partly successful bluff, Harcott's perceptiveness, and the cat's independent appetite; no training agreement or feeding coda.

## Beats

1. Establish the visible motorcycle, its retained ignition key in Harcott's pocket, the assistants, and the unobstructed route to the loading doors. The roof and doors comfortably exceed the cat's full height. Jovie's recent encounter supplies limited confidence: it followed her moving red spot here; she has never made it stop.
2. Deliver the prompt early and literally. The assistants laugh when she produces the simple pointer. Connected shade behind her rises into the enormous cat; extended obsidian claws and one damaged display object make the changed expressions credible. Jovie converts their fright into a demand for the bike.
3. Harcott delays with purchase offers and disputed ownership. A careless pointer movement brings the cat alarmingly close to Jovie's prized paintwork. His attention shifts from her threats to her hand. One assistant withdraws; another tries to protect a particular fragile object rather than becoming a generic coward.
4. Harcott obtains the pointer during the scramble and demonstrates a slower, more enticing stalk. Jovie briefly loses both leverage and the cat's attention. He tries directing the game toward her to secure her retreat. Establish each projected spot and the cat's route; destruction follows contact, not arbitrary explosions.
5. His attempted finish fails. Switching off the light does not dismiss the animal. It tracks the hand that supplied the game and pins his sleeve with one claw, waiting. Its behavior is demanding and dangerous without suddenly making it a moral judge. Jovie's temptation to enjoy his predicament costs time while the assistants abandon the warehouse.
6. Harcott offers the motorcycle key for help. Jovie must approach within reach, regain the pointer, and invite the cat onto a clear moving trail toward the open loading doors. It initially refuses an uninteresting route; her revised movement earns pursuit. Its chosen shortcut scars the motorcycle. She takes that recoverable loss instead of delaying for complete humiliation or immaculate property.
7. Resolve the exit bargain and recover the bike. Harcott remains furious among damaged possessions, without an apology or newfound respect. In the empty loading yard, the final meaningful exchange is Jovie offering a moving light and the cat choosing a fresh chase alongside her departure. Allow fear, vanity, and pleasure together; do not explain their relationship as ownership or a settled lesson.
