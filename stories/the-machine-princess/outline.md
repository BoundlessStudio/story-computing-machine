# The Machine Princess

## Promised experience

The government orders the global hero league to neutralize the robot overlord called the Machine Princess while a ceasefire holds and negotiations are still in progress. The order anticipates heavy casualties. I read “despite” as the source of dread. People have begun to behave as if they can get through this without another battle, and the government has made a deliberate decision to risk one. “Global hero league” and “overlord” suggest forces that can do terrible things to each other. I expect a confrontation worthy of them and an ending that makes the order's cost felt. The specified relationship is between opposing powers. Their motives, the teller, who takes the losses, and whether or how the Princess is neutralized are open.

## Takes considered

- **The capture.** A League commander gets the order at a cliffside passenger exchange where the sides are arranging a withdrawal. She fights the Princess there and has to bargain for the lives of the people who survive the assault. Chosen because the ceasefire has already brought these people together in the place where both powers will be exercised against them.
- **The procession.** After the League's defeat, a Princess-side interpreter accompanies her through the capital to return the bodies of the heroes. At the handover the interpreter has to tell the truth about the Princess's decision to attack the retreating League, in front of the surviving commander and families from both sides. The consequences would be immediate, with the order and its breach largely behind the reader.
- **The closed hearing.** The government official who signed the order faces an emergency vote while the League is still fighting. The Princess arrives with captured heroes and addresses the chamber herself. The strongest scene would put the signer and the target together, with the fighting and the League's choice further away.

## Provisional direction

The truce at occupied Halvek has brought civilians leaving the machine quarter and detainees being returned by the Princess to the same airship exchange. This passage has begun while negotiators argue over whether the Princess can withdraw with her fleet armed. The government thinks the fleet will be untouchable once it leaves and could take another city. It orders Varda Rell, the League's commander on the ground, to take the Princess out of the war before she goes. Varda could call off the League. She decides to try to take the Princess alive, knowing the forecast and wanting to believe her people can do it quickly.

Varda can hold what she grasps motionless while she has footing. The Princess directs her war engines with the movements of her hands. Varda offers terms at the exchange. The Princess refuses to leave her fleet and her people to a government that has chosen to break the truce. When the League closes in, the Princess tears through the first ranks and sends engines through the Exchange after Varda. Both sides lose many, including Varda's second Merrin, and a section of the occupied passenger deck begins to give way. Varda lets go of the Princess to hold it. The Princess can still fight or fly her own people to safety. She puts her engines under the deck in full view of the League and makes a new offer from that position of strength. She will go into the League's custody herself if its remaining heroes fly escort for her people and her armed fleet all the way out. Varda has to put the League's authority behind those terms in the hearing of both sides. The Princess thereby preserves her people's passage and her fleet while losing her freedom. The League takes her alive and carries through the withdrawal its order was meant to prevent.

## Craft plan

- **Teller.** Try close third past with Varda first. She counts the people she has to put in the air and is touchy about what they think she owes them. Try a retrospective first person Princess for contrast, precise about what was taken from her and happy to describe a feat the government would prefer to call an accident. Either teller can see that a rescue and a surrender occur in the same stretch of sky.
- **Varda Rell.** Good at looking for the last solid surface in a failing room. She wants a quick capture and, less willingly admitted, for Merrin to think she would undertake the same risk she asks of him. She knows that actual battles informed the government's fear and mistakes foreseen losses for losses she can avoid by leading from the front. She speaks in practical sequences and uses ordinary names for actions when she feels herself slipping into orders. Under pressure she gets exact about promises she has to make in her own name.
- **Merrin.** Varda's second and a gifted flier. He wants Varda to give him a real say in carrying out the order. He knows how readily the other heroes will fall in behind her and thinks this time he can talk her into sharing the choice before she acts. He never leaves anybody he has reached. He talks fast and companionably, pulls other people into his anecdotes, and can make a grievance quite funny on his way to asking a direct question.
- **The Machine Princess.** An original interpretation of the named robot ruler. She wants an armed withdrawal and expects Varda to honor their work on the crossing. She has mistreated people she calls her subjects and will defend her decisions at length. She uses full titles until she wants to put someone on the spot. Her articulated bronze hands go from careful courtesy to visibly commanding the engines and finally into League custody. Her dress has a layered metal hem that keeps sounding against the floor after she stands still. Her habit of announcing what she gives away makes the shift from offering passage to offering herself legible in her own voice.
- **Place.** The Halvek Exchange, built against an escarpment above the town, has two ranks of airship landings and a passenger deck projecting over the fall. Choose it over Basto Assembly, where the fighting would be a report, or Kestrel Parade, where the Princess could simply remove her fleet. Here the exchange of people is actually under way and her engines are close enough to become their only means of escape. Late winter, an hour before dusk. Baggage porters slide trunks down brass chutes, families wait inside the windbreaks, and a red glass clock hangs at the town end. Wet wool smells stronger as the waiting room fills. Cold air blows up from the open deck seams, the brass is greasy, and the signal bell has a cracked second note the regulars listen for. The drop beneath the street makes the two ways out easy to see. It becomes a place people can leave only if the powers that wrecked it agree to carry them.

## Scenes and ending

1. **The departures queue, 20%.** Varda receives the order while the crossing is in progress. Merrin recognizes that the heroes have already taken their places as witnesses and wants her to ask them to fight rather than count their presence as assent. She wants him to trust her with the call and to fly the families clear if it goes wrong. He can see the packed platform. She has read the casualty estimate and lets him read it before she gives the order. Load one ordinary trace of the life she expects him to have afterward, through what he says while waiting for her answer.
2. **The open deck, 65%, long scene.** Varda has to tell the Princess that her government has chosen the assault and hear what surrender would mean on the Princess's side. The Princess has an account to give Varda, who wants to know why she will risk the withdrawal over the armed fleet. Give it the time and order of one earlier failed handover involving her people, and give Varda her own reason to fear a second conquest. Families continue to pass, and the damaged bell interrupts what each can hear. The Princess refuses the initial terms and the fight changes what both can buy with their power. Let Varda see Merrin fly a passenger to safety and go back for someone else. After the fall, stay with the choice Varda faces as she releases the Princess to hold the deck and cannot reach him. The Princess raises her surviving engines under it, hears Varda name responsibility for the breach, and sets a price for ending the fight. Varda accepts aloud where the surviving league and negotiators can hear her.
3. **Off the edge of Halvek, 15%.** The Princess's machines lift the passengers and the fallen into the air while the surviving heroes take up escort positions. Varda had pictured coming home among those formations with Merrin. She travels within reach of the Princess, who presents the hands that moved the fleet for restraint under the terms she chose. The last image is the diminished League escorting the guns it came to seize, over the visible edge of the capital's authority. Load it from the early red clock behind the people meant to cross, Merrin's argument about asking those who must fly, and the Princess's insistence on sending her people home under guard.

Compress the war's history into what the two commanders have reason to remember or argue. Leave the government offstage after the order and stay at or within sight of the Exchange. No tour of battles elsewhere and no later reckoning scene.
