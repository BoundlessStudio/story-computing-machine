# The Machine Princess

## Promised experience

The government orders the global hero league to neutralize the robot overlord called the Machine Princess while a ceasefire holds and negotiations continue. Heavy casualties are expected. I read “despite” as the source of dread. People have begun to behave as if they can get through this without another battle, and the government has decided to risk one. “Global hero league” and “overlord” suggest powers that can do terrible things to each other. I expect a confrontation worthy of them and an ending that makes the order's cost felt. The prompt specifies the opposing sides and leaves motives, viewpoint, who takes the losses, and the form or success of neutralization open.

## Takes considered

- **The capture.** The League commander gets the order at the passenger exchange where the withdrawal is already beginning. Chosen for the confrontation in which each side risks destroying the terms it most wants to keep.
- **The procession.** After the League's defeat, a machine interpreter accompanies the Princess to return the heroes' bodies. At the handover the interpreter has to tell the families why the Princess attacked the retreating League.
- **The closed hearing.** The official who signed the order faces an emergency vote while the League is still fighting. The Princess arrives with captured heroes and directly confronts the person responsible.

## Provisional direction

At occupied Halvek, the truce lets the Princess release detainees and move civilians out of the machine quarter while the two sides argue over an armed withdrawal. Government ships at the edge of the valley allow civilian flights through under League escort. The government fears the fleet will be able to take another city once it gets beyond the cordon. It orders League commander Varda Rell to neutralize the Princess now. Varda decides to fight if the Princess refuses custody and insists on taking her guns out of Halvek.

With solid footing Varda can fix whatever part she grasps in place. The Princess directs her war engines with her hands. Varda takes her by the upper arm during the assault and the Princess uses her fingers to send her engines through the League and the deck. After heavy losses on both sides, Varda has to let her go and run to hold the failing deck from the stone quay. The Princess puts her fleet under the deck before asking for anything. She could shoot her way through the cordon, but her surviving engines are carrying refugees now. She offers to go into League custody if Varda's surviving fliers shield her people and her armed ships all the way through. Varda gives that order in front of both delegations. The Princess keeps her hands free until the government ships yield, then gives them into custody. Both commanders spend something they wanted to keep, and the League escorts the guns its assault was meant to seize.

## Craft plan

- **Teller.** First try close third past with Varda, who counts the people she has to put in the air and is touchy about what they think she owes them. Try a retrospective first person Princess for contrast, precise about what was taken from her and eager to describe a feat others would call an accident.
- **Varda Rell.** She knows what the Princess has done at Halvek and thinks leading the assault herself can keep its losses below the forecast. She wants a quick capture and Merrin's trust. She describes things in practical sequences, especially under pressure, and switches from orders to ordinary words when she makes a promise in her own name.
- **Merrin.** Varda's second and a gifted flier. He knows the others will fall in behind her and thinks this time he can get her to ask for their assent. Habitually companionable, he pulls people into his anecdotes and can make a grievance funny on his way to asking a direct question. He goes back for anyone he could still reach.
- **The Machine Princess.** She expects Varda to honor the work they've done on the crossing. She wants an armed withdrawal and stands behind holding the ferry crews to get her own people back from the government. She speaks at length when telling someone what she has given them or is owed, using full titles until she wants to put a particular person on the spot. Her articulated bronze hands go from precise courtesy to dispatching the engines, then into custody. The plates of her dress sound on the deck after she stops walking.
- **Place.** The Halvek Exchange, an escarpment quay with an iron passenger deck jutting out from it. Basto Assembly would receive the fight as a report and Kestrel Parade would let the fleet simply disperse. At the Exchange the people are already crossing and both armies can see the cordon. Late winter before dusk. Porters send trunks down chutes under a red glass clock. Wet wool steams inside the windbreaks, cold air blows up through the deck seams, the handrail is greasy, and the departure bell's cracked second note carries across the drop. The stone gives Varda a place to stand while the iron fails. The engines have to lift the people out by the only air corridor running past the cliff.

## Scenes and ending

1. **The departures queue, 20%.** A civilian ship waits at the cordon. League fliers shield it and the government ships open for it. Varda gets the order while Merrin still thinks they are on the quay to witness the crossing. He asks her to put the choice to the League. She wants him to trust her with it and take the families clear if she fails. She lets him read the casualty forecast, then gives the order. Give him one ordinary thing he expects to do afterward.
2. **The quay and deck, 65%, long scene.** The conversation the story owes starts when Varda tells the Princess why the ceasefire is about to end and demands custody and disarmament. Let the Princess tell in order how the government seized her unarmed people at an earlier handover and why she would still take Halvek's ferry crews hostage in response. Varda wants her to answer for what she did and can't grant safe passage with the guns. The Princess wants the person who can give that passage to make it binding. The cracked bell interrupts them while families keep moving. Varda holds the Princess's arm from the quay, but her fingers move. The counterattack breaks a League flight and shears the deck's outer supports. Merrin flies a passenger to stone and goes down with other heroes after going back. The iron starts to peel away several strides from Varda, and she lets go of the Princess to hold it. Stay with the cost of what she can't reach. The Princess catches the deck, then offers herself for an escort through the cordon with the guns aboard. Varda owns the breach and commits the remaining League in sight and hearing of the witnesses on the quay.
3. **In sight of Halvek, 15%.** The engines lift passengers and bodies from both sides. Varda sees the places where the fallen should have flown. Government ships hesitate at the sight of guns within the League's shield and the surviving fliers hold formation until they yield. The Princess offers the hands that moved the fleet. End with the guns still visible through the shield as the League escorts them across the line. The opening civilian flight, Merrin's expectation of coming back, and Varda's terms for the surrendered guns load that image.

The story stays within sight of the Exchange. The larger war enters through what the commanders have reason to remember aloud.
