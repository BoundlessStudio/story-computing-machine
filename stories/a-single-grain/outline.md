# Provisional direction: A Single Grain

The request promises the startling encounter between a microchip and a mage council: something almost too small to see contains an actual mind, and its maker can explain that achievement in the council's own language of sigils and golems. The explicit elements are the presentation, the quoted claim, the microchip as the golem's heart, and sentience carved into a grain of sand. I infer intellectual wonder, some audacity in “This here,” and a pleasure in the council discovering what the achievement makes possible. No particular relationship beyond presenter, creation, and council, degree of hostility, plot scale, origin of the technology, or ending is specified. The chosen scale below grows from tabletop demonstration to a first sight of another world; that is an invention in service of the premise, not a user requirement.

## Chosen direction

First person from the practical engineer who made the golem Ilex, about 3,500 words, taking place in the council's high observatory chamber during one examination. The engineer hopes to secure access to the council's knowledge; Ilex has a more particular ambition. Their relationship is a partnership still expressed in the maker's possessive workshop language. The council is formidable and curious, with different reactions to the demonstration, rather than a collection of fools awaiting correction. First Mage Orrel is the principal interlocutor.

The governing tension is whether the grain really contains someone who can discover something neither its maker nor the mages already knew. It develops into an attempted lunar doorway, a problem the council can explain and cannot yet solve. The machinery supplies thought and fine control, not unlimited magical energy: the living council must provide the power. Ilex does not become a universal hacker, conjure facts from nowhere, or solve an arbitrary emergency. The relevant diagrams and the golem's interest in astronomy appear before the attempt.

- The engineer holds up Ilex's visible silicon die in its clear protective carrier and speaks the prompt's line verbatim. A council magnifier shows a few lines, then thousands, then more beneath them. The engineer explains light-carved repeated patterns and returns the carrier to the waiting brass-and-ceramic body. Ilex comes awake with opinions about what the engineer has just claimed on their behalf. The tiny heart remains an intelligible, vulnerable physical object throughout.
- Council questions reveal both the achievement and its limits. Ilex has learned from experience rather than receiving every possible answer as a command. Their independent curiosity becomes concrete when they neglect a prestigious test problem to study the old lunar-door diagrams in the chamber. The engineer has brought the fabrication apparatus to demonstrate the manufacturing method, not secretly prepared a miracle. Orrel admits that the great door can be opened but cannot be kept aligned with a moving moon: each correction pulls the rest of the aperture out of shape.
- Ilex proposes replacing the single great aperture with a close lattice of minute linked apertures, each making a small correction. A full-sized version would require an impossible acreage of sigils. The engineer and Ilex can put the controlling pattern on a small glass plate with the same optical process that made the heart. This is Ilex's original design, requiring the council's geometric knowledge and the engineer's fabrication; the density introduced in the opening is what makes it possible. Ilex asks to try because they want to look at a landscape no maker has selected for them.
- The demonstration becomes a joint, consequential attempt. The council supplies and can cut off the living magic. Ilex directs the matrix through electrical controls, while the engineer sets and aligns the plate. The first aperture fractures into incompatible slivers of lunar ground. Ilex identifies a flaw in their own assumption and changes the coordination, correcting adjoining cells together rather than chasing each error separately. They ask the council to hold the spell through the adjustment, accepting responsibility for an uncertain invention. The slivers join into one stable doorway: black sky, fierce sunlight, a gray mountain beyond, with the chamber's air held by an established threshold ward.
- The council's response is practical astonishment: questions begin about the new pattern rather than demands for another imitation of human thought. The engineer, who came to obtain permission to study, is asked who should receive the first invitation to teach. Ilex wants the doorway held a little longer. Their mechanical hand passes through and returns with lunar dust. The final image puts new sand beside the heart that made reaching it possible, leaving the next discovery open rather than concluding with a speech about personhood.

Protect the continuous escalation from nearly invisible workmanship, to independent invention, to a physically opened possibility. The largest risk is that the lunar doorway could make Ilex seem only a fast calculator; the design choice, unassigned desire, fallible attempt, and self-directed correction must carry sentience as clearly as the feat carries density. A second risk is overexplaining the invented optics and spell mechanics until the demonstration loses its human pace.
