# The Town Beneath the Dance

Provisional direction: a second-person story in which the pleasure of being taught becomes a hunger the four friends cannot quite admit. You arrive in the seaside market town with Nort, Mysie, and Elspie wearing scarlet gowns pieced from the same length of cloth. You have read the sky alone all your life; you want, fiercely, to show someone what you see and have her show you something back. The first midnight dance is funny, intimate, and astonishing. When Mysie teaches you flame and you teach her a constellation, she makes a little burning star. When Nort learns your sky, roots begin to follow it. Teaching changes the teacher as well as the pupil.

The girls of the town join. A practical, generous market woman gives the four lodging and lets her daughter learn; the daughter's new gift supplies a wonder her mother could never buy. You and Mysie push each other toward bigger gifts, while Elspie begins to fear what the ground is doing. The old words emerge through the joined workings and are repeated beyond the circle. Beneath the market, very ancient living creatures hear what they once heard at the beginning of spring. Their slow breaths move the paving stones; Nort can hear roots caught in their fur. Their waking brings flowers and abundance, and breaks houses. There is no separate spell to cancel: once awake, these animals need to rise and migrate, and the knowledge is already traveling through other girls.

Your sky-reading makes you the first to understand where the creatures must emerge. Admitting it means admitting that you kept teaching after you had reasons to stop. The town divides over expelling the witches, preserving its valuable houses, or abandoning them to make room for the migration. The market woman's own home stands in the path; her attachment and anger should give the town's choice a face. Emptying the town is a costly human action, helped by gifts that cannot make the damage harmless. Some residents refuse the promise of a golden future; some leave and do not forgive.

Possible ending: the four get their host and the last endangered residents out, but the market is lost. Mysie and you cannot immediately recover the ease of the first night. The living migration continues inland, beautiful and immense; the old words are being sung somewhere neither of you can see. Another girl asks you to teach her. Her question still has the power to delight you, and your answer has to carry what that delight has already cost.
