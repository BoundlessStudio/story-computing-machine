# Yours to Keep

## Promised experience

Explicitly, the protagonist has no transformation trinket, regularly borrows other magical girls' trinkets for emergencies or identity shenanigans, accepts a personal one immediately, and finds that decision disastrous. The distinctive inferred pleasure is a familiar, playful borrowing arrangement turning sharply dangerous through the supposedly wonderful transformation that is “all yours.” Comedy, frightening consequences, and magical action seem compatible with the wording; their balance and scale, the offerer's motives, any central relationship, and the ending remain open.

## Provisional direction

First-person Romy has become a very good stand-in for her friends Briony (gold star brooch, Daystar) and Tansy (brass whistle on a red cord, Redshift). Borrowing reproduces the original girl's magical silhouette, voice, and powers. They lend willingly. The opening handoff demonstrates that an active form persists when its trinket is let go, transfers on a new bare-handed touch, and does not transfer through cloth. Other rules emerge when needed: one form cannot overwrite another; normal trinkets have a release; a form runs on its current wearer's living magic, through the trinket, which is a tool. Briony is patient and literal; Tansy likes the caper more than she admits. Their particular friendship is a writer's choice, not a requirement of the request.

1. Open with a concrete identity caper: Romy, as Daystar, keeps a public appearance going while Briony is somewhere her ordinary identity must be seen. During an emergency handoff the two exchange the brooch behind a banner without the crowd noticing. A quick second example establishes Redshift borrowing and Romy's intimate knowledge of both fighting styles. Underneath the jokes she wants to hear a crowd cheer a name that belongs to her.
2. At the shuttered Palace Theatre, a living, mothlike itinerant costumier offers a mirror compact that will create a form from Romy herself. Romy says yes and opens it before asking the obvious questions. No hesitation scene. Her own transformation is magnificent: a black-and-pearl magical empress with Romy's undisguised face, a floating broken-mirror crown, and a citywide announcement naming her Princess Romy and the girls' new enemy. The theatre unfolds into a stage across the street.
3. Let Romy inhabit the disastrous form through a developing action scene. Briony's rescue ribbon reaches her; Romy tries to guide it past falling scenery to save Briony, only to discover her new power turns the familiar pattern into the exact snare she knows Briony cannot cut. Her attempt to help Tansy's comet spell produces projectiles that damage the street and leave Romy choosing where to deflect them. She sends them into the empty theatre rather than the occupied pavement, destroying the stair up to the maker's raised machinery booth, the route she was trying to reach. She can speak normally. The personal power recognizes a committed, familiar magical sequence and prepares its learned reversal before the final discharge; it is neither foresight nor protection against ordinary objects. She sets down the compact and remains transformed, then tests its expected release and finds the maker deliberately omitted it. Her own form prevents her taking another. Her acquired experience is genuinely hers, which is why this tailored weapon is especially devastating. The living costumier controls the theatre's offensive machinery and orders her to collect the girls' trinkets as his trophies in exchange for stopping its bombardment.
4. Romy calls to her friends in ordinary speech, using an apparently surrendering instruction plus the old concealed-handoff signal rather than announcing the trick within the maker's hearing. Briony chooses to trust it and approach under his machinery's fire; Tansy chooses to lend her active whistle, knowing it will make her an ordinary, vulnerable person, and takes a theatre rigging line instead. The trophies arrive on cloth, so neither form transfers. The maker, who ordered Romy to receive them because her trapped form cannot use them, flies down from the isolated booth to claim Redshift's form for himself. His greed supplies the reason for bare hands; his belief that he designed Romy's only available choices supplies the misjudgment. Behind the fallen banner, Romy displays the real whistle and red cord as bait, conceals the compact flat behind it, and places the compact against his reaching palm. Her empress form transfers, freeing her to touch the whistle and become Redshift. Tansy becomes ordinary. Briony retrieves the still-wrapped brooch; Daystar has never transferred. Stage the physical exchange so the reader can follow it. The maker's borrowed form retains Romy's personal design and face, but runs on his magic, not an ongoing drain on her.
5. As borrowed Redshift, Romy must defeat her own transformation. Briony commits her magic to one side of its counter-defense, deliberately leaving herself vulnerable; ordinary Tansy uses the rigging to yank fallen scenery between the maker and his machinery. Romy starts Redshift's finishing pattern. The compact opens to reverse its expected final magical discharge. She aborts before that discharge and throws the actual brass whistle into the exposed glass. Her earlier failed finisher taught her where she can still interrupt the sequence; it did not teach the compact to predict arbitrary ordinary actions. The reversal is committed against the expected magic and cannot turn a physical object into a counterspell. Throwing does not dismiss her form, under the established persistence rule, but gives up control of the trinket and a second attempt. The compact breaks and the empress form ends; Briony catches the exposed costumier before he can resume his machinery. All three make consequential choices, with Romy's betrayal-by-knowledge and counter-caper controlling the turn and climax.
6. Consequences survive victory: the theatre and street are damaged, Romy's one personal trinket is broken, and people at the cordon recognize her face from the villain's announcement and fight. Show recognition obstructing her attempt to walk out as herself; unmasking the maker does not make the footage or the attention disappear. In a screened dressing room, Briony lends her the gold star and becomes ordinary herself; Briony remains out of public sight with Tansy and the prisoner while Romy can leave as Daystar. End on the bitterly funny predicament of borrowing a secret identity to escape the consequences of finally obtaining her own. No tidy lesson that wanting a personal transformation was wrong; the seller exploited an ordinary, understandable desire.

The provisional mechanism and caper can change in discussion. Protect the immediate acceptance, the specific betrayal of borrowed-form expertise, Romy's choices controlling the counter-caper and battle, and an aftermath in which this was truly a terrible mistake.
