# Yours to Keep

## Promised experience

Explicitly, the protagonist has no transformation trinket, regularly borrows other magical girls' trinkets for emergencies or identity shenanigans, accepts a personal one immediately, and finds that decision disastrous. The distinctive inferred pleasure is a familiar, playful borrowing arrangement turning sharply dangerous through the supposedly wonderful transformation that is “all yours.” Comedy, frightening consequences, and magical action seem compatible with the wording; their balance and scale, the offerer's motives, any central relationship, and the ending remain open.

## Provisional direction

First-person Romy has become a very good stand-in for her friends Briony (gold star brooch, Daystar) and Tansy (red whistle, Redshift). Borrowing reproduces the original girl's magical silhouette, voice, and powers. They lend willingly. An activated trinket can be handed directly to another wearer, transferring the form, a trick Romy has used to put the real heroine back before cameras arrive. Briony is patient and literal; Tansy likes the caper more than she admits. Their particular friendship is a writer's choice, not a requirement of the request.

1. Open with a concrete identity caper: Romy, as Daystar, keeps a public appearance going while Briony is somewhere her ordinary identity must be seen. During an emergency handoff the two exchange the brooch behind a banner without the crowd noticing. A quick second example establishes Redshift borrowing and Romy's intimate knowledge of both fighting styles. Underneath the jokes she wants to hear a crowd cheer a name that belongs to her.
2. At the shuttered Palace Theatre, a living, mothlike itinerant costumier offers a mirror compact that will create a form from Romy herself. Romy says yes and opens it before asking the obvious questions. No hesitation scene. Her own transformation is magnificent: a black-and-pearl magical empress with Romy's undisguised face, a floating broken-mirror crown, and a citywide announcement introducing her as the girls' new enemy. The theatre unfolds into a stage across the street.
3. The personal form has been tailored from Romy's experience of wearing the girls' magic. Its power automatically reverses their recognizable spells into exact counters. It uses Romy's living magic through the compact; the compact is a tool, not an independent person or magic user. Briony's rescue ribbon becomes a binding snare; Tansy's arresting comet becomes a storm of projectiles. Romy retains her mind and choices, but attempts to ask for magical help trigger destructive defensive responses. This is the living costumier's trap: he has made the girls' most knowledgeable substitute into a weapon against them. He controls the theatre's offensive machinery and wants their surrendered trinkets. Breaking contact with the compact cannot simply dismiss an unfinished villain transformation: it must be defeated or handed to a new bearer.
4. Romy stops trying to use familiar heroic moves in a body designed to betray them. Her friends trust the absurd handoff instructions because they know how she borrows their identities. She stages an apparent surrender of the girls' trinkets, using the theatre scenery and the same concealed exchange as the opening to put her compact into the costumier's hand while taking Tansy's whistle. The still-active empress form transfers to him; it remains Romy's authored transformation and face. The move is physical sleight of hand, not a new spell or moral conversion.
5. As borrowed Redshift, Romy must defeat her own transformation while its wearer tries to turn her friends' familiar attacks back on them. She knows the timing and blind spot of the Redshift finishing move because she has performed it badly before. She deliberately aborts its expected magical ending and uses the actual whistle, thrown by hand, to strike the exposed compact while the others force its defenses to commit. The compact breaks and the empress form ends. The costumier loses his weapon and the girls capture him. Show the action and the dangerous opening rather than settling it through an explanation afterward.
6. Consequences survive victory: the theatre and street are damaged, the city has watched Romy's own face announce itself as a villain, and her one personal trinket is broken. Briony lends her the gold star so she can leave the cordon in somebody else's face. End on the bitterly funny predicament of borrowing a secret identity to escape the consequences of finally obtaining her own. No tidy lesson that wanting a personal transformation was wrong; the seller exploited an ordinary, understandable desire.

The provisional mechanism and caper can change in discussion. Protect the immediate acceptance, the specific betrayal of borrowed-form expertise, Romy's choices controlling the counter-caper and battle, and an aftermath in which this was truly a terrible mistake.
