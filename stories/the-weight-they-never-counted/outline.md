# Outline

## Story

- Premise and central promise: You leave with a trading caravan the moment you turn eighteen, convinced that the village's weakest person deserves its cheapest work. On the first road day, the leader sees you carry two sealed hundred-kilogram blocks without effort or sweat. Resolve why the local bottom rank is extraordinary elsewhere and whether usefulness becomes another claim on your body. Give you chosen work and defensible limits, not spectacular portering.
- Focal pressure or attachment: You want distance and wages but seek safety by minimizing competence and accepting costs unseen. Usefulness may secure passage; notice may turn you into equipment. Separate chosen work from proving you deserve room.
- Counterforce or complication: The leader must protect animals, axles, deadlines, and pay. Capacity helps narrow margins, but an unmeasured worker is a safety risk. Crew members separately worry about shifts, loading, dropped cargo, and rations. Steady-load strength cannot prevent shifting-weight injury, hunger, or reflexive agreement.
- POV, distance, and information limit: Close second-person past through the unnamed protagonist, exactly eighteen throughout. You apply the village's verdict and read punishment faster than goodwill. Others' judgments remain external. Freight records, bodily comparison, and supervised work reveal the bounded history; its makers remain unknown.
- Governing movement and time shape: Seven days outward: birthday departure, roadside question, private assessment, caravan learning, then an unstable-load choice before market. Movement passes from assigned weight to measuring it and setting terms. Decide beside one tilted wagon, not before a crowd; end by choosing continued work.
- Speculative rule or ordinary-world constraint: The village descends from a story-local population altered in a lost age for sustained freight labor. Dense bones and load-distributing connective tissue let every villager carry enormous, slow, balanced weights; the protagonist has their lowest capacity but finds two hundred kilograms comfortable. The trait brings no speed, fighting skill, balance under shifting loads, or immunity to injury, and requires exceptional food, salt, rest, and conditioning. Outsiders are ordinary humans. This is inherited population history, not an awakening, selection, universal rule, or benefit created by bullying.

## Voice

- Narrative texture: Second person tracks weight, footing, portions, hands, and exits through the habit of ranking yourself last; growing agency widens attention to routes, balances, and choices without triumphal narration.
- Conversational texture: The leader asks direct logistics questions; crew talk overlaps around straps, meals, animals, wages, and weather. Village speech ranges from competitive taunts to bland official normalization.
- Rhetorical ownership: You minimize through qualifiers and concessions. The leader reasons in capacities, contingencies, and costs. Crew members speak from distinct jobs, not shared moral judgment.
- Pressure behavior: Alarm produces quick agreement, lost explanation, or silence; boundaries become brief and specific. The leader slows down to verify, while crew cross-talk contracts into task calls at danger.
- Anti-default: Avoid instant confidence, universal praise, a monolithic sneering chorus, self-worth speeches, and polished dialogue in which everyone explains the story's meaning.

## Beats

1. The instant village custom recognizes them as eighteen, the protagonist takes a hidden bundle and boards the waiting caravan. Fresh mockery and an authority's inaction show the harm left behind. Departure is their decision; they choose the cheapest job because it seems hard to lose.
2. A first-day rut forces cargo off a wagon. The protagonist carries two scale-marked hundred-kilogram blocks to firm ground, expecting criticism. The leader directly asks how, in all that's holy, they can lift and comfortably carry two hundred kilograms without sweating. Keep the question unmediated; they suspect a trap.
3. Privately at camp, the leader and a loader check marks, pulse, posture, footwear, and food. Village chores reveal that everybody there moves team-sized loads. A copied freight note identifies the regional adaptation, not its makers. The protagonist recognizes their rank as local without feeling instantly healed.
4. The protagonist requests controlled assessment, not display. Balanced increments establish a safe load, food and rest costs, and the divide between stable carrying and uneven motion. Crew responses remain mixed. The leader offers porter pay; the protagonist waits to understand duties and deductions.
5. Days of manifests, tie-downs, axle limits, animal pacing, and work calls leave the protagonist still the village's weakest and caravan's novice. Quick assent to extra tasks causes missed food and soreness; capacity is not unlimited availability.
6. A wagon tilts on a slanted road. The protagonist takes its weight, feels unsafe geometry, and stops. Using the manifest, they propose an unloading order, ballast change, and team carry protecting bodies, animals, and cargo. The leader backs sound reasoning, not a worth revelation.
7. At market, the protagonist negotiates a load ceiling, recovery time, full rations, porter premium, refusal of unstable lifts, and load-planning apprenticeship. Preserve friction: safeguards cost time, while capacity benefits the caravan.
8. They choose the continuing caravan, not return or vindication. The next assignment pairs a modest carry with checking a manifest and route grade. End in ongoing work under negotiated terms: they can use and limit this body while learning beyond it.

## Continuity

- Boundaries and unknowns: Connect nothing to existing canon; the makers remain unknown. Keep the protagonist exactly eighteen. The adaptation neither excuses bullying nor makes it training.
