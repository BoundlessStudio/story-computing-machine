# Right of Way — provisional direction

First-person comic family story, probably 2,000–2,500 words. The narrator has recently joined the Department of Moving Vehicles and voted for the new broom-license law. Their grandmother and two great-aunts summon them to tea: the annual birthday flight is approaching, the law's grace period ends soon afterward, and the relatives expect the family's new official to arrange exemptions. The narrator wants to join that flight after missing recent birthdays, but is also ashamed of how readily the relatives expect them to bend the rules.

The witches have considerable skill and different wants. Grandmother objects to being granted permission for something she taught the narrator; one aunt wants a practical explanation of what the test measures and exposes a flaw in its ground-vehicle assumptions; the other is preoccupied with the birthday outing and takes an unexpected interest in the photograph and license card. Their knowledge should complicate the narrator's case rather than merely supply funny objections. The narrator has to say plainly why they voted yes: cheap new brooms have filled the sky with riders who did not grow up knowing the same families, gestures, and flight customs.

Tea becomes an attempt to practice for the new exam, then a short shared flight while unlicensed flying is still lawful. The relatives can fly rings around the narrator. A specific test mismatch emerges through their demonstration, perhaps the illustrated right-turn signal disappearing beneath a rider's cloak. The narrator admits the fault instead of defending every departmental instruction. Away from the familiar roofline, two incompatible right-of-way customs bring everyone to a cautious, undramatic halt; there is no crash or rescue. Expertise alone does not let strangers predict each other. One readable signal and a deliberate yield get them moving again.

The relatives choose to take the test, with reservations, rather than being defeated by an argument. The birthday flight remains the story's personal destination; obtaining the cards need not occupy a second full administrative plot. A possible final image is the narrator flying in the family formation again, one elder ostentatiously signaling a familiar turn. The gesture should register as both mockery and an invitation to keep riding with them. Find an ending in their actual conduct, without a final paragraph explaining the civic lesson. The provisional title may change when the prose shows what matters.
