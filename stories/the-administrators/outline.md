# Provisional direction

The explicit promise is a world where every spell must be registered, authorized, and paid for; six mysterious cybernetic sorcerers appear above the Imperial capital, and the Imperial Archmages' identification attempt discovers that the entire party are “Administrators.” I infer pleasure in formidable strangers, a hierarchy suddenly inverted, and the Empire discovering that its apparently absolute magical authority is subordinate to something it never understood. The six originals support a striking, differentiated ensemble. Awe, danger, some dry imperial embarrassment, and an expanding sense of the world suit that pleasure; neither a moral, a particular motive, nor an intimate relationship is specified.

My choice is close third person through Archmage Hedran, a capable believer in the system. The central relationship remains the requested confrontation between the strangers and the Archmages. The six are the network's original living architects; their cybernetics sustain them and connect their nervous systems to its deepest routes. Their absence remains partly unexplained. They intend to end the Crown's monopoly, but Hedran discovers their intentions through their choices. Protecting users constrains their methods, not their authority. These motives and this liberation ending are inventions, not requirements.

The party, mapped to the supplied originals in prompt order: **Nhal**, the concealed black hood, opens paths and contains space; **Vesra**, dark-haired with the ornate headpiece and exposed cybernetic arms, separates living spells from imposed commands; **Brannoc**, broad and dark-haired in white-and-yellow harness, handles immense current; **Tavren**, fair-haired with a pointed hat and red mechanical glove, rewrites spell structures; **Ossan**, long blond hair, dark glasses, staff and red cloak, conducts simultaneous workings across distance; **Sathra**, dark-haired in the broad hat, chain and red cloak, leads and cuts the Crown's governing enchantment. These names, functions and histories are inventions, with each body's visual identity grounded in its original.

- Hedran registers a light spell, signs its authorization, and pays two silver pennies to the Imperial Treasury before spending her own living strength through the network. Coin is an access toll. Closed skyroad gates and the Hall's conquest maps establish the capital's isolation from distant cities. Six people appear above the palace without those transactions. The Archmages' inquiry returns six Administrators; arrest is refused for insufficient privilege. On Sathra's direction, Vesra is already separating users' connections from the Crown's license while the others answer the attack below.
- The Emperor orders a licensed attack. Nhal folds it into empty sky, holding its live pattern inside a ring; Tavren's red mechanical palm and lens open that captured pattern into a public receipt; Sathra follows its authority chain back to the Emperor. Their coordinated audit exposes the Crown's subordinate license. Hedran extends the identification query into the oldest grant records: six original architect signatures match the six living neural keys, predating the first Emperor. It explains their authority, not their absence or intentions.
- The work reveals why they cannot simply revoke everything: bodies under healing, occupied aerial coaches and the palace's own supports depend on active spells using that license. Brannoc braces the current while Vesra's articulated fingers preserve its living connections; Ossan listens through his wired staff for distant responses. The Emperor interrupts this work with **Sovereign Recall**, a licensed emergency spell Hedran helped authorize. It compulsorily draws living power through subjects' connections to annihilate the six. They adapt immediately, neither baiting it nor waiting for full destructive strength.
- Vesra refuses isolation that would drop those active spells; Brannoc takes the current through his augmented body instead. Ossan detects a delayed branch and makes him hold while Tavren turns Recall's collection route into a return path through their shared interfaces. Nhal unfolds the sealed palace heart into open air, exposing the physical Crown conductor that anchors the license. Brannoc carries the occupied palace's supports while Sathra cuts that conductor and Vesra releases the last bindings. This is one changing, interdependent working. Just before the cut, Hedran refuses the order to keep apprentices firing: their open attack connections expose them to Recall's draw. She makes them break contact and go inside, remaining at the open gallery doorway with the six and unfolded heart still in sight. Her refusal protects them without deciding the six's success.
- The seized power returns to its owners, with active spells preserved. Registration and expenditure of living strength remain; each caster now authorizes their own spells, and the Crown's coin toll is explicitly abolished. The palace loses its coercive enchantments, and the Emperor's attempt to command other people's magic is denied. The six reopen great skyroads through the imperial barriers, bringing the capital face to face with cities previously visible only on conquest maps. They depart together through those open skies: the Empire's supremacy is broken, without another court installed.

The chief risk is reducing these strangers to a lecture about fees or making Hedran's disillusionment the climax. The draft should center the six's incursion, the successive failures of imperial force, and their spectacular, deliberate unmaking of the monopoly; Hedran gives the reader proximity to the reversal rather than rescuing its agents.
