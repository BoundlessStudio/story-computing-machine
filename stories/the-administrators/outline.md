# Provisional direction

The explicit promise is a world where every spell must be registered, authorized, and paid for; six mysterious cybernetic sorcerers appear above the Imperial capital, and the Imperial Archmages' identification attempt discovers that the entire party are “Administrators.” I infer pleasure in formidable strangers, a hierarchy suddenly inverted, and the Empire discovering that its apparently absolute magical authority is subordinate to something it never understood. The six originals support a striking, differentiated ensemble. Awe, danger, some dry imperial embarrassment, and an expanding sense of the world suit that pleasure; neither a moral, a particular motive, nor an intimate relationship is specified.

My choice is close third person through Archmage Hedran, a capable believer in the system, during an attempted imperial suppression of the six. The central relationship is the requested confrontation between the strangers and the Archmages. The six are the network's original living architects, displaced when the Empire seized its surface institutions; their cybernetics have sustained them and let them return through the network's oldest physical connections. They have come to end the Crown's exclusive authority over other people's sorcery. They want the network intact and its living users unharmed. That chosen ethical restraint shapes their attack; it does not make them dependent on Hedran's help or permission. Their authority is real, with physical and political consequences, rather than an impressive label followed by a negotiation.

The party, mapped to the supplied originals in prompt order: **Nhal**, the concealed black hood, opens paths and contains space; **Vesra**, dark-haired with the ornate headpiece and exposed cybernetic arms, separates living spells from imposed commands; **Brannoc**, broad and dark-haired in white-and-yellow harness, handles immense current; **Tavren**, fair-haired with a pointed hat and red mechanical glove, rewrites spell structures; **Ossan**, long blond hair, dark glasses, staff and red cloak, conducts simultaneous workings across distance; **Sathra**, dark-haired in the broad hat, chain and red cloak, leads and cuts the Crown's governing enchantment. These names, functions and histories are inventions, with each body's visual identity grounded in its original.

- Open on Hedran buying and authorizing a small spell while the capital's public magical life demonstrates the same three requirements. Six people then stand unsupported above the palace without an entry, a permit, or a debit. Hedran and the other Archmages interrogate the network. Six separate answers identify six Administrators; an attempted arrest command is refused for insufficient privilege.
- The Emperor orders a licensed attack anyway. The strangers let the city see the difference between buying a spell and governing the machinery that executes it: Nhal folds the attack into an empty patch of sky, Tavren unfolds its registered pattern for public inspection, and Sathra identifies its purchaser. Their presence and the failed attacks disclose that the Crown's supposed divine ownership is a subordinate license.
- Confronted in the air above his own balcony, the Emperor invokes **Sovereign Recall**, a purchased imperial emergency spell that seizes every subject's prepaid magical reserves. He means to turn the whole world's accumulated power against the six. Hedran recognizes the spell she helped authorize as a defense against rebellion; the danger and her complicity become concrete as distant casters lose their connections. She cannot solve it by issuing a better permission.
- The six choose to meet the Recall at full strength and turn its own worldwide reach against the monopoly. Vesra detaches the command that binds each living caster, Brannoc catches the converging current in his augmented body, Ossan carries their working to the farthest nodes, Tavren changes the Recall's return path, Nhal opens the palace's sealed heart without dropping its inhabitants, and Sathra severs the Crown's exclusive license. All six exercise consequential authority in the decisive action. They return the seized reserves to their owners and distribute authorization to the living casters themselves. The registry and real expenditure of magical power remain; imperial permission and the Crown's fee no longer do.
- The palace loses its coercive enchantments, not its support or its people's lives. The Emperor's last command is denied. Hedran witnesses the six open great sky passages through the former imperial barriers, bringing the capital face to face with distant cities previously visible only on its conquest maps. The strangers depart through those open skies together. The payoff is the demonstrated end of the Empire's magical supremacy, carried in the same network that made it seem inevitable, with the six still formidable and not installed as another court.

The chief risk is reducing these strangers to a lecture about fees or making Hedran's disillusionment the climax. The draft should center the six's incursion, the successive failures of imperial force, and their spectacular, deliberate unmaking of the monopoly; Hedran gives the reader proximity to the reversal rather than rescuing its agents.
