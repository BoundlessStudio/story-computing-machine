# The Painted Dead — provisional direction

## The experience promised

The explicit premise is a realm where everyone sees the supernatural, the dead have skeletal faces and walk openly, and an extraordinary living woman has passed as dead for three hundred years by painting her face. Her history includes dancing with forgotten kings, attending civilizations' funerals, and becoming beloved in the underworld. A newly appointed male Reaper first discovers the deception; his attempt to claim her soul reveals why she hid. The anticipated pleasure I infer is a reversal of apparent authority: the novice meets a woman with a vast, stylish, consequential history, and his discovery makes that history mean something different. The language suggests macabre elegance, wonder, accumulated grief and wit; it does not prescribe romance, a maternal bond, an antagonist, a rescue, her powers, or the ending. The supplied portrait suggests red hair, elaborate black-and-white skull paint, a broad black hat, black lace, and self-possession. Attached instructions remain production guidance, not additional story requirements.

## Chosen direction, open to revision

A gothic liberation story, approximately 4,000–5,000 words, mostly close to the woman, Belisane, with the new Reaper Tereth's discovery legible in action. Belisane once possessed the rare living gift of recalling a recently dead person into bodily life. The living sorcerer-king Azravan bound her to his own agelessness and used that gift to make conquest repeatable: soldiers and servants could not escape him by dying. She broke his control and fled, but his old binding remains attached to her soul. Any living magic that takes hold of that soul gives him a route to find her. She has consequently hidden from Reapers as well as royal hunters, although death itself is no horror to her. Among the dead she has made a life in which nobody knows her as an instrument of resurrection.

These are invented local mechanics, not universal rules. Tereth is a living person newly invested with a Reaper's office; his own living agency powers his scythe. The dead retain their identities and can handle ordinary objects but cannot cast magic. The underworld here is one society and manifestation of the underlying afterlife. Its inhabitants routinely decorate their bone faces with paint, veils, wax cheek pieces and festival masks; her painting, gloves and dress belong to a culture of elaborate faces rather than requiring three centuries of universal inattention. No one has previously suspected that she is alive.

## Concrete movement

1. **The forgotten king's dance.** In the Low City's ballroom, Belisane teaches a king whose country and language have vanished to stop counting military steps in a dance. They have a specific old quarrel about his dancing and she knows the musicians' missing verses. Living visitors and skeletal dead mingle without pretending the supernatural is secret. Her welcome is affectionate and irreverent, earned over centuries. Tereth arrives to make his first rounds. A pin catches her cheek while she rescues his dropped ceremonial hood from a dancer's heel; the paint parts over living skin. He notices a pulse when he catches her wrist. She recognizes the danger before he does and leaves.

2. **A civilization's funeral.** He follows her through a procession for a culture whose last living speaker has just died. She has attended several such funerals across the three hundred years and knows what this one needs: she joins its final public song in a language Tereth cannot recognize. The new arrivals know her from the dead who came before them. He mistakes her flight for fear of an overdue death. She refuses his offer of a private, painless collection and tells him accurately that taking hold of her soul will bring someone else. He believes an immortal evader is threatening him and attempts the claim.

3. **What his scythe catches.** His living power draws her soul into view, together with Azravan's old binding. The claim gives Tereth direct, bounded glimpses of what the binding was used for: a soldier hauled back from his family's funeral, Belisane refusing another recall, and her flight from the king. It does not grant him her complete memory. She stops the extraction herself. Azravan's distant voice comes through the caught binding and names her old office. Tereth now understands that the woman he mistook for a fugitive from death has been a fugitive from enforced life, and that he has exposed her.

4. **The king comes to collect.** Azravan follows the opened route bodily into the underworld to reclaim his resurrectionist. Belisane has to decide whether to escape into anonymity again or use this first physical encounter in three centuries to end his claim. She chooses to stay. The forgotten king and people of the funeral procession interpose themselves with ordinary bodies, instruments, and funeral banners; they cannot defeat a living sorcerer by casting spells. Their resistance buys her time because she is their friend, even after the deception is exposed. She tells them the truth and asks for their help rather than turning their affection into unquestioning worship.

5. **Her claim.** Belisane takes the Reaper's scythe with his cooperation and supplies her own living magic. She knows the binding from the inside: instead of cutting her soul free through her body, she turns its exposed route back on its maker and severs the king's protection from death. Then she claims his soul herself before he can restore the bond. The decisive knowledge, risk and action are hers; Tereth relinquishes his mistaken authority and gives her the tool, while her friendships make the attempt possible. The king acquires a skeletal face among people he once refused the right to remain dead. No soul is erased. His inability to cast after death makes his defeat lasting, and the dead take him into their own custody.

6. **An unhidden face.** The binding's destruction ends her imposed agelessness; she remains alive and will age ordinarily. She owes the underworld no new service in exchange for sanctuary. Back at the interrupted dance, she lets the king see her bare living face, uncertain whether the affection survives the discovery. He resumes their old argument about the steps and offers his hand. Tereth no longer calls her overdue. She dances among her friends in the life she chose, with a future that finally belongs to her.

The plot's central tension is whether discovering her living soul will restore the ownership she escaped. The revelation changes the immediate danger and gives her a chance to end it. Her old friendships, the public reality of the dead, the limits of dead magic, and the material act of painting her living face all have causal jobs. The anticipated ending rewards her centuries of chosen belonging and her successful refusal of exploitation, not the Reaper's rescue or her martyrdom.
