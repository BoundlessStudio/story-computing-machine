# The Small Moon Rose First

## Full Story Outline

The attached Earth story remains the ending. The new material covers Nell Mercado and Sebastian “Bash” Wren’s 617 days in Ravel, ending when the Glass Gate tears them apart. The next chapter begins with Nell returning beside the frozen pizzas.

The Earth section should keep its current wording except for the two line-level continuity corrections listed near the end of this document.

## Story shape

- Target length: 100,000–115,000 words.
- Forty chapters, with Chapters 1–33 set in Ravel and Chapters 34–40 drawn from the attached ending.
- Close third person. A chapter or clearly divided section stays inside one child’s point of view.
- Nell and Bash’s bond is familial rather than romantic. They become the only witnesses who can confirm the other’s 617-day life.
- Nell cuts one tally into a leather belt each morning. Chapter headings carry the day or day range so the count never becomes vague.
- The Ravel chapters should vary in size. A siege may take twenty pages. A six-week winter can pass through a handful of hard scenes.
- Exposition arrives through danger, work, arguments, mistakes, and objects. Nobody stops to lecture for three pages.
- Humor comes from friction: Bash’s vanity, Nell’s suspicion of anything decorative, Saint’s teeth, Oren’s cheating, and the children’s inability to admit they need each other.

## Central premise

Nell Mercado vanishes from a Philadelphia supermarket on February eighteenth.

Sebastian Wren vanishes from a Massachusetts cello lesson on June seventh.

They land in Ravel on the same day, only seconds apart. Neither says the Earth date aloud. Each assumes the other was taken at roughly the same moment.

Ravel’s Glass Gate does not obey a single clock. It catches travellers through separate temporal anchors, drawing them from different Earth dates into one shared present. Nell and Bash live the same 617 days in Ravel. When they cross home, the Gate returns each child to the Earth moment tied to that child’s disappearance.

That is why Nell comes back in February and Bash comes back in June, even though less than a second separates them inside the collapsing Gate.

The Hollow Regent rules Ravel through complete identities. Nell and Bash are useful to the resistance because Ravel has no record of either child. Wards built to recognize registered people cannot read them cleanly.

Adults discover this.

They keep finding reasons to send the children where named adults cannot go.

## The identity rule

A complete identity requires three things:

1. Given name.
2. Family name.
3. Place of origin.

Ravelers often call the full three-line record a “true name.” A single part can help the Regent search. Two parts can narrow the hunt. All three are required before he can enter a person’s dreams, force obedience, or use that person as a path to family and home.

“Place of origin” means the place the person recognizes as home, named with enough accuracy to identify it. A broad region, a guessed city, a school, or a street heard out of context does not complete the line.

This distinction matters:

- Nell says “Frankford” often, but Bash does not know whether she means a street, neighborhood, town, or something else.
- Bash mentions Boston and lessons near Cambridge, but he does not live in Cambridge.
- Nell knows his given name is Sebastian.
- Bash knows Nell as Nell.
- Neither child knows the other’s family name until 30th Street Station.

Oren’s rule is simple: give people one word they can call you, then stop.

Sebastian becomes Bash. Nell uses Nell and withholds the rest. They do not exchange surnames, school names, streets, or exact home places.

The Hollow Regent erased his own given name, family name, and place of origin. Nobody can complete or command him. The cost is worse than death: he cannot hold one stable self. His ledgers keep him assembled from identities stolen from other people.

## The Glass Gate rule

The identity system and the Gate’s time system are separate.

The Gate follows a traveller’s private memory of departure. That memory is a temporal anchor, not part of a true name. A traveller does not need to speak the date, write it down, or share it with another person.

When the Gate returns someone to Earth, it rebuilds the traveller around the body-state held in that temporal anchor:

- Earth age and body size return.
- Muscle gained in Ravel disappears.
- Healed Ravel injuries remain as scars.
- Hair can return altered if the change has become part of the traveller’s remembered self.
- Departure clothing returns to its original state.
- A small Ravel object can cross if it is worn, bound to the body, clenched, or caught in the crossing.

Nell’s original red hoodie, jeans, and untied left shoelace return. Her spear scar remains. The copper crescent stays in her fist.

Bash returns at twelve with the ash-hound scars still on his shoulder. His copper half is pressed into his palm during the collapse and crosses with him.

Nell’s tally belt is a Ravel-made leather belt, not part of her departure clothing. She wears it over her restored jeans when she returns.

## Core cast

### Nell Mercado

Thirteen when she leaves Earth. She lives near the elevated train in Philadelphia and hears Frankford often enough that Bash remembers the word without understanding it.

Nell distrusts ornament, promises, soft beds, adults who call danger necessary, and gifts that cost too much. She learns routes faster than languages. Her first instinct is to count: doors, arrows, meals, steps, days.

She wants the Gate destroyed.

### Sebastian “Bash” Wren

Twelve when he leaves Earth. He comes from a wealthy Massachusetts family, attends private school, studies Latin, plays cello, and has lessons near Cambridge without living there.

Bash reads patterns before he reads people. He believes a problem can be solved if someone studies it long enough. He hates onions and pepperoni, cannot gut a fish, snores through his mouth, and cries when angry.

He wants the Gate preserved long enough to help the people left in Ravel.

### Oren

An old road guide who cheats at dice, sleeps in his boots, and refuses to give either child his family name or origin.

He once helped build the Regent’s identity system. He knows its weaknesses because some of them are his work. He has guided other outsiders before Nell and Bash. None survived.

He wants to make one decent choice before his past catches him.

### Saint

A gray mule with a torn ear, bad teeth, and a particular hatred of Bash.

Saint is transport, alarm system, source of several bad decisions, and the answer to the question that proves Bash is alive.

### The Hollow Regent

The former keeper of the Glass Gate. He escaped control by deleting his own identity from every record and living memory.

He now survives inside other people’s names.

### Recurring Ravel figures

Ravelers protect themselves with road names and chosen names. These are the names used in the outline.

- Kest, commander of Drosswall.
- Siva, a blind record keeper on the Salt Road.
- Caldrin, the Gate scholar at Sere.
- Tavra, a child from a village forced to remove its walls.
- Pell, a Regent official who offers Nell a bargain.

## Language continuity

Earlier variants should be dialects, ciphers, or damaged forms of these six. Do not introduce a seventh dead language by accident.

Nell learns one short Gate Cant severance command in Chapter 31. It is the phrase she screams during the collapse and again when she lands in the supermarket.

# Part I: Two Children Without Names

## Chapter 1: The Floor That Wasn’t There  
### Day 1  
POV: Nell

Nell’s last Earth sensations are freezer light, cold air on her hands, and her mother’s fingers around her sleeve.

Then the floor vanishes.

She lands in ankle-deep black water beneath a shattered arch. Her red hoodie and jeans are soaked. Her left shoelace is untied. Before she can decide whether to run or climb, a pale boy in a concert shirt drops beside her.

Sebastian’s last clear memory is Mrs. Halpern correcting his left thumb. The cello stays on Earth. He arrives with nothing useful.

They dislike each other at once. Nell thinks he is rich, loud, and built to die quickly. Sebastian thinks she is violent, irrational, and possibly part of the trap.

Men in blank porcelain masks enter the ruin. They ask Sebastian for his given name, family name, and place of origin. He says “Sebastian” before Oren throws salt into the water and drags both children through a drainage passage.

The reflections beneath the masked men stop matching their movements. That gives them half a minute.

Behind Nell, the broken arch fills with white light.

Then it becomes ordinary glass again.

## Chapter 2: Three Boxes in the Dust  
### Days 2–7  
POV: Bash

Oren takes them to an abandoned road station.

He draws three boxes in the dust and labels them: given name, family name, origin. One box is a clue. Two are dangerous. Three make a door.

A wounded soldier sleeps in the station. During a nightmare, he recites all three parts of his identity. Something answers through his mouth. He wakes biting through his own tongue while a second voice asks where his children live.

Nell stops treating Oren’s warning as a trick.

Sebastian does not want strangers using his full given name. Nell cuts it to Bash without asking. He objects. The name stays.

Oren trades for a narrow leather belt when Nell’s jeans begin slipping after several hungry days. She cuts one mark into the inside surface. The next morning she cuts another. Groups of five. One deeper slash every tenth day.

Oren also finds Bash a coarse red wool scarf. Bash hates the color, then becomes attached to it because it is the first warm thing anyone gives him in Ravel.

They agree to wait near the ruin until the Gate reopens.

It does not.

## Chapter 3: The Black Orchard  
### Days 8–18  
POV: Nell

The quickest road to Drosswall crosses an orchard whose trees grow memories instead of fruit.

The hanging shapes use familiar voices. Nell hears Celia asking why she walked away in the supermarket. Bash hears his father saying the problem can be handled if he follows instructions.

Oren orders them to answer nothing.

A thing made of bark, sap, and children’s fingers grabs Nell’s backpack from behind. When she turns, it laughs and says it is only playing.

Bash recognizes an Orissan funerary count carved into the trunks. He reads it backward, forcing the orchard to repeat its own dead instead of the children’s memories.

Nell sets the creature on fire. Burning sap strikes her jaw and leaves the scar Bash later remembers.

Outside the orchard, they find a gray mule tied to a dead cart. It has a torn ear and tries to kick Bash into a ditch.

The mule needs a name.

“Saint,” Nell says.

Their plan to wait for the Gate dies in the orchard. Now they need walls, food, and someone who understands what took them.

They start for Drosswall.

# Part II: The Salt Road

## Chapter 4: The Customs Priest  
### Days 19–27  
POV: Bash

A border tower blocks the Salt Road. Its customs priest asks every traveller for the three identity lines.

Oren gives a road name. Nell says nothing. Bash tries to talk his way through the inspection and makes it worse.

The priest reaches for Nell’s pack. Saint grows restless. Nell yanks Bash’s red scarf from his neck and feeds one end to the mule.

Bash lunges for it.

The priest grabs the other end.

Saint bites the priest hard enough to take skin and part of a finger.

They escape during the screaming. Bash does not forgive Nell for the scarf, though he knows she saved them.

This becomes the private answer only the real Bash can give on Earth:

> Saint bit the customs priest because you fed him my red scarf.

## Chapter 5: Salt  
### Days 28–40  
POV: Nell

The Salt Road is built from pale slabs that burn bare skin after sunset.

Oren teaches Nell to test saltstone against a tooth. Good stone rings faintly. Chalk gives. Poisoned substitute leaves the tongue numb. Months later, Dr. Kan’s polished therapy stone triggers the old test.

Nell learns to find water by watching insects, repair tack, and cut rot from Saint’s hoof. She also learns that Saint will behave for exactly as long as food remains visible.

Bash discovers that years of tuning strings make him good at judging distance by sound. He hears hollow chambers beneath the road and finds an old cistern.

He cannot gut a fish.

During a stay with the blind record keeper Siva, Bash copies Talric road inscriptions for meals while Nell loads stone at a quarry. Each thinks the other got the easy job. Both are wrong.

The settlement’s emergency food is a fibrous root dug from beneath the roadbed. Bash says it tastes like dirt.

“It is dirt,” Nell says.

Heat, lice, and dried blood finally drive Nell to hack off her hair with the skinning knife. Bash tries to correct her technique. She threatens to improve his haircut next.

## Chapter 6: The Small Moon  
### Days 41–53  
POV: Bash

One night without shelter, Nell and Bash argue over which moon rises first.

The large gold moon is brighter, so Nell insists it leads. Bash says the smaller blue moon clears the eastern hills eleven minutes earlier.

They stay awake to settle it.

Bash wins.

He is unbearable for days.

During the long watch, they trade incomplete pieces of home.

Nell mentions a train above the street, a mother who works nights, and the word Frankford. She never says Philadelphia.

Bash talks about cello, Latin, private school, a mother who hates wet shoes, Boston trips, and lessons near Cambridge. He never says his town or school.

They argue about pizza. Bash hates pepperoni. He hates onions more. Nell says a person who dislikes both has no right to be rescued.

These scraps later become search terms. None completes an identity.

## Chapter 7: Villages Without Walls  
### Days 54–69  
POV: Nell

They pass settlements ordered to dismantle their own walls as punishment for hiding names from the Regent.

People sleep in cellars. Doors are built to look like windows. Children carry messages because the Regent’s wards have trouble reading anyone without a completed record.

In one village they meet Tavra, a girl near Nell’s age who treats Saint as if he is reasonable. Saint likes her immediately, which Nell finds suspicious.

The village needs medicine carried through a named checkpoint. Oren refuses at first.

Then he sees the sick children and changes his mind.

Nell understands what happened. The adults found a reason to call the dangerous choice necessary.

She and Bash carry the medicine. A younger courier running a second route is captured. Nobody can tell Nell whether he survived.

Bash says helping was still right.

Nell says that does not make the adults right.

## Chapter 8: The Road Bell  
### Days 70–87  
POV: Bash

A Regent patrol controls the final approach to Drosswall with a bell that rings whenever someone with a completed identity crosses its shadow.

The bell stays silent for Nell and Bash.

They could continue.

Bash notices prisoners being marched into the tower. He convinces Nell to help break the bell before they leave. The plan fails early. A guard dies in front of Bash, and he freezes.

Nell pulls him behind the mechanism.

Bash recovers by listening to the bell’s repeating rhythm as if it were music. He identifies the weak interval. Nell cuts the supporting rope. The bell crushes the checkpoint gate.

Later, Bash cries because he is furious.

He denies crying.

Nell remembers both facts.

Drosswall appears the next morning.

# Part III: Drosswall

## Chapter 9: A Wall with Too Many Doors  
### Days 88–100  
POV: Nell

Drosswall is the first place that looks defensible.

Its outer walls are dark stone mixed with powdered mirror glass. Refugees crowd the inner courtyards. Siege engines wait beyond the fields.

Oren brings the children to the surviving Glass Gate archive. Archivists treat Bash as a translation problem and Nell as a map that can walk through name-wards.

Oren insists they are children.

He still lets the tests continue.

Commander Kest promises to search for a route home after the siege. Nell does not believe him. Bash wants to.

They are given beds.

Nell rejects hers, checks every door, window, drain, stair, and roof access, then sleeps beneath a mess table with a knife against her chest.

Bash hates the open dormitory. He moves his blanket into a drying room beside warm pipes, where the walls are close and he can see the only door.

## Chapter 10: Useful  
### Days 101–116  
POV: Bash

Bash studies Avenic archive folios and learns quickly. Curiosity drives him. So does the belief that enough knowledge can repair anything.

Nell maps service corridors, neglected stairs, cistern access, and doors that stick in damp weather. She learns which guards sleep after midnight and how many arrows each wall station holds.

Bash steals a silver button from Oren’s spare coat to prove the old man is less observant than he claims.

Oren notices almost immediately.

He says nothing.

Bash becomes too ashamed to return it.

During quiet nights, Oren cheats at dice by palming pieces and changing totals. He also sleeps in his boots. Nell distrusts both habits. Bash finds the cheating funny until Oren takes his last dried apple.

## Chapter 11: Glass Weather  
### Days 117–122  
POV: Nell

The siege begins with arrows made from thin green glass.

They splinter against walls and keep cutting after they break.

Nell guides trapped civilians across an exposed courtyard. Bash holds a shield over her while the arrows fall. A shard opens his scalp.

Nell cuts away a patch of his hair with the skinning knife and stitches the wound. Bash complains about her knots until she offers to remove them and start again.

Nell is assigned the remaining arrows for the north wall. By the second night she knows the count without looking. When Bash later remembers her in Ravel, he remembers the flat stare of someone who knew exactly how many arrows remained.

The siege engines strike through the night. Their low groan travels through the stone before each impact.

That vibration becomes the elevated train inside Nell’s returned body.

## Chapter 12: The North Stair  
### Days 123–134  
POV: Bash

A bombardment collapses part of Drosswall’s north stair.

Nell catches herself on a broken railing and snaps the little finger of her left hand. She keeps moving until the civilians behind her reach shelter.

Bash resets the finger from a half-translated medical gloss.

He is bad at it.

The finger heals crooked.

## Chapter 13: Three, Pause, Two  
### Days 135–154  
POV: Nell

Kest sends Nell and Bash through an old aqueduct to disable a siege engine. Every registered adult who tried the route collapsed beneath its identity wards.

Oren prepares them despite hating the plan.

A cave-in separates the children underground.

Bash taps against a pipe:

Three taps. Pause. Two.

Still alive?

Nell answers once.

For now.

They reunite in the dark, break the engine, and return through tunnels filled with wounded soldiers.

The signal remains theirs.

## Chapter 14: The Open Gate  
### Days 155–176  
POV: Bash

The Regent does not break Drosswall’s walls.

A captured messenger recites Kest’s given name, family name, and place of origin. The complete identity reaches him in sleep.

Kest walks to the main gate and opens it.

Drosswall falls in one night.

Nell guides refugees through the passages she mapped. Bash carries archive folios beneath his clothes and shields her while glass arrows fall through the courtyard.

Oren steals a ledger fragment before they flee. Nell sees him take it but has no time to ask why.

Saint carries two injured children through the drainage channel.

By dawn, Drosswall burns behind them.

Bash keeps looking back.

Nell does not.

# Part IV: After the Wall

## Chapter 15: The Ash Hound  
### Days 177–206  
POV: Nell

The survivors scatter along the Salt Road.

A Regent hunter releases an ash hound. The creature follows memories attached to partial names. It cannot command Bash, but it can track the sound “Sebastian” from the ruined arrival site and the road records that followed.

The hound attacks during a dust storm.

Bash pushes a younger child out of its path and takes a cluster of bites across his shoulder. The wound tears deep enough to expose muscle.

Nell kills the animal with a broken road marker.

Saint bolts at the smell of blood and takes most of their supplies.

For two days Nell drags Bash on a grain sledge. She sleeps in pieces, counts his breaths, and threatens him whenever he slips toward unconsciousness.

This is the strength her returned Earth body will lose.

## Chapter 16: The Brine Houses  
### Days 207–225  
POV: Bash

They shelter in abandoned salt-workers’ houses while Bash recovers.

The rooms are damp, narrow, and warm near the drying flues. Bash sleeps best inside a cupboard-sized salt room with one visible entrance.

During fever he begins to say “Wren.” Nell covers his mouth before the word becomes clear. She never asks what he almost said.

Bash snores through his mouth. Nell learns the sound while counting his breaths in the dark.

Oren works from Kesh medical glosses while Bash slowly learns to read them. The broth contains onions. Bash refuses it even while starving.

Nell tells him dying from principles is still dying.

He drinks around the onions.

He tries to gut another fish and ruins it.

## Chapter 17: Rain Going Up  
### Days 226–247  
POV: Bash

The hills outside Sere have inverted weather.

Water rises from puddles, wells, wet cloth, and exposed skin toward clouds that never empty. Travellers cross wrapped in waxed fabric and sleep beneath floors instead of roofs.

Bash compares damaged Talric warning stones. Nell realizes their placement forms a route when seen across the slope.

His language and her sense of space work together.

Neither child could cross alone.

## Chapter 18: Sere  
### Days 248–266  
POV: Nell

Sere is built beneath overhanging stone so the upward rain cannot strip its wells.

Caldrin studies the Drosswall folios and confirms what Oren feared: the Hollow Regent is trying to open a stable route to Earth.

Earth records identities everywhere. School rosters. Hospital files. Bills. Government databases. The Regent would enter a world already arranged into names, families, and origins.

Caldrin separates the identity system from the Gate’s time system. To send Nell and Bash home, three conditions must be met:

1. Recover the return sequence from Vey.
2. Break the Regent’s primary identity ledger.
3. Preserve each child’s private memory of departure as a temporal anchor.

The children do not have to compare those memories. Neither sees a reason to discuss Earth dates.

They still assume they left Earth together.

## Chapter 19: Oren’s Ledger  
### Days 267–302  
POV: Nell

Sere offers temporary homes.

Bash stays with scholars and sleeps badly in a room with four windows. Nell stays above a bakery, hides bread under her mattress, and loses part of the cache to mice.

Separation makes both of them worse.

Regent agents find Sere through a mark hidden inside the ledger fragment Oren stole from Drosswall.

Oren confesses.

He helped design the Regent’s three-line identity system. He built search wards, dream routes, and early jars that could hold a person’s record. He has known since Day 1 that the children’s arrival woke the Glass Gate.

He also guided other outsiders because wards could not see them cleanly.

None survived.

Nell wants to leave him behind.

Bash argues that Oren remains their best chance of reaching home.

Nell hears something else: useful people get forgiven.

Sere survives the attack. The children cannot stay. Caldrin gives them a route to the ruins of Vey.

# Part V: The Dead Roads

## Chapter 20: Second Winter  
### Days 303–341  
POV: Nell

Their second cold season begins.

They pass towns where residents have lost pieces of identity. One woman remembers every childbirth she attended but not her own children. A miller knows the work of his hands and nothing about the person who taught him.

Bash wants to stop at each settlement.

Nell counts the days.

Their argument has changed. Bash fears becoming someone who escaped while everyone else remained trapped. Nell fears Ravel will keep inventing reasons they cannot leave.

At a sloping road marker, Nell claims she is taller. Bash says she is standing uphill.

“There isn’t a hill.”

“There’s a slope.”

The argument survives longer than the camp.

Their food is mostly Salt Road roots. Bash complains every time.

## Chapter 21: Vey  
### Days 342–356  
POV: Bash

Vey was built down the walls of a crater. Most bridges have fallen. Rooms hang over open air.

The return sequence lies in an observatory near the lowest level.

During a collapse, Bash strikes his face against a stone rail and breaks his nose.

Nell tries to set it.

He refuses.

It heals badly enough to leave the soft whistle Nell later hears over the phone.

## Chapter 22: Six Dead Languages  
### Days 357–369  
POV: Bash

The observatory instructions appear in six dead languages. No copy is complete.

Bash builds the sequence by comparing Orissan, Talric, Avenic, Kesh, Veyan, and Gate Cant. This chapter completes the promised count of six.

Nell notices the writing forms a floor plan when viewed through a cracked ceiling lens.

Together they learn the Regent’s origin.

He once kept the Glass Gate. Enemies tried to control him through his identity, so he erased his given name, family name, and origin from every record and mind.

Nobody could command him afterward.

He could no longer remain one person either.

The stolen identities in his ledgers now hold him together. Breaking the ledgers will kill him or reduce him to whatever remains beneath the borrowed lives.

## Chapter 23: The Offer  
### Days 370–405  
POV: Nell

Indexers capture the group outside Vey.

The soldiers surrendered their identities in exchange for freedom from dream attacks. They follow orders because too little private self remains to resist.

Pell offers Nell immediate passage home if she completes Bash’s record.

Pell already has “Sebastian.” He wants the family name and exact place Bash calls home.

Nell cannot give either one.

She has Boston, Cambridge, cello, Latin, and private school. Those clues are not a family name or a true home place.

Pell refuses to believe two children could spend more than a year together without exchanging surnames.

Nell lets him assume she is lying.

During the escape, Oren admits that previous outsiders died on missions he approved.

He says he meant to save them.

Nell says intentions do not bury anyone.

## Chapter 24: Eight Days Without Speaking  
### Days 406–436  
POV: Bash

Nell and Bash travel for eight days without speaking except for warnings, distances, and watch changes.

Bash believes destroying the Regent is the only way to give their suffering a use.

Nell rejects the bargain. Saving someone now cannot make what happened to them right.

Their reconciliation is small.

Bash leaves the last clean bandage beside Nell without comment. Nell sharpens the iron spike he keeps hidden where guards would take a proper knife.

The spike becomes the habit behind the sharpened screwdriver in his Earth schoolbag.

On the eighth night, Bash taps three, pause, two against Saint’s saddle.

Nell answers once.

Oren’s map points toward Marn’s Bridge.

# Part VI: Marn’s Bridge

## Chapter 25: What Was Under the Bridge  
### Days 437–466  
POV: Nell

Drosswall survivors have gathered near Marn’s Bridge.

Beneath its foundations, Nell and Bash find rows of black clay jars sealed with wax. Each jar contains a stolen identity.

When opened, a jar whispers three lines in the owner’s voice:

- Given name.
- Family name.
- Place of origin.

Some jars have one line scratched away. Others have two names mixed together. This is why some victims recover cleanly and others return in fragments.

This is the true answer to Nell’s later authentication question.

Not treasure.

Thousands of people.

Oren admits he designed the first jars.

The resistance prepares an evacuation before breaking them. Nell insists that every adult hear the danger and choose whether to stay. She will make a plan. She will not make their consent for them.

## Chapter 26: Water Through the Stones  
### Days 467–478  
POV: Nell

The Regent attacks before the evacuation is complete.

Nell takes a spear beneath her right ribs while shielding Bash. The blade tears toward her navel.

Oren drags her into the chamber and receives a wound he knows will kill him.

Smoke works under the bridge. Water drips through the stones.

Bash tries to light a match so they can bind Nell’s wound. His fingers are too numb to bend. He ruins one match, then another.

Oren lies beside Nell.

Before he dies, Bash apologizes for stealing the silver button.

Nell hears. She pretends to be asleep.

By the time Bash gets a flame, Oren is already dead.

## Chapter 27: The Copper Crescent  
### Days 479–506  
POV: Bash

They bury Oren without speaking a family name or origin he never gave them.

Oren’s spare coat remains tied beneath Saint’s saddle. After the burial, Bash searches it for dry tinder and finds crooked dice, a dried apple core, and a worn copper gaming token.

He puts the stolen silver button into Oren’s grave.

The children open the identity jars. Some names return whole. Others return as pieces that may never fit.

Nell spends weeks recovering. The spear wound closes into a white, puckered scar.

They keep the copper token because throwing it away feels too much like forgiveness and too much like condemnation.

Neither says which feeling is worse.

## Chapter 28: Adults Decide  
### Days 507–534  
POV: Nell

Breaking the jars weakens the Regent’s control across the Salt Road.

Villages refuse orders. Former Indexers regain memories. Drosswall survivors propose an assault on the primary ledger and the Glass Gate.

Several adults try to put Nell and Bash at the center of the decision.

Nell refuses.

She gives them the maps. Bash translates the Vey sequence. Then both children leave the room while the adults vote.

The adults choose to fight.

That difference matters.

The final march begins.

# Part VII: The Glass Gate

## Chapter 29: Hollow Country  
### Days 535–566  
POV: Bash

They return toward the ruin where Nell and Bash first arrived.

The land around the Glass Gate has changed. Houses recite residents’ three-line identities when doors open. Wells ask who is drinking. Roads remember registered feet.

A door may say, “Mara Osset, born of Sere,” before it unlocks.

Nothing can produce a completed record for Nell or Bash.

The resistance travels mostly in silence.

Saint carries the Vey folios and bites anyone who reaches for them.

The flooded ruin now stands inside a city built around the awakened Gate.

## Chapter 30: The Houses That Know You  
### Days 567–596  
POV: Split into separate Nell and Bash sections

The first assault targets census towers that feed identities into the Regent’s central ledger.

The resistance must fight people who are compelled rather than loyal. Killing them is easier than freeing them. Nell’s plans use slower routes that leave exits.

Bash enters a translation chamber beneath the Gate.

The Gate, damaged and half-awake, traps each child inside a reconstruction made from sensory memory. These false homes come from temporal anchors, not the Regent’s identity magic.

Nell stands in aisle nine with Celia beside her. The false Celia never checks a price before putting food in the cart.

Bash returns to his cello room. The false Nell calmly says she likes onions.

The errors are small.

That makes them worse.

Each child escapes because they know the other in useless, irritating detail.

## Chapter 31: The Ledger Room  
### Days 597–611  
POV: Split into separate Bash and Nell sections

The primary ledger is not a book.

It is a circular chamber built from three moving bands: given names, family names, and places of origin. The walls rewrite themselves as people are recorded, stolen, erased, or forced into false combinations.

Bash uses all six dead languages to alter the return sequence.

Nell reads the room as architecture. The writing is load-bearing. Breaking selected columns will collapse the Regent’s control without destroying the Gate at once.

The Regent speaks through people they met on the road. He tries to make each child complete the other’s identity.

Nell knows Sebastian. She does not know Wren or his home town.

Bash knows Nell. He has guessed Philadelphia. He does not know Mercado, and Frankford remains uncertain.

Their missing information protects them.

Bash teaches Nell one Gate Cant command meaning sever the path. She memorizes it by rhythm rather than grammar.

## Chapter 32: For Luck  
### Days 612–616  
POV: Nell

Before the final assault, Bash strikes Oren’s copper token against a stone until it breaks into two rough crescents.

He gives one to Nell.

“For luck.”

Nell tells him luck is what rich people call it when somebody else did the planning.

Bash laughs hard enough that Saint tries to bite him.

The resistance destroys the supporting ledgers. Stolen identities tear free of the Regent. His body changes with each loss: old, young, wounded, healthy, sometimes no person at all.

There is no clean duel. The children dismantle the system that lets him exist.

The Regent dies asking which borrowed face was originally his.

Nobody knows.

The Glass Gate opens and begins cracking.

Bash wants to stabilize it long enough to preserve a future route. Villages along the Salt Road still have no walls.

Nell wants to cross before Ravel finds another use for them.

Neither persuades the other.

Bash’s Gate kit contains boots, water tablets, matches, two knives, and rope. These become the exact categories he stores beneath his Earth bed.

Saint refuses to approach the light. Tavra agrees to take him.

Bash presses his forehead against the mule’s torn ear and pulls away before Saint can bite.

The surviving adults tell the children to go.

## Chapter 33: Day Six Hundred and Seventeen  
### Day 617  
POV: Nell

Nell cuts the 617th mark into the inner surface of her belt.

She and Bash enter the Glass Gate holding hands.

The floor breaks beneath them.

A surviving identity-thread catches Bash’s ankle. He cuts it, falls, and strikes his mouth against glass. Blood fills his teeth.

Nell screams the Gate Cant severance command.

The light tears open behind her.

Their joined hands slip. Nell catches Bash by the wrist.

“I’m right behind you,” he shouts.

His wrist slides through her fingers.

Nell falls backward into white light with one copper crescent clenched in her fist.

Bash closes his hand around the other half. The collapsing Gate drives the rough edge into his palm as he crosses.

Inside Ravel, less than a second separates them.

The Gate follows Nell’s February eighteenth anchor.

Then it follows Bash’s June seventh anchor.

# Part VIII: Earth

The attached story becomes Chapters 34–40. Keep the existing prose and insert chapter breaks at the following turns.

## Chapter 34: Eleven Seconds

Nell returns beside the frozen pizzas on February eighteenth.

Her Earth body is thirteen again. She wears the same red hoodie and jeans. Her left shoelace is still untied. The spear scar is healed beneath her ribs. The copper crescent is in her fist. The tally belt comes back with her.

She calls for Bash.

Nobody knows him.

Hospital staff, police, school, sleep beneath the kitchen table, the bread knife, the lock checks, the trains, Jordan Price, and Dr. Kan belong in this chapter.

## Chapter 35: The Small Moon Rose First

Nell searches for Sebastian using the scraps she has:

- Boston.
- Cambridge.
- Cello.
- Latin.
- Private school.
- Shoulder scar.
- Two moons.
- A mother who hates wet shoes.

She does not know Wren or his true home place.

She creates the blog and posts the copper crescent. Her private question asks what Saint did at the border tower.

The impostor guesses treasure beneath Marn’s Bridge.

Nell nearly stops checking.

## Chapter 36: Twelve Seconds

Sebastian disappears from his June seventh cello lesson and returns twelve seconds later.

His Earth body is twelve again. The ash-hound bites remain healed on his shoulder. The copper crescent is embedded in his palm.

His family tries to name what happened in terms insurance will recognize.

He sleeps beside the dryer.

A private investigator searches for Nell using Frankford, the elevated train, and a mother who works nights.

## Chapter 37: The Message

Bash finds the blog created before he vanished.

He answers:

> Saint bit the customs priest because you fed him my red scarf.

Nell tests him with the north stair, her left little finger, Vey, and Oren’s silver button.

They compare Earth dates for the first time.

While Nell was searching for Bash, he had not yet left Earth.

## Chapter 38: 30th Street Station

The families arrange a meeting in Philadelphia.

Nell recognizes Bash by posture before she trusts his face. He stays close to the wall and counts exits.

They show the copper halves.

Bash taps three, pause, two.

Nell answers once.

Only then do they exchange family names.

Mercado.

Wren.

After 617 days together, these are among the last things they learn.

## Chapter 39: Finding Each Other Didn’t Fix Them

Their families clash. Their class differences return. Bash tries to help with expensive headphones. Nell hears pity and control.

They begin therapy together.

Old survival habits remain:

- Floors instead of beds.
- Backs away from windows.
- Hidden food.
- Exit checks.
- A sharpened screwdriver.
- A Gate pack beneath Bash’s bed.

Their disagreement about returning to Ravel survives the reunion.

## Chapter 40: Aisle Nine

One year after Nell’s return, Bash comes to Philadelphia.

They enter the supermarket together.

The cart wheel rattles. Freezer cases hum beneath white light. Paper towels stand at the far end. A stock clerk holds a yellow knife.

Bash taps three, pause, two.

Nell answers once.

They argue about pepperoni and roots, put a frozen pizza in the cart, and walk through the automatic doors.
