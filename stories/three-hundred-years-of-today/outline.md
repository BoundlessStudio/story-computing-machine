# Three Hundred Years of Today

## Promised experience

“I don’t see the future, I live it” turns an apparently easy gift into knowledge someone has paid for in deaths. Each death takes the speaker back to that day’s morning, and they have experienced three hundred years of this same day. Someone who took the power for clairvoyance asks why they are hearing the truth now. The conversational “Groundhog Day” and the exclamation suggest room for an irreverent voice alongside the shock of a lifetime many times over. I want the reader to experience the awful familiarity of a day the rest of the world is meeting for the first time, then see the difference between remembering a possible future and having to act in one nobody has lived. The relationship, cause of the trap, setting and possibility of escape are open.

## Takes considered

- **A flight through Nine Tines.** A celebrated clairvoyant and the captain of a passenger glider have to bet thirty-eight lives on an approach she has pieced together by dying. The conversation at the second flight’s controls turns knowledge gathered in hundreds of years of solitude into a live decision for two people. Chosen because the difference between a forecast and a memory changes how they fly, and the action can take us physically beyond the limits of the speaker’s knowledge.
- **An inquiry at Flintmarket.** An investigator returns a woman’s testimony to her in the past tense. The woman is the only witness to the deaths she has kept from happening and wants someone else to know what it took. The strongest scene would be the investigator slowly realizing why they have such different memories of the same person. The retrospective form makes the trap feel further away.
- **A last show at Otterlight.** An illusionist has kept returning to the morning of the one performance their partner can never walk away from alive. At the center is a practiced stage dialogue that breaks down when the partner learns what the illusionist has had to do to give them those extra lives. The emotional reversal is strong and the outcome rests more heavily on an intimate relationship the prompt leaves open.

## Provisional direction

Kitt Sayer, who makes a living selling her reputation as a clairvoyant, is a publicity guest aboard the *Mayfly* on its crossing of Nine Tines. A storm will take the glider down. Each time she dies she wakes aboard at daybreak, already beyond the last landing field. Only her memory returns. She has used it to learn to fly and to feel out the storm around these cliffs. Turning back down the ravine, taking the regular saddle and trying for the low breaks in the ridge have all killed her. The land below is broken stone. The earliest branches include calls for rescue, warnings to turn while there is altitude and flights she can scarcely bear to remember. No help can reach the glider in time.

On the latest failed passage, a strip of torn wingcloth flies upward beside a particular rock face while the glider is falling. Kitt has found the edge of an updraft with enough strength to climb clear, and seen that the angle from which they reached it carried them straight into a projecting shelf. This time she proposes an early descent onto a different line. She can fly it to the walls of the cleft. From there the next turn will depend on where the wing is in relation to the shelf, visible from the seat beside hers. Captain Bet Malk has let Kitt prescribe maneuvers before on the strength of a supposed vision of safe landing. Kitt needs her to know exactly which part she has lived, and to call the turn from what she actually sees even if it contradicts Kitt’s instinct. Kitt will be the one flying into the cleft and making the turn. Nobody has yet seen this glider emerge. It will cross the ridge and land on the far side, giving Kitt a first morning she has never lived.

## Craft plan

- **Teller.** Try Kitt in first-person present, generous with observations about her fellow passengers and cagey about how long she has known them. Try close third from Bet as an immediate, tactile contrast, with Kitt’s overfamiliarity alarming her before the first reset explains it. Kitt’s voice has a stage performer’s pleasure in noticing things an audience will miss. The harder it gets to promise a clean finish, the more her account will have space for bewilderment and the love she feels for living people who can hardly know her.
- **Kitt.** A woman accustomed to managing what the audience thinks it has seen. She was a publicity guest yesterday. From hundreds of mornings at the controls with Bet she can now fly the glider. She wants to get everyone off it and has trouble admitting how badly she wants somebody else to remember a flight with her. She knows the approach and has been treating Bet’s judgment as another part of the flight she can rehearse. She talks fast when she wants permission and names odd particulars when she has a difficult truth to tell. Her spoken comparison to the movie belongs to a performer reaching for the handiest way to make the impossible intelligible.
- **Bet.** She can tell by the seat of her trousers what the glider is doing. She wants to bring her people through in one piece and resents having been hired to make a commercial stunt look dangerous. At first she takes Kitt for a charming amateur whose visions show what will happen. She can talk at length about what went wrong on a flight. In flight she speaks in the order she needs things done and uses nearly the same tone with a frightened child as with an able second pilot. Her irritation is practical. If Kitt wants her to risk the wing against the stone, Bet wants to know what Kitt has actually seen happen to it.
- **Conversation owed.** Kitt tells Bet that she wakes here whenever she dies, why three hundred years of attempts have led to this particular flight, and what she needs Bet to do with the part of the future she hasn’t lived. Bet has a right to ask what Kitt has told her in previous lives and in what ways it was false. Kitt gets to tell a portion of her lived history in order, including the moment she mistook a version of Bet for the one in front of her. The account changes Bet’s work in the cockpit and the way they speak as things begin to go wrong.
- **Place.** The *Mayfly*, a wood-bodied wave-riding passenger glider crossing the jagged Nine Tines in late autumn, from dawn through the storm that afternoon. The Old Sedge night train would let its driver stop. The Crane Steps funicular could strand people but would reduce their useful choices to a single line. Here changing course encounters a different slope and different air. It takes two sight lines to fly beside the rock. Before the trouble there are paper breakfast trays, a manifest, and jokes about luggage. Wet wool and orange peel warm together in the cabin. A repair in the left cockpit window leaves a thin whine. The right window is clear down to the wing root, a useful oddity in this old glider. A gust raises the noise of the wing before its shadow twists over the seats. Weather leaves grit between Kitt’s teeth. Outside, upward-flying debris will show the hidden shape of the air. The first sight of the far face of the ridge makes the landscape she knows by heart strange again. Undated invented magic-active modernity with original characters and no asserted links to existing people, places or systems. No supplied originals.

## Scene briefs

1. **Late in one doomed flight, cockpit.** Kitt is already flying the approach she has taken Bet into, wanting the captain to trust the last turn. Bet wants the reassurance she’s been given that Kitt has seen a landing. Before the consequential maneuvers, Kitt catches the pencil shaken loose from behind Bet’s ear without turning her head. She senses lift for the first time on this route and sees the rising strip of cloth, then the rock they will hit. The cut from the impact to the same pencil being passed down the aisle at breakfast makes the rule an event. About 15 percent.
2. **The same dawn through the turn toward the ridge, cabin and cockpit. Long scene.** Kitt knows what she can do with the sign from the torn wingcloth. Bet knows how the ship will react in ways even Kitt’s long practice doesn’t encompass and wants a justification she can use. Kitt wants Bet’s eyes and her independent judgment, which she has spent prior runs trying to replace with a script. Here is the supplied exchange and the longer conversation it opens. Kitt has spent whole years sitting with people she couldn’t save, learning things they’d been holding in for decades. Let her tell one such story and mistake for shared ground something Bet once told her on a very different flight. Bet can still cut Kitt off or throw her out of the seat, and the passengers can hear more of the conversation than Kitt intends. Let wind and the loss of altitude interrupt after Bet has had the chance to react, including to how many times Kitt has watched her die. Turn when Bet accepts the risk and clears Kitt to fly the early descent. About 55 percent.
3. **Into the cleft, then on the far side.** The only dependable part of the passage is the entry Kitt designed from the earlier deaths. The air goes rough at the rock face and the shadow of the wing vanishes. Bet calls what Kitt cannot yet see. The call comes at a moment Kitt has learned in her body to dread from a similar fatal turn. Stay with her through working out what she has heard, choosing to act on it and feeling the ship rise. A short passage takes them through landing and the following dawn. About 30 percent.

Compress years of alternative flights into Kitt’s account and the way she moves and talks. Leave out the power’s origin and the specifics of any death that contributes neither to her relationship with Bet nor to this flight. Close on the morning after the landing with Kitt looking up from the ground at the far face of Nine Tines in first light. In the opening she knows where the shadow will be even with her eyes shut. Here the rock she has been expecting to kill her for three hundred years goes gold above a completely different shadow, and she stays to watch what it will do.
