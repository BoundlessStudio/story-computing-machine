# The Noon Hare

## Promised experience

“Some werewolves eventually evolved into weredogs” asks for a descendant kind with a recognizable dog form and a history that reaches back to werewolves. “Not limited to the full moon” gives those descendants times and choices their ancestors lacked. The pleasure I infer is seeing that freedom change what someone can do, with the familiar surprise of a wolf lineage producing a dog. A chase could carry that surprise from the first shift to the finish. The request leaves tone, scale, relationships, and the nature of the ending open.

## Takes considered

- **The Noon Hare.** A born weredog challenges her werewolf aunt to the family’s full-moon chase at midday. The race comes to a head at a sheep wall where the niece has one more change available than her aunt has reckoned with.
- **The Long Day.** Between full moons, a floe breaks away from a whaling harbor. A weredog from the families that once kept company with the crews alternates between a dog the stranded sailors will follow in the fog and a person who can lead boats back through it. The central scene is the two parties hearing the same rescuer from opposite sides of the fog.
- **The Inn Dog.** A cook thinks the same dog has been painted beside different innkeepers over a century. At noon the cook’s friend comes through the service door wearing the dog’s muddy footprints and a human face, and must say what she has been doing under both roofs. The central scene makes generations of resemblance apparent through a single friendship.

The chase gives both branches something they want to do together. The niece’s timing and the bodies she chooses determine who catches whom and give her a way to ask for a game the family has never played.

## Provisional direction

Efa inherited her dog form from her mother. Over generations, the werewolf families that settled among the shepherds began giving birth to people whose animal shapes were dogs and who could change at any hour. Efa’s father’s sister Avice still gets her wolf shape at the full moon. Their family has long sent a human runner off with a stuffed wool hare for its wolves to catch. Avice has a formidable record as a wolf and wants Efa to carry the hare at the next full moon. Efa wants to catch it today, a week after the full moon. Avice agrees to carry it to the finish stone herself and gives Efa her choice of feet. Each imagines a different use of the offer.

Efa tracks Avice in her low-slung dog form across two sheep folds, then uses human hands and feet at the stile where Avice expects to draw clear. The catcher gets to keep the hare until the next moon. Efa wants her aunt to run twice in one day, this time for the pleasure of shaking someone off her heels. She takes the hare in her teeth and goes as quarry, with Avice in pursuit on two feet. The old race stays visible through both reversals.

## Craft plan

**Teller.** Try close third past in Efa’s impatient, affectionate register, letting her attention move between human calculations and the sheer pull of scent. Trial against Efa telling it in first person years later, with the wry pride of somebody insisting on exactly how she won.

**Cast and speech.** Efa wants a clean chance to catch Avice and privately wants her to be as hard to catch as she is on a moonlit run. She has the nose and the confidence to change twice. She mistakes Avice’s wish to keep the customary race for a judgment on her dog body. Efa speaks in concrete bets and offers, underprices effort in front of Avice, and finds it much easier to ask for a race than for company. Avice wants a runner for the full moon and a challenge worth putting her own legs into today. She knows every course on the common and takes Efa’s choice of feet as a choice made once. She speaks in stories with exact, sometimes unfair details and uses a long way round to own up to having enjoyed herself. Efa’s dog body has short black legs and one turned ear. She can follow a scent at speed and can only call with a dog’s voice. Both characters are original.

**Conversation owed.** Efa asks how Avice will tell the family who caught her at noon. She wants her aunt to count this as a chase and to have run hard enough for the catch to mean something. Avice wants Efa to know how much of a fight she gave her. She tells how Efa’s mother once slipped in dog form through the same low hole. Avice followed in wolf form and came away with her teeth full of fleece, then made Efa’s mother wait for the next full moon to race again. Efa’s mother is alive and working on the far side of the common that day.

**Place.** Crowsteps Common, a cliffside pasture divided by high dry-stone sheep folds in late May. Bright, dry wind around noon. Grazing has moved to the far side of the common. The two run unwatched well back from the cliff edge. A drove lane bends around the wall between the lower and upper folds to a gate. The low sheep creep through that wall has fleece snagged on its lintel and sheep grease in its stones. The wind snaps the hare’s long ears and carries scent downhill with the salt and gorse. The upper fold slopes to another wall, with a high step-stile and a flat finish slate just beyond it. Avice used the outcrop beside the stile to leap the wall as a wolf last week. Loose grit shifts on the dry stone treads. Marram Strand would give both the same open straightaway. Gannick Wood would give the nose the whole advantage. Crowsteps gives the low dog speed through one opening and a person leverage at the next.

## Scenes and ending

1. **Drove gate at the lower fold, noon (20%).** Avice brings the hare out to offer Efa a place as carrier at the next full moon. Efa makes her counteroffer aloud. Avice knows a route that makes four feet a disadvantage at the end. Efa knows she can change feet whenever she likes. Avice takes off in her boots with the hare tucked into her belt. The first snap of its ears sets Efa going.
2. **Two folds and the finish slate, minutes later (65%, the long scene).** Avice runs around the dividing wall by the drove gate. Her scent blows down across the wall. Efa picks it up and races under the low lintel into the upper fold with her ears laid flat. Her mother used to clear the same fleece at a bound. Efa’s shortcut closes the head start. Avice knows the line through the gorse from her wolf runs and recovers enough ground to reach the uphill wall first. She plants a foot on her usual takeoff stone. Efa has seen her vault the wall from there in wolf shape. In boots Avice turns straight up the step-stile beside it. The treads take long legs and hands. Efa changes at their foot, climbs in time to grab the hare from Avice’s belt from the step below her, and has it in her hand when Avice’s boots land on the far-side slate. Stay long enough at the finish for Efa to ask what Avice will tell the family and for Avice to tell the story of the fleece, then let Efa feel the hare’s wool between her fingers and make her own offer.
3. **Finish slate and the upper fold, still noon (15%).** Efa asks Avice to try catching her and gives her time to get back over the stile. Efa chooses the dog again, this time carrying the hare. Avice dives after her down the slope. End with the dog swerving close enough to give her a second chance, both of them laughing or making the sound they have. The scent on the hare’s ears, the first offer of the carrier’s role, and Efa’s wish for the quarry to put up a fight all arrive at the same place.

Allow the older branches a few sentences on the move. Leave the next full moon offstage.
