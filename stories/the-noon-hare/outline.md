# The Noon Hare

## Promised experience

“Some werewolves eventually evolved into weredogs” asks for a descendant kind with a recognizable dog form and a history that reaches back to werewolves. “Not limited to the full moon” gives those descendants times and choices their ancestors lacked. The pleasure I infer is seeing that freedom change what someone can do, with the familiar surprise of a wolf lineage producing a dog. A chase could carry that surprise from the first shift to the finish. The request leaves tone, scale, relationships, and the nature of the ending open.

## Takes considered

- **The Noon Hare.** A born weredog challenges her werewolf aunt to the family’s full-moon chase at midday. The race comes to a head at a sheep wall where the niece has one more change available than her aunt has reckoned with.
- **The Long Day.** Between full moons, a floe breaks away from a whaling harbor. A weredog from the families that once kept company with the crews alternates between a dog the stranded sailors will follow in the fog and a person who can lead boats back through it. The central scene is the two parties hearing the same rescuer from opposite sides of the fog.
- **The Inn Dog.** A cook thinks the same dog has been painted beside different innkeepers over a century. At noon the cook’s friend comes through the service door wearing the dog’s muddy footprints and a human face, and must say what she has been doing under both roofs. The central scene makes generations of resemblance apparent through a single friendship.

The chase gives both branches something they want to do together. The niece’s timing and the bodies she chooses determine who catches whom and give her a way to ask for a game the family has never played.

## Provisional direction

Efa inherited her dog form from her mother. Over generations, the werewolf families that settled among the shepherds began giving birth to people whose animal shapes were dogs and who could change at any hour. Efa’s father’s sister Avice still gets her wolf shape at the full moon. Their family has long sent a human runner off with a stuffed wool hare for its wolves to catch. Avice has a formidable record as a wolf and wants Efa to carry the hare at the next full moon. Efa wants to catch it today. Avice agrees to carry it to the finish stone herself and gives Efa her choice of feet. Each imagines a different use of the offer.

Efa tracks Avice in dog form across two sheep folds, then uses human hands and feet to climb the stile where Avice expects to draw clear. In the old chase the catcher gets to keep the hare until the next moon. Efa wants her aunt to run twice in one day, this time for the pleasure of shaking someone off her heels. She takes the hare in her teeth and goes as quarry, with Avice in pursuit on two feet. The old race stays visible through both reversals.

## Craft plan

**Teller.** Try close third past in Efa’s impatient, affectionate register, letting her attention move between human calculations and the sheer pull of scent. Trial against Efa telling it in first person years later, with the wry pride of somebody insisting on exactly how she won.

**Cast and speech.** Efa wants a clean chance to catch Avice and privately wants her to be as hard to catch as she is on a moonlit run. She has the nose and the confidence to change twice. She mistakes Avice’s wish to keep the customary race for a judgment on her dog body. Efa speaks in concrete bets and offers, underprices effort in front of Avice, and finds it much easier to ask for a race than for company. Avice wants a runner for the full moon and a challenge worth putting her own legs into today. She knows every course on the common and takes Efa’s choice of feet as a choice made once. She speaks in stories with exact, sometimes unfair details and uses a long way round to own up to having enjoyed herself. Efa’s dog body has short black legs and one turned ear. She can follow a scent at speed and can only call with a dog’s voice. Both characters are original.

**Conversation owed.** At the finish Efa needs to ask what Avice counted as a real chase when she took the hare. Avice can answer in the terms of the run they just made and tell Efa why she kept her mother waiting for a rematch years ago. Efa wants an opponent good enough to have an objection. Avice wants Efa to know how much of a fight she gave her.

**Place.** Crowsteps Common, a cliffside pasture divided by high dry-stone sheep folds, in bright wind around noon in late May. Grazing has moved to the upper common, leaving the folds open for the race. A drove lane joins the gates on the shoreward side. A low sheep creep connects two folds and a taller step-stile crosses their upper wall. Sheep grease still hangs in the wall fleeces. Grit shifts on the stone treads. The wind snaps the hare’s long ears and carries the smell of salt and gorse across the wrong wall. From the stile a runner can see where a scent has been hiding from her. Marram Strand would give both the same open straightaway. Gannick Wood would give the nose the whole advantage. Crowsteps lets a dog go first through one opening and a person get through the next fastest.

## Scenes and ending

1. **Upper gate, noon (25%).** Avice brings the hare out to trade Efa a place as carrier at the next full moon. Efa has already decided to ask for the other end of the chase and says so plainly. Avice knows a route that makes four feet a disadvantage at the end. Efa knows she can change feet whenever she likes. Avice takes off in her boots with the hare tucked into her belt. The first snap of its long ears sets Efa going.
2. **Two folds and the finish stone, minutes later (60%, the long scene).** The hare’s scent blows across the wall. Efa puts her nose down and gets ahead through the sheep creep. Avice takes the high step-stile, where wet grit won’t give Efa’s claws purchase. Efa claims both kinds of footing, unfolds into human shape at the stone, and lifts the hare from Avice’s belt before Avice can get clear. At the finish Avice holds out a hand for the customary trophy ceremony. Stay for what each wants to know about how the other ran. Avice tells her own version of a race with Efa’s mother and offers Efa her due. Efa feels the hare’s wool with human fingers and makes her own offer.
3. **Finish stone and the lower fold, still noon (15%).** Efa asks Avice to try catching her and gives her time to put her boots back under her. Efa chooses the dog again, this time carrying the hare. Avice dives after her down the slope. End with the dog swerving close enough to give her a second chance, both of them laughing or making the sound they have. The scent on the ears, the first offer of the carrier’s role, and Efa’s wish for the quarry to put up a fight all arrive at the same place.

Allow family history a few sentences on the move. Leave the next full moon offstage.
