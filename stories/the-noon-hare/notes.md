# Editorial notes

## Request readings · 2026-11-20

**Codex promise keeper.** The request explicitly says some werewolves eventually evolved into weredogs that can transform outside the full moon. “Some” suggests a branch, with the mechanism and the survival of moon-bound werewolves open. The reader expected the dog form and the freedom of timing to work together. “Becoming a dog at a moment the old full-moon rule would have ruled out should open a possibility or cause a consequence that matters.” The premise has a playful turn, with tone, scale, and central relationship open.

**Claude outline reader.** “If weredogs are just ‘werewolves with a better schedule,’ the premise is wasted.” Claude read “eventually evolved” as time across generations and drew an analogy to domestication and living alongside humans. The analogy, a surviving werewolf foil, voluntary shifts, and a light tone were inferences. The prompt leaves the mechanism, relationship, and size open. The reader expected a turn where the dog's traits and the freedom to change matter.

## Outline discussion · 2026-11-20

**Codex promise keeper, first reading.** Both racers “read the same ground and make different bets.” Efa starts the contest, chooses the decisive shifts, and “spends her advantage on another chase.” Scent and four feet alone could belong to a wolf. The reader wanted the sheep creep to confer an advantage specific to the dog body Efa inherited and the finish conversation to recall its use by her mother.

**Claude outline reader, first reading.** The ending of the chase is “keep-away with someone you love.” Off-moon shifts decide both victory and the return chase. Claude wanted the absence of Avice's wolf body to be felt during the run, as well as simpler blocking at the stile and a direct question for Avice at the finish.
