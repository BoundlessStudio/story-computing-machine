# Opus review: The Wolf in the Mirror

Independent audit for a later editing pass. It is not a `review.md` verdict,
does not change canon state, and does not authorize edits by itself.

## Verdict

- Dialogue: REVISE — the wolf-state exchanges work; the human speech before moonrise and after dawn falls into shared clause-speak, thesis lines and a quip relay.
- Prose: PASS — clear and concrete, with only minor sweepable tics (negated alternatives, epigram closers, one-line paragraph runs).
- Continuity and prompt: REVISE — the narrator plans for the mirror reaction in advance, so half of "what you were not prepared for" never lands.
- Edit scope: targeted-dialogue
- Existing review.md said: Verdict PASS, "Dialogue: PASS" with "no final-exchange over-explanation"; disagree on dialogue and on the ending.

The story's best dialogue is nonverbal. Hand shapes, two tones, colored lamps, barking and the wolf choosing blue carry the decisive mirror exchange with real uncertainty and restraint, and the narrator's out-loud log-speech during the night is a good pressure habit. The spoken human dialogue is where it goes wrong. Before moonrise the partner takes on the narrator's clipped, rule-setting register ("That's the agreement", "I decided now. It expires."), corrects word choice, and twice states the story's personhood and autonomy point aloud. So the two of them sound like one careful person. After dawn the exhausted partner trades evenly weighted comebacks, and the final exchange settles as a traded concession capped with a quip and a basin callback the story has already made. The edit should keep the narrator's procedural voice, since that is their fear and it is funny. It should roughen the partner: let nerves and embarrassment show before moonrise instead of contract terms, and after dawn keep their speech short, tired and unpolished.

## Edit guardrails

- Prompt requirements: a full moonrise-to-dawn watch; tail-chasing for about two hours (9:18 to 11:16, "one hour and fifty-eight minutes", with the partner's "Two hours." nod to the prompt); barking at their own reflection after walking in front of the mirror; a narrator who is unprepared for both; a complete resolution; broadly accessible tone; 2,500–4,000 words (currently about 3,450, so keep additions small); active craft profile prospective-2026-08-21. The rewrite request targets dialogue.
- First-person past narrator. The narrator and partner stay unnamed, and the partner uses they/them. Add no names. Both are adults, and the transformation stays non-graphic (the narrator turns away).
- Story-local mechanics to hold constant. Hand shapes: space, orientation, and the approach request, which means cross into blue and stay (L63). Tones: one low note means orient; two descending notes mean break attention and find blue (L73). The blue lamp sits near the water and the amber lamp above the vestibule. The mirror cover can only be drawn when the strip beside the glass is clear. The camera deletes after dawn unless the partner chooses otherwise. Every threshold in L79 pays off later, so keep them all.
- The wolf never understands words, and the narrator never claims to read its mind (L149, L193, L281). Do not add recognition or understanding lines to the wolf scenes.
- Keep the timestamps arithmetically consistent: 9:18, 9:34, 9:47, 10:08 (fifty minutes in), 10:29–10:42, 10:56, 11:16 (1:58), 12:07.
- No LOCKED or CANON universe entry is touched. The basin is drinking water, not infrastructure; do not add water-works texture.
- What already works and must not be flattened: the nonverbal wolf exchanges; the narrator's procedural speech as their fear response; the dawn release protocol; the camera-deletion beat.

## Dialogue findings

### D1 · Major · Camera terms traded in identical clause-speak

- Where: L99–L109, the camera exchange before moonrise (same register at L45)
- Text:
> "No clips to the specialist. No anonymous training reel. No telling your sister a cleaned-up version that becomes funny at dinner."
> "People can make an anecdote without knowing what it means."
> "Live feed only. It expires. You decide afterward."
> "I decided now. It expires. If I change my mind when I can ask, then I ask."
- Problem: Identical Voices and a negotiation routine. This is the partner's real fear before the night, becoming a funny story at someone's dinner table, but it plays out as two clerks settling clauses. Both speakers use the same run of short declaratives. The partner repeats the narrator's "It expires." word for word, corrects "You decide afterward" to "I decided now", and closes the sister point with an aphorism. Cover the tags and L105 and L107 could belong to either speaker. The partner's nerves show only in the narration (L111 "They exhaled", L115 shoulders rising) and never in their speech. L45 ("we use amber. I move to blue. You pull it. That's the agreement.") is the same contract register.
- Fix: Keep the content: no clips, no sister anecdote, live feed only, and the partner decides. Keep the narrator's procedural "Live feed only" line as their voice. Rework the partner's side so it sounds embarrassed and specific instead of contractual. Cut the aphorism at L103 and the echoed "It expires." at L107, and let the partner's decision come out as a plain statement with some nerves in it. Illustrative only: "Your sister does voices for everyone. I'm not getting a voice." Loosen L45 the same way, into one plain instruction instead of a four-clause rule.

### D2 · Major · Partner states the story's thesis aloud

- Where: L81–L87 (thresholds rehearsal) and L111 (watching agreement), before moonrise
- Text:
> "I give you the hand shape for space."
> "Because I am myself the whole time."
> You don't hide in the hall because you think privacy means abandonment.
- Problem: The Philosopher, set up by a correction. The partner corrects "you give me space" to the more literal "the hand shape for space" mainly so they can deliver the personhood thesis. Then the "because" attaches to nothing the listener can parse. Being yourself the whole time is not a reason for the space-sign rule, so the reader has to reconstruct the intended point: looking like yourself is not the test. L111 turns the watching agreement into an abstract formula ("privacy means abandonment"). The outline says neither partner owns the moral summary, and the story already dramatizes both points: the dawn release (L321–L329) and the narrator watching without intruding all night (L237).
- Fix: Cut "Because I am myself the whole time." and the second "Yes.", and let the studied look at L89 carry the check. Either drop the correction at L81 or keep it without the thesis after it. At L111, keep "And you watch." (it pays off at L237) and replace the abstract clause with a concrete, personal request. Illustrative only: "And you watch. Don't go sit in the hall to be polite." Keep L27's complaint as the one direct statement of the grievance, since L205 depends on it.

### D3 · Major · Dawn report turns into a quip relay

- Where: L337–L359, the vestibule after dawn
- Text:
> "I am giving them in order."
> "Approximately."
> "Please don't rescue my dignity with arithmetic."
> "You told me to be accurate."
> "I have changed my mind about accuracy. What happened to the mirror?"
- Problem: Tennis Match with correction ping-pong and uniform fluency under pressure. The partner's voice is "scraped thin with exhaustion" (L323), and their first lines are rightly bare ("Open the gate." "Water," "Tell me,"). From L339 on, though, the two trade about ten evenly weighted one-liners, each correcting the one before, and the partner lands two polished comebacks in a row. The setup "You told me to be accurate." also misattributes. At L91 the narrator volunteered "I report accurately." and the partner only added "Privately." So the line exists to set up "I have changed my mind about accuracy." (Punchline Patch).
- Fix: Keep "You're arranging the facts." / "You're delaying.", which catches a real evasion (the tail came before the water). Keep "You chased your tail." / "For one hour and fifty-eight minutes," / "Two hours." as the prompt nod. After "Two hours." cut "Approximately." and keep at most one comeback. "Please don't rescue my dignity with arithmetic." is the better one if one stays. Cut "You told me to be accurate." or make it match L91. Let the partner reach the mirror question tired and abrupt. Contract "I am giving them in order" to fit the narrator's spoken voice elsewhere ("I'm staying out", "you'll hit it").

### D4 · Major · Final exchange closes on terms, a quip and a doubled emblem

- Where: L389–L403, the ending
- Text:
> Beyond the inner gate, the basin waited six inches left of where I had drawn it, beside a fan of wet pawprints.
> "Next month," I said, "we could revise the mirror condition."
> "Without a committee."
> "And leave the water where I put it."
- Problem: A negotiation routine, an epigram, and the Philosopher by callback, all in the final meaningful exchange. The real movement here is good. The partner refused the cover at L37 and now says "Cover it before moonrise." But the scene frames it as a clause revision ("revise the mirror condition"), answers it with a last-word quip, and then restates the opening basin grievance twice within fifteen lines: once as an image (L389) and again as a spoken demand plus the narrator's compliance (L401–L403). The ending explains a point the pawprint image has already made.
- Fix: Keep "Cover it before moonrise." as the partner's concession, and keep the knee lean, the head on the shoulder and the bottle. Let the narrator raise next month in ordinary words, not as a "condition". Cut "Without amber?" / "Without a committee." or replace them with a plain question and a tired plain answer. Land the basin once. Preferred: keep the L389 pawprint image, end on the bottle beat, and cut the spoken callback and "I left it there." Acceptable alternative: keep the last two lines and cut the basin sentence from L389. Do not add a new closing aphorism.

### D5 · Minor · "Eleven months out of the year" misstates a monthly curse

- Where: L41, the mirror-cover debate
- Text:
> It's my room eleven months out of the year.
- Problem: Ground slip in a literal claim. The partner changes at every full moon (L391 "Next month"), so they lose the room one night a month, not one month a year. In a story that counts minutes, the idiom reads as literal and briefly suggests an annual curse.
- Fix: Make the count true. Illustrative only: "It's my room every night but one."

### D6 · Minor · Recalled quotation does not match the original line

- Where: L205, recalling L23–L27
- Text:
> Don't make everything I choose sound like a defect you have to compensate for.
> "Making everything I choose sound like a defect you have to compensate for.
- Problem: At L205 the narrator gives, in italics, what the partner "had said", but the partner actually said "Don't start doing that." and then "Making everything I choose sound like...". It's a small slip, but in a story about reporting accurately a misquote is noticeable.
- Fix: Quote the actual words, or drop the italics and present it as the narrator's paraphrase. If an edit changes L27, update L205 to match.

### D7 · Minor · Pre-moonrise banter runs as matched comebacks

- Where: L13–L21 (the basin) and L49–L51 (the checkmark)
- Text:
> "If you turn on the west half of the room," I said
> "I have encountered water before."
> "I could also forget what yes referred to at three in the morning."
- Problem: A Tennis Match in miniature: four equal one-liners at L15–L21, then a narrator last word at L51 that the partner simply leaves behind. The wit suits this prompt, but the partner's mock-formal "Then I will discover water" / "I have encountered" matches the narrator's register exactly. So from the first page the reader learns that the two speak alike. "Turn on" can also read as "attack" or "transform", which blurs the story's first spoken line.
- Fix: Keep the joke and "Not with four feet.", which triggers L23–L27. Replace "turn on" with a verb that can only mean wheeling or spinning. Give the partner one looser, contracted line, or let the foot nudge answer one beat without words. L51 is optional; if it stays, let the partner react to it rather than leaving it as the button on the exchange.

### D8 · Minor · Sign gloss muddles the decisive request

- Where: L289–L293, the mirror standoff
- Text:
> I turned on amber and lowered my open hand: a request to approach the barrier, and with it permission for me to move the cover.
- Problem: Ground and staging at the key nonverbal exchange. L63 defines the open hand as a request to cross into the blue pool. L291 puts blue "across the room", and L293 and L299 judge compliance by blue. "Approach the barrier" names a different target, and "with it permission for me" credits the permission to the sign instead of the wolf's choice. From L293 on the reader can follow the action, but for one sentence the gloss contradicts it.
- Fix: Gloss the sign as the request to go to blue, as L63 defines it. Make clear that the wolf moving there, not the sign itself, is what lets the narrator pull the cover.

### D9 · Minor · Narration explains the narrator's procedural speech

- Where: L229, L263, L297, the night watch
- Text:
> Procedure crowded my mouth.
> I sounded procedural because I was afraid.
> My mouth wanted to add the rule, the threshold, the correct response.
- Problem: The log-speech at L139, L227 and L261 already shows that the narrator talks procedure when scared. The narration then says so three times, and L263 explains the line directly before it.
- Fix: Keep one. L297 works best because it comes with an action: biting the cheek and letting the sign stand alone. Cut the first sentence of L263, and trim L229 to the action and its result.

## Prose findings

### P1 · Minor · Denial-then-assertion constructions recur

- Where: L63, L219, L363 (also L149, L281)
- Text:
> It was a request, not a command.
> The look gave me no answer. It was simply attention.
> not grateful, not wounded. Checking.
- Problem: The "not X, Y" / "It was not X. It was Y." reversal appears five times. L149 and L281 serve the story's refusal to over-read the wolf and earn their place; the other three are the tic.
- Fix: Keep L149 and L281. Rewrite L63, L219 and L363 to state directly what the narrator sees, without the negated alternative.

### P2 · Minor · Epigram paragraph closers

- Where: L141, L213, L229, L235
- Text:
> It had only made me louder.
> Nothing had crossed a threshold except my patience.
> I repeated that last phrase until it began to sound like an excuse.
- Problem: Neat, wry closing sentences end several night-section paragraphs and do the reader's inferring for them. Together they give the narration the same last-word rhythm as the dialogue.
- Fix: Keep one or two at most (L185's laugh line is character-true and is not on this list). End the others on the action.

### P3 · Minor · One-line paragraphs become the default pulse in the mirror sequence

- Where: L241–L257, L269–L275, L283–L309; triplet negations at L249 and L277
- Text:
> My partner froze.
> One impact.
> So I did nothing.
- Problem: Nearly every beat of the mirror standoff gets its own paragraph, alongside stacked negations ("No scent came from it. No footfall." and "No more tones. No chain of instructions..."). Some isolation earns tension, but this much flattens it.
- Fix: Merge each action with its immediate consequence into short paragraphs. Keep isolation for two or three turns, such as the first bark and "They did not move to blue."

## Continuity and prompt findings

### C1 · Major · The mirror reaction is planned for, not unprepared-for

- Where: L33–L45 (setup) and L243–L263 (the mirror)
- Text:
> A dark cover waited in a ceiling track above it.
> "It removes one variable."
> "Still no. If I need it covered, we use amber. I move to blue. You pull it. That's the agreement."
- Problem: The prompt's second surprise is the partner barking at themselves in the mirror. The story has the narrator foresee exactly that: a cover on a track, an unnamed "variable" the partner jokes about, and a pre-agreed amber/blue procedure for covering the glass. When the wolf meets its reflection (L245–L253), the narrator runs the plan without any sign of being caught off guard. Only the tail-chase half of "what you were not prepared for" lands (L165).
- Fix: Keep the cover, the track and the amber/blue agreement, because the resolution at L289–L305 needs them. Give the cover a different, concrete reason before moonrise, such as glare from the lamps muddying the colored cues, or the glass as a strike hazard, so the narrator's "variable" is not a rival reflection. Then add one short beat around L245–L253 where the narrator registers that the plan covered a strike or glare, not a wolf picking a fight with itself. Do not reuse L165's wording. The partner's "because I might be ugly" joke and "That wasn't the variable." / "I know." can stay with the new reason.

### C2 · Minor · "Amber" names two different signals

- Where: L333 and L395 (lamp established at L45, L267, L289)
- Text:
> The live-feed light had shifted from green to blinking amber.
> "Without amber?"
- Problem: Amber is established as the mirror-cover lamp. At dawn the camera's expiry light is also amber, and a few lines later "Without amber?" refers back to the lamp. A reader may briefly connect the two.
- Fix: Give the camera light a different color or just call it "blinking". If D4 cuts "Without amber?", the collision shrinks but the camera color should still change.

### C3 · Minor · Vestibule and gate staging don't line up

- Where: L31, L115–L119, just before moonrise
- Text:
> an observation vestibule where the garden door had been. Two gates separated the inner room from the rest of the house.
> I went in, closed the outer gate, then the inner one.
- Problem: The vestibule replaces the garden door, yet the gates separate the room from the house. The narrator enters the vestibule from the inner room but closes the outer gate first. The moonrise alarm vibrates on "the table between us" inside a room built to be clear for a wolf. None of this breaks the plot, but on first read the picture of who is where is blurry right before the change.
- Fix: Settle which side the vestibule opens onto, describe the lock-in in the order the narrator actually moves, and put the table or alarm in the vestibule (or name what it sits on).

## Suggested edit order

1. C1 first. Choosing the cover's pre-moonrise reason changes the L35–L45 dialogue, and every later mirror beat depends on it.
2. D1, D2, D5, D7 together. These retune the partner's pre-moonrise voice in one pass so it sets the baseline for everything after.
3. D9 and D8: trim the narration's glosses in the night section and fix the sign gloss at the decisive request.
4. D3: bring the dawn report back to a tired, uneven exchange, keeping the evasion catch and "Two hours."
5. D4: the final exchange and the single basin landing. Do this after D3 so the ending's rhythm is judged against the revised report.
6. D6: re-sync the L205 memory with whatever L27 now says.
7. C2, C3, P1–P3: a mechanical sweep last.
