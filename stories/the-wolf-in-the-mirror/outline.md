# Outline

## Story

- Premise and central promise: The unnamed narrator has prepared their rented
  house, emergency kit, and nerves to keep their adult partner Teague safe
  through the first full-moon transformation they will witness together. The
  enormous wolf that arrives is strong enough to justify every precaution, but
  its governing urgency is not bloodlust: Teague spends two full hours chasing
  their own tail, then walks past an accidentally uncovered mirror and barks at
  the wolf barking back. Fulfill the prompt literally and make the comic
  surprise carry a complete private story: the narrator must keep a powerful,
  dizzy, overstimulated partner from accidental harm, discover that dignity does
  not require pretending the night was solemn, and tell Teague the ridiculous
  truth at dawn without reducing the curse or Teague to a pet joke.
- Intended reader experience: Begin with affectionate dread and the tactile
  satisfaction of serious preparation, then let each ominous sign tip into
  increasingly helpless comedy. Sustain the absurd two-hour duration through
  changing practical problems rather than repeating one gag. The mirror episode
  should be the funniest and briefly riskiest beat, followed by quiet relief:
  Teague remains a person whose altered impulses are real, the narrator's care
  remains necessary, and neither has to turn survival into tragedy to prove it
  mattered.
- Generating force:
  - Mode: Plot-led contained comic recurrence. The narrator has a contingency
    for every anticipated act of aggression; movement comes from those
    contingencies being repurposed one by one for play, dizziness, noise,
    hydration, and reflection-fixation while the wolf keeps circling. The
    transformation progresses toward dawn, but the narrator's understanding
    changes from controlling a predicted monster to attending to the particular
    creature Teague is tonight.
  - Focal attachment or contradiction: The narrator loves Teague and has made
    competence their answer to fear. They want to respect Teague's adult wishes
    and not sentimentalize a curse Teague dreads, yet their plan assumes respect
    means meeting the night with grave tactical discipline. The wolf's comic
    behavior is neither a false Teague nor the whole truth about them; care must
    accommodate both danger and absurdity.
  - Plot-led wants and useful limit, if applicable: The narrator immediately
    wants to get Teague through the night without injury, escape, or a broken
    promise, while also wanting Teague to wake without feeling watched as a
    spectacle. Their useful flaw is overcontrol: they mistake preparation for a
    script reality should follow, read every early movement as a precursor to
    violence, and suppress laughter until the effort makes them clumsy and less
    observant. Before changing, Teague wants the narrator close and wants the
    agreed safety barriers kept in place; in wolf form, speech and long planning
    are unavailable, while immediate canine impulses and recognition of the
    narrator coexist.
- Counterforce or complication:
  - Source: A very large transformed body can cause harm without hostility.
    Two hours of tight circling threaten claws, tail, furniture, water, and
    balance; attempts to interrupt the chase become invitations to turn faster.
    The narrator cannot ask Teague what hurts or rely on a human response to
    their name. The sheet covering the mirrored closet gradually works loose,
    creating a self-renewing barking contest just when both are exhausted.
  - Credible inside logic, if a person: There is no antagonist. Teague chose the
    precautions while human because previous solitary moons left bruises,
    scratches, and fragmented memory, but neither partner knew what produced
    them. The transformed Teague pursues immediate sensory interests, recoils
    from overwhelming artificial scent, and reacts to a reflection as another
    wolf; they can still seek the narrator's proximity and respond to the
    narrator's familiar laugh without becoming human-minded on command.
  - Pressure or change created: The narrator must decide which safeguards still
    protect Teague and which now create hazards. Blocking the circle risks a
    collision; leaving it open permits exhaustion. Covering the mirror requires
    entering the reflected wolf's apparent space without escalating the bark.
    At dawn, the practical problem becomes relational: tell the whole truth,
    including the comedy, without either concealing evidence from Teague or
    using their vulnerable night for ridicule.
- Narrative design:
  - POV, tense, distance, and limitation: Close first-person past through the
    unnamed narrator, with a retrospective horizon no later than breakfast.
    Their precise inventory language, catastrophic forecasts, and unwillingly
    comic comparisons create the voice. They can observe Teague's body and
    behavior but cannot enter their mind, translate every gesture, or declare
    how much human memory is present. Let Teague's agency before and after the
    transformation frame the period when speech is inaccessible.
  - Time shape and compression: One night from the last hour before moonrise to
    shortly after dawn. Render the final consent-and-safety check,
    transformation, first tail turn, failed interruption, mirror encounter, and
    morning disclosure in scene. Compress the middle of the literal two-hour
    chase through four accumulating changes at visible clock intervals: the
    cleared floor widening, emergency items finding absurd new uses, the
    narrator's fear giving way to concern about strain, and suppressed laughter
    becoming harder than vigilance. Do not leave the room for backstory scenes.
  - Information and reveal strategy: Establish the agreed barriers, release
    procedure, and Teague's limited memories before moonrise, but offer no
    expert lecture or universal curse account. Make the first transformation
    physically formidable enough that the reader shares the narrator's fear.
    Reveal the governing behavior within minutes and confirm the exact two-hour
    span through the narrator's incident log. Do not pretend the comic behavior
    proves the curse harmless: show weight, claws, heat, fatigue, and failed
    language. Withhold whether Teague will recognize the narrator until the
    narrator's involuntary laugh breaks the mirror loop; recognition is
    relational evidence for this night, not a diagnostic test of personhood.
  - Governing movement: Inventory; renew consent; seal the room; witness the
    painful change; brace for attack; notice the tail; watch one turn become a
    chase; deploy the wrong contingencies; clear a wider circle; protect paws
    and water without entering the loop; record increasingly absurd intervals;
    reach the exact two-hour mark; watch Teague stop and stagger; lose the mirror
    covering; hear the first bark and its reflected answer; try human commands;
    fail to suppress laughter; receive Teague's familiar attention; approach
    from outside the reflected line; cover the glass; settle beside the tired
    wolf; endure the reverse transformation; answer Teague's damage question
    honestly; let Teague decide what is funny and what next month needs.
- Speculative surplus: Teague's transformed sense of smell does not merely
  sharpen; artificial fragrances arrive as one inseparable, nauseating sensory
  mass. The partners therefore used unscented cleaner and sealed toiletries,
  but a forgotten citrus wipe inside the emergency kit triggers a spectacular
  sneeze and has to be bagged. This local effect neither reveals intent nor
  controls the curse, and it does not help solve the tail or mirror loops.
- Structural distinction: A private overnight comedy built as a contingency
  list losing its original meanings. The longest event is deliberately circular
  rather than escalating, while practical details change around it; the brief
  mirror sequence doubles the recurrence instead of widening the stakes. The
  decisive venue remains one padded spare room with a mirrored closet. There is
  no hunt, cure quest, public proof, converted witness, institutional rewrite,
  technical explanation, or polished symbol of acceptance.
- Decisive turn, deepening, or recognition: After two hours, Teague staggers in
  front of the newly exposed mirror, barks, and recoils when the other wolf does
  exactly the same. The narrator cycles through Teague's name, the stop word,
  and every practiced command, each of which only produces louder barking.
  Seeing their own armored reflection beside Teague's, the narrator finally
  loses the solemnity they have guarded all night and laughs. Teague turns from
  the glass toward that familiar sound, ears easing and tail striking the floor
  once. The response gives the narrator enough space to approach sideways and
  pull the sheet over the mirror. The recognition is modest: they cannot call
  Teague human-minded or harmless, but they can stop treating humor as a betrayal
  of care.
- Aftereffect or live uncertainty: Teague wakes sore, hoarse, thirsty, and
  embarrassed by not remembering the whole night; the room has claw marks and a
  dented water bowl, but no one was bitten and the mirror survived. The narrator
  reports the chase and barking rather than sanitizing the record. Teague laughs
  only after confirming the injuries and insists that next month still needs a
  barrier, a cleared turning radius, better hydration, and no exposed glass.
  One witnessed moon cannot establish that future changes will be gentle, what
  Teague remembers in wolf form, or whether other curses behave alike.
- Opening / ending relation: Open with the narrator assigning grave uses to
  every object in the emergency kit and rehearsing how not to panic if Teague
  attacks. End at breakfast when Teague asks what happened, listens through the
  safety facts, and gets stuck on the duration: “Two hours?” The narrator says
  yes. Teague asks whether they ever caught the tail; the narrator answers no,
  and Teague's relieved laughter ends the night without denying its bruises.

## Beats

1. In the hour before moonrise, the narrator finishes padding the spare room
   and inventories barriers, first aid, water, a bite-resistant coat, the agreed
   release procedure, and the incident log. Teague reviews the plan, confirms
   that restraint is a last resort, admits fear about the bruises left by prior
   solitary moons, and chooses to have the narrator stay.
2. The transformation is painful, hot, and physically imposing. The narrator
   follows the plan, keeps distance, and mistakes Teague's fixed attention on
   their hindquarters for the beginning of a predatory feint.
3. Teague catches sight of their tail and begins circling. The narrator's first
   interruption attempts—name, stop word, padded divider—make the chase faster
   or redirect it around new obstacles. A forgotten citrus wipe provokes a
   sneeze; the wolf resumes immediately.
4. Across the next two hours, the narrator shifts from defense to harm
   reduction: clears equipment from the orbit, slides water into pauses, pads a
   recurring skid point, protects Teague's tail from the wall, and records the
   exact absurdity while trying not to laugh. Teague remains powerful enough
   that none of this feels consequence-free.
5. At the two-hour mark, Teague stops, staggers, and crosses in front of the
   mirrored closet just as the loosened sheet falls. They bark at the reflected
   wolf; every bark returns, building a loop that commands and prepared tools do
   not break.
6. The narrator sees their own overarmored reflection, laughs despite themself,
   and draws Teague's attention away from the glass. Approaching obliquely, they
   replace the sheet, remove hard gear from Teague's reach, and sit nearby while
   the exhausted wolf settles against their boot.
7. Dawn reverses the change. The narrator handles water and soreness before
   giving a complete account. Teague checks that no one was hurt, absorbs the
   chase and mirror facts, helps identify practical revisions for the next full
   moon, then asks the one question the narrator's incident log cannot dignify:
   whether they caught the tail.

## Continuity

- Canon used: The story belongs to an unnamed modern-like era in which local
  magic may be real. The curse is enacted through Teague's living body and
  preserves their identity and moral standing through transformation. Its
  practical rules and costs remain specific to this curse.
- Boundaries and unknowns: Establish no universal werewolf biology, full-moon
  mechanism, curse origin, cure, magical consent rule, or taxonomy of
  transformed memory. The ordinary mirror neither uses magic nor creates a
  second being; Teague reacts to a reflection. Moon and glass imagery does not
  connect this story to established moon observations, bounded mirror incidents,
  other transformations, artifacts, eras, people, or places. Teague's response
  to the narrator and their next-morning account support continuity of this one
  person without making a universal test of personhood or harmlessness.
