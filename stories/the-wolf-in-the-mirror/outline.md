# Outline

## Story

- Premise and central promise: An unnamed adult narrator has helped their romantic partner prepare for a first full night as a werewolf. The barriers, signals, and consent plan work; the surprise is two hours of tail-chasing followed by a challenge to the mirror. Complete the night as dangerous care and intimate comedy without making vulnerability a spectacle.
- Focal pressure or attachment: The narrator promised to remain present and report honestly. Preparation manages foreseeable danger but encourages overreaction. Their partner wants protection without captivity or humiliation. Care must operate through restraint, accurate attention, and preauthorized choices.
- Counterforce or complication: The powerful, sensory-driven wolf cannot use human speech. Tail pursuit may be ridiculous or dangerously compulsive; the mirror barking may become an impact risk. Fatigue and ambiguous signals pressure the narrator to intervene too soon.
- POV, distance, and information limit: Close first-person past through the narrator, intimate with checklists and changing interpretations. They know the plan and their partner's human habits, not the wolf's thoughts or the curse's origin. Convey wolf-state meaning only through body, sound, distance, and response to cues.
- Governing movement and time shape: One night in the couple's reinforced home room: setup, non-graphic transformation, initial danger, a comic yet worrying two-hour loop, mirror confrontation, quiet watch, and human aftermath. Dilate the chase through practical interruptions rather than repeated jokes. Keep the turn private and behavioral.
- Speculative rule or ordinary-world constraint: A story-local curse forces transformation from moonrise until dawn. The same person persists, but the wolf cannot form human words and retains only partial learned associations. An inner room and observation vestibule contain risk. A safety camera deletes after dawn unless the transformed partner later consents to retention. The couple agree beforehand on hand shapes, two tones, colored lamps, intervention thresholds, and release. These aids indicate orientation or choice; they neither translate thoughts nor guarantee obedience.

## Voice

- Narrative texture: Practical first-person attention that begins in measured inventory, loosens into incredulous observation, and tightens whenever comedy threatens to become harm. Count elapsed time, contact points, and bodily changes without making the night a clinical log.
- Conversational texture: Before and after, mix ordinary household logistics, unfinished teasing, privacy negotiation, and sparse admissions. During wolf-state, let tones, lamps, pauses, barking, and bodily response carry incomplete exchanges.
- Rhetorical ownership: The narrator reasons through contingencies and thresholds; the partner frames choices through dignity, embarrassment, and sharp corrections to over-management. Neither owns the story's moral summary.
- Pressure behavior: The narrator becomes overly procedural and repeats cues; the partner is fluent before moonrise, nonverbal as a wolf, and terse from fatigue afterward. Misread signals require waiting and re-observation.
- Anti-default: Avoid a generic snarky couple, a stream of pet jokes, or polished human meaning smuggled into wolf behavior; tenderness should emerge from unglamorous restraint and specific shared preparation.

## Beats

1. Before moonrise, the couple complete their joint room check. Water placement and door timing create mundane friction. A disagreement about recording reveals the partner's fear of becoming an anecdote; they actively choose a live safety feed that will not persist by default.
2. The narrator enters the vestibule before the non-graphic transformation. The wolf tests the gate with enough force to justify it. One agreed tone turns its attention, and posture suggests recognition without human comprehension. The narrator follows the plan instead of entering.
3. As agitation ebbs, the wolf notices its tail. The narrator reads the circling as possible distress, tries the orientation tone and water cue, and gets mixed evidence: brief attention, a drink, then renewed pursuit. Comedy comes from elaborate preparation meeting this behavior; risk remains in breathing, footing, claws, and momentum.
4. Compress approximately two hours through the safety clock, short pauses, failed redirections, and evolving tactics. The narrator twice declines to intervene when the wolf voluntarily stops, drinks, and resumes. This preserves agency without pretending certainty or making the chase harmless pet play.
5. The wolf leaves the loop and crosses before the fixed, shatter-resistant mirror. It freezes at the reflected wolf, then barks with territorial force. The first cue fails. The narrator reduces visual stimulation, uses the second tone once, and remains visibly still behind the barrier.
6. The wolf tests the image by changing angle and distance; the rival never moves independently or carries another scent. No polished human realization follows. Attention shifts to the narrator, and the wolf retreats from the mirror. Only after the prearranged approach signal does the narrator cover it, making safety jointly completed across unequal understanding.
7. Near dawn, the wolf settles within view of barrier and covered mirror. After returning, the exhausted partner requests an accurate account and decides whether the expiring footage survives. Let embarrassment, practical reassessment, and earned teasing coexist. End with an ordinary choice for next month, not an explained joke or cure.
