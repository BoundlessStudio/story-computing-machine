# Opus review: The Door in the Ivy

## Verdict

- Dialogue: PASS — two clearly different voices, every reply follows what its listener actually heard, and each major exchange changes the relationship; one Minor ambiguous line.
- Prose: PASS — concrete, varied and well staged; three places where narration names or passes judgment on a feeling the scene has already shown.
- Continuity and prompt: PASS — delivers every prompt beat and resolves it when she goes in through the same door; ages, times and objects all track; one Minor water-texture nit.
- Edit scope: polish
- Existing review.md said: Verdict PASS, Dialogue: PASS; agree. Here the old PASS holds up under a line-by-line audit.

The house-voice failure the owner complains about is largely absent here. Belda talks in run-on domestic detail (the cook's girl, the washing cart, the blue shoes in the stove, the tooth pulled the day before her wedding). She makes small jokes and uses a servant's idiom ("Oh, love", "There was a war on", "taking me on"). Clarimond starts in stunned fragments and echo-questions, turns fluent and organizing when she offers rooms, and falls back on court formality when she is refused. Only when she talks about the millpond does she start telling a story in Belda's way. The misunderstandings are legible and resolved through uptake: "You were here" is heard as "today", and Belda gives the stairs as an excuse before she says she doesn't want to live there. No exchange ends on a quip, and no one corrects anyone's wording for sport. The final exchange is about the wedding and the pulled tooth, not a statement of the theme. The edit pass should stay light: fix one line, trim three sentences of narration and swap one detail. It must not tidy Belda's ramble or sharpen Clarimond's lines.

## Dialogue findings

### D1 · Minor · "But I do" first parses as "But I do know"

- Where: L199, Belda's answer after the rooms are refused and the handkerchief goes back
- Text:
> I'd like you near me too. I don't know how we do that now. But I do.
- Problem: Ground/referent. "But I do" comes straight after "I don't know", so on the page it first reads as "But I do [know]", which contradicts the sentence before it. The intended referent, "I do [want you near me]", is two sentences back. Spoken stress would make it clear; print does not. This is Belda's key reciprocal line after the refusal, so the stumble costs something.
- Fix: Put the reaffirmation next to the want it confirms and keep "that" pointing back to "near me". Keep "too" as the echo of Clarimond's L163 request. Don't add anything more. Illustrative only: "I'd like you near me too. I do. I don't know how we manage it now."

## Prose findings

### P1 · Minor · Narration names or judges the feeling at three peaks

- Where: L69 (first handkerchief), L225 (just after "For that moment, I did."), L247 (the embrace)
- Text:
> Clarimond had not known she needed it until she pressed it beneath her eyes.
> At nine she had run because she was afraid, and all the years between could not make that a different act.
> Clarimond was grateful beyond speech for that second, deliberate return.
- Problem: L69 uses the stock "hadn't known she needed X until" template, a close cousin of the breath-she-didn't-know-she-was-holding beat. At L225 the narration delivers a verdict the dialogue has just carried ("I kept going." / "For that moment, I did."). The verdict is Clarimond's own habit of self-judgment, and the story goes on to complicate it with Belda's fear and the age reckoning at L229, but the "all the years between" clause is summary abstraction. At L247, "grateful beyond speech" labels an emotion that "that second, deliberate return" already shows.
- Fix: L69: keep the action (Belda offers it; Clarimond presses it beneath her eyes) and drop the "had not known" frame. L225: keep "She had carried a sword across a battlefield that morning." and "At nine she had run because she was afraid"; cut ", and all the years between could not make that a different act." L247: drop "grateful beyond speech"; end the sentence on Belda coming close again, or on a physical response from Clarimond. Make sentence-level trims only.

## Continuity and prompt findings

### C1 · Minor · New tap used as incidental water-supply texture

- Where: L155, Clarimond looks into the service passage just before offering rooms
- Text:
> A pail stood beneath a tap that had not been there before.
- Problem: The style guide's "No default water infrastructure" entry (CANON) counts passing water-supply texture, and the prompt does not ask for it. The detail only needs to show that the passage has changed since she was nine, and it does not need plumbing to do that. The stakes are very low. The millpond (L263) is a swimming place tied to the miller's daughters and falls under ordinary ponds, so keep it. The pail clatter at L299 can also stay.
- Fix: Replace the sentence with a different sign of change that has nothing to do with water supply, and don't contradict the cupboard memory (L79) that happened in this passage. Illustrative only: "Someone had hung a lamp where there had never been one."
