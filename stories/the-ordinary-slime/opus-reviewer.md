# Opus review: The Ordinary Slime

Independent audit for a later editing pass. It is not a `review.md` verdict,
does not change canon state, and does not authorize edits by itself.

## Verdict

- Dialogue: REVISE. All four speakers share one clipped, correcting register, and several decisive lines fail Ground ("the blockage", "Not enough to scour", "Long rain, they blue there", "I thought pattern").
- Prose: REVISE. The ending states its lesson five times in forty lines, and a "Not X. Y." reversal tic recurs.
- Continuity and prompt: REVISE. The prompt is fulfilled, but the examined slime's path, the gauge sequence and the salt cart/trough have small gaps, and the whole premise runs on water infrastructure that current policy flags (owner decision).
- Edit scope: heavy-dialogue
- Existing review.md said: Verdict PASS; "Dialogue: PASS", with a note that the four roles "keep distinct reasoning and pressure behavior" and that the final exchange does not restate the argument. I disagree: their stances differ, but they all talk the same way, and the ending restates the argument in both dialogue and narration.

The people in this story want genuinely different things. The assay-master wants controls, the warden is racing the clock, the reed keeper speaks for his seasons and his beds, and the junior needs to have seen something real. Yet they all speak in verbless noun-phrase fragments, and the default move in almost every exchange is to amend the previous speaker's word. The lab scenes therefore read as one voice qualifying itself. Where that shorthand is earned (gate calls, gauge readings, crew orders, the bed-loss count at the wall), it works and should stay. The failures cluster at the decisive beats, where compression turns into Ground trouble, and at the end, where the master and the narration spell out the lesson. The edit pass should keep the plot and the work-talk and give the reed keeper and the junior their own sentence shapes. It should also cut roughly half the corrections and make every decisive line say plainly what the speaker means.

## Dialogue findings

### D1 · Major · Four speakers share one clipped fragment register

- Where: L131–L141 (tray controls), L153–L161 (findings), L205–L223 (pocket), L257–L271 (readings); the junior at L49, L145, L187, L209
- Text:
> "Same clay. Same thickness."
> "Pocket behind the bricks," you say.
> "Pattern to alarm."
- Problem: Identical Voices. Cover the tags on L131–L137 or L209–L223, and the reed keeper, master and warden cannot be told apart. The junior, just caught delaying an alarm while a wall split, speaks with the same composure and the same verbless grammar ("Relative water stress." "Impact noise."). The stances genuinely differ (controls, clock, seasons, witness), but every speaker uses the same sentence shapes: noun-phrase fragments, no hesitation, and nobody talks longer than two sentences except to explain. This is also uniform fluency under pressure.
- Fix: Keep the shorthand where the work earns it: gate calls, gauge readings, crew orders (L193–L201, L241, L253, L287–L291). Everywhere else, give each speaker a sentence shape from the Voice map. The master speaks in slow, complete conditionals; the warden asks short questions aimed at the clock; the reed keeper uses fuller plain sentences built from seasons and past rains; the junior runs longer and more eager early, and shortens only after L269.

### D2 · Major · Correction is the default move of conversation

- Where: L35, L63, L85–L89, L105–L107, L121–L127, L145–L147, L153–L161, L187–L189, L209–L211, L225–L227, L235–L239, L257–L259, L267–L269, L331–L333, L353–L355
- Text:
> "Nothing," the warden says.
> "Eleven," says the reed keeper.
> "Not gone." The reed keeper looks at her.
> "And load change," the master answers.
- Problem: Correction ping-pong. In most exchanges someone amends the previous speaker's word ("Not gone." "Evidence of one." "Eleven." "No." "In a connected wet film." "First bell."). The arc (a junior who overreads and learns to measure) justifies some of it. But the master, warden and reed keeper all correct in the same way, so the move stops characterizing anyone. The junior never pushes back, is never partly right, and is never simply agreed with. "Eleven" (L107) can only be decoded from the paragraph after it. The reed keeper's "Not gone" (L227) spends his stake on a word quibble before he names the loss. "You reported frost scaling." / "Same corner." (L237–L239) repeats the "You told me…/I told you…" template of L83–L85.
- Fix: Keep the corrections that carry character or plot: L35 ("That wasn't my question."), L123 (the master restating the result), L211 (without the tag; see D14), L269 ("Call the card, not the meaning.") and L355 ("First bell. Bank's soft."). Turn the rest into other moves: agreement, silence, a physical test instead of a retort (L147 already does this, so keep the tray turn and lose the "No."), a real disagreement the junior wins or half-wins, or a speaker stating their own point without first amending someone else's word. At L105–L107, either cut both lines or let the reed keeper say what he means by eleven. Keep only one of the two "what exactly did you say" arguments (L83–L89 or L235–L239).

### D3 · Major · The alarm-delay exchange plays as a deposition

- Where: L49–L69, assay room, the warden's arrival
- Text:
> The warden's gaze comes to you. "After how long?"
> "So your warning arrived after the wall opened."
- Problem: A procedure routine built on a manufactured misunderstanding. "After how long?" answers "They warned us" with a question the listener cannot parse. The junior's "What?" registers the confusion, but the confusion exists only so the warden can run a clipped cross-examination (pattern to alarm, pattern to crack) and close on a summarizing verdict. Cornered about the delay that let the wall open, the junior answers like a witness reading figures, with none of the defensiveness the narration later admits (L321). Then L69 explains scour logic to two people who work there ("If the main drain clogs, the lower houses take the reservoir"), which is as-you-know exposition.
- Fix: Keep the facts (seven minutes to alarm, crack at about five), "I saw it first." / "You did.", and the warden's demand "Give me a test that changes that choice." Replace the "After how long?" / "What?" / "Pattern to alarm." setup with a direct question. Then let the junior's answer run longer and more self-justifying (the colors, the light, fetching the reed keeper first) before the numbers come out. The numbers can make the point on their own; keep "So your warning…" only if it is not the closing word of the exchange. Trim L69 to what the warden is deciding. Illustrative only: "When did they come up, and when did you pull the cord?"

### D4 · Major · "Again" is never carried out, and the exchange ends on a comeback

- Where: L111–L127, first tray, the dripping eave
- Text:
> The warden leans out and closes the shutter. "Again."
> "It starts at twelve," you say.
> "We don't have time to qualify every word."
> "Then use fewer words."
- Problem: Ground/uptake and staging. The warden orders a repeat, and nobody repeats anything. The colors fade (L119), and the junior's "It starts at twelve" rests on a single run that the master has just called contaminated. Until the tray comparisons, the reader cannot tell whether the effect survived without the drip. The exchange then closes on a Punchline Patch: the junior's objection exists only to set up the master's paradoxical retort.
- Fix: Either carry out "Again" on the page (lift the twelfth out and the color drains; set it back with the shutter closed and a weaker pattern returns), or cut "Again." and let the fading itself force the controlled comparisons. Keep the master's restatement at L123 and "Write that." Cut L125–L127, or replace the comeback with the junior writing it grudgingly while the warden goes straight to the clock at L129.

### D5 · Major · The central finding is one sentence split across four speakers

- Where: L145–L161, tray comparisons
- Text:
> You draw the three states on the back of the gauge card. "Relative water stress."
> "In a connected wet film," she says.
> "And nothing across a dry break."
> "At twelve or more."
> "Twelve worked. Don't marry it yet."
- Problem: Identical Voices and Attribution. The story's key result is assembled as a relay in which each speaker appends a clause to the last, so nobody owns a view and nobody disagrees. "At twelve or more." and "Twelve worked. Don't marry it yet." are untagged after three different speakers, so alternation cannot recover who says them. "She" at L155 could be either woman in the room, so the reader has to trace it back to L151. The junior jumps from "They show where the water will go" (L145) to the master's own technical phrasing one beat later, with no sign of how they got there.
- Fix: Let one person own the formulation: the master writing the card, or the junior trying it and being amended once. Let the reed keeper object or add in his own terms (what he has seen on the wall in past rains) instead of appending a clause. Tag or rebuild the two untagged lines. "Don't marry it yet" sounds like the reed keeper, so it should be his if kept. Name the master at L155.

### D6 · Major · "Not enough to scour" reverses the decision it answers

- Where: L175–L181, end of the tray scene
- Text:
> "Useful enough to carry," he says. "Not enough to scour."
- Problem: Ground/uptake plus epigram. The warden's frame (L69) is that scouring is the default and the test must justify stopping it. Read literally, "not enough to scour" says the evidence does not justify scouring, which flips that burden. The likely intended meaning, "not enough to call off the scour", is not in the words. The line is shaped by its parallel with "enough to carry", and it comes at the beat that sends everyone to the wall.
- Fix: Keep the reed keeper answering for the master and lifting the tray. Have him say plainly what the trays justify: go to the wall, but don't decide the salt on trays alone. The warden's "Leave the salt cart shut" still follows. Illustrative only: "Enough to take to the wall. I wouldn't call off the salt on two trays."

### D7 · Major · "The blockage" has no referent at the wall

- Where: L185–L189, service gallery, first look at the colony
- Text:
> "It's pointing at the blockage."
> "Or responding to the stress beside it. Mallet."
- Problem: Ground/referent at an important beat. The only blockage the reader has heard of is a possible clog in the main drain (L69), but the fork points the other way, at the bricked old spill. The reader cannot tell whether the junior means the drain clog, the bricked spill, or something behind it, and the master's "it" inherits the same fog. The mallet and gate tests exist to check this claim, so it has to be clear.
- Fix: Have the junior name what they are looking at and what they think it means, and let the master's reply name the crack, so that both claims can be tested. Illustrative only: "It's pointing at the old spill."

### D8 · Major · The reed keeper's decisive line is compressed past recovery

- Where: L277–L283, after the handspan opening
- Text:
> He repeats what she has not wanted to hear. "Long rain, they blue there. The beds flood at two hands. At four, the lower bank goes. Today the crop's lost once you pass one."
- Problem: Ground/referent at the decision to sacrifice the beds. "They blue there" answers nothing the warden has asked, and "there" is not tied to a place. "Two hands", "four" and "one" silently switch from the reservoir's "hand" (L37) to the spill's "handspan" (L231). The narration says he "repeats" this, but only "All of it" (L249) was said before. The warden's "Can we hold one?" responds only to the last clause.
- Fix: Keep his idiom and his numbers, but make each clause say what it counts (handspans of spill opening, which terrace goes when). Either cut the slime clause or make its point explicit in his terms: every long rain the blue sits by that spill when the beds are full, so it tells him nothing new. Replace "He repeats what she has not wanted to hear" with a plain beat, or earn "repeats" by having him actually say it twice.

### D9 · Major · The accountability answer states the lesson in broken grammar

- Where: L315–L323, the record book on first repair watch
- Text:
> "I thought pattern before I checked load. Then I called it a warning."
- Problem: The Philosopher, plus syntax. On first pass "I thought pattern" reads as the noun "thought pattern". The line gives the story's lesson in vocabulary ("load") that the junior learned only that afternoon. Its second sentence is a confession about the later claim, not a reason for the seven-minute delay the warden asked about. The narration just before it (L321) promises "The answer she can use is shorter", so the line's job is to be short and factual, not thematic.
- Fix: Give the plain reason for the delay in the junior's own terms of that moment: they watched the colors and fetched the reed keeper before pulling the cord. Illustrative only: "I stood there watching it. Then I went for him before I went for the cord."

### D10 · Major · The final exchange with the master states the theme

- Where: L331–L339, first repair watch, the slime in the bowl
- Text:
> She closes her case. "Which slime?"
> "It answered nothing alone. Put it where you took it."
- Problem: The Philosopher and a Punchline Patch at the final meaningful exchange. "Which slime?" is a rhetorical zinger; the next lines show she knows exactly which one. "It answered nothing alone" repeats the reed keeper's "You'll learn nothing from one" (L75) and "They don't do it alone" (L81) as a closing moral.
- Fix: Keep the junior's question, the bowl and "Put it where you took it." Give the master a practical reason not to keep the slime that shows her character without summing up the story: she already has its weight and acid, and it would dry out in her room. Cut "Which slime?", or make it literal by letting the junior be unsure this is the same slime (see C2). Illustrative only: "I have its weight and its acid. It'll dry out in my room. Put it where you took it."

### D11 · Minor · The narration answers the scour question before the characters do

- Where: L213–L227, gallery, the scour dilemma
- Text:
> It will not open a sealed side spill.
> "The film goes. The pocket keeps pressing."
- Problem: Explanatory chorus. The warden asks what scour does to the pocket, the narration answers in full (L215), and then the reed keeper and the master each restate a piece of it. The dialogue is left with nothing to reveal.
- Fix: Cut the last three sentences of L215 (keep the trough and its location) so the reed keeper's answer carries the information. Let his cost at L227 open with the loss rather than "Not gone." (see D2), and keep his concrete list of stalk, bundles and matting.

### D12 · Minor · Salt-and-bucket beat: redundant rule, unclear referent, unclear speaker

- Where: L93–L101, setting up the first tray
- Text:
> The reed keeper gives you a look. "Never salt before touching drains."
> "Rinse again. Bucket, please."
- Problem: The reed keeper repeats the master's instruction as a proverb, and "drains" presumably means drain slimes but reads as the drains themselves. "Rinse again. Bucket, please." is untagged. By alternation it belongs to the reed keeper, but he is the one holding the bucket, so "Bucket, please" makes no sense from him.
- Fix: Cut the proverb at L95, or let him give his own reason (salt burns them). Tag the L99 line, and make the bucket an action: he holds it out, or the master asks him for it.

### D13 · Minor · The warden names the "trapped load" before anyone has found it

- Where: L167, end of the tray scene
- Text:
> "Can the wall colony tell us where the trapped load is now?"
- Problem: Ground/knowledge (pre-emption). Nobody has proposed trapped water yet. The gate test at L203–L211 is where it is found, and the master's "Trapped water, separate enough…" is presented as the discovery. The warden's wording hands the characters the answer early.
- Fix: Have her ask about what she can see. Illustrative only: "Can the ones on the wall show us what's pushing on that crack?"

### D14 · Minor · Tag and beat mechanics

- Where: L51, L111, L169, L211, L241
- Text:
> "Evidence of one," the master corrects.
> Your breath catches.
> The warden's mouth tightens.
> Under pressure, she becomes slower, placing each word as if it too requires a control.
- Problem: "Corrects" is a said-bookism that names the story's own tic. "Your breath catches", "The warden's mouth tightens" and "The warden's gaze comes to you" (L51) are stock beats. L169 narrates the master's speech pattern instead of letting her lines show it.
- Fix: Use "says" or no tag at L211. Cut the stock beats or replace them with a specific action (at L241 she has just been caught out on her own records, so give her something to do with the sheet). Cut the L169 gloss and let the three "if" lines carry her slowness.

## Continuity and prompt findings

### C1 · Major · The premise runs on water infrastructure the prompt never asked for

- Where: throughout; e.g. L21 (reservoir wall, main drain), L37 (sluice warden, gauge walk), L69 (scour), L197–L203 (main gate, sluice), L349 (gate order)
- Text:
> Outside the assay room, water knocks somewhere inside the reservoir wall.
> The sluice warden stands in the doorway
- Problem: `universe/style-guide.md` "No default water infrastructure" (CANON, 2026-09-09) bars reservoirs, drains, sluices, flood-control works and their operation as default plot, stakes, occupations or expertise unless the request calls for them. The prompt says nothing about setting, yet the story's setting, cast (sluice warden, gate tender, gauge clerk), stakes and climax are all reservoir, drain and sluice operation. The rule covers authorized revisions, so it reaches this edit pass, but the conflict lies in the premise, not in texture. No surgical dialogue edit can remove it without breaking causality.
- Fix: This is an owner decision. Under AGENTS.md a whole-story change is a Replacement, not an in-place edit. For this pass: add no new water-infrastructure detail in rewritten lines (no extra gate jargon, drain lore or reservoir history as color), and leave the existing plot mechanics intact.

### C2 · Minor · The examined slime's path, and the salt cart versus the trough

- Where: L109, L139, L177, L335; L181, L183, L215, L241, L309
- Text:
> It rests in the same clay bowl beside your elbow
> "Wall. Bring the mallet. Leave the salt cart shut."
> The covered ash-salt trough sits along the far wall.
- Problem: The examined slime goes into the tray at L109, is divided among twenty-four at L139, and travels to the wall with the tray at L177. At L335 it is "in the same clay bowl", but no bowl has appeared (it was held over "the assay tray" and fed on "the glass", L13), and nothing shows the junior picking it out again. The prompt's slime needs a traceable path, especially since the ending turns on "which slime". Separately, "salt cart" (L181, L241) and the "covered ash-salt trough" (L183, L215, L309) read as either one object with two names or two objects, and a cart that is "shut" is odd.
- Fix: Add a half-line when the trays are divided, or after the flood, showing the junior setting the examined slime apart in a bowl. Alternatively, let the junior be unsure it is the same one (which would make a question about "which slime" literal). Use one name for the salt source, or make clear that the cart feeds the trough.
