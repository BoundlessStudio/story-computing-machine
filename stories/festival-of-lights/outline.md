# Festival of Lights provisional outline

## Promised experience

A young princess who will one day rule slips away from her family’s palace festivities. Alone on the weathered battlements of an ancient watchtower, she sees her kingdom’s warmly lit royal castle, colorful fireworks in the moonlit sky, and thousands of lanterns in the streets below. Musicians, dancing children, merchants and families celebrate their annual Festival of Lights, cherished for generations. Her people laugh and dance unaware of her. Their happiness makes her wonder what life beyond royal expectations would feel like.

“Rare moment of solitude” suggests time to enjoy being above the crowd. “Simple freedoms” suggests an ordinary act can carry the payoff, with the bright city around it. The contrast can be tender as well as tempting, since both celebrations may matter to her. The request gives her a royal family and future subjects, and leaves other relationships, names, and the outcome of her wondering open.

## Takes considered

- **Two dances.** After enjoying the solitude aloft, the princess tries the festival on foot. The central scene puts an invitation from an unknowing stranger and a dance she owes her mother on either side of one street.
- **The hour aloft.** From the battlements, she watches a family teach a child to dance until the child invents a turn and draws the adults into it. The central act is hers alone, taking the hour she meant to give the castle and hearing the revel go on in both places.
- **The fiddler remembers.** Years later a street musician remembers the stranger who requested a tune he disliked on the night the court stopped playing it. Their argument over which ending to play would show the princess’s freedom obliquely, through someone who never guessed who she was.

Choose **Two dances**. The difference between a dance people can leave and one arranged in her name can be felt in the steps. The princess can act on a desire the whole festival has prepared and feel what it costs while the lights are still up.

## Provisional direction

Catrin means to open the final palace dance with her mother, her first year in the place reserved for the heir. Alone at Tallow Gate’s watchtower, she hears a street fiddler play an extra beat in the tune she knows from rehearsal. She goes down to see what people do with it.

Bess, a gingerbread seller, wants a turn leading the line through the gate. She invites Catrin to dance. Catrin turns early, then offers the habitual graceful excuse. Bess takes her at her word and lets go. At the foot of the palace road, with time to make the castle if she leaves now, Catrin goes back and asks Bess to dance again. Bess has other partners and takes her hand by choice. The gold fireworks that cue the palace dance come while they’re still dancing. Catrin has let a moment she genuinely wanted pass, and she’s still just the girl who asked.

## Craft plan

**Teller and trial.** Try close third past with Catrin’s trained attention to movement and the sensation of being watched, and first-person past in her freshly recollected, candid, amused voice. No supplied or established characters.

**Catrin** wants time in which nobody is waiting to see what she’ll do. She also wants to be as good a partner to her mother as her mother has been to her in practice. She hears the shape of a measure through other noise. Under pressure she starts with precise courtesies before she hazards a direct request. Her thin court shoes, knowledge of the tune and instinct to keep her hand held out after being released recur in action.

**Bess** wants the sheer pleasure of taking the line round the bend and, privately, her family to see her do it. She recruits boldly and lets go readily. She speaks in amused runs, bringing in the person who made the same mistake and what befell their toes. She can offer an opinion on a stranger’s feet in the same breath as an invitation.

**Place.** Tallow Gate in the capital, Vendrel, wins over Candlecourt’s exposed square and the North Curtain’s isolated lookout. The watchtower’s blind stair opens directly onto the fork between the palace road and the dance under the gate. In late autumn, between moonrise over the roofs and the last fireworks, candle merchants and sweet sellers push their boards back to dance. Willow lantern ribs show through blue paper. The worn parapet chills a palm. Hot ginger rises from the street. A drumbeat reaches the stair before the fiddle does. The arch swallows the tune for two steps. Families rehang lanterns on the same mismatched iron hooks each year. From above the line appears smooth. Down in it the paving and the lost music make people improvise. [Gatehouse architecture](https://www.english-heritage.org.uk/visit/places/thornton-abbey-and-gatehouse/history/description/), [processional social dance](https://www.efdss.org/learning/resources/beginners-guides/37-english-folk-dance/2195-social-dance-4).

## Scene briefs and ending

1. **Battlements, first fireworks.** A third. Catrin wants her breathing and her time back. Give the lanterns, the revelers below, and the warm castle their space. Recall her escape briefly and her mother’s palm waiting at rehearsal. Below, a child makes a turn the adults have to go around, and the fiddler adds his beat. Catrin goes down to see what else is invisible from here.
2. **Market fork and gate arch, late fireworks. Long scene.** Two thirds. Catrin wants to try a turn without drawing notice. Bess wants someone who can keep pace. Give them time to misread each other and dance badly enough to want another try. Slow down at the fork, with the climb still possible. When the castle’s gold finale lights the far mouth of the gate, Catrin takes the extra beat and swings Bess farther than either expected. Load that swing with the early turn, her mother’s steady hand, and the child she could see from above but couldn’t hear laugh. The palace’s response stays offstage.
