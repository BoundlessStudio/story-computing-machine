# Festival of Lights provisional outline

## Promised experience

A young princess who will one day rule slips away from her family’s palace festivities. Alone on the weathered battlements of an ancient watchtower, she sees her kingdom’s magnificent, warmly lit castle, colorful fireworks in the moonlit sky, and thousands of lanterns in the streets below. Musicians, dancing children, merchants and families celebrate their annual Festival of Lights, cherished for generations. Her people laugh and dance unaware of her. Their happiness makes her wonder what life beyond royal expectations would feel like.

“Rare moment of solitude” suggests time to enjoy being above the crowd. “Simple freedoms” suggests an ordinary act can carry the payoff, with the bright city around it. The contrast can be tender as well as tempting, since both celebrations may matter to her. The request gives her a royal family and future subjects, and leaves other relationships, names, and the outcome of her wondering open.

## Takes considered

- **Two dances.** After enjoying the solitude aloft, the princess tries the festival on foot. The central scene brings her palace habits into a street dance whose people are free to come and go.
- **The hour aloft.** From the battlements, she watches a family teach a child to dance until the child invents a turn and draws the adults into it. The central act is hers alone, taking the hour she meant to give the castle and hearing the revel go on in both places.
- **The fiddler remembers.** Years later a street musician remembers the stranger who requested a tune he disliked on the night the court stopped playing it. Their argument over which ending to play would show the princess’s freedom obliquely, through someone who never guessed who she was.

Choose **Two dances**. A tune heard from above carries the difference between what Catrin can watch, what she knows from court, and what she can try among strangers. She can feel all three in one night.

## Provisional direction

Catrin has already danced the festival’s palace measure with her mother. She slips away to Tallow Gate’s watchtower for the pleasure of watching the display alone. She means to see the whole gold finale from there. On the street below, a child turns early and her older partner laughs and swings her round anyway. Other pairs wheel round after them. Catrin hears the street fiddler’s extra beat in a phrase she knows from the palace and goes down wanting some of that delight.

Bess, a gingerbread seller, invites her into the line. Catrin turns too early. She gives Bess the graceful excuse she uses to end a dance at court, where partners hold their places until the heir drops her hand. Bess simply lets go and dances on. Catrin starts back up the stair toward the lovely full view she came for. She stops where she can still hear the music. She turns back to ask Bess directly for another dance, with no way to know the answer. Bess, who wants her turn to lead and has other partners, takes her hand.

Under the arch, the finale drowns the fiddle. Catrin carries the extra beat and gives Bess room to take a turn wide, as the older dancer did for the child. Their swing brings the old parapet into sight. She can see gold light on the empty place where she sat, and hear Bess and the pairs swinging with them laugh.

## Craft plan

**Teller and trial.** Try close third past with Catrin’s trained attention to movement and the sensation of being watched, and first-person past in her freshly recollected, candid, amused voice. No supplied or established characters.

**Catrin** wants time in which nobody is waiting to see what she’ll do. She loves the exactness of the palace measure and can hear its shape through other noise. A mild request from the heir can set the whole room in motion, so she’s learned to give people an out before she ever says what she wants. She expects the other person to wait for her to end the exchange. Her thin court shoes, the hand held out a beat too long, and her mother tapping the count against her wrist when she first learned the dance recur in action and memory.

**Bess** wants the sheer pleasure of taking the line round the bend and, privately, her family to see her do it. She recruits boldly and lets go readily. She speaks in amused runs, bringing in the person who made the same mistake and what befell their toes. She can offer an opinion on a stranger’s feet in the same breath as an invitation.

**Place.** Tallow Gate in the capital, Vendrel, wins over Candlecourt’s exposed square and the North Curtain’s isolated lookout. The disused watchtower’s public stair is hidden inside the wall and opens onto the market at the mouth of the arch. The plain wool cloak Catrin has thrown over her court clothes is familiar enough here. In late autumn, between moonrise over the roofs and the last fireworks, candle merchants and sweet sellers push their boards back to dance. Willow lantern ribs show through blue paper. The worn parapet chills a palm. Hot ginger rises from the street. A drumbeat reaches the stair before the fiddle does. The elbow under the arch muddles the tune. Children hang the family lanterns on the same mismatched iron hooks their elders still know by touch. ## Scene briefs and ending

1. **Battlements, first fireworks.** A third. Catrin savors the lanterns, the revelers below and the warm castle she loves looking at from here. Give her the pleasure of following an entire burst down to its last sparks and looking forward to seeing the full gold fan. Recollect slipping away, her mother tapping the count, and the watching eyes in the ballroom. Let her linger over the child’s turn and the older dancer’s laugh before she goes down wanting to try the dance herself.
2. **Market and gate arch, late fireworks. Long scene.** Two thirds. Catrin wants to try a turn without drawing notice. Bess wants someone who’ll keep pace. Make their first attempt quick and funny. Slow down when Bess takes Catrin at her word and the stair offers back the whole view and her own company. Catrin asks in her own words and stays. Carry the pleasure of the child’s turn into their wider swing at the far mouth of the arch, where the gold on the parapet shows how the same place looks from inside the dance. The palace celebrations before her escape enter only in brief recollection. End amid the other pairs still dancing.
