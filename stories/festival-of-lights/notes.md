# Editorial notes

## November 12, 2026

**Codex keeper, request reading.** The stated relationship is between the princess and the people she will rule. The inherited citywide festival, her castle still in view, and the crowd’s ignorance of her presence all belong to the solitary watchtower scene. The keeper heard “a wistful curiosity growing out of that enjoyment” and expected “a moment that makes an ordinary freedom particular to her and changes what she imagines is possible.” The request leaves the particular freedom, the pressure at court, and any descent or encounter open.

**Claude, request reading.** “Watching joy from a hidden height” was the distinctive pleasure. The moonlit panorama deserves time on the page, and the glowing family castle can be a warm counterweight to the princess’s longing. Claude inferred a “wistful, warm and luminous” story with citywide spectacle and an intimate turn. It anticipated a realization or brief taste of ordinary freedom and left open whether she descends, speaks with anyone, or returns.

**First outline readings.** The keeper found the promise in Bess letting go at Catrin’s word and taking her hand again by choice. “The beat currently specifies the movement and leaves their pleasure implicit.” Claude heard the same tune joining tower, street and palace, but worried that “the solitude becomes the overture” and that the invented obligation could make the climax a missed appointment. It wanted the view from the tower to govern the swing or reenter the last image. The keeper wanted a visible trace of generations passing the tradition on. Claude wanted the mother’s pleasure in dancing to register as concretely as the cost.
