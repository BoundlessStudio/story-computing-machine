# Provisional direction

A first-person comic caper, probably around 2,500–3,500 words. Nico arrives from an ordinary modern life with the clothes he was wearing, no supplies or explanation, and the single ability name **Shenanigans**. He wants dinner and somewhere safe to sleep. He is used to being the person who holds everyone's bags while they do something interesting.

At a small traveling fair, straightforward attempts to activate the ability produce nothing. A desperate, deliberately ridiculous bluff produces a small physical joke that helps and immediately complicates his escape from a suspicious guard. Further experiments suggest that nearby props can cooperate with committed pretense; Nico cannot simply order a desired result, alter someone's mind, or erase what the resulting mess has broken. This remains an imperfect guess from experience, never a revealed ability manual.

Tansy, a traveling performer, feeds him and lets him help with her wagon. Her trained goat has been seized after humiliating a visiting lord, who intends to display it in his ceremonial procession before taking it to his private menagerie. Tansy is planning a mundane, dangerous retrieval while the procession forms. Nico could keep his new meal and bed by staying out of it; instead, he offers to create a distraction. Tansy knows the animal, ropes, costumes, and how people at the fair actually behave. Nico knows only that he can sometimes make a stupid idea worse.

Their attempted rescue becomes one accumulating farce rather than separate random gags. A borrowed costume, a false procession role, and a small switch of physical props lead to Nico being accepted as the very dignitary he needs to distract. His attempt to enjoy or control that success strands Tansy on the wrong side of the guards and puts the goat beyond her reach. The goat's ordinary stubbornness also defeats the imagined clean escape. Each new bluff must use the mess the last one left.

For the turn, Nico abandons looking impressive and commits to an unmistakably foolish public stunt, giving Tansy the opening to act. The ability may enlarge the prank, but it does not decide to rescue her animal or supply a complete getaway. They finish with ordinary daring, Tansy's practiced handling, and a goat who can be persuaded by familiar food, not commanded by heroism. They escape the fair in her real wagon with damage, loss, and future trouble still attached; there is no crowd conversion or defeated system.

Possible ending: Nico is welcome to supper and a precarious place with Tansy, on terms that include paying for the wrecked costume and doing the unglamorous work. He makes one small last experiment on himself rather than an unwilling stranger, and it goes sideways. The ability is still called only Shenanigans. He has learned enough to attempt tomorrow and insufficiently much to deserve confidence.
