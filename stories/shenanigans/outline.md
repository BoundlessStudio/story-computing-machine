# Provisional direction

A first-person comic caper, probably around 2,500–3,500 words. Nico arrives from an ordinary modern life with the clothes he was wearing, no supplies or explanation, and the single ability name **Shenanigans**. He wants dinner and somewhere safe to sleep. He is used to being the person who holds everyone's bags while they do something interesting.

At a small traveling fair, straightforward attempts to activate the ability produce nothing. Nico tells a clean-shaven guard that his moustache is coming loose. A horsehair moustache leaps from a nearby puppet stall onto the guard's face; the joke helps Nico escape a demand for an entry fee, then lands him in trouble with the performer who owns the prop. Further experiments give conflicting results. Nearby physical props sometimes cooperate with committed pretense; requests for food and money fail, and Nico cannot order an outcome or alter people's minds. These remain guesses from experience, never a revealed ability manual.

Tansy, the performer, feeds him and lets him help with her wagon. During preparations he sees her goat's stolen-crown act with her dented oat-cake tin and pasteboard crown: it bows, demands its cake, then steals the volunteer's crown. In the performance that follows, it takes the visiting lord's plumed hat instead, pulling off his wig too. He seizes the animal and intends to take it to his private menagerie after the ceremonial procession. Tansy prepares her wagon and a way past the service gate where the goat is tethered. Nico could keep his new meal and bed by staying out of it; instead, he offers to occupy the guards while she retrieves it. She knows the animal, cues, ropes, costumes, and the fair. He knows only that he can sometimes make a stupid idea worse.

Tansy loans him the green tabard and crown from her King of Turnips act. His false claim to be a visiting festival official makes the tabard's stitched turnips acquire tiny crowns and its cheap fringe stiffen into an absurdly grand collar. The ushers are expecting an unfamiliar guest; costume and his bluff carry the mistake, not compelled belief. Their deference pleases the man who usually holds the bags. Although his job is only to occupy them, he asks to have the goat brought into the reviewing enclosure so he can demonstrate his supposed expertise for the lord. He chooses to prolong a welcome as someone important. This physically moves animal and guards away from Tansy's route, which Nico has been told to preserve. It is his preventable mistake, not random punishment by the ability.

For the turn, Nico turns his demonstration into the familiar stolen-crown act with himself as the crowned fool. He draws the lord and guards across the enclosure to see an official disgrace himself, giving Tansy a chance to reach the real goat. The collar's fringe becomes an inconvenient beard, the crown tips, and his attempted dignified retreat tangles him in the same tabard. The magic supplies comic complications during both his self-important bluff and his generous choice; it does not recognize humility or rescue the animal. Tansy uses the planted tin and cues, including paying the goat for its bow when its insistence on finishing the act delays them. Her handling and Nico's ordinary scramble complete the escape in her real wagon. The dignity he briefly enjoyed, the damaged borrowed costume, and future trouble are left attached; there is no crowd conversion or defeated system.

Possible ending: Nico is welcome to supper and a precarious place with Tansy, on terms that include paying for the wrecked costume and doing the unglamorous work. He tries claiming that the borrowed moustache is now his own. It sticks somewhere on him that is emphatically not his lip, echoing the first trick without confirming a rule. The ability is still called only Shenanigans. He has learned enough to attempt tomorrow and insufficiently much to deserve confidence.
