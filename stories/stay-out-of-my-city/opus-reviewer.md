# Opus review: Stay Out of My City

Independent audit for a later editing pass. It is not a `review.md` verdict,
does not change canon state, and does not authorize edits by itself.

## Verdict

- Dialogue: REVISE — most exchanges work, but the quarry climax turns on a redundant gloss and a dry retort, and the offer built on Elvi gets no uptake.
- Prose: PASS — concrete, warm and well paced; a few small physical-staging slips and one abstract summary sentence pair.
- Continuity and prompt: PASS — the prompt is fully delivered within the length band; the quarry positions blur and one arrival is only half explained.
- Edit scope: targeted-dialogue
- Existing review.md said: Verdict PASS; "Dialogue: PASS"; I mostly agree but disagree on the quarry pivot (L253–L257), which is the house correction tic at the story's key turn.

The dialogue here is better than the collection's default. Warren and Elvi talk like people who have shared a kitchen for years: she declines to argue ("Yes."), he complains pettily and loses gracelessly, and the running jokes (the goggle knot, the shark trilogy, "that noise") pay off without anyone explaining them. Candescent and Mordant are distinct in what they want and notice: she speaks formally when she is being an institution and plainly when she needs help, and he is transactional and arch in public and bitter in private. Everyone shares one clipped, understated rhythm. That mostly suits the story, but it leaves Mordant with little bravado to lose. The real failure is local. At the quarry, Candescent explains an offer Warren obviously understood so that Mordant can correct her, and that exchange stands in for the escalation that should be physical. Meanwhile the one offer that uses Elvi as leverage never touches Warren. The edit pass should cut that patch, make the pivot physical, let today's specific pull reach Warren before his unchanged refusal, and otherwise polish without rewriting.

## Edit guardrails

- Prompt requirements: Warren refuses both sides (L53–L57, L105, L129, L287) and threatens both to stay out (L47, L57, L273). Brackenport becomes "the most peaceful city in the world" and literally crime-free (L141). The story must stay complete, broadly accessible, with non-graphic threats, and within 2,500–4,000 words (now about 3,290). Craft profile prospective-2026-08-23.
- Keep both spoken instances of the title line ("Stay out of my city.", L47 and L273) and the refrain "I'm not joining you" (L53, L287). The echo is deliberate.
- Power rules the prose relies on: Warren must see what he holds (L277). He leaves breathing alone (L27). His threats target weapons and vehicles, never bodies (L57, L273). No edit should make him lethal or let him act out of sight.
- Timeline and geography: Elvi is 10, then 13, then 16, then 19. The story covers nine years, with five crime-free by year nine. The quarry lies just beyond the inland boundary and the coach station just inside it (L89, L135, L227). The coach leaves at ten, and Elvi gives him "twelve minutes" (L235). Elvi wins the race fairly, and Warren owes "three films".
- Warren is a living human using his own faculty, which is consistent with the LOCKED "Living agency and magic" entry in `universe/rules.md`. No other universe entry is touched. Do not extend Warren's protection beyond the city unless the editor deliberately wants that consequence. The story's shape depends on his letting Elvi go.
- Do not flatten Elvi's refusal to be impressed by super-drama ("Can we still go in?", and her silence about the quarry at the station). It is the story's quiet argument, and it must stay unspoken.

## Dialogue findings

### D1 · Major · Quarry pivot runs on a gloss and a retort

- Where: L253–L257, quarry, the two offers just before the escorts advance (setup at L243; payoff at L269)
- Text:
> I own a house overlooking that university's bay. No rent, no obligations for your daughter. A few obligations for you.
> Candescent put herself between them. “He's offering to buy you.”
> “He has understood the offer without assistance.” Mordant lifted two fingers toward his escorts.
- Problem: Punchline Patch and explanatory chorus. Candescent tells Warren, a grown man standing right there, what Mordant's offer is. The line has no listener who needs it, and it exists so that Mordant can land a dry correction. That is the collection's correction ping-pong, placed at the exact moment the scene turns to force, so the gunship advance reads as a reaction to a quip rather than to Candescent physically stepping in (which L243 "mine stays between me and hers" already motivates). There is also an uptake gap. Mordant has just said he knows which bay Elvi's university overlooks and has built his bribe on her. Warren, the listener this is aimed at, never registers it, from L253 until the vague "Get those things away from her" at L269. A reader can take the offer as a bribe or as a veiled threat, and nobody on the page does either.
- Fix: Cut Candescent's spoken gloss and Mordant's retort. Keep "Candescent put herself between them" and "Mordant lifted two fingers toward his escorts" so the signal answers her body, not her words. If Candescent must speak, aim her words at Mordant as a warning, not at Warren as an explanation. Illustrative only: “Step back, Mordant.” Then give Warren one short narration beat, not a speech, that registers that Mordant knows exactly where Elvi will be, so that L269 carries it. Do not add a thesis line, and do not have Warren answer the offer here. His answer is L287.

### D2 · Minor · "How did you hear?" gets half an answer

- Where: L245–L251, quarry, Candescent's opening pitch
- Text:
> “I came because I heard Elvi's leaving,” Candescent said.
> “How did you hear?”
> “She won a scholarship. It was in your local paper.”
- Problem: Ground/knowledge. Warren's question is about today: both factions are camped by the coach station on the morning Elvi leaves (L227). Candescent's answer explains only that Elvi won a scholarship, not how she knew the day, and Mordant turns up at the same moment with the same knowledge. Her next line, "I'm not trying to frighten you", shows she knows her knowledge is unsettling, but the reply gives Warren nothing that explains it. He then drops the question for a fond memory of the six copies.
- Fix: Make the reply cover what he asked. Either the paper printed the departure day, or Candescent admits someone watches the station. The admission gives "I'm not trying to frighten you" something to answer. Illustrative only: “It was in your local paper. So was the day she goes.” Keep the six-copies memory.

### D3 · Minor · Final refusal never meets today's offer

- Where: L279–L289, quarry, the decisive exchange with Candescent (offer at L245)
- Text:
> You may want a change yourself. We could arrange work near her university.
> “You know what you could do.”
> He did. He could feel the ease of it, all that weight awaiting his decision.
> “I'm not joining you,” he said. “I'm not joining him. That's still my answer.”
- Problem: Relationship movement at the decisive beat is thin. The refusal repeats the opening (L53–L57) and answers the general recruitment, but the thing that made this morning different is an offer to be near Elvi. That pull never reaches Warren. The narration's temptation is about the ease of the power, not about the daughter getting on a coach. So "still my answer" costs him nothing new, and the scene plays as a replay of the harbor rather than a choice.
- Fix: Leave the three spoken refusal lines as they are. At L285, let the near-Elvi option register as a real want in one concrete image, such as the empty kitchen or her beyond anything he can see, before he answers. The sight rule (L277) makes this a natural link. No thesis statement and no speech about letting go; the race and the station scene already carry that.

### D4 · Minor · Line-level tags and phrasing

- Where: L51 (harbor), L63 (harbor), L93 (quarry, Elvi 13), L127 (quarry, Elvi 16), L289 (quarry, final)
- Text:
> “You should be with us,” she called, with a new urgency.
> “I understand.”
> “Who looks after here?”
> His fingers tightened on the folder.
> She shut her eyes briefly. “Then let my people go.”
- Problem: Small mechanics. "with a new urgency" names the emotion that her straining against his hold (L49) already shows. "I understand." is untagged after Warren's "Answer me" to two parties, so the reader has to infer Mordant from the paragraph before. "Who looks after here?" reads as a dropped word rather than a voice. "His fingers tightened" is a stock beat, and the unopened folder already carries his tension. "Let my people go" has a strong Exodus echo that may pull focus at the quiet close (optional).
- Fix: Cut "with a new urgency". Give L63 a short Mordant beat or tag. Add an object at L93 unless dialect is intended. Cut or vary the finger-tightening beat. Consider a plainer verb at L289. Illustrative only: “Who looks after things here?” and “Then put my people down.”

## Prose findings

### P1 · Minor · Small physical-staging slips

- Where: L25 (harbor freeze), L27, L169 (Tuesday memory), L177 (ferry steps), L197 and L199 (the race)
- Text:
> an enormous orange fork whose nearest point hung over the roof of a fish shop. She hung motionless at its root
> It did not feel different from holding a dropped mug.
> She had hauled herself onto the platform and put her towel over her head.
> twisted her hair into a cap
> the platform sliding across the water while he paddled behind it
> he was still close enough to kick her feet
- Problem: L25 uses "hanging" and "hung" three times in three sentences. "Holding a dropped mug" should be catching one. On Tuesday Elvi has a towel on the swimming platform, but towels are left on the wall (L177). Hair is twisted and then tucked under a cap, not twisted "into" one. If Warren pulled the platform toward himself, he would not be paddling "behind it". He cannot "kick" her feet from behind her.
- Fix: One-word or one-clause repairs for each. Vary the "hung" verbs, say "catching", put her face in her hands or her arms over her head, write "twisted her hair up under her cap", and use "paddled after it" or "watched it slide to him" and "close enough to touch her feet".

### P2 · Minor · Abstract summary in the middle section

- Where: L87, the years of peace
- Text:
> Neighbors had time to become useful to one another. The changes seemed slow to Warren while he lived through them.
- Problem: Emotional and thematic commentary in a section that otherwise works through concrete evidence. The bicycles, the open tills and the marina woman at L143 do this job better.
- Fix: Cut both sentences or replace them with one concrete instance. Nothing else in the paragraph depends on them.

### P3 · Minor · Water-infrastructure texture (policy)

- Where: L143, council meeting
- Text:
> called Warren an obstructive fool because he opposed her marina extension
- Problem: Under `universe/style-guide.md` "No default water infrastructure", a harbor-works dispute counts as incidental water infrastructure in an authorized revision. The breakwater, ferry steps and swimming platform are the harbor the swimming plot needs and can stay.
- Fix: Swap the marina for a non-water civic quarrel, such as a car park, late licensing or a bandstand. Keep the joke's shape: insult at the meeting, favor the following week.

## Continuity and prompt findings

### C1 · Minor · Quarry positions blur before the talk

- Where: L227–L237, arrival at the coach station and walk to the boundary sign
- Text:
> On the other side of the quarry stood Mordant, two low black gunships, and a van with plated windows.
> He stopped by the boundary sign, close enough to speak without carrying his voice.
- Problem: Staging. Candescent is at the quarry mouth and Mordant is across the quarry, yet all three are soon at conversational distance ("Mordant stepped closer", "Candescent put herself between them"). The reader cannot tell when the two parties came up to the road, and the escalation at L259 ("All of them were coming toward the road") depends on knowing where the road is relative to both.
- Fix: Put the two parties on either side of the quarry mouth, or add one clause in which both walk up to the sign as Warren arrives while their escorts stay back.

## Recurring patterns

- Mordant riposting on someone else's line: 3 instances, L45, L243 and L257. The first two ride on genuine lines and are in character; only L257 crosses into the patch (D1).
- Deliberate refrains: "Stay out of my city" (L47, L273), "I'm not joining you" (L53, L287), and Elvi's "There" (L201) echoing Warren's (L33). Keep them exact.
- One clipped rhythm for every speaker: throughout. Mostly earned by the understated register; see the voice map for Mordant.
- Emotion tags and stock beats: L51, L127, L275 and L289. Light; sweep with D4.
- Physical-staging slips: L25, L27, L169, L177, L197 and L199 (P1).
