# Outline

## Story

- Proposed reader-facing title: *The Unfinished Sunset*.
- Premise and central promise: Twenty years after Hisae escaped a witnessed
  sunset duel by crossing the village boundary, the white-haired ronin returns
  to find the whole settlement held in the instant she left: Jinsaku's blade is
  still descending, chaff hangs above the threshing court, and every witness is
  waiting inside a single arrested breath. Crossing back restarts the moment
  but not the sun. Hisae must fight the nonlethal bout to its formally
  acknowledged result, free people who have lost twenty calendar years without
  experiencing them, and face the obligation she once chose flight over.
- Intended reader experience: Eerie stillness should snap into intimate shock
  when twenty years outside prove to have been one heartbeat inside. Let the
  resumed duel deliver clean physical suspense without spectacle: Hisae's
  accumulated skill contends with an older body, while Jinsaku remains the
  young opponent she abandoned. End with release rather than absolution—the
  village has its night back, but its people have been displaced into a future
  Hisae must help them meet.
- Generating force:
  - Mode: Plot-led duel in two clocks. Twenty years of Hisae's rehearsals,
    evasions, and wandering are compressed against the few resumed minutes in
    which everyone else completes the interrupted sunset.
  - Focal attachment or contradiction: Hisae wanted freedom from every master
    after becoming ronin, but she also wanted her home to bless that freedom.
    Jinsaku's old challenge made those wants compete: if he disarmed her, she
    would stay one winter and teach the village's defenders; if she forced him
    from the ring, the village would provision her departure and stop asking
    her to remain. She fled when defeat—and his pity—became imminent.
  - Plot-led wants and useful limit, if applicable: Hisae immediately wants to
    release the village and survive the bout; she also wants to leave before
    the people inside can measure what her freedom cost. Her useful flaw is an
    exit-seeking pride: she prepares explanations and escape routes more
    readily than she accepts a witnessed loss. Twenty years of practice make
    her more exact, but an old knee injury and the habits of her younger body
    limit what that skill can do.
- Counterforce or complication:
  - Source: The local witness-working admits only the absent duelist and will
    not release the held village until the original nonlethal terms produce a
    winner and the loser acknowledges the result. Hisae cannot substitute an
    apology for the bout. Jinsaku's unaged speed, Hisae's aging body, and her
    reflex to flee supply the immediate pressure.
  - Credible inside logic, if a person: Jinsaku is a counterforce, not an
    antagonist. Before the duel he wanted one winter of training for a village
    exposed by the collapse of Hisae's former lord; he also wanted Hisae's
    consent to have a definite meaning. After she returns, he will neither kill
    her nor declare a convenient result. He protects the witnesses' right to
    the promised finish, retains the right to be angry after winning, and sets
    practical terms for what her staying must now accomplish.
  - Pressure or change created: Hisae's rehearsed twenty-year apology meets
    people for whom no time has passed. The only usable answer is embodied and
    immediate: resume the exact descending strike, fight under the old rules,
    and remain present after the result instead of turning remorse into another
    departure.
- Narrative design:
  - POV, tense, distance, and limitation: Close third-person past through
    Hisae. Her attention reads exits, balance, blade angles, and signs of
    weathering; it cannot tell what the arrested villagers experienced or what
    Jinsaku now intends. Keep retrospective explanation subordinate to what her
    older body remembers while moving.
  - Time shape and compression: Begin with a short approach through the
    twenty-years-changed country, then enter a continuous span of roughly ten
    minutes from boundary crossing through the completed duel. Give the first
    half hour after release one compact movement. Use no full flashback; reveal
    the original wager and Hisae's flight through resumed dialogue, repeated
    footwork, and the strike she has practiced answering for twenty years.
  - Information and reveal strategy: Establish the unmoving sunset and held
    matter on approach, but withhold the villagers' subjective duration until
    Jinsaku completes the sentence and cut interrupted a heartbeat ago. Reveal
    the nonlethal terms early enough to make victory and defeat legible. Let
    Hisae initially describe flight as refusal to serve, then show through her
    second reach for the boundary that public defeat and pity were the sharper
    fear. Explain only the local working's observed limits; supply no cosmic
    cause, expert, or connection to another anomaly.
  - Governing movement: Follow the altered road; circle the inaccessible
    sunset boundary; leave an easy exit; cross; block the twenty-year-old cut;
    meet Jinsaku's shock at her white hair; discover that only one heartbeat
    passed inside; offer explanation; hear the original terms restated; draw;
    answer remembered technique with accumulated skill; drive Jinsaku back;
    overreach on the old knee; be disarmed; turn reflexively toward escape;
    stop; acknowledge his victory; endure the sunset's release; tell the
    village how much outside time passed; accept the winter owed and the first
    practical questions it now contains.
- Speculative surplus: The duel's working was activated by living duelists and
  witnesses; braided boundary markers only focused it. When Hisae fled, it held
  bodies, heat, and unfinished motion throughout the village while leaving her
  outside to age; only her return can restart the bout, and only its witnessed
  outcome can synchronize the village with the present. Unrelated to the
  grievance, every interrupted domestic motion resumes at release—a kettle
  boils over, a roof tile completes its fall, mill water hits its waiting wheel,
  and still-warm food remains edible—creating immediate practical disorder
  rather than evidence about guilt or intent.
- Structural distinction: A return story hinged on one discontinuity: a long,
  solitary approach collapses into the continuation of a sentence and sword
  stroke begun twenty years earlier. The village remains a startled ring of
  witnesses rather than a crowd to persuade; the decisive venue is the same
  chaff-filled threshing court, and the climax is an honestly lost nonlethal
  bout plus the loser remaining to name it. There is no technical proof,
  culprit reveal, collective conversion, policy rewrite, or ceremonial image
  of forgiveness.
- Decisive turn, deepening, or recognition: Hisae's practiced counter works and
  briefly drives Jinsaku to the edge of the ring, but she lunges as if her knee
  and reach were still twenty years younger. The knee locks; Jinsaku turns her
  momentum, sends her sword beyond the rush boundary, and asks for the
  acknowledgment the working requires. Hisae takes two reflexive steps toward
  the village edge, where she could repeat the flight, then turns back. She
  states that Jinsaku won and that she owes the promised winter. Only then does
  the fixed sun fall.
- Aftereffect or live uncertainty: Night arrives at once around an unaged
  village whose neighbors, roads, claims, and relatives have had twenty years
  to change. Hisae can report only what she knows and cannot restore the lost
  interval. Jinsaku does not forgive her on cue; he converts the old demand for
  sword lessons into an immediate need for current maps, news, and a safe route
  beyond the boundary. Hisae agrees to stay through winter, but whether the
  village can resettle and whether any relationship with Jinsaku survives
  remain open.
- Opening / ending relation: Open with Hisae mapping every route back out and
  placing her pack where she can seize it without slowing. End after the duel,
  amid resumed kettles and falling chaff, when the villagers begin asking which
  absent people still live. Hisae cannot answer every name; she gives the first
  hard answer she knows, then tells Jinsaku she will bring her current maps
  inside and lead whoever he chooses to the nearest road at first light. The
  story stops on a bounded act of staying, not pardon or restored belonging.

## Beats

1. Hisae follows a road erased by two decades of growth to a village still lit
   by the old sunset. Outside the boundary evening advances normally; inside,
   smoke, water, chaff, and people are motionless. She sets her pack beside the
   route out before approaching the threshing court.
2. The boundary yields only to her. Motion resumes inside without moving the
   sun: Jinsaku's suspended blade finishes its cut, a witness completes a gasp,
   and Hisae blocks the strike she has rehearsed for twenty years. Jinsaku
   falters at her white hair; his unfinished question proves he experienced
   only a heartbeat.
3. Hisae says she was gone twenty years and tries to turn explanation into
   settlement. The villagers test the boundary and find it still closed.
   Jinsaku restates the old bargain and the formal finish—disarmament or removal
   from the rush ring, followed by the loser's acknowledgment—and makes clear
   that the held light has not accepted her return as an outcome.
4. They resume. Hisae's economy and anticipation frustrate Jinsaku's remembered
   combinations, while his youth forces her to ration breath and protect the
   damaged knee. Short exchanges expose their incompatible goods: his demand
   for one winter of defense, her refusal to exchange one dead master for a
   village's permanent claim, and the pride that made her flee an ordinary
   defeat.
5. Hisae uses the answer she spent twenty years perfecting to turn Jinsaku's
   signature descending cut and drives him nearly beyond the ring. Attempting
   to finish, she trusts the reach and knee of her younger self; the joint
   locks, and Jinsaku disarms her cleanly without injuring her.
6. Hisae moves toward the boundary before thought catches her. The fixed light
   begins to tighten again. She stops, returns to the center, names Jinsaku the
   winner, and accepts the winter she promised. The sun drops at once and the
   village's interrupted material life resumes in noisy, mundane confusion.
7. In the first true dark, the villagers learn the date and begin asking about
   roads, kin, fields, and the fallen lord's aftermath. Hisae admits the limits
   of what she knows. Jinsaku rejects an apology offered as payment and asks
   instead what can be done before morning.
8. Hisae commits to bring in her maps, guide a small party to the present road
   at first light, and remain through winter. Questions continue beyond her
   answers; the story ends after her first concrete promise rather than a public
   reconciliation.

## Continuity

- Canon used: This is an undated independent era of the one deep-time world.
  The sunset arrest is one local system among many, culturally understood as a
  witness-working rather than a universal category; living participants
  activate it and nonliving markers only focus it. Violence remains non-graphic
  and its personal and communal consequences remain real.
- Boundaries and unknowns: Establish no placement relative to any named era and
  no connection to existing temporal systems, divinities, or anomalies. The
  origin, wider capabilities, and full cost of the witness-working remain
  unknown; the story needs only its observed village boundary, absent-duelist
  exception, held-state effects, and release condition. Hisae and Jinsaku are
  proposed as new people after noun-history checks; the village remains unnamed
  and no recurring person or place is proposed.
