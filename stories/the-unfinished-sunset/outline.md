# Outline

## Story

- Premise and central promise: White-haired Arveth, an Aderi-born former sword-retainer and literal ronin, returns with the sword she carried away twenty years ago. Inside the arrested sunset, young-bodied Ilessa has lived those years in memory and still claims their interrupted duel. Complete it, free Aderi, and leave Arveth answerable to fully living people.
- Focal pressure or attachment: Arveth returns to surrender the sword and learn who survived, yet reduces danger to routes and exits. She wants release without staying for judgment. Her age limits her reach; Ilessa's unchanged body and twenty years of retained practice embody the opposite cost.
- Counterforce or complication: Ilessa rejects ceremonial surrender and another unilateral solution. Halen will release the mechanism only safely. Neth, adult in experience but child-bodied, wants out immediately; a miller fears irreversible hunger and spoilage; an elder wants Arveth held. No villager speaks for all.
- POV, distance, and information limit: Close third-person past through Arveth, who reads feet, exits, blade wear, repeated work, and her stiffened body before relationships. Loop knowledge arrives through tasks, corrections, tests, and misremembered routines. Her motive emerges piecemeal, not as a cleansing confession.
- Governing movement and time shape: One apparent sunset: entry and reset; a circuit of altered recurring tasks; boundary test; interrupted challenge; contained terrace duel; first real dark. Matter returns while meaning accumulates. Keep the turn private and physical, not public proof.
- Speculative rule or ordinary-world constraint: Aderi's sun-catch is a mineral-pane array charged by living keepers to extend harvest light forty-three minutes. Arveth tried to overload it, flee, and draw a punitive patrol away; Ilessa opposed that unconsented risk. Their blade contact entered the array as opposed living motion. Arveth cut its release braid and crossed the ditch, closing the catch around everyone else. At the horizon notch, bodies and matter reset; memory, learning, relationships, and psychological harm persist. Residents cannot cross or age; repairs, injuries, hunger, and fatigue recur. The excluded combatant alone can enter or flee because her half of the motion remains open. Words, forgiveness, broken contact, substitutes, and dropped weapons cannot discharge it. Ilessa must separate Arveth's original sword from her hand through continuous opposed contact while Halen releases the vanes. Release resumes ordinary time without erasing memory or imposing catch-up aging.

## Voice

- Narrative texture: Tactile, compressive close third notices bleached light, recurring grain, reset nicks, stiff joints, and the worn sword. Let repeated details change meaning; delay history until action requires it.
- Conversational texture: Uneven task instructions, corrections, boundary observations, interrupted challenge forms, ordinary resentment, and silence. Arveth offers road facts and evasions; villagers speak from immediate work, never as a chorus.
- Rhetorical ownership: Arveth owns contingencies; Ilessa tests claims through action; Halen counts mechanisms and risk; Neth reasons from experiments and retained experience; the miller reduces claims to food and night.
- Pressure behavior: Arveth becomes terse, then inarticulate around names and absence. Ilessa moves instead of explaining; Halen repeats safety sequences; Neth accelerates and confuses an early routine.
- Anti-default: Avoid samurai-film pastiche, aphoristic combat, polished confession, opponent-as-exposition, repeated demands to finish, or a final forgiveness speech. Keep the invented culture material and local.

## Beats

1. Arveth enters with the old sword. The road rejects outsiders but admits her. Villagers withhold her name; unchanged faces and repeated challenge preparation expose the break.
2. The village resets at the horizon notch. Halen checks restored vanes while residents resume, abandon, or vary sunset tasks. Returning grain and wounds contrast with changed skills and relationships.
3. Child-bodied Neth demonstrates the boundary's return and adult reasoning. A miller presses for release; Halen warns that a bad discharge could destroy their recoverable starting state.
4. Ilessa resumes the challenge's opening movement, then stops when Arveth offers surrender without opposition. Tests and fragmented corrections establish failed shortcuts; Ilessa acts for present safety, not old code.
5. Pressure reveals that Arveth deserted rather than guide a punitive patrol home, then used the catch and her flight as an unauthorized decoy. She drew the patrol away but chose twenty years of uncertainty over returning. Ilessa supplies only witnessed facts.
6. Halen prepares the vanes while residents choose shelter or work. Ilessa's accumulated practice meets Arveth's age and road adaptability in a non-graphic duel. Arveth's exit opens again.
7. Arveth turns back and contests rather than seeking death or absolution. Ilessa earns the disarm; Halen releases the charge. Sunset advances without determining forgiveness or later judgment.
8. In ordinary dark, Ilessa keeps the sword and Arveth stays under watch to help secure the catch. Neth crosses successfully, then returns by choice for the night's work.

## Continuity

- Canon used: One deep-time world permits unrelated local systems. Living keepers supply magic; inert panes only store and shape it. Personhood and consequence persist. The sun-catch is unrelated to every recurring temporal system.
- Boundaries and unknowns: `Ronin` means a masterless former sword-retainer in an invented, non-Japanese upland culture; avoid imported ritual, accent, exoticized honor, and samurai-film shorthand. The loop affects only Aderi's captured forty-three minutes. Its origin stays unknown. Release restores neither lost years nor forgiveness. Violence remains non-graphic and accessible.
