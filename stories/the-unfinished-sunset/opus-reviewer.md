# Opus review: The Unfinished Sunset

Independent audit for a later editing pass. It is not a `review.md` verdict,
does not change canon state, and does not authorize edits by itself.

## Verdict

- Dialogue: REVISE — one line reports an experiment that could not have happened, and the long middle turn turns into rule briefings and correction tags.
- Prose: PASS — concrete, varied and free of em dashes; one word about the sun misleads.
- Continuity and prompt: PASS — the prompt is fully delivered at 3,579 words; only small reset-rule slips.
- Edit scope: targeted-dialogue
- Existing review.md said: Verdict PASS, "Dialogue: PASS"; disagree. It missed the impossible line at L143 and says there is no chorus, but Neth works as one.

Some of this dialogue is genuinely good. Arveth's road facts work as a shield, Ilessa speaks almost entirely through her body, and the duel and ending run on Halen's callouts and wordless choices. The problems cluster in the first full turn (L87–L233), where the village explains its loop to Arveth. In that stretch Neth becomes the rulebook, the elder exists only to suggest wrong fixes, and nearly every exchange ends on a word-correction or a dry tag ("She needs the safe version," "Words don't enter the rail," "No moths today," "That wasn't the question," "Road fact"). As a result, a child stuck at nine for twenty years, a hungry miller and an old keeper all sound like the same calm clerk, even with the person who trapped them standing in front of them. The edit pass should cut explanations of things the reset has already shown, strip the correction tags, and let each person's loss (Neth's childhood, the miller's hunger, the elder's fear) show in how they speak.

## Edit guardrails

- Prompt: a white-haired ronin returns after twenty years to a village held under the same sunset, everyone inside waiting for her to finish the duel she fled; a complete story that resolves it; 2,500–4,000 words (now about 3,580, so cuts are safe, but stay above 2,500); broadly accessible, with non-graphic violence. The active craft profile is prospective-2026-08-21, and the rewrite request is a dialogue review.
- Mechanism the plot depends on: the panes are inert stone and living keepers supply the charge (LOCKED "Living agency and magic" in `universe/rules.md`, so do not give the catch a will of its own beyond Neth's joke). The catch holds the opposed living motion of the interrupted duel. Release needs continuous opposed contact until Ilessa separates Arveth's original sword from her hand, while Halen opens the west pin and then the three lower vanes through the bridged release braid. At the notch, bodies and matter reset while memory and skill persist. Residents cannot cross the ditch; Arveth can. Release brings no catch-up aging.
- Neth uses they/them throughout. The miller and the elder are unnamed; there is no need to name them.
- Do not add a forgiveness or reconciliation speech, a thesis line, or explanations from Ilessa. Her last words are orders, and the story ends on carrying grain. Both choices work.

## Dialogue findings

### D1 · Blocking · Experiments that could not have happened

- Where: L143, threshing floor, Neth listing failed shortcuts after the dropped sword
- Text:
> We made a handle and had her pull against it
> We had Ilessa hold your wrist and take it.
- Problem: Ground/knowledge and Ground/referent. Arveth and her sword were outside the catch for twenty years, so nobody could have had Ilessa hold Arveth's wrist. The line states an impossible event as fact. In "had her pull against it" the "her" can't be recovered: Arveth is "you", and Ilessa is named separately a few sentences later, so the reader can't tell who pulled or what the handle was fixed to. The list only makes sense as tests with stand-ins, and the text never says that.
- Fix: Rewrite the list so every item was possible without Arveth. Make clear these were stand-ins for her half of the motion, name who played her (an existing villager), and name who pulled. Three items are enough. Keep "We asked the catch nicely" only if the list stays. Illustrative only: We had the miller stand where you stood with a borrowed sword, and Ilessa took it off her.

### D2 · Major · Ilessa's refusal is readable only through the narration

- Where: L155–L163, first resisted handover on the threshing floor
- Text:
> Arveth shifted her grip loose, intending to let the sword be pulled away.
> Ilessa tapped the flat of Arveth's sword with two fingers, then pointed to Arveth's feet.
> Her meaning was plain: she would not rehearse another escape disguised as cooperation.
- Problem: Ground (meaning decoded only by narration). Ilessa answers "You need it out of my hand." with a gesture the reader can't read: tapping the flat of the blade and pointing at the feet. The narration then has to translate it, and the translation, an abstract judgment about escape and cooperation, is more than the gesture carries. "Her meaning was plain" admits the opposite. This is the first direct Arveth–Ilessa exchange, and its meaning should live in the exchange.
- Fix: Cut the "Her meaning was plain" sentence. Make the gesture point at something visible that means escape. Arveth's back foot, already turned downhill, would also set up L285 ("Her feet were already turned correctly."). Or give Ilessa two or three plain words. Illustrative only: Ilessa tapped the flat of Arveth's sword, then pointed at Arveth's back foot, already turned downhill.

### D3 · Major · The reckoning at the ditch plays as a cross-examination

- Where: L193–L217, at the boundary ditch, Arveth's unasked report on the patrol
- Text:
> “Did you lose them?”
> I crossed the eastern river before the flood came through.
> “That wasn't the question,” Neth said.
> “That accounts for eleven days and a winter.”
> “Road fact.”
- Problem: Ground/uptake plus procedure routine. The miller asks whether Arveth lost the patrol, and Arveth's answer (across the river ahead of the flood) means yes. So Neth's "That wasn't the question" corrects nothing she said. It is a made-up correction that pushes Arveth to "They didn't come back here.", which answers a third question: whether the patrol reached Aderi. Then Neth, who has been nine for twenty years because of this woman, replies like an auditor ("accounts for", "Road fact"). The story's main reckoning becomes a hearing: claim, ruling, next claim. The exchange does move, and Arveth's "Yes." lands, but it gets there in the house register, and Neth's own stake never reaches Neth's speech.
- Fix: Cut "That wasn't the question." Let "They didn't come back here." answer the miller directly, with Arveth defending the decoy. Replace at least one of Neth's audit labels with something from Neth's own twenty years (the wall, the salt shelf, the calculation), said less neatly. If Arveth's second road fact stays, let silence and the loom at L219 answer it. Keep the miller's "You could have checked.", Arveth's "Yes." and Ilessa's closing lines exactly. Illustrative only: "I sat on that wall every turn the first year."

### D4 · Major · Neth as the story's rulebook

- Where: L91 (right after the first reset), L105 (the miller repeats it), L165 (after the refused handover); also L143 and L187–L191
- Text:
> “First boundary condition,” Neth continued, fast now.
> So do the hunger and fatigue we had at closure.
> “We can eat. The food comes back. So does the empty feeling.
> “If you're pretending, the opposed motion ends before the separation. We measured the pane response.”
- Problem: Exposition / explanatory chorus. Neth gives four rule briefings in one turn. The first is a numbered manual ("First... Second... Third") delivered right after the story has shown the reset: the sack reseals, the peg breaks, the blanket returns, Neth runs for the salt shelf. Much of L91 repeats what the reader just watched. The miller then repeats the hunger rule at L105, and L165 explains a refusal Ilessa has already made with her body. Neth becomes a delivery system for mechanics, and the grievance that makes Neth interesting gets crowded out.
- Fix: At L91, drop the numbered frame and keep only what Arveth couldn't have just seen: that she wasn't inside, and that they remember. Show "fast now" in the syntax (run-on, self-interrupting), not in ordinals. The fear line can stay if it becomes Neth's own fear rather than a general rule. Give the hunger rule only to the miller; her version is better and belongs to her. Cut L165 or shrink it to a few words so Ilessa's refusal stands. Keep the boundary demonstration at L185–L191, which is shown rather than told, but trim its list.

### D5 · Major · Correction ping-pong and last-word tags set the village's rhythm

- Where: L93–L103 (Halen/Neth), L145–L147 (elder/Halen), L167–L173 (moths); lighter at L109–L111, L179, L247, L255
- Text:
> “She needs the short version.”
> “She needs the safe version.
> “I stopped conducting them.”
> “Words don't enter the rail,” Halen said.
> “No moths today.”
> “I didn't bring any.”
- Problem: Identical Voices / Punchline Patch / Tennis Match. Through the middle turn, most exchanges end with someone correcting a word ("short" to "safe", "experimenting" to "conducting") or landing a dry tag. The elder's lines (L67, L145, L179) exist to propose a naive fix for someone else to correct. Arveth's "How?" (L167) sets up the moth list, and Halen's "No moths today." sets up Neth's comeback. The result is that keeper, child, miller and elder all speak in the same calm, precise register, twenty years into a trap. The moth bit also comes straight after Ilessa's refusal and pulls the scene's focus away from her.
- Fix: Sweep these rather than rewriting everything.
  - L93–L103: keep Halen's "Neth." and one line showing he distrusts Neth's experiments. Cut the short/safe and experimenting/conducting word games.
  - L145–L147: cut the elder's line, or give the elder a want of their own (fear that Arveth will leave again, or a need to see her answer in front of everyone). Halen can answer with the refrain or not at all.
  - L167–L173: keep "once with moths. Moths were unreliable." if the list survives D4. Drop Arveth's "How?" setup and the two-line tag.
  - L247: cut "If there is an after."
  - L109–L111, L179 and L255 can stay once the heavier ones go; one dry rejoinder per speaker is plenty.
  - Do not replace the cut tags with new aphorisms.

### D6 · Minor · The smoke line and day-words in a nightless loop

- Where: L53 (the child on the mill wall), L101, L171
- Text:
> The smoke is thick today, but it was thick at the start, so that doesn't help.
> “You proposed one yesterday.”
- Problem: Ground/referent. The reader has no source for the smoke. It is hinted at L193 but never explained, and "so that doesn't help" has no clear object: the estimate? an excuse? In a place where every turn resets, "today" implies the smoke changes from turn to turn. "yesterday" (L101) and "today" (L171) may be the villagers' habit of counting sleeps, but nothing says so.
- Fix: Keep the fussy hedge ("Maybe five and three quarters"), which is good Neth. Cut the smoke clause, or make it concrete (what the smoke hides). Decide once whether villagers still say "day" for a sleep; one narrated clause settles it. Otherwise use "last turn".

### D7 · Minor · "What closes when the blades meet" doesn't say what she wants

- Where: L177–L181, after the moths, before the walk to the ditch
- Text:
> “I want to know what closes when the blades meet.”
- Problem: Ground/referent. The catch closed when Arveth crossed the ditch, not when the blades met, and "what closes" has no clear object (the boundary? the catch? her way out?). The reader can later guess she was checking her exit, since she uses it mid-duel, but the line doesn't work as a readable pretext either.
- Fix: Give her a concrete request that fits her habit of reading exits, or cut the line and let her follow Ilessa, who is already walking downhill. Illustrative only: "Show me where it stops you."

### D8 · Minor · Arveth's spring timeline and no-smoke reasoning

- Where: L215 and L225, at the ditch
- Text:
> “In spring I was east of the river.”
> “I came within two ridges the next spring,” Arveth said.
> I saw no smoke. I thought the catch had failed. Or released.
- Problem: Ground/time and logic. "In spring" followed by "the next spring" reads either as the same spring, which is a contradiction (east of the river and two ridges from Aderi), or as a year later, which is unclear. No smoke fits a catch that is still hiding the village, so "released" doesn't follow. The reader can't tell whether this is a real inference or an excuse she knows is thin. The miller's "You could have checked." lands best if it is clearly an excuse.
- Fix: Make the second spring clearly later ("The spring after that"). Make the smoke inference plainly self-serving rather than muddled, for instance by dropping "Or released". Keep "Yes." exactly.

### D9 · Minor · The cost of a botched release can't be read

- Where: L107 (Halen to the miller), L141 (after the dropped sword)
- Text:
> “Then there's no return to recover.”
> Opposed pressure, unbroken until separation.
- Problem: L107 is the only statement of what a bad release costs, and "no return to recover" doesn't parse cleanly. Is there no reset to recover from, or nothing to return to? Without it, Halen's stop conditions in the duel carry little risk. At L141 Halen glosses his refrain "joined pressure" as "opposed pressure", which gives two names to one condition, and a reader may take them for two requirements.
- Fix: Give Halen one plain clause that names the cost (the outline's intent is that a bad discharge would lose the reset they can recover from) and keep his register flat. Use one term consistently, or let "joined" be his shorthand and explain it once.

## Prose findings

### P1 · Minor · A "fixed" sun that moves

- Where: L53 and L75, first minutes in Aderi
- Text:
> A child sitting on the mill wall checked the fixed sun against a split in the western ridge.
> The sun touched the notch.
- Problem: The narration calls the sun fixed, then moves it to the notch within six minutes. The reader needs to understand early that the held sunset replays and slides toward the notch each turn, and "fixed" says the opposite.
- Fix: Replace "fixed" with a word for the held sun, or add a clause near L17–L19 saying the held sun slides the same short arc every turn.

## Continuity and prompt findings

### C1 · Minor · The reset returns grain to a "torn" sack

- Where: L237, the second reset
- Text:
> The next reset returned Neth to the salt errand and the miller's grain to the torn sack.
> The sack sealed around it.
- Problem: At the first reset the sack reseals (L77), and Arveth sees it "unbroken" (L105). Calling it torn at L237 contradicts the reset rule.
- Fix: Change it to "the falling sack" or just "its sack".

### C2 · Minor · Arveth's bruise used as proof of release

- Where: L329, after the sun passes the notch
- Text:
> A bruise darkened across Arveth's knuckles and did not clear.
- Problem: Arveth was never subject to the reset (L89; L91, "You weren't inside."), so her bruise would never have cleared. Listing it among the signs that resets have stopped implies otherwise.
- Fix: Swap in a villager's injury that would once have reset (a shaving nick on Halen's hand, say), or keep Arveth's bruise but move it out of the list of evidence.

### C3 · Minor · Water infrastructure as texture

- Where: L191, Neth's list of failed crossings
- Text:
> Carried, pulled, unconscious, on a cart, under the culvert, above it with a ladder
- Problem: Policy. `universe/style-guide.md` "No default water infrastructure" (CANON) covers drainage works even as passing references, and the prompt didn't ask for them. The culvert also turns the dry boundary ditch into a drain.
- Fix: Replace it with a non-water route, such as a tunnel dug under the ditch or a rope over it. The dry "boundary ditch" itself is fine.
