# Provisional direction

Close third person, initially allowing two simultaneous sensations inside a single sentence; the grammar can gradually make room for two subjects. The chosen central relationship is the woman's emerging relation to her other self, still able to feel across the split. This is an interpretation, not a requested separate bond. The conflict is her understandable wish to be made whole against evidence that wholeness can no longer mean returning to one. Administrators initially intend a repair, not an execution; the system's refusal is a discovery neither side has engineered.

- An ordinary afternoon: she has settled into a floral chair by her bookshelves with a book she has long meant to finish. Her cheek rests on warm knuckles. At that same instant she stands on a high terrace in a fantasy kingdom, beneath a sky with slow rivers of light. Her right hand disperses into blue butterflies over the book; she also feels those wings moving above the terrace. Neither world replaces the other. Her first actions establish real simultaneous consequences: she catches a slipping book while the other hand closes on stone, and each touch arrives through the same startled awareness.
- She explores instead of merely waiting for a diagnosis. In the home she tests whether closing her eyes removes the kingdom; on the terrace she follows her butterflies into open air, discovering their dispersed body can fly while her warm body stays seated. The flight gives the kingdom weight and distance: crowded roofs fall away, bells pass below, the spired palace rises through light. The home remains equally present, an arm braced against the chair and a page stirring in wingwind. Pleasure and vertigo travel both ways; the decision to fly begins in one place and meets resistance in the other.
- She contacts Oasis support. An administrator appears in both simulations, explains the doubled assignment, and prepares reintegration with her relieved agreement. During the preparations she encounters the first unmistakable difference: the woman in the chair wants the flight to stop, while the woman over the kingdom deliberately turns farther into it. Each can feel the other's fear and exhilaration, but cannot choose for her. They are not distinguished by homebody/adventurer roles; both wanted the flight and both fear dissolution. The difference is who gets to make this next turn.
- The prepared reintegration command is refused: two separate people are present. The administrator checks again, expecting a damaged record, and receives two independent responses through what is still a shared field of sensation. The system recognizes lives that have already begun; its message does not create them. Returning to one would require overwriting a living person's choices. The woman who asked to be repaired must now answer someone she cannot dismiss as a stray thought.
- Both choose to continue, and separately choose to retain their shared awareness. The administrator registers the unprecedented arrangement instead of forcing a merger or severing the link. Their bodies become stable enough to inhabit both worlds freely, with blue wings still carrying sensation between them. End in an active double moment: one woman turns the book's page while the other turns above the luminous kingdom, each aware of the other's movement without mistaking it for her own. The new thing born is two people who can still experience one consciousness opening in two directions. The term *bifurcated* belongs to this discovery, not a final technical explanation that cancels either person's reality.

The imagined limits are local to this story: sensation can cross the fault, voluntary action belongs to each endpoint, and reintegration cannot merge separate people without overwriting one. They serve the birth in the prompt rather than adding a battle, a cure quest, or a new mandatory mythology.
