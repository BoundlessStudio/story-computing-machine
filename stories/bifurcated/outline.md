# Provisional direction

Close third person, initially allowing two simultaneous sensations inside a single sentence; the grammar can gradually make room for two subjects. The chosen central relationship is the woman's emerging relation to her other self, still able to feel across the split. This is an interpretation, not a requested separate bond. The conflict is her understandable wish to be made whole against evidence that wholeness can no longer mean returning to one. Administrators initially intend a repair, not an execution; the system's refusal is a discovery neither side has engineered.

- An ordinary afternoon: she rests on floral cushions by her bookshelves with a book she has long meant to finish, cheek on warm knuckles. Simultaneously she stands on a high terrace in a fantasy kingdom beneath slow rivers of light. Her left shoulder and the edge of her face dissolve into blue butterflies over the cushions; she feels their wings opening above the terrace. She catches the slipping book and grips stone. Neither world replaces the other, and neither body yet feels like somebody else.
- She explores instead of merely waiting for a diagnosis. Opening the home window and venturing to the terrace rail are one intention: she wants to fly and fears the height, recognizing both impulses as her own. She discovers the dispersed body can fly. Crowded roofs fall away and a palace rises from its cloud island. A royal wind-pennant parts to admit her among the kingdom's trained sky-riders, whose living mounts smell of hot feathers. One rider calls her a flight-path instead of a lady: the fantasy setting responds to the particular body she has. The home stays equally present through cushion warmth and the open window's breeze.
- She contacts Oasis support when two fields of vision will not settle. An administrator, assisted by a second voice, explains the doubled assignment and prepares reintegration with her relieved agreement. During preparation, the woman at home wants the flight to stop so that she can see her own hand clearly; the woman over the kingdom deliberately turns farther into it. This is the first action arriving without a decision she recognizes as her own, unlike her earlier mixed desire and fear. Each feels the other's fear and exhilaration but cannot choose for her.
- The prepared command is refused: two separate people are present. Asked which one authorized the repair, both answer “I did,” for different immediate reasons. There is no original who can dismiss the other as an extra. The system recognizes lives already begun; its message does not create them. The woman at home asks whether she would have got those other choices back. The answer is no: the proposed operation assumed only one chooser existed. Her requested wholeness has become impossible, rather than morally shameful.
- Enact their response before naming the new category. The woman in flight lands without being compelled, letting the other feel stone under their divided attention again. The woman at home then asks her to go up once more. What frightened her was an action arriving as though it were her own command; she wants that astonishing flight when she can recognize whose choice it is. The sky-woman does not immediately obey: she watches a rider thread the palace pennant and chooses her own route after him. Meanwhile the home-woman begins reading; the sky-woman, who remembers wanting to finish that book, asks her to wait at a sentence she cannot quite attend to while turning. Each now encounters something familiar arriving through somebody else's independent attention. Neither can obtain that experience alone.
- The administrators find no previous case of two people with this still-continuous field of sensation. Rather than promising a cure or effortless new bodies, they ask each woman separately whether she wants the simulations paused while they investigate. Each asks for the afternoon to continue: these are first acts of two people already born, not permissions that create them. The administrators record the unprecedented bifurcated consciousness. End within their unequal, physically simultaneous movement: a page held for someone crossing a sky, a turn above the palace taken by someone whose joy cannot be commanded. Two people remain distinct inside an experience neither has to call a mistake.

The imagined limits are local to this story: sensation crosses the fault, voluntary action has become distinct at each endpoint, and the proposed reintegration assumes one chooser where there are now two. They serve the birth in the prompt rather than adding a battle, a cure quest, or a new mandatory mythology.
