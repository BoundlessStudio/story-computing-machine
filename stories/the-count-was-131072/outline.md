# Outline

## Story

- Premise and central promise: Centuries after the western-bay invasion,
  Solstice Evening Bell visits a museum wing about the event and reads every
  caption before deciding whether to answer any of them. The wing gets the
  heroic silhouette right and the history wrong: it rounds 131,072 nonliving
  frames to “countless,” puts a sword in her hand, treats the crown's twelve
  donated final spells as twelve captive spirits, and repeatedly mispronounces
  her name. Fulfill the prompt through the last living witness's encounter with
  herself as an exhibit. Solstice must distinguish what only she can correct
  from what her own airborne, grief-shaped memory never witnessed, then choose
  whether and how to leave testimony without turning her identity into a public
  proof, a complete archive, or one more heroic performance.
- Intended reader experience: Begin with dry annoyance and the uncanny comedy
  of hearing a living person's name mishandled by her own audio guide, deepen
  through exact numerical and material counter-memory, and let awe coexist with
  resentment rather than restoring the battle as spectacle. The reader should
  feel the seduction of the museum's clean story, the specific grief hidden by
  “alone,” and the surprise that some small ground-level records preserve truths
  Solstice cannot supply. End with bounded agency rather than correction-as-
  triumph: the public wing remains wrong, but its subject stops being wholly
  silent inside it.
- Generating force:
  - Mode: Observational accumulation. Each caption makes a claim; Solstice's
    attention supplies a counter-caption in number, bodily absence, or remembered
    relation. Movement comes from the claims changing kind—from rounding, to
    invented object, to erased donors, to the word “alone,” to records made on
    the shore—and from Solstice's idea of an adequate correction growing less
    simple as she proceeds.
  - Focal attachment or contradiction: Solstice is attached to the exact count
    because royal hearing once made every frame a position she had to hold, and
    to the twelve adult donors as people whose freely given final works were
    neither possessions nor surviving souls. Yet she also wants memories that
    are still hers not to become raw material for another commemorative machine.
    She resents myth for replacing fact while knowing that her own perspective,
    however exact, is not the whole history.
  - Plot-led wants and useful limit, if applicable: This is not antagonist-led
    plot. Its local movement is attention followed by a choice: Solstice arrives
    intending only to inspect the entire wing anonymously and leave, while each
    error increases the pressure to contribute one bounded account. Her useful
    limit is the same instinct that once served the battle—she trusts exact
    counting and controlled force more readily than partial testimony. At first
    she treats “131,072” and “no sword” as sufficient corrections; the shore
    archive makes her admit that exactness needs a declared vantage and boundary.
- Counterforce or complication:
  - Source: The museum's account is persuasive because it is not uniformly
    false. Its simplified queen, sword, and “countless” enemy make one legible
    visual story, while mundane evacuation records preserve details outside
    Solstice's attention above the bay. Centuries have also left her as the only
    living first-hand source, so any correction can be dismissed as an
    unverifiable visitor claim or elevated into a new myth merely because she
    made it.
  - Credible inside logic, if a person: There is no antagonist. No curator or
    attendant appears as an opposing person. The wing reflects ordinary
    pressures of incomplete evidence, inherited imagery, compressed labels,
    and a public appetite for a weapon-bearing champion. The audio guide is a
    nonliving playback system with a bad pronunciation entry, not an aware
    insult or truth engine.
  - Pressure or change created: Solstice cannot make a complete public history
    simply by remembering harder, and a display of surviving power would prove
    only that she is formidable, not that her account is true. Remaining silent,
    however, leaves the museum with no direct record that the frames were
    counted, the sword was invented, and the crown held works rather than
    people. Her credible action must therefore be narrow, attributable, and
    explicit about what she did and did not witness.
- Narrative design:
  - POV, tense, distance, and limitation: Close second-person present through
    Solstice. The “you” makes her simultaneously the body moving through the
    wing, the museum's grammatical subject, and the person being addressed by
    an automated voice. Confirm her identity in the opening movement rather
    than withholding it as a twist. Stay intimate with irritation, remembered
    sensory absences where crown tones used to be, and the discipline of not
    demonstrating her retained force; do not claim perfect memory or enter the
    minds of donors, evacuees, curators, or invaders.
  - Time shape and compression: One late-afternoon visit continuing a little
    past the main galleries' closing cue. Move forward through five exhibit
    registers—entrance tableau, count wall, crown case, “alone” reconstruction,
    and shore archive—then into a small testimony alcove near the exit. Render
    the captions and Solstice's immediate reactions in scene. Let battle memory
    arrive only in short material shards keyed to each error: numerical
    reductions, straight routes between chimes, the irreversible silences of
    burnt tones, storm and altitude, the remaining 4,096, and the central
    release. Do not redraft the original battle as an extended flashback.
  - Information and reveal strategy: Put “countless,” the false sword, and the
    mispronunciation on the page early. Establish the canonical count and the
    guide's subject as Solstice before suspense can depend on identity concealment.
    Let each major track reintroduce her with the same plausible wrong
    pronunciation; repetition, not increasingly comic variants, supplies the
    abrasion.
    Reveal the more consequential distortion gradually: she was the only living
    combatant over the bay, but the crown carried twelve freely donated,
    nonliving final spells whose donors had passed into the underlying afterlife.
    The decisive new information is not secret lore but a limit of viewpoint:
    shore records describe the evacuated roads, weather, and sounds from below
    that Solstice never perceived while royal hearing tracked the host. Keep the
    invaders' builders and motives unknown, and let no artifact, scanner, or
    power authenticate her.
  - Governing movement: Accept the guide; hear the name wrong; face the
    sword-bearing silhouette; read “countless”; answer it privately with
    131,072; follow the museum's rounded battle stages while recalling the exact
    reductions; reach the crown case; reject its captive-spirit account without
    summoning the donors into scene; pass the triumphant sword reconstruction;
    feel each destroyed tone as absence rather than relive spectacle; read the
    shore archive; discover truths held by people whose experience she never
    shared; finish every remaining caption; approach the optional witness
    station; decline a complete life history and identity demonstration; record
    a bounded correction; mark her relationship to the subject as “self”; set
    narrow use permissions; leave before learning whether the museum accepts it.
- Speculative surplus: The museum's reusable directional-audio bead casts a
  narrow private sound field just behind the wearer's ear and triggers tracks
  by floor position. It is convenient but spatially fallible: crossing a room
  too quickly can make two tracks overlap, and adjacent beads can briefly leak
  into one another, so visitors use a common two-finger collar gesture to show
  they are listening and museums paint slow lanes at thresholds. The device
  cannot identify Solstice, evaluate testimony, recover memory, or detect
  truth; its failure to pronounce her name is a stored-language error, not
  agency. A late track overlap should supply one mundane irritation unrelated
  to the history rather than solve or symbolize it.
- Structural distinction: A forward museum promenade built from mismatched
  forms of record: heroic image, rounded count, object label, immersive
  reconstruction, ordinary shore archive, and finally a constrained testimony
  form. Unlike a recursive room comedy, descending memory treatment, permutation
  proof, or violent reckoning, the action does not repeat or widen; authority
  changes as Solstice passes from what the museum asserts, to what she alone
  remembers, to what others recorded beyond her view. The decisive venue is a
  cramped, unstaffed feedback alcove after the exhibition proper. There is no
  public power display, crowd conversion, curator confrontation, immediate
  institutional rewrite, recovered weapon, or closing monument.
- Decisive turn, deepening, or recognition: In the shore archive, Solstice
  begins by auditing another caption for errors and instead finds an ordinary
  evacuation account whose small particulars are compatible with the battle
  but absent from her memory: how the empty harbor roads sounded below the
  storm, when the last civilian transport cleared the ridge, and how little of
  the fighting could be seen from ground level. The record cannot correct her
  count, but neither can she replace it. She recognizes that “last witness”
  names a responsibility and a limit, not possession of the whole past. In the
  exit alcove she records only what her vantage can support: 131,072 nonliving
  frames; no sword; one living combatant, not one unaided person; twelve freely
  given final works, not twelve captive minds. When the form asks her relation
  to the subject, she selects “self” without adding a display of power or asking
  anyone present to believe her.
- Aftereffect or live uncertainty: The recording enters an unverified accession
  queue under narrow permissions. The wing will reopen with the same plaques,
  false sword, rounded count, and mispronounced guide; the museum may reject the
  claim, investigate it, excerpt it badly, or someday treat it with care.
  Solstice withholds the donors' names and memories not needed for these
  corrections, and no new witness inherits her certainty. She has not defeated
  myth or surrendered her private past, but she has made one usable distinction
  between silence and total disclosure.
- Opening / ending relation: Open when the guide speaks Solstice's name for her,
  wrongly, as it directs her toward the sword silhouette and the first use of
  “countless”; she lets the machine continue because she intends only to read.
  End after she has spoken her own name correctly into the raw recording and
  chosen “self” plus limited quotation permission. The automatic transcript
  still renders the pronunciation badly, and she leaves it attached to her
  audible voice rather than turning a software correction into the story's
  victory. The opening gives the museum sole control of address; the ending adds
  a bounded first-person source without pretending the public account has
  changed.

## Beats

1. At the new wing's entrance, Solstice accepts the directional-audio bead and
   hears its first confident mispronunciation of “Solstice Evening Bell.” A
   monumental silhouette gives the queen a sword. The opening establishes that
   this is Solstice herself, visiting without announcing that fact, and that she
   has committed to reading every caption before acting.
2. The count wall reduces eight wedges of 16,384 frames to “countless invaders.”
   The next track repeats the same wrong name. Solstice silently reconstructs
   131,072 and the early reductions. Brief
   sensory shards contrast the guide's smooth battle music with royal hearing's
   positions, false pulses, and the first permanent missing tone; avoid a full
   combat reprise.
3. At the crown case, a replica and caption imply that twelve dead warriors
   endured inside the queen and became her arsenal. Solstice remembers twelve
   adults and the compact's release clause but does not supply new names or
   biographical canon. The correction that matters is ontological and ethical:
   their souls and minds passed on; only their freely donated final spells were
   stored and later destroyed.
4. The immersive central reconstruction turns the battle into one sword stroke
   delivered from shore. Solstice's memory supplies altitude, fixed chime
   routes, storm, progressive losses, 4,096 synchronized final frames, and the
   crushing of the central resonator through her living agency. She refuses the
   temptation to interrupt the projection with retained force; spectacle would
   reinforce the museum's preferred queen.
5. In a quieter archive section, ordinary evacuation materials describe what
   happened below: empty harbor roads, the last route beyond the ridge, weather,
   and sounds from a battle the shore could barely see. Solstice cannot confirm
   these details from personal perception, but they fit the event and expose
   the boundary of her exactness. “Alone” becomes two facts the museum has
   collapsed: she fought with no other living combatant beside her, and she did
   not fight unaided.
6. Solstice finishes the remaining captions as promised. Closing tones begin;
   two audio tracks overlap because she crosses a threshold too quickly, adding
   a mundane failure rather than an omen. Near the exit, an optional station
   invites family stories and witness testimony. She nearly passes it because
   both categories seem built for the dead.
7. In the unstaffed alcove, Solstice records a narrow correction, refuses the
   prompts for a complete life story or demonstrative authentication, selects
   “self” as her relationship to the subject, and limits quotation while
   allowing preservation of the raw voice. The transcript still mishandles her
   name. She leaves before accession staff respond and before the museum can
   turn either acceptance or disbelief into a public climax.

## Continuity

- Canon used: Solstice alone defeated exactly 131,072 nonliving autonomous
  frames over an evacuated unnamed western bay; the host arrived in eight
  wedges through a stationary rupture and fell by established numerical stages.
  Her nonliving thirteen-tone crown stored twelve adult donors' freely made
  final spells and always required Solstice's living agency. Donor souls and
  minds passed into the underlying afterlife. Burning the twelve orbiting tones
  and crushing the central resonator permanently destroyed the stored works,
  royal hearing, and all demonstrated crown techniques, while Solstice retained
  her identity, offices, and immense unharmonized innate force. She personally
  remembers the donors, her historical circles later faded without a successor,
  and the battle precedes the later Glass-Sea era.
- Boundaries and unknowns: The prompt authoritatively establishes that the event
  is ancient by the museum visit, Solstice is the last living witness, the later
  commemoration calls it the “Battle of the Bay,” and she never carried a sword.
  Keep the museum, its city and country, the battle's civil date and Galactic
  Cycle coordinate, Solstice's exact age and longevity mechanism, her birth and
  pre-circle life, and her detailed post-crown capabilities unnamed or
  unresolved. Do not restore any crown faculty; place donor persons inside the
  destroyed crown; make the nonliving frames sentient; establish invader origin,
  motive, recovered debris, or later attacks; connect the rupture to another
  crossing system; or give Solstice authority beyond the faded circle
  tradition. The museum's later errors, interface, and unverified testimony are
  story-local facts, not evidence that any institution accepted new canon.
