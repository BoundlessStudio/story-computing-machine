# Canon brief

## Hard constraints

- **Disposition: CLEAR TO PLAN.** No canon ruling is required. The repository
  establishes no universal prophecy, fate, foreknowledge, or predestination
  system, so this story must present its binding prophecy as a defined local
  mechanism rather than as an already-canonical law of reality.
  [universe/rules.md — “System categories”; “Records, falsehood, and historical
  traces”]
- The story occurs somewhere in the one physical world that persists through
  deep time. A new culture or era may be an independent entry point, but it
  must not be framed as a separate planet or plane if it is identified as
  Earth, Ravel, or the Glass-Sea world. The latter two labels also carry
  chronology limits: the Glass-Sea era is long after the modern-like era, while
  Ravel's position remains unresolved. [universe/premise.md — “The deep-time
  shared world”; universe/locations.md — “The world across eras”;
  universe/timeline.md — “Established era order”]
- A prophecy or prediction cannot function as an arbitrary continuity patch.
  Its source, scope, binding force, breach condition, and causal consequences
  must be coherent on the page. Contradictory records, alternate timelines, or
  mythic retellings do not automatically prove that the prophecy was real.
  [universe/rules.md — “Records, falsehood, and historical traces”;
  universe/style-guide.md — “Continuity and evidence discipline”]
- Magic, technology, and divinity are viewpoint-culture labels for real
  systems, not mutually exclusive objective substances. The prophecy's
  mechanism may be described in any of those terms, but the story should not
  claim that its culture's label is a universal cosmological category.
  [universe/rules.md — “System categories”; universe/glossary.md — “Magic,
  technology, and divinity”]
- If a living actor uses magic to bind or break the prophecy, that use must
  obey the locked rule that only life can use magic. A nonliving device may
  store, focus, transmit, or shape magic supplied through living agency, but
  cannot simply be assumed to be a magic user. [universe/rules.md — “Living
  agency and magic”]
- If the story calls the prophecy's maker or enforcer a god, that being must
  combine worship, power, and authority; mere power, prediction, or belief is
  insufficient. Any divine constraint is local because no universal divine
  government exists. [universe/rules.md — “Divinity”]
- Sacrifice, suffering, desire, and consent have no universal power-generating
  behavior. The chosen cost may be metaphysically necessary only if this
  prophecy system specifically establishes why; its treatment of consent must
  remain consistent, and the ethical consequences cannot be waved away.
  [universe/rules.md — “Costs, desire, and local mechanisms”]
- The cosmic explanation must preserve the reality of the casualties, grief,
  responsibility, consent, and moral choice. The breach cannot reduce those
  stakes to an unreal branch, a machine error, or people who never mattered.
  [universe/style-guide.md — “Personhood and moral consequence”]
- The prompt contract separately requires a genuinely binding prophecy, an
  intentional and apparently impossible breach, a visible personal and social
  cost causally connected to the protagonist's choice, and a concrete final
  choice about responsibility. The prophecy may not be false, mistranslated,
  evaded through wordplay, or reset away.
  [stories/the-future-that-refused-us/00-prompt.md — “Story controls”;
  “Assumptions”; “Completion tests”]
- Violence and death may be central, but their treatment must remain
  non-graphic and consequence-focused, matching the prompt's mature-teen/PG-13
  ceiling. [universe/style-guide.md — “Audience and content boundaries”;
  stories/the-future-that-refused-us/00-prompt.md — “Story controls”]

## Useful established context

- Canon supports many unrelated, locally governed anomalous systems. A
  prophecy mechanism may therefore be new without sharing an origin with other
  magic, technology, divinity, portals, artifacts, or recurring motifs.
  [universe/rules.md — “System categories”; “Artifacts, motifs, and folklore”]
- The setting's central memory-versus-myth tension fits a society organized
  around foreknowledge: evidence may be controlled, distorted, partially
  recovered, or turned into sacred law, while people still make consequential
  choices. Truth alone does not guarantee justice.
  [universe/premise.md — “Memory, myth, and recoverable truth”]
- Characters and cultures may possess radically incomplete or incorrect models
  even while the backstage mechanism is coherent. The protagonist need not
  understand the entire cosmology in order to break this one system.
  [universe/premise.md — “Knowledge and connection”]
- Civilizational collapse can follow institutional failure and individual
  acts, but collapse is not inevitable or cyclic law. This supports visible
  damage from the loss of prophecy-dependent institutions without predestining
  total ruin. [universe/premise.md — “The deep-time shared world”]
- A standalone dark-mythic story may use a new era, local polity, and cast.
  There is no required crossover, flagship character, or mandated awareness of
  the wider setting. [universe/premise.md — “Knowledge and connection”;
  universe/style-guide.md — “Tonal range, scale, and connection”]
- Later cultures may misremember survival measures as sacred law. That is a
  compatible historical pattern for institutions built around an old prophecy,
  provided the story still demonstrates that this particular prophecy is
  genuinely binding rather than merely dogmatic. [universe/premise.md —
  “Memory, myth, and recoverable truth”]
- `stories/INDEX.md` currently marks every listed story canon `no`. Therefore
  no finalized `05-story.md` qualifies as a canon source for this brief, and
  candidate or archive record prophecy-adjacent material must not be imported as
  established lore. [universe/README.md — “Authority order”;
  stories/INDEX.md — “Story index”]

## Conflicts or ambiguity

- **No authoritative contradiction found.** No `LOCKED` or `CANON` entry
  defines prophecy, fate, determinism, foreknowledge, or the metaphysics of
  breaking such a system. This is an omission, not evidence that prophecy is
  impossible or that any particular model is canon.
- The prompt assumption that breaking the prophecy “frees people from a
  predetermined future” is compatible with current canon only as a proposed
  story-local fact. It would become an unsupported global claim if the plan
  asserts that all people, all eras, or reality itself were universally
  predetermined. [stories/the-future-that-refused-us/00-prompt.md —
  “Assumptions”; universe/rules.md — “System categories”; “Costs, desire, and
  local mechanisms”]
- Other dimensions and timelines may exist, but their existence, branching
  behavior, and relevance here are not established. The plan should not use an
  alternate timeline to explain away the casualties or the breach.
  [universe/premise.md — “The deep-time shared world”;
  universe/style-guide.md — “Continuity and evidence discipline”]
- If the plan uses Ravel, its exact chronological placement is unresolved; if
  it uses the Glass-Sea label, that era must follow the modern-like era. The
  safer course is a new unnamed era unless one of those established labels
  materially serves the story. [universe/timeline.md — “Established era
  order”; universe/glossary.md — “Ravel”; “Glass-Sea world”]

## Unknowns

- **Unknown:** whether prophecy is perception of a fixed future, a causal
  engine that enforces one, a contract, a divine authority, a living system,
  or another mechanism. Canon supplies no answer.
- **Unknown:** whether any future is otherwise deterministic, whether free will
  existed inside the prophecy's scope, and what “freedom” technically changes
  after the breach.
- **Unknown:** who created, authorized, interpreted, maintained, or benefited
  from the prophecy; no canon oracle, prophetic institution, faction, or
  recurring character is established for this role. [universe/characters.md —
  “Recurring characters”; universe/factions.md — “Factions and cultures”]
- **Unknown:** the prophecy's geographic, demographic, temporal, and moral
  scope. It is not established whether it binds one person, a bloodline, a
  city, a kingdom, a generation, or anything larger.
- **Unknown:** the exact impossible breach method and why no earlier attempt
  succeeded.
- **Unknown:** the causal form and scale of the cost, including what
  prophecy-dependent protections or institutions fail when certainty ends.
- **Unknown:** whether the prophecy record is reliable, self-updating,
  destructible, conscious, magical, technological, divine, or merely one
  interface to a separate system.
- **Unknown:** the story's era, place, calendar, cultures, characters, and
  relationship to any named setting. The exact Galactic Cycle coordinates of
  even the known eras remain unresolved. [universe/timeline.md — “Universal
  chronology”]
- **Unknown:** whether the dead encounter an afterlife in this story. A
  universal underlying afterlife is canon, but its local experience and any
  relevance to the casualties would require a defined story mechanism.
  [universe/rules.md — “Afterlife and continuation”]

## Safe invention space

The following are safe **proposed local inventions**, not established canon.
They should remain narrow in the plan and be recorded later in the canon delta
if they survive into final prose.

- Create one new era-local culture, city, kingdom, or small region on the
  shared physical world without assigning it an unsupported coordinate or
  claiming it is a different plane.
- Define one self-contained prophecy system with a finite constituency, clear
  evidence that it truly binds outcomes, an intelligible enforcement
  mechanism, stable limits, and a specific breach condition.
- Make the prophecy's binding depend on a local covenant, living network,
  institution, artifact supplied through living agency, divine office, or
  hybrid system, provided the selected model obeys the applicable magic or
  divinity constraints.
- Invent local prophecy-dependent infrastructure—allocation of food, defenses,
  succession, evacuation, medicine, ritual timing, or another concrete public
  service—whose failure visibly follows from the breach. Avoid making
  foreknowledge a universal support for every civilization or era.
- Give the protagonist a concrete, informed role in choosing the breach cost:
  for example, selecting who or what would be exposed when a protection
  failed. The mechanism must establish why that cost follows; sacrifice itself
  is not universal fuel.
- Allow institutions to disagree sincerely about whether security under a
  binding future was preferable to dangerous freedom. Canon supports competing
  models and morally consequential choices without requiring one institution
  to be secretly correct about the whole universe.
- Leave future outcomes genuinely unwritten within the system's former scope
  after the breach while preserving all deaths, damage, and obligations
  already incurred. Do not claim this rewrites other eras or the global
  cosmology.
- Use prophecy records, rites, offices, architecture, or public schedules as
  material evidence and social practice, while distinguishing what the system
  demonstrably does from what its culture merely believes.

## Name constraints

- The prompt supplies no character-facing names or aliases, and
  `stories/NAMES.md` contains no entry for this story yet. Every protagonist,
  opponent, relative, deity, oracle, office used as a name, named collective,
  construct, animal, epithet, and alias introduced by the plan must therefore
  be checked and later registered. A surname also needs its own reserved form
  if prose will use it alone. [stories/NAMES.md — “Rules”]
- Avoid all existing reserved forms unless the plan intentionally reuses the
  same identity or documents a meaningful distinct-identity collision and
  reader-disambiguation strategy. Candidate, abandoned, and archive record reservations
  still count even though they are not canon.
- The prophecy/time/divinity invention space has especially relevant reserved
  aliases: `the Goddess of Time`, `Goddess of Time`, and `Time`; `Queen
  Aurelia`, `Aurelia`, `the Goddess`, `Goddess`, `Goddess of the Sun`, `Sun
  Queen`, `Burning Queen`, and `Mother`; `the Daughter` and `Daughter`; `the
  Hollow Regent`, `Hollow Regent`, and `Regent`; `Sleepless Majesty`, `Majesty`,
  and `Your Majesty`; `Solstice Evening Bell`, `Solstice`, `Evening Bell`,
  `First Monarch`, and `queen of all magical girls`; `King Othran`; `Crowned
  Ancestors`; `Court Ghost`; and `Keeper Below`. Do not accidentally attach
  these to a new prophet, ruler, god, institution, or prophecy.
- Do not add another `Lena`, `Mara`, `Nisha`, `Pell`, or `Voss`. Each form
  already has an unresolved collision between distinct in-progress story identities.
  [stories/NAMES.md — “Known unresolved name collisions”]
- Check close mythic-fantasy forms, not just exact matches. Documented
  high-density clusters include `Irix` / `Ilya` / `Ivo` / `Eli`; `Sula` /
  `Siva` / `Solstice` / `Seris` / `Sabine` / `Sorel`; and `Talvi` / `Tavra` /
  `Tamsin`. The registry also separately reserves the easily confused `Leth`
  and `Letha`, and `Oren` and `Orrin`.
- The architect's `Name check` must list every proposed form, exact and close
  registry matches, whether any reuse is same-identity or distinct-identity,
  why it materially matters, and how readers will distinguish it. Accidental
  collisions should be renamed before drafting. The primary coordinator—not
  the architect or canon librarian—owns the later `stories/NAMES.md` update.

## Required checks after drafting

- Confirm the story demonstrates that the prophecy truly bound outcomes before
  the breach; it must not resolve as fraud, bad translation, metaphor,
  coincidence, wordplay, or ordinary political enforcement alone.
- Trace a complete causal chain: established prophecy mechanism → protagonist's
  informed breach choice → loss or failure of a concrete protection/institution
  → visible personal and societal casualties → protagonist's final responsible
  action.
- Verify that the breach is genuinely exceptional under the established local
  rules and that earlier non-breaches do not expose an easy loophole.
- Search for any line that universalizes the local mechanism—such as all magic
  requiring sacrifice, all futures being fixed, all gods enforcing prophecy,
  or every civilization relying on foreknowledge—and narrow it unless the
  canon delta explicitly proposes that expansion for review.
- If magic is involved, identify the living user or living source. If a god is
  involved, verify worship, power, and authority. If a cost or consent rule is
  involved, verify it behaves consistently in every scene.
- Distinguish demonstrated system behavior from institutional doctrine,
  propaganda, damaged records, interpretation, and myth.
- Verify that no branch, alternate timeline, resurrection, afterlife reveal, or
  record alteration erases the casualties or invalidates grief and moral
  responsibility.
- Verify era language and chronology: do not call the setting Ravel,
  modern-like Earth, or Glass-Sea merely for atmosphere; if one is named, obey
  its established status and placement limits.
- Verify non-graphic treatment of violence and grief and preserve the prompt's
  intimate, morally complicated, ultimately humane tone.
- Run the story-scoped name check after the plan is registered and again after
  final prose. Search all proper names, aliases, nicknames, epithets,
  capitalized collective identities, titles used alone as names, named
  artifacts with person-like agency, and surnames used alone; reconcile any
  draft-only or final-only forms.
- Ensure every reusable local fact that survives final editing is listed in
  `06-canon-delta.md`; none becomes canon merely by appearing in the brief,
  plan, draft, or candidate story.
