# Proposed canon delta

This file proposes reusable shared-universe facts. It is not canon until the
user explicitly approves promotion and the facts are added to `universe/`.

## New characters or character facts

All entries below are proposals from this candidate story, not established
canon.

- **Zadie Cleft:** Nilo Cleft's mother and the builder and operator of
  Sablewake's civic bells. She designs and coordinates the counter-cadence that
  breaks the Covenant of Hours. She knowingly conceals the undergate
  counterweight's untested condition, lies to Nilo that it is sufficient,
  later publicly names her actions and risk allocation, submits to judgment
  after the immediate rescues, and shifts from commanding outcomes to sharing
  observations and accountable options.
- **Nilo Cleft:** Zadie's thirteen-year-old son and the Covenant's scheduled
  undergate casualty. He survives the breached verse and rejects both
  retroactive sacrifice and being used as proof that the breach was worth its
  casualties. Angry with Zadie, he nevertheless chooses to join the first
  post-Covenant rescue with explicit redundancies, independent checks,
  withdrawal conditions, and no reconciliation or forgiveness.
- **Baird Ansel:** Sablewake's first reader. He survives the Covenant's breach
  with his prophetic sense ruined. He defends the old Covenant's real
  protective value without presenting its scheduled deaths as morally clean,
  insists that Zadie name the nonconsensual risk she assigned, and rejects both
  a civic cover story and killing Nilo after the verse has broken.

## New locations

- **Sablewake:** A floodbound city in an unnamed era of the shared physical
  world. Its civic core includes a bell tower and salt-glass schedule; its
  flood infrastructure includes a river embankment, a manual undergate and
  counterweight, a lower ward, cisterns, spillways, footbridges, floodboats,
  and drainage channels.
- **Lower ward of Sablewake:** A populated district protected by the undergate.
  When the counterweight jams during the Covenant breach, the district floods.
  The bell-tower square becomes an improvised rescue and casualty site while a
  shifting north cistern threatens the infirmary roof and west footbridge and
  a school cellar fills through a cracked drain.

## New factions or cultural facts

- **Covenant civic culture:** For three centuries, Sablewake organizes civic
  schedules, flood response, labor, deliveries, infrastructure spending, and
  expected casualties around the Covenant of Hours. The Covenant's exact
  guarantees make duplicate gate crews, surplus boats, discretionary timber,
  early medicines, and live tests of unneeded mechanisms appear wasteful.
  Scheduled deaths become accepted civic losses even though the people named
  by the verse receive no choice.
- **Readers:** Nine living readers renew and maintain Sablewake's Covenant.
  `first reader` is a lowercase common civic office held by Baird Ansel, not a
  character alias or a named faction.
- **Breath registry:** Residents entered in Sablewake's registry fall within
  the Covenant's witnessed outcome sequence and its survival guarantees.
- **Civic bell practice:** Bells transmit scheduled instructions and routine
  drills throughout the city. Zadie exploits that infrastructure to teach a
  hidden counter-cadence; after the breach, unsynchronized bells begin
  communicating observed needs rather than promised outcomes.

## New rules, capabilities, or costs

- **Covenant of Hours:** A local Sablewake prophecy system that binds
  registered residents to one witnessed sequence of events. It is renewed
  every thirty years by nine living readers. The system is local and does not
  establish a universal rule for prophecy, fate, magic, sacrifice, or consent.
- **Living agency and storage:** The readers' living minds and voices provide
  witnessed sequence, correction, and memory. The salt-glass schedule, breath
  registry, bells, and gate machinery are nonliving storage, focusing,
  transmission, or mechanical infrastructure; they do not independently use
  magic. The undergate counterweight can only fall after a living person
  releases it.
- **Reader cost:** Maintaining this covenant consumes autobiographical memory
  from its living readers. The losses are personal and specific, such as a
  remembered face or sensory memory, and are explicitly the working cost of
  this local system rather than a universal magical exchange.
- **Correction behavior:** Away from the renewal hinge, an isolated attempt to
  deviate from the witnessed sequence is forced back into it through
  coincidence, physical constraint, reflex, accident, or other people's
  actions. Attempts to remove or replace a scheduled casualty can become the
  causal route that returns that person to the foretold place.
- **Renewal hinge:** For forty-nine heartbeats at each thirty-year renewal, the
  nine readers must hold the closing and opening verses simultaneously. Their
  correction capacity during this overlap is vast but finite.
- **Counter-cadence breach:** Thousands of living participants can overload
  that finite capacity by making private deviations from the witnessed
  sequence that jointly demand thousands of simultaneous corrections during
  the hinge. Zadie coordinates such a breach by hiding a second language in
  six months of routine bell drills. The overload kills five readers, cracks
  the schedule, ends its corrective pressure, and leaves no intact opening
  verse with which the four survivors can renew it.
- **Prophecy-dependent fragility:** Centuries of exact forecasts remove
  practical redundancy from Sablewake. Once the binding schedule breaks,
  simultaneous unscripted failures become substantially deadlier because the
  city lacks spare materials, equipment, trained substitutes, and experience
  choosing under uncertainty.

## Timeline events

- **Breaking of the Covenant of Hours:** At the end of a thirty-year cycle, a
  major flood overtops Sablewake. The fixed verse would have killed ten people
  in total—Nilo and nine others—while guaranteeing every other registered
  resident's survival.
- During the forty-nine-heartbeat hinge, Zadie rings the counter-cadence and
  thousands of residents make private deviations from the witnessed sequence,
  jointly demanding thousands of simultaneous corrections. Nilo steps away
  from the manual undergate wheel, Zadie releases the mechanical counterweight,
  and it jams with the gate still open. The Covenant breaks and the lower ward
  floods.
- Nilo survives his foretold death. Five of the nine readers die, and the
  other four survive without an intact opening verse; Baird's prophetic sense
  is ruined. At Zadie's public confession, Sablewake has counted sixty-eight
  dead in total, explicitly including the five readers, with eleven more
  people missing. These figures are an in-scene count and may rise.
- In the immediate first post-Covenant response, Zadie rejects a statement
  blaming Nilo, publicly identifies herself as the breach's architect, admits
  her deception and concealed risk, and offers verified observations and
  multiple rescue options. Citizens respond unevenly, improvise safeguards and
  repairs, and use asynchronous bells to signal where living hands are needed.

## New glossary terms or aliases

- **Sablewake:** Floodbound city governed for three centuries by the Covenant
  of Hours.
- **Covenant of Hours:** Sablewake's local thirty-year binding civic prophecy.
- **salt-glass schedule:** A nonliving civic display and focusing surface that
  holds the sequence supplied and sustained by living readers.
- **breath registry:** The record defining which Sablewake residents fall
  within the Covenant's witnessed sequence and guarantees.
- **reader:** A living civic magic user who witnesses, sustains, and corrects
  the Covenant's outcome sequence at an autobiographical-memory cost.
- **first reader:** A lowercase common office among Sablewake's readers, not an
  alias for Baird Ansel.
- **hinge:** The forty-nine-heartbeat overlap during a thirty-year renewal when
  readers hold both the closing and opening verses.
- **counter-cadence:** Zadie's covert bell pattern directing thousands of
  participants to make private deviations from the witnessed sequence that
  jointly demand thousands of simultaneous corrections and overload the
  readers' finite hinge capacity.
