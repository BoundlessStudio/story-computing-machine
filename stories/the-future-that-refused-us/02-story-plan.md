# Story plan

## Premise and promise

- In the floodbound city of Sablewake, bell engineer Zadie Cleft coordinates
  thousands of simultaneous disobediences to overload a genuinely binding
  civic prophecy and save her thirteen-year-old son, only to survive the
  resulting flood among the people whose safety she knowingly gambled.
- **Central dramatic question:** After buying an unwritten future with other
  people's lives, will Zadie hide behind the freedom she created, surrender it
  to someone else's certainty, or accept public responsibility and help people
  choose what comes next?
- **Story promise:** Begin with the impossible victory and its bodies, prove
  that both the prophecy and its breach were real, then force Zadie to face the
  strongest case that the old fixed future was safer without erasing either
  the freedom won or the people lost.

## Story controls

- Close third person through Zadie; past tense.
- Dark mythic fantasy with concrete civic detail, moral pressure, and an
  ultimately humane but non-absolving ending.
- Target 3,200–3,600 words across six scenes.
- Mature teen / PG-13: drowned and injured people may be seen or counted, but
  violence remains non-graphic and grief—not spectacle—carries the weight.
- Use a new unnamed era of the one shared physical world. Do not label it
  Ravel, Earth, or Glass-Sea.
- The nonlinear structure may use two short, clearly signaled flashbacks, but
  the present-tense dramatic line is rescue, reckoning, and Zadie’s final
  public choice.

## Character engine

- **Zadie Cleft:** Sablewake's keeper of civic bells and the designer of the
  covert breach. She wants Nilo alive and wants human choices to matter. Her
  pressure is immediate: secondary flood failures are spreading, citizens
  trained to follow the prophecy are freezing without orders, and officials
  need a public account before anger settles on Nilo. Her blind spot is
  treating "freedom" as a moral quantity large enough to cover unequal,
  nonconsensual risk. Her movement is from secret unilateral control to
  truthful, accountable coordination that leaves other people room to choose.
- **Nilo Cleft:** Zadie's thirteen-year-old son, named by the binding verse as
  the person who would die turning the undergate wheel and thereby keep the
  lower ward dry. He joined the synchronized disobedience because Zadie told
  him the redundant counterweight should close the gate; she withheld how
  poorly it had been tested. Alive after the breach, he is neither a passive
  prize nor a moral oracle. He is furious that Zadie made him the price tag on
  strangers' deaths, yet he also refuses to be sacrificed retroactively.
- **Baird Ansel:** Sablewake's first reader, Zadie's former teacher in civic
  scheduling, and the clearest defender of the old Covenant of Hours. He
  survived the magical overload with a ruined prophetic sense while five of
  the nine readers died. He opposes absolution, not freedom as an abstraction:
  the Covenant's fixed flood path would have killed Nilo and nine named
  workers, while the breach killed far more people who never consented. He
  presses Zadie to tell the full truth and prevents the story from making the
  old order cartoonishly evil.
- **Collective pressure:** rescuers, lower-ward survivors, and frightened
  citizens remain ordinary people rather than a named mob or faction. Some
  participated in the breach; others did not. Their mixed reactions keep both
  mass consent and collateral harm visible.

## Causal arc

- **Beginning state:** The Covenant of Hours is a local civic prophecy renewed
  every thirty years by nine living readers. Through living magic they bind
  everyone entered in Sablewake's breath registry to one witnessed sequence
  of outcomes. People retain thoughts and intentions, but coincidence,
  physical constraint, and other people's corrections force any isolated
  deviation back into the witnessed sequence. For three centuries the system
  has made floods survivable enough that the city has removed costly
  redundancies and treats predicted deaths as scheduled civic losses.
- **Demonstrated rule:** Before the flood, Zadie tests a harmless deviation
  from the verse by taking the west stair instead of the east. A broken
  handrail, a porter, and her own attempt to help redirect her until she
  arrives at the foretold bell at the foretold moment. This proves the
  Covenant is not fraud or social obedience.
- **The fatal verse:** At the end of the current cycle, the river will overtop
  Sablewake. Nilo will remain at the manual undergate wheel until he drowns;
  his action will keep the lower ward dry. The verse names nine other deaths
  and guarantees all other registered residents survive the night. Repeated
  private attempts to move, restrain, or replace Nilo become causes placing
  him nearer the gate.
- **Breach discovery:** Maintenance records show that at each thirty-year
  hinge the readers must carry both the closing verse and the opening verse
  for forty-nine heartbeats. Their correction capacity is finite during that
  overlap: historical marginalia record several simultaneous deviations and
  violent corrections, but no one has coordinated enough incompatible acts to
  exceed all nine living readers at once.
- **Zadie's plan:** Over months she hides a counter-cadence inside routine bell
  drills. Participants are told to choose one mundane action the readers
  cannot enumerate in advance and, at the hinge cadence, do its opposite.
  Thousands knowingly consent to personal uncertainty. Zadie conceals a
  crucial unequal risk: because no preassigned substitute can remain at the
  undergate without giving the Covenant a replacement path, the untested
  counterweight must close it after Nilo walks away. Lower-ward residents who
  never joined the plan are exposed if it fails.
- **Inciting present disruption:** The story opens after the counter-cadence
  worked. The readers' song broke, the salt-glass schedule cracked, and Nilo
  stepped away from the wheel. Five readers died from the overload. The
  counterweight jammed; the lower ward flooded. Zadie stands above an
  improvised row of the dead while the city experiences events no verse names.
- **Escalation:** Unpredicted secondary failures begin. Citizens wait for
  assignments that no longer exist. Zadie first reaches for command and
  concealment, then learns that improvised volunteers solve problems the
  Covenant never allowed—but not in time to save everyone. Baird supplies the
  old future's exact, smaller death count and makes Zadie admit she understood
  the lower ward was the breach's collateral stake.
- **Crisis:** Officials prepare to tell the crowd that the Covenant failed on
  its own and that Nilo's refusal caused the flood. The lie would protect
  Zadie's network and perhaps Nilo temporarily, but it would turn him into a
  singular scapegoat and preserve the habit of letting an authoritative
  future absolve decision-makers. Baird refuses to endorse either the lie or a
  demand to kill Nilo now: the old verse is broken, and a new death cannot
  restore its guarantee or resurrect anyone.
- **Climax:** Zadie rings the unscripted emergency bell, publicly names herself
  as architect, discloses the known risk and the false assurance she gave
  Nilo, names the old future's ten deaths and the breach's current count, and
  submits herself to judgment. She does not ask forgiveness or claim the
  result proves her right. She then gives citizens verified observations and
  several rescue options instead of one commanded destiny.
- **Aftermath:** People choose unevenly: some help, some curse her, some carry
  Nilo's proposed rope line to a failing cistern. Zadie lets Nilo volunteer for
  a dangerous task with informed safeguards rather than forbidding him or
  assigning him as a sacrifice. The first post-Covenant bell receives no
  perfectly synchronized answer, but enough distinct answers to begin saving
  the living. Zadie cannot decide that the breach was "worth" the dead; she
  can decide that freedom will no longer mean hiding who paid for her choice.

## Setup and payoff

- The east-stair test demonstrates self-correction early; its mundane redirects
  contrast with the hinge overload's scale and prevent a "prophecy was only
  propaganda" reading.
- Routine drill cadences, tiny personal deviations, and the forty-nine-heartbeat
  hinge are seeded before the breach; together they pay off the apparently
  impossible synchronized contradiction without a last-minute power.
- The undergate counterweight is shown with rust, deferred maintenance, and an
  untested inspection seal before Zadie assures Nilo it will work. Its jam is
  causal, not random punishment.
- The city's lack of spare boats, duplicate gate crews, and discretionary
  supplies is established as a rational adaptation to centuries of exact
  forecasts; those absences create the present rescue failures.
- Baird's damaged prophetic sense leaves him with no supernatural solution. His
  remembered copy of the old verse supports the numerical comparison but
  cannot dictate the new future.
- Zadie's bell expertise begins as a tool of covert control and ends as a way
  to share verified information and invite multiple responses.
- Nilo's original question—whether he was worth the dead—is not answered with
  arithmetic. Its payoff is Zadie's confession that she made others pay and
  her refusal to use either his survival or abstract freedom as absolution.

## Canon constraints

- Keep the Covenant of Hours explicitly local to Sablewake; never claim all
  futures, people, gods, magic, or eras are deterministic.
- The nine readers are living magic users. The salt-glass schedule, bells, and
  registry are nonliving storage/focusing infrastructure, not autonomous magic
  users.
- Treat "magic," prophecy, and civic covenant as Sablewake's cultural model.
  The mechanism must behave coherently, but narration need not validate that
  model as a universal category.
- Do not imply that sacrifice, death, suffering, or consent is universal fuel.
  Reader memory loss, correction capacity, and hinge overload are costs of this
  one system.
- Preserve casualties and grief. No alternate branch, resurrection, afterlife
  contact, retrospective erasure, or repaired record can undo them.
- Keep the setting in an unnamed era and on the one physical shared world.
- Distinguish proven behavior (self-correction, limited hinge capacity,
  successful breach) from doctrine (that the Covenant chose the morally best
  future).

## Proposed inventions

- **Sablewake:** a floodbound city in an unnamed era on the shared world.
- **Covenant of Hours:** a local thirty-year civic prophecy created and
  continuously enforced by nine living readers, each of whom spends
  autobiographical memory to sustain one witnessed outcome sequence for
  residents in a breath registry.
- **Correction behavior:** isolated deviations are forced back into the
  witnessed sequence by local causal constraints; the readers have finite
  correction capacity during a forty-nine-heartbeat cycle hinge.
- **Breach method:** thousands of residents make simultaneous, privately
  selected contradictory choices during the hinge, exceeding the living
  readers' capacity and breaking the binding sequence.
- **Civic dependence:** centuries of exact flood forecasts led Sablewake to
  remove redundant infrastructure and normalize a small number of scheduled
  deaths, making the first unwritten emergency much deadlier.
- **Candidate event:** the Covenant breaks during a major flood; five of nine
  readers and many lower-ward residents die, Nilo survives his foretold death,
  and Sablewake begins operating without a fixed civic future.

## Failure modes to watch

- Do not let the prophecy turn out to be fraud, mistranslation, metaphor,
  political coercion alone, or a self-fulfilling guess. Include the stair test
  and at least one failed rescue attempt with physical causal correction.
- Do not make the breach a clever word-game loophole. It succeeds by an
  established finite-capacity limit, months of coordination, mass living
  agency, and Zadie's explicit decision to expose the undergate.
- Do not obscure Zadie's culpability. She must know the counterweight is
  untested, understand that lower-ward nonparticipants bear the risk, and lie
  to Nilo before proceeding.
- Do not simplify the comparison into tyranny bad / freedom good. State the
  old future's much smaller death count, show the Covenant's real protective
  value, and give Baird the strongest ethical argument.
- Do not imply every casualty chose the risk. Separate breach participants
  from lower-ward residents, children, readers, and other people denied a
  meaningful choice.
- Do not make Nilo forgive Zadie on cue, offer himself as retroactive payment,
  or become proof that the breach was worth it.
- Do not solve the ending with restored prophecy, Nilo's death, a new vision,
  an unseeded magical repair, or Zadie's redemptive death. The climax is public
  truth plus accountable, plural action under uncertainty.
- Keep institutional exposition embodied in bells, gates, drills, shortages,
  injuries, and decisions. Avoid a lore lecture.
- Keep all names to the checked forms above unless the registry is rechecked
  before prose introduces another character-facing form.
