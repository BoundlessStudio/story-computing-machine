# Prompt contract

## Verbatim writing prompt

> **[WP] You did it. You did what was thought to be impossible and broke the prophecy. But as you stare at the cost, you begin to wonder if was even worth it to break the prophecy.**

## Story controls

- Working title: The Future That Refused Us
- Target length: 2,500–4,000 words
- POV: Close third person, limited to the person who breaks the prophecy
- Tense: Past
- Tone and genre: Dark mythic fantasy; intimate, morally complicated, and ultimately humane
- Audience/content rating: Mature teen / PG-13; aftermath of violence and grief without graphic gore
- Required elements: A prophecy that is genuinely binding; an apparently impossible and intentional breach; a concrete, visible cost caused by that breach; a protagonist who helped choose the cost rather than merely discovering it; a sincere reckoning with whether freedom was worth the casualties; an ending that resolves the protagonist's next moral choice
- Prohibited elements: The prophecy merely being false, mistranslated, or satisfied through wordplay; an abstract or entirely off-page cost; a reset that erases the consequences; a purely triumphant ending; nihilism that makes the original choice meaningless by default

## Assumptions

- The story stands alone while remaining compatible with the shared universe.
- Breaking the prophecy frees people from a predetermined future, but the institutions and protections built around foreknowledge cannot survive unchanged.
- The cost follows causally from both the method of breaking the prophecy and the sudden loss of certainty.
- The ending may remain morally ambiguous, but the protagonist must make a concrete choice about responsibility in the newly unwritten future.

## Completion tests

- The result is a complete short story, not a synopsis or chapter fragment.
- The central promise of the prompt is dramatized and resolved.
- The prophecy is shown to have been real and is broken rather than evaded.
- The cost is personal, societal, visible in-scene, and traceable to the protagonist's choice.
- The protagonist confronts a credible argument that preserving the prophecy would have prevented the present losses.
- The ending answers what the protagonist will do with freedom after victory, without undoing the breach.
- The story passes canon and continuity review.
- Every character-facing name and alias is checked against `stories/NAMES.md`;
  any deliberate reuse has a documented narrative reason.
