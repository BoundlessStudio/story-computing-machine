# Opus review: The House of Possible Uses

Independent audit for a later editing pass. It is not a `review.md` verdict,
does not change canon state, and does not authorize edits by itself.

## Verdict

- Dialogue: REVISE. One line fails at the decisive reach ("Enna's arm is shorter"), the opening volley barely parses, and the family speaks Kezra's evidence register long before the story's turn.
- Prose: PASS. Concrete and well staged overall; only minor gloss, negation-fragment and naming tics.
- Continuity and prompt: REVISE. The prompt is delivered within the length band, but at L329 Sael re-covers the mothglass in a kitchen nobody has reached yet, and a few objects drift.
- Edit scope: targeted-dialogue
- Existing review.md said: Verdict PASS, "Dialogue: PASS". I disagree. Its claim that the dialogue is "speaker-specific" misses the Sael/Kezra convergence and two exchanges that do not make plain sense.

The story's engine is the gap between what someone saw and what they took it to mean. Its best exchanges run on that engine through each person's own interest: Tobren's objects, Sael's records, Daskar's floor. Examples are the inventory confession (L263–L281), the permission to break chairs (L307–L325) and "Use your forearm" (L351). The trouble is that before Enna's deliberate turn at L261 ("No more meanings"), almost everyone already talks like Kezra. Sael polices "interpretations", Enna reports "I observed the hall" seconds after seeing her own back, Tobren borrows "establishes", and Daskar hedges with "coincided". The family sounds like a panel of auditors, and the turn has nothing to turn from. The edit pass should return the evidence discipline to Kezra alone and keep the family talking about objects, rooms and each other until L261. It should also repair the arm-length line and the opening volley. The second half mostly works and should be touched lightly.

## Edit guardrails

- Prompt: a museum of dangerous artifacts sends agents into the hoarder uncles' house. Both uncles (Tobren, Sael) and both agents (Kezra Vonn, Daskar Avel) must stay. First-person past through the niece, Enna. Length is 2,500–4,000 words (currently about 3,400), for a broadly accessible audience, with a complete resolution. The rewrite request asked for all dialogue to meet the 08-21 profile, so dialogue edits are in scope.
- Artifact rules the plot depends on. The vane sounds as an opening forms nearby. The black glass lights when the stove flame shifts. The green stone pulses when enclosed volume changes. The seed stops the fold only once the outputs are suppressed and it is sealed in the dark, felt-lined, suspended jar. Movement and impacts speed the folding. Any rewritten line must stay consistent with these.
- Universe. `Heartseeds` (rules.md, CANON) are a different story's living seeds in jars, so do not let edits link the collector's seed to them. `Artifacts, motifs, and folklore` (CANON) holds that proximity proves no shared origin, and Kezra's L153 "Proximity doesn't prove they belong together" respects it; keep that. `Personhood and moral consequence` (LOCKED): the uncles' hoarding stays a human situation with real knowledge and choices, not villainy or diagnosis.
- Keep the incremental ending (one route, one category, a hold box). The house must not be cured or emptied.
- Enna's L261 adoption of Kezra's method is the pivot. Edits elsewhere should create contrast for it, not delete it.

## Dialogue findings

### D1 · Blocking · "Enna's arm is shorter" is the wrong reason for the reach

- Where: L339–L347, the original pantry, the decisive reach for the tin
- Text:
> "I can hold the vessel. I can't reach past that shelf without loading the floor."
> Sael said, "Enna's arm is shorter."
> "Thank you, Uncle."
> I lay across the pantry sill with my weight on the wall studs.
- Problem: Ground/uptake plus Punchline Patch at the decisive beat. Daskar's problem is reaching past the shelf without putting weight on the floor. A shorter arm makes that harder, not easier, so Sael's proposal doesn't answer the problem, and nobody questions it. The story then solves it with something nobody said: Enna is light enough to lie across the sill on the studs. The line exists to set up "Thank you, Uncle." / "It is.", and the joke costs the reader the logic of why the niece, not the agent, takes the seed.
- Fix: Give Sael the true reason in his own register (her weight, or which studs will carry her), so that the lying-across-the-sill staging follows from what was said. Cut "Thank you, Uncle." / "It is." unless a joke genuinely follows from the new line. Illustrative only: "She weighs less. The studs by the sill will take her."

### D2 · Major · The opening squabble barely parses

- Where: L15–L29, the hall before the knock
- Text:
> "It's leaving tomorrow. I found someone who needs the taps."
> "Hence the basin's staying here until they are."
> "The measurement began at the coat hooks. You began at the jamb."
> "A person cannot begin at the coat hooks."
> I had, by climbing over a crate of winter stove rings.
- Problem: Ground/referent and correction ping-pong in the first fifteen lines. On the plainest reading, Sael says Enna began at the jamb, then answers "You did" when she says nobody can begin at the coat hooks, and the narration confirms she had. The exchange only makes sense if the reader separates measuring from clearing, and the lines never mark that difference. The dispute also has nothing to do with Enna's complaint, which is about width. Tobren says the basin leaves tomorrow, then "Hence" it stays until the taps are on. That could be a hoarder's endless deferral, but Enna doesn't register the reversal, so it reads as an authored comeback. All three speakers share one clipped legal register ("The agreement was", "Hence", "A person cannot"), and that register is the reader's first impression of the family.
- Fix: Build the exchange around the basin. Keep Tobren's deferral if wanted, but make Enna notice it (a date he's missed before, a look, a flat repeat). Give Sael one claim he would really track, such as where the agreed width was measured from and that she had to climb the stove-ring crate to measure it. Cut the self-contradicting "You began at the jamb" / "You did" pair. Enna should sound like a niece two years into this fight, not a surveyor. Keep the layout paragraph at L29 from "I dragged the basin" onward, because the geography pays off later. Illustrative only: "It goes tomorrow, once I've got the taps back on."

### D3 · Major · The dining-room test runs on two correction volleys

- Where: L85–L107, dining room, the drawer-pull box and the first lantern pass
- Text:
> That establishes resemblance and chain of custody, not function.
> "It kept a pantry door from banging," Tobren said. "That establishes a function."
> "A use," Kezra corrected. "Not necessarily the original one."
> "There's a hollow behind the cabinet."
> Kezra said, "I heard a tone behind it."
> "That isn't the same claim."
- Problem: Correction ping-pong, Philosopher, and uniform fluency under pressure. In the first volley Tobren, whose voice elsewhere is plain nouns and repair history, mirrors Kezra's verb ("establishes") to score a point. Kezra answers by correcting his noun, and the tag "corrected" underlines it. The exchange exists to state the title's use/function idea, which the doorstop, the kettle glass and the vane already show. Resemblance also doesn't establish chain of custody (the sale record does), so the precise speaker is imprecise in the line built to show her precision. In the second volley, a whistle in the wall, a red flash from the kitchen and a thudding floor all arrive at once, and the reply is a four-line seminar on types of claim. The untagged "So did I." (L105) could be Tobren, Sael or Enna. The volley changes nothing, because Tobren pulls the cabinet anyway at L115.
- Fix: Keep "It kept a pantry door from banging." Let Tobren defend the stone in his own terms (how long it did the job, that it never hurt anyone) and let Kezra answer that, for example by writing it down, instead of correcting his word. Drop "That establishes a function." and the "A use" correction, and make "chain of custody" match the record or cut it. In the second volley, cut "So did I." and "That isn't the same claim.", and let Kezra's single line act as a caution; her L153 briefing plants her method better. Illustrative only: "Kept the pantry door quiet nine years. Nobody got hurt."

### D4 · Major · Calm reasoning about evidence at the front-door loop

- Where: L139–L151, the front door opens back onto the hall
- Text:
> "I observed the hall," I told Kezra, because she had made the distinction matter. "I do not know what that means."
> "It means the exit has folded," Tobren said.
> "No," Sael said quietly. "It means this door does not currently lead outside."
> The difference did not comfort anyone, but it kept Tobren from boarding the door shut.
- Problem: Uniform fluency under pressure, correction ping-pong, and narration that explains the subtext. Enna has just watched the back of her own shoulder vanish around the door and slammed it. Her next words are an uncontracted evidence report, and the narration explains why she chose Kezra's terms. Sael then corrects Tobren's sentence ("No. It means…"), which is the collection's signature tic, and the narration explains its effect. Nobody sounds frightened. The family has adopted Kezra's method more than a hundred lines before Enna's deliberate turn at L261, which drains that turn.
- Fix: Let Enna react like someone who has seen herself from behind: plain, contracted, maybe said to no one. Let Tobren's "folded" stand, or give it a practical answer. If Sael has to stop Tobren boarding the door, have him do it his way (a hand on the door, an instruction about the door itself) rather than by redefining Tobren's words. Cut "because she had made the distinction matter", and trim L151 once the action shows the result. Illustrative only: "That was me. That was the back of my coat."

### D5 · Major · Sael and Daskar speak Kezra's evidence register

- Where: L185–L189, the dining room after the wall moves; L219–L235, the stretched hall after Enna runs
- Text:
> "That is an interpretation," Sael said.
> "You interpreted fire."
> "There was no smoke."
> "More bare movement coincided with faster expansion. Treat that as hazardous until disproved."
- Problem: Identical Voices and a Tennis Match. Cover the tags and Sael's "That is an interpretation" and his "You saw… / You interpreted… / There was no smoke" questioning could be Kezra's. Sael's domain is counts and positions. Here, with his brother lost behind a wall, he cross-examines his niece in Kezra's vocabulary while she answers "Yes" twice. The pressure underneath is good: Enna's unspoken fear of fire at L233 is the story's emotional center. But the surface turns the moment into a deposition. Daskar then restates the lesson as a hedged finding ("coincided with", "until disproved") instead of an order, and "bare movement" does not parse. At L187 Sael's objection is redundant anyway, because Kezra flags her own guess in the next line.
- Fix: At L187, cut Sael's line and let Kezra's "I haven't observed it" carry the hedge. At L223–L231, keep Sael pressing Enna, but through what he measures (paces, doors, how far the kitchen moved), so her conclusion "My running made more rooms" comes from his numbers. Keep L233's unspoken fire fear word for word. Turn Daskar's L235 into a clipped command. Illustrative only: Sael, "You ran nine paces. The hall grew four."; Daskar, "Walk. Nobody runs."

### D6 · Minor · Kezra's "Correction:" corrects with something she didn't see

- Where: L245–L249, voices through the false pantry
- Text:
> "Correction: the shelves changed between observations. We didn't see the tag move."
> That was Kezra. Even misplaced inside an impossible pantry, she would not give a verb more certainty than it had earned.
- Problem: The one person entitled to correct claims labels her correction and then asserts something she also didn't see: that the shelves changed. The narration then explains her character for the reader. She is the right speaker for this move, so the beat should stay, but her correction has to be more careful than Tobren's claim.
- Fix: Drop the "Correction:" label and let her state only what she saw. Cut L249 or reduce it to a clause. Illustrative only: "It was on the third shelf. Now it's on the fifth. We didn't see it go."

### D7 · Minor · Jargon and referents the listener can't unpack

- Where: L129, L167, L185, L257, L297, L361
- Text:
> The wall occupies the yard.
> "Observed," Kezra said, "it produces sound while an altered opening forms nearby.
> Something else acted on the pattern.
> This is transmitted vibration, not a receipt from Tobren.
> the three responses may be feeding it a recurring collection pattern.
> Kezra counted the receipts from somewhere beyond the wall.
- Problem: Ground/referent. At L129 it is the shelves, not the wall, that occupy the yard, as L291 says. "Observed, it produces…" is broken syntax, and "what it detects" assumes a detection she has just refused to assume. "The pattern" at L185 has no antecedent. "Receipt" at L257 and "counted the receipts" at L361 use a private production word for a confirmation or a click. At L361, the climax, the reader has to guess that Kezra is counting the three clasps by ear. "Recurring collection pattern" gives Tobren and Enna nothing to act on. It also arrives as a crisp four-sentence instruction, though the narration says her words came "thin and out of order".
- Fix: Replace each with the plain thing: "the shelves"; "It whistles when an opening forms nearby"; name the three answers; "not Tobren knocking"; "Kezra counted the clasps as they caught". Either break up Kezra's L297 speech to match "out of order" or drop that clause. Keep her practical conditions (dark, sound-damped, off the floor, fewer than two outputs), because the climax depends on them.

### D8 · Minor · Untagged lines that force back-counting

- Where: L59–L61, L105, L287–L293, L317–L321
- Text:
> "I'm telling them the attic."
> "Because the original pantry shares the kitchen's lead pipe. These shelves occupy the yard."
> "There is no outside."
- Problem: Attribution. L59's "You told me the east bedroom" comes right after Tobren's attic line but names the room Sael just excluded. The reader has to work out that Tobren had earlier told her the east bedroom, and that the untagged reply is his. L105 is covered in D3. L291 answers Tobren's question to Enna but uses Sael's diction ("occupy the yard"), and Sael's pointing follows, so the reader can't tell who found the deciding clue. L319 could be Enna or Sael.
- Fix: Add a name or a beat to each. Decide who owns the clue at L291 and tag it. This is where the family's knowledge of the house beats the agents' instruments, so who says it matters.

### D9 · Minor · Kezra knows the niece's name on the doorstep

- Where: L41–L47, the knock
- Text:
> "Enna Laird?" Kezra asked.
- Problem: Ground/knowledge. The agents came about a purchase made by Tobren. Nothing shows how they know a visiting niece's name or expect her to open the door. The outline has Enna arranging the visit, but the prose never says so.
- Fix: Add one clause establishing contact (she answered the museum's letter, or she set today's time). That also sharpens her position between the agents and her uncles. Alternatively, have Kezra ask who she is.

### D10 · Minor · Echo and last-word lines

- Where: L299–L301, L369–L371, L395–L397
- Text:
> "May," Kezra agreed. "It fits what we have. It is not a maker's instruction."
> "Observed," Kezra said, breathing hard. "And I am not assigning ownership."
> "One category," Tobren said.
- Problem: Small closing lines that keep the correction rhythm going after the story has moved past it. L299–L301 is one more volley about claims at the moment of greatest urgency. L371 answers Tobren's unsettling "Those were not all ours" with a quip, and "Nobody laughed" admits it is a joke. L395–L397 gives the brothers mirrored sentences ("One route…", "One category…"), which reads as authored symmetry rather than two different men.
- Fix: At L301, keep the hedge and cut the aphorism, or let Tobren's "May" go unanswered. At L371, let Kezra register what "not all ours" might mean (a look, a note in her book) instead of joking. Break the mirror at L395–L397 so that each brother states his limit in his own shape. Keep "Hold box?" / "Hold box." at the end; that echo is earned.

## Prose findings

### P2 · Minor · Negation and triplet fragments

- Where: L33, L73, L329, L355 (L159 is in P1)
- Text:
> Not impatiently. Officially.
> not by defeating my uncles, but by waiting while Tobren carried the fans into the parlor

- Problem: The "Not X. Y." reversal and the "No A. No B. No C." triplet recur as a rhythm tic. L355 also brings in a dread of mouths and faces that the story never set up for a seed.

### P3 · Minor · Artifact names arrive without introduction

- Where: L51, L329, L377
- Text:
> Sael pressed the opaque cover flat over the mothglass.

### P4 · Minor · A staging sentence contradicts itself

## Continuity and prompt findings

### C1 · Blocking · The mothglass is re-covered in a kitchen nobody has reached

- Where: L329, the dining-room threshold before the final approach
- Text:
> At the dining-room threshold, Daskar retightened the wool around the whisper-vane. Sael pressed the opaque cover flat over the mothglass.
- Problem: The glass is in the kitchen behind the spice jars (L173), and "The glass is covered" at L209 refers to it there. The cupboards then unfold between the dining room and the kitchen (L215–L217), so the kitchen is out of reach. At L329 the group hasn't reached the kitchen yet; they find the pantry door at L333. Sael can't touch the glass from the dining-room threshold. The vane was buckled under the stair rail (L171), which is not obviously at that threshold either. The beat matters, because re-silencing all three signals is what stops the unfolding. The story also never says what the red glare at L207 was: Sael rules out fire at L231, and nothing takes its place.
- Fix: The smallest fix is to replace Sael's action with something he can do from where he stands, such as confirming that no red shows under the kitchen door. Also move the vane check to where the vane is, or state that Daskar carried it. If the L207 glare was meant to be the glass's cover slipping, say so in one clause there. In that case Sael re-covers it after they reach the kitchen, and the unfolding stops only then.

### C4 · Minor · Crushing the lid boxes contradicts the stated reason not to move them

- Where: L347–L353, the pantry reach
- Text:
> Moving either box would change the enclosed space near the stone three rooms away.
> I flattened the cardboard slowly.
- Problem: The green stone responds to changes in enclosed volume. Crushing a box changes volume as much as moving it, so the reader can't see why crushing is the safe choice. "Three rooms away" also undercuts the premise that a pantry box matters to the stone at all.
- Fix: Add one clause of reasoning that fits the rule (a slow change doesn't trigger it, or moving the boxes would mean stepping onto the floor). That also gives Tobren's "Use your forearm" its point.

### C5 · Minor · The side door first appears at the end

- Where: L385, L393 (the house plan at L29)
- Text:
> I'll call the blocked side door a danger.
> The side door was visible behind it, still blocked by six crates of drawer hardware.
- Problem: The house plan at L29 lists the front door, stairs, parlor, dining room, kitchen and back door. The side door that anchors the ending's "Front to side" route is never planted, and during the emergency nobody thinks of it or of the kitchen's back door.
- Fix: Plant the blocked side door in the L29 layout paragraph or in Daskar's "one clear exit" line at L65. That also sets up the ending.

### C6 · Minor · A water pipe is the navigation clue (policy)

- Where: L291–L293, L333
- Text:
> At floor level, a water pipe ran toward the kitchen.
> We followed the lead pipe.
- Problem: Under style-guide "No default water infrastructure" (CANON), an authorized revision should not keep a water-supply line as a plot device unless the prompt asked for one. Here a lead water pipe is the clue that separates the original pantry from the copies. It is domestic plumbing rather than public works, so this is a policy nit, not a defect in the story as written.
- Fix: The story already has two seams that have nothing to do with water: the calendar nail scars (L293) and the coal-scuttle gouge (L333). Let the clue rest on those and drop the pipe from L291, L293 and L333. This must be done together with D8's attribution fix at L291.
