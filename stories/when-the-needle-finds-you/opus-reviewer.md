# Opus review: When the Needle Finds You

Independent audit for a later editing pass. It is not a `review.md` verdict,
does not change canon state, and does not authorize edits by itself.

## Verdict

- Dialogue: REVISE — in two exchanges a reply answers something nobody said on the page. Sivra's questioning turns into word-traps with one-word moral cappers, room six trades quips while shot, and the studio ends on the Jester recapping the plot and then stating the theme.
- Prose: REVISE — the narration explains Olt's motives and, at the climax, Sivra's choice one beat before she acts. There are also a few garbled sentences and some water-infrastructure texture.
- Continuity and prompt: REVISE — the premise, the hard-R register, the exact title and the length are all met. But the clock from sunset to 11:38 doesn't hold, and in the truck the reader can't tell what Olt wants.
- Edit scope: targeted-dialogue
- Existing review.md said: Verdict PASS, "Dialogue: PASS"; disagree. It missed two Ground failures, room six's voice drifting into the Jester's, and the broken clock.

The dialogue has a real strength in Olt: he is glib, profane, flattering and contradictory, falls into run-ons when he panics, and in the end tries to sell his sister. The Jester's velvet-showman register is earned by the role, and his radio interruptions are the story's best dialogic device. The failures cluster in four places. Two replies answer claims nobody made on the page (L69–L75, L159–L161). Sivra argues through word choice ("empty" against "no bodies", "Adults" then "Alive") instead of through the bodies and tasks her trade gives her. Room six, burned and shot, speaks almost entirely in composed one-liners that sound like the Jester. And the studio stops for a plot recap and a "happen/true" thesis correction. The edit pass should fix the two Ground breaks, make Sivra plainer and more physical, make room six a witness who has rehearsed facts rather than jokes, and let the Jester want things instead of explaining them. It should leave Olt mostly alone.

## Dialogue findings

### D1 · Blocking · Reply to the radio's revision follows nothing

- Where: L67–L75, the tire-shop office, when the dead radio switches on
- Text:
> Our guest has chosen his sister. Predictable, but touching.
> "It was here."
> "You always bring the worst thing in the room and call it equipment."
- Problem: Ground failure: the referent can't be recovered and the reply doesn't follow. Olt's "He knew you'd come" is a fair reading of the Jester's line. But "It was here" has no recoverable referent (the radio? the Jester?). Whatever Sivra means, she is saying she brought nothing, yet Olt answers as if she had ("You always bring the worst thing in the room"). The reader can't tell whether he means the radio, her pistol or Sivra herself. The Jester's "has chosen his sister" is also loose, since Olt hasn't chosen anything and Sivra has just dragged him up. As showman gloss it can survive.
- Fix: Decide what Sivra is telling Olt. If the point is that the Jester speaks through any radio, plugged in or not, show it (she lifts the loose cord). Let Olt react to that fear, then go straight to "Back stairs. Now." Cut the "worst thing in the room" capper. Optionally make the Jester's line name what Olt actually did (let his sister take him). Illustrative only: she holds up the bare cord; "Nobody plugged it in, Olt."

### D2 · Blocking · "This thing wasn't listening" cites a claim nobody made

- Where: L157–L163, the truck, after the Jester answers Olt over the radio
- Text:
> "You said this thing wasn't listening."
> "Everything's listening."
- Problem: Ground failure on knowledge and referent, plus lost attribution. Nobody on the page has said the radio wasn't listening, and "you" could be Sivra or the woman beside her. The answer has no tag; only the tag on the next line ("the scarred woman said") hints that Sivra spoke it. To make the exchange work, the reader has to invent a conversation that happened off the page. "Everything's listening" then lands as an epigram rather than an answer. The Jester's interruption just before (L157) works as horror, but what he says is another word correction ("There is no halfway").
- Fix: Either plant the claim with a half-line earlier in the drive, or turn Olt's line into a reaction to what has just happened (the radio answered him). Tag the reply to a speaker and make it something that person would say or do. Sivra, who pulled the plug on her own receiver months ago, might reach over and yank the truck radio's wires and fail to silence it. Keep the Jester's interruption, but consider giving him appetite instead of a correction. Illustrative only: "He can hear us?"

### D3 · Major · Room six speaks in the Jester's quip register while shot

- Where: L85, L93, L121, L133, L145, L163; the tire shop and the truck
- Text:
> "Still counting better than you."
> It affected my manners.
> "Your concern is a rash. It'll pass."
> "You interviewed me."
> Room six gave it back.
- Problem: Too fluent under pressure, a voice too close to the Jester's, and a string of Punchline Patches. She has been burned, has a bullet through her arm, and has just watched two of Olt's fingers come off. Yet almost every line she speaks is a composed comeback that closes the exchange. Her cadence ("Your concern is a rash. It'll pass.") is the Jester's, which blurs the two people who jointly engineer Olt's death. Two lines don't decode at all. "Still counting better than you" means nothing yet, because the reader doesn't know she was a twelfth person in the building. "You interviewed me" points to something Olt never does on the page. Her strongest lines are her plainest: the testimony at L167 and "I won't make the ladder" at L243. Those show the target.
- Fix: Keep "Yes" and "You left me in a furnace." and cut "It affected my manners." Replace "Your concern is a rash" with pain or silence, or have her ignore Olt. Cut "You interviewed me", or make it a concrete accusation she can back up. Reduce L163 to plain fact (she gave the Jester his blood and his ledger). Replace "Still counting better than you" with something that shows she has kept count, or with nothing but a look. Allow her at most one dry line in the whole story, and let pain break her syntax at least once in the truck.

### D4 · Major · The blood-reveal sentence misparses

- Where: L89, the tire shop, as room six explains how she got Olt's blood
- Text:
> I took the rag, his ledger, and the thing I saw him do to the Jester.
- Problem: The referent is ambiguous. The natural reading is "the thing I saw him do to the Jester", as if Olt did something to the Jester, not "I took three things to the Jester". The intended meaning (she brought the Jester Olt's blood and his buried secret) is only confirmed at L273, so the misreading hangs over the whole middle of the story. The line also uses two verbs for the same rag ("I kept the rag" at L89, then "I took the rag").
- Fix: Reorder the sentence so "to the Jester" attaches to "took", and keep it in her factual register. Illustrative only: "I took the Jester the rag, his ledger, and what I watched him do."

### D5 · Major · A word-trap in the truck ends in a capper that doesn't parse

- Where: L173–L181, the truck, after room six's testimony
- Text:
> "You said the building was empty."
> "You just said no bodies."
> "Same fucking thing."
> Because you can only do one with that arm.
- Problem: Correction ping-pong and an unclear referent. Sivra builds a lawyer's catch out of word choice ("empty" against "no bodies"). That is the collection's house register, not a recovery driver holding a gun with her arm laid open. "You said the building was empty" points to something Olt said six years ago. Because the POV has hidden Sivra's part in the fire, the reader searches this conversation for "empty" and doesn't find it. Olt's capper doesn't parse either: holding court needs no arm.
- Fix: Make Sivra's line openly about the past and cut the second parse. That one line also plants her involvement fairly before L209 without giving away that she lit the stairs (see C3). Cut "You just said no bodies." and "Same fucking thing." Keep Olt's pushback but make its logic hold: she is bleeding and has to choose where to drive (see C2). Illustrative only: "Six years ago you told me that block was cleared."

### D6 · Major · "Adults." / "Alive." is a Punchline Patch at the bodies

- Where: L213–L219, the tenement, in front of the eleven
- Text:
> "Adults," Sivra said.
> "That's not what I mean."
> "Alive."
- Problem: A Punchline Patch and correction ping-pong at the story's moral hinge. Olt's "You don't know what they were" is a real plea. Sivra answers it with a deliberately literal category ("Adults"), and that line exists only so Olt can say "That's not what I mean" and she can land "Alive." It reads as authored courtroom rhythm, in the one scene where she stands in front of what she helped do. The narration has just shown that she can read these bodies (L205), so her response should come from that knowledge, not from a word game.
- Fix: Cut the two-step. Give Sivra one plain response or none: silence, with the flashlight held on the fingernails in the door, or a single instruction that makes Olt look. Keep Olt's "They were extorting everybody on the row" and the "witness" slip that follows. Illustrative only: "Look at the door, Olt."

### D7 · Major · In the studio the Jester recaps the plot, and the exchange ends on a thesis correction

- Where: L277–L283, the pump-room studio, after the membrane is played
- Text:
> I promised her a broadcast confession if she brought me blood and the ledger.
> She forced him through the one door he would never choose, and you kept him alive for me.
> "You make them happen."
> "I make them true. The city prefers verbs that wash their hands."
- Problem: Exposition followed by the Philosopher. After "Repeatedly", the Jester narrates a plot the reader has just watched: her deal, the pages, the door, Sivra keeping Olt alive. He closes on one aphorism ("Prediction is such a frail animal..."). Then Sivra hands him the theme in four words, so he can correct it with a second aphorism. That is two epigrams and a stated theme in one exchange, in the decisive scene. The only new fact is room six's bargain.
- Fix: Keep "Repeatedly." and one concrete boast (he heard every revision). Keep room six's bargain as one short clause, since it is new information. Keep at most one of the two aphorisms. Cut "You make them happen." and "I make them true...", or replace Sivra's line with an action (she looks at the shelf of membranes; she puts her hand on the pistol). The mechanism speech at L273 can stay, because she asked to see the record and he is a showman, but it must not grow.

### D8 · Minor · Olt's excuse contradicts itself

- Where: L221, the tenement
- Text:
> The rest wouldn't leave. What was I supposed to do, knock on every door?
- Problem: The two sentences don't fit together. "Wouldn't leave" means he asked them to go; "knock on every door?" means he never tried. A contradictory excuse can be in character, but here it reads like two drafts of the same line left side by side.
- Fix: Pick one version: he warned some of them and they refused, or he warned no one. Keep "I needed the witness gone", which is the line that matters.

### D9 · Minor · The route is told twice

- Where: L113 and L171, the tire shop and the truck
- Text:
> The service culvert under it reaches the Baths.
> The service culvert starts under the old tenement.
- Problem: Repeated exposition. Room six gives the route at L113 and again at L171. Only "surface doors are poured shut" and "the fire room" are new.
- Fix: At L171 keep only the new facts: the surface doors are sealed, and the way runs through the fire room. The cut buys words for other fixes. See P3 about the word "culvert".

### D10 · Minor · A stock line and a writerly comeback

- Where: L63, the office; L79, the room six entrance
- Text:
> "That's a sentence I never hoped you'd need."
> "Too late to run."
- Problem: L63 is a writer's comeback to Sivra's strong line and closes the beat on a quip. L79 is a stock villain entrance, spoken by a woman who says moments later that she wants Olt alive.
- Fix: Keep Sivra's "I know your voice with teeth missing." Cut L63, or replace it with Olt's deflection failing (he starts a joke and doesn't finish it). Give room six an entrance line that belongs to her, or none; she can simply step into view.

## Prose findings

### P1 · Major · Narration explains motives and the climax

- Where: L191 (the drive), L249 (entering the Baths), L339 (Olt's death)
- Text:
> He mistook that for belief.
> He had lied because the only way to the studio passed the dead.
> Both had mistaken what she was still willing to carry.
- Problem: The narration glosses subtext right after the action: Olt's reading of her drive (L191), Olt's motive for the wrong directions (L249), and, at the climax, a summary of what both men wanted from her and what she won't carry (L339). That last one explains her choice one beat before she acts it out, so it takes the inference away at the decisive moment.
- Fix: Cut the last three sentences of L339, from "The Jester had wanted" through "willing to carry." The stylus going into the eye says it. Cut "He mistook that for belief." For L249, fix the logic first (C2), then show it rather than state it: Olt knows every turn in the dark.

### P2 · Minor · Garbled sentences

- Where: L183, L231, L339
- Text:
> Olt died instead to glass entering his throat.
> they had cost eleven people more than Olt's neck.
> She could hear their own broadcast returning through his headphones
- Problem: "Died instead to glass" is ungrammatical. The comparison at L231 can't be parsed: did the answers cost eleven people more than Olt's neck? "Their own broadcast" has no plural owner.
- Fix: At L183, write that Olt dies of glass in his throat instead. At L231, say plainly why she lets him live: she needs him to reach the studio, and the record puts his death there. At L339, write "the broadcast" or "the studio's own broadcast".

### P3 · Minor · Water-infrastructure texture

- Where: L21, L113, L171, L239, L249, L359, L365, L369
- Text:
> His voice was velvet laid over a drain.
> blood found drains.
- Problem: The style guide's "No default water infrastructure" rule (CANON) applies to authorized revisions, and the prompt doesn't call for water infrastructure. Even so, a drain simile introduces the Jester, a "service culvert" is the route (six mentions, including the final broadcast), and drains close the aftermath. The drained municipal pool is a leisure building, not water management, and can stay as the Baths.
- Fix: Replace the drain simile. Replace "service culvert" with a passage that has nothing to do with water (a service tunnel or coal passage), consistently at every mention. Replace "blood found drains" (for example, "blood found the low places"). Keep the pool motor only as the platform's drive; renaming "pump chamber" is optional.

## Continuity and prompt findings

### C1 · Major · The clock from afternoon to 11:38 doesn't hold

- Where: L35, L41, L135, L185–L189, L195, L249
- Text:
> At sunset I begin reading the addresses he sold me
> "But at sunset I turn the next page."
> For three hours the Jester opened the ledger a page at a time
> At full dusk, Sivra drove toward it.
> as the last daylight thinned
- Problem: The timeline contradicts itself. The Jester promises addresses at sunset but reads the first one in the truck before sunset (L135; then L185, "at sunset I turn the next page"). Three hours of pages follow, and only then is it "full dusk", with the group entering the tenement "as the last daylight thinned". Full dusk can't come three hours after sunset, and daylight can't still be thinning after full dusk. Then, after a short tenement scene, they enter the Baths at 11:38 (L249), leaving about four hours unaccounted for. The story never says where the truck is during the three hours (the clinic? just driving?). Also, fifteen hours after midnight (L41) is 3 p.m., not "late in the afternoon".
- Fix: Pick one timetable and make every clock line agree with it. A minimal version: the first address comes early (the Jester can own jumping his own schedule in one clause), the paging runs until sunset, and they reach the tenement at dusk. One clause at L249 then covers the missing hours (the crawl through the passage, Sivra blacking out, or waiting out searchers). Adjust "late in the afternoon" or "fifteen hours". Keep both 11:57 bookends (L11, L363).

### C2 · Major · In the truck the reader can't tell what Olt wants

- Where: L127, L173, L183, L187–L191, L249
- Text:
> "Drive there," Olt told Sivra at once. "I can show you. No bodies, no locks.
> Olt saw the turn and grabbed the wheel.
> Olt had remembered the route after all.
- Problem: Olt's motive and the causality around it don't track. He asks for a hospital (L127). Then he urges the tenement and invites Sivra to inspect it (L173). Then, with no stated reason, he grabs the wheel when she turns toward a clinic (L183). Then he gives wrong directions to the very tenement he urged (L187). Finally the narration says he "remembered the route after all" and "lied because the only way to the studio passed the dead" (L249), though on the page he never claimed to have forgotten it. The wheel grab has to stay, because it causes the forecast revision (glass in the throat), but at that moment the reader can't say what Olt wants.
- Fix: Give Olt one steady want for the drive and one plain spoken reason at the wheel grab. The simplest consistent version: he wants to reach the Jester's red room (the invitation at L35) and bargain before the next page is read, so the clinic is lost time. "No bodies" is a bluff to discredit room six, and his wrong directions are an attempt to reach the Baths some other way. Then make L249 match what was shown, and drop "remembered the route after all" unless you add a line where he claims to have forgotten the way. Fold in D5 so that "save me or hold court" states the same choice.

### C3 · Minor · "Her own memory supplied the rest" treats a known memory as new

- Where: L209, the tenement
- Text:
> Her own memory supplied the rest
- Problem: A close-third POV cheat. Sivra has always known that she lit the stair rubbish and heard pounding, and she heard "beloved arsonist" at L35 and "eleven" on the record. Framing it as a memory that arrives now reads as the narrator holding something back, not Sivra. The reveal itself (L209) and the final confession (L373) are the story's spine and must stay.
- Fix: Recast the sentence as the thing she has not let herself think about: she knew her own part, but never that people were inside. Add a fair plant through D5's line. Do not move the reveal that she lit the stairs any earlier.

### C4 · Minor · "Witness" points to a motive the story never fills in

- Where: L221 (with L89), the tenement
- Text:
> Siv, I needed the witness gone.
- Problem: An unresolved referent. Olt's slip means the fire was set to silence room six, but the story never says what she witnessed before the fire; her testimony (L167) covers only the fire itself. The slip still lands (the fire was aimed at one person), so this is Minor, but the reader is left asking "witness to what?"
- Fix: Add one clause, in L221 or in room six's testimony, naming what she saw before the fire (for example, Olt drawing blood for the Jester). Keep it inside the word budget.

### C5 · Minor · Props and staging

- Where: L147, the truck window; L225, the tenement; L359 and L369, the sealed passage and the broadcast
- Text:
> From the portable radio clipped to Sivra's belt
> Bring cutters for the culvert
- Problem: A portable radio appears on Sivra's belt without her ever picking one up: she left her garage receiver behind, and the office radio sat on a shelf. Cutters don't clear the masonry that sealed the passage (L359). At L147 Olt writes MASTHEAD BATHS in blood and then says it aloud at once, and nothing explains why he wrote it; fear of the radio overhearing would need the "listening" beat to come first.
- Fix: Have her take the office radio at L75 (a few words). Change "cutters" to a tool that fits rubble, or point the cutters at the welded fire door. Either give the blood-writing a reason (he writes because he thinks the radio is listening, and only Sivra's look makes him speak) or replace it with a spoken line.
