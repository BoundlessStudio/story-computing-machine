# Outline

## Story

- Premise and central promise: The Crimson Jester airs a disc whose doomed voice is his own. Producer Salma Drey has one day to prevent his death while he plans his greatest show. Deliver the scream, confession, and final words in changed context; the witness becomes the city’s death spectacle.
- Focal pressure or attachment: Salma built the station and alone sees behind the persona. She wants him alive and last words treated as private speech. He wants the broadcast preserved but cannot admit that being witnessed matters more than being saved.
- Counterforce or complication: The record fixes an outcome but withholds its cause. The Jester’s legend attracts grief and a hostile caller locating the station. Silence and retreat offer Salma’s best chance; his performance reveals their position. The caller suffered a real violation but chooses retaliation.
- POV, distance, and information limit: Close third past through Salma, intimate with room tone, signal behavior, and his off-mic tells. She cannot replay the original, contextualize its fragments, or know his earliest dealings with the discs. Never enter his or the caller’s thoughts.
- Governing movement and time shape: Twenty-four hours contract from citywide broadcast to airless relay room. Four microphone returns enlarge danger while off-air exchanges expose the frightened man. End inside the station’s immediate aftermath, without public proof, investigation, succession, or reform.
- Speculative rule or ordinary-world constraint: Before midnight, a blank disc appears at the station. It holds three chronological fragments—scream, confession, final words—from one person who will die within signal reach before the following midnight. One needle pass erases the groove; mundane recordings persist. It gives no name, cause, or context. Every authenticated subject has died despite intervention. Nothing compels broadcast; neither disc nor transmitter is sentient. Source, selection, and causation remain unknown.
- Dialogue promise: A predatory show voice collides with untidy private familiarity: theatrical address, technical hand signals, grief-struck calls, petulant jokes, failed listening, and consequential dead air.
- Dialogic medium: Live speech, headphone talkback, callers, remembered broadcast fragments, gesture, and deliberate silence. The future recording cannot answer; the microphone keeps changing who possesses or overhears private speech.
- Dialogue engine: Salma needs station control; he needs her labor and witness without requesting care. Opening or muting the channel changes leverage, exposes history, and enlarges or narrows the caller’s route.

## Voice

- Narrative texture: Move by listening—needle grit, relay clicks, breaths under music, glove leather, and recontextualizing room tone. Salma notices sound first; visuals sharpen when costume contradicts body.
- Conversational texture: On air, extravagant menace meets interruption, mockery, grief, and poor explanation. Off air, familiarity yields instructions, stale jokes, unfinished accusations, and unpretty silence.
- Rhetorical ownership: The Jester owns theatrical metaphor, predatory humor, affectionate evasion, and assumed authority. Salma owns sonic evidence, dry correction, practical authority, and listening. Callers own rumor, accusation, gallows play, and misreading.
- Pressure behavior: He grows ornate on air and inarticulate off it, then loses timing. Salma frays into repetition and direct commands. Talkback overlap and muting cause misunderstanding.
- Relationship movement: He wants his legend preserved and motives absolved but cannot ask to be mourned. She wants trust and privacy but cannot promise forgiveness. Exchanges transfer feed control, expose his first violation, destroy professional loyalty, and leave her attached enough to deny his live death.
- Anti-default: Avoid polished investigators decoding prophecy or a public explanation that converts the city. No clever loophole: center complicity, performance addiction, poor explanation, and ownership of a dying voice.

## Beats

1. At midnight, the Jester frames the disc like an eye with a dark glove; red hat, wire glasses, grin, and red-black light make the mask. Salma recognizes his unperformed voice and an unheard confession. He lets the record finish.
2. Salma urges evacuation. He refuses, schedules more broadcasts, and calls his predicted death proof of the show’s honesty. She suppresses location cues and controls doors and power; he keeps a hidden transmitter route.
3. Morning calls bring disbelief, glee, imitation, and grief. The hostile caller tests building details. Salma learns the confession concerns the first doomed person, whose privacy request the Jester ignored once terror drew an audience.
4. Rescue and confrontation diverge. Salma can move him or hold the station, not both. He goes live to control his legend and give her a reason to stay. Talkback failures reveal she has shielded him from consequences.
5. Near dusk, tampered doors show the caller has found them. Salma kills the feed and attempts a silent descent. The costumed Jester restores transmission, drawing the intruder toward himself; protection and appetite for audience remain inseparable.
6. The confrontation produces the recorded scream and a fatal wound. Behind a locked relay door and disconnected microphone, the confession and final words become frightened private speech. Context complicates but does not excuse the midnight broadcast. Salma stays without absolving him.
7. Salma blocks the intruder and automatic loop while responders approach. End on her bodily refusal to narrate the death for the waiting frequency; introduce neither a new disc nor a successor.
