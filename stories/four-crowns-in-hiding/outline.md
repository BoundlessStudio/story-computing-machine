# Outline

## Story

- Proposed reader-facing title: *Four Crowns in Hiding*.
- Premise and central promise: Thirteen years after four royal sisters were separated among the poor river kingdoms of Dunbarrow, Greyfen, Bellmarsh, and Reedwater, Regent Vaust learns which kingdoms sheltered them but not their assumed identities. Ceyra, the youngest and now a freight-boat pilot, has one falling tide to collect her sisters in the kingdoms' shared grain punt and pass the outer marsh before Vaust's riders close the banks. Get all four beyond his immediate reach, with aliases and destinations chosen by them rather than another guardian. Their escape should depend on what their disregarded homes taught them, not royal magic or a triumphant claim.
- Focal pressure or attachment: Ceyra wants every sister aboard, but separation has reduced them to childhood roles she mistakes for knowledge: Miren commands, Selaune soothes, Avedra follows. The adults have local obligations and incompatible ideas about whether being princesses should govern their next lives. Ceyra's useful limit is treating rescue like freight—fixed stops, correct order, no room for a passenger to refuse or change the route.
- Counterforce or complication: Vaust wants the remaining heirs dead before his son inherits a disputed crown. He comes personally with a small mounted party because a public warrant would reveal that the sisters survived. He remembers the eldest two but has no magical test. The punt gains concealment from ordinary cargo and shallow draft, yet every pickup costs tide and gathers the targets together. Miren wants reunion to begin a royal return; Avedra will not abandon a sick foster parent without arranging care.
- POV, distance, and information limit: Close third-person past through Ceyra, attentive to current, load, pole depth, bank sounds, and the mismatch between memory and adult sisters. She knows the route and the warning's bare facts, but cannot enter the others' minds, confirm what Vaust learned, or hear conversations ashore. Keep the thirteen-year separation in gestures and expectations rather than scene-length flashbacks.
- Governing movement and time shape: One continuous night, moving downstream through four pickups and an exposed final reach. Each sister changes the boat's balance, plan, and Ceyra's understanding. Render the first reunion, one delayed departure, Vaust's closest pass, and the final route choice; compress travel by landmarks and diminishing tide. The decisive venue is a cramped punt in reeds, not a palace, court, battle, or assembly. End with practical forward movement, not coronation or symbolic closure.
- Speculative rule or ordinary-world constraint: Use no operative magic. Records identify placements and names but cannot authenticate blood or track aliases. The shared river's freight channel is navigable only on a falling spring tide; heavy patrol craft ground, horses detour around flooded cuts, and the final reed gate settles onto mud until the next cycle. Poverty means few guards, patched boats, and scarce medicine, but also thin registries and local skills Vaust undervalues.
- Dialogue pressure: Ceyra seeks immediate obedience; Miren wants agreement to return as claimants; Avedra bargains for care time; Selaune tests each plan's cost to locals. Ceyra uses load and timing language, Miren conditional commands, Avedra concrete care details, and Selaune questions. No sister holds the whole warning or route.

## Beats

1. In Dunbarrow, Ceyra receives the warning after the last legal cargo loads. Establish the patched punt, tide deadline, and her rehearsed memories; she leaves without permission that cannot arrive in time.
2. At Greyfen's mill landing, Miren boards with copied search orders and immediately redirects the escape toward their lost throne. The reunion misfires; Ceyra departs before resolving it.
3. Bellmarsh's landing is watched. Selaune reaches the punt inside an ordinary peat delivery and brings evidence that Vaust rides the north bank, expecting four frightened dependents rather than locally competent adults.
4. In Reedwater, Avedra refuses to board until substitute medicine and morning instructions reach her foster parent. Ceyra yields the schedule, and the sisters divide the tasks without revealing themselves publicly.
5. Vaust reaches the bank as they clear Reedwater and calls Miren by a childhood form of address. She does not answer; Ceyra cannot tell whether her stillness concealed or confirmed them.
6. In the outer reeds, their loaded punt cannot take Ceyra's planned channel. Miren proposes a deeper inlet leading toward Vaust. Avedra knows a neglected salt-work cut, Selaune identifies deliverable cargo, and Miren uses the copied orders as timing intelligence.
7. They redistribute cargo and poles, pass the reed gate before it grounds, and leave Vaust's horses and any heavier boat behind the tide. The success is physical and narrow, not proof they deserve to rule.
8. Beyond the marsh, they choose separate aliases but one coastal destination; any future claim will require four willing answers. Stop as Ceyra teaches the next steering shift. Vaust remains dangerous, but the sisters have become authors of their concealment together.
