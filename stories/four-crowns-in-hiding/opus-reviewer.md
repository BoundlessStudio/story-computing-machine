# Opus review: Four Crowns in Hiding

Independent audit for a later editing pass. It is not a `review.md` verdict,
does not change canon state, and does not authorize edits by itself.

## Verdict

- Dialogue: REVISE — the Oriel Strand evidence line contradicts what is on the frame (Blocking), and nearly every host–princess exchange ends the same way: the host rules, the princess concedes.
- Prose: REVISE — the prose is concrete and controlled, but narration keeps stating the theme and explaining lines the reader has already understood.
- Continuity and prompt: REVISE — the prompt is fulfilled, but at midnight Kalmid and the only palace shard are both at Foldgate station and with the riders below the fork.
- Edit scope: heavy-dialogue
- Existing review.md said: Verdict PASS, "Dialogue: PASS"; disagree — it missed the cut-gap incoherence, Kalmid being in two places, and the ruling pattern that runs through most exchanges.

The plot is never unclear in the dialogue. What is wrong is that the exchanges all run on one pattern. In each refuge a princess proposes something, the host answers with a crisp ruling about who owns the equipment or the decision ("My bells, my challenge", "Weavers decide what leaves this frame", "The road can. We built it."), and the princess gives way, sometimes by repeating his words back. As a result Breqen, Brikka, Rhevos and Ullet read as one dry official in four workshops. Among the sisters, only Mirdakai's flood of numbers comes through as a voice of her own. The narration tells us about Nuvaya's impatience instead of letting us hear it, and Meraine's quietness comes out as riddling lines that the next paragraph has to explain. The edit pass should fix the two coherence failures first (the cut gap and the climax geography). After that, keep the hosts' real authority, which is the story's point about "lowly" kingdoms, but show it through hands, evidence and local knowledge rather than rulings. Let the four sisters sound different under pressure, and give Kalmid a line with a purpose beyond stating the theme.

## Dialogue findings

### D1 · Blocking · Cut-gap evidence contradicts the band on the frame

- Where: L123–L127, Oriel Strand, Rhevos checks the band in the crosswind
- Text:
> One section fluttered while the rest pulled taut.
> The east gust drops every ninth group. We leave the loose strand in the record. Whoever joined this packet trimmed it clean.
> Meraine watched the loose strand dance.
- Problem: Ground/staging and referent. Rhevos says the tell is that the loose ninth-group strand is missing ("trimmed it clean"). But the demonstration shows a section fluttering, and the next sentence has Meraine watching "the loose strand dance", so the strand that is supposed to be missing is on screen. "Someone cut out a gap" can also mean either removing a gap or making one. A reader cannot work out what was tampered with, and this is one of the four findings the plan is built on (L145 "CUT GAP", L183 "Cut wind gap", L265 "inserted its loose ninth gap").
- Fix: Make the demonstration show an absence. The ninth-group places, which always hang loose after crossing the gusty strand, pull taut along the whole band. Rhevos's line should then state that absence, with no ambiguous "cut out" and no "it". Change L127 so Meraine watches the place where nothing moves. Keep the label consistent at L145 and L183. Illustrative only: "Every ninth group the gust drops a turn, and we leave it hanging. Look — nothing hangs. This never came through the gusts."

### D2 · Major · Every host scene closes on the same ruling-and-concession beat

- Where: L43 and L51–L53 (Nalevane, first scene); L97 (Rusk Hollow); L143 (Oriel Strand); L179 (Ternhold); L203–L205 (Nalevane, planning); L239 (Oriel Strand, bait); L253–L255 and L261–L263 (Ternhold, bait)
- Text:
> Everything that crosses my mirrors includes my tide.
> I will ask. You will read the answer beside me.
> My bells, my challenge.
> Weavers decide what leaves this frame.
> We can. We have not decided to.
> Those are acceptable. Stop calling them conditions as if you grant them.
> Contingencies, then.
> It appears to. The route must be Nuvaya's choice, and Ullet must permit it.
> The road can. We built it.
> Ternhold sends it. You set the route marks.
- Problem: Identical Voices plus correction ping-pong plus a procedure routine. Four hosts from four different trades speak the same terse jurisdictional sentence, and each exchange ends on a ruling or a comeback. "My bells, my challenge" answers nothing; Brikka is already turning the shutter and Mirdakai has not challenged her. "Conditions"/"Contingencies" is an argument about word choice, and "contingencies" is the wrong word for Ilvara's rules; the error carries into L219. Rhevos raises the one real compact stake ("That exposes Ternhold") and gets back a correction ("It appears to.") instead of an answer. "You can take five?" exists to set up "The road can."
- Fix: Keep the hosts in charge, but show it at most once per refuge, through action, local evidence or a plain question, and cut the princesses' echoing concessions. Specific cuts:
  - Cut "Everything that crosses my mirrors…" and let Breqen show the painted tide mark instead.
  - Breqen hands Ilvara the ledger without stating terms; drop "Agreed."
  - Cut "My bells, my challenge." / "Yes."
  - Rhevos starts weaving, or agrees in her own teasing idiom.
  - Replace Ullet's "We can. We have not decided to." with a concrete worry about his watch.
  - Cut the conditions/contingencies exchange and fix "three contingencies" at L219.
  - Give Rhevos's objection a real reply about the compact.
  - Answer "You can take five?" plainly.

  Keep one spoken ruling at most. The best place for it is Ullet's L261, because learning to wait is Nuvaya's arc. There, cut her parroted "Ternhold sends it." and have her hand over the route marks. Illustrative only (L255): "If four go onto the bridge road, yes. The gate's heavy, but two can drop it."

### D3 · Major · Nuvaya's haste is narrated while Ullet wins every line

- Where: L157–L169, Ternhold, the ladder
- Text:
> Ullet caught the ladder before she could jump the last three rungs. “Which warning?”
> The one that says riders are on the dry road.
> We don't have time for this.
> Then don't waste it being vague.
> Nuvaya bit back the next interruption.
- Problem: Uniform fluency under pressure, a Punchline Patch, and a Tennis Match. The youngest sister has just seen five armed riders heading for the place named in the summons, yet she speaks in tidy, complete sentences. Her impatience lives only in the narration ("bit back the next interruption"), even though she never interrupts anyone on the page. Ullet's "Which warning?" asks for nothing Nuvaya can supply; she has already said what the warning is. The line exists to draw her sarcastic repeat, and that repeat exists to set up his closing comeback "Then don't waste it being vague."
- Fix: Let her haste be heard: run-on sentences, talking over him, one detail missing or wrong. Have Ullet ask his real question (marker, speed, sightlines) straight away, and cut "Then don't waste it being vague." Shoving the route board at her already wins the point. Then "bit back the next interruption" refers to something we have actually heard. The L169 gloss is covered under P1. Illustrative only: "Five riders on the dry road, no lanterns — send it to every route, Ullet, now, they're nearly at the —"

### D4 · Major · Meraine's lines are teasers the narration has to decode

- Where: L117–L135, Oriel Strand, first reading of the band
- Text:
> You know it's a man?
> I know what he failed to imagine.
> Ilvara will ask whether the seal can be trusted,
> I want to know what answer he has already prepared for.
- Problem: Ground/referent, a Punchline Patch, and an explanatory chorus. "I know what he failed to imagine" does not answer Rhevos's question, and neither Rhevos nor the reader can tell what it refers to until the narration at L127–L129 explains it (the invitation names none of the hosts). Rhevos's questions ("You know it's a man?", "And you?") only prompt Meraine's next neat line. Her forecast of each sister also mostly replays scenes the reader has just watched. The real discovery, that the summons ignores the people who hid them, is never said to Rhevos, who is one of those people. That is the relationship beat this scene is missing.
- Fix: Replace "I know what he failed to imagine." with an answer Rhevos can follow: name the tutor, or deflect in a way she can read. Move the omission out of narration and into Meraine's speech to Rhevos. Cut the forecast down to at most one clause, and drop "And you?" as a setup so that Meraine's decision at L141 follows a practical question from Rhevos. The self-description at L135 can survive as a thought. Illustrative only: "Our names, our order, our old house. Not a word about you, or anyone else who hid us."

### D5 · Major · Kalmid speaks only the story's thesis

- Where: L289–L291 and L303, the east shelf
- Text:
> Any general could make an army of one of you.
> You cannot keep them hidden forever,
- Problem: The Philosopher and stock villain last words, at the decisive exchange. Kalmid thinks Nuvaya is about to step out onto the shelf. Calling out his reason for hunting her gives her a reason to stay hidden, so the line has no tactical aim; it restates L45 for the reader, and L291 then explains his silence (see P1). After the net, "You cannot keep them hidden forever" is a stock parting line that the coda answers directly. Ullet's "Put the glass down." is excellent, and it lands because it ignores the speech.
- Fix: Give his call a purpose that fits his belief that Nuvaya is arriving alone ahead of her sisters, such as a lure or a tutor's command, and let the half-drawn blade carry the menace. After the net, cut L303 or replace it with something his position produces (calling his escort, demanding who is there). Let "Put the glass down." answer the shard he is still gripping; if L303 goes, change "Ullet's answer" to "Ullet's voice". Illustrative only: "Nuvaya. Come into the light. Your sisters are close behind you."

### D6 · Major · Climax staging: where Ullet stands and whose shutters fire

- Where: L277–L305, Ternhold lamp roof and east shelf
- Text:
> Ullet glanced at Nuvaya. The last reversal was hers to time.
> the two shutter crews hidden above him
> Ullet reversed the east shutters.
> Ullet's answer carried from the rocks.
- Problem: Ground/staging at the key beat. Nuvaya is on the lamp roof watching the shelf "at that distance", and Ullet is next to her to exchange a glance. Two lines later his voice comes "from the rocks" near Kalmid. The light has to strike "from behind Kalmid", which only the hidden crews above him could do, yet the text says Ullet himself reverses the shutters. The reader cannot place the two speakers of the decisive exchange.
- Fix: At L287, state in one clause where the roof sits relative to the shelf (across the quarry gap, within shouting range). At L299, make it "Ullet signaled; the crews above him reversed the east shutters." Then his line carries across from the roof, and L341 ("Ullet climbed the ladder") still works. Alternatively, send Ullet down to the rocks before L293 and cut the glance.

### D7 · Major · The coda runs one stay-or-go exchange four times

- Where: L317–L347, dawn in all four refuges
- Text:
> No. Strike that. I remain here tonight. Tomorrow I will ask again.
> I'll still be here after breakfast. Do not become accustomed to the sensible part.
> If the offer remains.
> No. The shelf lamps will need filling when they bring Kalmid down.
- Problem: All four final exchanges have the same shape: leaving is raised, the sister declines, and she returns to a maintenance task. Each could be moved to another refuge unchanged. Mirdakai's second line is a last-word quip in Ilvara's formal register ("Do not become accustomed"), not in her own run-on voice. "I'll still be here" also states the staying outright, which her crossed-out repair times already show.
- Fix: Keep the four-refuge coda, the tasks, Breqen shutting the plate, Ilvara's honestly partial "Strike that" line, and the Meraine–Rhevos exchange, which should be the only place staying is said aloud. End Mirdakai's beat on "After breakfast." / "Sensible.", or give her a last line in her own flood-and-correct voice. For Nuvaya, either make Ullet's offer specific (see D8) or drop it and end on the wick and the three receipts.
