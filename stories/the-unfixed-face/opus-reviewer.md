# Opus review: The Unfixed Face

Independent audit for a later editing pass. It is not a `review.md` verdict,
does not change canon state, and does not authorize edits by itself.

## Verdict

- Dialogue: REVISE — the exchanges carried by Mekran's signals work, but the sisters argue in one shared correct-and-rule register, and three key exchanges (the recognize question, the trust break, "Dad's base?") don't deliver what they are there to do.
- Prose: REVISE — "not X. Y." reversals are the default way of adding emphasis, and the narration keeps labelling Erisel's flaw after each line that already shows it.
- Continuity and prompt: PASS — the prompt's premise, length (about 3,260 words), rating and craft profile are met; only minor mechanism, end-state and staging nits.
- Edit scope: targeted-dialogue
- Existing review.md said: Verdict PASS, "Dialogue: PASS"; I disagree on dialogue. It credits distinct voices that merge whenever the sisters disagree.

The story's real strength is its medium for dialogue. Mekran can't speak, so he talks through device glyphs, temple light and hand pressure. As his channels fail, the rules for giving consent get tighter, and this builds to Erisel's flare mistake and the left/right protocol. That thread mostly works and should be touched lightly. The failure is between the sisters. Whenever Erisel and Korrin disagree, each corrects the other's word and closes on a ruling ("There's a difference." "This building is client material." "It names a presence."). As a result Korrin, who is practical, acts first and says "Dad", argues in Erisel's clinical register, and their one real confrontation over their father's face plays out as a dispute about records. The edit pass should thin those volleys and move Korrin's side of each argument into action, duty and Dad. It should make three exchanges say what they mean (L165–L181, L207–L235, L349–L355), and it should stop narrating Erisel's over-specification after every instance.

## Dialogue findings

### D1 · Major · Sister disagreements run on correction ping-pong

- Where: L47 (arrival), L65–L71 (amber marks), L197–L199 (inventory of channels); L257–L267 is covered in D4 and L117–L121 in D7; L21 and L373–L381 are exceptions to keep
- Text:
> "They expired without you. I pressed the confirmation. There's a difference."
> "I didn't say client material."
> "This building is client material."
> "Don't inventory losses over him."
> I'm inventorying the channels we have.
- Problem: Identical Voices / correction ping-pong. Almost every time the sisters disagree, one rewords the other's verb or category and the other rules on it. Exchanges close on a last-word line ("There's a difference." "This building is client material."). With the tags covered, Korrin's lines here can't be told apart from Erisel's clinical register, although everywhere else Korrin is the blunt, practical one. L67–L71 also fails on Ground: Erisel's own winter-lungs preset is not client material. So Korrin's "I didn't say client material" is correct, and Erisel's reply escalates to a slogan instead of giving a reason. At L199 Korrin answers Erisel's rebuke by turning her word back on her, when Korrin's characteristic move is to act.
- Fix:
  - Keep L21 ("I didn't forget. I declined."), because it sets up the settling-salts payoff.
  - Keep the L373–L381 food banter. It is the one place where this rhythm is play, and it will read as release once the earlier volleys are thinned.
  - Cut "There's a difference." at L47.
  - At L67, give Erisel a reason that concerns her own preset, then cut L69–L71 and let L73 end the exchange (Lucian's face preparing an argument, Erisel turning away). Illustrative only: "I'll check it tonight. Let this one expire."
  - At L199, cut "I'm inventorying the channels we have." so Korrin goes straight to teaching Mekran the squeeze code. She answers the rebuke by doing the useful thing.

### D2 · Major · Recognize-or-hurt exchange doesn't track its own signals

- Where: L165–L181, west-cabinet search, second preview
- Text:
> "Do you recognize it, or does it hurt?"
> One finger opened.
> "He answered the last thing you said, not the choice I gave him."
> He tapped KNOWN. Then he tapped the bench twice and pointed at the next cabinet.
- Problem: Ground/uptake.
  - Erisel's either/or question is the ambiguous one. Mekran's own glyph (an eye and a question mark) raises "recognize", and Korrin's "Recognize?" just reads his glyph back to him.
  - Answering the last question asked is not an error, so Erisel's objection names the wrong fault. What is actually uncertain goes unsaid: a single opened finger is not his thumb-to-forefinger yes (L109, L137), and not clearly a no either.
  - "We separate questions" is followed by a two-option tablet. Its result, KNOWN, leads straight to the next cabinet, and the reader never learns why a recognized trait isn't his return pattern.
  - So the procedure change at L181 rests on a lesson the reader can't reconstruct.
- Fix:
  - Decide what Erisel doubts, the signal or Korrin answering for him, and have her say it plainly. Illustrative only: "One finger isn't his yes. Ask so he can point."
  - Keep Korrin's KNOWN/WRONG tablet as her practical fix, and let Erisel accept it without comment.
  - Add one clause that makes KNOWN readable, so that moving on follows. For example, he recognizes the trait from the copied gliding form he is wearing, not from his home body.
  - Replace the stock beat "Korrin's jaw set." with something specific to her, or cut it.

### D3 · Major · Trust break has no staged trigger and ends on a thesis line

- Where: L207–L235, fifth cabinet, Korrin soothing Mekran with Lucian's script
- Text:
> Then light moved beneath Korrin's temples: the old sister-code
> He could not tell them which part had broken trust, only that something had.
> "Korrin remembers it. The preset has his voice shape, not him."
- Problem: Ground/staging at a key beat.
  - Nothing visible prompts Korrin's "don't make this worse" code. Between L207 and L217 Erisel only remembers, so the reader can't tell whether Korrin is signalling her sister (about what?) or letting the code slip without meaning to.
  - Mekran has already been told twice that this is Korrin and that Lucian is dead (L89, L147), so the code can't reveal that. What it can reveal is two handlers signalling over his head while one soothes him in a dead man's voice, and the text never lets the reader see this. The narration then admits the vagueness instead of fixing it.
  - Erisel's reply is a Philosopher line. It states the story's premise (face and voice are not the person) to a man who already knows Lucian is dead, gives him nothing he can use, and needs the narration to claim "Mekran needed the fact."
- Fix:
  - Stage a trigger that Mekran can see. For example, Erisel moves or starts to speak when she hears her father's words, and Korrin flashes the code at her. Mekran's single squeeze then reads without explanation, so cut the last sentence of L227.
  - Cut Erisel's L229 line and the narration inside it, or reduce it to a plain statement Mekran can use. Her offered hand and his "stop that too" can carry the beat alone.
  - Keep "She did not apologize." and the stilled borrowed expressions at L237.
  - Nothing added here may suggest that Lucian is in the preset.

### D4 · Major · The sisters' one real confrontation is argued as record taxonomy

- Where: L251–L273, private shelf, after "You're wearing it."
- Text:
> "I knew there were fragments. Not that one was his."
> "The carrier index isn't a client record."
> "It names a presence. That was enough for me not to look."
- Problem: Procedure routine and Identical Voices at the decisive exchange between the sisters.
  - What is at stake is personal. Korrin has worn her father's face all day. She knew Lucian hid fragments from the leak period in it (L243), and she knew Mekran's last visit was during the leak (L157). Yet she let the search work through the west cabinets.
  - Erisel never asks the obvious question: why didn't you say so when he came in? Instead both sisters argue about which category of record Korrin was allowed to open.
  - "It names a presence" is jargon the reader can't pin down. Korrin, who says "Dad" and argues from duty and clients, is suddenly ruling on terms like Erisel.
  - The exchange changes what the characters know but not the relationship, and Korrin's silence during the search stays unexplained.
- Fix:
  - Aim Erisel's challenge at the real cost: Korrin stayed silent while Mekran waited.
  - Let Korrin's answer come from her attachment and duty (Dad, the closing, his seal). Keep it short, possibly evasive or unfinished, and in her own register. She need not win or confess.
  - Cut or replace L265–L267. Illustrative only: "It was Dad's seal. I wasn't breaking it on a guess."
  - Keep "You're wearing it." / "I know." and Mekran's knock at L269 as the interruption.

### D5 · Major · Narration pre-digests Erisel's flaw and the key lines

- Where: L227, L229, L255, L269, L273, L309, L321, from the trust break through the flare correction
- Text:
> Too much explanation already, but Mekran needed the fact.
> The words were hers. Flat, late, impossible to soften with anyone else's mouth.
> They had made him wait through a family quarrel while his breathing changed.
> She was over-specifying again; she could hear it and could not stop.
- Problem: Mechanics. Right after each important line, the narration spells out its subtext and names Erisel's behaviour under pressure again, so the reader has nothing left to infer. The speech at L321 already shows the over-specification. Mekran's knock at L269 already shows what the quarrel cost him. At L309 her recognition is spelled out twice: "Pain, not permission." and then a sentence explaining why she erred.
- Fix: Cut the asides at L229 and L321, all of L273, the second sentence of L255 and the second sentence of L269. Reduce L309 to one short beat of recognition. Keep "though there was nothing left to open" at L341 once the rest is trimmed; one gloss at the close is affordable.

### D6 · Major · "Dad's base?" closes the father thread on an epigram about an unclear object

- Where: L347–L357, sunset purge at the archive controls
- Text:
> "Dad's base?"
> Erisel looked once at the emptied gold cell. "It already did what it was holding."
> Gold drained from Korrin's pattern into the reader.
- Problem: Philosopher / last-word line at the final meaningful exchange about Lucian.
  - "It already did what it was holding" is a crafted aphorism with broken grammar: a preset cannot "do what it was holding". It also restates the theme (the preset was a vessel, not Dad).
  - The object is unclear. L291 drains the gold into the reader, L347 spares "the private cell in the reader," and L355 calls the cell "emptied". The reader can't tell what is left of Lucian's preset, or whether it is kept or sterilized.
  - The choice costs Erisel nothing visible, and Korrin, who wore the face, gets no say.
- Fix:
  - Settle the cell's state first (see C2).
  - Then make the question and answer plain and costly: someone with a stake chooses, and the sterilization shows the outcome. Illustrative only: after a look at the cell, Erisel asks "Do you want it?", and Korrin answers by keeping her fingers on the pad.
  - Keep "Your preset too?" / "The winter lungs stay." It is a good quiet reversal of L59–L63.

### D7 · Minor · Cloth exchange misdescribes the touch

- Where: L111–L123, first cloth on the bench
- Text:
> Korrin brought the cloth. He jerked from its touch, light flaring bright along his jaw.
> "I did. He didn't answer."
> "You touched while you asked."
- Problem: Ground. In the staging, Korrin touches first and asks "Too cold?" only after he jerks. Korrin's "I did" claims she asked before contact, and Erisel's "while" gives a third version. The gist survives, but both lines misreport the action at the beat that introduces "ask before contact". The volley is also another round of correction during triage.
- Fix: Cut L119–L121 so that "Ask before contact." leads straight to L123, where Korrin holds up the cloth and waits; that already shows she took the point. If a reply is kept, make it match the staging: she asked after the touch.

### D8 · Minor · "Two channels. Confirmed." counts a flare

- Where: L279–L287, release consent
- Text:
> His temple light gave three pulses, then broke into a white flare.
> "Two channels," Erisel said. "Confirmed."
- Problem: Ground and consent logic. Erisel counts as confirmation a light signal that breaks into the same kind of flare she later calls "pain, not permission" (L309). Either this is an unmarked earlier instance of her mistake, which weakens her later recognition, or the flare is an accident of wording.
- Fix: Make L283 a clean cadence (three, pause, three), so the release consent is sound and the error at L301–L309 is her first. Alternatively, keep the flare and let L309 register that she had already done this once.

### D9 · Minor · Small referents and verbs that don't fit

- Where: L31 (arrival), L151 (after "Lucian is dead"), L189–L193 (current capacity)
- Text:
> "Temporary face. The northbound carriage seals badly around narrow sinuses."
> Erisel swallowed the correction she had been about to make.
> "Current capacity," Erisel said. "Can you still distinguish those pulses?"
> He held up three fingers, folded one, then frowned at his hand as if it had betrayed him.
- Problem:
  - A carriage can't seal around sinuses; the reader has to guess that cabin pressure is meant.
  - "The correction she had been about to make" has no recoverable object.
  - "Distinguish" is the wrong verb for a question about producing signals.
  - His three-fingers-minus-one answer is never read, though Erisel's list of channels implies she understood it.
- Fix:
  - Say what the carriage does to narrow sinuses (pressure, dry air), in Erisel's clipped way.
  - Name the correction in a few words, or cut the clause.
  - Change "distinguish" to a verb about making the pulses.
  - Have Erisel either read his finger answer or register that she can't.

- Problem: Constructions of the "not X. Y." and "X, not Y" type carry most of the emphatic moments. They include triplet fragments (L149, L127) and the thematic statement at the resolution (L333), which explains what the concrete sentence after it already shows.

### P2 · Minor · One-line narration pulse in the middle

- Where: L219–L235 (trust break), L301–L307 (flare), L337–L345 (after the last stage)
- Text:
> Mekran saw it.
> Stop that too.
> So she stopped.
- Problem: In these runs, single-sentence paragraphs become the default pulse. Mekran's signals are his turns in the conversation and can stay on their own lines. Pure narration beats ("Korrin fell silent." "So she stopped." "Erisel disconnected the reader.") break up the reader's attention. "Stop that too." turns the narration's translation of his signals into a narrator's quip.
- Fix: Merge pure narration beats into the paragraphs next to them. Keep Mekran's signal turns, and the "Stop." translations where they mark a decision. Consider reducing "Stop that too." / "So she stopped." to one beat.

## Continuity and prompt findings

### C1 · Minor · Preview mechanism contradicts the consent limit

- Where: L139, L165, L183, archive search
- Text:
> "Only that pattern. No previews beyond what's necessary to identify it."
> Mekran watched the neutral previews cross the tablet
> Amber irises opened briefly in Korrin's borrowed face. A breathing lattice feathered across Erisel's ribs.
> Mekran watched his own hand acquire delicate climbing pads
- Problem: At first the previews are "neutral" images on the tablet. Twenty lines later, anonymous clients' traits appear in the bystanders' bodies and start climbing Mekran's hand, and nobody reacts. That reads as three people being physically revised by strangers' patterns. It cuts against Erisel's consent limit and against "Each anonymous cell answered only a consented cadence that matched its owner."
- Fix: Make the previews visibly non-bodily, for example light projected from the reader onto whoever is nearest, so Mekran can still knock it away. Or keep them bodily and have someone react to the risk. One clause either way.

### C2 · Minor · End state of Lucian's cell and the sterilization order

- Where: L275, L291, L347–L357, sunset
- Text:
> Every shelf glyph turned white except the private cell in the reader.
> Green for released. White for sterilized. Amber for disputed.
> The house sterilized the last unclaimed patterns shelf by shelf
- Problem: White means sterilized (L53), so every shelf is already sterilized when the sunset chime sounds. Yet the sisters then confirm, and the house sterilizes "the last unclaimed patterns". The cell in the reader is spared at L347 but called "emptied" at L355, after the gold drained into it at L291.
- Fix: Make the chime move the glyphs to a pending state, or have the confirmation come first. Decide whether the released gold lands in the reader cell or disperses, and describe the cell the same way at L347 and L355. This also settles the object behind "Dad's base?" (D6).

### C3 · Minor · Time and staging nits

- Where: L23–L25 (arrival), L215 (Lucian's script), L347 (archive controls), L383 (street)
- Text:
> Korrin came through the beaded curtain wearing their father's last face.
> the mourning preset had been stored years earlier
> as though the last six years had narrowed to a usable door
> Yet Korrin crossed to the archive controls without asking where they were
> Across the street, a sign brightened, and both of them squinted.
- Problem:
  - "Last face" conflicts with a preset stored years before Lucian died.
  - "The last six years" has no anchor: Lucian's death is recent and Mekran's visits are half-yearly.
  - "Mekran opened his eyes" (L215) comes with no closing since L149.
  - Korrin would never need to ask where her own studio's controls are, so "without asking" fails as a sign that she is still herself.
  - A brightening sign that makes them squint calls for glare protection, not low-light retinas.
- Fix:
  - Say "last chosen face", or drop "last".
  - Drop "six years" or anchor it.
  - Add a beat where he closes his eyes, or cut "opened his eyes".
  - Replace "without asking where they were" with a gesture that is characteristically Korrin's.
  - Make the street dimming, not a sign brightening, the reason for low-light retinas.
