# Outline

## Story

- Premise and central promise: The night before an unnamed young adult is due to receive an artificial birth-ink graft, an ultraviolet inspection lamp reveals a black-violet tattoo beneath their apparently bare skin. Everyone treated them as the first person born without one. Resolve the promise privately: the mark lets them refuse tattoo-mediated effects entering their body, making the graft unsafe, but it neither erases others’ marks nor defines the life they built without visible power.
- Focal pressure or attachment: They chose the graft after years of insisting they were not unfinished. Canceling could make old humiliation feel justified; proceeding could injure the hidden system. They want one reliable fact and control of disclosure. Their mother wants vindication, their younger sibling wants to celebrate, and the visiting practitioner refuses certainty from one exposure.
- Counterforce or complication: Competing relief, curiosity, and grievance turn a precaution into a claim on the focal person’s meaning. Before dawn, the practitioner must distinguish magical resistance from faulty equipment or a startled user. The focal person’s logistical answers invite others to mistake silence for agreement.
- POV, distance, and information limit: Close second-person past. Attention selects skin temperature, lamp hum, test surfaces, hands, and permission to touch. The focal person cannot identify the hidden fluid, see the full mark beyond illuminated skin, or know its limits before comparison.
- Governing movement and time shape: One pre-dawn hour in a family dye-workroom moves from graft preparation through revelation and controlled comparison to withdrawal of consent. Brief sensory memories arise only when present evidence reframes them. The decisive turn separates discovery from disclosure and changes the morning appointment, without public proof or reform.
- Speculative rule or ordinary-world constraint: A living body forms a birth tattoo from an ink-like fluid and uses its pattern to channel one faculty. Tattoos and tools never choose or cast. The ordinary ultraviolet lamp reveals transparent fluid but neither awakens nor powers it. When the focal person deliberately braces, their mark blocks another tattoo-mediated effect from crossing into their body; otherwise it can pass. It cannot suppress another user, undo created physical effects, prevent ordinary injury, or prove universal immunity. Graft ink crossing the hidden channels poses an unknown risk.

## Voice

- Narrative texture: Second person moves by tactile checks and revisions: sensation, visible evidence, then only the memories that evidence supports. Keep wonder edged by practical suspicion, not destiny language.
- Conversational texture: Talk overlaps at revelation, then breaks into ordinary requests, repeated questions, equipment checks, and incomplete answers. Care appears through making room and asking before touch.
- Rhetorical ownership: The focal person reasons through permissions and distinctions; the practitioner owns conditional comparison and uncertainty; the mother argues from chronology and accumulated wrongs; the sibling notices household analogies and social consequences.
- Pressure behavior: The focal person becomes clipped and logistical before stating boundaries. The mother stacks examples and stops listening. The practitioner slows down and repeats conditions. The sibling loses fluency when asked for secrecy.
- Anti-default: Avoid universal eloquence about identity or a chosen-one coronation. Preserve mishearing, single-purpose technical speech, family interruption, and the unresolved difference between vindication and consent.

## Beats

1. In the dye-workroom, the practitioner checks the artificial ink for tomorrow’s graft. The focal person wants the appointment settled; the mother reviews aftercare while the sibling handles the new ultraviolet lamp.
2. Light spills across the focal person’s wrist. A black-violet line appears beneath the skin and branches beyond the beam. The practitioner stops preparation. Repeated passes confirm a bodily pattern rather than surface dye; ordinary light hides it again.
3. The mother lists failed infant examinations; the sibling reaches for celebration; the practitioner claims only an unseen birth fluid. The focal person asks whether the graft can proceed, whether the mark responds, and who may examine it.
4. Old incidents become hypotheses: direct tattoo blessings slid off them, a sibling’s warming faculty sometimes failed against their skin, yet created flame burned. The practitioner proposes paired comparisons using that harmless warming faculty, a metal cup, and the focal person’s forearm; anyone may stop.
5. The sibling warms the cup. Against the focal person, the effect works while they remain receptive, then fails when they deliberately brace. Under ultraviolet light, concealed lines tighten around the contact point; an object control rules out lamp failure. Establish self-directed resistance, not suppression or theft.
6. The mother assumes the result should be announced; the sibling imagines peers’ reactions. The focal person finally states boundaries: no graft, no wider disclosure tonight, no claim beyond this test. The practitioner records only the medical reason to halt the procedure.
7. They keep the morning appointment as a private consultation. The mother remains dissatisfied, and the sibling must accept that exciting news belongs to someone else. End during ordinary preparation for morning, after the lamp is switched off.

## Continuity

- Canon used: This is a local system. Living people use its abilities; tattoos, inks, lamp, cup, and graft tools are nonliving components. Costs and consent behavior are not universal.
- Boundaries and unknowns: Do not connect birth tattoos to established faculty, identity, light, ink, or body-mark systems. Leave the hidden fluid’s origin, prevalence, anatomy, broader range, and interaction with unrelated magic unresolved. The test does not make black light a universal magic detector.
