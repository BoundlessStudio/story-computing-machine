# Standing Theory — provisional outline

## Promised experience

The original request supplies two speakers, strange fridge contents, and a casually offered theory about alternate realities stocking the fridge. The later direction explicitly asks for “dry humor based on vague obstavations, odd descriptions to others” and limits the setting to “the house/appartment,” rejecting the proposed excursion. The pleasure is now the disparity between an extraordinary possibility and the incomplete, oddly phrased observations people make about it. Understatement and continued uncertainty are supported interpretations; proof, danger, transformation, or a particular relationship is not required. The fridge should remain the subject of the discoveries and humor throughout.

## Chosen direction

A short first-person account of one evening in Bram's apartment. Romy is visiting; they await an ordinary takeaway meal. She tries to describe his fridge's contents to a mutual friend on the telephone. Bram supplies observations and corrects her comparisons without possessing a better explanation. All action stays in the kitchen and adjoining living room. There is no crossing, correspondence with another reality, or identified supplier.

Romy initially thinks Bram is being unhelpfully vague, then finds that definite descriptions make claims she cannot support. This comes from looking and speaking, rather than a lesson he teaches her. The foods remain mostly unidentified. Ordinary explanations stay plausible longer than is comfortable. The ending completes the conversation and evening rather than opening a fresh adventure.

## Concrete scene movement

1. **A request for something ordinary.** Looking for milk for tea, Romy delivers the opening question; Bram gives the supplied answer. Beside recognizable groceries is a covered dish resembling a folded vegetable whose outside she cannot quite locate, a jar of dark material that seems farther away than the glass, and a packet with an unhelpfully angled picture. These are impressions, not established supernatural properties. Bram points her to ordinary milk. He has not been eating unidentified things indiscriminately.
2. **The theory outruns the evidence.** Asked what he has actually seen, Bram reports things he does not remember buying and unfamiliar leftovers in familiar containers. One packet has been there either three days or since before he moved in. He distinguishes those recollections from one firmer observation: he once shut the door on a dish too long to let it close, and the door closed. He did not measure it. Romy can question his memory without explaining everything away.
3. **Description becomes the action.** Their mutual friend calls about joining them for supper. Romy mentions the fridge and is asked what is in it. Her descriptions acquire unhelpful qualifications. “A sort of cabbage” is corrected by Bram to “only from this side.” A comparison must be withdrawn when she looks again. The caller offers sensible identifications and ordinary advice about food storage. Bram answers a practical question from across the kitchen, showing that he has considered the obvious explanation. No one wins an argument.
4. **A modest piece of evidence changes the account.** While Romy explains that Bram may simply have forgotten his shopping, she notices a takeaway tub bearing his name in handwriting he recognizes. He has not ordered from that restaurant. Asked what is inside, she lifts the lid: a partly eaten brown thing in slices which seem to continue farther back than the tub. She starts to say “cut lengthwise,” but cannot say what would count as its length. She replaces the lid. His name makes the tub harder to dismiss without proving where it came from; her own incomplete observation has now made a confident description difficult. There is no creature reveal or demonstrated system.
5. **A dry landing.** Their own takeaway arrives within the apartment scene, unmistakably the meal they ordered. They eat in the living room. Asked on the phone whether she has worked out what the fridge contains, Romy now calls it “some leftovers.” Asked “From what?”, she looks toward the kitchen and says that Bram hasn't been specific. The phrase is inadequate, usable, and more accurate than her first confident comparisons. The fridge remains quietly unexplained in the next room. There is no knock, freezer reveal, or deferred adventure.

Provisional movement: ordinary question → inadequately supported theory → failed description to someone else → a small unsettling observation → a casual description carrying more uncertainty than it admits.
