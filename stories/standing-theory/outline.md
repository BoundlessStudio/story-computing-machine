# Standing Theory — provisional outline

## Promised experience

The request explicitly supplies two speakers, conspicuously strange fridge contents, and the offered working theory that the fridge opens to and receives stock from alternate realities. The gap between “weird stuff in your fridge” and the casual “Standing theory” suggests deadpan domestic absurdity, concrete surprises, and curiosity about an extraordinary situation already partly familiar to one speaker. Those are interpretations, not required genre or plot. The request specifies no relationship, explanation, danger, scale, or ending. A satisfying payoff should let the fridge and its alien contents produce discoveries and choices beyond the opening exchange; it need not explain the mechanism completely.

## Chosen direction

A comic first-person discovery story, provisionally around 3,000 words. The visitor, Romy, has brought ordinary groceries for a weekend with her friend Bram. Bram has grown accustomed to the fridge's unpredictable supplies without investigating them very well. Their friendship supplies different responses to the same evidence, rather than an emotional problem for the anomaly to cure. Romy's wish to find out where the food comes from drives an exchange, then a short excursion. The tension comes from learning what they are participating in and deciding how far to follow it. There is no required villain or public crisis.

The fridge will remain a specific, partly understood crossing, with no asserted universal cosmology or autonomous magic user. Its back shelf exchanges objects when the door closes. A larger opening becomes possible through cooperation with a correspondent on the other side. This is an invented local limitation to make discovery legible, not a user requirement. The ending rewards curiosity with an actual encounter beyond the kitchen, followed by a renewed mystery about the freezer. Neither person takes a job maintaining the crossing or serving customers.

## Possible scene movement

1. **Inventory becomes experiment.** Open with the supplied exchange. Romy finds a blue fruit that rolls uphill toward anything colder, a jar holding a small darkness that can be spooned onto toast, and a folded flatbread whose toasted sections float. Bram has practical observations and some conspicuously poor guesses. When Romy's unmistakably marked milk vanishes from the back shelf, she sends a written demand for its return rather than accepting Bram's explanations.
2. **The recipient was sending things too.** A reply comes back with the marked bottle, a sample of another food, and a request to identify what animal produces milk. A second exchange establishes that the correspondent has also been receiving inexplicable groceries and has found Bram's mustard particularly alarming. Their own disappearing leftovers are part of the phenomenon. The premise changes from unsolicited magical stocking to contact with other appetites and other kitchens.
3. **They choose an invitation.** Romy sends a small tasting plate with pictures showing how to eat its contents. The correspondent reciprocates, supplies an intelligible invitation to a gathering, and sends a simple diagram showing both parties sliding out their shelves and closing their doors together. Bram can keep treating the fridge as an unreliable cupboard; instead, he helps Romy test the diagram. The next opening reveals a walkable passage lined with the open backs of differently proportioned refrigerators. They take a picnic and keep their own door in view during the first crossing.
4. **The weird food has a world.** Beyond the short passage is an orchard gathering beneath translucent branches bearing the blue fruit. An enormous, broad-mouthed, politely curious correspondent greets them; its size explains several absurdly portioned deliveries. Other guests have arrived from other fridge doors. Floating flatbread carries a picnic above the trees, and darkness spread under it makes a cool place to sit. Romy must use what she and Bram learned in the kitchen to join the meal, rather than simply watch a display. Their ordinary mustard and milk are the startling items. This is an encounter with lively, specific realities, not a duty to make a common meal or solve the guests' problems.
5. **Return with a larger question.** They return through their still-visible route with food freely exchanged and an invitation to come again. Romy now knows who sent several things, but neither friend knows who connected the cupboards or how many worlds lie along the passage. Back in the kitchen, something knocks from inside the freezer. Bram admits he has never opened that part. Romy puts down her new picnic basket and reaches for the handle. End on that chosen next discovery, with the fridge still a consequential source of extraordinary possibilities.

The exact foods, voices, and route may improve in discussion or prose; the provisional structure to test is curiosity → reciprocal contact → a chosen crossing → participation in an impossible place → a fresh invitation into the unknown.
