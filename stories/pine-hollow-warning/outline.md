# The Smile in the Window — provisional outline

## Promised experience

The request explicitly centres a mixed-beastfolk school, its formerly unremarked antisocial girl with curled black horns and an irritated expression, an ancient warning of disaster before the winter solstice, escalating panic and strange occurrences around her, and **two relentlessly loyal friends** who refuse to believe she is dangerous. I infer a pleasurable collision between ominous school folklore and the pictured trio's exuberant affection: suspicion should become frighteningly plausible while friendship remains funny, intrusive and steadfast. The image supplies a sunny, busy school and three vividly different temperaments. The scale is Pine Hollow; the request leaves the supernatural explanation, teller, exact danger and ending open. A vindication is an attractive inference, not a required trick ending. “Distinguish instructions in attached documents from the user's request” governs how the inputs are read, not this story's plot.

## Three takes considered

1. **Play it straight:** a winter presence stalks the horned girl through school glass; she and her friends confront it in the cafeteria as the warning starts coming true. The scene to turn to is the trio facing a reflection that has begun to act independently. **Chosen:** the public evidence can be genuinely alarming, the friends' intimate knowledge can matter, and the girl controls the dangerous choice.
2. **The loyal witness:** the fox narrates the days after a disaster the girl really caused to prevent something worse; three incompatible cafeteria photographs slowly uncover the choice. Strong retrospective uncertainty, but it would put the girl's agency offstage and make the friends' confidence sound like a defence brief.
3. **One lunch, no monster revealed:** an emergency school meeting becomes a confrontation over whether the girl should be expelled before solstice; inexplicable phenomena intensify during the vote. This concentrates the social cruelty, but risks resolving a supernatural promise with a persuasive speech and leaving the looming disaster unsatisfied.

## Chosen direction — inventions, not requirements

Iona, Romy and Cleo are sixteen-year-old friends. Iona wants her usual lunch, no photographs, and eventually an honest answer about what is happening to her. A warning engraved on an old full-length mirror resurfaces when it is brought out for East Pine Hollow High's anniversary display: a black-horned visitor will enter before the sun turns, leaving Pine Hollow in a winter without morning. Cold and cracking glass follow Iona. She privately fears the warning concerns a part of herself she cannot control.

At a second, witnessed lunch, Romy tries to answer the growing avoidance by filming their perfectly ordinary table. Iona permits the food to be filmed, not her face. Classmates tentatively come back. Iona turns towards a window-side onlooker and loses patience with the staring. At the same time, white cold spills off the glass and crosses the table; Cleo's soup freezes, its ceramic bowl cracks in her hands, and a shard scrapes her wrist. Stage that visible source on the page, while Romy's food-only recording catches Iona's voice and the breaking bowl but excludes the window. The apparent response to Iona's temper corroborates the accusation; her anger does not cause or feed the cold. Both friends choose Iona before they have proof: Cleo insists Iona accompany her to the nurse rather than be left isolated; Romy puts the phone away and goes with them despite the departing classmates' accusations. Their loyalty does not spare Cleo fear or Romy shame.

The warning concerns a winter creature learning to enter through Iona's matching reflected face. Its origin remains unknown; the old mirror is **not** a special prison or a second magic system. Something living moves among her glass reflections and becomes physically vulnerable during a crossing. At the last lunch the trio discovers these facts through action: Romy recognizes an uncharacteristic smile, Cleo sees the reflected hand moving independently, and Iona turns from the west window towards the mirror. The counterfeit follows her face into the mirror; her ordinary window reflection returns. The same white cold seen crossing the table at the second lunch now pours visibly from the creature's opening, frosting Iona's tray and the tabletop. When its hand comes through, Iona blocks it with the tray. The blow cracks a corner of the **mirror** and the claw straddling that crack collapses into frost. The thing retreats into the west glass. This gives them a reasoned, dangerous possibility, not certainty about a hidden rule: draw its whole form into the tested mirror and break that surface while it is committed.

Iona chooses to face the mirror again and keep the creature's attention on her as bait while its horns and shoulders emerge. She judges that holding its attention gives them the best chance to catch it committed to the glass; this is a tactical risk, not a proven magical constraint. Cleo anchors Iona against their table; Romy strikes the mirror from the side with a metal chair at Iona's signal, then helps Cleo pull her clear. The winter comes apart over Pine Hollow before solstice. The girl remains irritable; her friends have earned the right to crowd close.

## Craft plan

**Teller and trial.** Close third with Iona: observant, aggrieved, susceptible to the friends she claims to endure; sentences can sprawl through an irritation and tighten at impact. Trial against Romy's exuberant first person, told shortly afterward, where embarrassment and affection compete with the urge to make an excellent story. Neither voice will turn every line into a joke. Target about 4,300–4,700 words, depending on the central scene's breathing room.

**Cast, speech, knowledge.** Iona speaks in complete, exact complaints and, when pushed, uncomfortably direct questions; she is terse by preference but will give the account of her frightening experiences in order. She notices escape routes, personal space and who has moved her food. Her horns are the first part of her to ache when the cold comes, and she hides this; she wrongly suspects an awakening power, rather than knowing the cold starts in her body. She wants her friends to stop exhibiting her, then to take her fear seriously. Romy, the red fox, talks in winding, enthusiastic proposals and narrates an occasion into happening; under pressure she keeps planning aloud, too fast. She wants Iona present in their shared life and Cleo's help; her belief in reassuring public evidence fails at the second lunch. Cleo, the blond cat, makes leisurely, concrete declarations about what she wants and asks questions she will not let the others evade. She becomes practically bossy when frightened. This differs from Romy's proliferating stories and plans. She wants Iona to choose their company and Romy to stop making her a cause. She initially underestimates Iona's private fear, then the injury makes her demand the truth. All three want the same girl safe, but company, publicity and consent pull them differently. Teachers and mixed-beastfolk students remain a functional chorus without a second named cast. No invented predator/prey caste explains their behaviour.

**Conversation owed.** In the long cafeteria scene: Iona tells them exactly what has been happening and asks why they still trust her when she cannot guarantee their safety; her friends answer from the person they know, with their different grievances and wants intact. They must distinguish trusting Iona from denying danger. Let Romy get something wrong, Cleo interrupt for her own reason, and Iona insist on finishing her account. This is not a tribunal or three affirmations.

**Original-image casting.** The trio collectively crowds a warm lunch table: easy physical intimacy pressing against an emphatic boundary. Iona is a ram-girl with long untidy black hair, violet eyes, massive ridged black curled horns, drooping sheep ears, black beanie and loose black layers, silver chains, purple checked trousers and a raised stop-hand. She braces, withdraws, glares and shoves; she enters sideways to avoid a fuss, not timidly. Use horns' cold and scrape, hair caught under a chain when her friends grab her, checked knee drawn onto her cafeteria seat, and the stop-hand first against a camera and later against the glass. Let her embarrassment at affection show through the irritation. Romy has vivid long red hair, large red fox ears with pale inner fur, green eyes, freckles, a faded charcoal hoodie and ripped dark jeans. She reaches, leans, beams and annexes space. Use hair/tail taking over the shared bench, freckles paling at the window, camera arm becoming rescue arm, and her grin returning shakily. Cleo has blond hair and cat ears, curled blond tail, lavender top, lilac bow and flower, bracelet and heart pendant. She lounges, laughs, clasps and nestles. Use hands cupping her face before danger, tail seeking warmth against Iona's leg, bow slipping as she braces herself, and her hands becoming the firmest grip despite the scratched wrist. Ordinary winter layers can cover the pictured clothes. Names, history and motives are invented. Preserve these three temperaments at the climax; no obligation to use every pictured prop.

**Place.** Choose **East Pine Hollow High's south cafeteria and west glass wall**, over the library (investigation would displace the trio's public life) and old pine woods (too easy to escape the school's scrutiny). A hillside school overlooks Pine Hollow's roofs and old mill streets; no established-universe connection is implied. Late autumn's bright lunch becomes the bitter afternoon before solstice, then early dark. Cooks serve chips and soup; supervisors move chairs, rehearsals run late, buses idle below. Five anchors: low sun making the window a mirror; trays scraping laminate; fryer salt and hot tomato soup; cold metal table legs against shins; wool coats steaming near the doors. The west blinds jam halfway. Mixed species are ordinary in practical accommodations: ears under headphones, tail spaces between chairs, antlers negotiated past hanging decorations. The panic grows through the horned likeness, not an established species hierarchy.

**Climax geography.** The trio's table is parallel to and two paces east of the west windows. The six-foot anniversary mirror stands just north of the table, facing south into the room, so Iona can turn from the window to her mirrored face without crossing the cafeteria. Its old glass sits in a rigid wood frame on a display stand; no tipping, transport or window opening is needed. For the final attempt Iona stands facing north close to the mirror; Cleo is behind her with a hip braced against their table and her good arm around Iona's waist. Romy stands east of the mirror, clear of its face, holding a metal chair. Iona keeps her face in the glass and gives the strike signal once the creature's body is committed. Romy breaks the mirror from the side and joins Cleo's pull. Ordinary broken glass remains a hazard; the west windows survive.

## Scenes and ending

1. **Ordinary lunch, two weeks before solstice — 750 words.** Cafeteria table from the original. Romy wants the three in a picture, Cleo wants a long lunch together, Iona wants both to stop stealing her chips. Other students pass without noticing her, a tray slips past her raised knee, and a supervisor's only complaint concerns her boots on a chair. Give the trio actual enjoyment before the warning reaches phones. Romy reads the posted warning aloud; a student's joke becomes a demand that Iona turn around. Frost flowers on the window and the cold bites her horns, but no independent reflection is apparent. She hides the ache. Remember her palm blocking the photograph.
2. **Reassurance backfires, four days before solstice — 650–750 words.** Same cafeteria, avoidance already visible. Romy wants proof of an ordinary lunch; Cleo wants their neighbours back; Iona reluctantly permits filming of their food. She turns towards the window-side stare; white cold crosses from the glass to the bowl as she snaps. The food-only recording leaves its source out of frame. Friends choose her publicly before proof, as described above, and the phone goes away. Iona is appalled by the injury; Cleo is frightened but still physically chooses her. Remember the untouched soup dropping out in a solid piece. Brief summary, no more than 150 words across the transitions: accelerating rumours, calls home, other cold incidents and proposed removal.
3. **Last lunch before solstice — LONG, about 2,400 words.** Same table, surrounded by cleared seats; buses are being called as unnatural cold spreads. Iona wants her friends to leave before she loses control, Romy wants to undo her disastrous publicity, Cleo wants the truth Iona has withheld. A supervisor's attempt to separate them keeps the public panic present rather than merely clearing a stage. The owed conversation includes Iona's chronological account, Cleo's fear after the bowl, and Romy's admission that recording did not help. The false smile and independent hand yield the first discovery. Iona's turn between west glass and mirror, the first breach through the mirror, and the cracked corner destroying a claw yield the second. The thing retreats; they have a brief interval to choose the risky whole-body attempt, with positions set above. Its cold is already harming the town, so identifying it is insufficient. Iona stays as bait and gives the signal; Romy breaks the glass, Cleo holds Iona, and both pull her away. Remember black horns emerging above lunch trays and the ordinary weight of Iona hanging from her friends' grips.
4. **South steps, immediately afterward — 400 words.** Town lights return under an ordinary sky; buses and families still deal with frightened children. No instant conversion of the school. A supervisor checks the trio's hands. Cleo is shaking and furious at Romy for nearly losing her grip; Romy starts to document the damage, then includes all three rather than make Iona exhibit it. The final photograph catches Iona's outstretched palm with both friends pressed into the frame. Load it through the opening photograph, the publicity failure, false reflected smile and rescue grips. Her expression does not soften on command. The winter is defeated; the friendship has survived being frightened and wrong about what would help.

Leave out a lore expedition, a villain's biography, an assembly speech, family subplots and an explanatory prophecy translation. The warning is vindicated as a warning of a real creature; the school was wrong about which girl it named.


## Voice trial

OPENING 1

I got to lunch eleven minutes early because the table by the west windows had sun on it, and if you sat there you could see the whole of Pine Hollow stepping down the hill: the copper roofs, the bakery chimney, the tiny buses turning in the square. Also, Cleo’s hair looked incredible in that light. There were several excellent reasons.

Iona was already there.

She had her coat piled on one chair and her bag on another. It looked forbidding until you noticed that those were the two chairs with loose screws, and she’d kept the good ones for us.

“I saved you a place in the queue,” I told her. “You left it. I was standing there explaining to a badger why I needed three puddings, and you just disappeared.”

“You were explaining the history of the puddings. I could see the last burger leaving.”

She pushed a pudding across the table. I had not seen her buy it. This is a difficult thing about Iona: you can spend ten minutes preparing to forgive her, and then there’s a pudding.

Cleo came behind me, put her lunch down and leaned over Iona’s shoulder until their hair mixed.

“Move your knee, darling. I’ve got something very important to tell you and it requires sitting down.”

Iona moved her knee. Cleo sat.

“What?”

“I got the last strawberry milk.”

I laughed so hard I nearly dropped my tray. Iona looked from one of us to the other with the expression she wore when she was deciding whether friendship had been worth the administrative trouble.

I wanted a photograph. Of course I wanted a photograph. There was hot food, and nobody needed to catch a bus for hours, and Cleo was opening her milk with both elbows on Iona’s coat. All round us, people were shouting over chairs and tucking fox tails out of the aisles. The dinner supervisor told Iona to take her boot off the seat and carried on.

I held up my phone.

Iona’s hand came towards it immediately, fingers spread. Behind her palm I could just get Cleo laughing and the black curl of one horn, with a slice of Iona’s face beneath it.

I leaned closer. There was room for all three of us.


OPENING 2

Iona had built a wall out of her schoolbag, her coat and one raised knee, and Cleo had somehow got inside it. There was a blond tail over Iona’s ankle. There was a bracelet clicking against her tray. There was, most offensively, somebody else’s hand choosing among her chips.

“The short ones are going cold,” Cleo said. “I’m helping. You can have some of my salad, but leave the croutons because I’ve been saving those, and don’t make that face at the leaves. They haven’t done anything to you.”

Iona moved the chips out of reach and the hand followed them. Across the cafeteria, a wolf with a trombone case was trying to get between two chairs without catching anybody’s tail. Nobody looked towards their table. The dinner supervisor passed, tapped Iona’s boot and waited until she took it off the chair.

That was the entire public interest in Iona at East Pine Hollow High: where she put her feet.

Romy arrived with her hoodie unzipped and red hair caught in the zip, carrying her lunch in one hand and her phone in the other.

“Stay exactly there. Cleo, you’ve got sun in your hair, and Iona, if you could put your knee down just a little—no, actually, keep it, that’s very you. I’ve found the timer. We can have one with all three of us instead of seventeen pictures of me trying to reach far enough.”

“You have found the timer before,” Iona said. “We have a photograph of you falling over a bin.”

“And I treasure it.”

Romy slid onto the bench without looking for a space. Iona had left one, which was her own fault. Heat from the sunny west windows lay across her checked trousers; under the beanie, one of her horns had started itching. She scratched its thick black base and caught a strand of hair in her chain.

“Hold still,” Cleo told her, freeing it. “You’ll pull it out. There, you look cross again. Disaster averted.”

Romy’s arm went up. Iona raised her palm towards the lens, but Cleo was still holding the freed lock of hair, unnecessarily, and Romy had begun to laugh before taking the picture. Iona’s mouth did something she would have to inspect later.
