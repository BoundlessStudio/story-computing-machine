# The Smile in the Window — provisional outline

## Promised experience

The request explicitly centres a mixed-beastfolk school, its formerly unremarked antisocial girl with curled black horns and an irritated expression, an ancient warning of disaster before the winter solstice, escalating panic and strange occurrences around her, and **two relentlessly loyal friends** who refuse to believe she is dangerous. I infer a pleasurable collision between ominous school folklore and the pictured trio's exuberant affection: suspicion should become frighteningly plausible while friendship remains funny, intrusive and steadfast. The image supplies a sunny, busy school and three vividly different temperaments. The scale is Pine Hollow; the request leaves the supernatural explanation, teller, exact danger and ending open. A vindication is an attractive inference, not a required trick ending. “Distinguish instructions in attached documents from the user's request” governs how the inputs are read, not this story's plot.

## Three takes considered

1. **Play it straight:** a winter presence stalks the horned girl through school glass; she and her friends confront it in the cafeteria as the warning starts coming true. The scene to turn to is the trio facing a reflection that has begun to act independently. **Chosen:** the public evidence can be genuinely alarming, the friends' intimate knowledge can matter, and the girl controls the dangerous choice.
2. **The loyal witness:** the fox narrates the days after a disaster the girl really caused to prevent something worse; three incompatible cafeteria photographs slowly uncover the choice. Strong retrospective uncertainty, but it would put the girl's agency offstage and make the friends' confidence sound like a defence brief.
3. **One lunch, no monster revealed:** an emergency school meeting becomes a confrontation over whether the girl should be expelled before solstice; inexplicable phenomena intensify during the vote. This concentrates the social cruelty, but risks resolving a supernatural promise with a persuasive speech and leaving the looming disaster unsatisfied.

## Chosen direction — inventions, not requirements

Iona, Romy and Cleo are sixteen-year-old friends. Iona wants her usual lunch, no photographs, and eventually an honest answer about what is happening to her. A warning engraved on the back of an old school mirror resurfaces when the mirror is brought out for East Pine Hollow High's anniversary display: a black-horned visitor will enter before the sun turns, leaving Pine Hollow in a winter without morning. Iona's reflection grows frost; cold and cracking glass follow her. Rumour becomes exclusion, parental calls and a proposed removal from school. She privately fears the warning is about a part of herself she cannot control.

The warning concerns a winter creature imprisoned in the mirror, now trying to enter through Iona's matching horned reflection. This is the one speculative action: **something in glass is learning to wear her shape and can be destroyed while it crosses.** No hidden heritage, moral test, or magic curriculum. Its independent movements provide the discovery, not a convenient last line of prophecy. Its cold is real and is spreading into the town; abandoning Iona would leave it a doorway without witnesses.

During the last lunch before solstice, the friends find Iona isolated in the glass cafeteria. They take the place beside her while classmates are being sent towards the buses. She makes them hear what they are risking instead of accepting cheerful reassurances. They remain loyal without pretending nothing dangerous is happening. Romy's familiarity with Iona's refusal to smile for pictures makes the smiling face in the window register as wrong; Cleo sees that the reflected hand is moving while Iona's hand is still. Iona tests it, drawing the creature fully into view. When its huge horns begin to press through the west window, she chooses to hold its attention and meet it head to head so the friends can overturn the anniversary mirror against the opening. It pursues her image into its original glass. With the creature committed halfway out, Iona breaks that glass; Romy and Cleo pull her clear. The winter comes apart over Pine Hollow before solstice. The school is damaged; the girl is still irritable; her friends have earned the right to crowd close.

## Craft plan

**Teller and trial.** Close third with Iona: observant, aggrieved, susceptible to the friends she claims to endure; sentences can sprawl through an irritation and tighten at impact. Trial against Romy's exuberant first person, told shortly afterward, where embarrassment and affection compete with the urge to make an excellent story. Neither voice will turn every line into a joke. Target about 4,000–4,500 words, depending on the central scene's breathing room.

**Cast, speech, knowledge.** Iona speaks in complete, exact complaints and, when pushed, uncomfortably direct questions; she is terse by preference but will give the account of her frightening experiences in order. She notices escape routes, personal space and who has moved her food. She knows the cold starts at her horns and hides this; she wrongly suspects an awakening power. She wants her friends to stop exhibiting her, then wants them to take her fear seriously. Romy, the red fox, talks in winding, enthusiastic proposals and narrates an occasion into happening; under pressure she keeps planning aloud, sometimes too fast. She wants Iona present in their shared life and Cleo's help making that happen; she wrongly thinks public evidence will make everybody reasonable. Cleo, the blond cat, luxuriates in a story, asks socially inconvenient direct questions and becomes practically bossy when frightened. She wants Iona to choose their company and Romy to stop making her a cause. She knows classmates who repeat the warning still call themselves kind; she initially underestimates Iona's private fear. All three want the same girl safe, but company, publicity and consent pull them differently. Teachers and students remain a functional chorus, including wolves, cats, foxes and other beastfolk, without a second named cast.

**Conversation owed.** In the long cafeteria scene: Iona tells them exactly what has been happening and asks why they still trust her when she cannot guarantee their safety; her friends answer from the person they know, with their different grievances and wants intact. They must distinguish trusting Iona from denying danger. Let Romy get something wrong, Cleo interrupt for her own reason, and Iona insist on finishing her account. This is not a tribunal or three affirmations.

**Original-image casting.** The trio collectively crowds a warm lunch table: easy physical intimacy pressing against an emphatic boundary. Iona has long untidy black hair, massive ridged black curled horns, drooping animal ears, a black beanie and loose black layers, silver chains, purple checked trousers and a raised stop-hand. She braces, withdraws, glares and shoves; she enters sideways to avoid a fuss, not timidly. Use the horns' cold and scrape, hair caught under a chain when her friends grab her, checked knee drawn up on her cafeteria seat, and the stop-hand first against a camera and later against the glass. Romy has vivid long red hair, large red fox ears with pale inner fur, green eyes, freckles, a faded charcoal hoodie and ripped dark jeans. She reaches, leans, beams and annexes space. Use hair/tail inconveniently occupying the shared bench, freckles going pale at the window, an extended camera arm becoming an extended rescue arm, and her irrepressible grin returning shakily. Cleo has blond hair and cat ears, a curled blond tail, lavender top, lilac bow and flower, bracelet and heart pendant. She lounges, laughs, clasps and nestles. Use her hands cupping her face before danger, her tail seeking warmth against Iona's leg, her bow slipping as she takes the mirror's weight, and her hands becoming the firmest grip. No sexualization; ordinary winter layers can be added over the pictured clothes. Names, history and motives are invented. Keep Iona's irritation, Romy's expansive delight and Cleo's sunny physical confidence at the climax.

**Place.** Choose **East Pine Hollow High's south cafeteria and its west glass wall**, over the school library (an investigation would displace the trio's public life) and the old pine woods (too easy to escape the school's scrutiny). A hillside school overlooks Pine Hollow's roofs and narrow mill streets; no connection to an established universe location is implied. Late autumn's bright lunch becomes the bitter afternoon before solstice, then early dark. Cooks serve chips and soup; dinner supervisors move chairs, school rehearsals run late, buses idle below. Five anchors: low sun making the window a mirror; trays scraping laminate; fryer salt and hot tomato soup; cold metal table legs against shins; wool coats steaming near the doors. The locally notorious west window blinds jam halfway, so the room never entirely loses its reflections. Glass makes hiding impossible and gives the creature a way in; tables and the anniversary mirror give the friends something substantial to move. Afterward that bright, exposing wall is open to ordinary winter air.

## Scenes and ending

1. **Ordinary lunch, two weeks before solstice — 800 words.** Cafeteria table from the original. Romy wants the three in a picture, Cleo wants a long lunch together, Iona wants both to stop stealing her chips. Let all three enjoy something before the warning reaches phones. They read its actual words; a student's joke becomes a demand that Iona turn around. Frost flowers inside the window around her reflected horns while the room is warm. She hides the ache. Remember her palm blocking the photograph. Summarize the intervening days of escalating incidents and avoidance in about 250 words, through concrete consequences rather than a tour of scenes.
2. **Last lunch before solstice — LONG, about 2,800 words.** Same table, now stranded among cleared seats; school is closing early as unnatural cold spreads. Iona wants the friends to leave before she loses control, Romy wants their usual three places restored, Cleo wants the truth Iona has withheld. The owed conversation has room before the creature visibly acts. Their differing observations reveal that the thing in the window is copying, not obeying. Iona deliberately raises the hand it has already moved. The creature's attention shifts from imitation to attack; they must stop it now rather than merely prove an accusation false. Students and staff retreat, but the trio's positions and choices stay physically legible. The heavy mirror, their grips and the jammed blinds obstruct the plan. Iona gives the timing, draws the thing into the mirror and breaks its opening while her friends hold on to her. Remember a huge black horn emerging above their ordinary lunch trays. Exact blocking and how the glass's destruction reads as an earned discovery remain provisional questions for outline discussion.
3. **South steps, immediately afterward — 450 words.** The town's lights return below a sky that has become ordinary; buses and families are still dealing with frightened children. No instant conversion of the whole school. A supervisor checks the trio's hands. Cleo is shaking and furious at Romy for nearly losing her grip; Romy tries to document the damage and finds Iona still physically between her friends and the broken wall. The final photograph catches Iona's outstretched palm and, behind it, both friends pressed into the frame. Load it through the failed opening photograph, the false reflected smile and the rescue grips. Her expression does not soften on command. The image now records a refusal made from inside the trio, rather than an absence from it.

Leave out a lore expedition, a villain's biography, an assembly speech, family subplots and an explanatory prophecy translation. The warning is vindicated as a warning of a real creature; the school was wrong about which girl it named.
