# The Last Stormweaver · Provisional outline

## Promised experience

For centuries the Stormweavers have kept the heavens from tearing apart. After a devastating war, the surviving nations execute every Stormweaver but one, who must hold back a world-ending storm alone. “The very storm they were born to control” brings the pleasure of seeing a formidable, practiced power used at a scale that would have defeated its bearer in any ordinary life. Our image sets a planted, solitary figure against an oncoming immensity of blue, violet and fire. I take its tone to be radiant and fearful, with room for angry resolve. I expect a confrontation whose outcome the survivor makes, and whose consequence reaches the world that killed the others. The request leaves the survivor’s relationships, the basis of the nations’ decision, and the outcome open.

## Takes considered

- **The Veyr Stair, chosen.** From the last working station, the survivor tries the old continental handoff and feels nobody take the weight. The scene I want to see is her climbing off the last step into the current she means to run down and catch herself. The executions matter at every point in the action, from the absent hands to the feat that has to take their place.
- **The day of the votes.** Open on the final death sentence at the Red Sill, then make a desperately long passage through the storm to the one still-usable station. The scene is the survivor calling up a corridor through the tearing air while bringing the people who emptied her order along with her. Their passage would compete with the lone working for the climax.
- **The witness at the Nail Market.** A slate dealer in a valley hit in the war tells the story of the night she saw the one person she feared worse than the storm turn it back. The scene is the town coming out into the street as the heavens frost blue at both horizons. This could do good work with memory and evidence, though it would put the surviving weaver’s precise choices and physical ordeal out of reach.

## Provisional direction

Sarve worked the Veyr station before the war. Then she brought a current down on an enemy town rather than pass it on. In the peace settlement, the surviving nations agreed to execute the order as the authors of the sky attacks they themselves commissioned. They spared Sarve by treaty, hoping one weaver could protect the towns around the signing place. Dannel, a signatory from the valley she struck, accompanies her to the Stair. He wants the last living weaver to promise him a safe sky for the people below. Sarve wants him to stop calling her survival a pardon and say why they kept one.

The old way was to turn raw currents toward the next station, where another weaver caught them. On the exposed Stair, Sarve tries it once. With nobody to take her turn, the current snaps back, and she has to yank the light over her own head to spare the valley. All the uncaught currents have now joined into one storm, and the fissure it’s cutting is widening beyond both horizons. Sarve can still do what she did in the war and discharge the near front through the valley. It would cost those lives and leave the rest of the storm loose over the world. For a chance at all of it, she sends Dannel down and steps off the rock into the current she has turned. Lifted by it, she takes it all the way round, overtakes the part she first bent, and catches it by her own hand. With the current running through that first turn and back into itself, it can keep flowing without an exposed front cutting the heavens. Sarve has to make the join under the full load and free herself before the loop closes on her.

## Craft plan

- **Teller.** Stay with Sarve. Try close third with room for long, physical observation and a sharp private impatience, and first person in the present with her wry, disputatious account of what her own hands are doing. Neither voice can see the crisis at a safe remove.
- **Sarve.** Proud of the competent thing done well, including one thing she wishes she’d never proved she could do. She wants a chance to work and wants someone to admit the nations prized her war work enough to keep her alive. She knows the missing hands are irreplaceable in the old working. She expects she can still buy time and takes Dannel for a man seeking forgiveness. She uses the words of her body and her trade, offers exact answers to the question she thinks worth asking, and has a mean streak of humor when someone is wrong at length.
- **Dannel.** Once kept army ration accounts for a town that ate its seed grain. He speaks in qualified, orderly particulars, starting lists again when distressed. He wants a definite promise for his people and has been telling himself the treaty drew a humane exception. He knows how the votes were won and assumes Sarve can save whichever land she chooses. He knows one thing she doesn’t, that the delegates picked her for her exceptional ability to bring the current down. He wants the story of the peace to stay bearable as much as he wants her help. He has to tell her in order how a proposal to keep one weaver at every station got worn down to keeping one woman who could hit a valley, and hear her say what she did the last time she brought it down.
- **Image casting.** One back-turned figure in a long dark blue coat on black stairs, feet set apart, with a thin forked shape over the left shoulder. Read the stance as bold and possessed of time even in great danger. Sarve plants, sights, reaches and leans into whatever has the larger claim on the room. The whipping coat first catches in rock and late tells her which way the current is carrying her. The broad stance that braces her against the first pull has to become a deliberate stride into empty air. Make the shoulder shape a slender hook that she sets against the stair to take strain and later leaves behind. Face, gender and age are open. Keep the figure’s attitude and silhouette.
- **Place.** The Veyr Stair climbs a black spur out from under the immense jaw of a split mountain. The Red Sill fortress would make the battle for custody too much of the story. The Nail Market would take away both the shelter under the rock and the extraordinary exposure a few steps on. On the last evening of the thaw, drovers at Veyr would ordinarily be guiding ewes through the sheltered pass below. Bells sound twice under the jaw, wool grease lingers on the old rubbing stone, melt slicks the treads, the first strike makes a copper taste, and electric blue runs down uphill-facing edges. The steps are cut broad for stock until the last narrow flight, where the upper current crosses close enough for a weaver to take it. The shelter prevents Dannel seeing the whole sky and offers him a retreat. Sarve’s old foothold lets her withstand the first pull and holds her back from the second.

## Scene briefs and ending

1. **Under the jaw, dusk, 25%.** Dannel tries to bargain for the valley and names the towns he has promised to protect. Sarve shows him the first rupture glimmering behind the throat of the pass. The owed conversation belongs here. Give Dannel his account of the votes and Sarve her own plain account of her wartime pull, which she has had years to get right and still hates telling. His promise to the valley begins to sound to him like something he had no right to give. The first bell of the climbing flock sounds twice. Sarve goes up to try to buy them time.
2. **Above the last step and out into the storm, 65%, long scene.** Sarve wants Dannel out of reach and gets rid of him as soon as the failed handoff tips stone into the sky. Alone, she considers the ease with which she could send the front through the valley, then leaves the foothold and catches the storm. Stay with her in the physical duration of the joining. She has to recognize her own first bend in the glare across the far side of the world and reckon with the moment her arms still expect another hand. The full sky opens above one dark figure before she gathers it into a wheel.
3. **The Stair at dawn, 10%.** Sarve comes clear low over the pass and lands on stone. The ring continues without her in a single vast sweep, already crossing into daylight on one side while stars come through again on the other. Coast and valley both lie below it. The folds of the blue coat settle after the force that tore at them. Load that end with the doubled bell, the first catastrophic return of the current, and the use Sarve makes of its second return. Let the world keep the changed sky. Summarize the war in the two participants’ accounts, and leave the treaty capitals and executions offstage.
