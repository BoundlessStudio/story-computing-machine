# The Last Stormweaver · Provisional outline

## Promised experience

For centuries the Stormweavers have kept the heavens from tearing apart. After a devastating war, the surviving nations execute every Stormweaver but one, who must hold back a world-ending storm alone. “The very storm they were born to control” brings the pleasure of seeing a formidable, practiced power used at a scale that would have defeated its bearer in any ordinary life. Our image sets a planted, solitary figure against an oncoming immensity of blue, violet and fire. I take its tone to be radiant and fearful, with room for angry resolve. I expect a confrontation whose outcome the survivor makes, and whose consequence reaches the world that killed the others. The request leaves the survivor’s relationships, the basis of the nations’ decision, and the outcome open.

## Takes considered

- **The Veyr Stair, chosen.** From the last working station, the survivor tries the old continental handoff and feels nobody take the weight. The scene I want to see is her climbing off the last step into the current she means to run down and catch herself. The executions matter at every point in the action, from the absent hands to the feat that has to take their place.
- **The day of the votes.** Open on the final death sentence at the Red Sill, then make a desperately long passage through the storm to the one still-usable station. The scene is the survivor calling up a corridor through the tearing air while bringing the people who emptied her order along with her. Their passage would compete with the lone working for the climax.
- **The witness at the Nail Market.** A slate dealer in a valley hit in the war tells the story of the night she saw the one person she feared worse than the storm turn it back. The scene is the town coming out into the street as the heavens frost blue at both horizons. This could do good work with memory and evidence, though it would put the surviving weaver’s precise choices and physical ordeal out of reach.

## Provisional direction

Sarve worked the Veyr station before the war. Then she brought a current down on a town beyond the mountains. In the peace settlement, the surviving nations agreed to execute the order as the authors of the sky attacks they themselves commissioned. Dannel, a representative of the Veyr towns, cast his vote for the executions and bargained to keep Sarve. He saw her wartime strike as proof she could empty a current wherever she chose. He comes to the Stair with a place for her to discharge the approaching front and wants her promise to do it. Sarve wants him to stop calling her survival a pardon and own what his vote did to her sister Heth, who should be waiting to receive the current at the next station.

The old way was to turn raw currents toward the next station, where another weaver caught them. On the exposed Stair, Sarve tries it once. With nobody to take her turn, the current whips back with its bend still in it, two bright angles whose second turn Heth used to tease her about. Sarve yanks it up to spare the valley. All the uncaught currents have joined into one storm, and the fissure it’s cutting is widening beyond both horizons. Dannel’s place is the uninhabited far slope already blasted by the war. Sarve could discharge the near front there with a motion she knows by heart, giving the drovers and the treaty towns time to take shelter under rock and allowing her to run down with Dannel. It would leave the rest of the storm loose over the world and cost her the one current she can reach from the ground. She sends Dannel down and uses it to enter the storm. Lifted by the current, she takes it all the way round, overtakes her first bend, and catches it by her own hand. With the current running through that turn and back into itself, it can flow without an exposed front cutting the heavens. Sarve has to make the join under the full load and free herself before the loop closes on her.

## Craft plan

- **Teller.** Stay with Sarve. Try close third with room for long, physical observation and a sharp private impatience, and first person in the present with her wry, disputatious account of what her own hands are doing. Neither voice can see the crisis at a safe remove.
- **Sarve.** Proud of the competent thing done well, including one thing she wishes she’d never proved she could do. She wants a chance to work and for Dannel to say what the nations thought they were entitled to keep when they killed Heth. She knows the other hands are irreplaceable in the old working. She expects she can buy time and takes Dannel for a man wanting reassurance. She uses the words of her body and her trade, offers exact answers to the question she thinks worth asking, and has a mean streak of humor when someone is wrong at length.
- **Dannel.** Once kept army ration accounts for a town that ate its seed grain. He speaks in qualified, orderly particulars, starting lists again when distressed. He wants a definite promise for his people and thinks Sarve will choose to save what is closest to her. He knows how the votes were won. He has to tell her in order why he withdrew support for keeping one weaver at every station and traded his vote for a single exception, a proven attacker. He wants her to accept the site he offers, which he can describe right down to the houses once there, and the sense that he made a workable bargain.
- **Image casting.** One back-turned figure in a long dark blue coat on black stairs, feet set apart, with a thin forked shape over the left shoulder. Read the stance as bold and possessed of time even in great danger. Sarve plants, sights, reaches and leans into whatever has the larger claim on the room. The whipping coat first catches in rock and late tells her which way the current is carrying her. The broad stance that braces her against the first pull has to become a deliberate stride into empty air. Make the shoulder shape a slender hook that she sets against the stair to take strain and later leaves behind. Face, gender and age are open. Keep the figure’s attitude and silhouette.
- **Place.** The Veyr Stair climbs a black spur out from under the immense jaw of a split mountain. The Red Sill fortress would make the battle for custody too much of the story. The Nail Market would take away both the shelter under the rock and the extraordinary exposure a few steps on. On the last evening of the thaw, drovers at Veyr would ordinarily be guiding ewes through the sheltered pass below. Bells sound twice under the jaw, wool grease lingers on the old rubbing stone, melt slicks the treads, the first strike makes a copper taste, and electric blue runs down uphill-facing edges. The steps are cut broad for stock until the last narrow flight, where the upper current crosses close enough for a weaver to take it. The shelter prevents Dannel seeing the whole sky and offers him a retreat. Sarve’s old foothold lets her withstand the first pull and holds her back from the second.

## Scene briefs and ending

1. **Under the jaw, dusk, 25%.** Dannel shows Sarve where he wants her to discharge the front and names the towns he has promised to protect. She gets him to stop reciting his promise and answer for the vote that killed Heth. The owed conversation belongs here. Give Dannel his account of the deal because he thinks it will convince her to use the station as he intended. When he praises the accuracy of the wartime strike, Sarve can tell him where it actually landed, because she wants him to tell her who lives around the mark he’s asking her to aim at tonight. A bell of the climbing flock sounds twice. Sarve takes the stairs to see what she can do.
2. **Above the last step and out into the storm, 65%, long scene.** After the failed handoff tips stone into the sky, Sarve sends Dannel to turn the flock under rock and away from the exposed stair. Alone, she could bring the current down and get to shelter herself. Before she chooses, let her see the tear lengthen clear of the front she can discharge, and loose stone travel up the only current she can reach. She could put out the immediate danger and forfeit that way up. Instead she leaves the foothold and catches the storm. Stay with her in the physical duration of the joining. Recognition of the two angles in the returning current means she can reach the bend she put there, at the very moment her arms still expect Heth’s hand. The full sky opens above one dark figure before she gathers it into a wheel.
3. **The Stair at dawn, 10%.** Sarve comes clear low over the pass and lands on stone. As the flock comes out from under the jaw, she hears the doubled bell again. The folds of the blue coat settle after the force that tore at them. The ring continues without her in a single vast sweep, already crossing into daylight on one side while stars come through again on the other. Coast and valley both lie below it. Load that end with the first catastrophic return of the current and the use Sarve makes of its second return. Let the world keep the changed sky. Summarize the war as it comes up between Sarve and Dannel, and leave the treaty capitals and executions offstage.
