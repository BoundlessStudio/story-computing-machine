This story does not have the collection's dialogue problem. Most of the talk is ordinary household traffic (bread, mustard, a latch, a trunk, an address) with the threat running underneath, and several lines work on two levels without narration decoding them: "Did it eat for her?" / "No." is true of the old spider and hides the loan one; "I'm warm enough" refuses both the warming pan and the working. Pelset never argues his case in polished speeches. He coaxes, gives concrete testimony about the sickroom, gives orders, and then puts his public manner back on for the carter. Ivrine answers narrowly and keeps things back, and that withholding is what drives the plot; the poucher and the carter sound like nobody else. The edit pass should make the four small fixes below and otherwise leave the dialogue alone: no added confrontation, confession, or explanation of Pelset's plan.

## Dialogue findings

### D1 · Minor · "Her" misreads as the mother at dinner; one speaker bump

- Where: L141–L151, midday dinner, the money exchange (setup at L43, first-morning kitchen)
- Text:
> Leave me enough for the roof. Just the back slope. I can manage the rest.
> You haven't paid her yet.
> I've told the woman at the sewing shop I'll be there tomorrow afternoon.
- Problem: Ground/referent. At dinner every nearby "her" means the dead mother ("her money" L135, "her savings" L137, "her mother's plate" L153, "her hardly cold" L163), so "You haven't paid her yet" first reads as the mother. The intended referent, the shop's seller, was last mentioned 100 lines earlier as "the woman at the sewing shop", a label for the reader that also leaves open whether Ivrine is taking a job or buying the shop. Just before, "Leave me enough for the roof" follows an Ivrine beat (L143) although it is Pelset's second line in a row. The content rescues the attribution, but it adds a second bump to the same short stretch.
- Fix: Replace the pronoun at L149 so the line needs no referent, and keep Ivrine's "I've agreed." as the reply. Illustrative only: "You haven't signed anything yet." Move "She broke a potato with her fork." to after L145, or give Pelset a small beat with the bills before L145. L43 can stay as written once L149 no longer depends on it.

### D2 · Minor · Pelset's repayment promise does not parse

- Where: L295–L301, kitchen at dawn, first beat of the confrontation
- Text:
> Leave her money here.
> I'll send it when the roof's paid for.
- Problem: Ground/referent. "It" can only be the money, but if the roof is paid with that money there is nothing left to send. The reader stops to work out whether he means repayment, the remainder, or something else. Ivrine's "Move, please." works as a refusal under any reading, so the exchange holds, but the confusion lands on the opening beat of the climax.
- Fix: Make it a plain bad-faith promise of repayment. Keep it short, and leave Ivrine's reply unchanged. Illustrative only: "I'll pay you back once the roof's done."

## Prose findings

### P1 · Minor · Narration states the recognition the scene already shows

- Where: L255, Ivrine's room after the bedside working
- Text:
> She couldn't sort the things he had done for her from the things he had been doing to her.
- Problem: Emotional commentary. The list of fireside kindnesses before it already reads as a list of chances to breathe on her, and the tea line after it ("She had already drunk half of it.") turns the point into a concrete, unsettling fact. The chiasmus between them is the story's only polished thematic statement in narration, and it explains what the reader has just worked out.
- Fix: Cut the sentence so the list runs straight into the tea. If the list then feels too neutral, sharpen one item with a physical proximity detail (his face near her neck, as at L219) rather than adding a summary.

## Continuity and prompt findings

### C1 · Minor · Basket opened off-page on the last morning

- Where: L259 (evening) and L281–L283 (before dawn)
- Text:
> and knotted the basket shut
> put her familiar spider, still in its own pouch, back in the basket
- Problem: The basket was knotted shut with the old pouch inside. At dawn Ivrine watches that spider pick at a thread and then puts it "back in the basket", but the story never has her untie the basket or lift the pouch out. The climax needs the reader to know where both spiders are, and this small gap blurs that just before it.
- Fix: Add one clause at L281 (she unties the basket and lifts the pouch out to look), or change L283 so she re-knots the basket over it instead of putting it back in.
