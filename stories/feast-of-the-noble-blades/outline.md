# Provisional direction

At the Feast of the Noble Blades, celebrated bladesmith Elska and the living rapier Scathe give their twentieth annual presentation, this time on an adjustable grip for aging hands. The convention is a place of professional vanity, loud craft arguments, oil tastings, overequipped spectators, and very particular weapons. Scathe and Elska have an excellent double act. Neither has fought a real bout with the other: their life has been workshop tests and these dazzling demonstrations. Elska's stiffening fingers make the new grip a personal achievement as well as a possible prize winner.

Scathe has quietly invited a prospective field wielder to attend. She wants to discover what she can do outside the workshop before another annual demonstration becomes her whole future. She agreed to demonstrate the grip but withheld her planned departure; Elska believes she has solved the problem that will let them keep doing this together. A comic live demonstration and its questions expose the concealed mismatch. The prospective wielder should be a credible individual with useful tastes and limits, not a bad choice invented to send Scathe home.

Elska must finish showing what her work actually does while being hurt in public; Scathe must ask openly for the life she wanted rather than use the lecture's momentum to secure it. Their practiced jokes begin to land differently. The crowd remains a crowd of enthusiasts, with partial attention and distinct concerns, not a public to be morally converted. The grip deserves a real technical presentation, with a comprehensible advantage and a limit; neither participant's life should reduce to an obstacle for the other.

A possible ending: Elska adjusts the grip for the new wielder and relinquishes the partnership she thought she was extending. Scathe leaves the stage in another hand. Their workshop years retain their value through a specific piece of mutual knowledge or affectionate irritation, and they establish a next contact that will change, rather than repeat, their old double act. Scathe's departure should be real if chosen; the emotional consequence belongs to both of them. Close third through Elska or Scathe, chosen in drafting; roughly 2,500–3,500 words, comic wonder with an emotional bite. Provisional throughout.
