# Provisional direction

Later in the season of *The Place Laid for Her*, in the same city, celebrated bladesmith Elska and the living rapier Scathe give their twentieth annual presentation at the Feast of the Noble Blades. Their subject is an adjustable thumb saddle and broad heel support fitted around an ordinary grip: it holds the hand's load without demanding tightly curled fingers, releases freely, and sacrifices some agility for endurance. The support can be removed for faster competitive work; it is an option for particular users and sessions rather than a universal improvement. This is real craft, meant to bring Elska orders as well as restore their own polished demonstrations. Elska's stiffening fingers make it personal. The convention's oil tastings, craft arguments, rival demonstrations, and very particular weapons will supply the talk's questions, interruptions, and audience rather than sit around it as decoration.

Scathe and Elska have an excellent double act, but no actual fencing life: their opponents in demonstrations know exactly what answer to give. Scathe has privately arranged a trial with a circuit fencer who also teaches. The candidate has a paid exhibition bout the next morning, a prior promise to try another blade and settle her choice at the next session break, and rehearsal afterward. She leaves the city after the bout. Scathe wants an opponent's unrehearsed reply before another annual demonstration becomes her whole future. She concealed this invitation because she dreaded changing their act before Elska's important talk; that reason does not excuse it. Elska thinks the grip will let them keep performing together. When Elska calls for a contrasting hand, the candidate volunteers herself; her questions about the next morning's use expose the separately arranged private trial. Scathe has not planned to put her on stage. The candidate has her own professional needs and alternatives; she will not exist merely to send Scathe home.

Ildesse attends for her own enthusiast pleasure. Her mannequin carries her sword, in its leaf-tooled green scabbard; she has kept enjoying beautiful clothes after her disappointing dinner, without this story curing or summarizing it. She asks whether Elska's support can fit wooden fingers with limited movement, making the invention's reach and limits interesting beyond human age. Convey her living flesh and bounded puppetry in the new prose so this story stands alone. Scathe's own living thread is nourished beneath a pommel cap; she speaks through a reed at her guard and cannot carry herself, a different embodiment rather than a universal rule for blades.

Scathe must take the decision away from the room: own the concealed invitation, ask the candidate to wait, and commit to finishing the agreed presentation before a private conversation. Her delay loses the chance of tomorrow's first booking because the candidate must honor her existing break and rehearsal commitment. A later offer of a provisional road trial as a spare or teaching partner remains possible, with no guaranteed circuit place. Elska must finish showing what her work actually does while hurt, including the agility compromise that could spoil an attractive sale or Scathe's choice of grip. Their practiced jokes begin to land differently. The crowd retains distinct technical concerns and partial attention.

A possible ending after the panel: Elska and Scathe have the conversation they should have had before it. Elska adjusts the grip for the candidate without using the work to guarantee her own continued partnership. Scathe's apology needs a practical consequence and departure, if chosen, must be real. Their workshop years retain their value through specific mutual knowledge and an affectionate irritation; neither must bless every aspect of the other's choice. A first unrehearsed bout the following morning is available if it earns its scene rather than adding a second ending. Close third through Elska; roughly 2,500–3,500 words, comic wonder with an emotional bite. Provisional throughout.
