# Provisional direction: The Wrong Sort

The explicit promise is a royal heir who rejects the life her inherited wing markings prescribe, tattoos them, wears cold iron, joins magical outcasts, and inadvertently changes the ancient laws themselves. My interpretation is an exuberant, dangerous fantasy of self-expression acquiring public force: punk pleasure, chosen company, consequential enchantment, and the exhilaration of breaking a system that has treated bodies as instructions. The scale begins with a band and reaches the realm. The central bond is the heir's friendship with the outcast tattooist. The anticipated payoff is visible release from hereditary compulsion and her continued life with the band, rather than her return as a kinder monarch. These are interpretations, not additional user requirements.

Close third through **Veska**, with a sharp, amused voice that can admit fear. The original informs her confidence, dark hair with violet streaks, large purple-and-gold moth-like wings with crescent eyespots, iron jewelry, and a moonlit harvest gathering. The crescents and their connecting bands carry the recognized wing grammar. **Nim**, a mixed-lineage fairy tattooist and fiddler, and **Hox**, a copper-winged drummer, are lovers forbidden to marry. Their band, the Wrong Sort, has its own audience and pleasures before Veska joins. **Regent Orset** controls the court and guards while preparing her accession. The previous queen is dead; Veska bears the sole living royal source, including its inheritance branch. Establish this before the final choice.

- Begin with the pumpkin-field show and Nim and Hox's attempted wedding, witnessed by their friends even though hereditary law has rejected their vows before. Veska's black briar tattoo was finished that afternoon; this is their first attempt since. She chose tattoos and iron before anyone knew altered patterns could change law. The band enjoys its new member, rather than merely hiding a princess. Nim and Hox brace for the marriage ward to reject their joined hands. Instead it opens: one briar thorn has interrupted the closed wing pattern that forbids marriage between their lines. The first proof of her power is somebody else's desired freedom.
- Orset's call imposes an obligation on Hox to attend the accession. A summons controls attendance at its named event, not every movement or later action. Veska's iron stopped the call entering her; she immediately shares iron with Nim and tries it for Hox. It cannot undo the obligation already lodged in his pattern. Make its imposition explicitly involuntary, never a consent disguised by words such as “accepted.”
- They test the revelation by turning a subsidiary rank-line back into its own origin. That original gold connection is consumed; ordinary ink cannot recreate living gold. Hox can now enter the elevated public music platform where his father was forbidden to perform, and wants the Wrong Sort heard there. The royal source could still issue a new subsidiary rule elsewhere. Consuming that source itself, including its inheritance branch, would leave no origin from which to rebuild a monopoly. Establish the irreversible loss and the narrower alternative before the climax.
- Orset offers real safety and recognized status to the band as Veska's royal household if she submits to sacred restoration and crowning. A clean, willing accession protects his political authority; physically restraining unprecedented living law risks damage he cannot predict. He can and eventually does use force. Nim rejects becoming royal property, and presses for freedom for other mixed-lineage people rather than their private rescue. Veska's first proposed design merely admits more people under her crown. Their final design ends that central authority.
- The band jointly chooses deception. Hox must attend; Veska apparently accepts the bargain provided her musicians may play and stand with her through restoration and crowning. Their permanent household oaths come afterward. Orset grants temporary safe-conduct because compliant royal outcasts make useful public spectacle. They prepare most of a source-loop beneath the decorative briar. The additions are visible but inert: they run through unmarked membrane and change no golden connection until a tiny remaining gap meets the source. Orset expects to remove more offensive ink, not a demonstrated source-destruction weapon.
- At the public accession, the restoring officer and guards are present. Nim's bone tattoo point is concealed in the fiddle bow. At the agreed phrase Veska refuses restoration and turns her source-bearing wing toward Nim. Orset immediately tries to restrain her. Hox uses his drum and body to obstruct the restoring officer, sacrificing the instrument and respectable platform he wanted. Nim deposits one final joining dot; a fresh tattoo need not be completed during a struggle. Veska holds the wing at the point despite the reaching guard. The decision, design, and risk belong to all three.
- The loop consumes the sole royal origin and its inheritance path. Compelled knees straighten, forbidden partners reach one another, and guards must decide whether to use their ordinary weapons without ancient force behind their orders. One guard's choice helps the band leave. The missing royal crescent is visible on Veska's wing: she cannot restore it with another lone edit. Other people use the freedom without waiting for her directions. Ordinary conflict and prejudice survive; hereditary compulsion and her monopoly do not. The band freely chooses her company after she has no royal benefit left to offer. End with their continuing sound and other people's actions, rather than a speech that settles the realm.

This remains provisional. Protect the intimacy of the first release, the band's pleasure and independent choices, and the visible difference between surrendering exclusive authority and retaining it to issue generous decrees. The prose may find a better route within the request.
