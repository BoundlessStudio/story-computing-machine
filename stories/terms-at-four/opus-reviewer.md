# Opus review: Terms at Four

## Verdict

- Dialogue: REVISE — The Prime Minister negotiation agrees to an objection and then quietly drops it, and the guest's one mid-story personal line cannot be decoded. Every argument runs on the same correction-and-retort rhythm, shared by host, guest and Prime Minister.
- Prose: PASS — The prose is concrete and controlled, with few dashes and good sensory work. There is one wrong idiom, an overused epithet for the host, some small staging slips and a closing recap.
- Continuity and prompt: REVISE — The prompt is fully delivered: second person, all four foods, the Prime Minister's line at L235, full resolution, about 3,270 words. But the negotiated order contradicts itself and the order actually carried out (filed as D1), and the hostages' link to the crew's detention needs one clause (C1).
- Edit scope: heavy-dialogue
- Existing review.md said: Verdict PASS, "Dialogue: PASS"; disagree. It checked the order in which things are executed but not whether the negotiation dialogue actually produces that order. It also missed the three-way voice convergence.

The dialogue splits cleanly in two. Where speech is tied to an action, it is tight, specific and often funny: the live challenge, "Intercom.", the cream, "Abort shadow?", "You can take that with you." Where people argue, all three principals make the same move. One makes a clipped claim, the other answers with a clipped correction or a mirrored retort ("They armed it" / "Of their weapon" / "hand the noun back"; "Not summaries." / "Complete documents."; "your terms" / "theirs"), and then the narration explains what just landed. The host's precision is deliberate and well built: hosting logic, formality that stiffens under pressure, and a habit of pushing agency onto "them". The edit pass should make it hers alone. The guest resigned over this machine and should argue plainly, with heat and some fumbling. The Prime Minister should sound like a politician with aides in the room, not a second engineer. The call itself needs rebuilding so that the deal agreed on is the deal that gets carried out.

## Dialogue findings

### D1 · Blocking · Negotiated order agrees to an objection, then silently drops it

- Where: L251–L303, the Prime Minister call; carried out at L331–L377
- Text:
> You're not listening. That sequence makes the hostages wait on a court server.
> The Prime Minister said, "Agreed."
> We open the court submission to public receipt. You release the doors.
> After the crew reaches counsel and the registry accepts the filing, I will state it.
> "The key goes first," you said to her. "Not the people."
> Proceed on the sequence your witness described
- Problem: Three linked Ground failures sit in the story's decisive negotiation.
  1. Ground/uptake at L259. The host has just objected that custody "can be staged", which has nothing to do with the court. "You're not listening" gives her nothing she could have missed, and "that sequence" is the Prime Minister's, not hers.
  2. Ground/uptake at L261 through L279. The Prime Minister instantly says "Agreed." to a criticism of the Prime Minister's own proposal, then offers an order that still puts a court step before the doors. At L277–L279 the host and the Prime Minister put registry acceptance back in front of the doors, and the guest who raised the objection says nothing. The finale follows that reversal: "Filing accepted" (L367), then "Release" (L373). So the one change the guest wins is agreed and then quietly undone.
  3. Ground/referent at L281 and L303. "The key goes first... Not the people." can mean "give up the key before the people", which is already agreed. It can also mean "don't hold the people as your last guarantee", which the final deal does anyway. Her reply ("If they take the crew back—") points to the second reading. "The sequence your witness described" has no referent: the witness never described a sequence; the Prime Minister did at L265.
- Fix: Pick one deal and make the negotiation arrive at it. The smallest route keeps the execution (L321–L403) as written: gate, then commit and key destruction, then filing accepted and the Prime Minister's statement, then doors, then ejection. The finale's "The agreed sequence was commit at the gate" already enforces that order. Then:
  - Rewrite L259 so the guest objects to what the host is actually doing. Her L129 plan and her "Custody can be staged" push hold the weapon until the paperwork clears; that is what the guest should object to, not the hostages waiting on the court.
  - Give the Prime Minister's acceptance a reason or a cost instead of an instant "Agreed."
  - If the guest also wants the doors ahead of the registry, let that objection be heard and lose at L277–L279, with the guest visibly accepting the cost.
  - Make L281 mean one thing.
  - Make L303 refer to the sequence the Prime Minister stated, or give the guest one line that actually restates it.
  Illustrative only (L303): "Go ahead the way I laid it out. The key goes when that van's through the gate."

### D2 · Blocking · "It used to be your table too" cannot be decoded

- Where: L143–L151, crumbs on the strawberry tablecloth after the "noun" argument
- Text:
> Clean the table while telling someone their system is morally indefensible.
> It used to be your table too.
> That landed. She looked toward the amber pulse.
- Problem: Ground/referent at the story's only mid-point personal turn. The table is literally hers, now, so "used to be your table" makes no literal sense. The figurative candidates are the control-room desk, the moral objection and the project, and "your" points at her in all of them. The two likeliest intentions would each need different words:
  - "I used to belong at this table." This matches "your old chair" at L29 and the bird plate at L173, and needs "my".
  - "You used to object too."
  The reader gets the weight only from the narrator's "That landed" and her glance at the satellite (see D7).
- Fix: Keep "You still do that." / "What?" / her description. That is her noticing, and it is good. Replace the reply with a line whose referent is literal and whose sting comes from shared history. Choose between belonging here and her own former objection, and check it against L29 and L173. Then cut "That landed." and keep her look at the amber pulse. Illustrative only: "I used to eat at this table."

### D3 · Major · Correction ping-pong and retorts are the host–guest default

- Where: L49–L53 ("You armed it"), L79–L89 ("Why them?"), L109–L117 (transfer drawer), L131–L141 ("Of a weapon"); lighter at L23–L29 and L185–L189
- Text:
> They armed it. I retained the session.
> That distinction won't help anyone under it.
> So did the maintenance crew.
> I thought leverage might.
> For anything that fits behind it.
> You don't get to hand the noun back to them. You have it.
- Problem: Identical Voices and Punchline Patch. Almost every disagreement runs claim, correction, then a neat last word, at the same length and from both sides. The guest's lines are crafted comebacks ("won't help anyone under it", "hand the noun back") that argue about her wording rather than the six people on the feed, and they sound like her. There are also local Ground slips:
  - "So did the maintenance crew" bends "came under orders" to fit the mirror. The crew did not come anywhere; they had orders and refused them.
  - In "For anything that fits behind it", the "it" could be the jacket or the drawer.
  - "They don't owe you patience" is an aphorism where a person would just push.
- Fix: Keep her side. "They armed it. I retained the session." and "Of their weapon." show her pushing agency onto "them", and L421–L423 pays that off. Rework the guest's side:
  - At L53, L87 and L141, answer with plain heat, repetition or a concrete fact on the table instead of a crafted line. Drop the metalanguage about "the noun".
  - At L83–L85, let her say the crew had orders and refused them, without the verb mirror.
  - Reduce the drawer run (L109–L117) to one push and her corridor check, with a clear object.
  - L23–L29 is light and affectionate and can stay as the one playful correction. L185–L189 is a working engineering check and can stay.
  Illustrative only (L141): "You're holding it. You are."

Give the voice a politician's attention instead: the six people and their families, the cabinet, what can be said publicly, and a clock. Let aides be audible. Do not let the Prime Minister echo her nouns ("dependency", "sequence") or trade one-word rulings with her; let the Prime Minister concede or push in human terms. Mark "dining room" as a guess or use something the government knows, such as her address. Illustrative only (L269–L273): "Everything your lawyer asked for. Nobody is editing it."

### D5 · Major · The decisive personal exchange is a setup, an inversion and a thesis

- Where: L283–L297, host and guest aside during the call
- Text:
> If they take the crew back
> You wanted someone who could prove you kept your terms.
> I wanted someone who would notice if they kept theirs
> It was the old division of labor, offered across porcelain
> "I'll notice," you said. "I won't call this necessary."
- Problem: Punchline Patch and Philosopher at the story's key relational beat. The guest cuts off her one unplanned fear (the crew) with "I know why you called me", then offers a guess that exists only to be inverted into a mirrored line ("your terms" / "theirs"). The narration then explains the exchange (L295), and the guest closes on a thesis sentence. The content is right: she wants a witness against the government, and the guest agrees to watch but refuses to endorse. The matched halves make it sound written.
- Fix: Let her fear about the crew run longer than half a line; it is the one thing she cannot plan away. The guest should respond to that fear, not to a guessed motive. Let her say what she wanted in her own idiom (she told the guest to bring nothing and set the old chair) without mirroring the guest's syntax. Cut L295 or keep only its last two sentences. Keep "I'll notice." It is short and concrete. Make "I won't call this necessary" specific to this act, or drop it, because L421 ("I'll say you confined them to force an agreement") already does the refusing.
