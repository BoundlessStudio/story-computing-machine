# Opus review: The Astral Valkyrie

Independent audit for a later editing pass. It is not a `review.md` verdict,
does not change canon state, and does not authorize edits by itself.

## Verdict

- Dialogue: REVISE — one Major: at the moral hinge Vasketh hands down a courtroom verdict that states what the feast memory already proved; the other dialogue problems are Minor slips in grounding and attribution.
- Prose: PASS — clean, concrete and varied; no em dashes and no water infrastructure; one repeated paired-sentence template and one unexplained gesture.
- Continuity and prompt: PASS — delivers the descent, the contested choice, the extinction and the later age at R+ with fourth-wall address, in about 3,300 words; one geography line cannot be decoded.
- Edit scope: targeted-dialogue
- Existing review.md said: Verdict PASS, Dialogue: PASS; I disagree, but only narrowly: one Major verdict line and a handful of Minor slips.

The dialogue here is better than the collection norm, and the old PASS was close.
The three main voices can be told apart with the tags covered: Nacorra performs,
then evades, then breaks into fragments. Vasketh dates, lists and accuses. The
Valkyrie speaks in plain sensory statements and states her limits. The ordinary
lines are some of the story's best: the wounded man's companion, the request for
shade, the pepper. The failure is local and belongs to Vasketh. He is written as
the story's prosecutor and wins nearly every exchange with a clean last word. At
the hinge ("That is not the part in doubt") he stops being a frightened old man
and rules on the case. The edit pass should make a few surgical cuts and add a
few tags. It should let Vasketh plead instead of adjudicate and leave the
confession fragments, the voices and the reader address alone.

## Edit guardrails

- Prompt promise: the Valkyrie descends as Theskar dies and chooses one soul to carry its memory. Nacorra's selection, the island's destruction and her arrival in a later age must all survive. The rating is adult/R+: keep the strong language (L99), the bodily injury (L21, L97) and the burning hall (L237). The fourth-wall addresses (L17, L179, L241, L263–L267) are expressly permitted and carry the story's argument with the reader. Keep first-person past, with Nacorra writing in the later age. The length band is 2,500–4,000 words (now about 3,300 by `wc`), and these edits are cuts and tags.
- The plot depends on these mechanics. She carries one living, embodied person. She cannot turn the impact aside or bring back the dead. Offered memory reaches everyone touching her, she cannot take what is withheld, and breaking contact ends reception (L39, L151, L171, L225). The exposure and the choice depend on all of them.
- Internal facts to keep consistent: Nacorra is 29 and her lover 31 at the victory feast (L127); the lover is 38 at the execution (L65); "older now than my lover was allowed to become" (L259); six centuries; the split lip that never heals (L15, L247); the changed word *armies* to *dancers* (L193) and its return at the close (L267); the queen's bracelet; the quarry and "the seventeenth year of the old king" (L73).
- Universe: LOCKED "The deep-time shared world" (premise.md). "We did not go to another world" is correct; keep her on the same world. LOCKED "Living agency and magic" (rules.md): the Valkyrie is alive (pulse L41, breath L19, damp skin L243), so do not recast her as a construct or a disembodied spirit. LOCKED "No More Magic terminal extinction boundary": the later culture's singers warm stones with a held note (L259), so the arrival era must stay before that extinction. Add nothing that implies the Long Dark. CANON premise notes that collapse is "recurrent, not a law": do not expand the Valkyrie from folk story and "Their ending has not come" into a universal office.
- What already works and must not be flattened. Nacorra's early crafted lines ("Let me show you whom they killed", "Whatever else you carry, carry that") are deliberate, because she came upstairs with a rehearsed speech (L15). Do not plane them down. Keep the Valkyrie's short, literal, sensory register. Keep the silent failed contact at L225.

## Dialogue findings

### D1 · Major · Vasketh hands down a verdict after the confession

- Where: L201–L203, western doors, right after Nacorra gives the prisoners' dance and the quarrel
- Text:
> “I didn't want her to die,” I said.
> “I know,” Vasketh said. “That is not the part in doubt.”
- Problem: Philosopher / last-word line in the collection's legalistic house register. Nacorra's plea is a real, defensive non sequitur, since no one has accused her of wanting her lover dead. "I know" answers it. "That is not the part in doubt" then names the moral question (complicity, not love) in courtroom terms. The prisoners' dance, the changed word, the bracelet and the stopped clapping (L189–L197) have already made that case. It is the last exchange between the two rivals before the choice. Turning it into a ruling makes Vasketh the author's judge rather than a man about to die, and it tells the reader what to conclude just before the Valkyrie decides.
- Fix: Cut the second sentence and let "I know" stand. If the beat needs weight, add a physical action instead of a gloss. The man on the cushions has just stopped moving his lips (L199), and Vasketh can look at him. Do not replace the line with another summary of her guilt. Illustrative only: “I know,” Vasketh said, and looked at the man on the cushions.

### D2 · Minor · "that" in the Valkyrie's rules has no referent until after the line

- Where: L31, roof, the Valkyrie answering Vasketh's terms
- Text:
> I can keep one person alive beside me. I cannot turn that away.” She looked west.
- Problem: Ground/referent. Coming right after "I can keep one person alive beside me", "I cannot turn that away" first reads as "I cannot refuse that". The intended meaning is "I cannot deflect the body falling in the west" (L13), and the reader can decode it only from the look that follows. This is the rule that says she will not save the civilization, so it should land on first reading.
- Fix: Put the look before the line, and use a verb that cannot be heard as "refuse". Keep the three-part shape of her limits: can keep one, cannot stop it, cannot bring back the dead. Illustrative only: She looked west. “I cannot turn that aside.”

### D3 · Minor · Unclear speaker for "Then offer them" at the exposure hinge

- Where: L155–L167, western doors, just after Nacorra pulls out of the feast memory
- Text:
> “You weren't there.”
> “There are accounts.”
> “Then offer them.”
- Problem: Attribution. By alternation the line is Nacorra's, a proud dare. By vocabulary it is the Valkyrie's, since "offer" is her term (L39). The two readings change the hinge. If Nacorra says it, she walks into her own exposure, and the underlined name is her pride backfiring. If the Valkyrie says it, she is demanding what was withheld. The reader has to guess, and Nacorra's motive for inviting an account of the moment she just hid is not shown either way.
- Fix: Decide and tag it. If it is Nacorra's, give it one beat that shows she expects the account to fail, and consider a verb other than the Valkyrie's "offer". If it is the Valkyrie's, a plain tag is enough. Leave the rest of the volley, which moves fast and changes leverage.

### D4 · Minor · Narration re-explains "Stay here a moment"

- Where: L143–L147, the feast memory
- Text:
> “Stay here a moment,” the Valkyrie said.
> She wanted another moment at the feast.
> Now I had given her something she wanted. I could feel it in the pressure of her fingers.
- Problem: The narration glosses the line right after it. The Valkyrie's want is stated twice in consecutive paragraphs after a line that the pepper exchange (L133–L139) has already made legible. That leaves the reader nothing to infer at the story's most sensual moment.
- Fix: Delete L145 ("She wanted another moment at the feast."). The next paragraph already makes clear that "here" means the memory. Keep "I could feel it in the pressure of her fingers." Do not cut both glosses and add a third.

### D5 · Minor · Vasketh's last plea turns into wordplay

- Where: L215–L223, western doors, the Valkyrie has just chosen Nacorra
- Text:
> I can go on. I have spent my life going on.
> “Then hear more.”
- Problem: A Punchline-style echo, and composure under pressure. Vasketh takes the Valkyrie's "go on" and turns it into a pun on perseverance. The uptake is coherent (he means he can keep offering), but it makes his last spoken bid a debater's rejoinder, when the light is widening and he is about to die. "Then hear more" repeats the "Then…" rejoinder shape of L77 and L165. The next two sentences ("There are eight hundred years behind her little court. There are things nobody knows now except me.") are petty, true and desperate, and they are the right register.
- Fix: Cut or roughen the "go on / going on" sentences so he starts with need rather than a riposte. Keep the two sentences that follow. Optionally break "Then hear more" so it does not share a rhythm with his earlier retorts. Illustrative only: Vasketh struggled to stand. “Please. There are eight hundred years behind her little court.”

## Prose findings

### P1 · Minor · Vasketh's sleeve-grab at the doors is never explained

- Where: L113–L119, western doors, before Nacorra offers the feast
- Text:
> He closed his eyes in the sudden coolness, then opened them sharply and caught my sleeve.
> Even with wounded people on the floor, my eye found the best place to stand.
- Problem: Unclear staging at a turn. Vasketh grabs her sleeve, and nothing he says or does explains it. The next paragraph gives a different cause for "There was more" (her performer's eye for the room). The reader cannot tell whether he is pressing her to confess, stopping her from walking off as she did at L111, or reacting to something else, so the motive for her offer is split.
- Fix: Either show what the grip is for (he holds her there as she turns to move on, which echoes L111's "I moved on before she could ask me for anything"), or cut "and caught my sleeve" and let the performer's eye be the only cause.

### P2 · Minor · Repeated paired-sentence template

- Where: L107, L117, L129, L141, L197, L237, L245, L261, L263
- Text:
> I knew this room in lamplight. I knew precisely how far a voice travelled
> I heard myself turn cruel when my excuses failed. I heard the door close after my lover left.
> I saw clothing catch fire. I saw a woman trying to drag a burning blanket
- Problem: Two consecutive sentences that open with the same words ("You could… You could…", "I heard… I heard…", "Sometimes… Sometimes…", "I have put down… I have put down…", "You may… You may…") appear nine times in 3,300 words. By the ending the rhythm is noticeable. In L107 and L129 it imitates song and dance structure and is earned by the narrator.
- Fix: Keep L107 and L129. Vary two or three of the others (L117, L197, L237, L261 are the easiest) by merging the pair into one sentence or changing the second opening. No rewording beyond that.

## Continuity and prompt findings

### C1 · Minor · The arrival geography cannot be decoded

- Where: L247, the beach six centuries later; compare L29 on the roof
- Text:
> The sea lay east of me; the islands had been to the west. I had to turn around twice before I understood where she had brought me.
> “To the mainland?”
- Problem: The line promises an understanding it never delivers. From the palace the sea was west (L13, L95). Here it is east, with the islands "to the west". The reader cannot work out whether she stands on the far side of the mainland Vasketh asked about at L29, on land the impact raised, or somewhere else. The disorientation is fine, but the specific compass facts invite a puzzle with no answer.
- Fix: Either let Nacorra state in a clause what she understood (for example, that she was on the far shore of the mainland), or cut the compass clause and keep "I had to turn around twice" as plain disorientation. Do not add a geography explanation longer than a clause.

## Recurring patterns

- Vasketh's clean last word: L77, L91, L163, L175, L203, L217. Only L203 must change (D1), and L217 should (D5). L77, L91, L163 and L175 do real work (accusation, evidence, the push that forces the confession), so keep them and do not add more.
- "Then…" rejoinder openings: L77, L165, L221. Three in the Vasketh scenes. D3 and D5 touch two of them, so vary them if you are editing there.
- Narration explaining a line just spoken: L145–L147 (D4). Elsewhere the story trusts the reader; keep it that way.
- Paired-sentence anaphora: nine instances (P2). Vary a few and keep the song-shaped ones.

## Preserve

- The opening line and the first reader address at L17 ("You would have been on my side before you knew my name."). The ending pays them off.
- The wounded man at the stair, L97–L111, the best dialogue in the story:
> “Don't make him,” the woman said. “He starts coughing.”
> “I'm not.” I stopped my hand.
- The pepper exchange, where the Valkyrie feels a sensation before she understands it:
> “It hurts, but you want another.”
- "Everyone did." (L59): past tense, Vasketh's dry dig. Keep as is.
- "May I have a little shade?" (L113), Vasketh's one ordinary, human request.
- The confession fragments, L181–L185. Do not smooth or extend them.
> “That night. When they came in. I liked all of it.”
- The Valkyrie's reason for her choice. It is concrete, in her own simple register, and earned by L143–L151 and L187–L197. Do not expand it or have anyone restate it.
> “You stopped where I wanted to stay. Then you made me go on.”
- The failed contact, when Vasketh pleads with nothing coming through (L225).
- The reversed "Will you come?" and "Their ending has not come." on the beach (L253–L255).
- The narrator catching herself aestheticizing the catastrophe:
> You can see how easily I do it.
- The closing image, which answers "My lover stopped clapping" without stating it:
> I sang it anyway. When I reached the changed word, she kept her hands still on the open book.

## Suggested edit order

1. D1: the only Major. One cut at the moral hinge changes how the choice reads.
2. D3: tag the exposure hinge so Nacorra's (or the Valkyrie's) motive is legible before her name appears on the page.
3. D2: move the look, or change the verb, in the Valkyrie's rules so the "cannot save you" limit lands on first reading.
4. D5: take the pun out of Vasketh's last plea while you are next to D1, so his final bid sounds like need.
5. D4: delete the one-sentence gloss after "Stay here a moment."
6. P1: give the sleeve-grab a visible purpose or cut it.
7. C1: resolve or trim the compass clause on the beach.
8. P2: on a final read-through, vary two or three paired-sentence openings, leaving the song-shaped ones.
