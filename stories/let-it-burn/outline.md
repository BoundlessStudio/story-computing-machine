# Provisional direction

First-person, roughly 3,000 words, within the waterfall's single rising minute. The dark-haired speaker, in a coral gown, lost her kingdom at seven, thirty years before this centenary. She has spent almost all her remembered life preparing a warning to the queen before the siege. The palace balcony survived and has been repaired under a successor country. Her mother was a loving, imperfect queen, not a concealed monster.

Her daughter has come with her. The prepared message could stop the decision that brought the army to the capital. But changing thirty years changes encounters, births, and attachments. The speaker met her daughter's father in exile. She cannot guarantee this child's survival in a restored kingdom, and has treated that uncertainty as something she can solve by refining the message. A smaller rescue of her mother also risks changing her childhood and later life. The risk is real; erasure is not asserted as certain.

The letter appears: "Whatever you do, let it burn." She will write it at the end of this same minute and send it to her own hand at its beginning, consuming her one message. The letter provokes the choice but supplies no new evidence or hidden evil. Its reason must arise in the present encounter.

Her daughter has helped prepare the message, trying to be generous about a grandmother she never met. Asked directly, she admits she is frightened. The speaker recognizes that she is turning her child into another dutiful girl who says whatever a grieving parent needs. Memories make the queen a person with homely pleasures and unfinished care, rather than a statue demanding restoration. Letting the kingdom burn abandons real loved people; their deaths are not declared good or necessary.

She chooses the people who are here without certainty that no better sentence existed, and sends herself the warning. The falls resume; her mother remains dead. End with an unpractised contact between mother and daughter and the unfinished grief, not a thematic speech.

Open pressures: fixed-loop agency; whether the daughter becomes a moral prop; the smaller-rescue alternative; and whether the minute discovers something emotionally new instead of restating a familiar time-travel dilemma.
