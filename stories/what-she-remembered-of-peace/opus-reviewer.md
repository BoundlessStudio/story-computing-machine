# Opus review: What She Remembered of Peace

Independent audit for a later editing pass. It is not a `review.md` verdict,
does not change canon state, and does not authorize edits by itself.

## Verdict

- Dialogue: REVISE — two lines that break the scene (the "It did." uptake and the wrong catcher at the ladder), both Olyne–Anvek decisions resolved by the same tidy terms-and-specifics routine, and Naro and the consent questions reduced to procedure tics.
- Prose: REVISE — the narration keeps certifying what characters can and cannot see and glosses lines the reader has already understood; otherwise concrete and well paced.
- Continuity and prompt: REVISE — the prompt is delivered, but the brass key and the painted cuff contradict earlier facts, and the service ladder appears only when the plot needs it.
- Edit scope: heavy-dialogue
- Existing review.md said: PASS; Dialogue: PASS. Disagree: it praised the "partial sightlines" and "individual consent" that have hardened into tics, and missed two broken lines and two object contradictions.

The story's own safety rules (sightlines, consent, budgets) are being spoken aloud as dialogue. Anvek's library voice is meant to sound like a mission briefing, and that contrast is the payoff the prompt asks for. But Naro, the narration and, more and more, Olyne all take up the same grammar of evidence and terms, so a burning building sounds like a procedure. Both Olyne–Anvek decision points run one script: Olyne claims to protect her, Anvek corrects her wording, Olyne gives in with a two-word line, and Anvek asks for "specifics." The edit should keep Anvek's library register as the one deliberately machine-like voice. It should give Olyne a frightened civilian voice that knows people rather than parameters, make Naro a busy human responder instead of a list of disclaimers, and keep consent to where it matters at the start and end rather than at every descent.

## Edit guardrails

- Prompt (acceptance authority): a war-built android taken in to save her from scrapping, rehabilitated, given a home, taught peacetime life; a new war; she draws on old programming. The library opening and the "Local objective" declaration (L135) are that old programming on the page and must stay visible. The story must stay complete and resolved, 2,500–4,000 words (now about 3,380), broadly accessible, with non-graphic violence. Active profile: prospective-2026-08-21. POV is close third on Olyne.
- Keep the backstory choice in L121: independent technicians did the rehabilitation at Anvek's request; Olyne supplied the appeal, the room, and everyday teaching (buses, landlord apologies, bruised pears). That paragraph is the story's only rehabilitation and integration material, so trim or move it but do not cut its content. LOCKED personhood protection: Anvek's choices must stay hers, not a program's.
- Internal ledger to preserve through edits: eleven residents in Gess's count, including Gess; Olyne and Anvek are extra (thirteen from the block). Rider order: husband first, wife second with the pack, washerwoman seventh, quiet man eighth, last divided-flat resident ninth, baker tenth, Gess by ladder. Library budget is 8 minutes of tracking (3:14 left at seven down) and 90 seconds of torque (22 used at the brake and shot, 8 at the weight). Anvek's right hand and shoulder are the damaged ones. Gess's right hand is weak. The marking pattern is three short flashes and one long, with fire 10–20 seconds later. The empty awning is the planted sacrifice.
- No universe entries bind these names or mechanisms; do not import any.
- Do not flatten what works: Gess's counting, the couple, the washerwoman, "Don't pull. You change the load.", the jacket-and-latch coda. With the library closed, Anvek can be looser and drier (contractions, domestic teasing). With it open, keep her clipped, and keep her slowing as the heat rises.

## Dialogue findings

### D1 · Blocking · "It did." answers nothing

- Where: L43–L49, Gess arrives with the count
- Text:
> “I’m eleven.”
> It did. Olyne checked faces anyway
- Problem: Ground/referent. "It did." has no antecedent. Nothing "did" anything in the previous line; it reads as the leftover of an earlier draft. The reader stops to work out what "it" is. The Gess setup itself works: her counting herself is character, and the refrain pays off at L329.
- Fix: Delete "It did." The paragraph already ends by confirming "Eleven." If a beat is wanted, make it Olyne's reaction to Gess counting herself. Illustrative only: "Olyne checked faces anyway:"

### D2 · Blocking · Olyne's ladder "specifics" name the wrong catcher

- Where: L293, final roof exchange (see also L271, L307)
- Text:
> Gess can catch you at the third landing if your shoulder slips.
> Naro climbed to the third landing, no farther than he could see, and called the placement of each foot.
> Below, Gess held the blue lamp in her good hand. Naro stood at the lower rail beside her
- Problem: Ground/scene. The line describes the scene wrongly. Naro was the one on the third landing; Gess has one good hand, has just been caught herself, and at L307 is at the bottom rail. These are the specifics Anvek asked for, and the narration then praises "a fact instead of a promise," so a wrong fact at the decisive exchange undermines the point of the beat.
- Fix: Name Naro, or cut the clause. Illustrative only: "Naro can reach the third landing if your shoulder slips."

### D3 · Major · The library decision is a debate won by a correction and an aphorism

- Where: L107–L129, roof, before the library opens
- Text:
> We got you away from people who wanted to cut you down for parts. We made a life here. I am not letting a war take it back.
> You helped me get an appeal. You gave me a room.
> Useful things. Not the source of the woman standing before her.
> “I know,” Olyne said.
> Then give me what the library doesn’t have.
> It was a better request than Olyne deserved.
- Problem: The moves are right: Olyne claims ownership and Anvek cuts the claim down to what Olyne actually did. The execution is too tidy. A backstory paragraph (L121) sits between Anvek's line and Olyne's answer while a drone is overhead and the fire has forty minutes. It then states the lesson in narration ("Not the source..."). Olyne gives up her whole position in two words, with no fear, bargaining, or hurt. Anvek closes with a riddle-like aphorism that only Olyne's next list decodes (Punchline Patch / Philosopher). The narrator then grades the line ("a better request than Olyne deserved"). The reader is left nothing to infer at the story's central turn.
- Fix: Keep Olyne's petition speech with its framing at L117, and keep Anvek's correction; that is the crux. Move L121 earlier, before the roof (for example after L41 or at L77), or cut it to two sentences, and drop its last two sentences. Let Olyne's concession cost her something: a silence, a smaller and less dignified protest, a hand on Anvek's sleeve. Replace the aphorism with a concrete ask and cut L127's first sentence. Illustrative only: "Then tell me who can't hold on, and what breaks under weight."

### D4 · Major · The final roof exchange replays the same negotiation script

- Where: L275–L305, Olyne and Anvek alone on the roof
- Text:
> Then you go. I’ll drop the counterweight.
> I don’t want you choosing the dangerous place for me.
> “What do you need?”
> “Specifics.”
> The burst can’t call the shelter.
> It’s fixed. It may draw a search pass away from the ladder. It may do nothing.
> “Do it once.”
> “That is my plan.”
- Problem: This is the same shape as D3: Olyne protects, Anvek refuses, Olyne asks what she needs, Anvek says "Specifics," Olyne gives a list, Anvek decides. Then:
  - The counterweight task appears without a purpose (Ground/uptake). The reader learns only at L293 and L317 that it blocks the roof door against the fire.
  - Anvek's refusal states the story's theme as a rule about Olyne ("choosing the dangerous place for me") instead of a decision about this roof (Philosopher).
  - "The burst can't call the shelter." answers a claim nobody made. It exists to let Anvek explain, and her "It's fixed" is ambiguous (repaired? fixed-pattern?) until the narration at L315.
  - "Do it once." / "That is my plan." is a clause-and-counterclause ending with a dry last word (Punchline Patch).
- Fix: Plant the counterweight's purpose earlier (for example at L261 when the fire breaks through, or in Olyne's line). Keep the opening "No." / "Yes." echo of L29–L35 if wanted, but break the template after it. Anvek states her choice about the task and leaves the relationship's principle to the reader, and L287's narration of Olyne's temptation can stay. Do not repeat "What do you need?" / "Specifics." Let Olyne give way through action, or angrily. If she objects to the burst, have her object to the risk. Cut "Do it once." / "That is my plan." Illustrative only: "They'll find you by it." / "Maybe. Go."

### D5 · Major · Naro speaks almost entirely in disclaimers

- Where: L93, L97, L103, L171, L251, L309, L333, radio and shelter
- Text:
> Only the lower terrace. I cannot see you or your cradle head.
> Don’t infer anything else from the lamps.
> I cannot promise interception or pickup.
> Receiving rail clear. I see the cradle. I do not see your brake.
> I cannot assess the roof anchors.
> I can’t replace that actuator here. I can’t tell whether the scoring is permanent until it’s cold.
- Problem: Identical Voices / procedure routine. Nearly every Naro line reports the edges of his knowledge. That is the reviewer's evidence rule spoken by a character. A responder running a shelter under fire would state his limits once and then be busy. As written he has no pressure, temper, or warmth, and he pushes the whole story toward the same hedged register.
- Fix: Keep the call sign exchange, one clear statement that he can see only the terrace (L93), and the marking-pattern briefing (L103), which is plot-bearing. Cut "Don't infer anything else from the lamps.", "I do not see your brake.", and "I cannot assess the roof anchors." Reduce L333 to one limit. Give him the texture of a crowded shelter: other traffic, a name he checks, brusque kindness. Illustrative only: "Blue. Rail's clear, bring him down, we've got hands."

### D6 · Major · Consent turned into a form read aloud at every descent

- Where: L167, L189, L215, L267 (setup L129, payoff L343)
- Text:
> Do you authorize us to use it for the shelter repeater?
> The cradle, carrying the washerwoman, hung one storey down.
> Rider, do you consent to continue?
> Naro’s crew can receive him. Do you consent to wait for the next descent?
> Do you agree to the ladder?
- Problem: The idea is earned. Anvek was once a body a remote officer could force, and her "Olyne, ask them. Don't answer for them." (L129) plus the paragraph of individual yeses (L133) are some of the story's best material. Repeated in the wording of a form, though, consent becomes a procedure routine. Anvek asks a woman swinging in a basket under a drone "Rider, do you consent to continue?". Gess is told to "consent to wait". Naro asks formal authorization for a battery Olyne has just sent him. The payoff "May I take off your jacket?" (L343) loses force because the question has been asked in this voice four times already.
- Fix: Keep L129, L133 and L343. Keep Olyne's ladder question to Gess (L267) only if it reads as Olyne having learned something, and phrase it as she would. Make the mid-air check a human one that still leaves the washerwoman a choice, and keep her answer. Make the Gess line an order she can swear at. Have Naro confirm the battery rather than ask for it. Illustrative only: "Still all right down there?" (answered by "Yes. And mind my bag.")

### D7 · Major · Olyne tops lines instead of sounding frightened

- Where: L187, L205–L207, L265–L267 (and L301, covered in D4)
- Text:
> “Not ours yet,” Olyne answered.
> “Your grip doesn’t.”
> “It goes to the shelter,” Olyne said.
- Problem: Correction ping-pong / uniform fluency under pressure. Olyne is a civilian whose building is burning and whose companion is being damaged, yet her lines are clean last words. She sharpens Gess's "Not ours" into a grim epigram and twice answers "Headcount goes with the building" with a wordplay correction. Apart from the framed petition speech (L117) and the jacket coda, her speech in the action scenes is interchangeable with Anvek's clipped reports.
- Fix: Replace the toppers with what Olyne wants from Gess: a plea, an order, or impatience. Let her speech fray where the pressure peaks (the drone scenes, the cable snap): names, repeated instructions, a sentence she abandons. Leave the report grammar for the two moments she deliberately feeds Anvek specifics (L127, L293). Illustrative only: "Gess. Please. Get in the basket."

### D8 · Minor · Opening: "the sealed library" is unanchored, and Olyne's first words are a definition

- Where: L15–L19, kitchen
- Text:
> It flagged the sealed library.
> Flagged isn't opened.
- Problem: Ground/referent. The reader meets "the sealed library" cold and cannot tell whether the burst marked Anvek or the library registered the burst. The story never says what the library is or who sealed it; L121 lists what the technicians did and leaves it out. Olyne's first reaction to a military pulse is a precise distinction rather than fear.
- Fix: Add one concrete anchor: a clause in Anvek's line, the next narration sentence, or L121 saying the technicians left her combat library sealed behind her access plate. Let Olyne's first line ask rather than define; Anvek's "No." can stay. Illustrative only: "Is it open? Anvek, is it open?"

### D9 · Minor · The limits recitation reads as the story's rulebook

- Where: L137–L139, opening the library
- Text:
> because she needed to hear Anvek speak them.
> No network access, machine control, invented data, or sensor repair.
- Problem: Exposition. The objective declaration at L135 is the prompt's payoff and works. The limits list that follows includes items the story never uses ("invented data", "sensor repair"). "Invented data" is a production rule rather than something a combat system would list. The narration explains why Olyne asks, which marks the question as a cue for the reader.
- Fix: Keep "And the limits," and the limits the story uses (cover, sound-only identification, heat slowing speech and fine movement, torque damage). Cut "invented data" and "sensor repair", and cut the gloss after Olyne's line.

### D10 · Minor · Narration glosses lines the reader has already read

- Where: L41, L173, L285
- Text:
> There was no argument in the words, and no invitation to one.
> He did not promise a third.
> Even with the delay in her speech, the answer came plain.
- Problem: Each sentence explains the line before or after it: tone, what was not said, how plain it was. The reader has nothing left to infer. (The glosses inside the decisive exchange are covered in D3.)
- Fix: Cut all three. At L285 keep the look that begins the paragraph and let the line follow it.

### D11 · Minor · Small Ground snags

- Where: L95–L97 lamp lanes; L195–L199 count; L239 line-driver
- Text:
> Why is there one blue?
> Because I can clear one lane.
> Gess repeated, “Seven down. Four here.”
> Olyne, clear the tool.
- Problem: "Lane" is never defined; there are three blue lamps but one cradle, and the lane count never matters again. "Gess repeated" has no spoken original, because L195 is narration. "Clear the tool" normally means make a weapon safe, the opposite of pulling the safety pin, which is what Olyne does.
- Fix: Say in a clause what the three lamps are, or make it a single blue lamp. Change "repeated" to "said", or make L195 Gess's own line. Replace "clear the tool" with "Pull the pin."

## Prose findings

### P1 · Major · Narration keeps certifying what people can and cannot see

- Where: L197, L271, L307, L311, L315
- Text:
> did not pretend the view had cleared
> no farther than he could see
> looking up only as far as he could actually see
> At last, a fact instead of a promise.
> Anvek did not claim to know why.
- Problem: The evidence rule behind D5 also runs through the narration. People are praised for not overclaiming, and a closing epigram ("a fact instead of a promise") states the idea. Together with Naro's dialogue, it makes epistemic caution the story's loudest texture, louder than fear, smoke or loss. "No farther than he could see" is also unclear staging: Naro climbs up a ladder to catch Gess.
- Fix: Cut these phrases. The actions already show the limits (Anvek shuts an eye against grit; the engine changes pitch with no explanation). Keep Naro calling each foothold and cut the qualifier. End L311 at "Olyne said."

### P2 · Minor · POV slip on the torque count

- Where: L315, Olyne on the third landing
- Text:
> used eight more seconds of high torque to wrench it sideways
- Problem: The close third is on Olyne, who is two landings down in smoke and cannot know the count. The number matters to the budget (22 + 8 of 90).
- Fix: Drop the number from narration and let Anvek report it once she reaches Olyne or in the shelter, or give Olyne only what she can see.

## Continuity and prompt findings

### C1 · Blocking · The brass radio key is given away, then back in Olyne's palm

- Where: L163 and L347
- Text:
> She gave her the brass radio key too, though there was nothing below it would unlock.
> The little brass radio key had stamped a rectangle into her palm.
- Problem: Object continuity. Olyne hands the key to the fifth-floor woman at L163, but it is in her hand in the shelter. The L163 gesture is also unexplained, as is sending the pack down, which Naro then has to ask about (D6).
- Fix: Cut the key clause at L163 and keep the key (and its mark in her palm) for the coda, or remove the key from L347. Give the pack handoff half a clause of motive (the shelter needs power to keep talking) so Naro can confirm rather than ask.

### C2 · Blocking · The final cut goes under the wrong cuff

- Where: L41, L237, L335, L341, L345, L361
- Text:
> The left cuff still carried blue paint
> Anvek’s right hand remained locked around the brake.
> Anvek’s right fingers remained curled against her palm.
> Cut the shoulder seam.
> Olyne slid the blunt emergency shears beneath the blue-painted cuff and did exactly that.
- Problem: The blue paint is on the left cuff, but the damaged arm is the right and Olyne is crouched on that side. The last line also starts the cut at the cuff when the instruction was the shoulder seam. The closing image contradicts both the setup and the instruction.
- Fix: Smallest edit: make L41 "The right cuff". Then either have Anvek ask for the sleeve seam (so the shears start at the cuff) or have Olyne start at the shoulder while the painted cuff stays in view. Keep "Cut only the seam" and the blue paint in the closing image.

### C3 · Major · The service ladder appears only when the cradle fails

- Where: L65–L77 (not mentioned), L91, L263, L293
- Text:
> Eleven to move. Stair's gone.
> You take the service ladder.
> Service ladder is intact to the lower terrace.
- Problem: Causality. The premise is that the stair is gone and only the cradle can lower people. Then, after the cable is cut, a service ladder to the same terrace turns out to be usable by a woman with one good hand, by Olyne, and by an android with a ruined arm. The story is exact about logistics everywhere else, so the reader will ask why the couple and the divided-flat residents rode a fraying cable under drone marking instead.
- Fix: Name the ladder when the group reaches the roof (around L65–L77) and give it a concrete cost that makes it the last resort, in terms the story already uses: slow, one climber at a time with the single harness, exposed on the east face to the drone's passes, impossible for the baker's knee. After that, the cable failure forces it rather than conveniently revealing it.
