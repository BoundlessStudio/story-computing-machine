# Opus review: By Tooth and Threshold

Independent audit for a later editing pass. It is not a `review.md` verdict,
does not change canon state, and does not authorize edits by itself.

## Verdict

- Dialogue: REVISE — one Blocking Ground failure (the shielding exchange), three more knowledge or uptake breaks, and both speakers share one clipped register that keeps correcting and closing on maxims.
- Prose: PASS — concrete, well staged, sparing with em dashes; only minor polish (water-infrastructure texture, "chose" used as an agency stamp, narration glosses).
- Continuity and prompt: REVISE — the prompt is delivered and the length is in band, but the reader cannot work out when the transmitter was broadcasting or why the setter arrives tonight.
- Edit scope: heavy-dialogue
- Existing review.md said: PASS, with `Dialogue: PASS` and `Internal: PASS`; I disagree on both dialogue and internal continuity.

The conversation is an interview between two specialists. Dev asks about eighteen of the story's twenty-one spoken questions and Audra answers them, both in the same terse, exact register. When Audra is not answering she corrects him or closes on a maxim: "You did. By moving it.", "The one coming up your drive.", "Ask the next question you can use.", "Most isn't a direction.", "Don't add to it." Under that polish there are real Ground breaks: Dev asks about shielding that nobody has proposed and gets a receiver bearing that a wolf could not have seen; the setter demands a box he never saw; and the scrape code changes meaning at the climax. Audra is bleeding, has spent four nights as a wolf, and runs a fever by the end, yet she speaks as smoothly as Dev does in his own mudroom. Neither of them says anything unguarded about the extraordinary fact between them. The edit pass should fix the Ground breaks first. After that, let Dev talk too much and let Audra say less because speaking costs her, not because she is scoring points. Replace the maxims and corrections with answers, refusals and silence. The plot, the action in the pen and the nonverbal signal system are sound and should stay.

## Edit guardrails

- Prompt: a woman the narrator has never met appears at his door claiming a debt, a few days after he freed a very large wolf from a bear trap. She is that wolf, and the debt must be resolved. The piece must be a complete story of 2,500–4,000 words (currently about 3,290) and broadly accessible, with non-graphic violence. The rewrite request asks for all dialogue to be revised to the last recorded profile, `prospective-2026-08-21`, which is the active one.
- The outline's Voice capsule is advisory but matches the rewrite goal. Dev reasons through conditions, evidence and the layout of his property. Audra reasons through routes, scent, timing and bodily limits. The setter gives immediate commands only. Pain makes Audra drop links, and "Neither gains eloquence." Keep out werewolf-romance diction, alpha hierarchy, matching aphorisms, tidy consent seminars and romance.
- Keep these story mechanics consistent: a seventy-second change that cannot pause; no speech or tool use during the change or in wolf form; fear and orders cannot trigger a change; injuries carry across bodies; a second change within an hour worsens bleeding and fever; the transmitter clicks before each pulse on a three-minute interval; one scrape means stop, a strike on the fence wire means take cover, and a boot touch gives direction; the pickup blocks the far end and the rope closes the road gate; the setter stays unnamed and escapes; Dev relocates.
- Universe: I found no LOCKED or CANON conflict. Do not tie Audra's change to Cody's LOCKED shapeshifting entry in `universe/rules.md`, and do not add species, pack or magical-debt lore. The story is scoped to one person and one device.
- Keep the architecture (doorstep, mudroom, transmitter, change, pen, retreat, departure) and the quiet logistical ending. The plot does not need restructuring.

## Dialogue findings

### D1 · Blocking · Shielding question with no setup, answered from impossible knowledge

- Where: L111–L121, mudroom, after the wound care
- Text:
> "How did you find out it was shieldable?"
> "He put the trap in his vehicle. The bearing vanished until he opened the rear door."
- Problem: Ground/uptake and Ground/knowledge. Audra has never said the transmitter can be shielded. The only mention of shielding is Dev's own dismissal at L73. So the question assumes a claim nobody made, and it exists to set up her answer. That answer is worse. A "bearing" is what the setter's receiver shows, and a wolf could not see it. The event also has no place in the timeline: Dev took the trap from the creek, so the setter could only have carried it in his vehicle before he set it. That means Audra watched the trap being transported and then stepped into it anyway.
- Fix: Cut L119–L121. Dev already reaches shielding himself at L149 ("If both seams close, it should cut most radio."), and that is enough to support the cash-box plan. If Audra must contribute, give her only behavior she could have watched, placed in a time that exists (see C1). Keep "How much farther?" / "I don't know." just before this; it is good. Illustrative only: "Steel might hold it in. Might not." (Dev, thinking aloud as he looks at the cash box).

### D2 · Major · Doorstep answers are riddles, and they leak knowledge

- Where: L25–L37 and L61–L65, the doorstep
- Text:
> "Who told you it was here?"
> "You did. By moving it."
> "How did you find my door?"
> "I followed his vehicle from the creek. I knew its scent. He followed your signal."
> "You checked the pan and the chain. Look under the swivel housing."
- Problem: Dev asks the same question twice. The first answer is a paradox that the second answer contradicts: she found him by following the setter, not because Dev "told" her. Nobody registers the riddle. "Your signal" means nothing to Dev or to the reader at L37, yet Dev doesn't ask "What signal?". He also asks nothing about "the man who set it", even though he has just been told that man is coming to his door. At L65 Audra knows exactly which parts Dev inspected in his own mudroom, which she had no way of seeing.
- Fix: Keep one of the two "how did you find me" questions and give it a plain answer, or keep the evasion and let Dev push back on it. Either have Dev pick up "signal", which leads straight into the transmitter explanation at L61, or hold the word back until L61. Give Audra a reason to know where the transmitter sits instead of knowing what Dev checked. Illustrative only: "I lay with my face against that chain. Look under the swivel housing."

### D3 · Major · The debt, the prompt's hook, lands as a pun

- Where: L73–L81, doorstep, just before the engine is heard
- Text:
> "Then what debt are you paying?"
> "The one coming up your drive."
- Problem: Punchline Patch. Dev's question is shaped to set up her wordplay, and "the one" makes the man himself the debt. The engine sounds on cue in the next paragraph, which makes the exchange feel staged. This is the only time the story asks directly what the debt is, and it answers with a quip.
- Fix: Keep Dev's question as real confusion: why does a stranger think she owes him? Let Audra answer through what she senses and intends, not through wordplay. Let the engine arrive without being announced. Illustrative only: "I can hear him on your lane. That's what I came about."

### D4 · Major · Two speakers, one clipped and corrective register

- Where: throughout; clearest at L51–L57 (the door), L83–L87 (terms of entry), L95–L99 (the trap tooth), L133–L137 (cutting), L203–L205 (the launcher)
- Text:
> "I didn't ask to come in."
> "Mudroom only. The house door stays locked.
> "I can leave by it whenever I decide?"
> "Then stop looking at my leg for proof."
> "He missed. Where you stand matters now."
- Problem: Identical Voices and correction ping-pong. Cover the tags and the only way to tell Dev from Audra is that one asks and one answers. Both speak in short declaratives with no hesitation, both favor exact wording, and Audra routinely corrects Dev's premises ("I didn't ask to come in", "Not the boot") or caps an exchange with an aphorism ("Where you stand matters now"). The terms-of-entry exchange plays out as clause and confirmation, which is the "tidy consent seminar" the outline set out to avoid. The unlatched door itself is a good idea. Audra's pain, her four days in another body and her fever never roughen her speech until L327.
- Fix: Follow the Voice map. Remove the correction replies at L57 and L99 (and L103, see D11), and let Audra simply refuse, ignore the point or do something. Keep the unlatched-door action at L83–L87, but make the talk rougher and less contractual (Dev can over-explain, and Audra can just test the latch). Give Dev at least one line that shows he is shaken or amazed. Do not replace one clever line with another.

### D5 · Major · A maxim becomes the story's lesson and is replayed twice at the end

- Where: L123–L127, the mudroom; L339–L341, the final scene
- Text:
> "What frequency? Is the signal directional? Does it log movement?"
> "Ask the next question you can use."
> The rebuke landed because it was precise.
> Dev swallowed all three and asked the one that changed what he should do.
> He chose the one he could use.
- Problem: Philosopher. Audra answers Dev's anxious pile of questions with an aphorism, and the narration endorses it for being "precise". The ending then turns it into a lesson: two consecutive sentences say the same thing, which looks like a leftover from revision. "Swallowed all three and asked the one" also contradicts itself. The unpunctuated question lists at L59 and L339 use the same device, and L59 switches between "you" and "she" mid-list ("What were you. What are you." against "How many doors had she tried").
- Fix: Let Audra's refusal come from pain and ignorance rather than doctrine, and cut L127. At L339–L341 keep one of the two sentences, or neither, and go straight to "Which route?". Make the L59 list consistent in person. Illustrative only: "I don't know. I only ever saw it from the wrong end."

### D6 · Major · "Most isn't a direction" cannot be decoded

- Where: L149–L153, sealing the transmitter in the cash box
- Text:
> "If both seams close, it should cut most radio."
> "Most isn't a direction."
> "It's what we have."
- Problem: Ground/referent and epigram. Literally, "most" is an amount and a direction is a bearing, so the line is a category error, and there is no figurative reading to recover. The reader can only guess the meaning ("a leak still gives him a bearing") from Dev's reply.
- Fix: Say the worry plainly, or cut Audra's line and let Dev's doubt stand alone. Illustrative only: "If any of it gets out, he still gets a line on us."

### D7 · Major · Planning and briefing run as alternating question and answer

- Where: L179–L225, from Dev's "I carry the box" through the rules of the change
- Text:
> "The pickup denies the forward exit. The closed road gate denies reverse."
> "Until he breaks one."
> "No. I have a phone and a fence post."
> "Seventy seconds. It doesn't pause. I can't speak or use anything while it happens."
> "I do. Fear won't. An order won't."
- Problem: Tennis Match and Exposition. L181–L205 is about twelve alternating one-liners of equal weight in the same tactical register ("denies"). "If he enters." / "Until he breaks one." could come from either speaker, and the run ends on a quip. L179 has Dev say "You choose the route", and at L187 he chooses it himself. L211–L225 is four question-and-rule pairs that restate the outline's rule text almost word for word. "What starts it?" serves the reader and the later payoff more than anything Dev needs to know right then.
- Fix: Let one person carry the plan. Dev talks through his own ground at some length (his gates, his truck, the posts he never finished), and Audra cuts in only where he is wrong about the setter (the wind, the high edge, the launcher). Either give her the route choice or cut "You choose the route". In the briefing, keep the signal codes and the seventy-seconds, no-speech facts, because Dev genuinely needs them. Drop the "Fear won't. An order won't." triplet, or attach it to real pressure, such as Dev trying to hurry her. The setter's failed "Down. Stay down." already shows the rule.

### D8 · Major · The two-scrape signal changes meaning at the climax

- Where: L213, L245 and L291–L299, from the briefing to the pen
- Text:
> One scrape: stop. Two: come to me.
> Dev pulled. The sorting gate swung its remaining foot and pinned the pole against the post.
- Problem: Ground in the nonverbal channel. Two scrapes is defined as "come to me". At L245 that roughly fits leaving together, but at L297 Dev reads it as "pull the gate now" and does not go to her. A reader who is tracking the code stumbles at the decisive action.
- Fix: Redefine two scrapes at L213 as "go" or "now". That fits both L245 and L297 without further changes.

### D9 · Major · The setter demands a box he has not seen

- Where: L271–L277, the pen confrontation
- Text:
> "Put the box down," the setter said.
> The setter looked toward the trough, then saw Dev's phone on the post.
- Problem: Ground/knowledge and staging. Dev is crouched behind the gate and is not holding anything. The cash box is sitting in the trough, and the setter only looks toward it after his receiver chirps. So he did not see it put there, and has no way to know it is a box. Dev's "It's down" goes along with the leak.
- Fix: Have the setter demand what he knows exists (his transmitter or his trap), or ask where it is, and adjust Dev's reply to match. Illustrative only: "Where's my trap?"

### D10 · Major · The final debt exchange ends on a line that doesn't follow

- Where: L335–L337, beside the culvert after the second change
- Text:
> "You don't owe me anything after this."
> "I decided what I owed before I knocked. Don't add to it."
- Problem: Ground/uptake plus Philosopher at the final meaningful exchange. Dev releases her from the debt. "Don't add to it" answers an addition he never made, so the listener hears a release and gets a riddle back. The first sentence states the story's thesis (her debt is her choice), which the action has already shown.
- Fix: Have her respond to the release itself, by accepting it, refusing it or brushing it aside for something concrete. Let pride or tiredness show rather than a principle. Illustrative only: "Good. I'm tired of owing you."

### D11 · Minor · The wound-care request doesn't fit the object

- Where: L97–L105, the mudroom bench
- Text:
> "May I cut those pins free?"
> "Not the boot. The wrap above it. Saline first. No alcohol."
- Problem: Nobody cuts safety pins; they get unfastened, or the cloth gets cut. "Not the boot" rejects something nobody proposed. The reader can't picture what is being cut until L105 reveals a wrap under the trouser leg. "May I" is also the only formal register Dev uses anywhere.
- Fix: Make Dev's request physical and specific (unpin the leg, cut the wrap) and have Audra's reply answer that request. Cut "Not the boot."

### D12 · Minor · The narration translates lines that should carry themselves

> She had been studying the angle, not instructing him in tools.
> Some of the links had fallen out of the sentence.

### D13 · Minor · Clipped referents

- Where: L69–L73, the first tick; L139, choosing cutters

> "Then use the small cutters. The big jaws will crush both against the plate."

## Prose findings

> "Give me the box. I take it south past the drainage cut.
> "We can take the bottom of the drainage line.

- Problem: The route runs along a drainage cut and a culvert. The prompt did not ask for either, so the style guide's "No default water infrastructure" rule applies to an authorized revision.
- Fix: Replace them with natural ground, such as a gully, a draw or a creek bottom, and replace the culvert with a cutbank or a rock ledge. Keep the geometry: low, downwind and hidden from the lane.

## Continuity and prompt findings

### C1 · Major · The transmitter and arrival timeline cannot be reconstructed

- Where: L11–L15, L37, L61, L73–L75, L201
- Text:
> It woke when you moved the trap. He had four days to follow the last locations."
> "If that's a radio, shielding it doesn't undo four nights of broadcasts."
> He used both at the creek after you left."
- Problem: One reading is that the trap has sat in Dev's mudroom for four nights, sending every three minutes. Then Dev somehow never heard a click that both of them hear across the room at L67. It would also leave unexplained why the setter, who was at the creek that same night, took four days to come, and why "There isn't a transmitter. I checked it." missed it. The other reading is that Dev moved the trap tonight, and then "four nights of broadcasts" and "four days to follow" are wrong. L11 reads as if Dev has only just brought the trap in, and L73 reads as if it has been broadcasting since the creek. "I followed his vehicle from the creek" (L37) puts both of them at the creek tonight without explaining why.
- Fix: Choose one chain and make L11–L15, L37, L61, L73–L75 and L201 agree with it. Here is a minimal chain. The transmitter wakes when moved and sleeps after lying still. It sent during the drive home four nights ago, then slept in Dev's truck bed or shed. The setter has been working the roads since the creek with those last fixes. Tonight Dev moved the trap to the bench, it woke, and the setter got a fresh fix. Audra has been shadowing his vehicle since the creek. That takes one clause from Audra ("it sleeps when it lies still") and replacing "four nights of broadcasts" with something like "whatever it sent on the drive home". It also removes the need for D1's vehicle story.

### C2 · Minor · Small physical slips

- Where: L267, L281 and L315 (the pen); L93 and L349 (the mudroom)
- Text:
> The setter's utility vehicle rolled down the old stock track without headlights.
> stopping when its beams found Dev's pickup across the far end
> The launcher and two darts remained inside the pen.
> and set the welding blanket aside so no responder would reach beneath it blind
- Problem: The vehicle has no headlights, yet "its beams" find the pickup. Only one dart is fired, and it lands in the mud by the pickup beyond the far gate, not inside the pen. The blanket was pulled off the trap at L93 and never put back, so there is nothing to set aside.
- Fix: Have him switch the lights on, or use another light source. Make it "the launcher and a spare dart", or place the dart correctly. Cut the blanket clause, or show Dev covering the trap again before they leave.
