# Outline

## Story

- Premise and central promise: **By Tooth and Threshold**. Four nights after adult Dev Kaylor freed an enormous wolf and brought its illegal trap home as evidence, injured Audra Liske appears. She is the same person in another body. The trap's transmitter is leading its setter to Dev's house. Resolve her voluntary debt—removing that danger—through this home crisis, without romance, ownership, or lore interview.
- Focal pressure or attachment: Dev wants to bar Audra, preserve evidence, and believe refusal ends involvement; he treats rescue as permission to demand answers. Audra wants to remove one danger without becoming dependent, captive, or servant; she wrongly assumes she must carry the tracker away alone despite her wound.
- Counterforce or complication: An unnamed trap-setter brings a receiver, dart launcher, and loop restraint to retrieve the trap and recapture Audra. Dev knows locks, drainage, and tools; Audra knows search patterns, wind, sound, and her limits. Injury, pulses, and incompatible plans close the window. The setter gives no lore speech.
- POV, distance, and information limit: Close third past through Dev, limited to doorstep, house, yard, and drainage lane. He reads injury, timing, tools, and cues but cannot share Audra's senses or thoughts, know the setter's larger motive, or generalize from one transformation. Audra chooses her proof.
- Governing movement and time shape: One escalating ninety-minute night: barred doorway, bounded entry, shed, yard, then livestock pen. Movement follows the transmitter and shifts control of access. Resolve privately; end with damage, wound care, approaching response, and Dev's temporary relocation—no friendship pledge, public proof, or thematic coda.
- Speculative rule or ordinary-world constraint: Audra deliberately changes between her human body and a very large wolf body. Change takes seventy seconds, cannot pause, prevents speech and tool use, and preserves identity, memory, and injuries. A second change within an hour worsens bleeding, pain, and fever; emotion and orders cannot trigger it. Wolf senses supply signals, never thoughts. The transmitter wakes when moved, clicks, then broadcasts every three minutes; tools can separate or shield it but not erase its last location. This establishes only Audra's body and this device—not a species, pack law, magical debt, or sentient threshold.

## Voice

- Narrative texture: Follow Dev's scan of locks, distances, blood, wind, and available hands; expose mistaken control through what he checks. Render change as effort and altered affordance, not spectacle.
- Conversational texture: Doorstep caution contracts into proof, tool names, route corrections, boundary checks, and wound care. Leave cues untranslated; avoid banter and polished disclosure.
- Rhetorical ownership: Dev reasons through conditions, evidence, and property geometry. Audra uses routes, scent, timing, bodily limits, and chosen obligations. The setter manages only immediate commands.
- Pressure behavior: Dev's questions multiply, then shorten as he listens. Pain makes Audra omit links; changing removes speech and forces agreed signals. Neither gains eloquence.
- Anti-default: Exclude werewolf-romance diction, alpha hierarchy, animalized attraction, matching aphorisms, and tidy consent seminars; keep two wary adults on one physical threat.

## Beats

1. At dusk Audra stays beyond the storm door and names a debt, not a shelter request. Dev keeps the chain engaged and overreaches with questions. She offers camping details, a matching wound, and warns that his stored trap is calling someone closer.
2. The beacon's click and an approaching engine support her warning. Audra chooses bounded proof without supplying an origin story. Dev explicitly permits her into the mudroom for tools and first aid, with a defined route and leave option; entry grants no bodily authority.
3. They expose the transmitter. Dev wants intact evidence; Audra wants to carry the beacon away as a wolf. His attempted command fails, while blood loss makes solitary repayment untenable.
4. Headlights stop beyond the bend as the setter begins a quiet sweep. Dev calls for help, secures the inner house, and offers a joint plan: separate and shield the trap, carry the beacon through the drainage cut, and use the livestock pen's gates to limit approach.
5. Crossing the yard, Audra chooses when to change. Pain and lost speech make agreed signals causal. Dev must follow her warning about wind instead of demanding explanation; Audra must let Dev carry the beacon rather than make herself bait.
6. At the pen, the setter attempts dart-and-loop capture. Dev uses gate geometry and his vehicle to deny retreat; Audra disables the line without mauling or pursuit. Her wound reopens and the gate breaks, but the setter loses the equipment and withdraws as responders approach.
7. Dev preserves the trap and gear without exposing Audra publicly. She accepts only treatment she chooses and counts this specific help as the whole debt, implying no bond or future service. Dev prepares to leave the compromised house temporarily; Audra sets her own route and timing. Stop on costly logistics, not a symbolic crossing.
