# Outline

## Story

- Premise and central promise: A few evenings after the unnamed first-person narrator freed an enormous wolf from an illegal steel trap, an injured woman appears at their small rented house on the edge of the same woods. The cord around her ankle is the orange camp cord the narrator tied above the wolf's wound, and she knows what the narrator whispered while opening the jaws. She was the wolf, but the debt she claims is not a wish, servitude, or reward: the rescue left her blood and scent on the narrator, and her adult sister is following that trail under the reasonable belief that a human captured or killed her. The woman owes the narrator one night at the threshold to meet her sister, show that she survived by the narrator's voluntary aid, and prevent a protective mistake. Resolve the promise when she changes, holds the boundary she chose, and sends her sister away without attack; the debt is paid precisely and creates no ownership or automatic friendship.
- Intended reader experience: Close, rain-cooled doorstep unease that becomes a tense exercise in listening. Let the matching wound and exact rescue details make the impossible persuasive early; the suspense should shift from “is she the wolf?” to “what does accepting her help require?” Keep the encounter private, bodily, and occasionally dry rather than romantic or portentous. Relief comes from a danger quietly leaving, with wariness and practical consequences intact.
- Generating force:
  - Mode: Plot-led held-threshold vigil. The protagonists barely travel; movement comes from a scent trail nearing the house, a wind that will not turn, and the same sill changing from defense against a stranger, to offered shelter, to the line the woman needs the narrator not to cross.
  - Focal attachment or contradiction: The narrator's useful courage is also a reflex to intervene whenever another body looks vulnerable. The woman values the aid that saved her, but repays it by retaining authority over her injured body and by taking responsibility for her own family's response—not by becoming available for whatever the narrator asks.
  - Plot-led wants and useful limit, if applicable: The narrator immediately wants the unknown woman off the step or inside where her injury can be treated, while also wanting proof, safety for the house, and no repeat of leaving a wounded creature alone. Their useful flaw is rescuer's momentum: once they decide someone needs help, they mistake action and hospitality for permission to direct that person. The woman wants to remain outside until her sister receives an unmistakable voluntary signal, keep both humans and kin unhurt, and leave without exposing her family or converting the debt into intimacy. Her useful limit is that the trap wound persists in both bodies; changing reopens it and costs enough heat and energy that she gets one reliable confrontation tonight, not repeated demonstrations.
- Counterforce or complication:
  - Source: Cold rain has thinned the blood-contact trail, while the wind blows from the woods toward the house and carries no confirming scent back. The sister is already searching. The woman can speak to the narrator in human shape but cannot give her sister the full bodily and scent evidence of survival without changing; indoors, the house would muddle the encounter and make the narrator appear to be containing her.
  - Credible inside logic, if a person: The unnamed adult sister is protective, not an antagonist. She has found steel, her sister's blood, and a human trail at the abandoned campsite, and has prior reason to distrust traps and the people who set them. She comes prepared to recover kin, not to punish humanity in general. Once she can inspect her sister's carried injury and freely chosen position, she withdraws without accepting the narrator as broadly safe or surrendering her own judgment.
  - Pressure or change created: Waiting exposes the injured woman to weather and makes the narrator's offer of shelter increasingly reasonable in ordinary terms but dangerous in this encounter's terms. When the sister finally appears and the changed woman falters on the bad leg, the narrator must decide whether helping means crossing the agreed line or trusting the woman to conduct a risk the narrator does not understand.
- Narrative design:
  - POV, tense, distance, and limitation: Close first-person past through the unnamed narrator, with gender left unstated. Their attention is good at injuries, tools, door hardware, and tasks, but poor at distinguishing another person's need from their own urge to be useful. The woman remains exterior: her clipped definitions, refusals, preparation, pain management, and choices disclose her purpose without access to her thoughts. The sister is legible only through approach, posture, sound, and response.
  - Time shape and compression: One continuous wet evening through the sister's departure before midnight, followed by a short pre-dawn aftereffect. Use no staged flashback. Reconstruct the camping rescue through the orange cord, damage to the narrator's multitool, the woman's precise corrections, and two or three brief sensory recollections triggered in the present.
  - Information and reveal strategy: Open on the debt claim through a chained door. Supply details only the trapped wolf could know, then show the corresponding ankle wound; do not sustain coy ambiguity after the evidence becomes sufficient. The woman defines the debt early as responsibility for the danger her survival trail brought, displacing the narrator's expectation of a boon. Confirm the bodily transformation in private before the sister reaches the yard, so the decisive question is whether the narrator respects the plan rather than whether the supernatural is real. Establish this as one local family's voluntary bodily ability and practice, with no universal werewolf taxonomy, curse, hierarchy, or hidden authority.
  - Governing movement: Chain the door; hear the claim; test an impossible detail; match cord and wound; misunderstand debt as a favor; learn the approaching risk; offer entry; hear refusal; arrange a porch vigil; wait through bad wind; exchange practical terms and silences; detect the sister before seeing her; prepare clothing and wound wrap; change; witness the agreed line become necessary; step across it on reflex; be stopped; retreat and hold still; let the sisters assess one another; watch the searcher leave unconvinced about humans but convinced about this rescue; keep the threshold until the trail clears; accept that payment is complete.
- Speculative surplus: A bodily change draws a brief film of condensation onto nearby glass regardless of air temperature. The family avoids changing beside windows because the fogged outline can betray a hiding place; tonight the storm door clouds from the outside and must be wiped before it drips into the frame. The effect does not carry messages, authenticate a debt, reveal emotion, aid tracking, or protect either body.
- Structural distinction: A stationary approach story organized by a held domestic boundary. Distance collapses through wind, scent, and small sounds while the central pair remain at one porch, rather than through a chase, room-by-room progress, repeated cycles, public proof, a crowd's conversion, or an institutional response. The outward action lasts minutes; most of the pressure comes from deciding what not to do.
- Decisive turn, deepening, or recognition: The woman has changed on a folded weather sheet, leaving her clothes and bandage within the porch rail; the huge wolf is unmistakably the one from the trap. When her sister appears at the wet edge of the yard, the changed woman advances on three sound legs, deliberately placing herself between the house and her kin. She slips, and the narrator steps over the sill with a blanket despite the explicit instruction to remain inside. The sister's posture hardens. The injured wolf turns and bares her teeth at the narrator—not as betrayal, but as the clearest boundary she has left. The narrator backs behind the sill and makes themself still. Given that space, the sisters complete a tense scent-and-posture exchange; the searcher inspects the trap wound, sees her sister return voluntarily to guard the house, and leaves without approaching the narrator.
- Aftereffect or live uncertainty: The specific mistaken pursuit ends and the woman has met her obligation, but the illegal trap line remains unresolved, her leg will heal slowly, and neither sister now trusts humans as a category. The narrator has no durable proof beyond damaged gear, a fog-marked door, and what they witnessed. No names, future favor, pack protection, romance, or promise of return is exchanged. The woman remains in wolf shape to conserve strength until morning, accepts water but chooses her own distance, and departs before the neighboring houses wake.
- Opening / ending relation: Open with the narrator holding the chained door nearly shut while trying to decide whether the stranger is dangerous or merely hurt. End after the wolf has gone, with the chain still hanging unused and the narrator sealing the blood-scented camping jacket in an ordinary storage bin because that is the practical task the woman told them remains. The opening makes the threshold a defense the narrator controls; the ending leaves it as a boundary whose meaning had to be negotiated.

## Beats

1. At rainy dusk, the narrator opens the chained door to a soaked, limping woman who says, “You opened iron for me. I owe you the closing of it.” She identifies the bent tooth on the narrator's multitool and repeats a phrase spoken at the trap.
2. The narrator demands proof and then tries to bring her inside. She shows the matching right-ankle wound and orange cord, refuses the doorway, and corrects the narrator's assumption that the debt is a favor they may spend.
3. She explains the mixed blood-and-contact trail and the sister following it. The campsite evidence resembles capture; the woman's debt is to correct the danger her family may bring. She gives three firm conditions: she stays outside, the narrator stays behind the sill when the sister arrives, and no one calls witnesses to see her change.
4. They prepare without turning the scene into a procedural manual: water and plain food within reach, a folded weather sheet for clothing, the porch light off, the bloodied jacket left where its scent already lies. The wind remains wrong. Conversation in the waiting gaps establishes that the ability, debt practice, and family suspicion are local, while the narrator repeatedly offers solutions the woman has already declined.
5. The woman hears the sister first. She secures her clothes, changes behind the porch rail, and the storm-door glass films with impossible condensation. The narrator recognizes the same size, eyes, injured leg, and deliberate patience as at the trap.
6. A second huge wolf appears at the yard's wooded edge. The injured wolf moves out to meet her and slips. The narrator crosses the sill with a blanket; the newcomer prepares to rush, and the injured wolf turns her teeth on the narrator until they retreat and hold still.
7. With the agreed boundary restored, the sisters inspect, circle, and answer one another. The newcomer tests the porch scent but does not cross it. The injured wolf returns voluntarily to the house-facing position; the sister accepts that evidence and leaves without reconciliation or attack.
8. The narrator keeps watch from inside while the injured wolf drinks, rests, and waits for the wind to carry the corrected trail away. Before dawn she departs in wolf shape. The narrator seals the camping jacket as instructed, unable to turn the completed debt into either proof or a second claim on her.

## Continuity

- Canon used: The story occupies an unnamed modern-like, low-magic locality within the one persistent world. The woman and her sister are living people who voluntarily perform their own bodily changes; transformation does not replace, copy, or diminish either identity. Their culture can be incomplete in the narrator's understanding, and no universal authority must regulate the encounter.
- Boundaries and unknowns: Treat this transformation, its costs, condensation effect, scent acuity, and family debt practice as new and strictly local. Establish no connection to Crown-era dragon guises, Northbridge transformations, Bellweather beast affinity, an existing faction, a universal werewolf species, or a named forest. Leave the family's origin, population, wider customs, the trapper's identity, the remaining traps, and the locality unresolved. Violence and injury stay non-graphic.
