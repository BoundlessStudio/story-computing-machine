# Opus review: By Wing and Welcome

Independent audit for a later editing pass. It is not a `review.md` verdict,
does not change canon state, and does not authorize edits by itself.

## Verdict

- Dialogue: REVISE — three Blocking Ground failures (the opening digit misread, the "Q beside P" complaint, and the parents citing an injury they could not have known about), plus sideways answers and clause-trading at the offer and at Pippa's decision.
- Prose: REVISE — concrete and well paced, but the consent theme is restated in narration, in the written terms, in a repeated "only after he agreed" sentence template, and in a closing list of who asked before doing what.
- Continuity and prompt: REVISE — the prompt is fully delivered (about 3,330 words of prose, in band). All findings are Minor: staging and prop slips, blackout logistics, and no explanation of why the parents arrive tonight.
- Edit scope: targeted-dialogue
- Existing review.md said: Verdict PASS; "Dialogue: PASS". I disagree: it checked the board spelling and the mechanics, but not whether the jokes, corrections and replies survive a plain paraphrase.

The dialogue engine is strong. A yes/no board, STOP obeyed at once, a father who over-defines and slowly learns to say "yes", and a narrator who asks the bat rather than the adults: the bat-form exchanges (L115–L235) are the best dialogue in the story. The failures fall into two groups. The first is small jokes and corrections written for rhythm that do not describe anything real: the 9.2/12.9 "decimal", "Q beside P", "See? One thing.", the room they are not in. The second is the human-voiced second half, where Teyo files a report, Corven answers Pippa's question with the adult's checklist, Olya delivers the moral, and Pippa's decision happens as a trade of terms with her parent. The edit pass should make every misreading and complaint literally true and give Teyo a sulky twelve-year-old's partial account. It should let "Why me?" land on one concrete thing Olya saw Pippa do, and let Pippa say what she wants (or is losing) before the terms. Keep the consent architecture and cut its restatements.

## Dialogue findings

### D1 · Blocking · Opening weight misread cannot be reconstructed

- Where: L11–L21, opening weigh-in
- Text:
> she read his weight aloud while I held him in my gloved hands.
> "Nine point two grams."
> "Twelve point nine. I knew that. The six looks like a decimal when the light catches it."
> "Then the nine looks like a six."
- Problem: Ground/referent and Punchline Patch. 9.2 → 12.9 is not a decimal error; the digits change and reorder. Neither reading contains a six, so "The six looks like a decimal", "It was a nine" and "Then the nine looks like a six" point at digits the reader never sees. The exchange exists to land Pippa's last word, and it is the first dialogue in the story, so it teaches the reader that the talk won't add up. Staging: if the narrator is holding the bat in gloved hands, what is the scale weighing?
- Fix: One misreading the display could plausibly produce, one check, then Pippa writes the correct number. Keep 12.9 g (reused at L109). Drop the closing quip about nines and sixes. Make it clear the bat is on the scale, for example in a weigh cup or cupped over the pan. The closing line (L395, "two sevens") mirrors this opening, so the bookend only works if the opening is literally true. Illustrative only: "Twelve point six—no, nine. The bottom bar's dim."

### D2 · Blocking · The parents cite an injury they could not have known about

- Where: L53 and L73, doorstep
- Text:
> "We believe the injured person in your care is our son, Teyo.
> He described a healed notch along the left ear and an injury to the right forearm, neither of which they could see from the step.
> I followed them too far, hit a wire over your lane, and came down on the step.
- Problem: Ground/knowledge. Teyo was hurt after he left home, hitting a wire (L283), and he never answered his parents' calls (L293). They had no access to "an injury to the right forearm", or strictly to "injured" at all. The narrator counts it as proof precisely because it could not be seen, so the evidence the scene rests on is the one thing the parents cannot know. The only explanation on the page is Pippa's "they could've watched us" (L79), which would make them suspects, and no one comes back to it.
- Fix: Take the forearm out of Corven's list. Keep the old ear notch and the hem-biting habit, which they know from home and which Pippa's log confirms. Make "injured" an inference, or drop it at L53. If the narrator wants more proof, the board scene already supplies it. Illustrative only: "He would have come home if he could fly."

### D3 · Blocking · The "Q beside P" identity beat names no mistake

- Where: L263–L277, first human exchange after the change
- Text:
> "Pippa said you could read," I said.
> "I put the letters closer on that side."
> "You put Q beside P."
> Pippa turned to me. "That was one time."
> This was the same particular patient who shoved cards aside when they inconvenienced him.
- Problem: Ground/referent and knowledge. (a) The narrator built the board with Pippa and watched him read (L29), so attributing that to Pippa is wrong; the line exists only to set up "I can." (b) Q always sits beside P in an alphabet, so the complaint identifies no error, and "That was one time" answers as if it were a one-off slip. (c) "Closer on that side" and "shoved cards aside" imply movable letter cards, but L29/L141 establish a fixed "alphabet strip". (d) L277 then says this bicker convinced the narrator more than the ears or the coat did, so the identity beat rests on a joke the reader cannot decode.
- Fix: Give the narrator an opener they would actually say, or cut it and let Teyo complain about the spelling unprompted. Make the complaint a real one-time error. Choose strip or cards and make L29, L141, L271 and L277 agree. Then "the corrected board" at L393 can point to this fix. Illustrative only: "You swapped the O and the Q."

### D4 · Major · The transformation rules arrive as an interview

- Where: L89–L111, vestibule just after the power cut
- Text:
> Corven began, "Our kin can execute a voluntary bodily transition no more than once in the interval commencing at sunset and terminating at the subsequent sunset
> "No name, touch, order, threat, appeal, or emotional extremity can initiate it in another person."
> "Plainly," Pippa said.
> You will tell me only what affects his immediate safety."
- Problem: Exposition/Explanatory chorus plus Punchline Patch. The narrator's line is about the room cooling and the visitors waiting; nobody has mentioned changing, yet Corven answers with a statute. The narrator then runs an interview ("How long?", "Can you trigger it?", "Can he stop once he starts?") and never reacts to learning that the bat will become a boy. The second clause-list mainly sets up "Plainly". "You will tell me only what affects his immediate safety" arrives after they have already told everything. This is the lore recital the outline's own anti-default warns against.
- Fix: Let Corven's first words answer what the narrator just said. He hears "cool faster" and, knowing the change spends heat, blurts the one fact that matters. Keep one legalism, then "Plainly" and "No. We can't make him." (that beat works). Cut the other catalogue. Give the narrator one plain human reaction before going procedural. Move or cut the "only what affects his immediate safety" demand. The facts listed in the guardrails must all still reach the reader.

### D5 · Major · Teyo's account plays as a deposition and loops

- Where: L281–L311, flight room, after the arm exam
- Text:
> "Olya's my mother. Corven's my father. I left through my bedroom window. They said I wasn't cleared to fly alone after dark.
> "It was the first dry one.
> "That's not a good reason."
> "You wanted release?"
> "I wanted to know if I could fly." He glanced at Corven. "Before the lecture."
> "Yes. I want you to check the arm first, and I want food. Then home. Medical care tomorrow.
> He let me examine the arm.
- Problem: Uniform fluency under pressure, plus Ground slips. He has just changed, is shivering hard enough to click his teeth on the cup, and is furiously embarrassed. Even so, he gives a complete chronological statement, introduces his parents by first name to the people who just verified them, and uses procedure words ("cleared"). "Why our step?" gets "the first dry one", which contradicts the fall he just described ("came down on the step"). L295–L305 loops: Pippa rules "That's not a good reason", he concedes at once, and an untagged "You wanted release?" (Pippa's or the narrator's?) makes him repeat L297 almost word for word. At L311 he asks the narrator to "check the arm first", although L279 already did, and then sets out an itinerary.
- Fix: Let him answer in pieces, embarrassed, leaving things out. "Mum", "Dad" or a look will do instead of an introduction. Make the step a place he crawled to, or cut the question. Cut the L303–L305 repeat or fold "Before the lecture" into L297. Tag whoever asks about release. Replace "check the arm first" with something not yet done (the sling, food). Keep "They can be angry after I eat." and Corven's "There will be—" / "Yes. Later."

### D6 · Major · "What do you get?" is answered sideways, then with the moral

- Where: L333–L337, the offer in the flight room
- Text:
> "What do you get?" Pippa asked.
> "No custody," he said. "No access to you or your home. No obedience, secrecy, service, or claim.
> Olya said, "And this is our gratitude to carry. Not yours. Not Teyo's."
- Problem: Ground/uptake and Philosopher. Pippa asks what they gain. Corven lists what they won't claim, which answers the question the narrator would ask, not hers. Olya then states the story's moral in "Not X. Not Y." form, the same point Pippa made in the hall (L319) and that the narration repeats at L373. At the key definitional moment the child's question gets the adults' policy and a thesis.
- Fix: Answer her words: something they actually get, specific to this family (a household that knows what Teyo is; somewhere he can be ordinary; a debt they can't otherwise put down). Keep the no-custody terms, but let them live in Corven's offer (L331) or the written terms (L373), not in the answer to "What do you get?". Cut Olya's thesis line or turn it into a concrete want. Illustrative only: "We'd like to know you. That's the whole of it."

### D7 · Major · "Why me?" never lands

- Where: L343–L349, the offer, the decisive question
- Text:
> "Because you kept noticing what he did. Because when we were foolish at the door, you
> "I told you about the board," Pippa said. "That doesn't mean you know me."
> "It is what I know tonight. The pledge can stay unmade."
- Problem: Ground/referent at the decisive exchange. Olya reaches for something Pippa did "at the door", but there Pippa only whispered, checked the log, and suggested the couple might have been watching. Pippa's cut-in refers to the board, which happened in the flight room (L137). The reader can't tell what Olya valued, so the prompt's implied question, why this child, gets no answer. Olya's recovery is a composed closing line from someone who has just lost two sentences in a row.
- Fix: Have Olya name one concrete thing she saw Pippa do, such as stopping her "sad noise" (L161–L171) or asking before the box went over him (L287). Let Pippa's interruption answer that thing. Keep "That doesn't mean you know me." Make Olya's reply shorter and less finished. Illustrative only: "Because you told me to stop that noise, and I did."

### D8 · Major · Pippa's decision is traded as terms

- Where: L319–L325 (hall) and L351–L371 (kitchen)
- Text:
> "Now they can ask the godparent thing," she said. "But not because I fed him. He would've got fed anyway."
> "And I talk first if it's for me."
> "You ask first. We decide together."
> "And no surprise visits. They knocked like a horror film."
> "No unsupervised visits or outings."
> "Not even flying ones," she said, and that decided it.
- Problem: Negotiation routine and correction ping-pong at the story's decisive choice. Pippa proposes a term, and the parent rewords or upgrades it ("talk first" → "ask first"; "no surprise visits" → "No unsupervised visits or outings"). Then a quip "decided it", and the reader cannot tell what it decided or why. No one says whether Pippa wants godparents, what she wants from Teyo's family, or how she feels about losing the patient she nursed for twelve days. The outline's own pressure ("her impending goodbye as real") never reaches speech. "Not because I fed him" is an eleven-year-old pre-empting a gratitude debt, the "miniature-therapist child" the outline's anti-default rules out. "Knocked" is wrong: they rang the doorbell (L39).
- Fix: Keep the summarized questions at L351, which compress well, and keep "That's annoying." / "It is.". Replace one term exchange with Pippa stating a want or a loss in her own words, and let the parent answer as a parent, not with a ruling. Make "that decided it" point to something, or cut it. Change "knocked" to "rang" or a similar verb. Cut the hall "not because I fed him" line or make it a question she is unsure about. Illustrative only: "I thought I'd get to watch him go out the hatch."

## Prose findings

### P1 · Major · The consent theme is explained again in narration and at the close

- Where: L221, L277, L373, L387; "only after" template at L201, L207, L261, L279, L317
- Text:
> Pippa noticing every time an adult let fear turn into permission they had never received.

- Problem: The style guide's "once a point is clear, do not routinely explain it again in narration, dialogue, policy, and a closing symbol" (Story craft defaults) describes this story's pattern exactly. The point about permission and gratitude is shown well in the board scenes, then stated in narration (L221), in dialogue (L319, L337), in policy (L373), and again as a closing inventory (L387). L277 also tells the reader what the Q/P exchange proved. The narration repeats the template "X did Y only after/when Z agreed" (L201, L207, L261, L279, L317), which turns permission into a narrative tic.
- Fix: Cut the thesis clause at L221; the three concrete items before it already make the point. Cut or shorten L277 once D3 makes the bicker coherent. At L373, keep the written terms and drop the "did not owe" sentence if D6 lands. At L387, keep Teyo's concrete choices (clothes, sling, walking if Corven carries the bottles) and drop the asked-before list. Vary or cut at least half of the "only after" sentences.
