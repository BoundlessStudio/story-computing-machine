# The Unread

The request explicitly promises a genuinely perfect fortune-teller of twenty years, an afternoon reading for a young stranger that blanks her entire enchanted deck, and terrified whispers showing that Fate cannot look upon this person without a destiny. I infer a pleasure of supernatural dread and discovery: a practiced expert meets something that frightens the source of her authority. The request leaves the stranger's nature, relationship to the teller, scale, and ending open. My chosen payoff is a dangerous encounter with the kingdom's living Fate in which the blankness changes events, rather than merely teaching the teller to accept uncertainty.

Provisional direction: close third through Meret. The supplied woman's purple headscarf and shawl, brown hair, white blouse, gold jewelry, and violet-and-gold cards inform her appearance. Her gift has genuinely helped people. The living being called Fate supplies its sight through her deck; its many card-voices are one frightened intelligence. It predicts events rather than enforcing them. Its embodied form is vulnerable, but perfect foreknowledge has always let it evade an attack before it begins. This is the kingdom's particular source of foresight, without a claim to govern every era or every person's universe.

- Open with an exact reading enabling a rescue: Meret tells a desperate parent where their missing child will be found alive, and the reunion begins before the parent leaves. Establish the familiar royal full-deck audience that she must conduct tonight. Meret offers embodiment; Fate must voluntarily accept. Once accepted, her trained, living attention holds its body above the full spread until she dismisses it or her concentration breaks. The inert deck focuses that exchange. Years ago she also read her own death tonight beneath the Hall's falling royal standard. All her completed predictions have come true. This one has not happened yet.
- A young adult stranger, Orrel, asks for a reading that afternoon. Every card turns white, including its back. The voices beg Meret to close the deck; one says Fate is looking everywhere except at the chair. Orrel wants to reach tonight's audience and make the source admit, before the king, that it cannot see everyone. They grew up on the border where the king has now promised a war: Meret herself delivered its exact victory and casualty count, and the crown has treated a real forecast as permission. Orrel's immediate purpose is to break that assurance, rather than assassinate Fate or claim the throne.
- Meret tests the blankness. When she changes the subject to an uninvolved person across the square, images return despite Orrel's physical presence. When Orrel deliberately intervenes in that person's danger, its outcome blanks and changes. Blankness follows the subject and consequential intervention, rather than radiating arbitrarily from proximity. Meret's own future becomes unreadable as she chooses to help them. She cannot obtain a fresh reliable death-reading; the old one is now a remembered sequence she might enter, survive, or misunderstand. Her old fulfilled readings remain true.
- Fate offers Meret something valuable through the cards: leave Orrel, and she can still guide the rescuers who will need her tomorrow. Its forecasts have saved real lives, including the child just found. Meret cannot dismiss that shared loss as the price of a corrupt profession. She nevertheless brings Orrel to the Hall because the crown is about to make more people die on the strength of her testimony. Her exact knowledge of the ceremonial court gets them inward; each necessary intervention by Orrel destroys another useful part of that map. They arrive asking for a public answer, not planning a killing.
- Fate at first refuses the offered audience, speaks through the surviving blank card-mouths, and demands Orrel's removal. Meret makes the blankness visible to the king. A guard's attempted arrest fails when Orrel's intervention overturns the exact maneuver Meret was expecting. Fate can no longer aim the court at a guaranteed outcome. Continued refusal would publicly concede that the source cannot promise its king a sure victory. It knowingly accepts embodiment to end the inquiry by force, choosing a controlled Hall with armed guards and one human reader whose death would release its body. It is frightened and intelligent, not tricked into appearing or magically compelled. Its physical attack turns the audience into self-defense.
- Meret holds the full deal open while Fate tries to break her attention and then kill her. Once attacked, she deliberately sets a trap. With new foresight gone, Fate attempts to recreate the one outcome it already knows: Meret dead beneath the standard, her hold ended. Meret recognizes that attempt and steps into her remembered place as bait, without a guarantee that the old sequence will recur or that she will live. Fate takes the court sword and physically cuts the standard's supporting cord; the falling pole is the consequence of that chosen act, not an enforcing prophecy. Orrel must run into the fall, seize the spear-ended pole, and turn the weapon against the body Meret is still holding above the spread. The source knows its old forecast and chooses to trust its lethal sequence, but cannot see Orrel's new action within it. That attack kills a being which would have foreseen and evaded an ordinary assassin. The cards lose their sight because the living source dies, not because looking at Orrel automatically destroys a god.
- The cost is common as well as personal: Meret survives, but the rescuers of tomorrow will have no exact guidance. The coming war has neither vanished nor become impossible. Orrel's intervention has changed its predicted sequence, and killing the source has ended access to reliable foresight; these are different consequences. The king must now order an uncertain war in his own name. End with his consequential refusal to give that order tonight, after Meret admits before the court what the new blank cards can no longer promise. Keep the dangerous wonder: Orrel remains a person with no known destiny or settled origin, and the kingdom still has to live with what two people have done.
