# The Light at the Empty Stair — provisional outline

## Promised experience

The explicit pleasure is the impossible photographic access in “Every photograph … reveals the same location as it appeared two thousand years ago,” discovered by an “adventurous photographer” and her “brilliant, purple-haired historian friend.” I read those words, and the bright coastal excavation in the supplied image, as an invitation to vivid historical wonder and a capable, affectionate friendship in action. “At first” invites a consequential development beyond a succession of lovely views. The request specifies no danger, scale, genre beyond its premise, emotional arc, or ending; the choices below are mine.

## Provisional direction

Close third through **Brinna**, the photographer; **Tamsa** is her historian friend. Both are adult field researchers. Their familiarity is lively rather than troubled: Brinna finds the angle and commits to the climb; Tamsa reads what the angle means and is adventurous in her own right. The setting is the fictional sunlit coastal ruin of **Halcyra**, in an unspecified modern-looking, magic-active era. Working title: **The Light at the Empty Stair**.

The antique bellows camera produces quick contact prints. Each exposure shows its actual viewpoint exactly two thousand years earlier, with past time moving at the same rate as present time. It cannot be rewound, entered, or used to move objects. An unexpected second property emerges through evidence: opening its shutter makes a little ring of light visible at the corresponding past viewpoint. The living photographer supplies the effect through her hand on the camera; its origin remains unknown. These are chosen story rules, not prompt requirements. The story will establish reciprocal light without changing an already witnessed event or inventing a replacement timeline.

- Under the excavation awning, Brinna photographs a broken colonnade. The print contains painted columns, striped shade cloth, and a baker balancing a tray where a lizard sits now. She tests several angles while Tamsa dates a festival notice and an unfamiliar calendar: two thousand years exactly. Their delight becomes a photographic expedition through the living city: a crowded theatre, a quarrel over a melon, a woman carrying enormous yellow paper birds for the evening procession. Tamsa's corrections become discoveries rather than a lecture.
- A later print shows embers blowing into that procession. The city is entering the great fire remembered only through its surviving ruins. A young woman in yellow, **Ivrin**, turns toward the lens's impossible light. Repeated exposures show her deliberately testing its position. Tamsa recognizes her answering gestures as the city's lamp-signals, documented on a fragment they had been recording that morning. Their first successful exchange establishes that someone in the past is seeing them back.
- Ivrin has gathered children and other people cut off from the lower streets. Brinna and Tamsa can see a way out in the exposed modern ruins: a builders' stair opening behind a blind wall of the theatre. They cannot simply signal from the safe excavation; intact ancient roofs block the sightline. To show Ivrin the hidden entrance, Brinna must take the camera across a narrow, broken upper terrace, while Tamsa finds and marks the route from the excavated plan and deciphers Ivrin's pictured replies. They choose the attempt together.
- At the exposed landing, photography governs the rescue. Brinna composes successive frames to locate Ivrin through moving smoke; Tamsa gives short lamp-code instructions for Brinna to blink with the shutter. A first instruction fails because the ancient entrance still has its plaster facing. A close exposure lets Tamsa read the builder's mark and specify where Ivrin must strike. Ivrin opens it, leads her group down, and makes her own decisions about whom to gather. The friends' combined work supplies knowledge and light, not control over the ancient people.
- The final rescue photograph shows the group emerging above the beach, their yellow paper birds visible through the smoke. In the present, the friends find Ivrin's brief carved acknowledgment beside the stair: a worn mark previously catalogued as decoration, always present rather than newly appearing. The ending returns to photography: Brinna takes a last, carefully framed picture from the ancient group's exit, and Tamsa holds the camera steady with her. The print contains Ivrin and the people she brought out, looking toward their rescuers' light. Historical wonder has become a particular human encounter, earned by the two friends acting together.
