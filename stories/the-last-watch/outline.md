# Provisional direction

A close first-person story told by Olla, now seventy-two. Fifty years ago her young husband Talven followed the promised champion through a newly opened rift. Olla helped catch its widening edges and stayed with the watch. She has imagined its eventual closure as the day Talven comes home and their interrupted marriage resumes. Their daughter has grown old enough to have grandchildren; Olla has kept postponing a permanent move into her daughter's house. The watch was necessary and has saved real people. It has also become Olla's way of refusing a loss.

What comes back is Talven, scarcely older than the man who left, frightened and carrying a hungry infant named Biri. Barely two years have passed for him. Biri belongs to someone he could not bring through; her particular appetite, fist and noise interrupt both the reunion Olla imagined and the watch's ceremony for the promised arrival. Talven finds an old woman where he expects his wife. Neither should respond with instant wisdom or a clean welcome.

The champion helped them reach this exit and stayed behind. Talven cannot say whether he survived. Avoid turning him into a villain: he may still deserve rescue. Talven has brought what the watch could not supply from this side, a living connection to the far edge, and is physically able to complete the closure. The seal needs the keepers to release their own bindings while he joins the edges. Those edges have been genuinely under pressure; releasing before someone returns with the other connection would have let the tear spread. Returning was a long, dangerous journey through disrupted space, not something a keeper could have done by stepping out and back.

Olla initially wants to keep the rift open for the champion, but her motives include keeping Talven attached to the one task she knows how to share with him. Talven wants to close it now, save Biri and rest. He cannot promise when another person could return or what is left behind. Olla must choose without learning the champion's fate or recovering the husband who would have aged alongside her. The other keepers have lives and wishes of their own; they are neither her voting bloc nor a uniformly enlightened chorus.

Possible ending: the watch lets go and Talven completes the seal. Olla leaves with him and Biri for the daughter's house, afraid of the reunion rather than restored to youth. Their first ordinary contact is awkward and exact: perhaps he offers an arm and discovers which pace she can manage, or she lets him take a hand whose familiar work-shaped calluses now meet seventy-year-old skin. No speech explains release. The former duty mattered; the remaining lives will require acquaintance.
