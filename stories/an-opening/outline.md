# An Opening

Provisional direction: a comic, close-third story through the goblin paladin, held mostly in the adventurers' guild on the morning these four register their first party.

The goblin has rehearsed the entrance and dressed his battered armor for it. He wants to carry an intact quartet out of the guild with a real commission, preferably the sort of commission people sing about. The orc wizard, elven barbarian, and Goliath rogue have traveled together with him long enough to have affectionate irritations and small habits of looking after one another. They have trusted him to choose their first job, without agreeing that he can settle its tactics. The room's surprise is immediate and physical: the small visitor is carrying the holy shield, the orc is protecting a book from the elf's axe, and the immense rogue has somehow arrived where nobody was watching. The registrar is startled rather than malicious.

Registration leads to choosing a commission: recover a stolen silver coronet from a disused hill fortress occupied by thieves. The supplied sketch reports the gate secured from inside and a narrow porter's hatch beside it; the coronet is believed to be in the thieves' locked pay chest. The goblin sees the hatch as his way to lead a real adventure, and eagerly accepts a contract that commits their modest purse to the expedition. The wizard likes the possibility of old spellwork; the barbarian likes the promised fight and starts preparing to leave. The rogue wants the chest's lock, actual work for his hands instead of watching smaller people have the adventure. He knows a small body is not an entry plan; being overlooked makes him slow to explain the risks. Their appetites and omissions collide, rather than producing four separate demonstrations. The goblin can fit alone, but his shield cannot. He is tempted to make the whole recovery a solitary feat.

The turn keeps this job. The rogue interrupts the rush to leave and takes responsibility for the entry plan; the goblin has to accept being directed. They propose reaching the gate's securing bar through the hatch, with a line the others can haul from outside. The rogue is helping the goblin open access for all four, so he can reach and work on the pay chest himself. A credible small rehearsal establishes what the goblin will have to do without pretending their sketch guarantees success. The rogue has to disclose and teach a piece of his craft; the wizard has to admit where exciting spellwork cannot replace rope; the elf has to spend their last equipment money on preparation rather than a quick start. The paladin leaves his showpiece armor and shield as collateral for the extra gear. He will still enter first, without the silhouette he imagined, depending on his companions rather than proving he can do without them. Objects and ownership must remain clear: pledged armor stays in the guild, loaned equipment goes with them, and his sword remains his.

Possible ending: the rogue helps the goblin fasten an improvised tool belt, and they leave with the shared map and their risk still ahead. Their departure is physically different from their carefully rehearsed arrival. No one awards the paladin a leadership lesson or invites him to lead before his work has produced a result. A small physical exchange should carry the affection. Avoid a guild-wide conversion, a speech about appearances, four consecutive competency demonstrations or flaw displays, and a perfectly settled team. The adventure should feel newly possible, with their disagreements coming along.
