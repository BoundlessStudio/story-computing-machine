# An Opening

Provisional direction: a comic, close-third story through the goblin paladin, held mostly in the adventurers' guild on the morning these four register their first party.

The goblin has rehearsed the entrance and dressed his battered armor for it. He wants to carry an intact quartet out of the guild with a real commission, preferably the sort of commission people sing about. The orc wizard, elven barbarian, and Goliath rogue have traveled together with him long enough to have affectionate irritations and small habits of looking after one another. They have trusted him to choose their first job. The room's surprise is immediate and physical: the wrong person is carrying the holy shield, the orc is protecting a book from the elf's axe, and the immense rogue has somehow arrived where nobody was watching. The registrar is startled rather than malicious.

Registration leads to choosing a commission: recover a stolen silver coronet from an abandoned hill fortress. The goblin has imagined the party entering through its famous broken gate, with him at the front. The wizard likes the possibility of old spellwork; the barbarian likes the promised fight and badly wants their adventure to begin. The rogue studies the actual contract sketch and quietly points out that the broken gate is blocked. The surviving crawlway might admit the goblin, but not the rogue. The paladin's eager public acceptance has got ahead of their plan. A nearby established party offers to take the wizard along on another job; splitting would let each of them find easier work and spare the goblin an embarrassing retraction.

The pressure is between preserving his splendid entrance and preserving the actual people who came through the door behind him. He withdraws his acceptance while the others are still arguing about how to make it work, then asks what they would need to take this job together. The exchange should expose the others' wants and mistakes as well as his: the wizard's exciting answer may be too dangerous, the elf's impatience may spoil a useful question, and the rogue has to say plainly what he wants rather than leave everyone to guess. They may discover another entrance worth scouting, or choose a less splendid first commission; the prose can decide which choice has real cost and energy.

Possible ending: they leave with a first job they have actually chosen together. The goblin has given up the imposing procession but keeps the brave first step. Their departure is untidy, affectionate, and visibly theirs; one of the others asks him to lead, now meaning a direction they have helped choose. Avoid a guild-wide conversion, a speech about appearances, four consecutive competency demonstrations, or a perfectly settled team. The adventure should feel newly possible, with their disagreements coming along.
