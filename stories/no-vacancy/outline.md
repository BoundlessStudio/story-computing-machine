# No Vacancy

Provisional direction: a close first-person story told by thirteen-year-old Fenna, with the comic hurt of someone who has prepared an excellent perch for a creature that will not come. Everyone else in her class has bonded. The offered familiars investigate Fenna, then withdraw; she tries to hide the empty perch and becomes increasingly desperate for a particular kite-fox.

Her teacher, Mr. Hasp, sits her down after noticing that the rejected creatures react to her as occupied rather than frightening. A practical test and questions about home uncover an early bond with Crumb, her elderly, quite ordinary brindled dog. Familiar animals need not look extraordinary: the school normally trains young prospective partners for its curriculum, while this bond grew before Fenna knew magic was involved. Recognition should make earlier, small incidents legible, without revealing a superior hidden animal or secret power.

Hasp can help dissolve the bond, provided Crumb also lets it go. Fenna can then seek the familiar and flying lessons she genuinely wants. Keeping Crumb will mean a different, slower course: he can lend senses and cooperate in small workings, but his stiff body cannot undertake the class's aerial exercises. Neither option makes her a monster. The teacher should have practical limits, not deliver the story's moral.

At home Fenna tries to sound out a release, finds that Crumb has inconvenient desires and is tired of what she wants him to perform, and confronts her own embarrassment about bringing him to school. A childhood memory of the lost girl and the dog that found her becomes active in the present decision. The likely ending is her return to class with Crumb and a revised arrangement for him, risking being seen with the familiar she already has. He does not earn her choice through a rescue or new power, and she does not stop wanting to fly. The final contact should carry the choice rather than explain it.

Risks to test: a familiar-bond technicality replacing the promised rejection; old-pet sentiment making ambition look shallow; the dog becoming only a loyalty emblem; an educational compromise that feels like a tidy reward for goodness.
