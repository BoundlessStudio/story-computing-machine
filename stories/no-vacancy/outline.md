# No Vacancy

Provisional direction: a close first-person story told by thirteen-year-old Fenna, with the comic hurt of someone who has prepared an excellent perch for a creature that will not come. Everyone else in her class has bonded. The offered familiars investigate Fenna, then withdraw. She privately tries a stronger invitation with the kite-fox she wants, believing a quieter meeting will help; its response exposes something her teacher can recognize. Small early incidents of borrowed scent, which Fenna considers ordinary attentiveness, become legible later.

Her teacher, Mr. Hasp, sits her down after noticing that the rejected creatures react to her as occupied rather than frightening. A practical test and questions about home uncover an early bond with Crumb, her elderly, quite ordinary brindled dog. Familiar animals need not look extraordinary: the school normally trains young prospective partners for its curriculum, while this bond grew before Fenna knew magic was involved. Recognition should make earlier, small incidents legible, without revealing a superior hidden animal or secret power.

Hasp can help dissolve the bond, provided Crumb also lets it go. Crumb's life, health, ordinary affection, and place in the household will remain intact. Fenna can then seek the familiar and flying lessons she genuinely wants. Keeping Crumb costs her place in this term's introductory flight block and her classmates' first ascent: he can lend senses and cooperate in small workings, but his stiff body cannot undertake aerial exercises. Later release and a new partner could let her join a later cohort; this is a choice for the present, not a lifetime vow or a requirement that Crumb die before she can fly. The teacher has practical limits rather than the story's moral.

On a walk Fenna deliberately listens through the existing bond. She must follow Crumb's wish for a shorter route and a prolonged investigation of an apparently boring patch. His smell-world is crowded, funny, and particular, including something appalling he would like to eat. Losing this way of sharing is a real loss to her, while ordinary walks remain available. Crumb can refuse a request within the bond; a supervised trial of loosening should let him yield without nobility and go attend to ordinary dog business while Fenna experiences the difference before final release. No childhood rescue or heroic renunciation settles the decision through debt.

At the school appointment after the walk, with the aerial group's first-ascent preparations nearby, Fenna resumes the shared attention after its brief quieting and tells Hasp to put her in the ground class. That is the visible decisive act. Her return with Crumb requires arranging his transport and rest rather than receiving a perfect accommodation. She risks being seen with the companion she finds embarrassing and still watches the aerial group longingly. He neither performs a rescue nor gains a power to reward her. His immediate interest finally calls her into a particular action; the contact carries the choice without explaining it.

Risks to test: a familiar-bond technicality replacing the promised rejection; old-pet sentiment making ambition look shallow; the dog becoming only a loyalty emblem; release remaining too cheap; a ritual or educational compromise doing the story's emotional work.
