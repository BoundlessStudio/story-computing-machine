## The request before the outline · 2026-11-23

**Codex promise keeper.** An imminent wedding, a refusal to marry a man, more than seven hundred guests outside, a demand to send them home, and a counteroffer of hard liquor and a pep talk are explicit. “I-I-I can’t” makes the panic immediate, “over seven hundred” gives a private decision public consequences, and “or, I could” is funny against that mismatch. The keeper expected comic urgency with room for genuine fear and a marriage decision that affects the waiting guests. The helper’s identity, motive for the refusal, use of the drink, and outcome are open.

**Claude outline reader.** The four lines are the scene’s spine and the helper’s “deadpan, slightly reckless counteroffer” is its distinctive pleasure. Claude anticipated a close, warm backstage scene with the helper and person getting married, and wanted to hear enough of the pep talk to learn what “can't” means. Ordinary nerves and a reason to cancel both fit. The count could be social scale or comic exaggeration, and the groom’s presence, the helper’s relationship, and the outcome are inventions available to the writer.
