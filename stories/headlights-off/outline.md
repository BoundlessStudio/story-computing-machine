# Headlights Off

## Promised experience

Someone declares they can't go through with a marriage as more than seven hundred guests wait. “Tell them to go home!” reaches for an instant solution at an absurd scale. The offer of liquor and a pep talk, made as if it were more practical, has room for warmth as well as bite. I expect the two speakers' exchange to change how the panicked person faces the choice, with the crowd exerting pressure and living through the result. Their relationship, the reason for the refusal, and whether the wedding goes ahead are open.

## Takes considered

- **A wedding at the Oriel Drive-In.** A woman's sister tries to talk her down the aisle and ends up bringing the groom to the booth to hear her. Chosen because the crowd can put pressure on the sisters' choices as well as the couple's and answer back at the turn.
- **The couple on the poster.** At a civic mass wedding the bride is one of dozens about to marry and the face of the publicity. Her fight with the other brides over leaving would give the crowd demands of its own and crowd out her encounter with her intended.
- **The groom comes around.** A man hears his intended refuse him from a banquet hall's coatroom. After time alone with the guests, he chooses to go back and hear her out. His hurt would take attention from the panicked speaker's choice.

## Provisional direction

Maris chose the drive-in where she and her sister worked the summer she met Ross. The couple have lived two hours apart for four years. They looked for a place together until Ross got a permanent job where he lives. Maris urged him to take it and said they could keep driving for a while. She built herself the big wedding partly from pride in what they'd kept going, partly because she wanted a date to count toward. Putting her flowers in Ross's car for their planned drive out together, she sees his thermos laid out for the morning drive. At her house on Sundays he packs it while she's still fixing their supper. Today she'd put the date in a different category. He hasn't changed the plan.

Nelle's pep talk gets Maris laughing and gives her one useful instruction about whom to look at when she speaks. Maris takes it seriously enough to ask for Ross in the booth. Nelle risks the embarrassment of stalling a ceremony she promised half the town and goes to get him. When Maris calls it off, Nelle offers to tell the crowd and Maris takes the mic with Nelle there to listen. The guests make far more noise going home than they'd been allowed to make at the wedding.

## Craft plan

**Teller.** Try close third in the half hour of Maris's panic, attentive to what her body is doing against her will. Try Maris telling it afterward to a willing listener who wants the fun of the turnout and whom she can put off about Ross. In both let her enjoy the full house.

**Cast.** Maris gladly welcomes people and stacks up particulars when she feels questioned. She wants Nelle to take her at her word and Ross to admit he thought the wedding would change something too. Nelle runs the Oriel. She wants one thing she can actually do for her sister and thinks the trouble is stage fright. She's spent the afternoon being congratulated on what she helped make and groups the options in a sentence, slipping in jokes she'll try to carry through. Ross is careful with time and generous with mileage. He wants Maris to count the effort he's made and keep their wedding. He heard her encouragement about the job as an offer to continue this way. He usually answers with something he can do and speaks freely about work he did well. All three are new characters.

**The conversation owed.** Maris must show her sister why a walk down the aisle and a drink can't fix this, and Nelle must decide what she can do instead. Then Maris must tell Ross to his face what she couldn't say in front of the crowd. Both encounters take place in the booth.

**Place.** The Oriel Drive-In in a market town, dry and turning cold at dusk in September. Popcorn oil in the booth, a papery radio hiss, tires on gravel humps, dusk chilling the bare steering wheels, light from open car doors. On ordinary weekends Nelle parks families clear of each other's sightlines and tells them to turn headlights off. Tonight nearly two hundred cars have a view of the wedding platform at the foot of the screen. The booth behind the rows looks over the lot. People can't hear inside it unless its mic is open. The side lane past the booth leads to the only exit. The Rosebank Hotel ballroom has service doors. Ryder Field's grandstand keeps the crowd in view and leaves the approach to the road clear. At the Oriel even the newlyweds have to wait to get out.

## Scene briefs

1. **About 40%, the long scene.** In the booth at dusk just after Maris sees the thermos. Nelle brings the count and expects to escort her out. In the pep talk after the four lines, she points out a couple of cars already on the wrong station and tries to get Maris to laugh and concentrate on one person, Ross. Maris says that picturing Ross alone is what makes the vows impossible. She could say the words to strangers and leave them thinking she and Ross were going home together for good. She's afraid he'd hear her promise more years of visits. She asks Nelle to send him up. At the first test horn, with time to hold the entrance music, Nelle puts the short film Maris made for after the vows on early and goes to the platform to get Ross. Now the couple can talk with the guests still in their cars.
2. **About 35%.** Ross comes from the platform to the booth knowing that Maris has asked to see him and Nelle has held the entrance. He expects cold feet. He tries to describe a Friday drive he wouldn't have made for anybody else, and Maris makes him hear how a Sunday with him ends while she's still making supper. When the film runs out, guests begin climbing out to look for the wedding party. Nelle calls from the booth door for an answer about what to tell them. Ross offers to stand back at the platform and wait, to give Maris more time. Maris asks him for weekdays they can plan on, hears what he can offer, and answers him and Nelle plainly. The job enters only as part of their competing ideas about the time they were going to spend in two towns. Leave the engagement offstage. Ross asks to reach his car before the public announcement. She gives him the time.
3. **About 25%.** Nelle offers to speak for her. Maris chooses the microphone and asks Nelle to stay where she can see her, trading the imagined face in the pep talk for her sister's. She tells the guests the wedding is off and they can go. A puzzled horn sets others off and headlights come on as the exit lane jams. The bride and the groom were supposed to drive out together over a volley of goodbye toots. Maris asks Nelle to walk out with her into the actual headlights. She stops at Ross's open window to say goodbye, in view of the backed-up cars, and watches him drive out with her flowers on the passenger seat. Everybody has to wait in the same line.
