# Outline

## Story

- Title: The Lights Beyond
- Premise and central promise: Exactly five hundred years after the horde's defeat and expulsion, human scouts Haskett and Dunley cross the Great Wall, discover illuminated Roskell and its flying machines, meet a descendant of the enemy, and become passengers themselves. Deliver sustained adventure and wonder beyond the initial skyline.
- Focal pressure or attachment: Haskett and her adult brother Dunley have saved their Wall-service pay to buy an inn together. She loves their shared competence and assumes their next life is settled. He honestly wanted that life until he saw another possible one.
- Counterforce or complication: Danger remains uncertain; neither scout can explain themselves fluently to the inhabitants. Dunley's fascination feels to Haskett like abandonment. Her caution contains useful knowledge and possessiveness; his appetite contains courage and embarrassing ignorance.
- POV, distance, and information limit: Close third past through Haskett. Stay inside a scout's sensory evidence, comparisons, tactical errors, and increasingly personal attention. No aerial omniscience, translated foreign thoughts, or authoritative technological lecture.
- Governing movement and time shape: Compress two days beyond the Wall, then expand one night from ridge to streets, waterfront, and flight. Brief return journey afterward. Movement opens outward instead of concentrating into an emergency or tribunal.
- Speculative rule or ordinary-world constraint: The Wall seals the southern pass, not the skyline. Several high ridges conceal a distant coastal basin; settlement and short-range flying follow the coast below those ridges. An enforced human prohibition on northern travel has lasted since the war; its five-hundredth anniversary brings permission for this first reconnaissance. Electricity and powered aircraft are material technologies requiring infrastructure, fuel, and skilled operators. No active magic is needed.
- Dialogue promise: Siblings' familiar field talk frays into an argument about an unexpected future. First contact adds awkward curiosity, misread offers, reciprocal fear, and humor that neither side completely shares.
- Dialogic medium: Human speech between scouts; unfamiliar speech, demonstrative sketches, pointing, offered objects, and refusals with pilot Pirvek. Engines eventually make sustained conversation impossible.
- Dialogue engine: The siblings can translate gestures for one another while misrepresenting each other's wishes. Establishing what Pirvek is offering forces Dunley to say what he wants; accepting the flight requires Haskett to relinquish control without pretending uncertainty has vanished.

## Voice

- Narrative texture: Begin with weight, slope, concealment, and distances; let uselessly abundant light overwhelm that sorting habit. Extend sentences during flight without turning wonder into an essay.
- Conversational texture: Shared references, ordinary irritations, economical field instructions, and teasing that occasionally hurts. Foreign speech remains meaningful action despite limited comprehension.
- Rhetorical ownership: Haskett owns practical comparisons and sisterly authority, but can misread affection. Dunley makes exuberant guesses and poor jokes, then becomes unexpectedly exact about his own desire. Pirvek owns the aircraft's immediate boundaries, grows impatient with weapons, and enjoys showing something loved; he supplies no historical verdict.
- Pressure behavior: Haskett repeats the plan more formally. Dunley first jokes away disagreement, then stops helping her pretend. Pirvek repeats physical demonstrations rather than becoming magically eloquent.
- Relationship movement: Haskett wants assurance she will still matter; Dunley wants release from an honest former promise without calling it worthless. Neither can initially ask. Pirvek wants the armed strangers to behave safely and stay curious. Their exchanges move from assumed obedience to a voluntary shared experience.
- Anti-default: Avoid a benevolent technological guide explaining five centuries while interchangeable scouts debate civilization. Keep discoveries partial and the sibling disagreement irreducibly personal.

## Beats

1. Cross at dawn. Establish the exact anniversary, unprecedented commission, return deadline, and affectionate inn plans through packing and familiar disagreement. Beyond the first ridge lies another: the scouts must actually travel to reach the discovery. The abandoned military road becomes a difficult, beautiful route rather than evidence of supernatural concealment.
2. Crest the last ridge at night. Roskell fills the basin with light; several metal birds move above it. Allow silence and sustained looking. Haskett initially counts defensive positions; Dunley notices small windows passing overhead. Their old map contains nothing large enough for this.
3. Descend and encounter the inhabited city: hot pavement, overhead cables, late food, exhausted passengers, tusked faces resembling enemy carvings but wearing ordinary clothes. Residents register the humans' strangeness. The scouts remain exposed strangers rather than invisible observers or instant celebrities. Follow the aircraft toward the waterfront.
4. At a small seaplane dock, meet Pirvek beside a three-seat craft. He is startled by their faces and bows; their gestures take time. He refuses boarding while weapons are held, demonstrates the seats and restraints, and sketches a short circuit returning here. Give him pleasure, impatience, and small comic failures. Nothing proves complete safety or political welcome.
5. Haskett wants to leave with what they have learned; Dunley wants the offered flight. Their quarrel finally exposes the inn's changed meaning. He cannot yet know whether he could live here, but he no longer authorizes spending his share. Haskett must absorb that actual loss rather than receive an easy reconciliation. She chooses to board from curiosity of her own.
6. Fly over the harbor and illuminated streets. Acceleration, rattling metal, cold air, and the startling descent of roofs make the promise bodily real. Dunley is frightened too; neither performs perfect bravery. Once airborne, let noise interrupt argument. Haskett and her brother can share amazement without resolving their futures.
7. Land; part from Pirvek with understandable thanks, not a diplomatic agreement. The scouts return within their allotted time. Haskett releases Dunley's savings from their joint plan. End on the particular difficulty and pleasure of bringing this impossible experience home together, with the inn undecided and no summary of national reform.
