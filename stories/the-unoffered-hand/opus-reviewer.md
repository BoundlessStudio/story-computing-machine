## Verdict

- Existing review.md said: Verdict PASS, "Dialogue: PASS"; I agree, and add four Minor polish notes.

This is a clean dialogue track: spare, but the spareness belongs to people under particular pressure, not to a shared register. The princess speaks in instructions, short contradictions ("He didn't." "He was balancing." "I could.") and literal dodges, which the narration tallies ("the fourth correction since breakfast") and which she drops after the injury ("I heard how that sounded", "I did. For most of that first day."). Sovelda speaks in wants, questions and broken-off explanations, Tirren speaks only to the bird in front of him, and in the orchard fright cuts everyone down to names and commands. Misreadings are legible and get taken up ("For this?" / "For him choosing me."), and the funniest beat (the meat cart) keeps Cresset an animal rather than a verdict on anyone's worth. The edit pass should stay light: untangle one quip in the final exchange, fix one loose referent in the confession, correct a direction phrase in the orchard, fix two word-level snags, and leave the rest of the dialogue alone.

## Dialogue findings

### D1 · Minor · Muddled quip in the final exchange

- Where: L401–L407, the high field, after Cresset's missed stoop at the ridge.
- Text:
> "You wanted him to catch it."
> "I didn't even see what it was."
> "Neither did he, soon enough."
- Problem: Ground/referent. "Neither did he" answers "I didn't even see what it was", which is about not identifying the quarry. "Soon enough" then turns it into losing sight of the quarry. The reader has to stop and work out the joke (the prey got away into cover). This is the last real spoken exchange before the chase, and it is the only line in the scene that doesn't make sense on first reading. The setup and the princess's laugh work; only the reply is tangled. L391 "Let him show you." already gives her one neat line in this scene.
- Fix: Have the reply say plainly what happened to the quarry, in her teasing register. Or cut it and let the laugh plus "You wanted him to catch it." carry the beat. Do not swap in another aphorism. Illustrative only: "Whatever it was, it's in the grass now. He'll go again."

### D2 · Minor · "Stolen your afternoon" points at nothing established

- Where: L327–L331, the mews after the injury, where the princess explains why she came back.
- Text:
> And I was tired of you looking at me as if I'd stolen your afternoon.
- Problem: Ground/referent. The lessons were in the mornings ("I'll come after breakfast", "By the fifth morning"). The only afternoon in play is the outing the princess herself granted. So "your afternoon" has no clear object. Sovelda gets the gist (you looked at me as if I'd taken something from you), but the word itself points nowhere, and this is the confession exchange, where precision matters.
- Fix: Aim the line at what Sovelda's looks actually accused her of: taking or holding back something that was Sovelda's. That also lets the irony land unstated, since the princess is the one who lost the bird. Keep "I wanted to see him fly." Illustrative only: "as if I'd taken something of yours."

## Prose findings

### P1 · Minor · Two small clarity snags

- Where: L29, the exposition on the choosing; L393, the high field as Cresset leaves.
- Text:
> Most chose young.
> She wiped them on her empty sleeve.
- Problem: "Most chose young" could mean that most falcons chose in their first autumn or that most chose young people. The next sentence implies the first meaning, but the reader has to work it out afterwards. "Empty sleeve" usually means a missing arm. Here it presumably means the sleeve of her bare arm, or of the arm the bird has just left.
- Fix: Make the first say that most birds choose in their first autumn. Change the second to her free or bare arm's sleeve. Change nothing else in either paragraph.

## Continuity and prompt findings

### C1 · Minor · Orchard direction wobble before the accident

- Where: L207 and L223–L231, the south orchard flight.
- Text:
> The orchard sloped away from the palace wall.
> She took three quick steps down the slope, following him
> Sovelda went uphill to see past it.
- Problem: Internal continuity (space). The wall is at the top of the slope. Cresset goes over it, and Sovelda climbs toward it to see him. Yet the princess "follows" him by stepping downhill. Otherwise the accident's layout is clear: grass below, trees above, the branch between Sovelda and the returning bird. This is the beat that most needs clear staging, so one direction phrase shouldn't make a careful reader re-map the slope.
- Fix: Keep her moving downhill, since that leaves her below on the grass for "Sovelda, come down here." Replace "following him" with a reason that fits moving away from the wall, such as backing off to keep him in sight above the trees. Do not otherwise change L231–L245.
