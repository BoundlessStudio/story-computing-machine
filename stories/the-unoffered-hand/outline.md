# Outline

## Story

- Premise and central promise: The fiercest royal falcon chooses a servant who has only put on a glove to clean it. His lifelong obedience gives her an exhilarating place in a world whose ordinary skills she lacks. Deliver the public choosing and a consequential first flight, keeping the bird an animal with appetites rather than an oracle of human worth.
- Focal pressure or attachment: Sovelda, twenty, polishes the court's ceremonial leatherwork. She likes fine things and being looked at, although she has learned to disguise both desires. The bird makes those desires suddenly possible; she wants the princess to witness her success more than she initially wants instruction.
- Counterforce or complication: The princess has waited several autumns for Cresset and knows him intimately. She cannot command him, while Sovelda can command him disastrously. Their unequal combination of status, experience, jealousy, and dependence outlasts the ceremony. Master Tirren has other newly chosen birds to oversee; the princess is the person available who already knows this one.
- POV, distance, and information limit: Close third past through Sovelda, with attention to fit, cloth, bodily awkwardness, and the pleasure of being obeyed. Let the princess's investment emerge through her behavior and admissions. Do not translate Cresset's thoughts or supply a definitive reason for his choice.
- Governing movement and time shape: The choosing afternoon, several days of handling, one ill-judged private flight, then an autumn interval culminating in another outdoor encounter. Compress repetition; expand shifts in their relationship. Target prose 2,500–4,000 words.
- Speculative rule or ordinary-world constraint: Each autumn the kingdom's unbound falcons may select one lifetime handler; birds that have already chosen keep their existing bond. A bird may refuse all candidates across multiple autumns. Chosen birds obey their person's deliberately directed simple calls and gestures, without understanding abstract human speech or gaining extra strength, judgment, or invulnerability. Other people may feed and care for them, but cannot summon or direct them. Ordinary equipment is not magical. No destiny, bloodline, telepathy, or moral-selection system is established.
- Dialogue promise: A servant abruptly possesses something the princess deeply wanted; instruction, resentment, borrowed finery, and mutual embarrassment let both women be difficult without making either a villain.
- Dialogic medium: Spoken conversation and physical demonstrations between humans; learned calls, glove offers, and animal responses between Sovelda and Cresset.
- Dialogue engine: Sovelda mistakes being addressed as important for being ready. The princess's instruction is accurate but sharpened by envy; Sovelda hears contempt in it. Their dispute affects the timing and location of the first flight. After its consequence, each must acknowledge a particular failure without settling their whole relationship.

## Voice

- Narrative texture: Tangible, supple close-third prose; Sovelda notices the inside of splendid things and discovers the bodily scale of a living bird. Let sudden appetite interrupt practiced deference.
- Conversational texture: Unequal lessons, awkward requests, irritation, and occasional unguarded pleasure. Keep ordinary questions and unfinished explanations; reserve the most direct speech for a concrete grievance.
- Rhetorical ownership: Sovelda can explain leatherwork fluently but guesses about birds. The princess assumes she will be understood, then finds that her demonstrations work better. Her teasing can wound. Tirren speaks to immediate handling needs and leaves emotional matters unattended. The falcon communicates through attention and movement, never human argument.
- Pressure behavior: Sovelda agrees before she understands, then becomes stubborn when embarrassed. The princess overexplains physical errors and initially evades emotional ones. Fright strips both to immediate calls and names.
- Relationship movement: Sovelda wants admiration and instruction from the same person she resents needing. The princess wants continued access to Cresset without admitting the hurt of losing him. Their exchanges expose these incompatible claims and permit limited companionship with envy still present.
- Anti-default: A hidden royal heir vindicated before a cruel court, or a novice who proves superior by articulating an animal-consent lesson. Preserve ignorance, pleasure, and the princess's genuine skill.

## Beats

1. Sovelda wears an ornate spare glove to reach its creases while preparing the choosing. Establish that she has never touched a hawk. Show Cresset refusing the princess, generals, and master falconers; he crosses to the servant without invitation. Her immediate problem is holding his weight without dropping the court's costly bird.
2. Public attention brings material change: relief from ordinary duties and a place at the mews. Let Sovelda relish it. The princess helps settle the bird, with an awkward claim on glove or equipment exposing their former relation. Cresset's fierce refusal of other hands continues.
3. Across handling lessons, show the bond's limitations, feeding, physical fatigue, and Sovelda's clumsy excitement. The princess's familiarity contradicts Sovelda's easy account of her as merely entitled. A sharp exchange makes Sovelda insist on seeing him fly before she is ready to read his approach.
4. At a sheltered outdoor practice place, a premature recall brings Cresset down along an obstructed route. The bird obeys and is hurt. Keep geometry, warning, call, impact, and response explicit; neither magical obedience nor the princess's skill prevents consequences already in motion. The injury must remain proportionate and recoverable.
5. During care, Sovelda admits what she ordered instead of blaming the bird. The princess admits a particular cruel remark or withheld kindness, not a grand moral error. Accurate instruction remains accurate. Give Cresset time to recover and let Sovelda attend him without applause.
6. Return to open autumn ground after recovery. Sovelda can fly him now but remains a learner. The princess joins for her own pleasure. Let Cresset's speed and the women's differing appetites produce an ending with exhilaration and continuing friction; do not close on a profession, policy declaration, or summary of lessons.
