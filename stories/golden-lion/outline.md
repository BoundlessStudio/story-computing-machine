# Outline

## Story

- Premise and central promise: At the gentlemen's annual gathering in Menagerie Hall, elderly Aldous Torven of House Heron sees Rafe Irlow enter with a golden-lion cane. Resolve claimant, fraud, or survivor: the cane is newly made, but Rafe's descent is real. House Lion was destroyed legally while children were reassigned and women escaped; Aldous was one such child. Rafe wants to find his grandmother's lost brother, not win a title.
- Focal pressure or attachment: Aldous owes his life and adult identity to the Herons who renamed him, yet the extermination story declares his sister and former self dead. He wants Rafe removed unharmed, but protection keeps becoming attempted purchase of silence. Rafe protects his grandmother's location from every House.
- Counterforce or complication: Admission rules, watching peers, a host containing scandal, degraded family memories, and Aldous's practiced evasion press them. Rafe's conspicuous tactic gains access but resembles theater; his poor House etiquette cannot sustain a polished case. No single villain is required.
- POV, distance, and information limit: Close third-person past through Aldous. Recognition arrives through bodily habits, an obsolete pronunciation, and reflexive corrections. Do not misrepresent his knowledge; let practiced Heron vocabulary defer naming the purge. Rafe's purpose, evidence, and grandmother's wishes remain external until disclosed.
- Governing movement and time shape: One gathering night: public animal-roll and interruption, contraction into a side room, then a brief threshold decision while the gathering continues. Domestic corrections make the relationship legible. End outside before reunion, public reckoning, or House restoration.
- Speculative rule or ordinary-world constraint: Ordinary gaslamp-era custom, not magic. A carved cane announces the House granting a gentleman standing, obligations, and gathering admission. Carvings can be copied; they assert identity but prove no bloodline. Keep the system local and its wider law, origin, and gender structure unresolved.
- Dialogue promise: Ceremonial courtesy collides with awkward family identification. Public talk treats scandal as procedure. Privately, Rafe loses his rehearsed sequence; Aldous sounds too elegant until petty corrections and involuntary memories expose him. Recognition stays uncomfortable, incomplete, and occasionally absurd.
- Dialogic medium: Ritual announcements, side-room speech, displayed or withheld animal canes, a folded message of domestic prompts, silence, glances toward the hall, and refusal to provide an address. The lion makes a claim that speech complicates.
- Dialogue engine: Rafe needs family answers before exposing his grandmother. Aldous controls whether the gathering calls Rafe an impostor; Rafe controls access to Aldous's sister. Every correction shifts credibility and leverage, while interruptions turn continued evasion into an immediate choice.

## Voice

- Narrative texture: Track ceremonial surfaces through Aldous: cane heads, seating gaps, practiced bows, small breaches. Let childhood memory intrude as sensory recognition, not explanatory flashback or lore.
- Conversational texture: Brittle public courtesy gives way to doubling back, misheard details, wasted time, poor explanation, and undignified irritation.
- Rhetorical ownership: Aldous owns euphemism, social authority, dry defensive humor, and threat-misreading. Rafe owns blunt purpose, disordered evidence, suspicious jokes, and growing willingness to hear correction. The host owns formula and selective deafness.
- Pressure behavior: Aldous nitpicks, repeats titles, and loses fluency around kinship. Rafe rushes prepared facts, then withholds and watches; interruptions degrade both men's listening.
- Relationship movement: Rafe wants kinship confirmed but cannot ask Aldous to choose his sister over the identity that saved him. Aldous wants mercy but cannot request secrecy or forgiveness. Exchange turns evidence into leverage, denial into commitment, and a protected location into conditional trust.
- Anti-default: Avoid forensic proof, polished debate, confession speech, converted crowd, restored House, or magical credential. Messy domestic memory and a choice to face one survivor decide the story.

## Beats

1. Establish Aldous's effortless reading of rank during the animal-roll. Rafe's entrance breaks it: worn tall black top hat, round blue-tinted lenses, shoulder-length wavy brown hair, broad moustache, dark layered formalwear, gloves, and the bright lion-headed wooden cane. Reactions vary from nervous amusement to anger.
2. The host diverts Rafe to a side room and recruits Aldous after noticing his response. Aldous sees that the gold head is recent. Rafe admits it, defeating a forgery test, but will neither surrender it nor identify its maker.
3. Rafe's fragmented explanation narrows the aim: his grandmother survived, remembers a younger brother taken by another House, and supplied private prompts no registry could answer. The gathering made every possible survivor see the lion. Aldous offers money and distant help instead of recognition.
4. Use several domestic prompts without prewriting them. Their premises are secondhand or degraded. Aldous's irritated corrections, one disputed household memory, and an embodied childhood habit accumulate into recognition; Rafe doubts one neat match.
5. In bounded pieces, reveal that most adult Lion gentlemen were killed, boys absorbed and renamed, and women and girls dispersed. Later rolls called the House exterminated because recognized gentlemen alone counted. Survival must not erase violence or the grandmother's agency.
6. The host demands the cane or threatens a public fraud accusation. Aldous neither authenticates an heirloom nor gives a speech: he rejects the label, identifies himself privately, and leaves publicly with Rafe, keeping his heron cane. Outside, one further family answer earns the sister's means of contact. Stop before reunion, forgiveness, or civic resolution.
