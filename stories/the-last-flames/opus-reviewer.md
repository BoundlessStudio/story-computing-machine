# Opus review: The Last Flames

Independent audit for a later editing pass. It is not a `review.md` verdict,
does not change canon state, and does not authorize edits by itself.

## Verdict

- Dialogue: REVISE — the cups-and-map scene has a Ground failure, the discovery scene announces the mechanism, and most secondary speech falls into the house quip register.
- Prose: REVISE — narration keeps restating the idea (difference breaks the pattern) after the action has shown it, including a polished emblem at the climax.
- Continuity and prompt: PASS — the prompt is fulfilled within length (about 3,090 words); only minor direction, referent and setup slips.
- Edit scope: targeted-dialogue
- Existing review.md said: Verdict PASS, "Dialogue: PASS"; disagree — it missed the Ground slip at the cups, the thesis lines in the discovery scene and the voice convergence.

This story has a real dialogic engine. Talking about the pursuer is dangerous,
so the youths keep answers short and communicate by tail-taps, throat-light and
scent, and the best exchanges use that limit well: the rite, the arrival, and
Ceryth's repeated order joining the hum. The problem is that nearly everyone
else still talks in the collection's house register. Ygdrane and Parrox are the
same dry food critic. Parrox closes scene after scene with a formal epigram,
and Ulune answers with "Then…" retorts. Alexstrasza is the one character who can
safely speak at length, yet outside the rite she gives the same clipped
fragment-list orders as Ceryth. The discovery scene should let the rules emerge
through Ulune's yes/no light; instead Alexstrasza states the mechanism in three
neat lines that overstate it, and the scene ends on a Parrox quip with no
decision. The edit pass should let the speech rule shape the voices. The youths
should be terse because they have to be, but young, scared, petty or funny in
different ways. Alexstrasza should sound fuller and slower, with the patience of
the rite. Discoveries should come as questions.

## Edit guardrails

- Prompt requirements: Alexstrasza is the immortal keeper who receives final flames and carries the memories; young dragons arrive in droves; they flee something they refuse to name. Never let anyone name it or explain it in a speech. Length band 2,500–4,000 words (now about 3,090). Broadly accessible rating. Active profile is `prospective-2026-08-21` (the last `Craft profile:` line, from the rewrite constraints).
- The name Alexstrasza is supplied by the prompt and is also a well-known video-game dragon. Keep the name and import nothing from that franchise.
- Memory mechanic: a carried flame is sensation only, "not a second mind" (L71). This keeps the LOCKED personhood protection intact, so the dead must never speak or advise through Alexstrasza.
- The pursuer should stay a resonance acting on living fire (moon-iron, the aurora, matched patterns), not a nonliving thing that casts or chooses. That keeps it inside the LOCKED "Living agency and magic" rule (only life uses magic).
- Consent logic: the rite asks whether the dying came "by your own choice"; the youths refuse the basin; no one gives up a final flame; all sixty-one survive; Ygdrane's burial is finished last.
- Fixed counts and patterns: 11 → 34 → 61 dragons; the five-knock alarm; Ygdrane's five pressures, broken into two, one, two at the climax; the final knock pattern of five, then three, one, two.
- The speech rule is load-bearing. The youths keep answers short and avoid speaking about the thing, and signals carry what speech can't. Edits must not make the youths fluent about the pursuer.
- What already works and must survive: the rite (L47–L59), Ceryth's arrival lines, the copper youth's cut-off attempt, the one-word questions on the hide, the whole crisis sequence of dialogue (L253–L279), and the final "Breakfast" / "Go count."

## Dialogue findings

### D1 · Major · Discovery exchange announces the mechanism and ends on a quip

- Where: L221–L239, eastern work chamber after Ulune's demonstration
- Text:
> "It followed the matched pitch," Alexstrasza said.
> "Speech makes one held shape. Crowds make it stronger."
> White again. Then Ulune scraped through Alexstrasza's second line and replaced it with a mess of short angles.
> "And difference breaks it."
> "Good. I was worried the answer would be convenient."
- Problem: Philosopher/Exposition. The scene has set up a yes/no language (white flash = yes), which gives Alexstrasza a real reason to *ask*. Instead she pronounces the rule in three polished declaratives, and Ulune only confirms them. "Speech makes one held shape" also claims more than the story shows. Everyone, the youths included, speaks sentences safely all through the story. What is shown to be dangerous is speaking *about it* (L105), a held or lengthened tone, and matched or repeated patterns (L247, L265). Ground/referent: "Alexstrasza's second line" has nothing to point to. She drew no lines in this chamber; her only lines were on the hide in the kitchen (L173). Finally, the characters have just learned that the thing follows crowds and is heading for a temple holding sixty-one dragons, and no one reacts. Parrox's last word closes the scene where a decision should be. The watchers' plan to split sleepers (L247) is never set up.
- Fix: Turn Alexstrasza's three statements into short, plain questions that Ulune answers with light. Limit them to what the scene showed: a held or matched pattern, crowds, and talking about it. Make the crossed-out mark something on this frost, either Ulune's own smooth crescent or a guess Alexstrasza scratches there. Replace Parrox's quip with the listeners taking in the danger and Ceryth deciding how they will sleep, which sets up her watchers at L247. This is also the natural place for C3. Illustrative only: "Does it follow a crowd?" (Ulune flashes white.)

### D2 · Blocking · "Closer to what?" answers a word, and the cup test points the wrong way

- Where: L161–L175, kitchen, Ulune's singing cup
- Text:
> Ulune ignored him. She shifted the singing cup a claw's width east. Its note weakened. West, and the surface shivered harder.
> "Not louder," she said when Alexstrasza approached. "Closer together. The little waves."
> "Closer to what?"
> "Then give me an answer that doesn't."
> Ulune added three hooked marks farther north. Her throat flashed once, white.
- Problem: Ground/uptake. Ulune says the ripples are *closer together*, meaning spacing. Alexstrasza answers the word "closer" as if Ulune had said the cup was closer to something. The intended inference (moving the cup tightens the waves, so a source lies that way) exists only on the author's side. The staging then contradicts it. The test the reader just watched runs east–west, with west stronger, but Ulune's answer marks the source north. "Not louder" corrects something nobody said. L171 is untagged with three speakers present, so it could be Alexstrasza or Ulune. It is also a "Then…" flip of Ceryth's rule, a comeback shape rather than a request.
- Fix: Stage the test along the axis the answer uses. Ulune could turn or slide the cup toward the north wall and have the ripples tighten. Then give Alexstrasza a plain question about what the water is responding to, so that Ulune's glance at Ceryth follows naturally. Either cut L171 (the hide at L173 already shows Alexstrasza's solution) or tag it and make it a plain request. Keep "The little waves." Illustrative only: "What is it listening to?"

### D3 · Major · Parrox speaks only in formal epigrams, doubling Ygdrane

- Where: L121–L129 (goat), L159 (cups), L239 (discovery), L341–L349 (morning)
- Text:
> "Then time has been unkind to it."
> Parrox hooked two cup handles over a claw. "A workable compromise. Six drinks and one musician."
> "There is no breakfast. There is evidence."
> "In meltwater?"
- Problem: Identical Voices / epigram closers / uniform fluency under pressure. Parrox is a young refugee who fled something terrifying, yet every line he has outside the crisis is a formal, contraction-free last word, delivered like an old butler. He also fills the same role as Ygdrane: a food critic who lands a dry correction on Alexstrasza, who answers both times with a curt retort (L39 "You asked for thick." / L127 "Eat or don't."). The two scenes could be swapped. "In meltwater?" exists only to set up Ulune's flood joke ("We have plenty."), since meltwater is the only water they have. Ground/knowledge (minor): the reported joke at L121 ("died before Ygdrane was hatched") needs Parrox to know the dead elder's name and age.
- Fix: Keep Parrox as the complainer and the practical one, but make him sound young: contractions, concrete disgust or hunger, exaggeration, sometimes wrong, and at least one line that betrays fear. Keep his crisis lines (L273, L277, L305) and "Parrox ate. He also found three blocked vents before dawn." Cut or replace "A workable compromise…" and "I was worried the answer would be convenient." Give L341 a plain report and drop the "In meltwater?" setup, so Ulune's "Boil it." carries the moment. At L121, have Parrox point to "that old one" or cut the name. Illustrative only (L341): "Goat's ruined. Ash all down one side."

### D4 · Major · Alexstrasza's orders sound exactly like Ceryth's

- Where: L113 (arrival), L127 (goat), L199/L203 (terms), L279, compared with Ceryth at L93, L197, L261
- Text:
> "North alcoves," she said. "Not the receiving chamber. Keep the floor channels clear."
> "Groups of seven. Different chambers. Watchers count out of order. Go."
> "Eat or don't."
- Problem: Identical Voices. Cover the tags and the ancient keeper's orders read like the refugee leader's: the same staccato lists, the same curt closers. Ceryth's brevity has a story reason, since long speech about the thing is dangerous for the youths. Alexstrasza's doesn't. She is the one speaker who can safely ask questions that take a sentence, and the rite (L47–L55) shows she has a patient, full-sentence register. Flattening her into Ceryth's staccato throws away the contrast that "No questions that take a sentence" creates. It also weakens the final "Go count." Her taking up Ceryth's idiom lands only if she hasn't sounded like Ceryth all along.
- Fix: Leave Ceryth's fragments, counts and repeated rules alone; they are her survival grammar and they pay off at L265–L271. Revise Alexstrasza's non-crisis lines toward the keeper's register: longer sentences, courtesy, an offer or a question about what the other dragon wants. This is not lore or poetry (the outline's anti-default rules out solemn speeches). Start with L113 and L127, and the second "Agreed." (see D7). Keep "I know." (L255), "Take the ash rake." (L279) and "Go count." (L373). Illustrative only (L113): "Take the north alcoves. Keep your little ones out of this room, and keep the floor channels clear."

### D5 · Minor · Ygdrane's complaint names an ingredient that isn't in the paste and ends on a correction

- Where: L31–L41, receiving chamber, salt paste
- Text:
> She mixed ash salt with warm fat and held the dish while he licked twice.
> "Too much resin."
> "Those are different failures."
- Problem: Ground/object. The paste is ash salt and warm fat, so "resin" refers to nothing the reader has seen. "You asked for thick" suggests resin is the thickener, but only after the fact. "Those are different failures" is the collection's correction-epigram closer, and it makes Ygdrane Parrox's double (D3).
- Fix: Put resin in the mixture at L35, or change the complaint to fit salt and fat. Cut "Those are different failures." or replace it with something bodily (he can't swallow it, he turns his head away). Keep "That's keeper talk for no." and "Don't start carrying me before I'm dead."

### D6 · Minor · Water-cup exchange is a tennis match that ends on a "Then" retort

- Where: L151–L157, kitchen passage
- Text:
> "There are thirty mouths waiting."
> "Then don't spill this one."
- Problem: Tennis Match / rejoinder. There are four evenly sized one-liners, closed by Ulune's "Then…" flip. Its logic runs backwards: scarce water is a reason to hand the cup over, not to keep it. The same "Then + retort" shape appears at L125 and L171. Ulune is absorbed in the cup, and a sharp comeback isn't how that absorption would sound.
- Fix: Keep Ceryth's call and "Take the other six." Replace the last retort with Ulune not engaging: she doesn't look up, or she curls a claw around the cup. Parrox's line is covered in D3.

### D7 · Minor · The demonstration is negotiated over Ulune's head

- Where: L195–L203, kitchen, after the pin test
- Text:
> Ceryth put herself between Alexstrasza and Ulune. "One attempt. Outside the sleeping rooms. Vent open. Basin shut. If her flame pulls, we stop."
> "And you don't ask her to say it."
- Problem: Negotiation routine. The exchange is a list of clauses answered with "Agreed." twice. Ceryth's conditions fit her character. But the keeper whose rite turns on "by your own choice" never asks Ulune, and Ulune never agrees. The one scene where Ulune risks her flame becomes a deal between two guardians.
- Fix: Keep Ceryth's conditions (trim them if you like). Replace the second "Agreed." with Alexstrasza turning to Ulune and waiting for her answer, such as a white flash. That one beat changes the scene from a contract into Ulune's consent.

### D8 · Minor · The "Metal?" guess has no visible basis

- Where: L181, kitchen map; setup at L75
- Text:
> "Metal?"
> The metal scent of a bitten tongue.
- Problem: Ground/knowledge. Alexstrasza's third question jumps to metal. Her only prior cue is Ygdrane's "metal scent of a bitten tongue", about a hundred lines earlier, and the story never shows her making the link. The "cold metal" residue at L191 comes after the pin, not before the question.
- Fix: Before "Metal?", add a single residue beat: the cup's note brings back the bitten-tongue taste. That makes the guess legibly hers.

### D9 · Minor · "Vent's open" exists to set up "We noticed," and the narration after it is false

- Where: L305, L321–L327, just after the aurora breaks
- Text:
> "Vent's open," he said.
> "We noticed," Ceryth said.
> It was the first thing anyone answered aloud.
- Problem: Punchline Patch, mild. Parrox already shouted "West open!" at L305, so repeating it mainly sets up Ceryth's sarcasm, which is Parrox's register, not hers. The narration then glosses the moment and gets it wrong: Alexstrasza answered Ceryth aloud at L255, and Parrox answered her at L277.
- Fix: Keep a small ordinary exchange here; it is the right instinct for a release. Give Parrox something new to say (the rake, his leg, a question), or give Ceryth a reply that belongs to her, such as a count. Cut L327.

### D10 · Minor · Burial exchange ends on a rejoinder and a narration gloss

- Where: L359–L365, outer shelf, Ygdrane's cairn
- Text:
> "So do you. This takes two."
> It did, though Alexstrasza had performed it alone for centuries.
- Problem: "So do you" hands Alexstrasza's own words back, the house rejoinder. The narration then explains Ceryth's kindness and its meaning, which "Ceryth held one corner against the wind without being asked" (L365) already shows.
- Fix: Cut L363. Consider letting Ceryth skip the argument and simply take the other end of the cloth, or give a short reason that is hers, not a turned-back phrase. Keep L365.

## Prose findings

### P1 · Major · Narration restates the idea after the action shows it

- Where: L211 (demonstration), L237, L299 and L315 (climax), L355 (cold room)
- Text:
> Alexstrasza understood none of those pieces alone. Their lack of agreement was the point.
> The unevenness mattered more than her count.
> They made a crowd: frightened, stubborn, badly coordinated, alive.
> The living had not made it less real; they had only made it wait.
- Problem: Philosopher in the narration. "Difference breaks the pattern" is shown in the demonstration, stated in dialogue (D1), stated again at L211 and L299, and then turned into a closing emblem at L315. L355 does the same for the living-and-dead theme. The style guide asks that a point not be explained again once it is clear.
- Fix: Cut the explaining sentences at L211, L237 ("Precision had cost her.") and L299. End L315's paragraph on the concrete sounds, or cut the colon list. Cut L355's second sentence. The action around each one already carries the meaning.

### P2 · Minor · Negation-then-reversal constructions as the default

- Where: L71, L131, L141, L189, L251, L287–L289, L293
- Text:
> Final flames moved through her, but the dead did not rise and offer counsel.
> Every procedure assumed the flame belonged here.
- Problem: The narration keeps defining things by what they are not ("not words, not a second mind…", "No bell, no shouted warning.", "a shadow made by warmth rather than light", "This one did not."). Taken one at a time they are fine; together they become a tic.
- Fix: Keep L71, which states the memory rule the story needs. Rephrase most of the others as positive statements.

### P3 · Minor · One-sentence paragraphs drive the climax

- Where: L285–L319 (L285, L289, L291, L297, L303, L311, L319); overall about a third of the narration paragraphs are a single sentence
- Text:
> The basin channels still gained light.
> The blue light faltered.
> She did the opposite.
- Problem: In the climax, isolated lines become the default pulse, so no single beat gets emphasis.
- Fix: Merge most of these into the paragraphs next to them. Keep one or two isolated lines, for example "She opened the memory gates."

### P4 · Minor · The memory-gates exposition interrupts Ygdrane's death and misstates what the gates are for

- Where: L71–L75, receiving chamber; payoff at L291–L295
- Text:
> They let a keeper return carried sensations to the ice during the burial.
- Problem: The gates paragraph comes between "Sensation, stripped of its explanation." (L71) and the sensations it introduces (L75). The purpose it states never happens: the burial (L365) is outside at the cairn and uses no gates. What the gates actually do is set up the climax, where Alexstrasza sends carried sensation into the channels.
- Fix: Move the gate sentences after L75–L77, or into L79. Restate their purpose so it matches L291–L295, or show them used at the burial.

## Continuity and prompt findings

### C1 · Minor · The aurora arrives over the southern ridge despite every cue pointing north

- Where: L243; compare L107, L175, L225
- Text:
> It came as a green band above the southern ridge, wrong only because it moved against the stars.
> Finally she dragged it south to the temple mark.
- Problem: The copper youth's flame pulls north, Ulune marks the source north, and her map drags it south toward the temple. So it should arrive from the north. Appearing over the southern ridge reads like a slip.
- Fix: Change it to the northern ridge, or add a clause showing it has already passed overhead.

### C2 · Minor · "The fracture" is treated as known but never set up

- Where: L351, morning; nearest cue L207
- Text:
> Three wanted to go north when their strength returned and find the fracture without speaking near it.
- Problem: Ground/referent in the narration. Only the outline names a moon-iron fracture. In the prose the nearest thing is Ulune's crosshatched gray crescent with the pin pressed into it.
- Fix: At L207, show the crescent once as a crack or split in the ice, or change L351 to something like "find where it began".

### C3 · Minor · Why only young fire is taken is left to one late clause

- Where: L309, climax
- Text:
> Immortality had not made her fire young, but it had made her carry more patterns than the temple had ever held at once.
- Problem: The prompt's premise is that young dragons are fleeing. The story never lets anyone find out that young fire is what gets pulled, so "had not made her fire young" reads like a rule the reader missed.
- Fix: Add one yes/no question in the revised discovery exchange (D1): Alexstrasza asks whether it is only young fire, and Ulune answers with light. Don't turn it into a lore speech.

### C4 · Minor · Water infrastructure used as background texture

- Where: L43, L119, L139
- Text:
> Alexstrasza checked the four shutters, the drain groove, the felt under Ygdrane's jaw.
> Two chipped ice from the meltwater drains.
- Problem: Policy nit. The style guide's "No default water infrastructure" rule covers drains, and the prompt didn't ask for them. The "waste trench" at L119 is borderline. Ordinary meltwater, cups and the flooded kitchen floor are fine.
- Fix: Swap in non-water chores: chipping ice from the doorsill or ledges, and checking the ash lines instead of a drain groove.
