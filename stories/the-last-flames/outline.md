# Outline

## Story

- Premise and central promise: Alexstrasza receives the final flames of dragons who choose death and keeps each life’s memories distinct. This winter, vigorous young dragons arrive by the dozen, request the rite, and refuse to describe their pursuer. Reveal that fragments of the dead are escaping in Alexstrasza’s winter breath and lodging in first-winter dragons, who follow the intrusion back to its source because the temple’s cold quiets it. Resolve whether she can stop their deaths without abandoning the memories she keeps.
- Focal pressure or attachment: Alexstrasza’s identity joins two goods that have never conflicted: honoring a dragon’s final choice and carrying the dead intact. The youths’ fear and crowded recollections make consent doubtful. Refusal may prolong confusion; acceptance would create a mass grave, increase the harmful burden, and let ritual obedience replace attention.
- Counterforce or complication: No person is the antagonist. Exceptional cold, accumulated final flame, and Alexstrasza’s habit of venting heat through high shafts have produced a new failure. The youths revere the keeper and fear losing their only refuge, so accusing her feels dangerous. Their body heat also thaws galleries built for one dying visitor, forcing action before meltwater reaches earlier flames.
- POV, distance, and information limit: Close third-person past through Alexstrasza. She recognizes recollections she carries but cannot inspect a living mind, distinguish silence from consent, or know events beyond the temple until shown through action or final flame. Keep the dead as memories, not ghosts, copies, or souls; Kelyth remains distinct despite foreign recollections.
- Governing movement and time shape: One polar night, moving inward against an outward leak. A solitary rite gives way to living bodies accumulating from threshold to galleries while Alexstrasza postpones more rites and works backward from clues. Give Kelyth’s collapse, consent, final flame, and the recognition inside it full scene space. Finish quietly by containing the source, sheltering survivors, and beginning individual recovery—not battle, public proof, conversion, or ceremonial reform.
- Speculative rule or ordinary-world constraint: At death, a dragon may voluntarily surrender a final flame; Alexstrasza receives its autobiographical memories irreversibly and cannot edit or discard them. Exceptional cold can condense sensory fragments from vented heat. Dragons before their first full winter molt may absorb them; the intrusion disorients but neither erases identity nor creates persons. Through shared flame, Alexstrasza can reclaim only fragments she already carries, never the youth’s own memories. Each return makes her relive and retain that fragment’s heat, so recovery is individual and the shafts cannot stay closed indefinitely.
- Dialogue pressure: Kelyth wants a swift rite without accusing the keeper who may be the others’ only refuge; Alexstrasza wants enough truth to distinguish chosen death from flight-shaped surrender. Alexstrasza uses exact ritual questions and patient silence. Kelyth answers through weather and bodily effects, withholding the source; foreign recollections sometimes lend archaic phrasing that Alexstrasza recognizes, while Kelyth’s own diction is brief, young, and concrete.

## Beats

1. Alexstrasza receives an old dragon’s expected flame at dusk. While she settles that life, Kelyth and several unwounded youths arrive and request death but cannot answer her questions about cause or last wishes.
2. More land through worsening snow. First-molt scales, poor coordination, and reactions to unknowable temple details make Alexstrasza delay the rites. Cold alcoves calm them; warmth intensifies borrowed sensations.
3. Living heat thaws the galleries. Meltwater approaches the memory chambers, closing the doors would trap more heat, and expulsion means the storm. Kelyth presses for death before losing track of their own recollections.
4. Kelyth repeats a sensory detail belonging to the old dragon received at dusk. They indicate red-lit snow falling downwind of the temple, but cannot directly identify its source.
5. Kelyth collapses after weeks of irreversible damage. Alexstrasza verifies the choice still available, receives Kelyth’s flame, and experiences Kelyth’s life alongside foreign fragments arriving on breath from the temple shafts. Kelyth remains a person, not merely evidence.
6. Alexstrasza closes the shafts despite the heat she must retain, opens a colder lower gallery, and refuses more rites while the arrivals can still choose. She requests neither forgiveness nor public assent.
7. Guided by Kelyth’s memory, Alexstrasza draws one escaped fragment from another youth. Their own memories remain and orientation returns, proving a limited recovery rather than an instant cure.
8. The storm, Kelyth’s death, and lengthy work remain. With the leak contained and immediate deaths stopped, Alexstrasza moves to the next living dragon for a practical check of body, memory, and consent rather than another funeral question.

## Continuity

- Canon used: Memory mechanisms must remain local and specific, and carried or intrusive memories cannot erase the personhood, consent, grief, or moral weight of either the living dragon or the remembered dead. Death remains broadly accessible and non-graphic.
- Boundaries and unknowns: The prompt establishes Alexstrasza, the unnamed frozen temple, her immortality, and the final-flame office only for this story. Do not connect this temple, its dragon biology, its molt, or its memory transfer to existing dragon settings or systems. Leave the temple’s geography, Alexstrasza’s origin, the office’s creation, and any wider dragon taxonomy unresolved.
