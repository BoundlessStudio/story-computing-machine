# Provisional outline — The Window in the Wood

## Promised experience

The complete request is: "[SP] The woods are scary at night..." Its explicit elements are woods, night, and fear. I infer a pleasure in entering that fear rather than explaining it away: darkness should make the woodland consequential, with a disturbing discovery or encounter that earns the trailing ellipsis. The wording leaves scale, viewpoint, length, characters, relationships, the source of danger, and the ending open. A supernatural threat and an unnerving survival ending are choices below, not user requirements.

## Provisional direction

A compact, close-third story following Tessa, an adult who knows a footpath through the wood well enough to take it after sunset. She wants to reach her cottage on the far side. No central human relationship is proposed; her familiar home gives the threat something recognizable to imitate. The wood contains a living, concealed hunter that offers a counterfeit glimpse of home. Its nature remains unknown. Its observed physical movement matters; no safe boundary or universal rule about forests, darkness, or magic is proposed.

1. Tessa returns from an ordinary errand with a small hand lantern. Establish the familiar turn, fallen beech, and thorn hedge between the wood and the field below her cottage. The path reaches a stile; downhill from it lies a badger hole where she has fetched windblown washing in daylight, reaching an arm through but never crawling through herself. At dusk, her lantern shows one little piece of the shortcut at a time. Something moves when she moves, just beyond that piece.
2. Her kitchen window shines ahead. Its yellow curtain has the familiar triangular tear, and a spoon taps her blue cup. She calls "Who's there?" before noticing she has not reached the beech: her cottage should be uphill beyond the field. She lives alone and left it dark. A branch crosses the apparent window without interrupting its light.
3. She retreats. The window goes dark, then reappears between nearer trunks. Her lantern catches a pale, jointed limb supporting it and gripping a tree fork; it must wrench free to turn after her. Her question returns in her own voice. The window physically moves across the route to the stile. The ground has not rearranged: the hunter is intercepting her.
4. Tessa leaves the path, gambling that she can find and fit through the hole downhill. She keeps the lantern burning and low; its light cannot show both the roots at her feet and the branches above. The pale limb grips successive forks, drawing the lit window alongside her. What carries it remains largely hidden. The spoon taps although the pictured cup tilts. The counterfeit home is no longer persuasive, but its impossible domestic noises accompany the pursuit.
5. The gap is too low to judge from above; Tessa must lie down with the approaching window behind her in the wood. She pushes the lantern through and commits herself headfirst. Her shoulders stick until she turns onto her side and forces them through. Now her head and shoulders are in the field, her hips beneath the hedge, and her legs still in the wood. A pale hooked end descends behind her on the wood side and catches the loose back of her coat. Pulled backward, she releases its fastening and drags herself forward out of it, drawing her legs through last. As the limb jerks the empty coat upward, the window and its concealed bearer strike the upper hedge; the thicker stems bend and hold for the moment. She snatches up the lantern and runs while that obstruction buys seconds. Reaching grass supplies distance, not immunity; she does not look back to establish whether it can follow.
6. At her actual cottage Tessa locks the door and extinguishes the lantern so she can see out. She cannot bring herself to light the kitchen lamp: she has been fleeing that exact bright square. Through the real window she sees the counterfeit still shining at the hedge, her abandoned coat now hanging beside it. Keep the physical garment as the final disturbance, without interpreting it as improvement or revealing why the hunter waits.

The possibility to protect is familiar safety becoming a hunting lure, then remaining frightening after its deception fails. The chief risk is a neatly prearranged escape or a decorative last image. Tessa's remembered gap is uncertain, the hedge is a temporary physical obstacle, and the closing light contaminates her experience of her real home.
