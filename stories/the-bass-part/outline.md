# The Bass Part

## Promised experience

A witch really did acquire an elephant familiar, an exception to the prompt’s list of raven-, cat-, spider-, and dog-sized companions. “So tell me” invites a good story straight from somebody who was involved. “How did you manage” promises surprise about the way it happened. The pleasure I take from it is the mismatch in scale becoming a particular affinity, with room for physical comedy, an elephant’s capabilities, and a frankly wondrous piece of witchcraft. The payoff should let us recognize why this witch and this elephant would choose each other again. The question leaves the teller, mechanism, setting, and stakes open.

## Takes considered

- **A road in the air.** A rockfall cuts an ancient game trail. A witch and a matriarch walk out over the break together, the witch laying the spell and the elephant giving the vanished road its shape through the soles of her feet. The scene to turn to is the first full-weight step over open space.
- **The familiar’s side.** A free elephant who regularly slips away to listen to town music auditions witches in a nearly wordless tale from her perspective. The hinge is her refusing a parade musician’s offered fruit and seeking out the woman who stopped playing in order to listen.
- **The bass part.** A sound witch intent on performing a solo across a whole basin finds that a visiting elephant has a part of her own. In the long scene the two have to invent a way to hear and answer each other while the fair waits. I’d take this one for the friction between two performers, the literal reach of their shared magic, and the chance to watch the elephant insist on the partnership that supposedly took so much managing.

## Provisional direction

Neris makes her living sending sounds from one spot to another, as far as she can hear. Tonight at Whitewheel she means to ring a little bell and make the entire dry basin ring back. Mallow, a full-grown elephant who has walked to the fair ahead of her herd, keeps spoiling her rehearsals with a disconcertingly low note. Mallow can feel the herd calling beyond the far lip of the basin and wants a reply they can locate. Once Neris realizes what Mallow is listening for, Neris has the skill to send her voice, and Mallow has the hearing to give that spell a reach Neris never dreamed of. In this local practice a witch and her familiar can use each other’s hearing by mutual invitation. Neris gives up her solo and invites Mallow to do this together. Mallow seals the invitation herself, gives the next call on her own timing, and gets a reply. The next day she calls for Neris.

## Craft plan

- **Teller and voice.** Try Neris telling it firsthand, with the quick, expansive patter of a person who has sold an echo to somebody who made their own. She enjoys letting the story catch her out. Try the same afternoon in close third, where her frantic attention to the crowd and her bell leaves room for the reader to notice Mallow’s design before she does.
- **Neris.** She wants the fair to remember her name. She knows how to place a sound in a crowded space and assumes the elephant is there for windfalls. She can improvise all day for a customer and hates being heard making a mistake. At first she wants Sefra to keep the slot for her and Mallow to move. Once she understands whose note she has been talking over, she wants Mallow to make music with her again, including after tonight. Her bargaining sentences keep acquiring particulars until she has risked saying more than she meant to.
- **Mallow.** Curious, particular, and willing to wait most people out. A pear stem goes round and round in her trunk before she bothers to eat. She knows the sound of her family through the ground and what Neris can do to a nearby sound. At first she wants the witch to quit distracting her. After Neris hands her note back to her from another part of the basin, she keeps returning to Neris to ask for something farther. Her turns are changes of attention or position, a planted foot and its interval of stillness, the low call and how she varies it. No supplied or established characters.
- **Sefra, the fair caller.** Knows whose supper is cooling and how long a standing crowd will wait. She wants Neris, whom she put forward for the slot, to give her something she can promise aloud. Starts out assuming the elephant can be fed and moved. Speaks in deadlines, provisions, and names of people she expects to be obeyed. She can handle complaints for Neris, but needs Neris to trust her with a request that sounds ridiculous.
- **Setting.** Whitewheel’s open basin beats the cramped East Clapper bell shop, where the premise would be mostly getting through the door, and the windy Reedstep caravan field, where a performance and a distant answer would compete with too much traffic. A dry basalt basin rimmed with pale clay, at the end of a hot summer day and again the following morning. Potters cool their wares on the rim. The gold rims of cooling bowls, lime-white dust, bitter kiln smoke, warm stone under Neris’s palms, and the dry tick of wares cooling. A peculiar shelf of exposed rock runs uninterrupted from below the fair to the far lip. The fair keeps trying to time the echo off an air-facing wall. Mallow puts her feet down on that shelf and listens in a direction that the paying audience never faces.

## Scene briefs

1. **The warm upper rim, late afternoon (20%).** Neris shows Sefra her trick on a small scale to keep her place for dusk. Sefra knows how short a slot she can arrange. Mallow wanders right through the listeners and interrupts a note with a rumble that Neris sends back from beside the empty pear cart. Mallow abandons the fruit and searches out the witch. Neris assumes the next trick will get her to move again. The pear stem turning in the trunk before the animal will look up.
2. **The bare shelf below the fair, dusk (65%, long scene).** Sefra wants a start time she can announce and Neris wants one more minute. Mallow repeatedly orients toward the far rim, walks off from Neris’s attempts to redirect her with bell and pear, then comes back to make another bid. Neris follows her down. One of the far calls is high enough to hear in air, briefly, and Neris hears Mallow’s reply die below the shelf. The owed exchange happens here. Neris has to say what she wants from this elephant and what she’s offering her, aloud and in words that cost her something. She offers Mallow her bare hand and pulls it back after their first touch, giving Mallow the choice of reaching again. Once Mallow does so, they each pick up sounds at the other’s distance. Neris hears the separate callers in the soles of her feet and lets Mallow set the timing. Sends the first call from under the feet of the herd at the far lip. The herd stops, swings toward them, and calls back in voices that the fair finally hears. Stay in Neris’s surprise at recognizing one animal in the answer before she can give it a name. Give Sefra time to decide what to do with a crowd waiting to hear a bell.
3. **The same rim, next morning (15%).** Neris has made her first little sound for a customer. Mallow, now back with her family, reaches for Neris from beyond the far rim. Neris had half expected the elephant to have got all she came for. A question from the fair side about the elephant invites the account. Neris answers the question and then sends an answer of her own out to where Mallow is listening. She goes out by the long way that can take a whole elephant. Leave the history of the fair and ordinary familiar-making offstage.

The ending turns the first misplaced sound and Mallow’s repeated returns into an invitation from somewhere Neris could once have sworn was silent. A whole elephant is still going to want company, and can now ask for it across the basin.
