# The Bass Part

## Promised experience

A witch really did acquire an elephant familiar, an exception to the prompt’s list of raven-, cat-, spider-, and dog-sized companions. “So tell me” invites a good story straight from somebody who was involved. “How did you manage” promises surprise about the way it happened. The pleasure I take from it is the mismatch in scale becoming a particular affinity, with room for physical comedy, an elephant’s capabilities, and a frankly wondrous piece of witchcraft. The payoff should let us recognize why this witch and this elephant would choose each other again. The question leaves the teller, mechanism, setting, and stakes open.

## Takes considered

- **A road in the air.** A rockfall cuts an ancient game trail. A witch and a matriarch walk out over the break together, the witch laying the spell and the elephant giving the vanished road its shape through the soles of her feet. The scene to turn to is the first full-weight step over open space.
- **The familiar’s side.** A free elephant who regularly slips away to listen to town music auditions witches in a nearly wordless tale from her perspective. The hinge is her refusing a parade musician’s offered fruit and seeking out the woman who stopped playing in order to listen.
- **The bass part.** A sound witch intent on performing a solo across a whole basin finds that a visiting elephant has a part of her own. In the long scene the two have to invent a way to hear and answer each other while the fair waits. I’d take this one for the friction between two performers, the literal reach of their shared magic, and the chance to watch the elephant insist on the partnership that supposedly took so much managing.

## Provisional direction

Tulla makes her living picking up a sound and making it come from somewhere else. She has to be able to hear something at the spot where she wants to send it. Tonight at Whitewheel she means to ring a little bell and make the whole dry basin ring back. Mallow, a full-grown elephant who has come to the fair ahead of her herd, interrupts her rehearsals with a disconcertingly low note. Mallow feels her family calling through the ground and wants to reach them before they pass out of the basin. Tulla can send her voice, and Mallow can hear far enough to guide it. In this local practice a witch and her familiar grant each other the use of their hearing when they choose each other. Tulla gives up her solo to ask Mallow to do this with her. Mallow takes up the offer, calls the herd on her own timing, and then makes a separate bid for Tulla’s company while her family is in plain sight.

## Craft plan

- **Teller and voice.** Try Tulla’s firsthand account to Sefra, with the expansive patter of a person who has sold an echo to somebody who made their own. She enjoys letting the story catch her out. Try the same afternoon in close third, where her frantic attention to the crowd and her bell lets the reader notice Mallow’s design before she does. Sefra sees the results from the fair. She misses the exchange on the shelf and is the natural person to ask about it the next morning.
- **Tulla.** She wants the fair to remember her name. She can place a sound in a crowded space and assumes the elephant is there for windfalls. She can improvise all day for a customer and hates being heard making a mistake. At first she wants Sefra to keep the slot and Mallow to move. Once she understands whose note she has been talking over, she wants Mallow to make music with her again, including after tonight. Her bargaining sentences acquire particulars until she has risked saying more than she meant to.
- **Mallow.** Curious, particular, and willing to wait most people out. A pear stem goes round and round in her trunk before she bothers to eat. She wants to catch up with her family and takes the fair for a pleasant detour. Once Tulla hands her note back to her from a different place, she asks Tulla to try something new. Her turns are changes of attention or position, a planted foot and its interval of stillness, the low call and how she varies it. No supplied or established characters.
- **Sefra, the fair caller.** Knows whose supper is cooling and how long a standing crowd will wait. She vouched for Tulla and wants a show she can promise out loud. She thinks the elephant can be fed and moved. Speaks in deadlines, provisions, and names of people she expects to be obeyed. Tulla needs Sefra to stop the noise of the fair and turn the crowd to face the basin, even if she cannot promise what they will hear.
- **Setting.** Whitewheel’s open basin beats the cramped East Clapper bell shop, where the premise would be mostly getting through the door, and the windy Reedstep caravan field, where a performance and a distant answer would compete with traffic. A dry basalt basin rimmed with pale clay, at the end of a hot summer day and again the following morning. At the annual Clay Fair, potters knock samples with their knuckles to sell their wares. Gold glaze on warm clay, white dust, bitter kiln smoke, a fine note from sound bowls, and the hot stone under Tulla’s palms. Elephants use the basin as a passage. The fair keeps a wide ramp clear for the occasional visitor. An unbroken shelf of exposed rock runs about two miles from below the fair to the far lip. The human audience arranges itself in front of the nearer wall for its echo. Mallow puts her feet on the shelf, listening the other way.

## Scene briefs

1. **The warm upper rim, late afternoon (20%).** The neighboring act is a witch whose familiar can hear the tap of a pot behind a shutter. The cat has to come out of the shade and offer to listen, something Tulla has seen the pair do often. Sefra suggests a raven or a cat that might serve Tulla for years. Tulla wants to show what she can do with her own ears. Mallow interrupts Tulla’s rehearsal with two short grunts that Tulla mischievously returns from beside the pear cart. Mallow passes up the fruit, swivels around, and leans in for an encore. Tulla gives it to her and sneaks in a variation for the pleasure of making Mallow turn her head again.
2. **Fair edge and bare shelf, dusk (65%, long scene).** The fair starts to ring people to their places. Mallow reaches over Tulla’s head and holds the fair bell’s clapper with her trunk until the ringing dies. Tulla follows her down the ramp. From the shelf she can see dust at the far saddle where the herd is filing out. Mallow plants a foot, goes still, and lets out a sustained, growing rumble quite unlike their two little notes at the pear cart. Tulla cannot hear an answer. She sends Mallow’s next call as far as the wall where she can hear her own echo. Mallow keeps listening the other way. Tulla calls up from the foot of the ramp to Sefra at the bell, where the closest rows can hear. She calls off her advertised solo and asks Sefra to stop the pot-tapping and turn the audience toward the far rim. Sefra hushes the fair, because this time Tulla is willing to ask in front of the people she might disappoint. Below them, the owed exchange. Tulla has to ask Mallow aloud to be her familiar, say she wants to be called for herself, and offer her own ears in return. She pulls her hand back after the first touch so Mallow can reach for it on her own. Tulla feels the herd through her soles and waits on Mallow’s breath. Together they put the next long call under the herd’s feet. Far off the files swing back toward the shelf. Linger as Tulla hears a particular voice through the stone and begins to recognize a caller by sound. The approaching animals’ trumpets ring through the fair and make the crockery hum. Now Mallow can go and join them. She turns her full attention back to Tulla, gives her the two short notes they played with at the pear cart, and waits through Tulla’s surprise for Tulla’s own answer.
3. **The same rim, next morning (15%).** Sefra wants to hear how Tulla got an elephant for a familiar. Mallow is back with her family. Out beyond the far rim the two-beat sound comes again, from somewhere Tulla could once have sworn was silent. Tulla hears it through the stone while Sefra hears only the answer Tulla sends. Tulla takes the wide ramp to meet Mallow. Leave the fair’s history and the many uses of ordinary familiar magic offstage.

The last call has the reach of the great performance, and the intimacy of Mallow turning back for a private greeting while her family approaches. Tulla can give her an answer the size of the landscape and now has good reason to leave the fair by the elephant ramp.
