# Opus review: The Last Day of the Watch

Independent audit for a later editing pass. It is not a `review.md` verdict,
does not change canon state, and does not authorize edits by itself.

## Verdict

- Dialogue: REVISE — two Ground failures: Osmund's first answer about Kostral doesn't answer the question, and the gate-rush plan ignores the key he has just mentioned. The rest mostly works.
- Prose: PASS — concrete, varied and restrained; only small staging slips and one ambiguous pronoun in the last sentence.
- Continuity and prompt: PASS — the prompt is fully delivered; one wording inconsistency about whether the chambers will be opened or only inspected.
- Edit scope: targeted-dialogue
- Existing review.md said: Verdict PASS, Dialogue: PASS; I mostly agree, but it missed two Major Ground slips.

This story's dialogue is better than the collection default. It has three voices you can tell apart: Phelnis interrogates in short commands and questions with an arrow on the string. Osmund pleads, complains, bursts out and lowers his voice again. Kostral praises and instructs in the calm voice of an old teacher, even while shooting. The terse texture is earned by the situation, and the bait exchange before the final shot is the best dialogue in the story. The failures are local: a few lines are there for effect or momentum rather than as replies to what the listener has just heard. Examples are Osmund's teaser answer at first contact, a plan that forgets the locked gate, and two quips where a frightened man would just answer. The edit pass should fix those lines one at a time and leave the rest of the spare rhythm alone.

## Edit guardrails

- Prompt promise: the horned elves choose one archer each century, the new guardian finds fresh footprints leading inside, and she realizes someone has lived there for years. That realization currently comes through the blanket, beans, patched shoe and "Thirteen years." It must survive intact.
- Close third on Phelnis, past tense; broadly accessible; non-graphic danger and death; 2,500–4,000 words (currently about 3,400). Craft profile prospective-2026-08-23.
- Reference-image traits in the prose: blue-gray skin, pale hair, swept dark horns, black scale armour, and an ornate bow with dull gold carving. Keep them.
- Story-local facts other lines depend on: Kostral served a hundred years and leaves at dawn with an escort. There is a single causeway gate that is locked at night, and the key is on Kostral. The city is enclosed by clefts. Osmund has been there thirteen years and has hidden food caches ("the other five"). The alarm whistle's three notes are set up in the first line and paid off twice. The name carved over the gate is a motif.
- No universe LOCKED or CANON entries are touched. Do not introduce water infrastructure in any rewrite.
- Kostral's early aphorism ("Good. Pretty things give you away.") is the story's one earned aphorism: it is in character for him and it foreshadows the gold. Keep it, and don't add more.

## Dialogue findings

### D1 · Major · First-contact question and answer don't meet

- Where: L61–L71, Osmund on the stair, before he shows her the tomb
- Text:
> “Why does he keep you here?”
> “Because I was quicker than the others.”
> “What others?”
- Problem: Ground/uptake plus a teaser patch. So far Osmund has only said that Kostral "knows I'm somewhere" and that he wants the gate opened. Phelnis's "keep you here" assumes Kostral is deliberately holding him, which nothing has told her. Osmund's reply doesn't answer that question. "Quicker than the others" explains why he is still alive, not why Kostral keeps him. It is an answer to the author's secret (the murders), worded as a hook so she will ask "What others?". Neither character registers the mismatch. This is the exchange that first makes Kostral a threat, so the step in logic has to hold.
- Fix: Make her question follow from what he has said: he wants Kostral to open the gate but is hiding from him. Then make his reply answer that question plainly and let "What others?" follow naturally. Keep "I can show you. It's close. Then you'll take me out." and the "Please" beat unchanged. Illustrative only: “Then why not ask him to open it yourself?” / “He'd shoot me. Same as the others.”

### D2 · Major · Gate-rush plan ignores the key Osmund just mentioned

- Where: L185–L207, cache room window, planning the run for the gatehouse
- Text:
> Locked every night. Key on him.
> “If we get through that door first,” she said, “we can bar him out. The gate handles are inside.”
> “He'll be waiting.”
- Problem: Ground/knowledge. Eight lines earlier Osmund says the gate is locked every night and the key is on Kostral. It is already evening. Phelnis's plan assumes they can reach the gate handles and open the gate. Osmund, who knows the gate best, objects only that Kostral will be waiting. Later (L333–L339) she really does need Kostral's keys before "the gate finally moved", so the plan as stated could not have worked, and neither character noticed. The rush is the story's turning point, so the reader should be able to trust its logic.
- Fix: Give the plan a goal that doesn't need the key, or have one of them deal with the key in a single clause. The cleanest option is to hold the gatehouse until the escort arrives at dawn; that fits what she knows (L15) and keeps "He'll be waiting." / "I just saw him. He's still north of us." as they are. Illustrative only: “If we get through that door first, we can bar him out and hold it till the escort comes.”

### D3 · Minor · "Halfway across the city" doesn't match the trail shown

- Where: L47–L49, first exchange after he comes down
- Text:
> “I put those prints halfway across the city. I thought you'd walked past.”
> The footprints left the avenue through a broken doorway.
- Problem: Ground/referent. The trail on the page is one line of prints from beyond the first fallen column, north up the avenue and through a doorway to his room. "Halfway across the city" suggests a long or scattered trail the reader never saw. It also makes the reader wonder how a man hiding from a patrolling killer laid fresh prints on the main avenue. The line is where the prompt's footprints are explained, so it should be exact.
- Fix: Match the line to the trail that was shown, and if possible tie the timing to Kostral being away on his wall circuit (L15). Keep "I thought you'd walked past."; it is natural and good.

### D4 · Minor · "You think that." exists to tee up the retort

- Where: L81–L85, brushwood at the tomb mouths
- Text:
> “You think that.”
> “I wasn't going to wait underneath and find out.”
- Problem: Punchline patch (mild). "You think that." is an unnatural, clipped way to doubt someone, and it mainly serves to set up Osmund's comeback. The comeback itself carries a real point: this is why he came out today. "Underneath" is also vague; it means inside the tombs behind the brushwood.
- Fix: Give Phelnis a real question about his evidence so his reply answers it, and replace "underneath" with a word the reader can place. Illustrative only: “Did you see him light any?”

### D5 · Minor · Rabbit line has no visible reason

- Where: L189–L197, cache room, between the provisions talk and Kostral appearing
- Text:
> “He said he hunted rabbits for food.”
> “So do I.” Osmund tried to straighten his wounded leg and stopped.
- Problem: Inert exchange. We can't tell why Phelnis brings this up while Kostral is hunting them. It doesn't contradict "At the far end of the causeway", so it doesn't test Kostral's story, and it interrupts the escape planning. Osmund's answer is useful: the pigeons, the orchard on the west roofs and hoarding before winter show how someone survives thirteen years. It just needs a question that actually prompts it.
- Fix: Trigger the survival details from something she can see (the baskets or the bitten pear), or move the exchange up to when they first enter the food room (L171–L173). Then delete "He said he hunted rabbits for food." or change it so the reply follows it.

### D6 · Minor · Osmund answers practical questions with quips

- Where: L149–L153 (behind the coffin, under fire) and L177–L179 (food room)
- Text:
> “It comes through,” he whispered. “Go.”
> “Anywhere that isn't here.”
> “Outside a room. Outside a tomb. Always another wall after it.”
- Problem: Epigram / house-voice drift. She asks whether they can get outside from here. A bleeding, frightened man who knows every hole he has cut would say no and say why. Instead he gives a three-part aphorism, which is the only place his voice turns into the collection's neat register. The narration and his north-face story (L181–L183) already show there is no way out. "Anywhere that isn't here" is milder and can pass as impatience, but he knows exactly where the passage goes (his cache room), and "It comes through" is an odd verb with no destination.
- Fix: Replace L179 with a plain answer based on his knowledge of the passages, then go straight into "I tried the north face." Illustrative only: “No. Every hole I've cut comes out inside the walls.” For L149–L153, "It goes through" at the least; letting him name the room is optional.

## Prose findings

### P1 · Minor · Last sentence's "She" has two possible referents

- Where: L363, escort arrival
- Text:
> The woman looked towards the gate.
> She put out her free hand, and he took it to steady himself.
- Problem: The last female subject named is "The woman", and Phelnis appears only as the object in "Behind Phelnis". "Free hand" points to Phelnis, who is holding the bow, but the mounted escort woman also has a free hand. The final gesture matters: it shows Phelnis choosing Osmund in front of her own people. The reader should not have to work out who it is.
- Fix: Name Phelnis in the final sentence. Change nothing else.

### P2 · Minor · Small staging slips

- Where: L111/L133, L147, L199, L201, L249, L273, L281
- Text:
> Someone was crossing the wall above the gallery.
> Kostral's voice carried easily from the western colonnade.
> almost hidden by the fallen lid
> He reached for the bitten pear, thought better of it, and put it in his pocket.
> Kostral appeared at the far end of the avenue.
> until Osmund dragged the door's fallen timber across their legs
> at the torn pocket holding half a pear
> Kostral still watched the statue through his northern slit. The corner of the guardroom hid his body.
- Problem: Each one is small, but they add up in a chase that relies on geography.
  - Kostral is on the wall above the gallery and then, seconds later, on the western colonnade across the avenue.
  - The lid "lay on wooden rollers" at L89 and is "fallen" at L147.
  - "Thought better of it" contradicts pocketing the pear.
  - "Far end" of an avenue she is looking south along reads as the gate end, yet she says he is "still north of us".
  - The timber goes across their legs, but later it blocks the doorway (L311).
  - One bite has become "half a pear".
  - She can see him watching even though his body is hidden.
- Fix: One-phrase repairs. Let the L111 sound be distant or unlocated, or make the colonnade reachable from where he was. Say "the lid" at L147. Have Osmund think better of eating the pear. Say "the north end of the avenue". Have the timber dragged across the doorway. Say "the bitten pear". Reduce what she sees at L281 to his outline at the slit, as at L307.

## Continuity and prompt findings

### C1 · Minor · Chambers "opened" vs outer seals "inspected"

- Where: L15, L81, L227
- Text:
> they would inspect the outer seals on the burial chambers
> He means to smoke me out before you open the chambers.
> Tomorrow we can open the chambers together.
- Problem: The handover is set up as an inspection of outer seals. That is why Kostral's resealed fronts matter ("You'd never know from the avenue"). Osmund then speaks of the chambers being opened, which would expose the theft and which he has no clear way of knowing about. Kostral's line can stand as a lie to lure her. Osmund's cannot.
- Fix: Change Osmund's timing to something he could know from watching the gate (for example "before he leaves" or "before the escort comes"). Either leave Kostral's line as an obvious lure, or change it to the seal inspection so all three lines agree.

## Recurring patterns

- Quip in place of an answer: 3 instances (L153, L179, and the setup at L83), all from findings D4 and D6. Once these are fixed, sweep the rest of Osmund's lines for the same move.
- Questions from unearned premises: 2 instances (L65 in D1, L203 in D2). When a Phelnis question or plan is revised, check it against what she has actually been told.
- Interrogation alternation at L37–L75: a long run of short alternating lines. It is carried by the arrow, the evasions and the repeated "How long?", so it is not a defect. Don't pad it or make it more even.
- Small-object and position continuity: 7 slips grouped under P2.

## Preserve

- The mentor setup, which is ironic once the gold turns up:
> “Good. Pretty things give you away.”
- Osmund giving his name and then, when she doesn't move, "Please." (L75).
- The tomb exchange from "He shot them there." to "We were thieves. There. Can we go now?" (L93–L109), including the outburst and the lowered voice.
- The narration at L141 that separates suspicion from fact:
> She could still be wrong about everything except the second arrow striking beside her fingers.
- The bandage beat: "He'll follow the blood." answered by her folding the coat under his leg, a reply made without words (L163–L167).
- "That's the ankle." (L183) and "Osmund bit her palm. She took it away." (L225).
- Kostral's justification. It is his one fuller speech and gives the antagonist something he values. It hooks into her name on the gate (L239–L241):
> Do you suppose I did it for the little line they'll carve over the gate?”
- "No." followed by "You couldn't fit." (L261–L265).
- The bait exchange, L287–L305, whole: "Call him again." / "She let that pass." / "That's open ground." "Yes." / "And this time you'll stop him?" / "I'll have a shot." / "Don't whistle. Just tell me when you're there."
- Working the key cord free "as though she might hurt him" (L333), and the turned-down bed.
- The final exchanges, which avoid explaining: "Did you get your pack?" "No." "Good. Don't go back for it." and "Kostral is dead. I shot him."

## Suggested edit order

1. D1: repair the first-contact question and answer, because that exchange establishes Kostral as the threat.
2. D2: fix the gate-rush plan so the turning point doesn't rely on a gate that Osmund has just said is locked.
3. C1 with D4: they touch the same brushwood exchange at L81–L85, so align "open the chambers" and replace "You think that." in one pass.
4. D3: make the footprints line match the trail on the page.
5. D6 and D5: clear Osmund's quips and give the rabbit/orchard exchange a trigger.
6. P1: name Phelnis in the last sentence.
7. P2: sweep the staging slips.
