# Outline

## Story

- Premise and central promise: Working title: The Last Day of the Watch. On the centennial handover, newly chosen horned elf archer Phelnis follows fresh footprints into a supposedly uninhabited ruined city. Osmund has survived there for thirteen years. He made the trail deliberately: her celebrated predecessor intends to leave tomorrow, and his hidden witness will not survive the departure. Deliver discovery becoming a dangerous pursuit through the civilization's tombs and broken ceremonial streets.
- Focal pressure or attachment: Phelnis has earned the hundred-year appointment through difficult shooting trials. She wants to belong among the archers whose names she learned as a child; Kostral is one of them. Her willingness to credit his account initially outweighs evidence from an evasive stranger.
- Counterforce or complication: Kostral used his secluded watch to rob sealed burial chambers, recruiting illicit excavators and killing them when their usefulness ended. Osmund helped loot the tombs before escaping into passages he had opened himself. Kostral has failed to find him, but controls the city's only usable gate. Retirement brings outside witnesses and an approaching inspection; he needs the survivor dead before handing over a clean city.
- POV, distance, and information limit: Close third past through Phelnis. Let readers doubt Kostral before she can accept that doubt. Osmund's account remains partial until his knowledge and Kostral's actions corroborate it; no access to either man's thoughts.
- Governing movement and time shape: One afternoon and night, ending with the retirement escort's arrival at dawn. Begin just after the formal greeting; expand investigation, the mistaken summons, and pursuit. Thirteen years enter through physical accumulation. Target 2,500–4,000 words.
- Speculative rule or ordinary-world constraint: These elves live long enough for a century of service; neither archer has infallible senses or supernatural arrows. Deep clefts surround the forest-grown citadel. Its surviving gatehouse can be locked and held by one archer. Hidden passages connect interior burial galleries, not the world outside. The original image supplies Phelnis's blue-gray skin, pale hair, turquoise eyes, swept dark horns, scale armor, ornate bow, and sunlit forest-stone atmosphere.
- Dialogue promise: A proud newcomer tries to question someone terrified of the authority she trusts; her predecessor expects deference from a successor he has already judged.
- Dialogic medium: Uneasy spoken questioning, an ordinary handover whistle, calls across cover, and visible gestures during pursuit.
- Dialogue engine: Osmund conceals his own theft until omissions damage his credibility. Phelnis's attempt to verify his story summons the danger. Kostral's familiar instructional manner keeps influencing her even after his first attack.

## Voice

- Narrative texture: Follow feet, interrupted sightlines, bow clearance, and small failures of expected order. Longer attention suits the inhabited hiding place; action changes paragraph pressure without becoming constant fragments.
- Conversational texture: Osmund digresses over concrete grievances and resents being hurried. Phelnis asks short, increasingly inconvenient questions. Kostral speaks easily while expecting compliance; pursuit permits unfinished and purely practical speech.
- Rhetorical ownership: Phelnis owns shooting judgments but misreads social confidence. Osmund has proprietary pride in passages and stolen objects, defensive humor, and little patience with reverence. Kostral uses praise and remembered service to claim authority; he listens chiefly for what others have discovered.
- Pressure behavior: Phelnis repeats a question when frightened. Osmund becomes louder when quiet would help. Kostral loses his patient teaching register when his quarry stops behaving predictably.
- Relationship movement: Osmund needs armed protection but cannot comfortably admit complicity. Phelnis wants Kostral's recognition and then needs him to stop hunting. Kostral wants her obedience without scrutiny. Failed questioning becomes costly dependence between people who still dislike parts of each other.
- Anti-default: Avoid a courteous disclosure followed by an agreed exception to the rules. Confidence in the wrong witness must alter the physical pursuit.

## Beats

1. Kostral leaves Phelnis near the gate while completing his final exterior circuit. Beyond the threshold, fresh prints interrupt undisturbed fallen pollen. Their patched sole differs from his boots. Following them, she finds a screened cooking niche, successive repairs to bedding, and vegetables planted in old ceremonial planters: sustained habitation, not a recent trespass.
2. Osmund emerges where she can see his empty hands. He wants her between him and the gatekeeper immediately. His familiarity with the buried city proves residence; his unwillingness to explain how he entered makes his accusation sound rehearsed. He shows the chamber where his companions died, with excavation tools and funerary gold still inside. He finally admits they came for treasure at Kostral's invitation.
3. Phelnis uses the agreed whistle to call her predecessor, believing his presence will settle the contradiction. Osmund bolts. Kostral's answering arrow wounds him; the next is meant to finish him. Phelnis drags him behind masonry and loses her defensible position. Kostral attempts to restore obedience while moving to cut off their retreat.
4. They retreat through burial galleries. Osmund's concealed passages explain how he remained unfound; their confinement within the citadel explains why hiding never became escape. Establish the locked gate and the gatekeeper's commanding sightline concretely. Phelnis sees stolen grave ornaments packed for Kostral's departure.
5. Their attempted rush toward the gate fails under Kostral's superior position. Phelnis must use the city differently from its keeper. Osmund reveals an excavation connecting two galleries behind the processional avenue. Passing her horns, armor, and bow through costs time; keep the geometry readable. He draws Kostral's attention by crossing a short exposed gap while she gains an unanticipated angle. Neither has a guaranteed safe role.
6. Phelnis waits for an actual opening and shoots to kill when Kostral turns his bow on Osmund. Her tracking, movement through cover, range judgment, and ability to hold aim under fear determine the outcome. Keep injury non-graphic and confirm the pursuit has ended.
7. Open the gate and establish Osmund's freedom before closing. The arriving retirement escort encounters the living witness and the dead keeper. Phelnis must face what she has done before beginning her century. Give the immediate aftermath room without a tribunal, institutional reform, collective conversion, or scripted final explanation.

## Continuity

- Canon used: The deep-time world's successive civilizations; living-agency limits; continuity and personhood protections; non-graphic audience boundary. Place this local fantasy history before terminal magical extinction, without identifying a terminal root.
- Boundaries and unknowns: Long elf lifespans, this watch, and this citadel are story-local proposals. The dead civilization's larger history remains unknown. No autonomous magic, water infrastructure, recovered extinct system, or connection to another ruin is implied.
