# Outline

## Story

- Premise and central promise: Newly chosen forest guardian Evradis follows fresh footprints into supposedly deserted ruins and discovers Osmereth, a living man who has spent many years reconstructing the vanished city in miniature. Tracking opens into a search for the ordinary house his model cannot place.
- Focal pressure or attachment: Evradis coveted a century among remnants older than anyone she knows. She expects her patience and accuracy to make them intelligible. A resident who knows the city intimately but cannot explain it to her is more compelling, and more frustrating, than untouched antiquity.
- Counterforce or complication: Osmereth remembers rooms, neighbors, and particular views more securely than distances. His model combines observation with childhood proportions. Forest growth conceals the smaller buildings; their languages have diverged. He wants to locate his childhood house without surrendering the details that may be misleading him.
- POV, distance, and information limit: Close third past through Evradis. Reveal Osmereth through behavior, material evidence, and gradually established words. Her guesses remain guesses until corrected; neither the narrator nor her tracking grants access to his memories.
- Governing movement and time shape: Three days: pursuit through progressively older habitation traces, difficult contact around the model, then an outward search beyond the monumental precinct. Target 2,500–4,000 words. Expand looking, walking, and two consequential attempts to communicate.
- Speculative rule or ordinary-world constraint: The horned forest elves appoint one archer every hundred years. Both participants are living, long-lived people; Osmereth remained after the city's abandonment. Neither has supernatural recall or translation. Bow, miniatures, and masonry are ordinary objects. One perimeter guardian cannot observe every inner courtyard.
- Dialogue promise: Someone prepared to challenge an intruder must discover what a city's resident is trying to show her. Curiosity, impatience, mistaken reference, and small pleasures survive their incomplete understanding.
- Dialogic medium: Unshared speech, a few recognizable ceremonial roots, drawn plans, pointing, models handled in view of both participants, and demonstrated routes.
- Dialogue engine: Questions about identity and intrusion initially receive answers about particular buildings. Establishing what each gesture refers to changes the search; correcting the model requires Osmereth to distinguish a remembered sight from a measured location.

## Voice

- Narrative texture: Patient, tactile, spatially alert. Follow impressions into tentative interpretations, then let fresh evidence interrupt them. Sunlight, broken sightlines, leaf movement, and surfaces carry scale.
- Conversational texture: Osmereth can be expansive in language Evradis cannot follow; her short questions are no more transparent to him. Repetition sometimes helps and sometimes irritates. Permit ordinary naming and visible amusement.
- Rhetorical ownership: Evradis owns bearings, animal traces, and the appointment's ceremonial language. Osmereth owns domestic particulars, corrections, affectionate exaggeration, and an impatience with monumental grandeur. Neither owns authoritative history. His laughter can precede her understanding.
- Pressure behavior: Evradis initially repeats herself more slowly, then learns to change the demonstration. Osmereth protects a model piece when misunderstood, grows voluble, and eventually tests her proposed alignment.
- Relationship movement: She wants his knowledge but dislikes displaying ignorance. He wants help finding a house without being treated as a lost old man. Exchanges move from mutual obstruction toward shared investigation; dependence remains specific.
- Anti-default: Avoid turning the discovery into a polished debate over permission to remain, followed by a meal or guardianship reform. The encounter should change what they can find.

He keeps returning to an unplaced little house and two curved marks; Evradis initially mistakes their reference to her horns. Drawing the marks above a horizon corrects that mistake.
5. Seek a high terrace where the forest no longer blocks the distant skyline. Osmereth knows an inner route; Evradis manages the overgrown approaches and judges exposed distances. From above, the two marks resolve into paired crags. Align that surviving feature with two verified buildings, rather than trusting the model's scale. His remembered house lies beyond the monumental precinct that generations of archers have called the ruins.
6. Follow the resulting bearing through trees to low foundations. Establish the house through several converging particulars: an unusual lintel, three unequal wall niches, and its view beneath the paired crags. Some remembered dimensions prove wrong. Let the final meaningful exchange occur while Osmereth demonstrates a former room and Evradis checks what remains. Finish within this discovered place on incomplete but supported recognition, allowing amusement or wonder without restoring the city or completing its history.
