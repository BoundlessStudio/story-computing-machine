# The Last Loop

“Today, you found out your mom is a retired magical girl” asks for the discovery in the child's present and makes the mother-child relationship its point of contact. “Magical girl” suggests the recognizable pleasures of transformation, a colorful fighting identity and feats with a signature style. “Retired” makes it worth finding out what she gave up and what she still wants. I read the promise as the jolt of seeing somebody familiar become improbable and then recognizing her more fully. The prompt leaves age, voice, stakes, tone, and the reason she quit open.

## Provisional direction

First person from Nola, a sixteen-year-old getting her first driving lesson. Start with the comedy of being stuck in the car with a mother who grips the door handle at fifteen miles an hour. Build toward a fast, exhilarating shared flight over the town. Mom has retired from rescue work and still has the skill to fly on an old ribbon of her own making. She has kept her first route coiled for years. Once ridden, it unravels behind the rider.

- Mom sends Nola up to the empty hillside lot where she herself learned to fly. Nola expects more drills. Mom pulls a thumb-wide pink ribbon out of a glasses case, flicks its end into the open air, and it writes an enormous curling track over town. She steps onto it and the ribbon dresses her in her old bright boots and star-lined jacket. Nola recognizes Comet Lace from the old footage of her catching a falling tram in a U of ribbon and pulling the curve into a level exit. The way she braces for the fall is the way Mom braces in the passenger seat.
- Mom wanted to mark Nola's permit by taking her to the place where she learned to go somewhere under her own power. She offers Nola the old practice course, which can guide a single rider safely home. Nola, who thought Mom's caution meant she'd never wanted to go fast, asks to see what Mom did on it for fun. Mom shows her where she added a ridiculous extra turn because she liked the view upside down. She admits she chose to quit after she realized she'd started flinching at the sound of her call signal. She kept the practice loop because she'd loved the part where she flew just to see what she could do. Nola can see she still wants the ride she's offering away.
- Nola asks to go with her. To make two handholds, Mom has to unpick her favorite loop for its length. Nola sees her pause with her thumb under the knot before pulling it free. The huge loop falls out of the air. Mom uses her old tram maneuver to carry them over the streets and turn their drop toward the lot. At the crest of the return curve she offers Nola a choice between a straight landing and one brief inversion. Nola recognizes the brace, hands her strand back on Mom's count, and leans into her. With both ends in her hands, Mom banks them together and Nola keeps her eyes open on the town upside down.
- Back at the car, the ribbon and its route are gone. Mom is delighted and winded. Nola offers her the keys. Mom goes around to the passenger side and puts her hands in her lap. There's still the drive home and Nola wants to hear about the tram from the person who caught it.
