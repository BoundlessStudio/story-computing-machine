# Prompt contract

## Verbatim writing prompt

> **[WP] Introducing a boyfriend or girlfriend to the family can be either very pleasant or very stressful. When you come from a lineage of necromancers, these feelings are amplified, as relatives who died decades ago might show up for a visit.**

## Story controls

- Working title: A Place for the Living
- Target length: 3,000–3,800 words
- POV: Close third person, centered on the living partner entering the family
- Tense: Past
- Tone and genre: Warm dark comedy with gothic domestic fantasy, mounting social
  pressure, and an emotionally sincere ending
- Audience/content rating: Teen; death and the visible dead are present, but no
  graphic gore, sexual content, or cruelty
- Required elements: A serious romantic partner is introduced to a necromantic
  family; the meal begins plausibly pleasantly and becomes increasingly
  stressful; at least one relative dead for decades attends in an active,
  consequential role; the supernatural complications expose an ordinary
  relationship fear; the central family conflict reaches a decisive resolution
  during the visit
- Prohibited elements: No “all necromancers are evil” framing; no resolution
  based solely on a convenient spell failure or villain defeat; no treating
  deceased relatives as disposable joke props; no contradiction of established
  shared-universe rules

## Assumptions

- The lineage may practice a local, household-scale form of necromancy only if
  canon research finds room for it; its limits and costs must remain consistent
  with the shared universe.
- The visiting partner already knows the family practices necromancy, so the
  dramatic pressure comes from intimacy and family judgment rather than a
  prolonged hidden-magic reveal.
- The story will stand alone without requiring knowledge of other stories.
- Romantic gender will be chosen during planning after the name registry and
  canon brief are checked; the relationship will be affectionate and
  established enough that this introduction matters.

## Completion tests

- The result is a complete short story, not a synopsis or chapter fragment.
- A living partner meets the necromantic family in a sustained on-page visit,
  and a relative dead for decades materially changes the encounter.
- The ordinary emotional question—whether the partner has a place in this
  family—is answered by a costly choice, not merely by reassurance.
- The pleasant and stressful sides of the introduction both appear, with
  escalating social and supernatural complications.
- The story passes both draft and final canon/continuity review gates.
- Every character-facing name and alias is checked against `stories/NAMES.md`;
  any deliberate reuse has a documented narrative reason.
