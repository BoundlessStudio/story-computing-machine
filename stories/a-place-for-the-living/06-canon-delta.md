# Proposed canon delta

This file proposes reusable shared-universe facts. It is not canon until the
user explicitly approves promotion and the facts are added to `universe/`.

## New characters or character facts

All facts in this section are **proposed, local to the unnamed Morrow
household, and non-canon**:

- **Imani Crowe:** A practical assessor of old houses and Greer Morrow’s
  girlfriend of eighteen months. She is the daughter of an unnamed late mother
  whose ginger-and-pear cake recipe Imani still makes. At her first dinner with
  Greer’s family, Imani refuses both an undisclosed ancestral audition and the
  coerced continuation of a dead guest’s visit.
- **Greer Morrow:** Imani’s conflict-avoidant girlfriend and Davina Morrow’s
  daughter. Greer was raised as the expected successor to the Morrow family
  table, house, invitation records, and table key. She concealed the planned
  partner vetting and the house-based duties from Imani, then publicly refused
  succession and surrendered the key rather than accept those coercive terms.
- **Davina Morrow:** Greer’s living mother, host of the dinner, current
  custodian of the Morrow table, and sole magical anchor of Aunt Zephra’s visit.
  Her real hospitality and care for family records coexist with controlling
  behavior shaped by grief and fear that an incoming partner will abandon the
  practice. She has turned an older hospitality custom into a partner-vetting
  test and, during this dinner, refuses a dead guest’s request to depart.
- **Aunt Zephra Morrow:** Greer’s deceased great-great-aunt, dead for
  seventy-three years at the time of the dinner. She married into the Morrow
  family—“by invitation, not by blood”—helped shape the kept-place supper as
  hospitality, and rejects its use as a test of incoming partners. She is the
  same continuing person before, during, and after the visit; she remembers,
  chooses, jokes, grieves, refuses a verdict, and requests release, but she
  cannot cast, sustain, or dismiss the living-supplied working.
- Greer’s living father and Imani’s late mother remain unnamed. No reusable
  personal names or aliases are proposed for either person.

## New locations

- **Proposed local, non-canon location:** an unnamed Morrow family house in an
  unnamed modern-like, low-magic era. Its dining room contains the family table,
  indexed invitation records, personally associated objects retained for dead
  relatives, and the prepared settings used for kept-place suppers.
- **Proposed local, non-canon custodial fact:** possession of the house is tied
  to care of the family table. The person who succeeds as custodian is expected
  to live in the house. The story does not name the house, its settlement, its
  world-era coordinate, or any wider necromantic institution.

## New factions or cultural facts

- **Proposed local, non-canon family practice:** the Morrows discreetly preserve
  a household tradition called the kept-place supper. The story establishes
  inherited teaching, archives, objects, obligations, and custodianship; it
  does not establish genetic magic, bloodline destiny, exclusive biological
  access, or a global necromantic culture.
- **Proposed local, non-canon succession custom:** a brass table key symbolizes
  future authority over the house, invitation preparation, remembrance
  choices, and access to family visits. A person who gives up the key also gives
  up assured custodial authority and may attend future suppers only when the
  next custodian invites them.
- **Proposed local, non-canon historical fact:** Aunt Zephra helped develop the
  supper as hospitality for absent dead relatives: the living offered a place,
  the dead chose whether to accept, and a tired guest was allowed to leave.
- **Proposed local, non-canon change:** Davina converted that hospitable custom
  into a means of vetting Greer’s serious partner for marriage, residence in
  the Morrow house, and future custodial duty. Aunt Zephra rejects both the
  interview and the demand that she provide a verdict.

## New rules, capabilities, or costs

Every mechanism below is a **proposed rule of this one local Morrow household
practice, not a universal law of ghosts, names, necromancy, magic, consent, or
the afterlife**:

- **Visitor ontology:** a kept-place supper temporarily reaches the same
  continuing person who entered the universal afterlife at death. The visitor
  is not bodily resurrected, reconstructed from the anchor’s memories,
  projected as a copy, or removed permanently from the afterlife. The visitor
  returns as the same person when the visit ends.
- **Targeting:** a living practitioner writes the intended visitor’s familiar
  name on a place card, supplies a table object personally used by that person,
  and prepares a place at the Morrow table. The familiar name and object direct
  this practice toward the intended person; neither implies universal true-name
  power. The card, object, plate, table, and key are nonliving foci or symbols,
  never independent magic users.
- **Living agency:** a living anchor initiates, supplies, and sustains the
  entire manifestation. During the recorded dinner, Davina is the sole anchor.
  The dead visitor’s speech, movement, memory, judgment, and refusal are
  personal agency expressed through the living-supplied field, not spellcasting.
- **Acceptance and release:** the invited dead person may accept or refuse the
  initial invitation. Once manifested, the visitor may request release but
  cannot cast themself free; the living anchor may release the working, and
  physically breaking the prepared setting also ends its focus. Delaying
  release after an unambiguous request is possible for an unwilling living
  anchor and is treated as an ethical abuse of the practice, not as permission
  to disregard consent.
- **Range and contact:** one dead guest manifests at a time, seated within
  arm’s reach of the prepared place. Leaning beyond that narrow range frays the
  manifestation. Through the anchored field the visitor can perceive the room,
  speak, gesture, touch offered hands, handle items within the setting, and
  experience offered tastes. The visitor cannot roam the house, cast magic, or
  independently manipulate objects beyond the supplied contact.
- **Duration and cost:** about twenty minutes is a comfortable visit. The
  living anchor pays in body heat and stamina; pallor, whitening breath, frost,
  trembling, and exhaustion intensify when the visit is prolonged. The story
  establishes no sacrifice, corruption, or universal magical price.
- **Frequency:** the same dead person may make no more than one visit between
  the Morrow family’s yearly remembrances. This is a limit of the household
  practice, not a general limit on ghosts or the afterlife.
- **Departure and interruption:** the visit ends when the anchor releases it,
  when the visitor is dismissed through the practice, or when the prepared
  place is broken. Turning the visitor’s plate face-down is a nonmagical
  physical interruption that collapses the focus; it is not a spell cast by
  Imani or by the plate.
- **Bounded visitor knowledge:** a visitor knows their own life, identity,
  afterlife experience, prior visits, and information sent as news with an
  invitation. A visitor does not automatically know living events, thoughts,
  the future, or the wider cosmology.

## Timeline events

- **Proposed local, non-canon event; undated within an unnamed modern-like
  era:** Imani attends her first dinner at the Morrow house to meet Greer’s
  parents. Davina invites Aunt Zephra to a kept-place supper without Imani’s
  informed agreement and redirects the visit into an ancestral interview about
  marriage, children, residence, and custodial duty.
- During the same dinner, Aunt Zephra refuses to judge Imani and asks to leave.
  Davina continues anchoring the visit past its comfortable duration. After
  confirming Zephra’s wish directly, Imani turns the moth-painted plate
  face-down and breaks the prepared focus, returning Zephra to the afterlife.
- Greer then refuses succession, places the table key beside Zephra’s butter
  knife, and accepts the loss of the house, custodial authority, and guaranteed
  future access to family visits. Imani does not immediately forgive her; the
  couple agrees only to a living-only breakfast and a full discussion of the
  future Greer concealed.
- Before they leave, Davina makes one limited change: if Imani returns, Davina
  will not set an extra place without asking first. The conflict remains only
  partially resolved.

## New glossary terms or aliases

- **Kept-place supper** — proposed, local, non-canon Morrow household term for
  a meal at which a living anchor prepares and sustains one place for a
  consenting temporary visitor from the afterlife.
- **Kept place** — proposed, local, non-canon related term for the prepared
  table setting and living-supplied focus used during such a supper. It is not
  a person-like title or character alias.
- No new character alias, global term for necromancy, named ritual, named house,
  faction name, or universal supernatural category is proposed.
