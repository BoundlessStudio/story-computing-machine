# Canon brief

## Hard constraints

- **Only life can use magic.** A deceased relative may speak, choose, remember,
  judge, refuse, and otherwise act as a person, but any summoning, anchoring,
  warding, dismissal, or magical alteration must come through a living agent.
  Nonliving portraits, heirlooms, table settings, or house fixtures may store,
  focus, transmit, or shape living-supplied magic; they may not independently
  use it. Do not assume an unusual being qualifies as alive.
  (`universe/rules.md` — `## Living agency and magic`)
- **The universal afterlife exists and ghosts are possible, but their
  relationship is system-specific.** The household mechanism must coherently
  distinguish a visiting dead person from reincarnation, reconstruction,
  projection, memory-copy, branch, or bodily resurrection.
  (`universe/rules.md` — `## Afterlife and continuation`;
  `universe/rules.md` — `## Memory, transformation, copies, and branches`)
- **“Necromancy” is a cultural label, not an objective cosmic category.** This
  family's practice must remain one bounded system rather than proof that all
  magic, technology, divinity, ghosts, or death-work share an origin or rules.
  (`universe/rules.md` — `## System categories`;
  `universe/glossary.md` — `## Magic, technology, and divinity`)
- **Cost, desire, and consent have no universal magical behavior.** Any price,
  fuel, invitation rule, or consent rule must be established as local and
  applied consistently. Ethical consequences still matter even when consent
  has no mechanical force. (`universe/rules.md` —
  `## Costs, desire, and local mechanisms`)
- **The dead retain moral and emotional standing.** The mechanism cannot erase
  the visitor's identity, relationships, grief, choices, or consent by reducing
  the person to only an error, appliance, or disposable joke.
  (`universe/style-guide.md` — `## Personhood and moral consequence`)
- **Names are not universally magical.** A name-keyed invitation is possible
  only as an explicit feature of this local system, not a general true-name
  law. (`universe/rules.md` — `## Names and identity binding`)
- If the story names Earth, Ravel, or the Glass-Sea world, they are eras of one
  physical world, not automatically separate planets or planes.
  (`universe/premise.md` — `## The deep-time shared world`;
  `universe/locations.md` — `## The world across eras`)
- Death and gothic unease must remain non-graphic and consequence-centered.
  The prompt's stricter Teen rating, ban on cruelty, and respect for deceased
  relatives also bind the story. (`universe/style-guide.md` —
  `## Audience and content boundaries`;
  `stories/a-place-for-the-living/00-prompt.md` — `## Story controls`)

## Useful established context

- Reality remains coherent even when characters hold incomplete or mistaken
  explanations. The living partner need not understand the larger cosmology,
  and the story can remain a standalone household-scale entry point.
  (`universe/premise.md` — `## Knowledge and connection`;
  `universe/style-guide.md` — `## Tonal range, scale, and connection`)
- Distinct supernatural systems may coexist, interact, imitate one another, or
  be unrelated. A narrow family practice therefore needs no global guild,
  species, source, or taxonomy of necromancers. (`universe/rules.md` —
  `## System categories`)
- If planning chooses the **modern-like era**, it is a low-magic trough where
  ordinary life generally treats magic as myth, though relic anomalies survive.
  A discreet household tradition fits more readily than openly routine,
  society-wide necromancy. This world resembles contemporary Earth but is not
  necessarily literal Earth. (`universe/timeline.md` —
  `## Established era order`; `universe/glossary.md` —
  `## Modern-like era`)
- Testimony degrades and may become myth or distortion. A decades-dead relative
  may offer valuable but partial evidence; age alone does not make the visitor
  infallible. (`universe/premise.md` —
  `## Memory, myth, and recoverable truth`; `universe/rules.md` —
  `## Records, falsehood, and historical traces`)
- No recurring character or culture is established in the topical character
  and faction notes. This is not proof none exist; it means no crossover or
  named organization is required. (`universe/characters.md` —
  `# Recurring characters`; `universe/factions.md` —
  `# Factions and cultures`)

## Conflicts or ambiguity

- **No authoritative contradiction was found. The story is not blocked on a
  canon ruling.** Ghosts are possible, and living practitioners can operate a
  local visiting system without violating the living-agency rule.
- **“Necromancy” is undefined in the glossary.** It carries no established
  assumptions about corpses, souls, resurrection, command of the dead,
  heredity, or morality. (`universe/glossary.md` — `# Glossary`)
- **Ghostly agency is not magical agency.** Canon does not decide whether a
  particular ghost is alive, whether ordinary ghostly movement counts as magic,
  or whether a living working supplies the manifestation through which the
  ghost acts. Resolve only this household case. (`universe/rules.md` —
  `## Living agency and magic`; `universe/rules.md` —
  `## Afterlife and continuation`)
- **Identity is mechanism-dependent.** Canon chooses no universal answer among
  soul, ghost, reconstruction, projection, copy, or branch. The local answer
  must be coherent and preserve personhood. (`universe/rules.md` —
  `## Memory, transformation, copies, and branches`)

## Unknowns

- **Unknown — era and place.** The prompt chooses no era. A local setting is
  safe, but it must not assert an unresolved Galactic Cycle coordinate or
  contradict the established era order. (`universe/timeline.md` —
  `## Universal chronology`; `universe/timeline.md` —
  `## Established era order`)
- **Unknown — public status.** Canon establishes no society-wide law, religion,
  medicine, acceptance, or secrecy around ghosts or necromancy. Keep attitudes
  household- or community-scale unless broader facts are proposed.
- **Unknown — inheritance.** “Lineage” does not establish genetic transmission,
  predestination, a curse, or exclusive blood access. Inherited teaching or
  custodianship is safest; biological or metaphysical inheritance would be a
  new reusable local fact.
- **Unknown — visitor state.** Canon does not say whether the dead retain
  bodies, age, sleep, eat, feel pain, stay conscious, know post-death events, or
  can be called against their will.
- **Unknown — reach and recurrence.** Range, frequency, duration, dismissal,
  number of visitors, and access after death are all undefined.
- **Unknown — price and failure.** No universal energy source, sacrifice,
  corruption, danger, emotional fuel, or failure model exists.
- **Unknown — institutions and species.** No necromancer faction, school,
  government, religious authority, caste, or undead species is established.
  Absence is invention room, not proof that none canonically exist.
- **Unknown — names and roles.** The prompt supplies no personal name, surname,
  alias, named house, title, or named ritual.

## Safe invention space

- Create one **household-scale visiting practice**. Define only the rules the
  plot needs: which living person initiates or sustains it, what medium focuses
  it, who can answer, how the visit ends, and one or two limits or costs. Frame
  these as this lineage's practice, not “the laws of necromancy.”
- Let the **living family supply all magic** while the deceased relative
  supplies choices and conversation. A household object may focus or store the
  living working. These are safe categories, not required story details.
  (`universe/rules.md` — `## Living agency and magic`)
- Choose one coherent relationship between visitor and afterlife. A genuine
  temporary visit or another clearly defined continuation may be introduced
  locally without contradicting canon, but neither is already established.
  Do not combine ontologies only to escalate jokes. (`universe/rules.md` —
  `## Afterlife and continuation`; `universe/rules.md` —
  `## Memory, transformation, copies, and branches`)
- Treat “lineage” primarily as family practice, custodianship, recipes,
  etiquette, and accumulated relationships with the dead. A narrowly inherited
  attunement is possible, but it must remain local and enter the canon delta.
- A specific local limit or price may be living effort, duration, preparation,
  scarce material, duty, or relationship consequence. The prompt's costly
  choice may be social or emotional rather than magical. Whatever is chosen
  must be causal and consistent. (`universe/rules.md` —
  `## Costs, desire, and local mechanisms`;
  `stories/a-place-for-the-living/00-prompt.md` — `## Completion tests`)
- Family etiquette, domestic objects, grudges, recipes, heirlooms,
  anniversaries, burial customs, and private vocabulary are safe local
  additions. Prefer one home and family over a global institution.
- The dead relative may change the resolution through testimony, refusal,
  information, or a relational choice without becoming the magical operator.
- The viewpoint may remain uncertain, but production artifacts must maintain
  one coherent underlying mechanism. Character uncertainty is permitted;
  authorial contradiction is not. (`universe/premise.md` —
  `## Knowledge and connection`; `universe/style-guide.md` —
  `## Continuity and evidence discipline`)

## Required checks after drafting

- Verify every caster, sustainer, or supplier of magic is living and every
  nonliving focus only stores, shapes, transmits, or focuses that magic.
- State the production-level answer for what the visitor is and how they relate
  to the afterlife. Check arrival, departure, memories, physical interaction,
  and limitations against that mechanism.
- Confirm the deceased relative has personhood and consequential agency rather
  than serving as a disposable gag, exposition device, or magical appliance.
- Check invitation, targeting, range, duration, cost, material focus, number of
  visitors, interruption, dismissal, and failure behavior for consistency.
- Remove unsupported universal claims such as “all ghosts,” “necromancers
  always,” bloodline destiny, inherent evil, mandatory sacrifice, universal
  true names, or global necromantic authority.
- If modern-like, check that visibility fits a low-magic trough. Otherwise,
  avoid inventing an unresolved place in the master timeline.
- Audit dates, generations, living characters' ages, kinship terms, and the
  decades-dead visitor's knowledge. Post-death knowledge needs an explanation.
- Confirm the partner's fear of belonging is exposed by supernatural pressure
  and answered by a costly choice—not solely spell failure, villain defeat,
  reassurance, or discarding the dead.
- Run exact, alias, case-insensitive, surname-as-label, reversal, and
  close-spelling checks against `stories/NAMES.md` for every person-like name.
- Record the household mechanism, ontology, limits, costs, named practice, and
  any cultural claim in `06-canon-delta.md`, distinguishing local additions
  from proposed global rules.
