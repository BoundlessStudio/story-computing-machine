# Story plan

## Premise and promise

At her first dinner with girlfriend Greer Morrow's necromantic family, Imani
Crowe must decide whether to keep auditioning for a place in their future or
risk that future by ending the coerced visit of a long-dead relative.

**Story promise:** A warmly funny, increasingly painful family dinner makes the
supernatural problem inseparable from an ordinary romantic one: Imani does not
want acceptance she can earn only by surrendering her boundaries, while Greer
must show whether she will choose a living relationship over inherited family
approval.

**Central dramatic question:** When the Morrows turn dinner into an ancestral
test, will Imani keep performing for them or act on the conviction that both the
living and the dead deserve to choose whether they stay at the table?

## Story controls

- POV: Close third person through Imani Crowe
- Tense: Past
- Length budget: 3,200–3,650 words; hard contract range 3,000–3,800
- Tone/genre: Warm dark comedy, gothic domestic fantasy, mounting social
  pressure, sincere romantic/family reckoning
- Rating: Teen; visible dead, grief, coercive family pressure, and mild magical
  exhaustion; no graphic decay, cruelty, or sexual content
- Era/scale: An unnamed modern-like, low-magic era; one discreet family home,
  no public institution or global necromantic taxonomy
- Ending mode: Hopeful but earned. Imani and Greer do not solve their whole
  future in one night; they choose an honest next conversation after Greer
  takes a concrete, costly stand.

## Character engine

- **Imani Crowe — protagonist and visiting girlfriend.** Practical, observant,
  and dryly funny. She wants to be welcomed by the family of the woman she
  loves, but she also wants proof that Greer has imagined a shared future with
  her. Her private fear is that she is being evaluated for a role Greer has not
  honestly described. Her blind spot is treating composure as the safest form
  of courage: she would rather pass an unfair test than risk looking difficult.
  Movement: from performing gratitude to naming the breach and acting on a
  boundary even when it may cost the relationship.
- **Greer Morrow — Imani's girlfriend.** Loving, conflict-avoidant, and trained
  to anticipate her mother's disappointment. She told Imani the family
  practiced necromancy but withheld the ancestral interview and the expectation
  that Greer's eventual partner help maintain the family home. She wants both
  family blessing and Imani without admitting those wants may conflict.
  Movement: from strategic silence to a public refusal of the coercive custom,
  accepting loss of her expected custodial role and of privileged future
  visits.
- **Davina Morrow — Greer's living mother and dinner host.** The living
  initiator and sole magical anchor of the visit. Warm in ways that are real,
  controlling in ways she calls stewardship. She believes ancestral scrutiny
  protects the household from partners who romanticize its practice and leave
  when duty becomes inconvenient. She is opposition, not a villain: grief,
  fear of institutional loss, and love have hardened into entitlement.
- **Aunt Zephra Morrow — Greer's great-great-aunt, dead for seventy-three
  years.** A genuine temporary visitor from the universal afterlife. Witty,
  curious, and tired of being summoned as a seal of approval. She married into
  the Morrow family and helped shape the kept-place supper as hospitality for
  absent dead, not a trial for incoming partners. She cannot cast, sustain, or
  dismiss magic. Her consequential choice is to request departure and refuse to
  give the verdict Davina wants.
- **Greer's father — unnamed supporting presence.** Genuinely welcoming but
  accustomed to smoothing conflict instead of challenging Davina. Keep him a
  common relational description, never a capitalized or substitute proper
  name. He provides pleasant texture, then falls silent when the test begins.

**Stakes:** If Imani submits, she may gain surface approval while confirming
that Greer can conceal major expectations. If she resists, she may end the
relationship and become the person blamed for cutting short a rare visit. If
Greer supports her, Greer loses her assumed succession as keeper of the family
table and may forfeit future contact with beloved dead relatives.

## Causal arc

1. **Beginning state:** Imani arrives determined to be an easy, appreciative
   guest. Greer promises there will be “no surprises,” meaning no uncontrolled
   manifestations; Imani reasonably hears a broader promise.
2. **Inciting disruption:** At dinner Davina sets a fifth place and reveals
   that Aunt Zephra has accepted a formal invitation to meet Imani. Greer knew
   this was possible and did not warn her.
3. **Pleasant turn:** Zephra manifests as a coherent guest within the kept
   place. She enjoys Imani's food, jokes about the family's inflated ceremony,
   and makes the impossible encounter feel intimate rather than horrific.
   Imani decides she can absorb the surprise.
4. **Escalation:** Zephra's questions, initially affectionate, become an
   audition under Davina's direction: work, marriage, children, moving into the
   Morrow house, and sharing future anchor duty. The questions expose plans
   Greer discussed with Davina but not Imani. Davina keeps feeding the working
   despite visible exhaustion because Zephra refuses to endorse the match.
5. **Crisis:** Zephra explicitly asks for her place to be closed. Davina refuses
   until Zephra gives a verdict. Greer freezes. Imani realizes that earning this
   family's approval by staying polite would make her complicit in using a dead
   person and accepting an undisclosed future.
6. **Climax:** Imani asks Zephra directly whether she wants to go, receives an
   unambiguous yes, and turns Zephra's plate face-down—the physically simple,
   socially taboo act that breaks the prepared focus. It does not cast magic;
   it interrupts Davina's living-supplied working. Imani accepts that this may
   end the visit, the dinner, and her relationship. Before the manifestation
   closes, Zephra refuses a verdict and reminds the room that a kept place was
   meant to welcome guests, not keep them.
7. **Decisive family turn:** Davina declares that anyone who rejects the table's
   rules cannot inherit its care. Greer finally answers in public: she will not
   inherit a practice that requires ambushing her partner or detaining a guest.
   She leaves her inherited key on the table and accepts the loss rather than
   demanding immediate forgiveness from Imani.
8. **Aftermath:** Outside, Greer apologizes specifically for her deception.
   Imani does not reward one brave act with instant certainty; she agrees only
   to a living-only breakfast where they will discuss the house, marriage, and
   duty honestly. Davina returns Imani's cake tin at the door and says that, if
   Imani comes again, no extra place will be set without asking. It is a narrow
   concession, not full absolution. Imani leaves with Greer, having refused an
   assigned place while making room for a possible chosen one.

## Setup and payoff

- Greer's “no surprises” promise is affectionate shorthand in scene 1 and the
  exact language Imani returns to when the concealed test is exposed.
- The wrapped fifth plate first reads as gothic family clutter, becomes the
  invitation focus, and is turned face-down in the climax.
- Davina tells Imani that a guest never rearranges a Morrow place setting. Imani
  complies while trying to please, then knowingly breaks that etiquette to
  release Zephra.
- Greer's inherited table key is handled whenever she defers to Davina. Leaving
  it on the table makes her family choice materially costly.
- Imani's cake, made from her own late mother's recipe, gives Zephra a sensory
  pleasure transmitted by the anchored manifestation and lets the dinner begin
  with real warmth. The returned cake tin supports Davina's limited concession.
  Do not imply Imani's mother appears or that recipes are magical.
- Zephra's early correction—she is a Morrow “by invitation, not by blood”—seeds
  that the custom once admitted outsiders and prepares her refusal to act as a
  bloodline gatekeeper.
- Davina's cooling hands and whitening breath establish the anchor cost before
  it becomes dangerous; no new cost appears at the climax.
- Zephra twice glances at the face-down stack of cleared dessert plates before
  she asks to leave, making Imani's final physical action inferable rather than
  an unseeded spell solution.
- The title resolves on two levels: Imani refuses a place assigned by the dead
  and asks whether Greer can make a place for their living relationship.

## Canon constraints

- Only living Davina initiates, supplies, and sustains the working. Zephra's
  speech, memory, preferences, and refusal are personal agency expressed
  through the living-sustained manifestation, not spellcasting.
- The kept-place supper is one Morrow household system and “necromancy” is
  their cultural word. Do not claim universal necromantic laws, a shared source
  with other magic, or an objective magic category.
- Zephra is the same continuing person who entered the universal afterlife at
  death and temporarily answers an invitation. This local system reaches her;
  it does not reconstruct a memory-copy, resurrect her body, or remove her from
  the afterlife permanently.
- Local targeting and consent: a living Morrow writes the intended guest's
  familiar name on a place card and supplies a personally used table object.
  The object focuses the working; the dead person may accept or refuse. The
  name is not a universal true name.
- Local reach/frequency: the working is prepared at the family table; the
  invitee manifests only within arm's reach of the set place. One dead guest
  may answer at a time, and the same guest may be invited no more than once
  between yearly remembrances. These are practice limits, not cosmic facts.
- Local cost/duration: the living anchor loses heat and stamina while the place
  remains kept. Safe visits last about twenty minutes. Strain increases with
  duration and resistance to closure; no sacrifice, corruption, or universal
  cost is implied.
- Arrival/departure: Zephra appears gradually in the chair when she accepts,
  perceives the room through the anchored field, and can speak, gesture, and
  experience offered tastes within it. She cannot leave the place, cast, move
  independent objects beyond ordinary contact mediated by the field, or remain
  after the focus is broken. She returns to the afterlife when dismissed,
  when the anchor releases the working, or when the prepared place is broken.
- Post-death knowledge: Zephra knows her own afterlife experience and what she
  has been told or witnessed during prior visits. She does not automatically
  know living events, minds, the future, or the broader cosmology.
- Davina's refusal to honor Zephra's request is an ethical abuse and a breach of
  the custom, not proof that consent lacks moral weight or that the dead are
  objects. Zephra retains identity, relationships, judgment, and choice.
- Keep the setting unnamed and domestic. Do not locate the house on literal
  Earth, Ravel, the Glass-Sea world, or an unresolved Galactic Cycle coordinate.
- Maintain the Teen/non-graphic camera and treat grief, coercion, and family
  control through character consequence rather than spectacle.

## Proposed inventions

- The modern-like Morrow family maintains a discreet household practice called
  a **kept-place supper**, using living agency, a written invitation, a table
  object personally associated with the dead guest, and a set place as focus.
- A kept place can host one genuine temporary visitor from the universal
  afterlife within a narrow table-bound manifestation; acceptance is voluntary,
  and only a living anchor performs or sustains magic.
- The anchor pays in body heat and stamina, with a roughly twenty-minute safe
  duration; breaking the prepared place collapses the focus and returns the
  visitor to the afterlife.
- The same dead person is invited at most once between yearly family
  remembrances as a limit of this inherited practice.
- The Morrows treat an inherited table key as the symbol of future custodianship.
- Davina has converted an older hospitality custom into a partner-vetting
  ritual; Zephra, who married into the family, disputes that reinterpretation.
- Character facts, relationships, the unnamed family house, the dinner events,
  and Greer's refusal of succession are local proposed facts for the canon
  delta, not established lore.

## Failure modes to watch

- Do not let Zephra cast, supply, sustain, enlarge, or dismiss the working. Her
  agency must be personal; every magical operation belongs to living Davina.
- Do not describe the plate as independently magical. It is a nonliving focus
  whose arrangement can be physically broken.
- Do not make Zephra omniscient, prophetic, or automatically aware of events
  since her death. Davina's briefings and prior visits explain what she knows.
- Do not make the climax an accidental spell failure. Imani deliberately takes
  a socially and romantically costly action after asking Zephra what she wants.
- Do not let Zephra's history function as surprise information that solves the
  conflict. Seed her outsider status and philosophy early; the climax depends
  on Imani's and Greer's choices.
- Do not make Davina a cackling tyrant or flatten the whole lineage into evil.
  Preserve the genuine welcome, the rarity of contact, her grief, and her fear
  that an unprepared partner will abandon the duty.
- Do not reward Greer with immediate forgiveness. Her specific apology, loss of
  the table key, and willingness to have the withheld conversations earn only
  a next step.
- Keep the father unnamed and relationally described; do not accidentally turn
  “Father,” “Dad,” “host,” “keeper,” or “anchor” into character-facing aliases.
- Avoid generic banter, lore exposition, sentimental abstractions, and
  repetitive dining-room beats. Every exchange must shift Imani's understanding
  or narrow her available choices.
- Ensure both halves of the prompt appear: the family meeting must be genuinely
  pleasant before the ancestral visit becomes stressful, and the decades-dead
  relative must materially change the outcome.
