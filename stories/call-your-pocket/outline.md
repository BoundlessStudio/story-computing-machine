# Promised experience

One game of eight-ball in a pool hall. The redhead offers the winner whatever they desire from the loser. Her wicked smile and threefold reputation matter. She has never lost, refused a wager, or accepted money in payment. “Trying the make them mess-up” and the later “touching and flirting trying to mess with each other physically, sexually, emotional” call for both adults to use their desire and nerve against each other on the page. The tone should be gleefully lewd and the match hard fought. The user puts the climax at “the whom wins” and defers sex. The relationship, winner, and prize are open.

## Takes considered

- **A straight challenge.** A capable local accepts the undefeated woman's open wager in front of people who have seen her collect stranger prizes. The match turns on the pleasure each takes in making the other miss.
- **The champion's mistake.** The redhead accepts a former lover's challenge. An invitation she recognizes throws her off on the decisive shot, and the former lover takes her first loss in a story told from the champion's side.
- **The hall keeps score.** A bartender tells the match around conflicting rumors of wagers they've personally paid. The climax makes a raconteur revise her account of what the redhead likes to take.

Choose the straight challenge. Both players' appetites can change the match, and the final shot takes everything the redhead can do.

## Provisional direction

Riva Tallis and Jory Cassell know each other by sight from the Bobbin. Jory would take her stained-glass table lamp if he were the first to beat her. She won it off the owner and left it hanging. Riva wants the black silk tie Jory cut and made for himself. She notices he keeps setting its knot straight when he wants to look unruffled. Jory uses her own invitation to touch and the appetite he usually keeps to himself to get inside her timing. She comes back harder and costs him his last stripe. She has to survive his last attempt to rattle her and make a called bank to take the game and the tie.

## Craft plan

**Teller and voice.** Try two alternatives for the story's teller. Jory in close first-person past is fastidious and amused by his misplaced confidence. Riva in close third present is expansive and alert to changes in her opponent's attention. Slow into the chosen teller's physical sensations and unsettled calculations under pressure.

**Cast and speech.** Riva, thirty-eight, wants to keep her streak, wear his tie, and find out how much this particular opponent can give back. She’s seen him run most of a table and thinks she can read his desire before he reads hers. She unspools a dirty invitation and folds what she just saw into it. Jory, thirty-five, fits suits. He wants the lamp, the standing a win would get him here, and her full attention. He thinks she’s never noticed him play. He talks in the particulars of the thing in his hand, exact about a corner or the fabric, and grows recklessly frank when the bench can't hear. Leave the crowd at the rail unnamed.

**Conversation owed.** In the long scene she has to tell him what she means to take and let him try to show her how much he means to keep it.

**Casting.** The image gives Riva a swept copper bob, a sidelong grin, a low stance with a left-hand bridge, rose tattoos, a white tee, short tartan, lace thigh-highs, and patent ankle-strap heels. She plants, turns, holds, and dares. The look back stops Jory at the first rail and returns just before the eight. Her inked bridge is steady early and at the bank she deliberately sets it down again. Her opening lean gives him a flash of red. His sight of the pink bow tattoo above her stocking turns into invited touch later. The heel taps let him hear her coming into the quiet end. Keep the clothes and her command of what she shows.

**Place.** The Bobbin in Ternwick, an old mill club, beats the noisy Crossbuck Bowl annex and the Velvet Hill Hotel room with no local witnesses. A bolted bench behind the head rail keeps the watchers together. Beyond the opposite short rail, a shallow payphone recess beside the chalk shelf is well in sight and usually out of earshot. At ten on a wet October Friday there’s damp wool, orange peel by the bar, balls knocking in the return, heels tapping across seams in the boards, and amber light on green cloth. Riva owns that stained-glass shade. The glass rattle covers talk when the barback loads racks of empties.

## Scene briefs

1. **The offer, about a fifth.** Riva looks back over a practice shot. Jory wants to be treated as a threat and sees the lamp as a prize worth naming. He knows she’s never turned down a wager. Riva has seen how well he plays and watched him keep setting his knot straight. She wants him to say yes while the bench can hear. She gives the open terms. When he names the lamp, she tells him how the owner tried to settle its loss in cash. Jory takes the challenge.
2. **The rack, the long scene.** He handles her first visual feint and makes a good early shot. He holds out his tie as they cross at the chalk shelf, then waits for her invitation and runs his fingers over a pink bow on the back of her thigh. He tells her he'd like both those bows over his shoulders and wants to hear what she sounds like then. She answers filth for filth out of the bench's hearing and walks back along the rail with the suggestion still in her head. He steps out of her way before she bends. Her next cut catches the jaw.

   Before his last stripe, she follows him around the foot to look at the angle. She gets her fingers inside his collar and draws him close. He closes the gap for the kiss. She breaks away and tells him how she’ll wear his tie with nothing else and how badly she wants him to try to keep it. He knows he can wait, wants her to see him make the cut right now, and leaves it in the mouth. She pots her final solid. While she's settling for the eight, he murmurs a callback to the bows over his shoulders. Riva chooses to look right at him, then sets her own pace, calls the bank, and sinks it. Compress routine pots. Keep earlier games in the talk and later encounters outside the story.

## Ending

In view of the bench, Jory has to undo the knot he’s been keeping straight. Riva takes the tie with the same hand that held her bridge steady and puts it on over her white shirt beneath the lamp he wanted. The hall can see the prize and his open collar.
