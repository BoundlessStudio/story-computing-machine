# Promised experience

The request gives a redheaded pool player a wicked smile and three parts to her reputation. She has never lost, refused a wager, or accepted money in settlement. She offers her challenger one game of eight-ball, with the winner taking whatever they desire from the loser. “Trying the make them mess-up” and the later “touching and flirting trying to mess with each other physically, sexually, emotional” make the lewdness a mutual competitive tactic. The charge should build from the pleasure of being looked at to the difficulty of sinking a ball with somebody's hands and words still on your mind. The whole story stays in the hall, and “cinmax being the whom wins” puts the decision at the table. The later direction defers sex. Their prior relationship, the winner, and what the winner takes are open.

## Takes considered

- **A straight challenge.** A capable local takes the undefeated woman's open wager in front of people who have seen her collect stranger prizes. The scene to stay for is the game itself, when each gets the pleasure of making the other miss and the match comes down to whether she can still sink the eight.
- **The champion's mistake.** In close third on the redhead, she accepts a former lover's challenge and finds that the other woman has learned to use their old appetites as effectively as she can. Their final exchange would turn on an invitation the redhead recognizes from before and a decisive shot she fails to recover from.
- **The hall keeps score.** A bartender tells the match around two conflicting stories of wagers they've personally paid, until the table goes quiet and the story of what the woman will ask for becomes immediate. The spectacle would make a comic tall tale of the hall's legend and force the teller to revise their own version of it in real time.

Choose the straight challenge. There is room to inhabit both consequential misses and the winning shot, with the redhead's appetite operating as a skill a good opponent can answer.

## Provisional direction

Riva Tallis and Jory Cassell know each other by sight from the Bobbin, where they both play. Jory would take her stained-glass table lamp if he were the first to beat her. She won it off the owner and left it hanging. Riva wants the black silk tie Jory wears home from the suit shop. She discovers how much of his composure is invested in keeping its knot in place. Each invites the other to handle them between shots, with the stroke itself left to the shooter.

Jory gets inside her timing by meeting her showmanship with specific, dirty attention and a touch she encourages. Riva misses a ball she can usually make. Riva turns his collar into a much more effective distraction, letting him into a kiss, saying exactly what she wants when she wins, and needling him to make the shot she knows he can make. He leaves his last stripe in the jaws. She clears her last solid and beats him on a called bank of the eight, with his final attempt to upset her still in her ear. The rack decides the actual prize. He hands her the tie in view of the hall, and she gets to wear it.

## Craft plan

**Teller and voice.** Try Jory in close first-person past, fastidious and amused by how badly his own precautions work. The alternative is close third on Riva in present tense, expansive and delighted, sensitive to the instant somebody stops being merely an audience. In either, give the talk a few full stretches and slow down into particular sensations and unfinished calculations at the last shot.

**Cast and speech.** Riva, thirty-eight, wants her win, his tie, and an adversary who can make her work for all of it. She saw him run most of a table one night and correctly rates his skill. She expects to read his appetite before he reads hers. She draws boasts and indecencies out into invitations and lets a fresh detail interrupt her own sentence. She takes pleasure in being obvious and is proud enough to be shaken by a compliment that sees past her performance into her work. Jory, thirty-five, fits suits for a living. He wants the lamp, the standing a victory would give him in his own hall, and her undivided attention. He comes in thinking she hasn't watched him play. He is composed around an audience and specific when he talks, down to the fabric or the corner pocket, then startlingly frank when nobody but his target can hear. Under pressure he catches himself trying to put his clothes back in order. Keep the other players as a chorus at the rail.

**Conversation owed.** Terms first, then in the long scene Riva has to tell Jory what she means to take and he has to show her how badly he wants both to keep it and to beat her. Let them relish the mutual provocation before one of them has to shoot.

**Casting.** The image gives Riva a copper bob, a sidelong, amused look over her shoulder, a low and practiced stance with her left hand bridging and right hand on the cue, roses inked on both arms, and a white tee, short dark tartan, lace thigh-highs, and patent ankle-strap heels. She plants, turns, holds, and dares. The look back stops Jory at the first rail and returns when she asks him to watch the eight. The rose on her steady bridge marks skill early and steadiness regained at the bank. A flash of red follows her opening lean and the pink bow tattoo above her stocking gives Jory somewhere to look and, at her invitation, to touch. Her heel taps let him keep track of her in the hall's quiet end until she stops close. Keep the bold clothes and the player's control over what she shows, with attention on chosen movements and what they do to the match.

**Place.** The Bobbin in Ternwick beats the Crossbuck Bowl annex, where the match would be swallowed by noise, and the Velvet Hill Hotel billiard room, where the streak would lose its local witnesses. At the Bobbin, a former mill club, a bolted bench keeps spectators at the bar end. At the other end, the empty payphone recess lets two people speak low between shots in sight of everyone. At ten on a wet October Friday, there is the smell of damp wool and orange peel from the bar, the knock of spent balls in the return, heel taps over the seams in the boards, and amber stained glass light on the green. Riva owns the shade. The barback collects empties and the whole bench has a good view.

## Scene briefs

1. **The offer, about a fifth.** Riva looks over her shoulder from a practice stroke beneath the lamp she's already won. Jory wants her to treat him as a threat. She wants him to say yes in front of the bench. Jory knows the lamp is the thing he'd claim. Riva knows how he plays and that he's been straightening his tie since he came in. She offers the single game and its open terms. He names the lamp, and she tells how the owner tried to settle in cash to keep it and how she chose otherwise. Jory steps in.
2. **The rack, the long scene.** Jory stands up to the first visual feint with a good early shot. In the recess Riva takes his offered knot in her hand. Jory gets his own opening when Riva invites his hand to the bow on her thigh. He traces it, steps clear, and tells her low what he'd been looking at all along, including how much he likes her absorbed in a hard shot. The barback loading a rack of empties briefly covers her reply. Her next cut catches the jaw. Later, as Jory waits to try his final stripe, Riva draws him close by the tie, lets him close the gap for a kiss, and breaks away to tell him how she plans to wear it and how hard she wants him to make her earn it. He wants very much to make the shot, but overhits the stripe and leaves it in the mouth of a pocket. Riva pots her last solid. Jory takes a last deliberate run at making her look back when she is set for the eight. She looks, resets on her own time, and calls and makes the bank. Give the roll and the fall their full weight. Compress the routine pots and keep earlier games and any later encounters offstage.

## Ending

In the few lines after the eight falls, Jory undoes the knot they've both been handling and slides the tie over the rail. Riva takes it with the hand that held her bridge steady and puts it on loose over her white shirt beneath the lamp he wanted. The first look across the table, her prediction, and his failed stripe have made that small public transfer legible. End at the table.
