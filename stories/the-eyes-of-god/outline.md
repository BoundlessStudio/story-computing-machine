# Provisional direction

The speaker has learned to count the Eyes of God's passes from the shifting minutes of its glint and the scorch marks it leaves. They go into the open only after it has crossed beyond the city's horizon. Neighbors call those exposed errands a test of faith. The speaker lets them: the reputation gives them access to food and shelter for a person they love, and contradicting the story would make every past silence harder to defend. Their desire is modest and urgent: to keep that person alive without making anyone else trust a miracle that does not exist.

Someone who has watched them decides to make the same crossing. The speaker can stop this person once, but a private warning will not undo the public example. Worse, the next safe interval is shorter than it was yesterday. The story's action could unfold through one crossing and the wait for the following pass: the speaker must decide when to say aloud how they know the hour, whom to ask to believe their count, and whether to stand beside that person long enough to prove it. The weapon remains real even after its orbit is understood; a missed minute has a bodily cost.

A possible ending is a shared, carefully timed walk back under cover, with the speaker's supposed holiness lost and a usable method passed to someone else. The emotional turn should be smaller than a city's conversion: a person who was asked to trust the speaker's faith learns what that trust concealed, then chooses what to do with the knowledge. The distinctive pleasure is the difference between the crowd's reading of a figure in full daylight and the figure's exact, nervous awareness of minutes, angles, and the shadow moving toward them.
