In the round-robin, the writer agreed the outline had invented no reason a private “wait” would fail. The loved adult has copied yesterday's safe hour; the real orbital pass shifts daily. From cover, the speaker can predict a glint at a fixed mark and teach how to update the count. The other adult should make the final timing choice. Codex accepted this causal repair while cautioning that spiritual gatekeeping would need groundwork and an inaccurate count would dilute the prompt's secret. Claude explicitly withdrew its fallible-count and gatekeeping proposals after hearing the writer and Codex: the gap between complete knowledge and a copied hour is a stronger cause. Both readers left execution tests for the draft: why the speaker cannot simply do the errand, how hospitality is materially lost if the adult learns, a usable sightline and checkable timing, and the adult's agency. The coordinator accepts the revised direction because it keeps the prompt's double view and makes the deception itself create the risk; no staged injury or citywide conversion is needed.
