# What the Ocean Keeps

## Promised experience

A daring woman finds a fortune under a forgotten island and takes the first gold coin in defiance of a rule every pirate knows. The breaking waves go wholly silent at that instant, and a voice from the depths offers whatever she can carry in exchange for something of equal value. I read the pleasure as the lurch from getting away with a theft to discovering that her adversary is the ocean itself. “Little thief” suggests a dangerous presence with an amused interest in her. The painting gives her delight the same weight as the peril. The bargain should change what she dares to take and what the sea actually keeps, at a scale that can run from one coin to a moving ship. The request leaves price, ending, names, and form open. The later attachment direction concerns the handling of inputs. The painting provides casting and atmosphere.

## Takes considered

- **The ship's price.** In real time, the hunter discovers the ocean covets her ship as keenly as she covets the fortune. The parley sends her to deliver the price herself under sail.
- **The purchased harbor.** Start years after the theft, at the dedication of the harbor she bought. When the sea comes to collect, she climbs a bell tower with a chest while the streets below disappear.
- **An old thief's supper.** An old hunter tells a cabin boy how she carried off a king's ransom and the sea took her years for it. The long crossing is a physical fight to keep hold of the sacks as her hands age.

I choose the first. Both thieves make consequential moves from the first coin through the taking, and paying the price is itself an adventure.

## Provisional direction

Ness Calder built the little cutter *Spite* up from a stripped hull. She wants fast ships at *Spite*'s flanks so she can take a convoy she has only ever been able to watch go by. Under Adder's Kettle she finds more than she can lift. She can tell the hoard came from wrecks, and she thinks the rule is an excuse pirates give for leaving behind what they lack the nerve to reach. She toasts the breakers with the first gold coin and pockets it. The surf goes wholly silent. The ocean speaks the offer.

For that coin alone, the sea would take the copper earring Ness hammered from the first money she stole. A rise of water reaches for her ear and she claps a hand over it. One proof of a theft for another. The price is for what she carries away. Trying to sell the sea on a richer object from the shelf, she watches it raise *Spite* on the swell and sees how tenderly it holds the hull. Ness names the possession she values as much as the portable fortune she wants. The sea usually gets wrecks. It wants her cutter whole and still sailing. Ness chooses a haul of gold, pearls, and stones she can bear to the tender. She will bring *Spite* through the reef under sail herself if the sea gives her the water to do it and lets her loaded tender clear before it collects. The cutter is the one price she pays.

## Craft plan

**Teller.** Try close third past with Ness's covetous eye and real-time first person with the brio of a thief who expects to be contradicted. Let the approach unspool, let the bargaining wander, and move closer to Ness's hands and thoughts as she relinquishes the cutter.

**Ness.** In the painting she crouches into her prize with a wide grin, wind-loose dark braid over her right shoulder, and cutlass up in her right hand. She braces, scoops, leans in, springs. She loves the little ship she jokes about and will admit to greed sooner than fear. She talks in expansive sales pitches with comparisons from chandlers and taverns, then gets plain when she names her price. Keep the bold silhouette, coat, and ruffled work shirt, with attention on her face and hands. Her red coat's pocket catches the first coin, and its wet sleeve catches the tiller at the parting. Her braid blows into her mouth as she takes the cut. Ringed fingertips testing the pearl strands have to let go of the stern rail. The cutlass prises a chest and cuts the towline.

**The ocean.** A living presence with the cave as its mouth and the water as its reach. It covets a vessel taken at speed and enjoys letting its quarry discover this. Its unhurried, full sentences carry the amused courtesy of “little thief.” It names what it has seen ships and people do. Its care for a possession can be finer than its care for the person losing it.

**Place.** Adder's Kettle, a forgotten sealer's island on the convoy's outer sea, early autumn before sunrise into daylight. The cut in the hooked outer reef leads from open sea into a sheltered basin. From there a low shale arch opens into the island's cave. Refracted swell still hits the arch and surges up to the wreck-strewn shelf. An old sealer's cleat holds a tender. Bladderwrack pops underfoot, old seal oil haunts the bolt hole, cold shale stings the palm, foam pencils the black stone, and dawn picks out copper in the arch. There are ship ribs and bones among the coins. High Needle's buried mint keeps the ship and tide offstage, and the flooded stair at Gannet's Step leaves little room for the delight of the find. Here Ness can see the hull from her gold shelf and must take it back through the cut, where a sideways current carries a badly pointed bow onto the reef.

## Scene briefs

1. **The find, 25%.** In predawn, Ness feels the cross-set through *Spite*'s tiller, waits for the stern to lift, and points the bow into the pull so the little cutter slips through the cut. She knows exactly when it will answer. She anchors inside the reef, rows under the arch, and ties up to the old cleat. Let her enjoy sorting what lies among the ship ribs. She sees the sea's mark in the barnacles, makes her toast, and pockets the gold. Suddenly she can hear cloth move over her knuckles. The voice comes from below the shelf.
2. **The parley, 50%.** In the cave she tries the sea's offer against one coin. The earring stays with her. She tries to sell the sea on things she already has her hands on, then sees how it holds *Spite* and understands what it would like. Let the narrow wet shelf interfere while she sorts a fortune she can carry. Stay through the moment when she names her ship and makes the sea agree to take it sailing and give her tender passage. The hull briefly lifted in clear water is a sight she has only ever seen when *Spite* was out of commission.
3. **The taking, 25%.** At sunrise, *Spite* tows the tender with the fortune in it. Ness recognizes the cross-set and feels for the same response from the cutter as she heads back through the reef. As soon as they clear, she sets the tiller, drops into the tender and cuts it free. The sea gives her space to peel away, then drops a reach of water beneath *Spite* and takes her down upright and under sail. Stay with the whole descent. Earlier voyages come in passing thoughts. The convoy raid belongs after this story.

**Last image.** Ness pulls the tender's wake across the track *Spite* just sailed. Far below that surface line she sees the peak of the cutter's sail turn green and pass the other way. The two crossings of the reef, the familiar answer at the tiller, and Ness's view of the hull the sea raised have put a whole ship in that moving scrap of sail. She rows on with a fortune at her feet.
