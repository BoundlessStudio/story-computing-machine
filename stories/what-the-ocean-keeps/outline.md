# What the Ocean Keeps

## Promised experience

The request gives us a daring woman who finds a real fortune under a forgotten island and takes the first gold coin in defiance of a rule every pirate knows. The breaking waves go wholly silent at that instant, and the voice from the depths offers what she can carry in exchange for something of equal value. I read the pleasure as the lurch from the delight of getting away with a theft to the thrill of finding that her adversary is the ocean itself. “Little thief” suggests a dangerous adversary with an amused interest in her. The painting makes her excitement and physical nerve as important as the threat. The bargain should affect what she dares to carry and what she has to leave in the sea's possession. Its scale can run from the weight of one coin to the power that moves ships. The request leaves the price, outcome, names, and point of view open. The later direction about attachment instructions governs how the inputs are used. The painting gives us casting and atmosphere.

## Takes considered

- **The ship's price.** Follow the hunter from the find through the exchange in real time. In the long parley she discovers the ocean covets her ship in the same way she covets the gold. She chooses her haul and must bring the ship out under sail to deliver the price.
- **The purchased harbor.** Begin years after the theft at the dedication of the hunter's richly built home port. The ocean comes to collect, and the key scene is her climbing a bell tower with a chest as the streets she bought disappear beneath her. The delay gives the sea a terrifying reach and moves attention from the theft to what she built with it.
- **An old thief's supper.** A much older hunter tells a doubting cabin boy how she carried off a king's ransom and how the sea took her years for it. The scene that earns the telling is the crossing, when the young woman keeps changing her grip on the sacks as her hands age and her ambition stays young.

The first keeps both thieves present and making choices from the first coin to the final taking. It makes the price itself an adventure she can attempt.

## Provisional direction

Ness Calder sails the little cutter *Spite*. She wants enough gold to put a squadron of her own on the horizon and take a convoy that a lone pirate has to let pass. At Adder's Kettle she finds more than she can lift, pockets a bright coin as proof that her hunt has paid, and hears the surf go dead and the ocean speak the offer. She can walk away with that one coin. The ocean takes the bent copper earring she made from the first money she ever stole. Both pieces mean a successful theft, which is why she chose that coin before the pearls. The sea puts a value on what Ness would miss.

Ness's first instinct is to load up and buy her way out with other people's old treasure. The sea brings *Spite* into view on the arrested swell and makes plain what it wants for a fleet's worth of gold. An intact ship under sail is a rare acquisition for a collector that usually gets wrecks. Ness could leave with the coin. She refuses to yield the cutter as dead freight. She will choose a portable fortune and bring *Spite* to the reef herself, with her tender and the haul in tow, if the ocean lets her and the tender clear the taking. The sea gets a vessel in full sail and can keep it forever. Ness actually leaves with the gold and jewels, and she knows what they cannot buy her back.

## Craft plan

**Teller.** Try close third past with Ness's delighted, covetous eye and real-time first person with the brio of a thief who expects to be contradicted. Give the approach, the parley, and the farewell distinct rhythms. At the loss stay close enough for the reader to feel what her hands are used to doing.

**Ness.** Her coat and the cutlass in her right hand give the painted figure a wide, bold outline even crouched. She braces on a bent knee with her ringed left hand open on the coins. Dark hair is partly braided over her right shoulder. The grin and wide, direct eyes read as glee with room for astonishment. She wedges, scoops, leans in and springs. She loves the ship whose smallness she jokes about, knows how to make it point into a sea, and would rather admit to being greedy than afraid of losing it. She fills silence with vivid sales pitches, pulls comparisons out of a tavern or chandler's stall, and names what she wants bluntly when the jokes fail. Keep the ruffled work shirt and vigorous posture, with attention on face, hands, and motion. Her red coat's inner pocket catches the first coin and its soaked sleeve later has to be got clear of the tiller. The wind puts the braid in her mouth during the last tack. Her ringed fingertips test a pearl string and then have to let the stern rail go. Her cutlass pries open a salt-swollen chest and cuts the towline.

**The ocean.** A living presence with the cave as its mouth and moving water as its reach. It wants to possess the cutter whole and have the pleasure of taking her at speed. It speaks with the exact “little thief” courtesy of the prompt. It lets Ness pitch at length and replies in unhurried, complete sentences, naming what it has seen ships and people do. It has the vanity of a collector and can be punctilious about a taking while indifferent to the feelings it costs.

**Place.** Adder's Kettle, an uncharted sealer's island on the outer edge of the convoy's sea, in the first sharp weather of autumn from predawn into sunrise. The hooked reef shelters a deep run under a low shale arch. The hoard lies on a rock shelf inside where the sea can reach on a surge. Old cleats from the abandoned sealery still hold a tender. At the two alternatives, the buried mint at High Needle keeps ship and tide offstage, while the flooded stair at Gannet's Step crowds out everything the hunter wants to touch. The Kettle lets her see ship and gold from the same wet perch and makes the bargain pass through a reef cut she can sail only with the tide. Bladderwrack pops under a boot, rancid oil clings in a sheltered cleat hole, the shale stings a bare palm with cold, white foam draws a line across black stone, and the rising light strikes a copper seam in the arch. The reef that saved the ship from the swell gives the sea a place to take it.

## Scene briefs

1. **The find, 25%.** In the dark before sunrise Ness leaves *Spite* anchored beyond the reef and rows through the arch. The shelf is a wreck-strewn room with enough gold on it to make the whole voyage sensible. Stay with the pleasure of touching and sorting. She palms the first coin, pockets it, and suddenly can hear cloth move over her knuckles. The offer comes up from below the shelf.
2. **The parley, 50%.** At the shelf, Ness tests the charge with one coin and loses her earring. She tries to sell the sea on other prizes and gets a glimpse of what *Spite* looks like from below as the ocean moves her across the anchorage. Let Ness talk the way she talks to a seller when she covets the one thing on the counter, then take time over the turn when she puts her own ship on it. The narrow ledge makes her sort by touch and weight while arguing. She keeps the coin and a carryable fortune in compact pearls and jewels, and sets the terms on which she will bring *Spite* through the cut.
3. **The taking, 25%.** In the cutter at sunrise, the sea holds an outward run through the cut while Ness gets both her ship and the loaded tender moving. She sets the tiller long enough to drop into the tender and cut it free. The ocean lets the tender clear, then drops a reach of water away beneath the cutter and takes her down at speed, upright under full sail. The cost has the length of the whole descent. The tenders and prizes of earlier voyages belong in passing thoughts. Leave the convoy raid outside the story.

**Last image.** From the tender she sees the *Spite*'s wake crossing hers well below the surface. A scrape of the tiller on her sleeve earlier, her glimpse of the familiar hull from below, and the effort to set the last sail make that parting particular to her. She sets her own oar for the open sea with the fortune at her feet. Both thieves carry something away.
