# A Safe Place to Burn — provisional direction

The promised pleasure is the contrast between a magical girl's familiar, beautiful power and an output so far beyond normal that saving the people she loves makes her dangerous to them. I read the tone as urgent, intimate, and potentially tragic, with room for spectacular rescue on the scale of a spacecraft and a bare moon. The central relationship will be an established adult romantic partnership: Hedra, the ship's magical girl, and Romy, its captain and pilot. The anticipated payoff is a successful rescue followed by a farewell whose cost the story does not revoke, with Romy helping Hedra reach a truly empty place rather than leaving her to die unheard.

Explicit in the request: magical girls normally use 10 or 20 percent; the speaker is at 195 percent and rising; she intends to get the addressee and crew to safety, then find somewhere safe to burn up; the goodbye expresses profound love. Interpretations, not further requirements: a spacefaring setting, two adult women in love, first-person narration by the surviving beloved, literal non-graphic death rather than a last-minute cure. General is the starting content target, not a limit on the rescue's grandeur.

Possible scenes:

- On a survey ship approaching an uninhabited moon, Romy watches Hedra work at an ordinary output. The little transformation details and the crew's teasing establish a person who likes this life and intends to keep it. A specific shared plan extends beyond the voyage. Her brooch measures the living magic she channels; it does not originate it.
- A debris strike wrecks the drive. Hedra catches its lethal discharge before it can kill the pressure cabin, forcing her channel fully open. The crew makes useful attempts to stop the feedback while she keeps the broken ship together in enormous ribbons of light. Establish early that beyond the channel's breaking point it draws more power by itself; spending magic faster cannot empty a finite battery.
- Romy asks why the output is still climbing. Hedra delivers the supplied speech at 195 percent. Its calmness collides with the crew's frantic work and their knowledge of her usual limits. Romy searches for a practical rescue for her, finds the already-established options exhausted, and must keep captaining instead of making a suicidal pursuit.
- Hedra's growing power is consequential throughout the escape: she parts the debris, contains the crippled ship, and accelerates it onto a safe orbit with a rescue rendezvous. Romy and the crew help release her final grip only after they can survive without her. No one is saved by an unrelated modest solution.
- Romy supplies a route to a verified empty crater beyond the moon's far limb, where the moon itself will shield the ship and nearby traffic from the release. Hedra makes the last flight under her own power. They speak as lovers rather than delivering matched speeches; Romy stays on the radio after she can no longer do anything useful. The far-side burn is witnessed through its light or lifted dust at the rim, not graphic bodily destruction. The ending stays near the moment the crew is safe and Hedra is gone, with the abandoned shared future felt rather than explained.

Protect: power that stays magnificent and useful while the person wielding it becomes unreachable; reciprocal love expressed by difficult, concrete acts on both sides.

Watch: a captain narrator could reduce Hedra to a beautiful sacrifice, or an explanation of runaway magic could sound like a convenient declaration that no rescue is possible. Give Hedra her own wants, fear, choices, and resistance before the farewell; make attempted remedies and physical geography legible without turning the climax into a technical report. The ending remains discoverable in prose.
