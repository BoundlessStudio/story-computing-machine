# A Safe Place to Burn — provisional direction

The promised pleasure is the contrast between a magical girl's familiar, beautiful power and an output so far beyond normal that saving the people she loves makes her dangerous to them. I read the tone as urgent, intimate, and potentially tragic, with room for spectacular rescue on the scale of a spacecraft and a bare moon. The central relationship will be an established adult romantic partnership: Hedra, the ship's magical girl, and Romy, its captain and pilot. The anticipated payoff is a successful rescue followed by a farewell whose cost the story does not revoke: Hedra chooses where and how to spend her remaining control, while Romy helps make those choices possible.

Explicit in the request: magical girls normally use 10 or 20 percent; the speaker is at 195 percent and rising; she intends to get the addressee and crew to safety, then find somewhere safe to burn up; the goodbye expresses profound love. Interpretations, not further requirements: a spacefaring setting, two adult women in love, first-person narration by the surviving beloved, literal non-graphic death rather than a last-minute cure. General is the starting content target, not a limit on the rescue's grandeur.

Her decisions after the accident must change what happens; wants and fear alone do not supply agency. Keep the rising readings, transformed magical-girl imagery, attempted remedy, and shielding geography legible without turning the climax into a technical report.
