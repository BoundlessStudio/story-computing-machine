# Outline

## Story

- Premise / central promise: In first-person past tense, night-shift carcass grader Kessa Morren discovers that the profitable refinery keeping her district brightly defended is also concentrating a signal that draws fresh monsters; when one of the newly arrived, nearly silent fighters tries to stop the process, Kessa must decide whether to protect the industry that sustains her family or break its self-feeding harvest.
- Character want and pressure: Kessa needs the refinery's hazard bonus and a promised move to day shift to support her recovering younger brother. Record intake means security for her household, while ruined carcasses, shutdowns, and the strange fighters' interference mean docked pay. Her employer insists that carcass-derived lamp oil is the only reason workers survive after dark, making skepticism feel both disloyal and suicidal.
- Turn: During a monster surge, Kessa briefly shuts one refining line after reading the fighter's gestures and sees the pack veer away from the cooling tank. A supervisor immediately restarts it to preserve the floodlights and the night's record yield, proving that the supposed defense is sustaining the attack and that the industry will not stop itself.
- Ending: Kessa frees the detained fighter and triggers the refinery's emergency containment shutdown, sacrificing her promotion and spoiling the harvest. The fighters hold the yard until the concentrated signal fades; the remaining monsters scatter instead of being endlessly replaced. At dawn Kessa releases the plant data publicly, forcing a wider shutdown inquiry. The fighters depart toward another distant glow, leaving their origin and the first emergence unknown but resolving Kessa's local choice and the night's causal loop.

## Beats

1. Open at the refinery intake late in the monster era's first year: Kessa weighs trucked-in carcasses, grades hide and luminous organs, and watches crews celebrate a record “night harvest.” Concrete routine establishes the corpse economy, its wages, and the floodlights fueled by rendered monster tissue; Kessa signs the overtime sheet because her brother's recovery has consumed their savings.
2. A harvest truck arrives damaged, followed by one of the odd fighters who have recently begun appearing after sunset. The fighter kills the monster clinging beneath the truck, ignores applause and payment, then slashes open the carcass's luminous organ and points urgently from it to the refinery tanks. Security reads this as sabotage and detains the fighter; Kessa notices the stranger never attacks a person and keeps tracking the tanks' pulse.
3. Management orders the brightest possible run because packs are converging and every corpse is valuable. Kessa tries literal questions and sketches with the detainee; the fighter barely responds but repeats a simple sequence—organ, tank, approaching pack, extinguished light—and marks many small circles flowing toward one large circle. Kessa remains unconvinced because shutting down would darken the yard and cost everyone's bonus.
4. As the surge reaches the fence, the refinery gauges begin pulsing in time with cries outside. Kessa tests the warning by pausing one secondary line under the pretext of a blockage. Monsters on that side abandon easy human targets and angle toward the still-running main tank, while the pause produces no breach in ordinary emergency lighting.
5. The supervisor overrides Kessa and restarts the line, arguing that correlation cannot outweigh contracts, payroll, and immediate defense. The tank flares; a fresh wave appears at the yard's edge. This locks the turn: the refined organs do not create the monsters or explain their first arrival, but their concentrated signal gives each night wave a stable destination and makes the local supply look inexhaustible.
6. Kessa releases the fighter and initiates full emergency containment. The refinery loses its corpse-fueled floodlights, but battery lamps and vehicle beams preserve enough visibility. The fighter retrieves their weapon and, joined by two silent companions, fights only the monsters pressing the shutdown controls and workers' escape route; Kessa must stay at the panel long enough for every hot tank to quench rather than flee with the shift.
7. In the climax, the supervisor attempts one final restart as monsters mass at the gate. Kessa commits to the shutdown, locks the luminous stock in shielded containment, and accepts that the batch, bonus, and promotion are gone. The tank pulse dies; no new silhouettes replace the fighters' final kills, and the creatures already present scatter toward weaker distant signals.
8. At dawn, management prepares to blame the fighters and reopen by nightfall. Kessa sends the timestamped gauge and camera record to workers, local officials, and reporters, making concealment harder and prompting suspension of other refiners using the same process. The fighter acknowledges her with the same organ-to-horizon gesture, then leaves with the others toward a far-off industrial glow. Kessa goes home unemployed but able to tell her brother that their district may face an ordinary dark night for the first time in months.

## Continuity

- Canon used: The story occupies an otherwise unspecified modern-like period of the one deep-time physical world. Its new anomaly can coexist with other distinct systems without sharing their origin. The odd fighters remain people with agency and moral consequence despite the narrator's limited understanding of them. Violence and carcass processing stay concrete but non-graphic and broadly accessible.
- Boundaries and unknowns: Treat the monsters, their nocturnal appearances, the corpse signal, and the fighters as one new story-local phenomenon with no implied connection to the Glass Gate, Deepmarket, the stormhart, or any existing portal, creature, faction, or anomaly. Refined dead tissue carries or concentrates a bounded residual signal; it is not an independent magic user. Do not identify the setting as literal Earth, fix it relative to other eras, claim the outbreak is planet-wide, explain the monsters' ultimate origin, define their sapience, identify the fighters' homeland, or establish that every refinery everywhere has the same effect. Kessa proves and interrupts one local feedback loop; wider shutdowns are an investigation, not a guaranteed cure.
