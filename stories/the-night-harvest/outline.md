# Outline

## Story

- Premise and central promise: Three months after apparently inexhaustible monsters began appearing at night, grader Brelva and her brother Perren live from selling their corpses. Unfamiliar fighters then arrive, ignore questions and salvage, kill efficiently, and smash the throat nodes humans prize most. Resolve the collision without translated lore: commercial brining keeps the dead nodes' directional vibration active, so the cold yard draws monsters along collection routes. Brelva must choose between the stock that could free their cart from debt and destroying it before the convergence reaches nearby homes.
- Focal pressure or attachment: Brelva taught Perren measured risk after two harvest partners died. She wants their hauling partnership independent, but only the node contract can pay for the cart. That promise makes evidence against their best product hard to accept; protecting Perren also cannot mean deciding every risk for him.
- Counterforce or complication: Harvesting supplies fuel, cloth, medicine, wages, and safer streets; useful carcass parts need not be destroyed. The unnamed supervisor protects stock bought from indebted crews, and losing the siblings' load may bankrupt them. Without shared language, the newcomers' necessary destruction resembles vandalism.
- POV, distance, and information limit: Close third past through Brelva, attentive first to weight, grade, spoilage, and price, then to vibration, routes, and repeated choices. She cannot translate the newcomers, enter their minds, know either group's origin, or explain the worldwide night. Recurrence supports only a local inference.
- Governing movement and time shape: Six nights with narrowing intervals. Repeated intake actions—counting loads, disputing damage, tracking fights—turn rivalry into pattern. Render the first node-smashing, an attack following a shipment, the siblings' contract dispute, and the cold-yard convergence. Compress days through invoices, repairs, and Brelva's route map. End in morning cleanup and limited working practice, not public proof or global victory.
- Speculative rule or ordinary-world constraint: Locally, monsters appear after full dark and leave paired throat nodes vibrating toward their point of emergence. Death weakens the vibration; sunrise ends it. Cold mineral brine preserves both the nodes for lamp fuel and their signal; stockpiles reinforce it, directing nearby monsters without creating or controlling them. Cracking a node and heating its inner membrane stops it; hide, bone, and tendon remain usable. The newcomers demonstrate only specialized equipment and experience, not magic or fluency.

## Voice

- Narrative texture: Move through hook strain, brine crust, tallies, and vibration felt through wood; let routes and absences displace prices as Brelva's governing measures. Vary syntax; speed comes from changed priorities, not constant fragments.
- Conversational texture: Yard talk ranges from rates, supper, repairs, and sore hands to hazard calls. The siblings' practical shorthand breaks into unfinished disagreement. Newcomer contact stays gesture, placement, gaze, and unparsed sound.
- Rhetorical ownership: Brelva reasons by quantities and sequence; Perren by remembered bodies, promises, and routes; the supervisor by obligations to crews and purchased batches. The newcomer demonstrates one physical relation at a time.
- Pressure behavior: Brelva becomes overprecise, then blunt. Perren repeats the debt consequence before shortening into action. The supervisor speeds up and listens less. The newcomer waits for imitation, then acts alone.
- Anti-default: Avoid quip-ready apocalypse crews, a greed sermon, interchangeable grimness, or a suddenly fluent outsider explaining invasion lore; keep competence partial and talk attached to work.

## Beats

1. At intake, the siblings discuss repairs, food, and the payment that could clear their cart. A strange fighter kills a monster outside, smashes Brelva's valuable node despite warnings, attempts one directional gesture, and leaves toward another attack.
2. More fighters arrive singly. Brelva sees attacks cluster behind cold wagons; Perren sees fighters place themselves between node loads and housing. The supervisor deducts damage and orders guards around the next shipment.
3. A broken axle diverts a brined load, and monsters follow. The recurring fighter cracks one node; its vibration stops under Brelva's hand while pursuers veer toward the yard's stronger stock. Brelva gains a local inference, not an explanation.
4. Brelva wants to refuse the final contract but calculates that losing it forfeits their cart. Perren challenges her habit of deciding his risks. They remove ordinary salvage but leave contracted nodes at the yard, concentrating the danger.
5. Reinforced vibration draws attacks along several roads. Moving stock would lead them through homes. Through observed placement, the siblings and fighter block carts, crack nodes, heat membranes, and defend workers. Human disagreement continues; the newcomer never becomes commander or interpreter.
6. The signal breaks, but the wider night and debt remain. At morning cleanup, useful parts are separated, earnings recalculated, and the local yard abandons brining because its batch is gone. One repeatable stop/approach gesture permits future cooperation without fluency.
