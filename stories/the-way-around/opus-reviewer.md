# Opus review: The Way Around

Independent audit for a later editing pass. It is not a `review.md` verdict,
does not change canon state, and does not authorize edits by itself.

## Verdict

- Dialogue: REVISE — the first exchange after the door opens does not parse, and the four key arguments fall into short correction volleys that end on a neat last line, with the story's lesson spoken outright at the end.
- Prose: REVISE — the turning-point shot into the enclosed chamber is staged in a way that contradicts itself, and the ending explains its point several times over. Otherwise the prose is concrete and physical.
- Continuity and prompt: PASS — every rung of the prompt's escalation is there and the ambition resolves; only Minor setup gaps (rope, arrow retrieval, capstone plant, two staging slips).
- Edit scope: targeted-dialogue
- Existing review.md said: Verdict PASS, "Dialogue: PASS"; disagree. It missed the door exchange that doesn't parse, the rebuttal-and-topper arguments and the ending that states its own lesson.

The three voices are genuinely separable. Mazin's complaining, bodily humor is the best thing in the dialogue, and several exchanges work as they are: "Rock-shaped," "From inside or outside?", "don't tell me yet." The failure is concentrated. Whenever Ivara and Tressa argue (at the slab, after the separation, over the capstone's tilt, and over the record at dawn), both drop into the collection's clipped rebuttal register. Each line corrects the last and the exchange closes on a crafted last word, even when they are lost, frightened or falling. The one exchange that is actually broken is the first after the door opens (L27–L29). The edit pass should repair that exchange, roughen the four arguments so fear and wanting come through (Tressa's language stripping down under pressure, Ivara's hunger audible), and let the returned arrow and the ledger line end the story without anyone stating the moral.

## Dialogue findings

### D1 · Blocking · Door exchange does not parse

- Where: L27–L29, entrance, just after the first shot sinks the door.
- Text:
> “Now ask it to open again from this side.”
> “That was opening.” Ivara slid through before the sinking slab could reconsider.
- Problem: Ground/referent and Ground/uptake. The door is already sinking open and Mazin is still outside, so "open again from this side" has no recoverable meaning. "It" could be the door or the arrow, and "this side" is where they stand, where the door has just opened. Ivara's "That was opening" answers nothing a listener could have understood; it reads like a pun on "opening" with nothing set up. The outline's intent (Mazin doubting they can shut it or get back out) never reaches the page, and the reader stalls on the story's first real exchange.
- Fix: Give Mazin a plain, literal worry. The most useful one is whether this door will let them out again from inside, which plants the lost-exit problem that pays off at L237–L241. Then either cut Ivara's reply and let her slide through, or give her a brief cocky answer to that question. Keep "before the sinking slab could reconsider." Illustrative only: “And when we want out? Does it open from in there?”

### D2 · Major · Slab argument runs on rebuttals and an epigram, then narration explains the hurt

- Where: L73–L85, ledge at the third window, the decision before the shot through the slab.
- Text:
> “Ours, if we stop making it easy to deny.”
> “A door and some circles are a rumor with measurements.”
> “You came because I don't need to.”
> Tressa's face closed in the particular way that meant Ivara had struck what she aimed at and should have aimed elsewhere.
- Problem: This decision starts the escalation, but it plays out as six evenly weighted rebuttals (Tennis Match / correction ping-pong). Tressa's "easy to deny" leaves the reader to work out what they are doing that makes the claim deniable. Trespassing further? Breaking in? Ivara replies with a polished epigram in Tressa's own evidentiary register, so the two women sound alike exactly where they should diverge (Identical Voices). Her last line is a comeback that wins the round. The narration then explains its effect with an archery metaphor, so the reader never has to infer the sting. And since the story never shows why Tressa came, the sting is asserted rather than felt.
- Fix: Keep the content: Tressa wants to leave and return with a lawful party, Ivara fears losing the claim, Ivara hears the chain as a latch, and Tressa objects that she can't see it. Have Tressa say concretely what makes their claim deniable. Replace Ivara's epigram with the fear L49 gives her (other people's names on the wall maps), said as a hungry young hunter would say it. The jab can stay if it is less of a clean win: let her overreach or half-retract. Replace the L85 gloss with a physical reaction. Illustrative only: “A lawful party brings its own names. They'd put mine at the bottom, if they put it anywhere.”

### D3 · Major · Quarrel after the separation stays composed and evenly volleyed

- Where: L167–L185, after the wall drops and the chalk mark vanishes, with Mazin trapped behind stone.
- Text:
> “The ledge moved.”
> “After your shot.”
> “I noticed.”
> “Did you? You looked pleased.”
> “I would prefer,” he called, “that you save the quarrel until I can enjoy it properly.”
- Problem: Tressa has just lost Mazin behind a wall and their chalk mark back to the exit, yet she and Ivara trade seven short, evenly sized lines with a pickup ("I noticed." / "Did you?"), like fencers (Tennis Match; uniform fluency under pressure). With the tags covered, either woman could say "The ledge moved." or "After your shot." Mazin's interjection then reuses "prefer" for a neat topper, the second echo-joke of this kind after "recorded"/"record" at L15–L19. The content is right. Tressa's accusation that Ivara enjoyed it, "Would you prefer I couldn't do it?", and the honest "At present, very much." make the strongest relationship turn in the story.
- Fix: Keep "Would you prefer I couldn't do it?" and "At present, very much." Roughen what leads up to them. Let Tressa's fear show in how she speaks (the outline's own plan has her language stripping down under separation): she can fixate on the lost mark instead of scoring points. Cut the "I noticed." / "Did you?" pickup so the accusation lands without a volley. Mazin can still interrupt through the wall, but with need or complaint (he is alone, with one skin of water), not a crafted echo of "prefer."

### D4 · Major · Climax bickering closes on a polished topper

- Where: L307–L315, collapsing bridge, Tressa and Mazin disputing the capstone's tilt.
- Text:
> “Eleven?” Mazin said. “It tilted a bit.”
> “Your bit is not reproducible.”
> “Neither is eleven degrees while we're falling through the world.”
- Problem: The clash is structurally needed, because Ivara must stop them and hold both versions. But it is written as correction ping-pong ending on an epigram (Punchline Patch at a key moment). Tressa corrects his word, and Mazin turns her phrase back on her for the last word. The bridge is dropping and a page has just fallen into another space. A few lines later Mazin can only manage a "short, frightened laugh" (L329), yet here he produces the story's most polished comeback. As written, Ivara's "Stop." interrupts a witty exchange rather than a frightened tangle.
- Fix: Keep "eleven degrees" against "a bit." Make it messier: overlap, interruption, someone not finishing, Mazin insisting on what his hand remembers rather than scoring off her wording. Cut or blunt "Neither is eleven degrees while we're falling through the world." so that "Stop." at L315 cuts through noise, not a punchline. Keep L315–L319 as written.

### D5 · Major · Final exchange states the lesson as Tressa's credit list

- Where: L389–L399, dawn outside the entrance, deciding what goes in the record.
- Text:
> “Feeling is not location.”
> “Mazin saw the stone. I fixed its angle. You did whatever that was.”
> “The finding needs a primary name.”
- Problem: Philosopher at the final meaningful exchange. Tressa's triad spells out the story's point (the target was assembled from three people's perception), which L319–L341 already dramatized. The outline's own anti-default asked for coordinated attention to land "through action, not a teamwork speech." "Feeling is not location" is one more correction-epigram in the Ivara–Tressa volley pattern. "The finding needs a primary name" exists mainly to set up Ivara's silence, which the narration then explains (see P2).
- Fix: Keep Tressa refusing to record an impact she did not see. That is her integrity, and it sets up the arrow as evidence. Keep "You did whatever that was." (it is funny and hers) and Mazin's water line. Cut "Mazin saw the stone. I fixed its angle.", or turn it into Tressa writing without announcing the tally. Make "Feeling is not location" a plainer refusal. If "primary name" stays, let the arrow's arrival carry Ivara's non-answer, with no narration explaining it.

### D6 · Minor · Mazin corrects a tooth count nobody mentioned

- Where: L215–L221, rescue setup; payoff at L231.
- Text:
> “I said the fourth tooth,” Mazin called.
> “You said perhaps the fifth.”
- Problem: Ground/uptake. Tressa's instruction just before (L215) says "The loop is here" over a folded paper Mazin cannot see and names no tooth, so his correction answers nothing he heard. It works as a comic runner that pays off in "Fourth," he gasped, but as written the setup is manufactured.
- Fix: Give Mazin something audible to correct: have Tressa say the tooth aloud as she places the loop ("by the fifth tooth"). Keep "It became the fourth when I counted again." and the "Fourth" payoff.

### D7 · Minor · Short correction volleys between Ivara and Tressa

- Where: L209–L213 (Tressa's failing diagram), L283–L287 (Tressa reading the route in the hollow).
- Text:
> “It is a room.”
> “You need a target.”
> “I need the stone itself.”
- Problem: Each volley makes sense on its own and carries a real disagreement about method (map versus feel). Combined with D2–D5, though, they make flat contradiction answered by flat counter the default Ivara–Tressa rhythm. In both, Tressa answers with a crisp correction and Ivara caps it.
- Fix: Vary one or both. At L211 Tressa can keep drawing, or answer by action (the torn margin already does the real work). At L285–L287 Tressa can keep reading the route aloud over Ivara, or Ivara's line can come as a plea rather than a counter. Word-level changes suffice.

### D8 · Minor · An explained joke and a modern-idiom quip

- Where: L41 (entrance), L261 (central plinth).
- Text:
> “I could also draw you taller.” Tressa stepped into the dark. “Neither would improve the evidence.”
> “Perhaps is doing heroic work,” Mazin said.
- Problem: "I could also draw you taller" lands by itself. "Neither would improve the evidence" explains it and gives Tressa a second last word. "Perhaps is doing heroic work" is a contemporary idiom (the "doing a lot of heavy lifting" family), neat and writerly. Mazin's humor elsewhere is blunt and physical.
- Fix: Cut "Neither would improve the evidence." At L261, give Mazin a plainer, more physical doubt, or cut the line and let Tressa's "perhaps" hang.

### D9 · Minor · Loose referents and untagged lines in three-way runs

- Where: L157–L163 (after the separation), L197–L201 (thumb exchange), L303 (hand on the plinth).
- Text:
> “Describe what you see.”
> “They fit an arrow nock,” Ivara said.
> “Everything comes back to arrows with you.”
> “Put your hand how it lay.”
- Problem: At L159, "A wall." could be Ivara (sulky) or Mazin (deadpan). Both readings work, but the reader pauses. At L201 the untagged line could be Mazin teasing or Tressa continuing the quarrel, and those mean very different things. At L199, a thumb does not literally fit a nock, so the retort reads slightly off. At L303, "it" could be his hand, the water skin or the stone, and Mazin's demonstration only resolves it afterward.
- Fix: Tag L159 and L201, or give them a beat. Decide whether L201 is affection or a barb (Mazin is the likelier speaker). Make L199 a plainer brag about her hands. Make L303 name the stone.

## Prose findings

### P1 · Major · The enclosed-chamber shot is staged in a way that contradicts itself

- Where: L115, L133, L139, the chamber on the way back.
- Text:
> It stood beyond a waist-high opening, near enough to touch and entirely enclosed.
> Cold air breathed from the opening, although no opening existed on the chamber's side.
> The arrow crossed the opening, entered bare stone, and emerged inside the chamber without breaking either surface.
- Problem: This is the turning-point shot, and the reader cannot picture it. If they can see the sand, peg and relief through a waist-high opening, the arrow should fly straight in. If bare stone lies between the opening and the chamber, how can they see inside? "Near enough to touch and entirely enclosed" and "no opening existed on the chamber's side" suggest an intended one-way window, but the text never commits. L139's "arrow-thin line joined the ledge to the sealed chamber" depends on knowing where the barrier is.
- Fix: Decide the geometry and state it in one concrete sentence. For example, the opening shows the chamber but ends in stone they can touch, or they see the chamber through a crack too narrow for an arrow. Then make L133 match: the arrow meets the stone they can touch and comes out inside. Keep the impossibility and lose the contradiction.

### P2 · Major · The ending explains its point in narration and emblem after the action has landed

- Where: L337, L341, L401, L411, L413 (climax and final beat; coordinate with D5).
- Text:
> Neither knew the stone whole. Ivara let their incompatible pieces remain incompatible and held them together anyway.
> Yesterday, Ivara would have answered before the pencil finished rising.
> Only when both of them had seen the arrow standing there did Ivara reach for it.
> Beside the fresh entry, she ruled one line wide enough for three names.
- Problem: The story delivers its lesson five ways: in narration (L337, and L341's "failed to notice"), in dialogue (L319, L395), in an explained gesture (L411), in a before-and-after comparison (L401) and in a closing emblem (L413). The style guide's story craft defaults warn against exactly this layering: "once a point is clear, do not routinely explain it again in narration, dialogue, policy, and a closing symbol." The returned arrow and Ivara holding back already show the change.
- Fix: Cut L401. Rewrite L411 as action without the "Only when … did" construction that explains her motive: her hand stops, she waits, then she reaches. Keep one closing carrier. The three-name line answers the prompt's "place among the great explorers" and can stay once D5 removes the credit list. Trim L337's second sentence, which explains what the next paragraph shows.

### P3 · Minor · Negation reversals and fragment runs used for emphasis

- Where: L99, L135, L267, L271, L341 (lighter instances at L63, L265).
- Text:
> There was no slit. No hole.
> No wall. No angle too sharp.
> It did not break; it was simply gone.
> A picture of a closure was not a closure.
> Not north. Not above. Not a distance.
- Problem: In a story about impossible geometry some negation is natural. Here, though, the "No A. No B." fragment and the "not X; Y" reversal are the default emphasis at every escalation, and L271 becomes an aphorism in narration.
- Fix: Keep one or two where they earn it (L341's run at the climax is the best case). Turn the others into plain description of what was there: whole stone, powder on the head. Cut L271, since "The target felt dead beneath her attention" already does the work.

### P4 · Minor · One-line paragraphs as the default rhythm at every turn

- Where: the climax L339–L373; also L89–L97 and L131–L141.
- Text:
> She did not choose among them.
> She held the outer stone and drew.
> For a breath, nothing happened.
> Then an impact struck from beyond every wall.
- Problem: About 40 of roughly 100 narration paragraphs are one sentence long. In the climax nearly every beat stands alone, which flattens emphasis instead of creating it.
- Fix: Merge some pairs (L347 with L349, L357 with L359, L363 with L365). Keep isolated lines for the two or three beats that deserve them, such as "Ivara released." and "The rings stopped."

### P5 · Minor · Narration explains lines that already landed

- Where: L243, L335.
- Text:
> It was the first admission that silenced both of them.
> It made no sense. It was exactly what she needed.
- Problem: L243 tells the reader how to weigh Ivara's "I don't know where they are," and "first" is ambiguous. L335 explains Mazin's blanket simile, which already works. (L85 is covered in D2.)
- Fix: Cut L243 or replace it with the others' physical reaction. Cut or halve L335.

### P6 · Minor · A well used as a simile (water-infrastructure policy)

- Where: L43, the entrance stairs.
- Text:
> Below, Azhra swallowed light as if the desert had been built over a well.
- Problem: Policy nit. The `universe/style-guide.md` "No default water infrastructure" rule explicitly covers metaphor, and the prompt does not ask for it. This well is default texture.
- Fix: Take the depth image from the story's own materials (stone, dark, the rings), or just say the dark ate the lamplight. Avoid swapping in another public-works image such as a mine shaft.

## Continuity and prompt findings

- Where: L143; it carries the rescue at L189, L203–L205, L213, L223.
- Text:

- Problem: The rope has not been mentioned before (L15 shows Mazin only with water skins), yet it becomes the rescue mechanism. The reader first meets it at the moment it matters.

### C2 · Minor · The hardened arrow is never retrieved or introduced

- Where: L97–L105 (slab arrow), L273, L343, L405.
- Text:
> Her arrow lay on the other side.
> Only one had the narrow hardened head that had survived the stone.
- Problem: Ivara stares at the slab arrow (L105) but is never shown picking it up, and "hardened head" first appears at L273 as if already known. The climax and the final image both depend on this arrow.
- Fix: Add half a sentence around L105–L113 in which she retrieves it and notices the head survived the stone. L273 can then stand.

### C3 · Minor · Two physical staging slips

- Where: L143 (the wall drops), L227–L229 (Mazin comes through).
- Text:
> A wall dropped between the three of them.
> The partition rose one handspan.
- Problem: One wall cannot drop "between the three of them"; it separates Mazin from the other two, as L145 says. And a handspan gap is too narrow for Mazin to come through "sideways, shoulder first," even with gravity misbehaving.
- Fix: Put the wall between Mazin and the other two. Raise the partition to a gap a body can pass (knee-high, say), or state that he squeezed through a warped gap wider than it looked.

### C4 · Minor · The capstone is recalled in detail but never planted, and L341 contradicts the opening line

- Where: L291 and L307 (recall), L341 against L11.
- Text:
> “There was a dark cap near the entrance,” he said.
> She found the first page of her entrance sketch.
> A place Ivara had stood beside and failed to notice because she had been looking at the door.
> At the mouth of the Azhra Labyrinth, Ivara aimed away from the door.
- Problem: The climax target is built from memories of a stone and a sketch that the opening never shows; L11–L47 mention only the ledger, the scripts and the warning pillar. Keeping the stone out of Ivara's attention is right for close POV, but a glancing plant through what Mazin or Tressa is doing would make the recall fair. L341 also says she was "looking at the door," while the first sentence has her aiming away from it, which muddles the irony.
- Fix: At L15 or L47, let Mazin set the water skins against a hot dark rock, or have Tressa sketch the entrance as well as the scripts, without Ivara looking. In L341, replace "looking at the door" with what she was actually watching (the latch, the chip, the slit).
