# Opus review: Not About That

Independent audit for a later editing pass. It is not a `review.md` verdict,
does not change canon state, and does not authorize edits by itself.

## Verdict

- Dialogue: REVISE — the line that sets off the prompt's outburst doesn't fit its own setup, a neighbour identifies the transformed Lowen with no way of knowing, and the confrontation depends on a fact the reader isn't given until L251.
- Prose: REVISE — narration explains the meaning of nearly every emotional beat right after it lands; there are also a few muddled sentences.
- Continuity and prompt: PASS — the required line is verbatim at L121, the crowded magical-girl world is delivered, and the story runs about 3,300 words; only Minor staging, object and policy issues remain.
- Edit scope: targeted-dialogue
- Existing review.md said: Verdict PASS; "Dialogue: PASS"; I disagree. It missed two Ground failures and the late reveal of Sarena's transness.

The dialogue is livelier and better differentiated than the collection's norm. It has sibling comedy with real mishearing, ordinary coordination lines ("Where are you going?" / "To put these inside."), a plain, specific apology, and a strong wordless rescue built on gesture. Every major exchange changes something between the siblings. The failures cluster at the hinges. Sarena's cut-off "You like being—" can't grammatically end in "flying", so her sincere correction reads like a cover. The auntie's first line gives her knowledge she can't have. The reader learns Sarena is trans only from narration at L251, so the outburst and "How would you know?" land as a quarrel about "assuming" rather than as Lowen wounding his sister. Right after the outburst, the siblings trade balanced, clipped rebuttals, when a stuttering Lowen should babble and a hurt Sarena should say less. The edit pass should repair these few exchanges and anchor the final "Do you believe me?", then cut about half of the narration that glosses each beat. Keep the physical comedy and the sibling pettiness intact.

## Edit guardrails

- The prompt line must stay exactly as it is at L121, including "J-Just" and both sentences: "J-Just because I have to turn into a girl to use my magical powers doesn't mean I'm trans! Being a magical girl isn't about that!"
- Keep the crowded, ordinary magical-girl sky (L29, L233, L281) that the prompt asks for ("a world full of magical girls"). universe/factions.md "Solstice's historical magical-girl circles and coronation compact" (CANON) leaves the circles' fading undated, so the modern texture (phones, trams) does not contradict canon. Do not add a date, a queen, or any link to Solstice's crown that would pin the era.
- universe/rules.md "Living agency and magic" (LOCKED): only life uses magic. Keep L13's rule that the focus "had no mind with which to want him" and only shapes what Lowen supplies. Edits must not give the focus intentions.
- universe/style-guide.md "Personhood and moral consequence" (LOCKED) and the outline's intent: the body and the focus settle no label. Lowen's stated boyhood stays authoritative, with no egg-crack reveal. Sarena's girlhood does not depend on magic, and she must not become a spokesperson who explains being trans. Her anger stays unforgiven at the end ("I'm still mad").
- Keep the rating broadly accessible and the length in the 2,500–4,000 band; it is about 3,300 words now, so small additions are fine.
- Close third-person through Lowen; never enter Sarena's head.
- What works and must not be flattened: the slapstick (the oranges, the pillowcase, the basil, the bloomers, the turnip wand), Sarena's consent-based injury check, the wordless wind-knot rescue, the plain apology at L301–L307, and the photograph ending.

## Dialogue findings

### D1 · Blocking · "being—" cannot end in "flying"

- Where: L115–L129, the opposite roof, the setup and denial around the prompt line
- Text:
> “You like being—”
> “I was going to say flying,” she said.
- Problem: Ground/uptake at the story's hinge. "You like being—" needs a state to finish it ("a girl", "like this", "up there"); "You like being flying" isn't English. Lowen's misreading is therefore reasonable, but Sarena's correction doesn't fit her own words, so it reads as a quick cover. That clashes with L159, where the narration insists she had no "diagnosis" and no "secret knowledge". The reader can't tell whether Sarena is sincere, and the whole rupture depends on her being sincere.
- Fix: Make the setup and the correction match, and change nothing else. Option A (preferred): cut "being" so the setup is just "You like—", and keep "I was going to say flying" as written. Option B: keep "You like being—" and change the completion to a state that fits it (Illustrative only: "I was going to say up there."). Keep L115, where her gaze moves from his face to the panes and back, as the ambiguous evidence.

### D2 · Blocking · The auntie recognises a transformed, veiled Lowen

- Where: L55–L59, the first rescue beside the fifth-floor balconies; echoed at L205
- Text:
> the pillowcase over both their heads
> From an open kitchen window, the fifth-floor auntie shouted, “Sarena, why is your brother in my clean wash?”
> “Boy,” she called, “if you stretch those, you buy them.”
- Problem: Ground/knowledge. Lowen has a different face, long black hair and a girl's voice (L19–L21), and a pillowcase covers his head. Nobody has spoken his name yet; "Lowen" is first said at L89. The auntie has no way to know this girl is Sarena's brother. This is the only outside reading of Lowen's gender in the story, so it can't rest on authorial convenience. L205 is supportable once the auntie has heard Sarena call him "Lowen", which she does at L199 within earshot.
- Fix: Give the auntie access before L59 and keep both of her lines. The smallest fix is for Sarena to shout his name as she catches his coat at L55, so a neighbour who knows the family can make the link. Another option is a clause earlier (around L31 or L43) placing the auntie at her window when he transformed or launched. Don't just change her line to "a girl"; her "Boy" is a light, useful beat once she has grounds for it.

### D3 · Major · The reader learns Sarena is trans only after the confrontation

- Where: L123–L161, the rupture exchange; L243–L251, "How would you know?"
- Text:
> Sarena’s hand dropped from his wrist.
> “How would you know?”
> Sarena had told him what his hip was doing, and he had dragged her transness into the answer as if it were an accusation.
> Once could have been panic. Twice was something uglier.
- Problem: Ground/context. In close third-person through Lowen, he knows from the first word of the outburst that he has shouted "doesn't mean I'm trans!" at his trans sister. The narration withholds that fact until L251. As a result, L129–L161 reads as an argument about whether she "assumed", and "How would you know?" (L247) reads as a fair retort until the narration decodes it afterwards. The central injury of the prompt line only lands in hindsight, and one paragraph of summary has to carry it. L251 then ends on a narrated epigram.
- Fix: Put the fact in Lowen's awareness at the moment it matters. At minimum, add a sentence or two in L123–L127 where Lowen registers whom he just said it to. Optionally, add an earlier, unemphatic plant (around L13 or L49) so the outburst stings as it happens. Present it as his knowledge, not as a speech from Sarena. Then shrink L251: keep "The words left the same silence as his outburst." and cut the explanation and the "Once/Twice" epigram, because "How would you know?" will now read as cruel on its own.

### D4 · Major · The final "Do you believe me?" has a loose referent

- Where: L387–L389, after Lowen reverts, the final meaningful exchange
- Text:
> “Do you believe me?” he asked.
> “You’re my brother.” She looked down at her phone. “You’re also blurry.”
- Problem: Ground/referent at the ending. The question could mean the original denial (that he isn't trans), his more recent "I wasn't scared of being you" (L335), or his apology. The last two claims are closer on the page. "You're my brother" works as an answer to all three, so the reader can't be sure what Sarena is granting. That matters because this is where the story resolves the prompt's line: she accepts his stated boyhood without pretending he didn't hurt her.
- Fix: Anchor the question to the claim, lightly, and let Sarena answer before he has to say it again, which mirrors the earlier cut-off. Illustrative only: "Do you believe me? That I'm not—" with Sarena's "You're my brother." cutting in. Keep "You're also blurry."

### D5 · Major · The post-outburst exchange is a clipped tennis match

- Where: L129–L145, the opposite roof, right after the prompt line
- Text:
> “I’m just saying you shouldn’t assume.”
> “You were about to.”
> “Apparently you can air-step and hear the future.”
> Her movements had gone careful and plain.
- Problem: Tennis Match with uniform fluency under pressure. Seconds after a stuttered, mortifying outburst, Lowen defends himself in tidy, even rebuttals ("I'm just saying you shouldn't assume." / "You were about to."), and Sarena closes the exchange with a polished sarcastic line. The narration says her movements have gone "careful and plain", but her speech does the opposite. The two voices match in length and register in the one exchange where they should split furthest. "I looked at you." and "No. I don't think I do." are good and should stay.
- Fix: Let Lowen run on and tangle: an over-explaining defence that gets worse the longer it goes, true to the "J-Just" in the outburst. Illustrative only: "I'm just saying people see a skirt and decide stuff, and then it's this whole— you don't have to make that face." Cut "Apparently you can air-step and hear the future." and let Sarena answer with silence and the grocery-gathering already at L147. Don't add a new retort for her.

### D6 · Minor · Zingers that close exchanges

- Where: L69 (first rescue), L221–L223 (the laundry-line apology), L349 (after "Don't make me the thing…")
- Text:
> “Today has been surprising.”
> “You never listen long enough.”
> “See, this is why apologies take you all evening.”
- Problem: Several exchanges end on a neat last word. L349 is the clearest: "See, this is why…" explains its own joke and turns the petty "Our practice box." into a sitcom button straight after the story's most serious request. L223 carries a real grievance and can stay. L69 is harmless comic banter before the rupture.
- Fix: End the L345–L349 exchange on Lowen's "Our practice box." followed by a look, a shove, or silence, and cut or roughen Sarena's explanation. Leave L223. L69 is optional; if it's trimmed, the "drop you" threat can stand on its own.

### D7 · Minor · Stiff or mismatched isolated lines

- Where: L93 (lift talk), L187–L189 (the basil planter), L213 (the laundry lines)
- Text:
> “You cannot revert until you put it back into a controlled landing.”
> Sarena’s mouth twitched.
> “Do not laugh.”
> “You float. That focus wants to spend the lift. Let it.”
> It had no mind with which to want him
- Problem: L93: you can't "put" lift "back into" a landing (verb and object don't fit), and "You cannot" is stiff for a clipped safety call. L189 follows a paragraph about Sarena's action with no tag, so it can briefly be read as Sarena speaking; the uncontracted "Do not" is also formal for a panicking fifteen-year-old. L213: Sarena, who drilled into Lowen that the focus has no mind to want anything (L13), says it "wants to spend the lift". That's a small conflict with the story's own rule (see guardrails).
- Fix: L93: make it plain, e.g., spending or grounding the lift in a controlled landing. L189: tie the line to Lowen with a beat or tag, and use "Don't". L213: give Sarena a non-intentional verb (the lift runs down or bleeds off).

## Prose findings

### P1 · Major · Narration glosses each beat's subtext

- Where: L159, L201, L229, L295, L333, L343, L351, L359 (and L251, covered in D3)
- Text:
> not a diagnosis, not secret knowledge. Anger.
> He heard instruction as command, the change in her tone as proof she had decided what he was
> The apology arrived without polish and without asking anything from him. That made it harder to refuse.
> She was offering exactly what she had, not an answer to anything else.
> Sarena gave him a look but did not finish his thought for him.
> There was no clever answer hiding behind his teeth.
> though the set of her mouth warned him not to mistake care for pardon.
> That would have been the same mistake in a fresh coat.
- Problem: This is systemic. After nearly every weighted exchange, including the climax and the ending, a sentence tells the reader what the line or gesture meant. Several are neat inversions or epigrams ("not X, not Y. Z."; "mistake care for pardon"). The dialogue and action already carry these meanings, so the reader has nothing left to infer.
- Fix: Cut about half. Cut outright: the second sentence of L229, all of the L295 sentence, "There was no clever answer hiding behind his teeth." (L343), the "though…pardon" clause (L351), and "That would have been the same mistake in a fresh coat." (L359). Trim L201 to the physical fact that he kicked harder than he needed to, and L159 to "Anger" plus at most one clause. Replace nothing with new commentary.

### P2 · Minor · Muddled sentences and staging

- Where: L57 (first rescue), L101 (injury check), L167 (Lowen alone on the roof), L219 vs L373 (the turnip)
- Text:
> the pillowcase over both their heads
> Lowen resented the courtesy more than grabbing would have deserved.
> admit that the thought of anyone enjoying this body on his behalf scared him more than the five-story drop
> as if she had hidden a turnip there during battle
> Sarena took the battered turnip from her coat pocket
- Problem: A pillowcase can't cover two heads. "More than grabbing would have deserved" is garbled. "Anyone enjoying this body on his behalf" is unclear at the one place the narration states what Lowen fears. The "as if" simile half-denies the turnip that L373 then produces.
- Fix: Keep the pillowcase on Lowen alone. Rephrase L101 as a plain comparison (he resented the courtesy more than he would have resented being grabbed). Make L167 say concretely what scares him, which is being seen enjoying it and having someone else decide what that means, without new commentary. Make L219 plainly a turnip so the L373 payoff still works.

### C3 · Minor · Water infrastructure used as default texture (policy)

- Where: L27, L33, L43, L99, L291, L295, L315, L323 (the rooftop water tank as landmark and climax anchor); L29 (rain towed to a vegetable district, borderline)

- Problem: universe/style-guide.md "No default water infrastructure" (CANON) covers water-supply works as incidental texture, and the prompt doesn't call for them. The rooftop tank is a recurring landmark and the anchor at the climax. Rain towed to a vegetable district reads as magical irrigation. The rain-calling comb (L13) and the rain globe (L233) are weather, not infrastructure, and are fine.
- Fix: Replace the tank everywhere with one non-water roof fixture that can take a wrapped strand and a hard shoulder hit. The chimney already on this roof (L21) or an aerial mast would work. Update every mention together so the climax geography stays intact. Optionally give the oilskinned woman a non-irrigation errand.
