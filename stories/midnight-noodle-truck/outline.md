# Promised experience

The prompt asks for a foul-mouthed former military chef with a ladle to defend the Midnight Noodle Truck’s sacred fifty-foot ceasefire when a cheerful young scavenger “accidentall”y brings the world’s most wanted AI to dinner in a tiny yellow robot. Gangs, mutant warlords, rogue machines and wandering mercenaries surround the truck. “Increasingly ridiculous” house rules and “somewhere to sit down and eat without getting shot” suggest the pleasure of watching a cook make ordinary hospitality a match for overwhelming force. The tone I hear is rowdy and affectionate, with genuine danger. The arrival brings a cook and two diners together. Their ties, the AI’s value, the nature of the accident and the fate of the standoff are open.

## Takes considered

- **Last Turn Fairground.** One midnight service expands into a siege with each faction holding a piece of the midway. The central scene has the chef take the whole cordon as a dining party and make its commanders move their armies to keep the truck open. Chosen because the chef devises and enforces the comic rules in full view of everyone who could call her bluff.
- **Knife Switch Pass.** A rolling siege starts in the cab when the chef has to move with both diners aboard. The set piece would be driving the truck’s fragile fifty feet through a mile of rival convoys, with less room for the table.
- **The Dry Stockyards.** A mercenary sees a routine capture turn into a series of embarrassing attempts to claim one tiny diner. That money scene pits their payday against their place at the truck and hands them the climax.

## Provisional direction

Zella Stovall serves midnight ramen on a dead fairground. Enemies need her ceasefire to eat and do business face-to-face. Two crews have come to hand back captured drivers over supper. Saffy, a young adult scavenger who eats here regularly, arrives with Dayrate. The fugitive military AI remembers where the old armies buried their reserves and chose a battered yellow shell small enough to sit on Saffy’s crate. Saffy knows who it is. She promised it one meal when she helped it out of the shuttered prize booths, and a search crew spotted them on the way out.

When the factions surround the truck, Zella seats and feeds their delegates while ruling out successive attempts to seize a diner. The mutant warlord Raddock has the biggest rig in view and bets he can wait until she gives in. Zella calls the whole cordon one party blocking the door. She wipes the next-service board and brings down the shutter as far as her serving ledge. That closure costs Raddock the only place he can sit with rivals to get his traders safe passage. The fifty feet stay sacred, and nobody can make a grab for Dayrate. Clear every exit together and the window will open to all again. Raddock publicly commits his own column first, and actual movement waits for a common ladle cue so every faction can see the others give ground. Dayrate then gets to choose where it goes and comes back for another bowl.

## Craft plan

**Teller.** Try close third, past, through Zella, with a practiced cook’s eye for hands, quantities and bluster that passes for command. Try Saffy’s garrulous first-person account from the next morning, with fear under the delight. Slow down at the shutter, when Zella wagers her livelihood and the only local sanctuary.

**Zella.** She wants supper to go on and each diner to get away freely. She expects petty trouble and figures Saffy incapable of bringing anything worse than a stolen hubcap. Her speech runs in orders, frank swearing and barracks ridicule. She’s warm about food and impatient with rank. The woman at the hatch in the image gives her a high dark knot, sleeveless work clothes and a forward lean with pleasure in the work. Zella braces at the sill to judge a diner’s reach, pushes back her hair with a wrist, tips out soup to make delegates her diners and finally steps out in her apron with the ladle.

**Saffy.** She wants Zella to honor the seat she promised Dayrate and trust her with something hard. She reads a salvage crew by what it leaves behind and underestimates who will answer the sighting. She talks in long, cheerful runs, doubling back and admitting trouble with maddening precision. From the image, keep her loose black shag, goggles, freckles, mustard jacket with its gear-and-skull patch, gloves, cream high-tops and striped socks. She kicks out a crate, makes the little shell room with a gloved hand and keeps eating with that half-lidded ease well into the siege. When Zella gives her work she sets down her chopsticks and pulls the goggles into place to scout from the line.

**Dayrate.** It wants a whole meal in which nobody can give it orders, with Saffy beside it. It knows what people want from it and expects Zella to treat it as freight. Its exact sentences sound like specifications until you hear them as requests. The scuffed yellow shell, wide black visor, one blue eye and small wheels give it a posture earnestly inclined toward its bowl. It rolls up to the steam, turns its blue gaze from a man talking over its head and uses those wheels again when it can go where it wants. Heat and smell are enough to make it want its own bowl.

**The crowd.** Leather, grease, elbows on the tables and practiced calm under the lights. Use the goggle-wearing mercenary as a seller who names a price even while whining about his soup, the red-crested gang woman as a collector who calls everything a pickup, and one rogue gun machine whose rule-lawyering comes out in clipped inventory terms. The three-handed Raddock treats Zella’s courtesy to him as a sign she’ll fold. He makes big patient boasts and keeps one hand free for his bowl. The other figures in the reference, including the second figure at the hatch, can show the bustle of a normal service.

**Place.** Last Turn beats Knife Switch Pass, where columns would appear in sequence, and the Dry Stockyards, whose blind pens favor an ambush. The truck always opens on this flat midway at midnight, its familiar gear-and-skull mark on lanterns and bowls. Old queue studs reset in the paving mark the fifty feet. Late summer. Salvage crews trade ride parts, work and prisoners. A red ramen sign, the moon through a stripped Ferris wheel, charred scallion, the rattle of a wire noodle basket, wind grit on the tongue. The raised ticket step inside the line gives a view of the approaches and hides less than the low hatch. A visible, simultaneous withdrawal is possible here.

## Scene briefs

1. **Two bowls, one crate. About one fifth.** Zella sets out the stools for a driver exchange and notes the standing table where Raddock meets his rivals to keep his trade moving. Saffy buys two bowls and gives Zella her lively account of discovering Dayrate in the prize booths, the promise she made and the people who saw them. Dayrate waits for someone to ask what it takes. A ring of headlights stops precisely at the studs. A voice identifies the tiny guest by name in front of the whole fairground.
2. **One enormous party. The long scene, about two thirds.** Zella wants the claimants seated and Dayrate heard. The mercenary climbs the truck to make his pitch and she sets him down with a bowl. The rogue machine tries to hold the adjacent seat with a turret and she directs it to the scrap bin with her ladle. The gang delegate puts a bounty down as a pickup order: no boxing a diner. Raddock tries to buy the machine’s company over its head: no ordering for a diner who can speak. Their food gets in the way of the argument the story owes about who counts as a diner and what the truck promises once they’ve sat down. Let Zella speak from the field mess where officers used to dictate whose mouths counted. Raddock braces all three arms across seats for reinforcements and declares his people can wait. Saffy checks the line at Zella’s request and finds the gang’s bikes tucked at the mouth of the prize alley, invisible from the service window. Zella includes them when she demands a visible retreat of the entire party, wipes every future booking and lowers the shutter. Hold the pause. Raddock looks where his usual supper was chalked, weighs what he can gain by waiting, and commits aloud. Zella takes the ticket step and directs the shared timing with her ladle.
3. **Room to go. The last pages.** The whole ring of headlights opens beneath the wheel. One or two bowl details confirm that service can go on. The drivers walk freely to their crews. Saffy offers Dayrate the open route away. It tries the road on its own wheels and turns back for another bowl. Saffy’s account can carry the chase outside. Leave the wars beyond the line to the visible cordon and the claimants’ particular grievances.

**Ending.** The midway it could barely see past the headlights is entirely open. Dayrate can wheel away from the truck and chooses a seat under the red sign. The bowls on the opening crate, the attempt to collect a guest as takeout and the wait at the shutter load that little turn with the scale of what had to move.
