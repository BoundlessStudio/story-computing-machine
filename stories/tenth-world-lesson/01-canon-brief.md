## Hard constraints

- The story must deliver the captured prompt contract: this is the protagonist’s tenth assigned world; the visibly exhausted goddess has an interior motive; nine prior successes have produced competence but not self-worth; the tenth conflict forces self-valuation apart from usefulness, victory, or praise; and the protagonist makes a costly, self-directed choice. The resolution cannot rely solely on defeating a dark lord, romantic validation, ridicule of shut-in isolation, or an instant cure. (`stories/tenth-world-lesson/00-prompt.md` — “Required elements”, “Prohibited elements”, and “Completion tests”)
- The setting’s established Earth, Ravel, and Glass-Sea labels describe eras of one physical world, not separate planets or planes. Do not identify them as three of the protagonist’s assignments. Other dimensions or timelines may exist, but canon does not yet define them. (`universe/premise.md` — “The deep-time shared world”; `universe/locations.md` — “The world across eras”)
- Treat “world” as a local, character-facing term unless the story deliberately and narrowly establishes more. Ten assignments do not prove ten universes, a multiverse taxonomy, or a universal portal network. (`universe/rules.md` — “Portals and crossing systems”; `universe/glossary.md` — “Realms”)
- Any transfer system must be bounded and internally consistent. Its access, direction, limits, recurrence, bodily effects, return conditions, and operator belong to this local mechanism and must not be presented as rules for every crossing. It is unrelated to the Glass Gate, Queen Street seam, and other established crossings unless explicit evidence establishes otherwise. (`universe/rules.md` — “Portals and crossing systems” and “Artifacts, motifs, and folklore”)
- Magic, technology, and divinity are cultural labels for underlying systems. If a nonliving assignment device, ledger, portal, or office appears to wield magic independently, establish that it is alive or identify the living agent supplying the magic. (`universe/rules.md` — “System categories” and “Living agency and magic”)
- Objective godhood requires the continuing conjunction of worship, power, and authority. Calling the character a goddess may instead be a local or claimed title, but the prose and canon delta must not silently certify objective godhood without satisfying that taxonomy. No universal divine government spans all systems or eras. (`universe/rules.md` — “Divinity”; `universe/premise.md` — “Knowledge and connection”)
- Do not identify this goddess with the existing woman claiming the `Goddess of Time` title or with Queen Aurelia, whose cultural alias is `the Goddess`. Their identities, authorities, and mechanisms are distinct and partly unresolved. (`universe/characters.md` — “The woman claiming the Goddess of Time title” and “Queen Aurelia”; `universe/glossary.md` — “Goddess of Time (claimed title)”)
- If assignment follows death, reincarnation, resurrection, soul transfer, or repeated bodily reconstruction, its relationship to the universal underlying afterlife must be coherently defined. The safer prompt-compatible course is living transport with continuous memory and personhood. (`universe/rules.md` — “Afterlife and continuation”; `universe/style-guide.md` — “Personhood and moral consequence”)
- Cost, desire, consent, sacrifice, praise, and usefulness may matter to this local system, but none is a universal metaphysical fuel. Once introduced, their local effects and ethical consequences must remain consistent. (`universe/rules.md` — “Costs, desire, and local mechanisms”)
- The protagonist’s isolation, coping behavior, identity, consent, and recovery remain morally real. A divine explanation cannot reduce the protagonist to a defective instrument or make the repeated assignments an automatic therapeutic cure. (`universe/style-guide.md` — “Personhood and moral consequence”)
- Keep the treatment teen-accessible and non-graphic, with character and consequence prioritized over spectacle. (`universe/style-guide.md` — “Audience and content boundaries”)

## Relevant established context and precedents

- Reality is coherent backstage even when characters possess incomplete or incorrect cosmological models. The protagonist and goddess may call assignments “worlds” without the narration confirming their ultimate ontology. (`universe/premise.md` — “Knowledge and connection”)
- Independent audience entry points are permitted. This story needs no crossover, named established era, or explanation of the whole shared universe. (`universe/style-guide.md` — “Tonal range, scale, and connection”)
- *Realms* establishes that poorly understood destinations reached through anomalous doors may receive a collective local name without proving distinct worlds, planes, or one network. Its Queen Street mechanism is not available for reuse by implication. (`stories/realms/05-story.md` — “Realms”; `universe/glossary.md` — “Realms”)
- *The Trouble With Tuesdays* establishes a tired, dryly comic woman using a claimed goddess title, constrained by a local contract, who teaches without coercing the mortal’s final choice. This is a relevant continuity and originality precedent, not a template to repeat. The new goddess requires a different identity, domain, mechanism, emotional motive, and dramatic relationship. (`stories/the-trouble-with-tuesdays/05-story.md` — “The Trouble With Tuesdays”; `universe/rules.md` — “Fenwick watch ownership contract and audit”; `universe/characters.md` — “The woman claiming the Goddess of Time title”)
- The existing Time-title bearer’s personal name, origin, worship, objective godhood, and wider authority remain unresolved. Those omissions cannot be filled by making the new goddess her colleague, superior, subordinate, or alternate form. (`universe/glossary.md` — “Goddess of Time (claimed title)”)
- Queen Aurelia’s cultural title `the Goddess` also does not prove taxonomic godhood. Shared goddess language creates no relationship among these characters. (`universe/characters.md` — “Queen Aurelia”; `universe/rules.md` — “Artifacts, motifs, and folklore”)

## Conflicts and collision risks

- **Contradiction:** None in the captured prompt. The story may proceed without a user canon ruling.
- **Omission, not contradiction:** Canon does not define what the ten “worlds” are, where they sit relative to the shared physical world, or whether they are planets, dimensions, timelines, constructed environments, or culturally labeled regions.
- **Conditional contradiction:** Declaring Earth, Ravel, and the Glass-Sea world to be separate assigned worlds would contradict locked authority.
- **Conditional contradiction:** Presenting the goddess as objectively divine without the required worship-power-authority conjunction would contradict the established godhood taxonomy.
- **Conditional contradiction:** Establishing a universal isekai administration or divine bureaucracy spanning all eras would contradict the absence of a universal hidden authority and universal divine government.
- **Title collision:** `the Goddess of Time`, `Goddess of Time`, and `Time` identify one existing character. `the Goddess` identifies Queen Aurelia in character-facing use. These forms must not name the new goddess.
- **Structural collision:** A tired goddess conducting a dryly comic lesson while being unable to force the mortal’s choice closely resembles *The Trouble With Tuesdays*. Differentiate the tenth-world story through its repeated-assignment history, self-worth focus, distinct local system, and the goddess’s unique personal stake.
- **Terminology collision:** `Realms` and `the Realms` are established Runner shorthand. Avoid using them as the name of the ten-world program unless an intentional crossover is approved.

## Unknowns

- The protagonist’s name, age, original era, home culture, duration of isolation, and circumstances of the first assignment.
- Whether the protagonist has remained continuously alive and embodied across all ten assignments.
- The nature, location, chronology, inhabitants, and survival stakes of the first nine assignments.
- What the goddess means by “world,” who authorizes assignments, how destinations are selected, and why ten attempts were possible.
- Whether the goddess objectively qualifies as a god, merely uses a cultural title, or occupies some other living role.
- The goddess’s source and limits of power, costs, worship, authority, obligations, peers, and relationship to the transfer system.
- Why the goddess is exhausted and why the protagonist’s self-worth matters personally to her.
- What the tenth world needs, why conventional heroism is insufficient, and what costly self-directed choice becomes available.
- Whether powers, rewards, bodies, injuries, memories, or social relationships persist between assignments.
- How the protagonist returns, stays, refuses, or exits; canon supplies no default isekai mechanism.

These are omissions and safe planning questions, not evidence for global lore.

## Safe local invention space

- Create nine compact prior-assignment memories and one fully dramatized tenth assignment, provided their mechanics do not silently become universal rules.
- Use “world” as the protagonist’s and goddess’s practical label while leaving the destinations’ ultimate cosmological classification unresolved.
- Create one local, living-agent-operated assignment system with explicit limits. A waiting room, threshold, office, token, ledger, or portal may support the system but cannot independently wield magic unless established as alive.
- Make the new goddess a distinct living person with a unique name or title, bounded authority, and a motive rooted in this relationship. Her exhaustion may arise from repeated care, failure, constrained stewardship, personal identification, or costs of transfer.
- Let the goddess be called a goddess culturally or by claim while leaving objective godhood unresolved. If objective godhood is important, explicitly establish worship, power, and authority within her local scope.
- Prefer living transfer over repeated death. If death or reincarnation is essential, define continuity, consent, memory, embodiment, and afterlife implications without erasing personhood.
- Make the tenth world’s problem resist “hero as weapon” logic: it may need witness, refusal, repair, relinquishment, ordinary participation, accountable rest, or a choice that cannot be optimized into victory.
- The costly choice may involve surrendering accumulated power, refusing another assignment, accepting an uncertain ordinary life, relinquishing praise or guaranteed return, or remaining responsible without being indispensable. The exact choice belongs to planning.
- Keep the cast and setting independent of established characters, factions, locations, crossing systems, and divine figures.
- Record only narrowly reusable facts in the proposed canon delta. Missing cosmology should remain unresolved rather than becoming a multiverse rule.

## Concrete canon recommendation

Proceed as a standalone, locally bounded portal-fantasy entry. Treat the “ten worlds” as the protagonist’s assignment term rather than an objective cosmological census. Keep their relationship to the shared physical world unresolved. Make the goddess a new, living, person-like figure with a unique character-facing name or domain title, explicit local limits, and no relationship to the Goddess of Time, Queen Aurelia, or a universal divine administration.

Transport the protagonist alive with continuous memory unless the story has room to define an afterlife-compatible alternative. Center the tenth world on a crisis where competent intervention would reproduce the protagonist’s usefulness trap, while the costly resolution requires choosing a life, boundary, or responsibility because the protagonist values themself—not because victory or praise proves their worth.

## Name constraints

- `the Goddess of Time`, `Goddess of Time`, and `Time` are reserved for the existing chronal visitor.
- `the Goddess` is reserved as Queen Aurelia’s cultural alias. Do not use it as the new character’s sole capitalized identity.
- `Aven` is reserved for the existing canon hero in `the-last-thing-i-could-fix`; do not reuse it for this protagonist.
- Generic lowercase `goddess` and `hero` remain usable as role descriptions when they are not functioning as a reserved proper name.
- The prompt supplies no personal names. Default to unique, readily distinguishable names and titles. Check full names, given names, mononyms, aliases, titles used as names, named constructs, and person-like entities against every reservation in `stories/NAMES.md`.
