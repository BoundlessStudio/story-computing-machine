# Opus review: The Tenth World Needs No Hero

Independent audit for a later editing pass. It is not a `review.md` verdict,
does not change canon state, and does not authorize edits by itself.

## Verdict

- Dialogue: REVISE — the three exchanges that carry the prompt's emotional load (Eiren's confession, Jori's diagnoses of Ryo, the aftermath settlement) come out as composed aphorisms and mirrored clauses.
- Prose: REVISE — from the failed seam attempt onward, the narrator follows most beats with a one-line aphorism explaining what they meant.
- Continuity and prompt: PASS — every prompt requirement and completion test is delivered; one Minor slip in when the return door opens.
- Edit scope: targeted-dialogue
- Existing review.md said: `04-review.md` pass 2 final, PASS with zero findings and no Dialogue line recorded; I disagree on dialogue and on the closing narration.

The dialogue is strongest where the story is lightest and weakest where it matters most. Ryo has a real voice: status reports, lists, literal corrections, and requests for the next task or for certainty. The threshold-room comedy, the blue-grain thread, the "Stop helping!" clash, Jori's practical instructions and Ryo's final text message all work. The failure comes at the story's key moments. Eiren, bleeding at a shrinking door, delivers her confession as four finished epigrams. Jori, a stranger, twice sums up Ryo's lesson in the house "not X, but Y" pattern. The exhausted pair settle their relationship with a mirrored "You are not allowed" / "Agreed" routine. Jori also borrows Eiren's arch, uncontracted buttons, so two of the three voices blur. The edit should keep every LOCKED content beat but roughen delivery at those points (shorter, interrupted, contracted, concrete, with one plain hard sentence left standing). It should then trim the narrator's matching aphorisms so the action can land on its own.

## Dialogue findings

### D1 · Major · Eiren's confession is polished aphorism under collapse

- Where: L227–L245, the stillness after Ryo's failed solo attempt; rain arriving, Eiren's palms cut, her door failing.
- Text:
> "This is not the moment for your biography."
> "It is the moment for my confession." Her voice shook, but did not soften.
> I mistook the cost for evidence that I loved people properly.
> Your success made my mistake look like care.
> "So I was a lesson."
> I used those facts to avoid asking what our yes was serving.
- Problem: Ryo's "biography" line exists only so Eiren can correct it to "confession". That is a Punchline Patch, and correction ping-pong at the story's central reveal. Eiren fell to one knee moments earlier and her palms are bleeding, yet she delivers four finished epigrams in a row (uniform fluency under pressure; Philosopher). Narration says her voice shook, but her sentences never do. "What our yes was serving" is abstract enough that the reader has to translate it: she used his consent to avoid asking whose need the assignments met. Ryo's "So I was a lesson" picks up the teaching framing and skips the part that should hurt most, that his asking for another world reassured her.
- Fix: Keep every content beat listed in the guardrails (LOCKED under "Eiren" in `universe/characters.md`). Replace Ryo's "biography" line with plain urgency that she overrides, and cut "It is the moment for my confession." Let the strain break her sentences: say one hard fact plainly, then fragments, a restart, a glance at the door. Rewrite at most one of the epigrams as plain speech and cut at least one other. Consider having Ryo's uptake answer "it reassured me" rather than "lesson". Keep "Tell me the correct choice now." and her refusal unchanged. Illustrative only: Ryo: "Eiren. The door." / Eiren: "When you asked for another one, I was glad. I was glad, Ryo."

### D2 · Major · The aftermath settles the relationship by contract

- Where: L385–L403, threshold-room floor after the return.
- Text:
> "Thank you for bringing me back," he said. "That does not make everything you did right."
> "You are not allowed to make me your project again."
> "Agreed. You are not allowed to make yourself mine."
- Problem: This is the final meaningful Ryo–Eiren exchange. They have just collapsed, he is "extremely singed", and both want to sleep for a week. Yet it runs as ten balanced, alternating lines and closes on a terms-and-conditions routine ("not allowed" / "Agreed. … not allowed"): a negotiation routine and a tennis match. Ryo's thanks is a balanced sentence that sounds like a legal ruling, not a tired man speaking. "Make yourself mine" is an elided mirror, so the reader has to reconstruct "my project". A quick reader can take "mine" as possessive, which grazes the prompt's ban on love substituting for self-worth.
- Fix: Keep the LOCKED beats (`universe/timeline.md`, tenth-assignment note 6: neither is fixed, care does not erase harm, the project-and-hero relationship ends). Also keep "No." / "You are extremely singed." and "You should rest." / "An outrageous proposal." Make the thanks-plus-anger plain and contracted. Ryo's "not allowed" request can stay in contracted form ("Don't make me your project again."). Replace Eiren's mirror with an unsymmetrical agreement that spells out her half and is slowed or interrupted by exhaustion. Illustrative only: "Thanks for getting me out. I'm still angry." / Eiren, after long enough that he thought she had fallen asleep: "And don't volunteer for it either."

### D3 · Major · Jori explains Ryo's lesson to him

- Where: L175–L177 (engine lighting up, crowd praising) and L247–L249 (Jori's crew with their hands in station nine).
- Text:
> Their expression changed from anger to something worse: understanding without admiration.
> "It is not choosing you because you are destined. It is choosing you because you keep volunteering for the whole load."
> "We do not need you to be sure," Jori called. "We need you to stop taking away everyone else's chance to act."
- Problem: Jori has known Ryo for an hour, yet twice delivers the story's thesis about him in the house antithesis template ("It is not X. It is Y." / "We do not need X. We need Y."). This is the Philosopher, and it duplicates Eiren's role as the one who diagnoses Ryo. The first instance repeats what narration (L157) and Jori's own shout (L163) already established, and the narrator explains Jori's face before Jori speaks. The second is shouted across a storm by someone with their hands in a live housing, yet it comes out as a balanced maxim. Jori's own stake never enters their speech: the years spent building the stations, and six crew members risking their hands right now.
- Fix: Keep the LOCKED content: Jori sees that Ryo's volunteering is re-centralizing the engine, and asks him to stop taking away everyone else's chance to act. The second sentence at L249 can stay nearly verbatim. At L175–L177, cut the gloss and the "destined/volunteering" antithesis. Keep "Every job you take disables a station," and add what Jori is looking at. At L249, replace "We do not need you to be sure." with something concrete about the crew and the housing. Illustrative only: "Every job you take kills a station. Look at them!"

### D4 · Minor · The pre-crossing briefing restates what Ryo knows

- Where: L49–L53 and L81–L85, threshold room before the tenth door.
- Text:
> You have been alive through every crossing. Same body, same memory, same extremely poor habit of landing on your face.
> I cannot see the whole problem from here.
> "I do not know the full circumstances. That is the truth."
> "I consent," he said.
- Problem: Ryo has lived through nine crossings. Telling him he has been alive through them, same body and same memory, is "As you know" exposition unless the story marks it as a ritual. Eiren's limited knowledge is stated twice (L53 and L81). "I consent" is the collection's legal register coming from a young ex-gamer; this point is optional.
- Fix: Mark L49 as the recital Eiren makes before every crossing, so it reads as a ritual he has heard nine times. That also plants "I asked each time" (L241). Keep the LOCKED content (continuous body and memory; one brief return during the storm peak; not knowing the full circumstances), but cut one of the two duplicate disclaimers, preferably the L53 sentence. Keep the "Shoulder." / "A magnificent distinction." beat; it is character-true. "I consent" can stay if read as his quest-accept reflex; otherwise use a plain yes. Illustrative only: "Before you say yes, listen. I say this every time."

### D5 · Minor · Jori's arch buttons blur into Eiren's voice

- Where: L101 (first meeting), L117 (plan exposition), L199–L201 (plan offer), L359–L365 (after the strike).
- Text:
> "Unless the storm has developed a throat, that will be decorative."
> "Easier to praise one person than maintain nine stations. We chose maintenance."
> "Correct. Welcome to engineering."
> "I remain angry about the first part."
- Problem: Cover the tags and these could be Eiren's lines ("A magnificent distinction.", "An outrageous proposal."): no contractions, arch, and each closes an exchange on a button (Identical Voices; epigram last word). Jori's best lines are the practical ones, and these quips pull Jori into the house register. The L359–L365 four-liner gives a tower just cracked by lightning a sitcom tag while Jori is "laughing or coughing".
- Fix: Keep Jori practical, contracted, busy and specific; replace each button with a reason, an instruction or a stake.
  - L101: a steel sword next to lightning conductors is a real hazard; say so.
  - L117: keep "People prefer a miracle with a face" if wanted, but replace the button with the years the stations took.
  - L201: a plain "No." and a physical beat.
  - L363: keep the substance (LOCKED: Jori does not recast Ryo's interference as heroism), but let the damage reports already at L367 pull Jori away instead of closing on "That seems fair."
  - Illustrative only: "That's steel next to a lightning conductor. Sheathe it."

### D6 · Minor · Eiren's arrival line has no clear referent

- Where: L179–L185, the return door flickering at the edge of the control circle.
- Text:
> "Ryo," she said. "Stop proving the problem correct."
- Problem: Ground/referent. "The problem" has no stable antecedent (the engine? her project? his belief?), and "proving a problem correct" is a mixed idiom. The reader recovers only "stop". Ryo's reply ("If I leave and anyone dies, it's my fault.") answers the open door, not her words. Her follow-up, "That sentence has eaten nine years of my life, and considerably more of yours.", is strong and grounded in LOCKED facts; keep it.
- Fix: Make her first line a plain, urgent recognition of the pattern that his reply can answer directly. Illustrative only: "Ryo. You're doing it again."

### D7 · Minor · "that" is resolved only by the next narration

- Where: L335–L345, Ryo outside the circle, the door too narrow to pass facing forward.
- Text:
> "To cut that."
> Eiren stared at the core.
- Problem: Ground/referent: the line is decoded only by the narration after it. With the door, the filament, the circle and the core all in view, "that" could be any of them. Nothing shows Ryo pointing, so the reader learns what he meant only when Eiren stares at the core.
- Fix: Give Ryo a gesture toward the knot, or name it in the line. Keep the LOCKED beat that he chooses the core over his own extraction.

### D8 · Minor · Narration glosses lines that already landed

- Where: L273–L275 ("I want to live"), L349 (filament request), L391 (apology), L415 (blue-grain callback), L167 (residents). L175 is covered under D3.
- Text:
> The statement sounded embarrassingly small beside the storm.
> It was not small to him.
> "That's the point," he said, and then, because it was more honest, "I am terrified. Please do it."
> Eiren's apology arrived without defense.
> It was, Ryo realized, a preference.
> "A Necessary One," someone breathed.
- Problem: After a line lands, the narrator explains how to read it, leaving the reader nothing to infer. "I want to live" carries its own weight. The jump from "That's the point" to "I am terrified" already shows bravado giving way. "I know. I am sorry." already has no defense in it. The "Anything but that." callback is clear without being labelled a preference. "Breathed" is a said-bookism.
- Fix: Cut L273–L275 or reduce them to a physical beat. Cut "because it was more honest" from L349 and cut "arrived without defense" from L391; her apology itself stays free of defense, per the LOCKED Eiren note. Trimming L415 is optional; it is the least harmful. Use "said", or a beat, at L167.

## Prose findings

### P1 · Major · The narrator restates the lesson after each beat

- Where: L219 (after the seam attempt), L255 and L263 (circle as apartment), L317–L319 (brow mark), L375 (leaving the tower).
- Text:
> He had fixed nothing. His certainty had merely become another conduit.
> But disappearing into it would still be disappearance.
> For the first time, not knowing could not postpone the choice.
> The old gift had never been wrong about how to win.
> It had never been asked whether Ryo should survive the winning.
> He left before certainty.
- Problem: From L219 on, nearly every major beat is followed by an aphorism explaining what it meant, often as its own one-sentence paragraph. Together with the dialogue that states the theme (D1, D3) and the narration glosses (D8), the story states its lesson in narration, speech and closing image. The action is strong enough to carry it: the count, the powers leaving one by one, Jori guiding his hand, stepping out of the circle.
- Fix: Keep L255's apartment-and-circle comparison through "The control circle had an audience."; the recognition is required. Cut that paragraph's final sentence. Cut L263, L375 and the second sentence of L219. Reduce L317–L319 to one sentence, or fold them into the action at L321. Keep L323 "Certainty went dark." (it is literal as well as figurative), L435 "That seemed sufficient." and the final line, which is LOCKED.

### P2 · Minor · Fragment lists, and the ending says "no applause" three times

- Text:
> No one crowned him. No crowd began a chant.

- Fix: Keep one "no applause" sentence; L367, in the tower, is best placed. Cut or recast the other two. Turn one or two of the fragment runs into full sentences. Keep L121's "No one even seemed interested in Ryo's sword."; it is funny and it comes first.

### P3 · Minor · "inert doorway" misdescribes the open return

- Where: L353, the filament cutting the core.
- Text:
> The living filament lashed through the inert doorway, bright with the last strength in her bleeding palms.
- Problem: The only other use of "inert" is the dead copper vane at L105. Here the door is open and shrinking, and at the climax a reader takes "inert" to mean closed or dead.
- Fix: Use "narrowing doorway" or "open doorway". The LOCKED rule that doors have no agency is unaffected.

- Where: L53 (Eiren's terms) against L179, L203, L257 and L337 (the door in the tenth world).
- Text:

- Problem: Eiren promises one brief return at the storm's peak. The door appears while the storm is still "approaching" and stays open from L179 to L373. A reader may put this down to Eiren straining past her own limit, but nothing on the page says so.

The door's long, shrinking life then reads as Eiren holding the peak window past her strength.
