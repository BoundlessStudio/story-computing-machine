# Outline

## Story

- Premise and central promise: Carys can teleport deliberately while awake to a place she knows well, but every onset of sleep caused by her narcolepsy sends her to a genuinely random location. On the first night after her longtime friend and roommate, Talune, moves into a nearby apartment, Carys insists on sleeping alone and treating every accidental jump as a problem she can immediately undo. Across several vanishings and returns, resolve whether the two friends can replace Talune's exhausting all-night watch with a practice that leaves Carys supported without making either person's freedom depend on preventing sleep. The condition and random teleportation remain; the night ends with a workable, bounded response rather than control, cure, or proof that randomness has a hidden meaning.
- Intended reader experience: Intimate speculative suspense with dry domestic comedy. Begin with Carys's overprepared confidence and make each absence abrupt: a sentence or small task resumes after an unknowable interval in a freight shed, a damp field, a hotel linen closet, or another unchosen place. Let the danger come from exposure, disorientation, and fatigue rather than treating narcolepsy itself as a joke. The release should be modest but real: Carys gets through the first separate night, Talune is allowed to sleep, and an unfamiliar destination can be assessed rather than automatically fled.
- Generating force:
  - Mode: Relationship-led recurrence. Each cycle repeats four actions—prepare, fall asleep, wake elsewhere, deliberately return—but changes what Carys reports to Talune and what each friend is asking the other to carry. The story moves when the repeated return home stops being evidence that the arrangement works and becomes the habit keeping both women awake.
  - Focal attachment or contradiction: Carys wants Talune's move to be an ordinary expansion of both their lives, not abandonment or proof that Carys cannot live independently. She also relies on the particular attention Talune has learned to give after a jump. Talune wants to remain Carys's friend rather than her permanent night watch, yet fears that silencing her phone could leave Carys asleep in danger.
  - Plot-led wants and useful limit, if applicable: Not principally plot-led. The moving attachment is their attempt to separate companionship from constant surveillance. Carys's useful limit is her habit of making competence visible: after every jump she teleports home before checking her body, surroundings, or level of wakefulness, then reports only that she is back. Talune's limit is anticipatory vigilance; she asks for continuous updates that neither of them can sustain and begins solving each location before Carys has described it.
- Counterforce or complication:
  - Source: Sleep onset cannot be postponed reliably, and the involuntary destination has no emotional, geographic, moral, or chronological pattern. Clothes and items secured close to Carys travel with her; the sleeping body arrives on a supporting surface, but the effect does not account for weather, privacy, ownership, moving machinery, or nearby hazards. A quiet landing may let her remain asleep, while cold, noise, or impact may wake her. Deliberate return requires Carys to be awake enough to hold a clear sensory memory of the destination; rushing it while disoriented increases ordinary mistakes even though the chosen destination itself is reliable.
  - Credible inside logic, if a person: Neither friend is an antagonist. Talune remembers the occasions when Carys woke exposed or confused and believes prompt confirmation is the smallest responsible request. Carys knows the watch has consumed Talune's nights and refuses to make a friend's continued proximity the price of her own adulthood. After their disagreement, Talune retains the right to sleep and Carys retains authority over whether a safe landing requires retrieval.
  - Pressure or change created: Immediate homeward jumps reassure Talune but interrupt any sleep Carys might safely continue, deepen Carys's disorientation, and train both friends to equate “home” with “safe” without checking either fact. Turning off contact would protect Talune's rest but discard a useful line of support. They need a smaller protocol that begins with Carys's observation at the arrival site and distinguishes notification from permission-seeking.
- Narrative design:
  - POV, tense, distance, and limitation: Close first person present through Carys. Her narration is exact about textures, temperatures, carried equipment, and the indignities of waking in the wrong place, but initially evasive about fear and about how little she observes before jumping home. Talune is heard through calls and brief voice messages; her care, impatience, and fatigue remain legible without access to her thoughts. Sleep itself creates clean gaps rather than dream passages.
  - Time shape and compression: One night from the friends' final meal in the half-emptied shared apartment to early morning. Render the first onset and comic-but-unsettling awakening in full, compress one safer cycle, then give the exposed third landing, the argument, a deliberate period of waiting, and the final sleep/awakening full scene space. No flashbacks; the room's remaining duplicate objects and their practiced vocabulary imply the years of shared management.
  - Information and reveal strategy: Establish the trigger and awake-use ability through action rather than an explanatory history. Let the reader initially accept Carys's brisk “back” messages as competence, then reveal through Talune's questions that Carys has not checked for injury, temperature, exit, or whether returning to sleep where she landed was possible. State the operative limits by what travels, what wakes her, and what her kit is designed to survive. Never disclose a destination pattern, emotional compass, prophetic dream, special person-anchor, cure, or connection to any established Gate.
  - Governing movement: Divide possessions; rehearse the old check-in; refuse a watcher; fall out of the sentence; wake; jump home; minimize; try again; wake somewhere tolerable; abandon possible rest to report “back”; return more tired; land exposed; assess too late; misjump only in posture and judgment, not destination; argue; name what the old protocol costs; separate observation from rescue; prepare once; sleep without fighting onset; wake elsewhere; check; choose to stay; notify; let Talune keep sleeping.
- Speculative surplus: Every teleport scrambles the magnetic domains in cards and small magnets carried with Carys. Hotel keys, magnetic clasps, and stripe cards fail afterward, so her close-worn kit uses knots, snaps, cash, and an embossed metal identification tag. The effect cannot indicate, predict, rank, or control destinations and has no bearing on the friends' relationship.
- Structural distinction: A sequence of absences organized by changed reports rather than a chase, countdown, public emergency, or escalating catalogue of spectacular destinations. The decisive work occurs across a quiet phone call after a bad landing and is tested by the next ordinary recurrence. There is no technical demonstration, pursuing crowd, institutional reform, discovered pattern, single climax venue, or symbolic defeat of sleep.
- Decisive turn, deepening, or recognition: After waking on a rain-slick service platform, Carys teleports home before checking a bleeding palm or noticing that the platform had an unlocked shelter several steps away. Talune hears the confusion behind Carys's claim that she is fine and says she cannot keep serving as proof of safety from another apartment. Carys first hears this as withdrawal, then recognizes that her instant returns have been performances for Talune rather than assessments for herself. Together they reduce the old watch to three questions Carys already carries on her metal tag: “Awake? Hurt? Safer here or known place?” Talune will answer one call if Carys asks for help, but no longer waits for a successful return after every onset. Carys bandages her palm, puts on the weather layer she had been saving for a hypothetical worse place, and lets herself sleep before she feels ready.
- Aftereffect or live uncertainty: Carys wakes near dawn in the carpeted corner of an unlocked office storeroom. She is cold but unhurt, does not know whose building she occupies, and still must leave without alarming anyone. She checks the room and herself before deciding that remaining for a few minutes is safer than teleporting while barely awake. Her condition, the random destinations, trespass risk, and the possibility of a much worse arrival persist. The first night demonstrates only that the revised practice can survive one recurrence; it does not bind Talune to future watch or make Carys self-sufficient in every landing.
- Opening / ending relation: Open with Carys placing her emergency tag, shoes, and coat beside the bed while telling Talune she intends to wake at home, a claim neither woman challenges directly. End after Carys wakes elsewhere and uses the kit she is already wearing: she sends “awake, unhurt, staying five minutes,” turns the phone face down without requesting a reply, and studies the storeroom exit before choosing her next movement. The opening prepares to erase displacement; the ending treats location as a practical fact that must be observed.

## Beats

1. In the half-emptied apartment, Carys and Talune finish dividing mundane duplicates. Talune tests the old overnight check-in; Carys rejects a live call and promises an immediate message after any jump. Their affection is practiced, but both are performing confidence about the first separate night.
2. Alone, Carys dresses for bed in her close-worn kit despite resenting it. Sleep cuts off an ordinary task. She wakes on bundled packing material in a freight shed, startles at unfamiliar noise, deliberately returns home at once, and sends “back” before checking anything else.
3. Talune asks whether Carys is hurt and what the shed contained; Carys cannot answer. She turns the gap into a joke and resets the apartment exactly, treating repetition as control.
4. A second onset leaves Carys asleep long enough to gain some rest in a damp field. On waking she could remain safely under a nearby farm awning, but she jumps home to make the expected report. Talune's immediate reply reveals she has not slept at all.
5. The third onset puts Carys on a wet service platform. She scrambles, cuts her palm, glimpses a shelter without evaluating it, and returns home while too disoriented to land on her feet. The chosen destination is correct; her body and judgment are not ready.
6. In the ensuing call, Carys demands that Talune stop monitoring while continuing to ask whether Talune believes she is safe. Talune refuses both roles—permanent watcher and ceremonial approver—and identifies the missing step: Carys reports the success of returning, never the conditions from which she returned. They pare the protocol down to self-assessment, optional help, and no required reply.
7. Carys treats the cut, changes into the weather layer, secures the nonmagnetic kit, records the three questions on her tag, and goes to bed without arranging another performance of control. The narration ends cleanly at sleep onset.
8. Near dawn, Carys wakes in an office storeroom. She inventories body, temperature, exits, and immediate risks before touching her deliberate power. She chooses to stay five minutes, sends one factual message that does not demand Talune wake, and begins planning a quiet exit; the story stops before homecoming.

## Continuity

- Canon used: The story occurs in an unnamed modern-like era of the one shared physical world. Carys is the living user of a story-local teleportation faculty. Established portal capabilities are system-specific, and separate crossing systems remain unrelated unless authority connects them.
- Boundaries and unknowns: Do not identify Carys's faculty with the Glass Gate, another crossing system, universal portal mechanics, divinity, or a global authority over anomalies. Keep the sleep destinations genuinely random and story-local; do not use dreams, emotion, need, Talune, or hidden geography to steer them. The outline establishes only the carried-item boundary, supporting-surface arrival, awake targeting, and magnetic scrambling needed for this story; origin, inheritance, maximum range, long-term treatment, and broader prevalence remain unresolved. Narcolepsy is a lived condition, not a moral failure or comic mechanism, and no medication or safety practice is presented as universal medical guidance.
