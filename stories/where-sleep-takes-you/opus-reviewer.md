# Opus review: Where Sleep Takes You

Independent audit for a later editing pass. It is not a `review.md` verdict,
does not change canon state, and does not authorize edits by itself.

## Verdict

- Dialogue: REVISE — Cira, Pevin and the attendant share one clipped readback register; the rule talks play out as contract clauses; Cira never sounds tired even though one of her rules depends on it; the attendant posts rules nobody asked for; the decisive and final exchanges lean on narration and gesture.
- Prose: REVISE — the teleport rules are restated like a manual at each arrival, the narration keeps explaining what Pevin's lines prove, and negative constructions run throughout.
- Continuity and prompt: PASS — the premise is delivered and resolved, the length is in band (about 3,100 words) and the countdown arithmetic holds; only Minor clarity gaps remain.
- Edit scope: heavy-dialogue
- Existing review.md said: Verdict PASS, "Dialogue: PASS"; disagree. The lines mostly follow one another, but the voices have converged, the talk is procedural, and narration does the relationship work.

Nearly every reply here follows from the line before it. The dialogue does not fail on sense. It fails because everything is spoken in one voice: a terse readback-and-confirm protocol that Cira and Pevin share word for word, and that the attendant speaks in a more formal version. The outline chose this register on purpose, and part of it is earned. Two practiced colleagues running a safety routine under a clock would use shorthand for onset codes, countdowns and label calls. But the story uses it for every exchange. As a result nothing between Cira and Pevin changes. Cira sounds the same at 4:04 as she does after waking on a roof. The narration keeps telling the reader what each of Pevin's lines proves. The edit pass should keep the shorthand where it really is protocol and loosen everything around it. Pevin should get his own speech: spatial, offering, asking. Cira's speech should slip after she arrives somewhere, so that her "Not if I sound tired" rule actually gets tested. The attendant should answer requests instead of posting rules. The decisive and final exchanges should each carry one spoken thing that is not an instruction. Do not add banter or speeches.

## Edit guardrails

- Prompt: sleep onset teleports a narcoleptic to random places, and the story must be complete and resolve that promise. Keep it broadly accessible and within 2,500–4,000 words (now about 3,100). The active craft profile is prospective-2026-08-21. The rewrite request is specifically a dialogue rewrite. Keep close third person on Cira.
- The story reads "the power of teleportation" as entirely involuntary, and the plot depends on Cira being unable to steer home. Do not add voluntary teleportation or any way to bias where she lands.
- Mechanism facts other lines depend on:
  - Only an actual sleep onset moves her. Drowsiness, closing her eyes and cataplexy do not, which is why the clear strip exists.
  - Secured items travel with her. Loose items and other people stay behind.
  - She lands at random within 1.5 km, and the range resets from wherever she arrives.
  - The tag transmits her location after arrival, with no floor number.
- The timeline must stay consistent if lines move:
  - 4:18 first onset. 5:02 re-prioritisation. 5:12 "Forty-eight minutes." 5:14 second onset.
  - Countdowns of 41, 32, 21, 14 and 6 minutes.
  - Last corridor entry 5:52. Witnessed abandonment 5:55. Gate closes at 6:00. Roof door opens 6:04.
- Keep the outcomes: case, personal box and records saved; bench and both fixtures lost; the `18C` crate mistake; one missing sleeve; the taxi and clearance costs.
- What already works and must not be flattened:
  - Cira keeps authority over every loss and Pevin never overrides her. Any "sounds tired" beat must end with Pevin deferring to her rule, not managing her.
  - The outline's anti-default was a good one: no comic arrival vignettes, no inspirational disability speech, no slick rescue talk.
  - Keep the onset codes ("Tray down.", "Clear."), the countdowns and "Witnessed". They are real protocol.
- No universe entries bind this story (no teleport or narcolepsy canon found), and `canon: false`.

## Dialogue findings

### D1 · Major · One clipped readback voice for every speaker

- Where: throughout; clearest at L15 (opening), L101 (cutting the bench), L139–L147 (roof call)
- Text:
> "Van nose out. Lift route clear."
> "Plus straps. Plus the corner. No."
> "Witness and time," Cira said. "Nothing else."
- Problem: Identical Voices. With the tags covered, Cira and Pevin cannot be told apart. Both talk in verbless noun strings, readbacks and one-word confirmations ("Confirmed.", "Active.", "No bench.", "Keep. Next."). At L101 Cira answers in Pevin's physical domain and in his register. The attendant is a more formal version of the same thing. Protocol shorthand is plausible, but it is used in every exchange. Apart from Pevin's "Do you need me there?" (L69), nobody ever says anything that is not an instruction, an estimate or a readback.
- Fix: Keep lines that really are protocol: onset codes, countdowns, "Witnessed", the "Keep. Next." label calls. Everywhere else, tell the two apart by what they notice and what they feel free to say, not by line length. Pevin estimates, offers and asks in his own words. Cira decides by client and obligation. Each scene should have at least one ordinary line from each of them. Do not add banter or speeches.

### D2 · Major · The rule talks are contract clauses and corrections

- Where: L17–L19 (opening rules), L71–L75 (laundry-room call), L103–L111 (after the bench is cut)
- Text:
> "If I stop answering, stop moving stock," Cira said.
> "Last confirmed load only."
> "You can build the next one. Don't send it down."
> "And stop if I can't make item decisions. Not if I sound tired. If I can't answer the item."
> I don't substitute my judgment if you're gone. I don't take an extra run without a confirmed list, even if it fits.
- Problem: Negotiation/procedure routine. The question underneath is real and good: how much may Pevin decide, and will he treat her as impaired just because she sounds sleepy? But it comes out as clauses, amendments and a four-part readback. "I don't substitute my judgment" is Cira's legal register in Pevin's mouth. L73–L75 is correction ping-pong. The call has not dropped, yet Pevin reads back the call-drop rule, which lets Cira amend it. "Answer the item" is not something anyone says. The rules also pile up: three at L17, an amendment at L75, another at L105. The reader has to reconcile "stop moving stock" with "finish the last confirmed load" on their own.
- Fix: Set the rules once at L17, with fewer and plainer words. Make L105 the one amendment that matters (sounding tired is not the same as being unable to decide), and have Cira say it as something she needs from Pevin, not as a clause. Cut Pevin's L107 readback to one line in his own words, or to a nod. Cut the L73–L75 correction, or make Pevin's reply a real question. Illustrative only: "If I'm slurring but I can still tell you which tray, keep going."

### D3 · Major · Cira never sounds tired, so her own rule is never tested

- Where: L105 (the rule); L139–L179 (roof call, minutes after her second sleep attack)
- Text:
> Not if I sound tired.
> "Roof," Cira said. "Controlled door. Wet areas, low parapet, fan. I'm clear of all three."
- Problem: Uniform fluency under pressure. The narration gives her a slow waking ("She lay still until the room held one shape"). Yet her first line after each arrival is a crisp situation report. At about 5:20, on a windy roof after two onsets, she is as precise as she was at 4:04. L105 sets up the story's best relationship test: Pevin must tell "sounds tired" apart from "can't decide". Nothing ever makes him do it.
- Fix: In the first minutes of the roof call, let the aftermath show in how she talks. She might repeat a word, restart a sentence, or ask for a label twice. Once is enough. Then give Pevin one beat where he must decide whether to keep working on her instruction. He keeps going because she can still answer the item. That pays off L105 and gives the pair their one real movement in trust. Her decisions stay correct; only her delivery slips.

### D4 · Major · The attendant posts rules nobody asked for

- Where: L21–L25 (opening), L145 (roof call), L199 (last-entry rule), L217 (six minutes)
- Text:
> I can witness authorization by live call, but I can't accept blanket disposal instructions.
> I cannot delay close for access at your location.
> Anyone past the gate after six delays contractor possession and incurs an additional lock-in charge. I cannot authorize that.
> The remaining contents transfer to the contractor at six. Clearance fee will apply.
- Problem: Explanatory chorus / Exposition. Each rule arrives just before the plot needs it and answers a request no one has made:
  - At L21 he offers remote authorization before anyone has said Cira might leave.
  - At L25, L145 and L199 he refuses extensions nobody asked for.
  - At L217 he repeats what L21 already said.

  His formal register suits the job, but these lines exist to brief the reader. Because the refusals have no target, they create no pressure between people.
- Fix: Keep his countdowns and "Witnessed" lines. They are his job and a good clock. Make each rule a reply. At L21, Cira asks whether she can authorize by phone if she is called away, which also shows she expects to be. At L199, Pevin asks whether he can make one more run and gets the last-entry time as the answer. Cut L145's refusal. Reduce L217 to the countdown.

### D5 · Major · The decisive fixture exchange is a tennis match with contradictory staging

- Where: L193–L213, remote loading of the tool case, personal box and fixtures
- Text:
> "I can take one fixture with the case. It makes the cart wide at the corner."
> "One fixture by hand after."
> "No extra trip."
> Cira pointed at the screen before remembering he could not see her hand in an audio drop.
- Problem: This is the story's decisive choice: records and the personal box over saleable equipment, and not risking Pevin past the gate. It plays as four one-liners of equal weight with no stake stated on either side. Pevin pushes twice without a reason of his own. Cira refuses twice without saying that she is protecting him from the L199 lock-in. The turn in their relationship then rests on a wordless camera move (L211). Its question is left to guesswork: the box instead of a fixture, or just a routine confirmation? And L213 puts her in "an audio drop" while she is watching his live picture. The reader cannot tell which way the video is running.
- Fix: Settle the staging once (for example, her outgoing video has been off since L137 while his camera works) and make L213 match. Give Pevin one spoken reason for pushing. Give Cira one reason for refusing that is about him, not procedure. Keep the camera on the box and "Box confirmed", but make his question visible, for example by framing the box next to a fixture. Illustrative only: "They're worth more than the box, Cira."

### D6 · Major · "Mine" needs the narration to decode it, then the narration states the lesson

- Where: L243–L245, in the van, the `18C` crate opened
- Text:
> Pevin held one housing, looked at the label, and put it back. "Wrong crate."
> "Mine," Cira said. The choice had been hers, made from a broken picture under a clock.
> That did not make the contents different.
- Problem: This is the final meaningful exchange. Right after "Wrong crate", "Mine" most naturally reads as a claim to the crate, not an admission. The next sentence exists to translate it. The sentence after that is a Philosopher line in the narration, explaining what the strike-through in the ledger already shows. Pevin's quiet move to the sleeve (L247) is good, and it depends on the admission being clear.
- Fix: Make the admission unmistakable in her own words and cut both narration sentences. Let the ledger correction show that she owns the mistake. Keep Pevin's non-blaming move to the sleeve. Illustrative only: "My call."

### D7 · Major · The narration explains what Pevin's lines prove

- Where: L15, L69, L95, L109, L181, L227
- Text:
> The cart rolled straight now, which was the sort of answer he preferred to give.
> Pevin moved it without defending the first placement. That was another reason he was here.
> He was overconfirming. Once, as agreed, while there was still room for correction.
- Problem: Glossed subtext. After almost every Pevin beat, a sentence tells the reader what it proves: he is reliable, not defensive, restrained. So the dialogue never has to carry the trust itself. L109 cannot be understood at all. Nothing on the page sets up an agreement that he would overconfirm once, and the sentence reads like the outline's voice note ("Pevin overconfirms once") leaking into the prose.
- Fix: Cut L109's second sentence outright and most of the other explanations. His actions (moving the case, waiting, showing each buckle) already show who he is. Keep at most one: L227's "He did not tell her what she should have chosen." It reads as Cira noticing, not the narrator vouching for him.

### D8 · Minor · Laundry-room call: Pevin's task is dropped, and Cira claims what she hasn't seen

- Where: L67–L79, laundry room
- Text:
> No. Call the number I'm sending. Tell them an adult is locked in their laundry room and can verify by video.
> Then she called the same number herself.
> Address matches your dot.
- Problem: Staging and knowledge. She gives Pevin the call, then makes it herself, and his call is never mentioned again. She has also just told him to keep sorting. "Address matches your dot" compares against a map on his screen that she has not seen; her phone rang before she finished reading the service card. "An adult" is stiff.
- Fix: Choose one caller. Either Cira calls and Pevin is told only to keep sorting, or Pevin's call is the one that gets her out. Make the address line a report of what she has in front of her (the street number on the card), not a claim about his screen.

### D9 · Minor · "Case on the floor" is said before Pevin is back and only the narration explains it

- Where: L113–L123, second onset
- Text:
> "Case on the floor," she said.
> Pevin came back around the corner.
- Problem: Staging and decoding. Pevin is pushing a load to the bay (L113) and returns two paragraphs later, so she says the code to an empty unit. What it means (the case stays behind because it is too big to clip on) is available only from the narration at L119. Yet Pevin knows it at L141 ("The large case stayed.").
- Fix: Bring Pevin back before her line, or have her say it as he comes round the corner. Let what he can see show that the case is staying.

### D10 · Minor · Clipped lines whose referents can't be recovered

- Where: L33 and L37 (sort-order debate), L143 (roof call)
- Text:
> "Fastest is records, tools, metal by weight. We can sort the rest at the bay."
> "More crossings."
> "Good. Keep it for the next load. Put the attendant on speaker."
- Problem: Ground/referent.
  - "Metal by weight" does not say what gets sorted or why.
  - "More crossings" could mean trips across the threshold or across the aisle. It is also ambiguous because L45 uses "crossing" for falling asleep. Its meaning only surfaces at L87.
  - At L143 Cira says to keep the large case "for the next load", but the load Pevin shows at L157 leaves it out and nobody remarks on it. It goes on the load after that.
- Fix: Give Pevin's objection a concrete noun (cart runs, trips to the van). Clarify or cut "metal by weight". At L143, name which load she means, or have Pevin say at L157 why the case is waiting.

### D11 · Minor · Epigram closers

- Where: L163 (label check), echoed by the narration at L169; L147 (to the attendant)
- Text:
> "Camera moves. Stock doesn't."
> He shifted the camera, not the stock.
- Problem: Last-word line. Pevin's worry is real: turning the stock would lose the load order. The chiastic reply closes the exchange neatly where Cira would simply tell him what to do, and the narration then repeats the same figure. "Witness and time. Nothing else." at L147 has the same shape, aimed at an attendant who has offered nothing.
- Fix: Replace the chiasmus with a plain instruction and cut the echo at L169. At L147, drop "Nothing else." Illustrative only: "Don't turn anything. Walk the phone round to them."

### D12 · Minor · Attribution slip in the label run

- Where: L169, roof video check
- Text:
> The second case held setting blocks whose certificates had expired, but the blocks could be recertified. "Keep. Next."
- Problem: Attribution. The paragraph opens with Pevin as its subject ("He shifted the camera"), so by convention the quote is his. But the judgment and the "Keep. Next." belong to Cira.
- Fix: Start a new paragraph for Cira's line, or put her in as the subject before the quote.

### D13 · Minor · The closing line repeats an order already given

- Where: L249–L257, van, ending
- Text:
> "Open that next. Then photograph every tray before unloading. Labels facing camera."
> Pevin put his hand on the second crate's lid and waited.
- Problem: Cira has already told him to open the other crate next (L249). His pause and her final "That one." decide something already decided, so the last exchange reads as a closing button for her authority rather than a choice. The thing that would make it a real choice, the car due at the gate (L255), has not been mentioned before (see C3).
- Fix: Change L249 so the order is left open (for example, photographs first), which makes her final pointing a real choice between the crate and the uncounted trays with the car coming. Keep the quiet, unexplained ending.

## Prose findings

### P1 · Major · The teleport rules read like a manual

- Where: L61, L81, L83, L131, L183 (after the rules are first set out at L45 and L59)
- Text:
> The selector excluded arrival inside solid matter, in liquid, on a moving vehicle, or anywhere without support. Those were the exclusions.
> No medication schedule, spoken instruction, training or device could predict the random choice or lean it toward the storage facility.
> What remained was the sequence after arrival: find where she was, make the call, decide what happened elsewhere.
- Problem: L45 and L59 introduce the rules well. After that they are restated in spec-sheet form at each arrival. "The selector" is never defined. The roof arrival stops for a list at its most physical moment. L183 explains again that nothing can steer her, then ends on a thesis sentence describing what the story is about.
- Fix: Keep L45 and L59, and keep L133's range reset (it explains why sleeping again would not bring her home). On the roof, let the hazards she sees (water, parapet, fan) imply the exclusions, and cut the list and "Those were the exclusions." Cut L183's last two sentences. Trim L81 and L83 to what she notices.

### P2 · Minor · Defining things by what they are not

- Where: L45, L59, L61, L69, L81, L83, L95, L113, L123, L169, L179, L181, L191, L227, L245
- Text:
> Drowsiness did not move Cira. Closing her eyes did not move her.
> The attendant repeated the count, not her reasons.
- Problem: The narration has more than thirty negative constructions. It defines people and rules by what they did not do or are not. After the first few this becomes a rhythm, and it overlaps with the explanations in D7.
- Fix: During the D7 sweep, recast or cut negatives that are not rules. Keep the mechanism negatives at L45.

### P3 · Minor · The opening backstory is unclear

- Where: L27–L29, unit description
- Text:
> Four years of a closed instrument-repair shop had been compressed into twelve square metres
> The labels all faced inward, because a month's notice had made speed feel simple.
- Problem: It is unclear whether the shop ran for four years or has been closed for four, and what the month's notice was for. It is also unclear which way the labels face and why. That matters, because the hidden labels drive the video check at L157–L171.
- Fix: One plain clause for each: how long the shop ran or has been closed, who gave notice, and that the stock was packed in a hurry with its labels hidden from the aisle.

## Continuity and prompt findings

### C1 · Minor · The witness procedure is never explained

- Where: L21, L31, L113, L179–L181, L219–L221
- Text:
> Cira read the authorization, chose the item-by-item option, and signed.
> The attendant witnessed her authorize the first grouped transfer, then returned to the corridor desk.
- Problem: The reader is never told why a storage attendant must witness an account holder moving her own goods. Presumably it is because Pevin is not on the account and the facility must record what is removed and what is abandoned for the clearance charge. The tablet also offers an "item-by-item option" right after the attendant says he cannot accept blanket instructions, which leaves the form's choices unclear. The witnessed counts carry the climax (L179–L181, L219–L221), so the rule should be clear.
- Fix: Add one clause at L21 or L31 saying why witnessing is needed. Fold it into the D4 rework so it comes as a reply, not a lecture.

### C2 · Minor · "The crate had taken the space of both optical fixtures" is asserted, not set up

- Where: L241, van; setup at L157–L179 and L191–L207
- Text:
> The crate had taken the space of both optical fixtures.
- Problem: The fixtures were never candidates for the first cart. They first appear at L191. They are lost to the cart's width at the corner and the 5:52 last-entry rule, not to space the crate took. The ending's irony depends on this claim of cause.
- Fix: Plant at L157–L161 that the fixtures could have ridden in the crate's place on that trip. Pevin can say it, since loads are his domain. Otherwise, change L241 to the cost the crate actually had.

### C3 · Minor · The car at the gate appears from nowhere

- Where: L255, van
- Text:
> Cira checked the time, the uncounted trays, and the car due at the outer gate.
- Problem: No car for Cira has been mentioned, and the van is already there. The reader cannot tell why she is leaving separately, or when.
- Fix: One earlier line (at L231 or when she climbs into the van) saying that a car is coming to take her home and why she is not riding in the van.

## Preserve

- Cira's reason for sorting client by client. It shows her values concretely and belongs to her, not to Pevin:
> "Fewer calls tomorrow from people whose gauge we lost."
- Pevin's one plain human question, the model for his voice:
> "Do you need me there?"
- The onset protocol: the chalked clear strip (so cataplexy can drop her safely), "Tray down.", and "Clear," before sleep takes her.
- The video label check through a breaking feed, with `18C` and the end of `CLAIMS` seen through the blur. This is the right texture for remote decisions.
- The formal abandonment. The bench named first, and the irony of "obsolete housings" on a list read while she saves a crate of them:
> "Witness abandonment," Cira said. "Steel bench, two optical fixtures, shelving, obsolete housings, unselected contents. No further access by Pevin."
- The reveal and its physical evidence:
> Inside were obsolete switch housings wrapped in invoices from eight years ago.
- Pevin not blaming her and turning to the missing sleeve. Cira keeping the personal box private:
> Cira moved the personal box out of the shot and buckled it to the wall ring beside her.
- Pevin's silence after the gate, kept as the single place the narration names what he holds back:
> He did not tell her what she should have chosen.

## Suggested edit order
