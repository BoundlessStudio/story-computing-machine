# Opus review: The First Wound

Independent audit for a later editing pass. It is not a `review.md` verdict,
does not change canon state, and does not authorize edits by itself.

## Verdict

- Dialogue: REVISE — three shared-reality breaks at load-bearing beats (tram, replay, slate), and the confrontation and battle run on correction duels that narration then explains.
- Prose: PASS — concrete, controlled sensory prose; only a minor "not X. Y" habit and one water-infrastructure policy nit.
- Continuity and prompt: REVISE — the prompt is delivered, but the scar is absent 83 years ago yet made 25,000 years ago; small count/staging slip in the climax.
- Edit scope: targeted-dialogue
- Existing review.md said: Verdict PASS; "Dialogue: PASS" (Internal: PASS); disagree. It missed the scar and slate contradictions and the correction-duel pattern.

The dialogue here is better built than the collection average. The pre-descent protocol is real procedure, the battle countdown builds pressure until the young man's voice cracks, and the therapist's repeated "I couldn't tell" at the end is the right refusal. The client's compulsive precision is earned, because counting is the wound. The draft, though, lets that precision become everyone's way of talking. The therapist and the commander answer his numbers with neat counter-corrections and mirrored comebacks, so the confrontation and the battle both play as word duels, and the narration then explains each beat. Under the style problem are three concrete breaks. The tram exchange can't be staged (outer doors or forward partition?). The replay hunts for a "missing" voice the reader already heard say "Now." The closing slate request refers to audio that was never recorded. Fix those first. Then keep the correcting habit for the client alone, let the therapist's fear reach her speech once, and cut the glosses.

## Edit guardrails

- Prompt: the therapist enters the client's memories, follows the trauma back through them, and finds its source in a battle about 25,000 years ago. The facts that carry this must stay: "Approximately twenty-five thousand years" (L141), the young, unscarred palm (L211), and the sealing of the barrier (L287–L297). Length band 2,500–4,000 words (currently about 3,045). Broadly accessible audience, with non-graphic violence: the deaths beyond the gate stay implied by the blows. Active profile: prospective-2026-08-21 (last `Craft profile:` line, under the rewrite request).
- POV is close third on the therapist, and she perceives only what he perceived (L219, L223, L259, L331). Fixes must not give her faces, words or numbers he never sensed, and must not resolve the outside count. The uncertainty is the point, and it fits LOCKED "Memory, myth, and recoverable truth" (premise.md).
- Mechanics the plot depends on: her faculty is living and the slate is inert (consistent with LOCKED "Living agency and magic", rules.md). Each has an independent hold (her rug braid, his three cuff stitches). She exits by releasing his wrist, and he must find his own way out. Consent is renewed before the second descent. The slate records room sound only. The three-note motif recurs in the tram doors, the factory alarm, the cooling barrier and the office latch.
- Nobody is named. Don't add names; the review inventory records none.
- Keep what works: the protocol opening, the "You moved him" dispute, her exit line, the "I couldn't tell" refusals, and the wordless ending at the door (see Preserve).

## Dialogue findings

### D1 · Blocking · Slate request refers to audio never recorded

- Where: L411–L415, the closing exchange about the recording, with L123 (after the first exit) and L139 (the age question).
- Text:
> She switched off its green point.
> “How old are you?” she asked.
> “Keep the tram. Remove everything after you asked my age.”
> “Done.”
- Problem: Ground/continuity and uptake. She switches the slate off at L123, before she asks his age at L139, and her own question at L411 ("the earlier room audio") assumes that. So nothing after the age question exists to remove, and her "Done." accepts an impossible request without noticing. The request also fails his evident aim. What the slate did record includes the dates he said aloud during the first descent ("This is eighty-three years earlier", L83; "Six hundred and twelve years", L95), so his edit wouldn't protect his secret. A reader tracking the slate, which the story keeps in view, hits this in the final exchange.
- Fix: Change his request so it points at audio that was actually recorded, most naturally the spoken dates after the tram. Keep her "Done." and the rest of the exchange. Illustrative only: "Keep the tram. Take out where I said the years." (Alternative: move the switch-off from L123 to after L141. That is a larger change and also touches L157 and L183.)

### D2 · Major · Tram exchange: which door, and what "Not yet" expects

- Where: L49–L65, the tram memory, with L173 in the confrontation.
- Text:
> Not to open the doors. To hold them shut.
> “Clear the door,” he told her.
> “There is no door open.”
> I nearly sealed the forward partition while people were moving through it.
- Problem: Ground/referent and staging at the inciting incident. The narration says the lever holds the outer doors shut. He tells a woman at his elbow to "clear the door". She says no door is open, and he answers "Not yet," which implies a door is about to open, the opposite of holding one shut. Later he says the danger was sealing the forward partition on people moving through it, a different barrier the tram scene never showed. The reader can't tell what the lever does, which door he means, or what "Not yet" expects, so the modern echo of the ancient seal doesn't land.
- Fix: Decide that the lever drops the forward partition. That matches "How many in the forward car?" / "Its rated capacity." (L71–L75), the people pushing forward (L47) and L173. Revise L51 to say he means to bring the partition down, not hold the outer doors. Then "Clear the door" means the gangway, and the woman's reply should answer what she can actually see, so "Not yet" fits. Illustrative only: "Nothing's closing." Keep the attendant's lines and "Its rated capacity."

### D3 · Major · Confrontation runs on corrections and mirrored comebacks

- Where: L125–L177, the confrontation after the first exit.
- Text:
> “That correction is not helping you.”
> “I’m asking what happened to me five minutes ago.”
> Compressed is your inference.
> “I didn’t say cure.” His head came up. “I said source.”
> “You asked me to follow you there without telling me where there was.”
- Problem: House-voice convergence and Punchline Patch. This is the story's largest exchange, and it changes real things: trust breaks, she gains leverage and the terms are reset. But almost every turn is shaped as a correction or a reversal of the other person's words. He corrects "six centuries" to the year. She comments on the correction. She mirrors his "what happened to everyone else" into "what happened to me". He corrects "compressed". He answers her caution with an "I didn't say X. I said Y." couplet. She lightly rebuts with "where there was." His corrections are in character. Hers make her sound like him, and together the pattern reads as a debating exercise. She has just lost the floor inside her client's six-century memory and moved her chair away from him, yet she speaks with flawless composure (uniform fluency under pressure).
- Fix: Let "Six hundred and twelve years." (L131) stand. It's his tic, so let it hang and have her react to the number, not the correction. Cut L133. Cut "Compressed is your inference." and keep the useful information that follows it ("Some periods remain detailed…"). Replace the L155 mirror with her own plain concern, in words that show the fright. Illustrative only: "I couldn't find the floor. I couldn't find the floor in there." Let him narrow the ask at L177 by agreeing, not correcting. Illustrative only: "I know. I just want to know where it starts." Keep L127, L141, L153, L167–L169, L173 and the substance of L183.

### D4 · Major · Narration explains each confrontation beat

- Where: L147, L165, L171, L179, L189, in the confrontation and re-consent.
- Text:
> Recognition of a consequence he had expected and hoped to postpone.
> Not from his words; those became more exact as the rest of him failed.
> The plain admission left them nowhere easy to go.
> There it was: the thing he had asked for, narrowed until it could not promise more than it was.
> His jaw shifted. He had wanted assurance and found a boundary instead.
- Problem: Five glosses in about 40 lines tell the reader what each exchange just meant: his expectation, his precision, the admission, the narrowed request and the boundary. The dialogue and physical beats already carry these, so the reader has nothing left to infer, and the scene reads as annotated. "His jaw shifted" is also a stock beat.
- Fix: Cut the quoted glosses. Keep the physical beats that precede them: the chair and rug (L145), the face changing (L147, first clause only), the cuff stitches, "His head came up", the slate turned facedown. Keep L143, her working-through of the number, and the second half of L179 ("She could refuse…") if the scene needs her decision point. Make these cuts after D3, because some glosses exist to prop up lines D3 changes.

### D5 · Major · Commander's retorts turn the source battle into a word duel

- Where: L241–L247 (second break) and L311–L319 (after the seal).
- Text:
> “I counted movement.”
> “You counted dust. Hold for the third break. Then seal.”
> “You saw the line. How many?”
> “I saw the fracture.”
- Problem: Punchline Patch, plus a Ground/referent failure at the source moment. "I counted movement." exists to set up "You counted dust.", a neat last word in the middle of an evacuation under bombardment. After the seal, "You saw the line" has three competing referents in the last 100 lines: the wall's "pale lines" (L217), the scar ("the white line", L299, less than twenty lines earlier) and the column of people. Only the commander's pun-answer ("I saw the fracture") tells the reader he meant people. The decisive aftermath of the prompt's central event becomes wordplay. The talk-past just before it ("Release it." / "How many were outside?" / "Release the wheel.") is strong and should carry the scene.
- Fix: Keep "You can see five." and "Hold for the third break. Then seal." That reprieve is real movement. Cut "I counted movement." and the "You counted dust." retort, or let the young man simply repeat his count. At L317–L319, drop the line/fracture pair and give the commander a plain refusal or admission, still tied to the fracture reaching the housing (L285). Illustrative only: "I didn't count them. Let go of the wheel." L403 ("The commander was right about the fracture.") still works, because L285 shows the fracture.

### D6 · Major · Replay hunts for a voice the reader already heard

- Where: L267–L269 (first pass), L325–L355 (replays).
- Text:
> “Now,” he yelled. It was not clear whether he meant the gate or their feet.
> “There was a voice after the third break.”
> “I don’t hear one,” she said.
> The man stood in both places for a breath
- Problem: Ground/uptake and attribution at the climax. In the first pass the rear man plainly yells "Now" after the third break, and the narration flags the ambiguity: gate or feet, permission or plea. When the client later says "There was a voice after the third break", the therapist, who heard "Now" through his hearing, answers "I don't hear one". Nobody mentions the word both of them heard. So the search at L355 ("warning or accusation, permission or plea") reads as a hunt for something missing when it is really a hunt for what a known word meant. Attribution also blurs. At L269 "he yelled" follows several "he"s meaning the young man. Later, "the man" means the client (L11, L49, L81, L375) and also the rear man (L339, L349), so "The man stood in both places" can momentarily read as the client.
- Fix: Tag L269 to the rear man explicitly. Make the replay's search the meaning of "Now", or the words after it, and make her reply register that this replay has lost the sound rather than deny it existed. Illustrative only (client): "He said 'now.' To me, or to them?" Illustrative only (therapist): "I can't hear him this time." Replace "The man" at L349 with "The rear man" or similar. Adjust "missing voice" at L355 to match. Keep "You moved him." / "No." / "He was behind the litter." / "He was beside it." That dispute is the story's best dramatization of memory being rebuilt.

### D7 · Major · Ending explains the answer he just gave

- Where: L403–L413, the return to the room.
- Text:
> “That’s where it begins,” he said.
> It was the first answer he had given without a measurement attached.
> He considered the little black square as if it were another barrier under his hand.
- Problem: The Philosopher, done by narration. The final meaningful exchange already lands through "The commander was right about the fracture." and "That's where it begins," which is the source the prompt demands, said in his own mouth. The narrator's sentence then states the lesson. It is also untrue: he has answered without a measurement many times ("I know." L27, "Yes." L169, L153). The slate simile at L413 glosses the same way.
- Fix: Cut L409. Cut "as if it were another barrier under his hand" from L413, leaving him looking at the slate before he answers. Keep "The commander was right about the fracture." and "That's where it begins."

### D8 · Minor · Survivor quips and an untagged three-speaker run

- Where: L231–L257, the litter survivors approaching the gate.
- Text:
> “Then use your legs,” she answered. “Lift on mine.”
> “Get your hand back on.”
> “You’re walking.” The front survivor caught his harness and dragged him upright without stopping. “Walk faster.”
- Problem: Two of the front survivor's replies are comeback-shaped closers under fire ("They're through the lower cut." → "Then use your legs."; "I'm slowing it." → "You're walking."). They make her sound like the commander and the therapist. L251–L255 is a four-line untagged run among at least two speakers, and "Get your hand back on." has no clear owner.
- Fix: Keep "Keep it high. Two on the frame. Don't crowd the turn." (L231). It is exactly her voice. Make the two replies practical and effortful, not witty: a count, an instruction, a grunt. Tag or beat "Get your hand back on."

## Prose findings

### P1 · Minor · Factory memory uses a floodgate as texture

- Where: L81–L85, the factory memory 83 years earlier.
- Text:
> floodwater shoved against a factory gate on the other side
> Water struck his knees, his hips, his chest.
- Problem: Policy nit for authorized revisions. A wheel-operated gate holding back floodwater is flood-control works, which `universe/style-guide.md` "No default water infrastructure" excludes unless the prompt calls for it. The prompt doesn't.
- Fix: Keep the beat (a wheel, a gate, workers running for it, him holding it open, releasing the wheel to grab the last worker, the gate slamming, the alarm ringing three times) but change what presses on the gate. Smoke or fire behind a fire door, or a crush of bodies, both fit the barrier motif. "Rain hammered a sheet-metal roof" can stay.

## Continuity and prompt findings

### C1 · Blocking · Scar absent 83 years ago but made in the ancient battle

- Where: L81 (factory memory), L199 (his request), L211 and L299 (battle).
- Text:
> The white line below his thumb was not there.
> “The pressure before the scar.”
> Heat tore the white line into his palm.
- Problem: The scar is the story's thread back to the source: he asks her to follow "the pressure before the scar", and the barrier's heat makes it at L299. L81 explicitly notes the scar missing from the same hand 83 years ago, which tells the reader the scar is less than a century old. Nothing on the page (no mention of bodily renewal) explains the gap, so the two facts contradict and weaken the scar as a clue at L199.
- Fix: Smallest fix: cut the L81 sentence (the other coat already dates the scene) or make it say the line was already there. If the author wants the scar to come and go, that needs one legible sentence in the present-day scenes. It can't be left implied.

### C2 · Minor · "Seven through" doesn't match what the narration shows

- Where: L267–L275 and L291, the third break.
- Text:
> The first survivor crossed beneath the suspended barrier.
> “Seven through,” the young man said.
> The litter struck stone, half across.
- Problem: When he says "Seven through", the narration has shown only the lead survivor under the barrier, and the litter party is still crossing at the moment of sealing. "Through" has no clear reference point (the bend? the gate? the measure?). The counts are the client's obsession, so the reader will try to reconcile them.
- Fix: Either show more people crossing before L271 or change "through" to name the mark he is counting past. Keep the 11 / 7 / 4 arithmetic and the "three… or four" uncertainty at the gate.

- The wordless ending at the door (L423–L429): counting footsteps, the three-note latch, the stitches, standing aside. Add no commentary.
