# Outline

## Story

- Proposed reader-facing title: *The First Wound*.
- Premise and central promise: Memory therapist Seska enters the joined recall of Tammel, a man whose intake record admits only that he has lived much longer than he appears, to trace the four dull blows and falling metal that trigger his violent waking panic. She expects the association to end within one difficult lifetime. Instead, the same adult hands and palm scar lead her backward through successively older memories until Tammel brings them to a river-gate post during a battle fought 25,000 years before the session. Fulfill the prompt by making the descent literal, the deep-time interval unmistakable, and the source specific: Tammel released a defensive flood after the fourth retreat horn while part of his own rearguard was still beyond the gate. The story resolves the discovery and returns both participants safely to the clinic; it does not erase the trauma, prove the memory as public history, or excuse or condemn Tammel by simple verdict.
- Intended reader experience: Begin with contained clinical suspense and the vulnerable strangeness of walking through another person's recall. Let curiosity widen into deep-time awe as the remembered cultures recede, then contract the apparent scale of an ancient battle to one lever, one unfinished retreat, and one choice Tammel has spent millennia arranging himself not to see. The finish should offer sober intimacy and a usable next question rather than cathartic cure, battlefield spectacle, historical pageant, or revelation before witnesses.
- Generating force:
  - Mode: Plot-led stratigraphic descent. Each layer begins with the same linked sensations: four impacts, metal descending, river silt, and pressure in the left palm, but leaps farther backward and supplies a different, incomplete explanation. Movement comes from deciding whether to cross the next associative threshold while the safe session window contracts, not from collecting evidence for outsiders.
  - Focal attachment or contradiction: Seska is attached to the therapeutic promise that a terror becomes more workable when patient and witness can locate it together, yet joined recall is ethically useful only while the patient retains control over what is witnessed. Tammel wants the waking attacks to stop harming his present life, but he has protected himself by treating every later echo as the source and by remembering the ancient battle as something done to him rather than a choice he also made.
  - Plot-led wants and useful limit, if applicable: Seska immediately wants to identify the earliest linked memory and bring them both out with enough time for bodily reorientation. She also wants to honor Tammel's request not to make him watch more than he chooses and to preserve a reliable route back to the present. Her useful limit is interpretive rather than moral: inside joined recall she receives sensation and attention, not another person's intention or objective history, and she initially mistakes chronological depth for explanatory certainty. Tammel wants relief and an end to the session before Seska sees his hand on the flood lever. When ashamed, he redirects attention into an earlier echo instead of naming what he fears; that defense carries them closer to the memory he is avoiding.
- Counterforce or complication:
  - Source: Recall is associative, incomplete, and actively shaped by Tammel's present attention. Each deeper layer increases sensory and motor bleed between them, while a monitored seventy-minute window limits safe immersion. The repeated cue has accumulated genuine later traumas, so no single memory can simply be declared false and discarded. Tammel's shame and Seska's urge to finish the sequence can both outrun consent if she treats his resistance as an obstacle rather than information.
  - Credible inside logic, if a person: There is no antagonist. Tammel's concealment protects him from reliving the moment when survival, duty, and responsibility became inseparable; he may close a path, refuse a question, or end the session at any point. Seska can invite attention toward a threshold but cannot force it open, move Tammel's remembered body, or substitute her judgment for his exit choice.
  - Pressure or change created: The later memories plausibly explain the symptom one by one, tempting Seska to stop early, but each ends just before the fourth impact and leaves the left hand clenched around something absent. Going deeper risks a rough return and temporary loss of bodily orientation. Stopping preserves safety but leaves the governing omission untouched. The decisive progress therefore depends on a renewed choice by Tammel after he understands what Seska has begun to infer.
- Narrative design:
  - POV, tense, distance, and limitation: Close first-person past through Seska. Keep her exact about present-session protocol, bodily changes, and what Tammel permits, but increasingly cautious about interpretation. Within memories she feels Tammel's sensations without gaining his thoughts automatically; his intentions arrive only through what he says from beside the remembered self or chooses to direct her toward. Give Seska a vocabulary of sequence, threshold, present orientation, and consent; give Tammel sparse spatial language, evasive corrections of dates, and military verbs that emerge before their context does. Do not grant an omniscient account of the battle or the opposing side.
  - Time shape and compression: The outer action occupies one seventy-minute session plus a short recovery. Inside it, move backward through a recent door incident, a retreat from a burning settlement several centuries earlier, a sealed shelter thousands of years earlier, and brief fragments whose material cultures Seska cannot name. Make the temporal gaps grow while the memory scenes shorten, then give full scene weight only to the gatehouse minutes 25,000 years ago. Return through the layers in one rapid continuous ascent with no new revelation or future coda.
  - Information and reveal strategy: Establish the limits and exit signal of joined recall before entry. Let the first two memories suggest that Tammel is merely associating similar frightening events. Reveal his abnormal longevity through continuity of body: the same scar, reach, and left-handed grip, rather than exposition about an immortal species. At each threshold withhold what his hand holds and what follows the fourth blow. Before the oldest layer, Tammel himself identifies the depth as twenty-five thousand years, making it part of his experienced chronology rather than an instrument's authenticated date. Show the battle only from the gate post: retreat horns, people crossing an embankment, the approaching line, civilian barges below, and the lever. Withhold until the decisive moment that Tammel, not an enemy or commander standing beside him, released the water.
  - Governing movement: Establish the exit signal; join breath and pulse; enter the recent door panic; isolate the four-beat cue; cross into an older retreat; notice the unchanged hand; accept that Tammel's life exceeds his intake account; follow the absent lever through wider temporal gaps; stop when present bodily orientation begins to fray; ask Tammel whether concealment or witnessing serves him now; let him choose the oldest threshold; arrive at the gate post; reconstruct the competing lives at risk without expanding into battle spectacle; see his hand release the counterweight after the fourth horn; remain for the blows from the stranded rearguard; let Tammel name what he chose; use his exit signal; return; orient their bodies; identify one bounded next focus rather than promise a cure.
- Speculative surplus: Joined recall is a local practice created by the living attention, breath, and pulse of patient and therapist; the clinic's nonliving frame only monitors present bodily divergence and breaks contact when either participant withdraws. It can render accessible memory as shared sensory space but cannot retrieve what Tammel never perceived, read another remembered person's mind, or certify history. Unrelated to the trauma's meaning, sessions also leave participants briefly borrowing one another's preferred hand and balance bias, so the clinic uses soft cups, handrails, colored wrist bands, and a simple walking check before either person leaves. This aftereffect neither reveals truth nor advances treatment.
- Structural distinction: A descending sequence of repeated thresholds in which every apparent explanation is real but insufficient. The calendar opens outward while the narrated space contracts from settlements and evacuations to the interior of one gatehouse. The decisive venue is private remembered ground, and the crucial action is sustained attention to a few seconds rather than victory in the battle, a technical demonstration, a public confession, a converted crowd, an institutional rewrite, or a closing monument. Unlike a mystery solved by outside evidence, the story stops at the limit of what Tammel actually remembers and chooses to share.
- Decisive turn, deepening, or recognition: At the oldest gate, Seska first assumes Tammel was trapped by the falling barrier. The remembered self instead braces his left hand on the release while the fourth retreat horn sounds. His rearguard is still crossing, but if he waits, the opposing force will reach the lower channel before the last civilian barges clear. A present-session warning tells Seska that she should begin the return. Tammel, now aware of both choices, asks her to remain. He releases the counterweight; floodwater takes the crossing, and four hammer blows sound from the far side before the memory cuts away. Tammel says plainly that he chose the barges and left his own people beyond the gate. Seska neither calls the decision necessary nor names him monstrous. She asks what the waking Tammel needs her to remember; he answers with the choice, the people he cannot count, and the date, 25,000 years, then gives the exit signal himself.
- Aftereffect or live uncertainty: The session locates the first wound but does not establish a complete ancient history, recover the names of the dead, or remove later layers of fear. Tammel may still panic at closing metal and may never decide whether his action was forgivable. He now knows the symptom is tied not only to being trapped but to the instant he decides someone else must remain outside, giving future work a concrete focus. Seska retains only a witness's partial sensory knowledge, not ownership of his memory, and Tammel keeps control over whether anyone else learns of his age or the battle.
- Opening / ending relation: Open with Seska asking Tammel to choose and test the exit signal before she enters; he complies but asks her to find the source without making him look at it. End after their unsteady return when Seska asks where they should begin next time. Tammel answers, "At the lever," then uses the exit signal again to end the appointment before she can turn recognition into another demand. The opening treats stopping as a protocol he fears he may need; the ending makes it a present choice he can exercise after naming where the work lies.

## Beats

1. In the unnamed clinic, Seska reviews joined-recall limits with Tammel, whose apparent middle age conflicts with an intake age marked only as unknown. His recent waking episode began when a metal service door fell and someone struck it four times. He asks her to remove the cause without forcing him to see it; she says she can help follow what he permits, not cut memory away.
2. They establish a spoken exit signal and two bodily backups, make living contact through breath and pulse, and enter the recent incident. Seska experiences Tammel's left hand crushing empty air while four blows turn an ordinary door into mortal danger. He directs her toward the sound's earliest edge.
3. The cue opens a retreat from a burning settlement centuries earlier. Tammel again appears as the same adult man and refuses to look behind a descending barrier. Seska notices the identical palm scar and realizes this is continuous autobiographical memory, not an ancestor, symbolic dream, or role Tammel has borrowed.
4. Shorter layers follow: a sealed shelter thousands of years earlier, an evacuation path whose language Seska cannot understand, then fragments of river silt and a lever-shaped absence. Each offers a credible later injury but ends before the fourth impact. Present monitoring begins to warn of motor and balance bleed.
5. Seska stops rather than press through. Tammel admits that he has counted about 25,000 years from the memory he keeps diverting them around. She asks whether he wants to return now or witness it with her; after testing that the exit remains available, he chooses the oldest threshold.
6. They enter a gatehouse during an ongoing battle. Keep the camera tight: four retreat horns, a rearguard crossing an embankment, an opposing line closing, civilian barges in the lower channel, Tammel at the counterweight, and no complete strategic overview. His remembered body knows that waiting may save his unit and expose the barges, while releasing the flood will cut off both attackers and his own people.
7. As the present-session warning sharpens, Tammel asks Seska to stay. He pulls after the fourth horn. Water breaks the crossing; the stranded rearguard's four blows reach the gate. Seska recognizes that later memories have preserved him as the person outside a closing door because he could not bear remembering himself as the one who closed it.
8. Tammel names the choice without claiming certainty that it was right, identifies the battle as 25,000 years old, and gives the exit signal. Seska guides one rapid ascent through the later echoes, using current breath and touch rather than rewriting any scene.
9. Back in the clinic, borrowed balance and handedness make recovery physically awkward and ordinary. Seska distinguishes a working clinical focus from historical proof; Tammel chooses the lever as the point for any future session, then ends this appointment on his own signal. No cure, public disclosure, or further descent follows.

## Continuity

- Canon used: The story uses the locked deep-time premise that one physical world contains many civilizational layers, the locked tension between degraded memory and recoverable partial truth, and the canonical allowance for cultures to hold incomplete models. Joined recall follows the canonical requirement that memory phenomena have a local mechanism and preserve personhood. If the practice is culturally understood as magic, only Seska and Tammel provide living agency; the frame stores and monitors but does not choose or act.
- Boundaries and unknowns: The battle's culture, geography, opponents, larger outcome, Galactic Cycle coordinate, and relationship to any established era or battle remain unresolved. Tammel is one continuous person rather than an ancestor, copy, branch, reincarnation, archive, or proof of a universal memory substance; the origin, limits, and prevalence of his longevity remain unestablished. Joined recall exposes accessible subjective memory, not an objective record, universal therapy, historical authentication method, mind-reading power, or permission to bypass consent. The ancient choice does not invalidate Tammel's present identity or reduce every later trauma to a single mechanical error.
