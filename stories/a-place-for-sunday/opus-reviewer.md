This story mostly avoids the collection's house voice. Nobody negotiates, rules
or lectures. Nerina talks like a six-year-old: literal questions, food
specifics, answers that don't help ("When you had those trousers."). Cosimo is
an expansive host who talks while he cooks and embroiders the family anecdote.
Ilario is short and reactive, and the story carries his feeling through what he
does (the apron, the chair, holding still while his father checks his hair)
rather than through speeches. Where lines misfire ("Is that all of it?", "Did I
what?"), the next reply picks up the misfire, which is good. The edit pass should do
only light polish: fix one tease whose logic slips, one question whose scale
the reply doesn't match, and one mock-formal button. It must not smooth the
father–son bickering into politeness or add lines where Ilario says what the
move means to him.

## Dialogue findings

### D1 · Minor · Ride question and reply differ in scale

- Where: L123–L129, walk through the village, after the shed crosses
- Text:
> "Could we ride home like that?" she asked.
> "You'd be tired of it by the first afternoon," Cosimo said.
- Problem: Ground/uptake. For Nerina, "home" is the narrow house fifteen minutes back across the lane. Cosimo answers as if she'd proposed a journey of several days ("by the first afternoon", walking an hour and still seeing where they had breakfast). It could be read as a grandfather's exaggeration, but the reader has to stop and work out which home she means.
- Fix: Change only Nerina's question so it doesn't name a short destination. Keep Cosimo's reply and his "Twenty-three mornings ago" line, which fit him. Illustrative only: "Can I ride on one?"

### D2 · Minor · Ilario's rebuttal in the hen tease repeats Cosimo's own premise

- Where: L279–L287, kitchen, hen-in-the-blankets anecdote
- Text:
> I heard him from upstairs. Sounded as if he was trying to shout without opening his mouth too far
> "You weren't there. You were upstairs."
> "I was there soon enough."
- Problem: Cosimo has already said he heard it from upstairs. Ilario's objection just hands that fact back as if it disproved something, so it reads as a setup for Cosimo's "I was there soon enough" (a mild Punchline Patch). The bickering itself is earned play and should stay; only the logical link is slack.
- Fix: Keep the exchange and Cosimo's last line, which quietly says he came running. Aim Ilario's objection at the part of Cosimo's account he could only have seen (the mouth), so "I was there soon enough" answers it. Illustrative only: "You couldn't see my mouth from upstairs."

### D3 · Minor · Mock-formal button caps the child's complaint

- Where: L289–L297, kitchen, Nerina wants to be shown the branch
- Text:
> "You always say that."
> "And then we have breakfast, and then we do things. It's a very successful arrangement."
- Problem: "It's a very successful arrangement" is the collection's dry, procedural last-word register and sounds more like Cosimo or the narrator than Ilario. Nerina's wordless answer (climbing back up and turning her chair toward the tree) already closes the beat better than the quip.
- Fix: Cut the second sentence, or replace it with something plain a parent says to a child who has slid off her chair. Keep her chair-turning. Illustrative only: "And then we have breakfast. Sit down, please."

## Prose findings

### P1 · Minor · Small repetitions and one staging nit

- Where: L37–L39 (the reveal), L79 (at the gate), L107 (the wall crew), L147 and L223 (reaction beats)
- Text:
> went to open the front door.
> Beyond the front door, the field was full of roofs.
> Smoke was rising from the bakery chimney. Someone had started baking.
> She waved back with her free hand
> His father nodded, considering this.
> She considered this while her grandfather lifted the egg onto a plate.
- Problem: "front door" appears in consecutive sentences at the story's biggest reveal. "Someone had started baking" repeats what the smoke already shows. "Considering this" and "considered this" give two different listeners the same reaction beat. At L107 nothing occupies the woman's other hand once the wall is down, so "free hand" contrasts with nothing.
- Fix: Recast L39's opening without "front door". Cut "Someone had started baking." or make it specific. Give one of the two "considered" beats a different action. Drop "free", or give her something to hold, such as bread from the basket.

### P2 · Minor · Narration states the meaning of the glaze-crack moment

- Where: L263, kitchen, the fritter dish
- Text:
> He had forgotten the flower. He had forgotten hundreds of things that were within reach of his hand.
- Problem: "He had forgotten the flower" works on its own. The next sentence turns it into a general statement about what the return gives back, the only place the narration explains the story's feeling.
- Fix: Cut the second sentence, or replace it with another specific recovered object rather than a summary.
