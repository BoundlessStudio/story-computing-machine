# Outline

## Story

- Premise and central promise: **A Place for Sunday.** A father who can seldom afford the journey home opens his door to his seaside village arriving outside the city. His own father has kept his place at the table and now wants to feed his granddaughter. Deliver the impossible arrival, joyful recognition, and an ample, particular Sunday brunch.
- Focal pressure or attachment: Ilario loves city life with six-year-old Nerina and misses unhurried time with Cosimo. Letters and occasional visits sustain affection; fares and days away from work prevent another visit. He wants Nerina to know his childhood pleasures.
- Counterforce or complication: Ilario cannot take everything in. Hungry Nerina gets impatient with adult embraces. Cosimo wants to feed them, unpack, and investigate the afternoon brass concert. Their appetites and attention pull the morning in different directions; no antagonist is needed.
- POV, distance, and information limit: Close third past through Ilario: hands, food, and approaching his old house from a new direction. Others' intentions arrive through speech and action.
- Governing movement and time shape: One Sunday, waking through brunch, with brief remembered departures. Accumulate discovery and company. Expand reunion and kitchen/table scenes. Target 2,500–4,000 words. End within the meal.
- Speculative rule or ordinary-world constraint: Trained living villagers raise houses with bounded garden plots and guide them slowly while maintaining contact. Crews work in relays, lowering loads to rest. The relocation took weeks; it cannot supply quick personal travel. Buildings remain inert. Villagers collectively chose rented fallow land near their families and city customers. The sea stays behind.
- Dialogue promise: Familiar affection becomes newly physical, while a child turns accounts of her father's childhood into questions, food preferences, and experiments of her own.
- Dialogic medium: Face-to-face family talk, overlapping kitchen conversation, touch, pointing, and the remembered promise at departure. Letters provide existing familiarity, not a revelation packet.
- Dialogue engine: Cosimo's brunch question lets Nerina make demands directly. Requests, competing recollections, teasing, and interruptions determine what they cook, what they explore, and who gets whose attention. The adults also enjoy talking to each other.

## Voice

- Narrative texture: Warm, bodily observation with room for a longer wondering sentence. Let old familiarity interrupt the scale of the marvel. Notice worn cloth, flour, garden smells, and the changed view beyond familiar windows.
- Conversational texture: Affection already works. Include ordinary requests, unfinished anecdotes, repetition for a distracted listener, food talk, and pleasure in an unnecessary story. Give the child intelligible uptake, not precocious adult conclusions.
- Rhetorical ownership: Ilario knows Nerina's habits and enjoys dry exaggeration. Cosimo owns childhood anecdotes, practical cooking confidence, and unabashed enthusiasms; he can listen without surrendering his turn. Nerina claims attention through concrete questions, tasting, literal experiments, and cheerful interruption.
- Pressure behavior: Astonishment briefly makes Ilario lose his question. Hungry Nerina becomes repetitive. Cosimo talks while doing things and sometimes needs a question repeated. Ease restores expansive speech.
- Relationship movement: Ilario wants time with his father but hesitates to compete with Nerina for it. Cosimo wants company and a guide to city pleasures; he can ask openly. Nerina freely asks for food and access to her father's childhood. Exchanges turn remembered relatives into present company without repairing a breach.
- Anti-default: Avoid a restrained disclosure-and-accommodation sequence. Let the central exchange be an untidy, enjoyable cooking conversation whose affection needs no verdict.

## Beats

1. Begin Sunday in Ilario and Nerina's modest home near the city's edge. Make the fare problem concrete; briefly recall Cosimo's table promise. An unfamiliar scrape and familiar smell draw Ilario to the door. Reveal roofs and gardens descending into the fields, then his father approaching.
2. Give recognition and bodily reunion room. Cosimo asks what his granddaughter likes for Sunday brunch, and Nerina's answer interrupts Ilario's accumulating questions. She wants a fried egg with crisp edges and bread cut into narrow fingers; Cosimo has brought ingredients for Ilario's beloved fig fritters. Neither breakfast displaces the other. Establish that Cosimo and Nerina already know each other from visits and letters.
3. Walk toward the village, Ilario holding Nerina's hand until curiosity frees it. Living movers lower the last plot; tied cupboard doors and a displaced nesting hen supply material comedy. Neighbors have their own reunions. Explain the collective move through relevant questions. Cosimo misses the sea and already anticipates the concert.
4. Expand the kitchen scene. Nerina investigates the room where her father was small while preparations overlap. Cosimo recruits Ilario through old kitchen familiarity; Ilario knows where things go. Tell one modest childhood escapade with interruptions and competing remembered details, not a buried confession. Nerina contributes her own account of present-day Ilario. Let fritters, eggs, and tasting cause physical business without manufacturing a cooking crisis.
5. Reach the table. Ilario's accustomed place has remained part of daily life. Make room for Nerina beside him. Allow second helpings, concert talk, and an anecdote resumed after interruption. Ilario enjoys being fed while attending to his daughter. Stop amid their specific shared pleasure, without summarizing a lesson.
