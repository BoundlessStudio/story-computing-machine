# Opus review: The Morning Her Hand Moved

Independent audit for a later editing pass. It is not a `review.md` verdict,
does not change canon state, and does not authorize edits by itself.

## Verdict

- Dialogue: REVISE — the two plot hinges (the wave count before the hand moves, and the counterweight plan) rest on evidence and a proposal that never reach the page, and the decisive and final exchanges turn into briefings that end on thesis lines.
- Prose: REVISE — a "not X but Y" reversal habit runs through the narration, and the ending explains an uncertainty the action already showed.
- Continuity and prompt: REVISE — the prompt is delivered and internal continuity mostly holds, but water infrastructure supplies the opening image, Zunel's first orders and a plot beat, against the no-default-water policy.
- Edit scope: targeted-dialogue
- Existing review.md said: Verdict PASS, "Dialogue: PASS"; disagree — it missed two Ground failures at the story's hinges and took the decisive round-robin for distinct work speech.

In the middle of the story the dialogue is the best thing on the page. On the outer-wrist ledge it is physical and unequal: Aksor's ceremony breaks into bodily orders, he overrides Zunel outright, Polaq stops a crowd by shouting names, and a girl insists her goat won't leave her. The trouble sits at the hinges. In the lead-in to the hand moving, Yeshal reports shafts, clocks and a nine-second interval she cannot see from where she stands, and she is answering a question with no object. The counterweight drop is forbidden, then awaited, before anyone has proposed it. After that, the decisive exchange runs as a department round-robin (ritual precedent, technical limits, cost, audit, ruling). The last exchanges are a methods lecture and a quartermaster meeting in which Aksor, Polaq and Yeshal lose their voices. Yeshal's uncertainty is meant as fear, but every line she speaks has the same fixed walk-back shape. The edit pass should put the missing causality on the page, turn the decisive and final exchanges back into people who want things from each other, and cut Yeshal's hedges down to the ones that matter. It should leave the ledge alone.

## Dialogue findings

### D1 · Blocking · Wave count before the hand moves relies on unseen evidence

- Where: L37–L53, heel ward, the three compressions and the countdown to the first finger rising
- Text:
> “One,” Yeshal said. She caught her plumb reel before it fell. “Up-arm shafts crossed first. I think they crossed first.”
> “Two. The interval is shorter here. Or the clocks disagree. They may disagree.”
> “Three,” Yeshal said. “Three. There were exactly three.”
> “How long after the third up-arm?”
> “Nine seconds at the first shaft. Perhaps eight. I can't promise the clocks.”
- Problem: Ground/knowledge and Ground/referent at the prompt's central moment. Yeshal has just walked down from the wrist gardens behind Aksor. She stands in the heel ward holding a plumb reel, and no shafts, clocks, runner or bell relay are on the page. Yet she reports what "the up-arm shafts" registered and compares an interval "here" with one measured somewhere else. She quotes a timing "at the first shaft", and it matches the finger rising "At eight". "There were exactly three" comes the instant the third wave lands, as if she already knew the size of the set. Zunel's "How long after the third up-arm?" has no recoverable object (after the third wave, until what?). The reader can only work back from "At eight, the middle finger rose" that some earlier up-arm event followed the third wave by nine seconds, and the story never shows that event.
- Fix: Decide what Yeshal can know from where she stands. The smallest fix: she reports only what her reel and her feet give her (direction, count, whether the gap is shortening), "There were exactly three" goes, and the "At seven… At eight" beats become Zunel counting seconds with no prediction behind them. If the remote timing is worth keeping, stage its source in one clause before the first wave (the story already uses hand-bell codes at L197), and make Zunel's question name the event it asks about. Illustrative only: Yeshal: "Three. That's three. Is it stopping?"

### D2 · Blocking · The counterweight drop is forbidden before anyone proposes it

- Where: L187–L213, forearm end of the ledge while Polaq is missing, through the start of the counterweight scene
- Text:
> “No weights until he closes the register.”
> Only then did Zunel look up-arm toward the bridge counterweights.
- Problem: Ground/knowledge and Ground/referent at the story's causal hinge. At L189 Zunel tells Yeshal there will be "no weights" until the count closes, and at L205 "Only then" treats the drop as a plan everyone has been waiting on. Nobody has proposed it. The counterweights are first described at L209, and Aksor's pitch arrives at L213, after the count. The listener at L189 could understand "weights" only if the plan had been discussed off the page, and the reader has no way to recover it. So the waiting on Polaq before an irreversible act has no tension, because the reader doesn't know an irreversible act is pending. (The outline put the proposal before the crossing. The prose moved it and left these two references behind.)
- Fix: Put a short version of Aksor's proposal on the page before L189, either at the forearm end when the last group is across or mid-crossing while he counts touches against the wall, and have Zunel's refusal answer it. L213 then becomes his case for it, shorter, not a first pitch. The cheaper alternative, cutting L189's line and L205's "Only then", throws away a good delay. Illustrative only: Aksor: "The bridge weights. Four of them, one at a time, like at the tables."

### D3 · Major · Decisive exchange is a round-robin of positions ending in buttons

- Where: L209–L223, forearm gardens above the counterweights
- Text:
> “At the dawn tables, we set four bowls from forearm to wrist,” he said. “For the rooms below, I touch the shelf once at each setting.
> Four spaced touches. Not speech. Enough to be unlike one fallen tray.
> I can say the impacts will enter the stone. I can't say where they go after. I can't say they're felt.
> “Four weights close the bridge,” Zunel said.
> “The bridge is closed down-hand already,” Polaq replied.
> Nothing living is uncounted.
- Problem: A procedure routine. Each speaker gives one department's position in turn (Aksor the ritual precedent, Yeshal the technical limits, Zunel the cost, Polaq the audit) and then Zunel rules. Three of those turns end on a neat closer:
  - "Not speech." answers an objection nobody raised. It is the outline's "notice, not language" rule, spoken by the character.
  - Yeshal's "I can say / I can't say / I can't say" is a rhetorical triplet.
  - Polaq's "Nothing living is uncounted" is a last-word line.

  Aksor never actually asks for anything or says who the touches are for, and "For the rooms below" is an unexplained referent. Yeshal's "the cistern hammers prove that" introduces an unexplained device. The narration at L209 has just told the reader what the drops will do and cost, and the bridge crew already knows it. So "Four weights close the bridge" is "as you know" exposition, and it exists to be corrected ("closed down-hand already"). What makes the decision hard for Zunel is that she is handing her last route to the man whose warning she ignored at the brace. Only narration (L221) carries that.
- Fix: Let Aksor ask for the drops plainly, in his tactile terms, and say who they are for, while still claiming nothing about what she will do. Cut "Not speech." Collapse Yeshal's triplet into one sentence about what she can and can't promise, and cut "cistern hammers". Replace "Four weights close the bridge" with something that belongs to Zunel (the brace, the route, the palm she can no longer certify), or with a silence before "Release one." Keep Polaq's breathlessness and the tool chest and gate keys detail, but drop "Nothing living is uncounted." Illustrative only: Aksor: "Let me knock on her. Four, spaced, the way I do at the tables. So she knows someone's on her hand."

### D4 · Major · Yeshal's uncertainty is one fixed walk-back in every line

- Where: L37–L49 heel ward; L109 avenue; L171 ledge; L187 forearm end; L215 counterweights; L245 after the drops
- Text:
> Not another three—this isn't another set, I don't mean that.
> It may narrow again in forty seconds. Or thirty. The line catches wind here; it may be thirty.
> The seventeen may be there. They may be; the pump-gallery mark isn't a count.
> Polaq answered before Yeshal could qualify again.
- Problem: Identical Voices within one speaker. Every Yeshal speech is an assertion followed by one to three sentences that retract or restate it ("They may disagree", "Perhaps eight", "it may be thirty", "They may be"). The outline means this as fear, but it never changes with pressure. She hedges the same way at dawn, on a ledge that is closing under her, and in the calm afterward. So it reads as the collection's hyper-precise register with a quirk attached, and the narration has to remark on it (L113). "Not another three—this isn't another set, I don't mean that" corrects a misunderstanding nobody voiced. The walk-backs also crowd her one clean warning, "The thumb shafts are next", in the same speech. "The pump-gallery mark" is a referent the reader never sees.
- Fix: After D1, D3 and D6 have rewritten her key lines, sweep the rest. Keep her comparisons and numbers, allow at most one hedge per speech, and let the shape change with the danger: clipped on the ledge where seconds matter, more spilling at dawn when she is frightened, quiet afterward. Leave the thumb warning unhedged so it registers. Explain or cut "the pump-gallery mark". Illustrative only (L171): Yeshal: "Thirty seconds. Maybe less."

### D5 · Major · Polaq's tense fake-out on the inner route

- Where: L149–L155, the ledge after the thumb destroys the inner stair
- Text:
> Polaq coughed dust into his sleeve. “Sixty-two from the basin wards were on it.”
> Zunel stared at him.
> “Were. They turned at the potters' arch. I counted them onto this ledge.
- Problem: Punchline Patch. The man whose whole voice is exact counts says the one sentence that implies sixty-two deaths, waits out Zunel's stare, then corrects his own tense. The setup exists to produce the reversal, and the reversal is a precision correction: the collection's correction tic in a single exchange. The scare is the author's. Polaq has no reason to phrase it that way.
- Fix: Let Polaq lead with the fact he would lead with (they are off it, and he counted them onto this ledge). Keep the itemized sixty-two and the old dog in the grain sling. If the scene wants a jolt, let it come from Zunel's look at the shattered stair before he answers, not from his grammar. Keep "His next breath hitched" and the undercroft worry.

### D6 · Major · Final exchange about the goddess is a methods lecture

- Where: L241–L247, after the fourth drop
- Text:
> “The rate changed,” Yeshal said. “After the impacts, yes. Also after the thumb completed its turn.
> We don't know which comparison matters. We don't know that any of them matter.
- Problem: The Philosopher, at the final meaningful exchange. The action already carries the uncertainty: four tones, the long wait, the lowered stone tone, Aksor's bowl going still. Yeshal then lists the confounds and states the conclusion outright, and the narration restates it on both sides (L241, L247; see P2). "After the thumb completed its turn" names an event the reader never saw finish; the thumb was last shown turning at L145–L147. Nobody answers her, and Zunel nods. Aksor, whose idea the drops were, doesn't react at all.
- Fix: Let Yeshal report what she measured (it slowed, and when) and stop. Cut both "We don't know" sentences. If the thumb stays as a confound, show it settling in the narration at L243. If anyone reacts, give it to Aksor or Zunel as a gesture or a plain line, not a conclusion. Illustrative only: Yeshal: "It's slower. Since just after the fourth."

### D7 · Major · Closing work scene flattens everyone into one logistics voice

- Where: L257–L285, forearm gardens, late morning
- Text:
> “Upper cistern to the fever shelter first,” Aksor said.
> The goat trough gets drainage, not drinking water.
> “Three bells for the waves?” Aksor asked.
> “One bell, three counts,” Zunel said.
> No one uses the wrist ledge without my inspection.
> Polaq looked toward the cupped hand, then down at his full register cords. “And the outer route?”
> “Measure it every hour. Don't promise it.”
- Problem: Identical Voices plus a procedure routine. Cover the tags on L257–L285 and Aksor, Zunel, Polaq and Yeshal all speak in the same sets of short imperatives. Aksor is tactile and ceremonial everywhere else, but here he sounds like a quartermaster ("gets drainage, not drinking water"), and Yeshal's protocol line has none of her voice. Replies run on corrections ("Not the one who lost the gate keys", "One bell, three counts"). "And if the bowls warm?" is the only line that touches what happened, and it goes straight into a bell protocol with no human response. Polaq's "And the outer route?" asks about what Zunel has just ruled on (the outer-wrist ledge). The reader can't tell whether it is a different route, and the question exists to hand Zunel the closing line.
- Fix: Keep the practical ending, the terrace keepers' squabble, the copyist's son, the old dog, and L271–L273. Give Aksor his hands back (pouring, dividing, deciding who gets the bowl), keep Polaq on names and particular needs, and let Yeshal sound like herself once. Before the bell rules, let "And if the bowls warm?" get a pause, or one answer that isn't procedure. Cut Polaq's "And the outer route?" or point it at something new (the palm, the way home), and let Zunel answer with a plain order rather than a closer. Illustrative only: Aksor: "Fever shelter first. Then a cup each. I'll pour."

### D8 · Minor · Aksor's brace warning ends on a word-slip retort

- Where: L85–L93, main wrist avenue, the cross-brace
- Text:
> This one is rolling toward my heel.
> “No more weight on the center!” she shouted. “Carts to the edges.”
> “The edges are what are parting,” Aksor said.
- Problem: Zunel's "edges" are the sides of the roadway. Aksor's are the two crease lips, so his reply scores off her word instead of warning about the thing, and "are what are" stumbles aloud. "Rolling toward my heel" is unclear: he is crouched with his fingertips on the stone, so "my heel" reads as his own foot rather than the palm heel. The warning itself is right, and "Those sides don't rise together" is good.
- Fix: Have him say which sides are moving and how, in his bowl-leveling terms, without echoing her word. Make "my heel" "the palm heel", or show the direction with his hand.

### D10 · Minor · Polaq's two ledger speeches repeat the seventeen

- Where: L199–L203, top of the garden steps
- Text:
> “The seventeen crossed before the main avenue failed,” he said as soon as he had breath enough to speak.
> Five thousand two hundred eighty-one assigned to the inhabited hand districts at last night's close.
> Seventeen entered the pump gallery under Temru's count and are present in the shelter.
- Problem: Polaq's exactness is earned, and the full count is what permits the irreversible drop, so the numbers should stay. But the second speech reads like an audit statement ("assigned to the inhabited hand districts at last night's close", "present in the shelter") from a man who has just run up the garden steps, and it re-reports the seventeen he itemized one breath earlier. "As soon as he had breath enough to speak" sidesteps the breathlessness instead of letting it shape his lines.
- Fix: Keep the itemized seventeen and "I touched every register tile." Compress the full count to the total and its two sources, in his own words, and let his breathing show in the rhythm.

## Prose findings

### P1 · Major · "Not X but Y" reversal habit in the narration

- Where: L23, L25, L35, L77, L125, L181, L233 (the ending's instances are in P2; the dialogue instances at L213 and L257 are in D3 and D7)
- Text:
> It was not an impact from above. The pressure came lengthwise through the goddess
> Not a prediction, only a sequence the body had given them often enough to use once.
> Yeshal counted under her breath, checking not the goddess but the interval they themselves could control.
- Problem: About a dozen sentences in about 3,260 words are built as a negation followed by a correction. The rest are L23 "not sun-warm, not yet, but", L25 "Not a struck note.", L77 "Not yet a jointed lift—only" and L125 "for lamp trimmers, not a city". With the ending (P2), this becomes the narration's default way of making a point. Several instances explain what the reader already has (L181, L233).
- Fix: Keep two or three where the contrast carries real information: L35's direction of force is useful, and L125 works as a section opener. State the rest positively or cut them. L181's "Not a prediction…" sentence and L233's "not the goddess but" clause can simply go.

## Continuity and prompt findings

### C1 · Major · Water infrastructure used as default texture and plot machinery

- Where: L11–L21 opening; L63, L101, L107, L119 the aqueduct under the bridge; L187, L199, L203 the pump gallery; L191, L195, L255 irrigation; L215 cistern hammers; L219, L269 lower-gate keys; L257–L259, L267 cistern, drainage, waterkeepers

> “Sever the aqueduct on the forearm side. Save the upper head.”
> Irrigation water climbed one row and drained from another.
- Problem: The prompt asks for none of it. Even so, cisterns, sluices, a gate wheel, waterkeepers, an aqueduct, a pump gallery and irrigation supply the opening image, Zunel's first orders, a plot beat (the torn slab cracking the aqueduct), and much of the closing logistics. The "No default water infrastructure" entry in universe/style-guide.md (CANON) counts even passing texture, and it applies to authorized revisions, which is what the edit pass is. Many of these nouns sit in dialogue lines the pass will rewrite anyway.
- Fix: Before rewriting any dialogue, draw up a short substitution list so revised lines don't carry these nouns forward:
  - Detect the tilt with something the story already has (a plumb, a standing bowl, a lamp-oil gauge).
  - Give the two waterkeepers another trade; the story already has lamp trimmers and bell keepers.
  - Cut the aqueduct, and let the torn slab hit the bridge deck or the counterweight housing.
  - Rename the pump gallery as a passage with no hydraulic job.
  - Turn the irrigation rows into garden rows, and cut "cistern hammers".
  - Ration drinking water from carried jars instead of an "upper cistern".

  Ordinary water is allowed and should stay: Aksor's bowls, the jars, the floodwater lake, the drinking rations.
