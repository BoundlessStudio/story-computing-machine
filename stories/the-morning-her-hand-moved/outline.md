# Outline

## Story

- Premise and central promise: For a thousand years, Neshra's people have made
  their temples, streets, cisterns, and gardens in the motionless stone body of
  Ammara, whom they worship as a goddess dead before their oldest record. At
  dawn, while Neshra and her adult child Odal work in the gardens of the Open
  Hand, the entire palm begins to turn inward. Interleave their effort to endure
  the changing gravity with Ammara's first returning sensations: the reader
  learns that she is a living stone person, not a statue, and that her attempt
  to complete one ordinary waking gesture has taken longer than a civilization.
  Resolve the immediate promise when Ammara recognizes patterned life upon her
  and stops her hand half-cupped before closing it, preserving people she cannot
  yet address while permanently changing their inhabited ground.
- Intended reader experience: Begin in an old, practical intimacy with sacred
  landscape—soil worked into a lifeline, stair treads recut by generations,
  ordinary irritation between parent and adult child—then let scale arrive
  through water running the wrong way and the horizon rising past a fingertip.
  Alternate bodily peril with Ammara's slow, fragmentary attention until awe
  becomes mutual vulnerability rather than spectacle. End with relief that
  comes from restraint, not safety: the hand has stopped, the goddess is awake,
  and neither scale of person yet knows how to live with the other.
- Generating force:
  - Mode: Relationship- and attention-led convergence across incompatible
    scales. Each change in the palm's angle forces Neshra and Odal to revise
    their relation to home, while the same footfalls, watercourses, roots, and
    falling structures become increasingly legible to Ammara as organized
    life. The two strands meet in an act of attention rather than dialogue.
  - Focal attachment or contradiction: Neshra loves Ammara's Palm through the
    exacting work of keeping its thin soil, old trees, and household terraces
    in place; she also loves Odal, who has long argued that Neshra treats the
    garden's needs as permission to decide for both of them. The movement makes
    preservation and care diverge: trying to keep every rooted thing fixed
    endangers the person beside her, while leaving the garden does not make the
    attachment false.
  - Plot-led wants and useful limit, if applicable: Not principally plot-led.
    Neshra immediately wants to keep Odal alive, retain enough water to survive
    the next tilt, and prevent the loaded trellises from dragging them over the
    new downslope. Her practiced limit is to answer danger by fastening,
    pruning, and preserving in place. It makes her resourceful during the first
    movement but slow to accept that survival now requires cutting their packs
    and bodies free of the garden she knows how to save.
- Counterforce or complication:
  - Source: The mismatch of body, settlement, sensation, and time. A slight
    flex for Ammara changes gravity across an inhabited district; stopping
    midway drives fresh fractures through a stone wrist that has not borne
    muscular strain for a millennium. Neshra and Odal have no route, ritual,
    or instrument capable of reaching Ammara as speech, while Ammara initially
    experiences buildings, roots, and people only as an indistinguishable
    crust of pressure.
  - Credible inside logic, if a person: Odal is a countervoice, not an
    antagonist. They came to help with the garden because they love Neshra and
    know the work, but they want a life not organized around inheriting every
    root and retaining wall. Once the hand moves, Odal insists on abandoning
    property before the next tilt and refuses Neshra's attempt to send them
    ahead alone; their competence and anger remain active after Neshra yields.
  - Pressure or change created: Each effort to preserve the old orientation
    stores more dangerous weight in trellises, jars, and saturated soil. Each
    release destroys part of a home that might still be needed if the hand
    stops. Ammara faces the inverse pressure: completing the gesture may relieve
    her seized joints, but doing so will crush or shed the lives she has only
    just become capable of distinguishing.
- Narrative design:
  - POV, tense, distance, and limitation: Asymmetric close third person. Keep
    the human strand in past tense through Neshra, intimate with horticultural
    judgment, changing balance, old pain, and what she cannot admit to Odal.
    Use brief present-tense Ammara interludes that begin as bodily fragments and
    gradually recover a coherent self; do not enter Odal's interior or give
    Neshra access to Ammara's choice.
  - Time shape and compression: Roughly two hours from first light to the
    arrested gesture, braided with what Ammara experiences as one interrupted
    attempt to curl her fingers. Give the first tilt, Neshra and Odal's argument
    over what to release, the major orientation change, Ammara's recognition,
    and the hand's stop full scene space. Compress the civilization's thousand
    years into sensate discontinuities—scratches becoming streets, isolated
    taps becoming daily traffic—rather than a historical montage or lore
    summary.
  - Information and reveal strategy: Establish the body-as-landscape through
    unremarked daily practice before any movement. Let the reader understand
    before Neshra that the interludes belong to Ammara, but delay coherent name,
    scale, and intention until the first movement links the same spilled water
    and uprooted tree in both viewpoints. The existence of inhabited patterns,
    not a single chosen signal, lets Ammara distinguish people. Reveal no
    ancient enemy, engineering solution, objective proof of godhood, or full
    account of why her body became stone.
  - Governing movement: Tend; disagree; tilt; fasten; feel; misname the event as
    fracture; release one load; perceive repetitions; turn farther; lose the
    old floor; choose the living person over the fixed garden; recognize lives;
    arrest the gesture; take stock in a new orientation.
- Speculative surplus: Flexing living stone expresses warm alkaline brine from
  capillary seams. It dries into brittle blue scales that numb bare fingertips,
  foul shutters, and clog narrow drains, so Neshra and Odal must wrap their
  hands and keep scraping the water channel even after the main movement stops.
  The brine neither communicates, measures devotion, nor determines who
  survives; it is an inconvenient bodily consequence of this local being.
- Structural distinction: A held-place braid rather than an evacuation trek or
  assembly plot. The same small palm garden is successively floor, slope, wall,
  and precarious shelf while brief passages from the body beneath it lengthen
  into personhood. The decisive act occurs inside Ammara's attention and is
  witnessed by Neshra only as cessation; there is no public demonstration,
  converted crowd, technical control room, institutional rewrite, or polished
  declaration that settles what the civilization should believe.
- Decisive turn, deepening, or recognition: A final increment of curl pulls the
  trellis sideways. Neshra first tries to lash Odal, their water, and the oldest
  rootstock together, repeating the preservation habit that has kept the garden
  alive. When the root mass starts to peel the whole terrace loose, she cuts
  both carrying packs and the loaded trellis free, allowing soil, jars, and the
  old tree to fall while she and Odal remain wedged in the bare irrigation cut.
  The sudden release separates falling material from two bodies that keep
  adjusting together. Within Ammara's strand, that distinction completes a
  larger accumulation of patterned motion: she recognizes not an infestation
  or weathering but lives inhabiting her. She holds the painful half-curl rather
  than finish it.
- Aftereffect or live uncertainty: Ammara's Palm remains habitable only in a
  provisional, sharply tilted form; gardens and streets are damaged, routes to
  the wrist may no longer join, and distant movements elsewhere on Ammara's
  body are possible. Neshra and Odal survive with one shared water jar and no
  promise that Ammara can keep still, speak, or understand the harm already
  done. Ammara knows lives are present but not their language, history,
  worship, number, or needs. Neshra agrees that they will leave the ruined
  terrace together when the wristward drain can be tested, without turning
  that immediate choice into a verdict on the whole civilization's future.
- Opening / ending relation: Open with Neshra correcting Odal's placement of a
  water jar because the garden's established slope has carried every spill the
  same way for generations. End in the stopped, half-cupped hand with the one
  surviving jar braced between their feet while blue mineral scale begins to
  choke the only drain. They scrape it clear in turns and listen to a remote
  strain under the wrist; fixed ground has become ongoing bodily work, and the
  story stops before either person can call that work worship, trespass, or
  home.

## Beats

1. At first light, Neshra and Odal thin roots and reset water jars in the Open
   Hand. Their practiced cooperation carries an old disagreement: Neshra assumes
   Odal will inherit the terrace; Odal says helping today is not consent to a
   lifetime. Ordinary details establish temples, homes, traffic, and gardens
   carved across the apparently dead body without a lore lecture.
2. A short Ammara interlude registers pressure, warmth, and an unfinished wish
   to bend fingers. No name or scale is initially supplied. In Neshra's scene,
   water climbs out of its groove, the sunrise drops below the fingertip, and
   the entire garden acquires a slope no local fracture can explain.
3. Neshra fastens jars and trellises while Odal clears a person-sized irrigation
   cut for shelter. Distant bells and falling masonry establish consequences
   beyond them without shifting into a civic-response plot. Neshra keeps calling
   the motion settling until the visible finger rotates against the horizon.
4. Ammara tries to complete the curl. Millennial discontinuities sharpen: tiny
   abrasion became repeated paths; roots entered painless outer seams; countless
   pressures now move in coordinated daily patterns. She still cannot separate
   architecture from weathering or living bodies from loose debris.
5. The palm turns far enough that the former courtyard wall becomes their
   footing. Odal wants to cut free everything above them; Neshra releases only
   expendable jars and tries to save the old tree and seed packs. Warm brine
   opens from a seam, numbing their hands and clogging the drain they need as a
   refuge.
6. A pause creates false hope. Neshra and Odal argue while scraping the drain:
   Odal refuses to flee alone or inherit by default; Neshra admits that saving
   the garden has become her only fluent answer to losing any part of it. They
   agree on one more release sequence, but not yet on the oldest rootstock.
7. The last, stronger increment loads tree, trellis, packs, and both people onto
   the same failing anchor. Neshra sees that her lashings have made Odal part of
   the garden's load. She cuts the packs and trellis away; the tree and much of
   the terrace fall, while she and Odal shift together into the bare channel.
8. In the corresponding Ammara passage, falling objects continue without
   correction, but paired bodies brace, release, regroup, and protect one
   another across many sites. Pattern becomes intention and intention becomes
   people. Ammara accepts the strain of stopping midgesture. The hand arrests
   with the palm half-cupped.
9. Neshra cannot know why the movement stopped. She and Odal inventory water,
   injuries, the altered route, and what is gone. They decide to test the
   wristward drain together after the stone strain quiets; meanwhile, they take
   turns clearing fresh blue scale so their only channel does not seal.
10. End in practical suspension rather than public interpretation: the jar must
    be kept level, distant districts remain audible, and a new strain travels
    under the wrist while human and goddess each attend to a scale they cannot
    yet comprehend.

## Continuity

- Canon used: The story occupies one unresolved era and region of the single
  physical deep-time world. It uses the locked distinction between cultural
  labels and underlying systems, the possibility that later cultures mistake
  beings or artifacts for gods and the dead, the requirement that any magical
  agency shown belong to life, and the canon taxonomy in which worship alone
  does not establish objective godhood.
- Boundaries and unknowns: Neshra, Odal, Ammara, Ammara's Palm, the civilization,
  and Ammara's living-stone condition are story-local and new. Establish no
  connection to Queen Aurelia, the Daughter, the woman claiming the Goddess of
  Time title, the Crowned Ancestors, any dragon, any prior sleep mechanism, or
  any other named deity, artifact, polity, or location. Ammara's awareness,
  physical power, one arrested gesture, expressed brine, and millennium-long
  immobility establish no species, universal divine biology, general living-
  stone rule, shared mechanism, or objective godhood verdict. Leave unresolved
  her origin, the cause and full nature of the stone state, exact relation to
  other eras, former authority, whether the rest of her body will move, whether
  communication is possible, and how the civilization responds beyond the
  focal morning. Her returning personhood and choice remain real rather than
  being explained away as a device fault or automatic reflex.
