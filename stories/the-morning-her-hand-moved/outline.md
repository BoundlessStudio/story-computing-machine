# Outline

## Story

- Premise and central promise: **The Morning Her Hand Moved**. A civilization occupies a supine stone goddess: knuckle temples, palm streets, forearm gardens. When her outstretched right hand cups at dawn, Zunel must evacuate its districts across the stretching wrist. Deliver scale, awe, disruption, survival, and a bounded signal; leave motive and next action unknown.
- Focal pressure or attachment: Zunel wants to preserve the main wrist avenue and its waterworks, but every brace assumes inert ground. Her devotion is repair; respecting a possible living actor requires abandoning structures, not fastening her joint.
- Counterforce or complication: Each pulse changes slopes, gaps, and drainage. Finger streets tip inward, cisterns converge on the palm, wrist cracks widen, and the turning thumb removes a route. Aksor wants a non-commanding signal; Yeshal refuses false certainty; Polaq finds needs that break clean counts.
- POV, distance, and information limit: Close third past through Zunel, attentive to strain, loads, water, dust, and a two-mile palm's horizon. Bells and Polaq carry evidence from the forearm. She cannot see the goddess's face, know divinity or intent, or distinguish response from movement cadence.
- Governing movement and time shape: One morning in successive strain fronts, moving hand to wrist to forearm: first curl, failed stabilization, reroute, last crossing, signal, and temporary resettlement. Keep decisions among four workers on the limb, not a council. End in practical shelter and water allocation after the hand rests differently, without unanimity or a settled future.
- Speculative rule or ordinary-world constraint: Her stone is continuous living lithic tissue. Before voluntary joint motion, shallow warmth and three compression waves travel torso-to-limb; cisterns, plumbs, and stone tones reveal arrival and local tension seconds before movement, never thought or motive. Surface pressure can travel inward as touch. Four spaced counterweight impacts can therefore signal presence, but dropping them destroys the wrist bridge's balance and return route. A later pause may be observed, never translated as consent, intention, prophecy, or proof of understanding.

## Voice

- Narrative texture: Follow Zunel's balance and work-trained attention. Loads become slopes, streets become anatomy, and awe arrives through distance, warmth, water direction, and structures suddenly too small for their ground.
- Conversational texture: Keep talk mobile, interrupted by bells, tilting steps, dust, carrying, and relays. Central speech mixes instructions, partial observations, corrections, and decisions; residents ask about one person, animal, medicine, or bundle.
- Rhetorical ownership: Zunel reasons in routes and failure conditions; Aksor in tactile sequences and practice without doctrinal claims; Yeshal in timing, comparison, and uncertainty; Polaq in names, bottlenecks, and particular consequences.
- Pressure behavior: Zunel grows terse and stops listening; Aksor loses ceremonial fluency for bodily correction; frightened Yeshal repeats qualifications; breathless Polaq becomes exact when counts decide a route. Listening improves unevenly through labor, not conversion.
- Anti-default: Avoid faith-versus-science debate, aphorisms about godhood, or technical talk that makes the goddess infrastructure. Let logistics carry evidence and keep the ending practical.

## Beats

1. Before sunrise, water climbs one side of a palm basin and the eastern fingers rise against the sky. A knuckle temple tilts; street bells swing themselves. Zunel orders movement before anyone names the event.
2. At the wrist gardens, Aksor's dawn bowls warm forearm-first. Yeshal's up-arm plumbs confirm three approaching pulses. They establish likely motion and a shrinking interval, not life, awakening, purpose, or prophecy.
3. Evacuation starts from palm heel to forearm. Polaq reports litters, opened pens, and residents retrieving water. Zunel braces a wrist crack until its separating edges show the bridge joins surfaces moving apart.
4. Fingers curl; water and debris run toward the palm center; the thumb closes a stair; an emptied knuckle temple sheds columns. Zunel cuts the aqueduct and shifts evacuation to a narrow outer-wrist ledge, abandoning most goods and stored water.
5. Aksor proposes four bridge-counterweight impacts as notice, not language. Yeshal can promise transmission, not perception. Zunel delays the irreversible signal until Polaq has checked every inhabited terrace, accepting that it will close return.
6. A warm front opens the ledge's last span while the forearm stays comparatively level. Using the warning, Zunel staggers the crossings instead of rushing. Polaq closes the count when the missing residents are found sheltering up-arm.
7. From the forearm, they drop the counterweights onto exposed warm stone. Each dwarfed impact travels as a distinct tone; the bridge falls out of balance. The next strain slows or pauses before the hand settles cupped, with causation unknowable.
8. By late morning the empty palm ward is tilted and flooded across a new gulf. Zunel assigns forearm gardens as temporary shelter; Yeshal keeps pulse watch, Aksor rations, and Polaq resumes routes. Survival and relocation are secured; motion, contact, motive, and long-term habitation remain open.
