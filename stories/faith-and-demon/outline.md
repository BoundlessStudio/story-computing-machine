# Provisional direction

Anwen and Mikkel are hired to lower a cracked bell from a hilltop chapel before the building is closed. Mikkel belongs to the chapel's faith; Anwen has a different, equally lived faith, one in which a demon may be called as witness and enforcer. They need each other's hands and judgment on the rigging. Mikkel's concern for Anwen is sincere, but he keeps turning instructions and pauses in the work into invitations to convert. The prompt's warning comes from Anwen when she can no longer tell whether he hears anything she says.

Mikkel trusts his reading of the rigging over Anwen's explicit warning about the cracked bell's movement; the delay to their safety step lets its weight shift. In that charged interval he presses again, and Anwen summons the demon. The bell is already hanging over the chapel floor; neither can safely abandon the other line. The demon is real and willing to carry Mikkel below, but Anwen must decide what she actually meant by the threat while the two workers still have to get the bell down. The witness must depart before Mikkel's decisive choice, so fear of its immediate grip cannot pass for respect.

Possible end: Anwen sends the demon away without a magical fee. Mikkel must do one difficult thing Anwen instructs him to do, despite his confident instinct to do the opposite. They finish lowering the bell without converting, reconciling, or explaining away either faith. The outline leaves open whether his subsequent silence feels like respect, fear, or both; the prose should discover an ending more particular than a lesson in tolerance.
