# Opus review: The Apes Above

Independent audit for a later editing pass. It is not a `review.md` verdict,
does not change canon state, and does not authorize edits by itself.

## Verdict

- Dialogue: REVISE — the working-floor talk is good, but the decision exchanges close on flips and rulings, most of them Sulekk's, and one accusation (L217) describes something that never happened.
- Prose: REVISE — concrete and well staged; the revision needed is one consistent sweep of the water-infrastructure texture plus a few triplet and "not X. Y." cadences.
- Continuity and prompt: PASS — the prompt is met in full at 3,934 words; three minor slips (runner count, lens staging, late role labels).
- Edit scope: targeted-dialogue
- Existing review.md said: Verdict PASS, "Dialogue: PASS"; disagree on dialogue. It missed the L217 ground failure and the flip-and-ruling pattern at every decision point.

This story's dialogue is better than the collection's usual failure: wherever crews and instruments are at stake it works. Orders run into obstacles, people cut each other off, Brinnt talks in hinge pins and laminate, and Oxaul's hedging falls apart under the clock. The trouble sits where the council decides. The pivot question (L177), the beacon rationale (L197), the authorization (L307–L319), the aftermath (L453) and the final ruling (L465–L485) each end on a neat reversal or a stated rule, Sulekk owns nearly all of those last words, and secondary councillors mostly exist to feed them. The edit should leave the working-floor dialogue alone and make the decision exchanges plainer and messier: Sulekk ends arguments by handing out work rather than by winning the line, the road councillor gets to be petty about his ramp in his own words, and Oxaul explains the beacon logic at hedged length instead of in an aphorism. The prompt's "pesky apes" gives the council room for some pettiness, and council dissent is the place for it; do not add jokes elsewhere.

## Edit guardrails

- Prompt: a Dino Council meeting about apes in orbit throwing rocks, resolved in a complete, broadly accessible story. The last `Craft profile:` line is prospective-2026-08-21. The rewrite request is scoped to dialogue.
- Length band 2,500–4,000 words. The prose is 3,934 words, so every edit must be word-neutral or a net cut. D7 and D5 give back the most words.
- POV is close third through Qemri. Keep her interior (L249, L387, L423) as the place where her reasoning lives.
- Mechanics the climax depends on, which must survive: the ballast is compacted station shielding thrown backward to change station speed; the transponder and cold-gas valves obey station commands ("Nothing in the object decides"); the targeting aims at an old coordinate in a basin that used to be empty; the seventh descent got a late pulse; one full charge either fragments the rock or drives a full echo, and narrowing the pulse leaves a weak third; the three beacons sit south, north and in the quarry; Qemri fires if the rock crosses the yellow line; a new command sends the rock to the quarry; Oxaul cannot prove intent; there are no dead; the three-pass trial follows.
- Universe: the story contradicts no LOCKED or CANON entry. "Earth" is the era-bound shared-world label (locations.md, "The world across eras", LOCKED). Keep the outline's boundaries: nothing establishes human motive, machine sentience, translation or a lasting peace.
- Keep the unequal fluency. Oxaul's collapse (L369, L411, L419, L443) and Qemri's short commands are the best voice work in the story.

## Dialogue findings

### D1 · Major · Pivot question answered with a word-choice quip

- Where: L177–L185, trace alcove, "Can they hear us?"
- Text:
> "Can they hear us?" Sulekk asked.
> "Hear is not the word I would choose."
> "Choose a shorter one."
- Problem: Punchline Patch at the story's turning point. Oxaul's line exists to set up Sulekk's comeback, and the comeback does not make literal sense: "hear" is already about as short as a word gets, so all the listener can take from it is "stop lecturing". The room has just gone still (L179), and the exchange spends that moment on correction ping-pong about vocabulary.
- Fix: Put Oxaul's hedge and his answer in one turn, or have Sulekk cut him off by name without a quip. Keep "Their receiver measures returns. We can make returns." Illustrative only: "Not the way you mean. But their receiver measures returns, and we can make returns."

### D2 · Major · Beacon rationale delivered as aphorism

- Where: L189–L197, trace alcove, Oxaul's beacon plan and the road councillor's questions
- Text:
> The delays mark two locations. Repeated, organized receivers. It would not identify us. It would not say stop.
> "Because two points are a line. Three points show a choice of ground."
- Problem: This is the story's central mechanism, and the reader needs it to understand why the rock ends up in the quarry. "Repeated, organized receivers" is a clipped noun phrase with no verb, so the reader cannot tell what the stations would conclude or about whom. The answer to "Then why put one there?" is an epigram, not a reason. It has to be decoded into "a second place that answers gives them an alternative", and "two points are a line" does not describe what the basin beacons do, which is bracket an occupied area. The road councillor is acting as explanatory chorus here, but his question is fair and he has a stake in the answer, so he can stay.
- Fix: Turn Oxaul's claim into a full clause about what the stations would detect, and replace the quarry answer with his actual hedged reasoning: he cannot make the beacon say "safe", he can only put a second answering place on their map. Illustrative only: "It would show them something down here answering, from fixed places, on purpose." / "So their map has somewhere else that answers. I can't make it mean safe."

### D3 · Blocking · Qemri's accusation describes an event that did not happen

- Where: L209–L221, trace alcove, the coil-cost reveal
- Text:
> "You were waiting until I had carried it this far."
> Oxaul looked down at the moving arc. "Yes."
- Problem: Ground failure at the beat where Oxaul admits he withheld the coil cost. Read plainly, the line says Qemri had championed or carried the beacon idea. Nothing in the scene shows that. Sulekk asked the question, Oxaul proposed the plan, the road councillor and Brinnt questioned it, and Qemri's only move was to ask whether the coil was involved. Nothing on the page is an "it" she has carried. Oxaul's "Yes" then confirms an event the reader never saw, so the shift in trust has nothing to stand on.
- Fix: Have the accusation name what he actually did: he let the room take in the plan, and let the clock run from 84 to 72 minutes, before he mentioned that it spends the only full charge. Keep "Yes." and "Qemri had expected another qualification." Illustrative only: "You wanted us to like it before we knew what it cost."

### D4 · Major · Road councillor's ramp objection runs as rulings and reverses his stance

- Where: L233–L247, trace alcove, road councillor against Sulekk (compare L67)
- Text:
> The road councillor wanted the north ramp reserved for egg sledges.
> "And while you tap at the sky, my ramp is blocked by egg sledges."
> "I have asked nothing yet."
> "My district hears the difference poorly."
> "Go give them one."
- Problem: At L67 he wanted the north ramp reserved for egg sledges. Here he complains that it is blocked by egg sledges, and the reader cannot tell whether his grievance is the sledges, the delay or the precedent. The exchange then turns into hair-splitting over asking versus not asking, in the same dry legal register Sulekk uses. "Go give them one" has to be traced back to "a cart count" in the narration two lines earlier. This is the one place where council pettiness (which the prompt's "pesky apes" invites) could show, and it comes out as procedure.
- Fix: Make his grievance consistent with L67. Either he wants his sledges moving and the experiment keeps them parked until the last line, or the complaint moves to the granary member, who "needed that same ramp for draft teams". Let him be concrete and self-interested (numbers, his district, his ramp) rather than epigrammatic, and give Sulekk a dismissal whose words point clearly. Illustrative only: "She wants a cart count. Go give her one."

### D5 · Major · Authorization exchange closes on two flips

- Where: L307–L319, defense rail, Sulekk's terms and Qemri's acceptance
- Text:
> "Terms," Sulekk said. Her formal cadence was gone.
> "Against Brinnt's own warning?" asked the granary member.
> "Against the rock."
> "You are handing me both mistakes."
> "I am naming whose they are."
- Problem: The decisive commitment exchange runs as a procedure routine with two Punchline Patches. The granary member's question exists so Sulekk can flip "against" into "Against the rock". Qemri's fair, wry line gets a matched aphorism back instead of a reaction. The narration also contradicts itself: Sulekk opens with the formal word "Terms" while the narration says her formal cadence is gone, and the reader has never heard a formal cadence from her anyway. The conditions themselves are necessary, because the climax runs on them.
- Fix: Keep the four conditions, "Do you accept?", the palms and "Yes." Answer the granary member plainly, or have Sulekk ignore him and move on. Cut "I am naming whose they are." so that Qemri's line is met only by Sulekk's palm on the plate. Drop or rewrite "Her formal cadence was gone." Illustrative only: "Yes. If it crosses, she fires."

### D6 · Major · Aftermath closes on Sulekk's thesis line

- Where: L445–L453, after the impact, Oxaul reports the new command
- Text:
> "Chosen because of them?" she asked.
> He looked at the blank quarry mark. "I can't give you that."
> "Good. Keep the part you can."
- Problem: The first two lines are excellent: at the story's interpretive climax, Oxaul stays inside his evidence. Sulekk's reply turns that into a lesson. "Good" praises his discipline about evidence, and "Keep the part you can" is a neat inversion of his "give". This is the Philosopher as last word, stating what the whole trace-alcove thread has already shown.
- Fix: Keep the question and Oxaul's answer. Replace Sulekk's reply with a practical act or instruction that shows she accepts the limited claim, or give her no line at all. Illustrative only: "Copy both traces. The council will want them."

### D7 · Major · Final council scene states the terms twice and ends on rulings

- Where: L463–L489, map well after the impact, the three-pass trial
- Text:
> "Three passes," she said. "Paired basin beacons and one quarry beacon each pass.
> "Three passes cannot establish a stable practice."
> "They establish three passes," Sulekk said.
> Oxaul records changes, not intentions. Brinnt owns the bank.
- Problem: The last meaningful council exchange is a negotiation and procedure routine. Sulekk lays out the trial at L465, Qemri adds a three-clause firing rule (L469), Oxaul objects and gets a tautological last word, and then Sulekk lays out the trial again at L485. She says "three passes" three times in twenty lines. "Oxaul records changes, not intentions" states the story's theme as a clause. The tram and draft-team squabble in the middle (L475–L483) is the only stretch that sounds like a room of tired, dusty people with competing needs.
- Fix: Keep one statement of the trial: merge L465 and L485, keeping the conditions that matter (an unannounced descent ends it, Qemri keeps firing authority, Brinnt owns the bank). Cut "They establish three passes." and let Oxaul's objection either be run over by the tram argument or go unanswered. Give Oxaul a concrete job in place of the thesis clause, such as keeping the traces and logging every course change. Qemri's three-clause rule can stay as her one statement of doctrine. Keep the tram squabble and the steward's "I object." / "Recorded..." / "They're already moving." This finding should free roughly 40–60 words.

### D8 · Minor · Stray correction comebacks outside the decision scenes

- Where: L23–L27, council well opening; L263–L273, quarry runner
- Text:
> "The gallery stood after the strike," he said.
> "For eleven breaths."
> "They're not to hold it. They're to come back and tell us where it starts."
- Problem: Correction ping-pong as reflex. The road councillor's minimizing has no motive the reader can see, so it reads as a setup for Qemri's rebuttal. Sulekk's "not to X, they're to Y" is another of her last words. Neither line is wrong on its own, but both add to a rhythm that D1 and D4–D7 already make dominant.
- Fix: Give the councillor's first line a visible motive (he is already counting what the evacuation will cost his roads), or cut the pair and let Qemri's grit and the messenger carry the damage. Make Sulekk's instruction to the runner a plain order without the flip. Leave Brinnt's "It was wrong last meeting." (L53) as it is: it admits his own error and is in character.

### D9 · Minor · Small ground and attribution slips

- Where: L139, trace alcove; L199, beacon plan; L477–L483, map well tram argument
- Text:
> "At this coordinate." His claw circled the basin. "It was empty when their surface maps were made.
> Brinnt interrupted. "If the pulse reaches orbit."
> "Then find draft teams."
- Problem: (1) Oxaul, the story's guardian of evidence, states as fact when the human surface maps were made. What he can know is that the basin was empty until some point; the date of the maps is his inference, and his next sentence ("moderately confident") treats it as one. (2) "Brinnt interrupted" when no one is speaking, since the preceding exchange has just ended. (3) "Then find draft teams." and "Get them out." are untagged after the granary member's lines, so the reader has to guess whether Brinnt or Sulekk is giving the tram orders.
- Fix: (1) Frame the line as what the dinosaurs know (the basin was empty until so many seasons ago) so that the map date stays his inference. (2) Use a plain tag or a beat. (3) Tag the first reply once.

## Prose findings

### P1 · Major · Water infrastructure as default texture (policy)

- Where: throughout. The council office "water steward" (L23, L33, L59, L243, L275, L299, L325, L461, L487); the hatchery's warm-water trunk, ponds, tower, store and loop (L33, L173, L263, L281, L389, L461, L497, L509); the water stairs (L57, L73, L455)
- Text:
> The west loop cracked where it joins the warm-water trunk.
> The water steward wanted the apes' ranging bursts answered only after the warm-water trunk was repaired.
> Below it, the hatchery district leaked steam from the cracked water loop.
- Problem: The prompt does not ask for water infrastructure, and the CANON "No default water infrastructure" entry in universe/style-guide.md bars it, in authorized revisions, as default plot, stakes, occupation or incidental worldbuilding. Here it is material, not passing: it supplies a recurring speaking council office, a beacon site, a council demand and the closing image. It also creates a continuity blur. "West loop" reads as a pipe that "cracked where it joins the warm-water trunk" (L33) and also as a route, with six sledges on it (L175) and a broken cart axle lying there (L299).
- Fix: Do one consistent, word-neutral rename sweep. The steward's actual job is carts and counts, so rename her office to match (a cart or supply steward, for example). Change the hatchery's warm-water system to a heat system that uses no water (heat trunk, vent tower, warm-sand beds), and give the water stairs a plain stair name. Make the steward's L461 demand legible after the rename. Decide whether the west loop is a route or a heat line, and use it only that way. The quarry team's "water skin" (L269) is drinking water and can stay; steam from a heat line can stay if it does not imply plumbing.

### P2 · Minor · Triplet fragments and "not X. Y." cadence

- Where: L133, L249, L347, L351, L423, L497
- Text:
> Evacuation line. Fragment line. Firing line.
> Nothing in the strokes for eggs, burns, apes, dinosaurs, permission, or regret.
> Not safe. A distance.
> too high for faces, too near for neglect
- Problem: There are only a few instances, not a systemic habit, but they cluster at points of emphasis and give the narration the same polished cadence the dialogue edit is removing. L497 is a balanced closing emblem, and L133 ("Dead things could travel very far when a living hand let go.") lays an aphorism over a concrete image.
- Fix: Keep the content and break the pattern in two or three places. Fold the line triplet into Qemri's sentence, let the L347 list stop sooner, and replace the L497 antithesis with a concrete observation. At L423, change only the cadence and leave the decision logic alone.

### P3 · Minor · Isolated unclear phrasing and staging

- Where: L13, opening; L221, trace alcove; L431, impact
- Text:
> The thing was no larger than a sleeping egg
> Sulekk stepped between them, forcing her horns back from the instrument frame.
> Then the quarry struck.
- Problem: "Sleeping egg" gives neither a size nor a clear image. "Her horns" could belong to Sulekk or to Qemri, so the reader cannot tell who is forced back. "The quarry struck" makes the target the subject of the impact.
- Fix: Give the egg comparison a concrete meaning or drop "sleeping", say whose horns, and make the ballast the subject of the strike.
