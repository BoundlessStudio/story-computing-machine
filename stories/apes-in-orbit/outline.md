# Outline

## Story

- Premise and central promise: **The Apes Above**. Long after humans abandoned Earth for orbital stations, the Dino Council meets around fresh fallen ballast to stop the “pesky apes” throwing another rock into a hatchery district. The reversal stays comic and literal, but the council must distinguish attack from dangerous disposal and make one response work before the next descent.
- Focal pressure or attachment: Impact warden Qemri wants the next object broken in the sky. The last strike cracked a heat gallery; her crews are moving eggs while stone still settles. A communication attempt will consume charge needed for interception. She must choose which uncertainty the hatchery can survive, not learn generosity toward apes.
- Counterforce or complication: Observer Oxaul sees scheduled launches toward a basin once empty, not evidence of human intent. Engineer Brinnt can shatter the next object, but fragments would cross nesting terraces. Chair Sulekk must turn incompatible tolerances into a timed decision while members protect roads, water, and evacuation.
- POV, distance, and information limit: Close third past through Qemri, attentive to impact scars, load paths, foot-felt vibration, and shelter. She understands orbital traces only as Oxaul demonstrates them. No human viewpoint appears. She cannot know whether the stations recognize dinosaur intelligence or whether a changed trajectory means caution rather than recognition.
- Governing movement and time shape: One council session during the final ninety minutes before descent, moving from the low chamber’s impact table to its instruments and defense controls. Evidence changes evacuation, coil alignment, beacon placement, and the last firing option. End after a displaced impact and a three-orbit decision, during hatchery work rather than celebration.
- Speculative rule or ordinary-world constraint: Human stations mass-launch compacted shielding rubble retrograde for disposal and small orbital corrections. Each ablative “rock” has a transponder and cold-gas guidance; remote operators send range bursts and steering commands toward an obsolete empty-basin coordinate. Dinosaur instruments are passive except for a defense coil with one discharge. Three portable beacons can echo the range pattern from the occupied basin and an empty quarry, proving organized receivers and marking locations without translating language. The coil can amplify one reply or fully fragment one rock, not both. Every device is inert; a new command and altered course prove only that this trajectory received active control.

## Voice

- Narrative texture: Keep Qemri’s close narration weighty and quick: tremors, horn clearance, dust, instrument light, and orbital motion register as questions about load and shelter. Let administrative inconvenience carry dry humor without making dinosaurs a sketch.
- Conversational texture: Council talk overlaps work through interruptions, partial reports, requests for one usable number, and narrow worries about routes or clean water. Bodies must reposition at shared controls.
- Rhetorical ownership: Qemri uses scars, crews, and distance; Oxaul changing traces and confidence; Brinnt affordances and failure spread; Sulekk deadlines, conditions, and reversible authority. Minor members stay practical.
- Pressure behavior: Qemri shortens into commands. Oxaul overqualifies, then falters when no course change appears. Brinnt interrupts only to correct danger; Sulekk grows plainer as procedure runs out. Some members stop listening when their district is named.
- Anti-default: Avoid quippy parliament, polished position statements, anti-human allegory, or unanimous conversion by one scientist. Signal and rock must do work speakers cannot summarize away.

## Beats

1. A scorched fragment occupies the map well while grit drops from Qemri’s hide. A messenger reports the cracked hatchery loop. The next object arrives before evacuation can finish.
2. Qemri requests the coil. Brinnt rolls a fragment-spread model across the floor: destroying the center threatens three terraces. General outrage breaks into specific road, water, and shelter work.
3. At the instruments, Oxaul layers launch intervals, radio bursts, late corrections, and a recovered guidance fin. Together they indicate obsolete targeting, not harmlessness or apology.
4. Oxaul proposes answering the next burst. Brinnt realigns the coil as crews place beacons at settlement and quarry. Echo timing can show two deliberate sites but convey no identity, grammar, or consent.
5. Sulekk forces a conditional plan: clear the fragment fan, amplify one echo, and preserve a weaker shot if the rock crosses a final line. Qemri accepts firing authority. Dissenters secure doors and water instead of becoming a chorus.
6. The echo goes out; its packet returns, but the trajectory holds. Oxaul’s qualifications collapse. Near the firing line, a new steering burst shifts the object toward the quarry, and Qemri withholds the shot.
7. The quarry impact cracks windows, starts brushfire, and destroys the beacons. Its reduced shock confirms deliberate correction, not who chose it or what later launches will do.
8. The council authorizes three passes of paired beacons, guarded evacuation windows, observation, and a recharged coil; any unannounced descent ends the trial. Qemri returns to the hatchery and revises crew release and roof-bracing times.

## Continuity

- Canon used: One physical world persists through deep time; Earth is an era-bound name, not necessarily literal real-world Earth. Cultures may hold incomplete models. Technology here is nonmagical and nonliving.
- Boundaries and unknowns: Do not place this era relative to Ravel or the Glass-Sea era, name the stations or settlement, or connect their systems to established technology. Human station culture and dinosaur emergence remain local history. The altered course establishes neither mutual translation, machine sentience, human motive, nor durable peace.
