# Outline

## Story

- Premise and central promise: Many generations after humans abandoned Earth
  for orbital stations, sapient dinosaurs govern the recovering surface. When
  another human ballast stone breaks over nesting country, ankylosaur
  councillor Ketta brings an emergency motion to the Dino Council: use the
  surface's one ready launcher to cripple the lowest station before the
  `pesky apes` throw anything larger. The meeting must decide what the stones
  are, what risk the orbiting humans actually pose, and whether the dinosaurs
  should retaliate, defend, or answer. Resolve the promise with a concrete but
  conditional choice: the council spends its launch window throwing one
  instrumented rock back on a safe co-orbital path, offering a marked drop
  corridor over Quiet Caldera while funding ground shelters and retaining the
  means to intercept any future stone aimed elsewhere. The humans' response
  remains unknown.
- Intended reader experience: A dry, physical council comedy in which species
  anatomy, old extinction jokes, parliamentary evasions, and the indignity of
  orbital litter stay funny without making the impacts harmless. Let the
  reader's first assumption of deliberate bombardment become less secure, not
  simply wrong: the humans appear to be trying to miss settlements, yet their
  fragments still damage homes. The decision should feel shrewd, risky, and
  dinosaur-made rather than like a lecture about reconciliation.
- Generating force:
  - Mode: Plot-led deliberative compression. Each successive motion must name
    where mass goes next: into a station, into the atmosphere, into an empty
    caldera, or onto the ground the councillors are sworn to protect. Debate
    keeps turning rhetoric back into trajectories, material, and budgets until
    Ketta can no longer describe retaliation as making the danger disappear.
  - Focal attachment or contradiction: Ketta's district was struck, and she
    believes public office means putting a shell between danger and a nest.
    She is also the sponsor of the Upthrower's long-promised surface-survey
    launch; using that scarce window to contact the humans protects her people
    only by surrendering a project they can see and trust.
  - Plot-led wants and useful limit, if applicable: Ketta immediately wants to
    stop the falling stones before the Fern Basin hatching migration, pass a
    forceful motion before the session dissolves into species jokes, and keep
    the Upthrower slot assigned to her district's survey package. She also
    wants to avoid bringing an entire failing station down on Earth. Her useful
    limit is ledger-minded literalism: she refuses to credit good intentions
    that cannot be measured, which makes her an excellent critic of Pellik's
    hopeful inference but initially lets her count a destroyed station as a
    solved line item without counting where its mass would fall.
- Counterforce or complication:
  - Source: The lowest station is shedding stone ballast backward to slow its
    orbital decay; stopping the ejections without another solution may bring
    the much larger habitat down whole. The humans have not answered dinosaur
    radio or light signals. Their drops increasingly favor uninhabited terrain,
    but atmospheric breakup makes even a careful aim unreliable. The
    Upthrower has one seasonal launch window and insufficient energy both to
    send the survey package and to attempt contact.
  - Credible inside logic, if a person: Feathered skywatcher Pellik wants the
    contact shot because the descending orbit will soon remove every safer
    option, but admits trajectory patterns cannot prove humane intent or
    coordination among all the stations. Defense marshal Othrik wants the low
    station disabled because a near-miss is still falling ammunition and no
    surface population consented to serve as its dump. He does not convert
    after challenge: he votes against contact, then uses his office to make the
    shelters and interception readiness in Ketta's compromise real.
  - Pressure or change created: Every proposal protects one valuable thing by
    spending another. Waiting preserves the launch slot but increases impact
    frequency; attacking may turn debris into catastrophe; contact consumes a
    dinosaur project and may be mistaken for an attack; trusting the caldera
    corridor without defenses asks nests to bear the cost of an inference.
- Narrative design:
  - POV, tense, distance, and limitation: Close third-person past through
    Ketta, intimate with floor vibration, the weight of her tail club, impact
    accounting, and the political pain of spending a promised launch. Human
    intent is never entered or confirmed. Pellik's and Othrik's private stakes
    remain legible through what they emphasize, omit, and continue doing after
    they lose an argument.
  - Time shape and compression: One continuous council session during the
    ninety minutes before a predicted ballast stone enters the atmosphere,
    followed by a short same-evening launch coda. Give Ketta's opening motion,
    Pellik's orbital reconstruction, the stone's breakup over the chamber, and
    Ketta's replacement motion full dramatic space; compress lesser proposals
    through increasingly absurd amendments and the clerk's shrinking list of
    physically possible outcomes.
  - Information and reveal strategy: Establish human abandonment, visible
    stations, recent impact damage, and the failure of attempted signals in
    plain working context. Pellik then reveals the backward ejection pattern
    and decaying orbit, but not as infallible proof of motive. Let Ketta force
    the hopeful model to admit fragment risk, and Othrik force the punitive
    model to admit the mass of a falling station. During the meeting, the
    predicted stone's main body reaches Quiet Caldera while one fragment
    punches through the Stone Choir roof: a single event supports Pellik's
    claim that the apes are aiming away and Othrik's claim that aiming is not
    enough. No message arrives to settle who the humans are now.
  - Governing movement: Catch a pebble; accuse; propose a strike; reconstruct
    an orbit; count the missing mass; offer incompatible motions; hear the
    incoming stone; survive its divided evidence; price a reply; withdraw;
    replace; vote without unanimity; throw upward; resume counting.
- Speculative surplus: The Upthrower's induction rails temporarily magnetize
  the iron-rich osteoderms of some plated dinosaurs. Launch crews issue reed
  spacers, ban loose steel tools, and pry stray fasteners from Ketta's tail
  after the shot. This is a practical nuisance of local technology and biology,
  not evidence about human intent or a metaphorical reward for her choice.
- Structural distinction: A parliamentary farce tightened by orbital physics:
  motions, objections, and amendments successively collapse into one bounded
  allocation before the object under discussion physically interrupts the
  room. The required collective decision is split and provisional, not a
  technical spectacle that converts a crowd, exposes a villain, rewrites the
  institution, or certifies peace. The decisive venue is the council chamber;
  the brief launch coda shows cost and uncertainty rather than victory.
- Decisive turn, deepening, or recognition: The incoming stone divides. Its
  main mass strikes the empty caldera close to Pellik's projection, while a
  hot, insulation-flecked shard tears through the chamber roof and smashes
  Ketta's prepared strike tablet. She refuses both easy readings: the apes are
  neither proven attackers nor excused because they missed most dinosaurs.
  When Othrik demands that she restore the attack motion, she applies her own
  accounting to the station's full mass and recognizes that `knock it down`
  has only hidden the largest impact. She yields her survey launch and writes
  a narrower replacement: send an instrumented counterweight beside, not at,
  the low station, broadcasting the caldera's safe windows and a warning;
  prepare shelters and authorize interception until drops conform.
- Aftereffect or live uncertainty: The replacement passes by a narrow species
  roll call. Othrik records his dissent, then requisitions roof panels rather
  than sabotaging the decision. Fern Basin loses its survey launch and still
  has damaged nurseries to repair. The counterweight enters the intended
  co-orbit, but the council receives no immediate acknowledgment; the next
  scheduled ballast event remains on Pellik's board, and compliance by one
  station would say nothing certain about the others.
- Opening / ending relation: Open with Ketta catching a human pebble that has
  fallen through the Stone Choir gutter and setting it on her motion tablet as
  self-evident proof. End after the dinosaurs have thrown that recovered mass
  upward in their own deliberate trajectory: Ketta returns to the chamber with
  launch washers stuck harmlessly along her magnetized tail, asks the clerk for
  the next impact estimate, and begins the ordinary roof-repair allocation.
  Direction reverses, but uncertainty and maintenance remain.

## Beats

1. Ketta lays a Fern Basin impact fragment before the Dino Council and moves
   to use the Upthrower against the lowest human station. The long-neck chair's
   attempt to define `against` opens a comic flood of ducking, armor, evacuation,
   and extinction-prevention amendments.
2. Pellik interrupts the rhetoric with the observed sequence: each rock leaves
   the low station backward, each ejection briefly raises its orbit, and recent
   main masses trend toward Quiet Caldera. Ketta extracts every uncertainty;
   Othrik stresses the fragments and the humans' silence.
3. The council considers waiting, shooting the station, intercepting every
   stone, or sending material up. The clerk reduces each plan to mass, energy,
   destination, and which promised surface work it displaces. Ketta discovers
   that only her survey launch is ready in time.
4. In a recess that cannot pause the orbital clock, Pellik admits the humans
   may be indifferent rather than considerate. Othrik admits destroying the
   station could create the impact he fears, but argues that dinosaur-controlled
   timing may still be safer. Neither becomes a mouthpiece for the answer.
5. The predicted stone arrives while debate continues. The chamber feels the
   caldera strike through its foundations; seconds later a fragment pierces
   the roof. Delegates shelter according to body rather than dignity, then
   emerge to evidence that makes both principal interpretations incomplete.
6. Ketta finds her attack tablet broken by the fragment and recalculates the
   motion from destination backward. She gives up Fern Basin's launch slot and
   drafts the conditional counterthrow, paired with shelters, interception
   readiness, and a review after the next two drops.
7. The motion passes narrowly. Othrik votes no and immediately begins arguing
   for thicker shelter slabs; Pellik must encode the corridor without claiming
   it is a universal language or proof of friendship. Ketta faces her district's
   justified anger over the lost survey package.
8. At the Upthrower, the recovered ballast and passive beacon launch on a path
   beside the station. Nothing answers before the launch team demagnetizes its
   tools. Back in the damaged chamber, Ketta deals with stuck washers, the next
   forecast, and the roof budget while the experiment remains live.

## Continuity

- Canon used: The deep-time setting allows many civilizations and recurrent
  collapse on one coherent physical world. `Earth` is an era-bound name for
  that world and does not require literal real-world Earth. Cultures may have
  radically incomplete knowledge, and technology need not share an origin or
  connection with any existing system.
- Boundaries and unknowns: Treat the human abandonment, sapient dinosaur
  societies, the Dino Council, orbital stations, ballast practice, named
  surface places, and Upthrower as new story-local facts. Leave this era's
  position relative to the modern-like, Ravel, Glass-Sea, Crown, and other
  established eras unresolved. Do not connect these stations to Ruth's
  afterlife station, the Glass-Sea solar network, any crossing system, or the
  unnamed station in another non-canon story. The prompt establishes multiple
  human stations but not that they contain every human, share one government,
  or share one motive; focus inference on the single low station. Keep orbital
  operation credible at story scale—backward ballast ejection can buy altitude,
  atmospheric breakup remains uncertain, and destroying a habitat does not
  erase its mass. Preserve human and dinosaur personhood even while the council
  uses the comic, irritated label `apes`.
