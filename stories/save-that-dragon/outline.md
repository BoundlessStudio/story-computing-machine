# Outline

## Story

- Proposed reader-facing title: *One Night's Mercy*.
- Premise and central promise: Months ago, the articulate dragon Dovra freely drew a lethal surge of fire out of adult Princess Neris and agreed to remain through one night. Queen Ivara then ordered disgraced dragon physician Ordran's emergency heat-harness locked around Dovra's throat; Neris, terrified of burning again, has refused to let it be removed. The harness now forces Dovra to drink every flare before Neris feels it, vitrifying the dragon's scales and making the next transfer likely to kill it. Ivara secretly recalls Ordran and opens on her knees with the prompt's exact plea. Resolve the rescue when Ordran gets all three people to stop disguising compulsion as an unfinished emergency: Dovra states that its original yes expired, Neris releases the harness despite the real pain and danger that return to her, Ivara admits and bears her share of the confinement, and Dovra reaches the cold night air alive and free to leave without owing the royal family a return.
- Intended reader experience: Begin with the startling reversal of a sovereign begging a dismissed servant to save a dragon from a princess. Move through an eerily comfortable captivity—warm rooms, beautiful glass by-products, courtly talk that keeps avoiding the word *forced*—into a close, bodily ordeal in which every available choice costs someone. Let readers feel for Neris without allowing her fear to erase Dovra's personhood, and finish with relief that is sharp, incomplete, and private rather than triumphant.
- Generating force:
  - Mode: Plot-led extraction that repeatedly fails until “save” expands from removing Dovra to ending a three-way habit of making the dragon carry the family's danger, guilt, and decisions.
  - Focal attachment or contradiction: Dovra still cares whether Neris survives and therefore will not accept an escape plan that simply abandons her; Neris loves the dragon that saved her but has converted gratitude and terror into entitlement to its body; Ivara condemns her daughter's refusal while hiding that the queen made the temporary harness compulsory. Ordran wants a clean rescue because clean blame would spare him from facing what his own device enabled.
  - Plot-led wants and useful limit, if applicable: Ordran immediately wants to examine Dovra, cut off the harness, and get it airborne before its chest scales turn fully to glass. He also wants Neris kept alive, Dovra allowed to choose the risk it will still accept, and Ivara made to own the order that began the captivity. His useful flaw is punitive certainty: guilt makes him treat truth as a blade and rescue as simple reversal, so he initially speaks over Dovra's continuing concern for Neris. Neris wants immediate protection from her next fire surge and Dovra close enough that she need not feel the warning pain; she also wants Dovra to love her willingly and knows the harness has made that impossible. Her limit is fear-trained control: she calls each delayed release prudence and keeps moving the definition of “safe.”
- Counterforce or complication:
  - Source: No simple villain. Neris's fire is a genuine bodily danger, the harness automatically diverts each surge once it begins, Dovra is near a fatal heat saturation, and anger or forced entry can provoke exactly the flare everyone fears. Ivara's evasion and Ordran's haste make the physical problem worse. Dovra itself refuses a secret removal unless someone tells Neris what will happen and gives her a survivable alternative.
  - Credible inside logic, if a person: Neris remembers waking without skin blistered because Dovra volunteered for the first transfer; since then she has protected her life, nearby attendants, and the stability of a court that has seen what her uncontrolled fire can do. She is neither mistaken about the danger nor entitled to indefinite rescue. When challenged she argues, bargains, and attempts one last command, then retains the agency to release Dovra and endure the result. Ivara fears both her daughter's death and public panic, but her decisive responsibility is personal: she must confess that she locked Ordran's temporary buckle after he refused to make it permanent, then remain beside Neris when the danger is no longer displaced.
  - Pressure or change created: Cutting the harness during a transfer could send the accumulated heat back through both bodies; waiting for another calm interval will likely leave Dovra unable to fly. The only workable window requires Neris to feel an oncoming surge, choose release before it peaks, and cross the short distance to a disused tiled bath chamber while Dovra is still free to depart. Ordran can reduce harm but cannot guarantee that choice will be painless or safe.
- Narrative design:
  - POV, tense, distance, and limitation: Close third past through Ordran. His clinical attention makes changes in Dovra's breath, gait, scale clarity, and Neris's temperature legible, but his resentment lets him misread Dovra's refusal to flee as submission and Ivara's kneeling as sufficient remorse. Keep the queen, princess, and dragon exterior until each states what they want. Ordran is dry and diagnostic; Ivara uses royal plurals and contingency language when ashamed; Neris turns fear into possession and future conditions; Dovra speaks slowly, distinguishing what it offered, what it endured, and what it chooses now.
  - Time shape and compression: One continuous predawn interval of roughly two hours. Open with Ivara's plea in Ordran's shuttered workroom, cross the sleeping palace through service passages, spend the central movement in Neris's overheated apartments, and run the release in real time through the adjoining old baths to first light. Fold the original rescue and harness order into three brief, conflicting accounts before Ordran names the shared factual sequence.
  - Information and reveal strategy: Establish immediately that Neris is an adult, Dovra is a speaking person, and the danger is ongoing. Let Ivara first imply that her daughter alone imprisoned Dovra; reveal the queen's original order when Dovra calls the buckle “your mother's mercy” and Ordran recognizes his altered clasp. Reveal that Dovra could have broken furniture or injured attendants but could not remove the locked harness without triggering it; its lack of violence is not consent. Delay Dovra's continuing wish to help Neris until Ordran offers a secret escape, complicating his clean victim/rescuer picture without weakening the expired agreement. Keep the origin and wider taxonomy of Neris's fire and Dovra's heat-taking unresolved.
  - Governing movement: Hear Ivara's kneeling plea; refuse absolution but accept the medical emergency; cross kitchens where servants cool harmless glass bulbs made by Dovra's overloaded breath; enter Neris's rooms without guards; see apparent luxury and the dragon's failing translucent scales; examine the locked harness; hear Neris call it temporary and Dovra specify how many temporary mornings have passed; offer a quiet cut and learn Dovra will not disappear without warning Neris; force Ivara's confession of the original order; let Neris bargain for one more week and then one more transfer; have rising emotion start that transfer early; jam rather than sever the clasp, buying minutes while returning warning pain to Neris; move all four into the wet, stone-lined baths; make Ordran discard his attempt to decide for Dovra and ask each participant separately; let Dovra refuse further drinking, Neris release the royal lock, and Ivara take the practical supporting role she had displaced; open the high vent and cut the now-inert harness; get Dovra into cold air while Ordran and Ivara keep Neris conscious through the crest; stop when Dovra has flown beyond recall and Neris has survived the first unborrowed ebb.
- Speculative surplus: When carrying too much heat, Dovra involuntarily fuses cooking sand and airborne grit into hollow glass bulbs with each cough. Palace kitchens cool drinks in the durable ones; misshapen bulbs roll unpredictably, sing at different pitches as they cool, and make hot rooms hazardous underfoot. The effect gives Dovra's condition material and domestic consequence but cannot reveal truth, control Neris's fire, or solve the release.
- Structural distinction: A four-person private extraction whose route contracts from workroom to bedchamber to one tiled cooling room even as responsibility broadens. Its decisive action is the withdrawal of an old permission before witnesses, followed by difficult care rather than combat. There is no public proof, crowd conversion, institutional rewrite, species-wide reconciliation, remote expert, or ceremonial reward; unlike the recent threshold, containment, recurrence, romantic-approach, and road-loop designs, this story moves once toward an open vent and does not bring the freed being back to certify the humans' change.
- Decisive turn, deepening, or recognition: With the jammed harness clicking toward automatic transfer, Ordran prepares to cut it and tells Dovra when to brace. Dovra asks why the physician is still giving orders over its body. Ordran stops, states the risks without recommendation, and asks what Dovra wants. Dovra refuses another transfer but asks whether Ordran can keep Neris alive. He can promise effort, not safety. Neris then asks Dovra for one final rescue; Dovra says no. After trying to convert “no” into “not yet,” Neris feels the fire rising and unlocks the clasp herself. Ivara does not countermand her; she receives the released key and goes with her daughter to the baths while Dovra chooses the window.
- Aftereffect or live uncertainty: Dovra reaches the unnamed cold marsh beyond the palace and survives, but its glassed wing edges may take a season to shed and it makes no promise to return. Neris lives through the first surge with burns no worse than Ordran judged survivable, yet future attacks remain dangerous and will require treatment that does not depend on an unwilling person. Ivara must face her daughter's anger and her own concealed order; Neris must live with having prolonged the confinement. Ordran agrees to attend the next cycle only under Neris's explicit consent and without rebuilding the harness. The story does not settle criminal, dynastic, or public consequences.
- Opening / ending relation: Open with Ivara kneeling as though humility can hand Ordran sole responsibility for fixing her family. End with Ivara still on the bath floor for a practical reason—bracing Neris through the ebb—while Ordran watches Dovra vanish toward the marsh and deliberately does not call it back. The repeated posture changes from performed surrender to sustained care; Dovra's absence, not a return gesture, completes the rescue.

## Beats

1. In Ordran's workroom, Ivara delivers the exact prompt line on her knees. Her partial account establishes Dovra's rapid decline and Neris's refusal while withholding the queen's role. Ordran notices old burn damage on Ivara's gloves, realizes another surge is near, and agrees only if she comes without guards.
2. Their service-passage approach shows the arrangement's normalization: kitchens use Dovra's accidental glass bulbs, attendants have learned to pad trays against heat, and everyone calls the sealed apartments a convalescence. Ivara keeps proposing euphemisms; Ordran keeps asking who holds the harness key.
3. In Neris's rooms, comfort and captivity coexist. Neris is alert, adult, and genuinely affectionate; Dovra is articulate, weak, and physically tethered only by the heat-harness, whose forced transfer makes escape lethal. Ordran's examination sets the deadline: one more full surge may vitrify Dovra's chest and wing joints.
4. Ordran offers to cut the harness during a quiet interval. Neris forbids it. Dovra also refuses the secret plan—not because it consents to remain, but because leaving Neris uninformed would reproduce the same disregard. Its precise account exposes that one volunteered night became a locked season.
5. Dovra's description of the clasp forces Ivara to admit she ordered Ordran's temporary safety catch converted into a royal lock. Neris uses that shared culpability to argue that no one has standing to judge her; Ordran's harsh answer spikes her heat and triggers the harness.
6. Ordran jams the transfer midway, giving Dovra relief but returning pain and flame to Neris. With glass bulbs cracking underfoot and only minutes before the harness resets, the group makes the short, difficult passage to the disused baths. Ivara abandons orders for physical work: water, soaked cloth, cleared footing, and support.
7. At the bath vent, Ordran starts directing another procedure and Dovra stops him. He gives bounded risks and asks rather than commands. Dovra refuses further heat. Neris begs it for one last exception, hears an unqualified no, then unlocks the clasp before the surge can decide for everyone.
8. Ordran removes the inert harness; Dovra labors through the high opening and flies toward cold marsh air. Neris's unborrowed fire crests in the tiled room. Ordran cools without promising a cure, and Ivara stays in bodily reach despite Neris's rage.
9. At first light, Neris survives the ebb and accepts only one limited next visit from Ordran. Dovra is a diminishing shape rather than a returning reassurance. Ordran does not summon it, and Ivara remains beside the daughter whose danger the dragon no longer has to carry.

Existing dragon authority establishes no universal species taxonomy and is not otherwise imported; no recurring person, place, faction, artifact, or system is used.
- Boundaries and unknowns: Ivara's unnamed kingdom, palace, baths, and cold marsh are new local setting material and remain unnamed.
