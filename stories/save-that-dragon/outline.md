# Outline

## Story

- Premise and central promise: Open with Queen Urrava kneeling in Imer Quast's forge before guards and Hovan Setch, begging: `Please, save that dragon from my daughter.` Princess Daziel has caught Thelrax in a three-anchor quarry snare and will drive a killing spear at first light. In one moonless night, Imer must reach the rig, keep dragon and humans alive, and force a real choice about the hunt.
- Focal pressure or attachment: Imer built the quarry winches and taught Daziel trapping before Urrava dismissed him for opposing a sealed nest raid. He rejects further royal service but cannot leave a creature inside machinery derived from his work. Urrava's witnessed kneeling and surrender of the original raid writ damage royal invulnerability and create evidence against her; neither act cleanses her concealment.
- Counterforce or complication: Thelrax killed Daziel's husband during that raid and later burned an occupied supply shelter. Daziel has evacuated the quarry and waits for first light, when cross-ravine wind drops enough for a clean killing thrust; the throat wound may not last longer. Learning Urrava authorized the raid cannot erase those losses. A storm-blocked road means no royal order can arrive in time; only Imer knows the inspection chute and safe release sequence.
- POV, distance, and information limit: Close third past through Imer, intimate with load paths, workmanship, and court evasions. Urrava's full motive, Daziel's choice, and Thelrax's intent remain external. Hovan distinguishes observed posture from inference; narration does likewise.
- Governing movement and time shape: A descending one-night pursuit: costly petition, cramped approach, contested inspection, release work under worsening strain, brief dawn consequence. Keep the decisive turn among five principals at the abandoned quarry, without public proof or institutional settlement.
- Speculative rule or ordinary-world constraint: Thelrax cannot speak human language. Hovan knows only immediate signals for pain, space, danger, wait, and approach; other movement stays ambiguous. A pierced throat permits at most two short eight-pace fire bursts, and weighted wings require a clear downslope launch. The ordinary rope, iron, springs, and counterweights kill the dragon if released out of order.

## Voice

- Narrative texture: Move through weight, angle, abrasion, breath, and handwork; let Imer notice status through who touches tools, carries risk, or waits for an answer. Moral judgment follows conduct rather than labels.
- Conversational texture: Mix rank-marked negotiation with blunt route checks, rope counts, injury questions, corrections, unfinished replies, and necessary silence. Mechanical failures and misread movement interrupt talk.
- Rhetorical ownership: Urrava reasons through bargains and selective admission; Daziel through witnessed sequence and preventable risk; Imer through load paths and concrete alternatives; Hovan through bounded observation and explicit uncertainty.
- Pressure behavior: Urrava's balanced clauses contract into imperatives; Daziel stops defending herself and issues work calls; Imer interrupts abstraction with tasks; Hovan grows slower and less willing to translate. Thelrax's refusals remain bodily.
- Anti-default: Do not let everyone become equally polished advocates for mercy, turn the quarry into a quote-card moral debate, use a quip chorus, or make the nonverbal dragon automatically innocent or omniscient.

## Beats

1. Urrava gives the required opening, sealed raid writ, and access token, admitting only what Imer forces open. He tests route, deadline, and mechanism rather than accepting kneeling as proof. Hovan confirms Thelrax is bleeding and joins him; Urrava remains with the witnesses she created.
2. Imer and Hovan descend the inspection chute through rain and loose stone. Talk stays on lamp oil, knots, footing, and breath range. They find Daziel has coupled the spear to the lower brake, invalidating Imer's remembered release order.
3. Daziel presents evidence of the death and shelter fire. The writ changes what she knows about Urrava, not what Thelrax did. She refuses to stand down, so Imer enters the loaded frame while Daziel keeps the firing lever.
4. Hovan refuses to counterfeit a submission signal. Thelrax refuses the practiced wait signal and loads the downslope side. Imer follows the changed strain rather than inventing intent, discovering that cutting the upper net first fires the spear. Cable creep, a splitting frame leg, and one defensive fire burst shrink the workspace.
5. Imer pins the flywheel while Hovan clears the launch path. Daziel can stabilize the break by driving the spear through Thelrax or dump spear and counterweight into the empty shaft, releasing her only certain hold with Imer in reach. She chooses the dump. Imer frees the anchors; Thelrax takes the open downslope rather than treatment or attack.
6. At dawn Thelrax clears the ridge alive but wounded. Daziel ends this hunt and takes Urrava's writ back by the long road, without forgiving dragon or mother. Imer and Hovan treat their injuries, account for the quarry crew, and disable an accidental second firing.
