# Opus review: One Night's Mercy

Independent audit for a later editing pass. It is not a `review.md` verdict,
does not change canon state, and does not authorize edits by itself.

## Verdict

- Dialogue: REVISE — the decisive dilemma turns on an unrecoverable "both", the writ revelation is muddled over who authorized the weapons, and Imer and Daziel share one clipped correction-and-comeback register through most of the quarry.
- Prose: PASS — concrete and controlled; only minor slips in rig vocabulary, a few narration logic nits, and one drainage reference.
- Continuity and prompt: REVISE — the coda re-arms a spear mount that fell down the shaft and calls a never-fired carriage's next shot a "second" firing; Hovan's role is never established. Prompt line, length and resolution are delivered.
- Edit scope: heavy-dialogue
- Existing review.md said: Verdict PASS, Dialogue: PASS; disagree — it missed the coda contradiction, the climax referent and the house-voice convergence.

Much of the talk is right for a rigging rescue: short calls, counts, confirmations, and Hovan's carefully bounded observations are the best voice in the story. The failure is that the same register governs everything else. A queen on her knees, a widow who has just learned her mother let her dead husband carry the blame, and a smith lying under a loaded drum all speak in composed, clipped declaratives, correct each other's wording ("Of a sealed nest." / "Of the copper behind it."; "That is not a measure."), and close exchanges on a neat last word. Daziel's grief reaches the page only as a case file and a three-part ruling, and the climax is conducted as crew status reports around a line the reader cannot parse. The edit pass should keep the work calls and the object-carried history, strip the comeback caps and narration glosses, and let Daziel (and, once, Urrava) speak from inside the pressure rather than above it.

## Edit guardrails

- Urrava's first words stay verbatim and on her knees, as the prompt requires: "Please, save that dragon from my daughter." Active craft profile is prospective-2026-08-21 (the last `Craft profile:` line). Length band 2,500–4,000 words (now about 3,160). Broadly accessible. Close third, past tense, through Imer.
- Story-local facts the plot rests on: Urrava signed the command to open the sealed nest and let the court blame Daziel's husband; he broke the seal and Thelrax killed him; twelve days later Thelrax burned the occupied east shelter; Imer was dismissed for opposing the raid; Hovan's vocabulary is pain, space, danger, wait and approach, with no submission signal; the throat wound allows at most two short bursts of about eight paces, and one is used.
- Resolution to keep: Daziel chooses the dump pin over firing at risk to Imer; anchors come free in the order left, right, upper; Thelrax escapes wounded and unconverted; Daziel ends the hunt without forgiving dragon or mother and takes the writ back by the long road.
- No universe dependencies. Thelrax is story-local; do not connect him to Ersene alliance law, Nyrzath, Kharost or any canon dragon, and do not state species-wide dragon biology.
- What works and must not be flattened: Hovan's voice; the work calls in the release sequence; the rig as the moral mechanism; the teacher-student history carried through objects (the rabbit snare, the wrong crease, "Foot back"). The fix is mostly subtraction of comebacks and glosses plus a real voice for Daziel's anger, not added speeches or explanation.

## Dialogue findings

### D1 · Major · Who authorized the weapons is left contradictory

- Where: L51–L55, forge (Urrava's admission) and L195–L199, quarry (Daziel reads the writ)
- Text:
> I authorized an armed survey.
> She told me he added the weapons himself.
> He did add them. The inventory proves it. But she sent him to break the seal.
- Problem: Ground/knowledge at the key revelation. Urrava tells Imer she authorized an armed survey, so the weapons were royal policy. Daziel then reports that Urrava's story was that her husband added the weapons himself and confirms it as true ("The inventory proves it"). No one registers the conflict, and the writ as described at L65 ("open, inspect, and secure") mentions no arms. The reader cannot tell what exactly the queen concealed, which blurs the one fact the writ exists to deliver.
- Fix: Make the weapons fact single-valued. Smallest edit: drop "armed" from Urrava's admission at L51, so the writ's revelation is purely that she ordered the seal opened and Daziel's concession about the weapons stays true. Alternatively keep "armed survey" and rewrite L195–L199 so Daziel realises the weapons were ordered too. Either way "Of a sealed nest." must still be the point that lands.

### D2 · Blocking · The decisive dilemma hinges on an unrecoverable "both"

- Where: L325–L351, climax, Imer pinned at the flywheel, Daziel holding the stay and the lever
- Text:
> "It won't hold both."
> He knew. The whole changed load had come into his arms
> "Firing now braces the leg," Daziel said. A work call. No defense left in it.
> He had no useful answer. "Pin needs both hands. Twist left before you pull."
- Problem: Ground/referent at the story's decisive beat. "It" is presumably the stay rope, but "both" names nothing on the page: Daziel holds the stay and the lever, the stay holds the upright, Imer holds the flywheel, and the narration that follows lists five loads, not two. The reader has to skip the line and rebuild the dilemma from L331. "The leg" is new here; the part has been "the left upright" and "the split upright". The exchange then runs as status reports ("It does." / "You cannot get out." / "Not while the wheel turns.") repeating what L331 already said, narration glosses Daziel's line, and "He had no useful answer" is followed at once by a very useful answer.
- Fix: Have Daziel say plainly what is failing; call the part the upright (or plant "leg" earlier). Cut "You cannot get out." / "Not while the wheel turns." or make that the one place her fear for him surfaces. Keep the substance of "Firing now braces the leg" and Imer's honest "It does."; his refusal to lie to steer her is the beat. Cut "A work call. No defense left in it." Replace "He had no useful answer" with something true (he has no answer to what she is really asking, only the mechanics). Keep both bare "Imer." lines and "Pin needs both hands. Twist left before you pull." Illustrative only: "The stay's slipping. The upright's going."

### D3 · Major · Daziel's grief arrives as a case file and a ruling

- Where: L175–L181, her account of the nest and shelter; L191–L203, reading the writ
- Text:
> My husband entered the nest with six people. He broke the inner seal. The dragon killed him before the second person crossed.
> That changes her part. It does not change his neck in that nest. It does not change the shelter roof.
- Problem: Uniform fluency under pressure, then The Philosopher. Daziel recites her husband's death with counts and intervals to a man who answers "I know what he did", so the recital is there for the reader (Exposition). Seconds after learning her mother ordered the raid and let the court blame her dead husband, she delivers a balanced three-part judgment that is the outline's thesis almost word for word ("The writ changes what she knows about Urrava, not what Thelrax did"). The one personal line in the sequence, "She told me he added the weapons himself.", is the right register; everything around it is a verdict from the bench, and her mother's lie gets no reaction beyond "That changes her part."
- Fix: Keep "She signed this before he left." and "She told me he added the weapons himself." Cut the anaphoric "It does not change…" ruling; let the anger go at her mother (or at Imer for bringing it now) and let her refusal to stand down stand without explaining itself. Trim the L175 recital to what she wants from Imer (for him to admit the dragon is a killer) and lose the briefing precision. Illustrative only: "She let me tell people it was his pride."

### D4 · Major · Imer and Daziel trade the same clipped register

- Where: L199–L235, stand-down argument; also L243–L247 and L263–L267
- Text:
> Stand down until I inspect it.
> The crosswind falls. I get one clean thrust before his throat closes or he tears it wider.
> Your crew?
> I can fire before it parts.
- Problem: Identical Voices / Tennis Match. From L199 to L231 the two alternate some fifteen short, mostly untagged lines of equal weight ("Then stand clear." / "Stand down…" / "No." / "At dawn?" / "The crosswind falls…" / "Your crew?" / "Evacuated…") with no interruption, no one losing ground, and no difference in vocabulary, length or directness. Cover the tags and they swap freely: both refuse with a bare "No." (L201, L207, L225, and Hovan at L171), both command with "Again." (L103, L267). Daziel, six wet hours at a lever over her husband's killer, is exactly as level as the engineer. The exchange does move (she lets him into the frame), but it reads as two officers comparing notes.
- Fix: Keep the information (dawn rationale, crew count, rubbing line) but break the alternation: let Daziel dominate or cut him off, let Imer wait out a refusal instead of matching it, let one of them lose the thread. Give Daziel contractions and something unprofessional (sarcasm at her old teacher, fatigue, a jab at her mother for sending him). Stop making "No." everyone's word. Keep "I've held it since dusk." and "Evacuated. Nine at the shepherd sheds, two at the bridge watch. I counted them out."

### D5 · Major · Forge: Imer explains Daziel to her mother, then trades corrections

- Where: L41–L61, forge, after Urrava offers terms
- Text:
> The truth she doesn't have.
> That is why she can grieve him and still call his death a consequence of his own pride.
> Of a sealed nest.
> Of the copper behind it.
- Problem: Three problems in one exchange. (1) "The truth she doesn't have." is a riddle-demand decoded only by Imer's next speech (Ground: meaning recoverable only from what follows); Urrava's uptake, a glance at Hovan, implies he shares a secret the story never gives him (see C2). (2) Imer then tells the queen what her own daughter believes and why she can grieve that way: As-you-know plus Philosopher, since Urrava authored that belief. (3) "I authorized an armed survey." / "Of a sealed nest." / "Of the copper behind it." / "The writ." is correction ping-pong in fragments, the collection's house rhythm, from a kneeling queen.
- Fix: Make Imer's demand plain and cut the explanation of Daziel's grief. Keep "You would not be kneeling here if that were true" as his one deduction, ideally anchored in what he can see (she knelt in front of witnesses; she brought the packet). Let Urrava resist in her own register (a counter-offer, a qualified clause) instead of completing his sentence. Keep "The paper, or get out of my forge." and "Give it to him." Illustrative only: "Show me what you signed."

### D6 · Major · Setup-for-comeback is the default way exchanges end

- Where: L81–L85, forge; L103–L107 and L113–L125, chute; L137–L143, arrival; L241–L249, under the drum
- Text:
> She has heard you all her life. Tonight she needs to see the paper before she sees another royal face.
> The road wasn't trying to peel you.
> Usually I'm right.
> She sent something more useful.
> It will be when I find where it goes.
- Problem: Punchline Patch / epigram last word. Exchange after exchange hands Imer a plain line to cap: "It held on the road." exists to be answered by the road line; "You usually dislike it." tees up "Usually I'm right."; "How much?" / "Enough." / "That is not a measure." exists to reach "It will be when I find where it goes" (whose "it" is itself unclear). "What Daziel does after that is hers." closes the forge scene on the story's ethic as a maxim. The pattern makes Imer an aphorist while he climbs a wet chute or lies under a loaded drum, and it is exactly the collection-wide tic the owner is complaining about.
- Fix: Cut most caps and let exchanges end on the task, a beat, or silence. In the chute keep the relationship information in "You asked me to say when I know." and let Imer take the correction without topping it. Under the drum, answer "How much?" with a real measure or an honest "I don't know yet" and drop "That is not a measure." At arrival one dry line from Imer is enough, not two. At the forge keep "Bring her back." and let Imer answer about the rig, not with a maxim about Daziel's autonomy.

### D7 · Major · Narration explains lines right after they are spoken

- Where: L75, L173, L221, L269, L309, L335
- Text:
> That was as near as Urrava came to saying she had chosen it.
> The word did not challenge her. It ended the request.
> The certainty in her voice was not court certainty. It belonged to six wet hours with a lever under her hand.
> because refusal once was not refusal forever
- Problem: Subtext gloss, usually in the "not X. It was Y." shape. Hovan's "No." and Daziel's "I've held it since dusk." already carry their meaning; the glosses tell the reader how to take them and lay a second, writerly voice over the dialogue. At the climax (L335, "A work call. No defense left in it.") the gloss does the emotional work Daziel's line should do. L309 ("not in submission, not in anything Hovan could name") is the mildest, a callback, and may survive on its own.
- Fix: Delete the glosses at L173, L221, L269 and L335; the lines stand alone. At L75 cut or reduce to a physical beat. If a line then seems unclear, fix the line, not the narration.

### D8 · Minor · Clipped lines with unclear referents

- Where: L105, chute; L209, stand-down; L259, wing cradle rises; L271, after the aborted fire; L369, anchor release
- Text:
> It held on the road.
> At dawn?
> He knows what that means.
> "No more," Hovan said.
> Right now.
- Problem: Ground/referent. L105: "it" could be the lamp knot or the anchor knot, and neither was tied on the road. L209: Imer already knows the dawn deadline (L21), so "At dawn?" is decoded as "why dawn?" only by Daziel's answer. L259: "that" (the pawl click? the open road?) resolves only through Hovan's reply. L271: "No more" could mean no more signals, no more pushing, or no more fire. L369: "Right now." reads first as "immediately" rather than "the right anchor now", costing a beat mid-action.
- Fix: One or two words each: name the knot or drop Hovan's objection; "Why dawn?"; let Daziel point at the pawl or the road; attach a beat to "No more" showing whom Hovan is refusing; "Now the right."

### D9 · Minor · The submission demand has no visible aim, and Hovan answers with a manual

- Where: L161–L167, arrival at the rig
- Text:
> Make him put his head down.
> I can show pain, space, danger, wait, and approach.
- Problem: Daziel wants the dragon's head lowered, but the spear is aimed between his shoulders and she means to kill him at dawn; the reader cannot tell why she wants submission now. Hovan's reply is a five-item inventory she must already know from working with him (Exposition). The refusal that follows ("Make one." / "No.") is good and should stay.
- Fix: Give her demand a visible reason in the moment (for instance, she wants the head down before Imer walks under the gantry). Replace the inventory with Hovan's plain limit, which L167 already states; the signals are named in narration as he uses them.

### D10 · Minor · "Those knots have never been wet" contradicts the staging

- Where: L89, forge, choosing rope
- Text:
> And the thin hemp, not that court rope. Those knots have never been wet.
- Problem: The guards are "wet to the shoulders" and carried the court rope through the storm, so the literal claim is false; the intended meaning (never tested under load in the wet) has to be supplied by the reader.
- Fix: Name what is actually wrong with the court rope (new, stiff, untested, knotted by someone who has never climbed on it). Keep the swap for the blackened line.

### D11 · Minor · Final exchange restates what the action already showed

- Where: L415–L425, dawn
- Text:
> I haven't forgiven him.
> There was nothing useful Imer could add.
- Problem: Mild Philosopher. "I'm ending the hunt… If he comes at another occupied roof, I'll answer that." already makes clear that mercy is not forgiveness; "I haven't forgiven him." / "I know." restates it in stock form. "There was nothing useful Imer could add" repeats the L351 template. "She can read this with me." is the right closing line.
- Fix: Cut "I haven't forgiven him." / "I know." or replace it with something she would actually say about the dragon or her mother. Vary or cut the "nothing useful" narration.

## Prose findings

### P1 · Minor · Rig parts renamed or first introduced at the climax

- Where: L133, L307, L321, L331, L333, L335, L347, L427
- Text:
> Imer shoved his pry bar through the flywheel.
> Hovan dropped behind the stone wheel.
> dragon, wet net, wing stone, spear spring
- Problem: The spear's timber mount is a "stock" (L133), a "carriage" (L333) and a "bed" (L347); its drive is a counterweight (L133) but also a "spear spring" (L331) and a "cocked spring" (L427); the upright becomes a "leg" (L335); a flywheel and a stone wheel appear for the first time at the moments they matter. At the climax the reader loses which part is which.
- Fix: One name per part. Plant the flywheel (and whatever the stone wheel is) in the L129 rig survey. Decide whether the spear is spring- or counterweight-driven and keep it consistent; this also resolves part of C1.

### P2 · Minor · Stray narration logic slips

- Where: L19, L33, L51, L149, L351
- Text:
> Careful, even now. Nothing metal to cut her when she knelt.
> Urrava rose no higher.
> Anger steadied Imer's hands.
- Problem: Rings would not cut a kneeling woman, so the logic of her carefulness fails. L33 ("The chute Imer had cut through the hill when…") is a verbless fragment that reads as a broken sentence. "Urrava rose no higher" implies she had partly risen; she is still on both knees. "Anger steadied Imer's hands" labels the emotion. L351's "He had no useful answer" contradicts the answer that follows (handled in D2).
- Fix: Small wording repairs; do not expand.

### P3 · Minor · Water-infrastructure texture

- Where: L301, Hovan clearing the road
- Text:
> rolled a dropped maul into the drainage cut
- Problem: Policy nit under "No default water infrastructure": a quarry drainage cut is the passing drainage texture the style guide asks authorized revisions to avoid, and the prompt does not call for it.
- Fix: Roll the maul off the track, onto a spoil heap, or over the shelf edge.

## Continuity and prompt findings

### C1 · Blocking · The coda re-arms a spear mount that fell down the shaft

- Where: L357–L361, the dump; L427–L433, dawn, disabling the frame
- Text:
> The spear bed tipped into the shaft. Timber, iron spear, and counterweight fell together.
> The spear was gone, but the empty stock still held a cocked spring
> One turn of the drum could fire the carriage a second time into whoever stood at the rail.
- Problem: Continuity contradiction. At L359 the timber mount, spear and counterweight all fall into the shaft; at L427 the "empty stock" is still in the frame with a cocked spring, and the "carriage" could fire "a second time" although it never fired a first time (L353: "The trigger arm jumped but did not fall"). The story's final action depends on this hazard, so the contradiction sits in the last paragraph.
- Fix: Decide what physically remains after the dump (for example the trigger arm and its spring, or the carriage rail) and name only that; replace "a second time" with what is true (it could still snap into the rail). Say why Hovan pours oil there or drop the oil. Keep the three-person action and the last line, "On the third blow, the pin bent flat."

### C2 · Major · Hovan's role, allegiance and signal knowledge are never established

- Where: L13, forge introduction; L23, L45, L91, L119, L165, L235, L303
- Text:
> Hovan Setch stood with them, rain running off his cropped hair.
> Urrava's gaze shifted once, toward Hovan. He did not help her.
> You told me a lowered head meant submission.
> Then he gave the broad retreating motion Thelrax had accepted before.
- Problem: A principal the reader must reconstruct. Hovan arrives with the queen's guards, has seen Thelrax's wound at the quarry, knows where Imer keeps his rope and has a history of correcting him, has advised Daziel on this dragon, and has a signal vocabulary Thelrax "had accepted before" (when?). The queen looks to him for help; Daziel wants him watched. Nothing says whose man he is, why he came with the queen, or how he knows this dragon. His careful knowledge stance is the story's model of evidence, but the page never supports it.
- Fix: One clause at L13 and one line in the forge or chute: what he is (for instance Imer's former hand now on Daziel's hunt crew as its dragon-watcher) and that he left the quarry to fetch the queen. That also explains the glance at L45 and "Hovan stays where I can see him."

### C3 · Minor · The shelter victims' fate is unstated

- Where: L175, Daziel's account; L415, her condition for ending the hunt
- Text:
> burned the roof while eight workers were inside. They had not gone near the nest.
- Problem: Whether the eight workers died sets the weight of Daziel's grievance and of the rescue, and she would say it. "If he comes at another occupied roof" implies harm without stating it.
- Fix: One clause in her account stating the outcome.
