# Outline

## Story

- Proposed reader-facing title: *The Mercy Circuit*.
- Premise and central promise: At closing time, an unnamed independent repairer accepts a rush job from Veska Doss: her bionic left hand has locked around an aging plasma blaster, and both must pass checkout before her dawn freight-escort shift. Veska asks for the blaster's troublesome “mercy circuit” to be bypassed. Working backward through weapon, hand, nerve cuff, and reflex implant, the repairer discovers that the safety is detecting a timing mismatch between an early fire packet from Veska's recently accelerated implant and the motor signal from her living nerves. Resolve the repair-shop promise privately: the repairer restores Veska's hand for ordinary use and isolates its weapon link; Veska leaves the blaster behind and accepts that neither the implant nor her employment problem can be fixed overnight.
- Intended reader experience: A tactile after-hours repair puzzle with dry counter-service humor and tightening unease as the apparently defective gun proves to be the component refusing a dangerous shortcut. Make the cramped kiosk competent and ordinary in a world where artificial joints, implant ports, and energy weapons all acquire lint, wear, and bad prior repairs. Release comes through small restored hand movements, not a spectacular firing test or a speech about technology.
- Generating force:
  - Mode: Plot-led diagnostic unbraiding. Each test isolates one layer of a fused person-hand-implant-weapon chain and changes the answer to “what is broken”: blaster, hand, interface timing, then the assumption that repair must restore every old connection.
  - Focal attachment or contradiction: The repairer built an independent livelihood on returning control of costly equipment to its owner, yet uses the requested specification as a shield against asking what reconnection will do. Veska needs the hand and weapon that earn her living, but also needs her present account of when she chose not to fire to matter more than a latency patch designed to move first.
  - Plot-led wants and useful limit, if applicable: The repairer wants to free Veska's hand, finish a profitable weapons rush, and put the fee toward an overdue quarterly stall bond; the competing want is to keep a regular customer's body usable without making the weapon obedient to a disputed command. Their useful flaw is “bench neutrality”: treating a narrow work order as separation from consequence, so they prepare the bypass after evidence warrants another test. Veska wants the dawn shift and hazard premium, while also wanting to know why the blaster fired into a freight-gallery ceiling after she decided to lower it. She compresses fear into fault codes and initially omits her recent speed patch because it may cost her clearance.
- Counterforce or complication:
  - Source: Four compatible systems have aged differently. The hand freezes its firing grip after contradictory commands; the charged plasma cell must be bled before mechanical release; the aftermarket reflex patch predicts familiar motions early; and the mercy circuit is a simple two-channel coincidence gate with one safe inhibit cycle left. Full implant recalibration or removal requires clinical work outside the kiosk.
  - Credible inside logic, if a person: There is no human antagonist. Veska chose the patch after slower reaction scores threatened her routes, and it worked during drills; wanting the income and trusting a familiar augmentation are reasonable. The repairer has sound reasons to honor a lawful rush request. Veska retains the decision after diagnosis: bypass, arm-only repair, or stop once the weapon is safe.
  - Pressure or change created: Bypass is the only route to weapon checkout by dawn, but would let the implant energize the blaster before Veska's current motor signal arrives. Isolation restores the hand but costs the shift; refusal leaves a disabled hand. The stall bond makes the lucrative option attractive without creating a public countdown.
- Narrative design:
  - POV, tense, distance, and limitation: Close first-person past through the unnamed repairer. The voice is exact about connectors, heat, grime, warranty language, and stressed hands, but hides in job-ticket categories. The narrator measures signal order, not intention or thought; Veska's statement remains primary, with timing as bounded support rather than an objective personhood test. Veska speaks in clipped shift times, tolerances, and pass/fail terms.
  - Time shape and compression: One night from half-lowered shutter and last transit departure to the first morning queue. Render weapon safing, release, diagnostic reversals, the dummy-load test, the options conversation, and mundane hand checkout. Compress cooling and rebuilding through small bench jobs, machine-vended broth, the silent concourse, and morning cleaning carts. Wear, old service seals, and Veska's evasions carry the past without flashbacks.
  - Information and reveal strategy: Establish the kiosk's scope through work already present—a bionic fingertip awaiting tactile mesh, an implant reader under a dust cover, a scorched weapon cradle. Let reader and repairer first blame the mercy circuit because its fault light is visible; each isolation test removes a convenient explanation. Veska reveals the patch after the hand responds without the weapon. Explain late and briefly that the circuit compares physical inputs and knows nothing of morality, targets, truth, or souls. No sentient gun, erased crime, hidden victim, or universal consent detector.
  - Governing movement: Close; accept fused rush job; safe and bleed; open the hand; test weapon, actuators, and cuff separately; learn of the patch; watch it predict a harmless reach; build the requested bypass; pause for one dummy-load test; see the implant issue fire while Veska's open hand does not; let the mercy trace interrupt itself; state choices; isolate the weapon bus; rebuild local hand control; test ordinary functions; reopen on an incomplete but chosen repair.
- Speculative surplus: Bleeding a plasma cell makes loose ferritic screws warm and creep toward the grounding braid in clicking steps, so the repairer uses heavy ceramic parts bowls. The nuisance cannot diagnose the mismatch, indicate intention, or solve the job.
- Structural distinction: A stationary bench story that moves inward by separation and subtraction. Its sharpest event is a low-energy test pulse that never reaches the dummy load. It avoids pursuit, repeated exposure, travel, crowd conversion, institutional rewrite, public technical proof, and symbolic destruction of the weapon.
- Decisive turn, deepening, or recognition: With the bypass jumper finished and the profitable repair one connection away, the repairer spends the mercy circuit's last safe inhibit cycle. Veska pictures drawing while keeping her palm visibly open. The implant issues fire; her nerve cuff does not send matching initiation; the mercy trace opens and dies rather than pass the packet. This cannot prove every past feeling, but supports Veska's clear account that she chose to lower the weapon. The repairer stops treating a missing connection as the defect and offers three honest scopes. Veska chooses arm-only restoration and later clinical care, losing the shift; the repairer removes the weapon-rush charge, losing the easy answer to the stall bond.
- Aftereffect or live uncertainty: Veska regains grip, release, temperature warning, and fine control, but her weapon bus remains capped and the implant remains in her body. She still needs a clinic and may lose the route. The safely discharged blaster stays in the kiosk under an ordinary claim ticket, neither destroyed nor absolved. The repairer remains short on the stall bond.
- Opening / ending relation: Open with the repairer lowering the shutter while sorting jobs into those returnable whole and those awaiting one connection, confident that repair means restoration. End with Veska using her hand to fasten her cuff, count the returned deposit, and lift a hot cup while the blaster stays disconnected. The rising shutter admits the next customer as the repairer records two accurate lines: hand returned; weapon diagnosis pending.

## Beats

1. Near closing, the repairer clears a cramped counter of a bionic fingertip, implant reader, and weapon cradle. An overdue stall-bond reminder beneath the till establishes the cost of independence and the narrator's appetite for closed tickets.
2. Veska Doss arrives with her bionic hand locked around a scorched plasma blaster. No one was hurt by the ceiling discharge, but she needs checkout before a dawn escort shift and pays a deposit for an explicit mercy-circuit bypass.
3. The repairer grounds the emitter and bleeds its cell; loose screws creep toward the braid. Mechanical release frees the weapon, but Veska's fingers remain weak and mis-timed.
4. Separate tests clear the plasma chamber and hand actuators. Tracing contradictory command order to the nerve cuff prompts Veska to disclose the two-week-old reflex-speed patch and insist that she had chosen to lower the weapon.
5. With the blaster disconnected, the implant predicts Veska's harmless reach for a cup just before her living motor signal. The hand controller reconciles it; the weapon's tighter coincidence window could not. Clinical calibration is beyond the kiosk.
6. Wanting the fee and refuge in Veska's specification, the repairer fabricates the bypass. Veska asks only whether it will pass the shift check; both keep the dangerous option alive by speaking narrowly.
7. Before reconnection, the repairer runs the final dummy-load test. Veska imagines drawing while holding her palm open; the implant issues fire, her nerve channel stays quiet, and the sacrificial trace burns open.
8. The repairer gives the bounded finding and three choices without deciding for Veska. She chooses an arm-only repair, sends a terse shift withdrawal, and accepts uncertain employment; the repairer returns the weapon-rush portion of her deposit.
9. Through the quiet night, the repairer caps the weapon link and rebuilds local hand control while Veska dozes in the customer chair. No implant surgery or weapon firing occurs.
10. Morning checkout uses coins, a cuff fastener, a hot cup, and a dropped washer. Veska leaves with a usable hand and claim ticket for the stored blaster. The narrator raises the shutter and records the partial repair plainly.

## Continuity

- Canon used: The `LOCKED` system-categories rule permits distinct local technologies without making them a universal cosmology. The `LOCKED` personhood protection is observed by treating Veska's testimony, consent, livelihood, and present choice as irreducible to signals.
- Boundaries and unknowns: Veska Doss is new and unconnected to established people; the transit concourse, kiosk, employer, and locality remain unnamed. All bionics, implant, weapon, interface, and service mechanics are story-local and imply no universal standard, law, intent detector, or link to existing systems. The nonliving mercy circuit compares only timing; it cannot read minds, prove consent, determine truth, or act morally. The kiosk isolates interfaces and rebuilds the bionic controller but performs no implant surgery. Era, geography, licensing, employment consequences, and later treatment remain unresolved.
