# Opening trials

## 1

When people ask what I want from the Box, I say the power to get a piano down a flight of stairs without ever having to hear my sister tell me she's got it. Bess said it once on a set of steps in a house where nobody had thought to put a light switch at the bottom. She had a finger caught between the piano and the banister. Her idea was that if I let the piano back an inch before she asked me to, she would have to put up with me saying I told you so, on top of the pain in her finger. She held on till she was ready.

Bess is waiting with me at North Sill. She's got her coat done up to the chin and every time the line rounds a corner she pushes a little farther ahead to see the other end. At the gate they gave us three numbered slips, for Garth and me and her. You can go to the Box for free. You can even ask the woman at the gate for a second slip if the first one gets wet, and for a minute while she gets that out of a drawer you can stand where it's warm.

A man in front of us is trying not to stare. He's seen me get the three slips out and put them away twice already. I was looking for the one with Garth's name on it, to remember what order we're going in.

“Which of you is going?” he says.

“All three.” It comes out readily, like the price when you've already seen the stairs.

Garth gives the man a little wave. He reaches into the breast pocket of his coat and holds out his van key.

“You'll want to leave that side door alone till you get back into town,” he says. “It sticks going uphill and I've parked facing the fence.”

I put out my hand. I can feel Bess turning to look, though all I can see is his palm and the pale marks on it from hanging on to the cold rail.

## 2

There were fourteen steps at Mr. Auden's house. Dena had carried an upright up all fourteen the winter he moved there. At the turn she'd had to wait for him to take the ferns off the landing, and he'd gone round thanking each person in turn as if he'd been given the piano instead of buying it.

This time he said there were five steps and asked if she'd come down on the price.

She put in a hundred and eighty and took it out again. Behind her the queue moved, and Bess caught her at the elbow before she walked into a stanchion. One of the roof lights in the old maintenance hangar at North Sill let in a thread of rain. It broke on the concrete beside her boot and ran along the yellow stripe where they'd once towed planes in. At the end of the stripe stood the Box. There was something so ordinary about its height that you could imagine the person who'd have to tilt his head to get inside. Garth might have to.

“Dena. That woman over by the guard.”

The woman was pinching at the cuff of her coat. It was brown with a print of green leaves, and where she pinched, the leaf came up between her fingers with a stem on it. She let go. It stayed upright. Bess got an arm through the rail to point, and Dena pulled her back by the belt. The guard had already told somebody twice to keep their arms on this side.

“If I get that,” Bess said, “I'd like it to work on the drawing of an orange, so I'd have an orange to eat while I tried the drawing of a horse.”

Dena got as far as thinking where a horse would fit, with the shoe rack out of the hall, before the phone buzzed. Mr. Auden had another price. The other mover had told him he could send the piano straight from the room it was in to the room where he wanted it. Mr. Auden was just giving Dena first refusal.
