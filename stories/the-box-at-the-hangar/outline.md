# The Way Back Down

## Promised experience

“Free opportunity” and “50/50” make the same door available to everyone and equally unforgiving once it closes. The later direction gives that door a physical address. There is only one Box, in a controlled site with room for a queue. A person who survives gets a random power. The user asks for “choice, the pressure to pick, and the everyday consequences,” with a rating that takes death seriously. I read that as the suspense of waiting among other people who have opinions about your gamble, then the shock of seeing a plain working day rearranged by the result. The queue gives the choice a public scale. No particular relationship, power, viewpoint, or verdict on entering is specified. Mature is the working intensity target.

## Takes considered

- **The movers.** Two sisters take their places in line as their livelihood contracts around them. Their fellow driver goes ahead of them. The central scene asks whether the sisters can still make separate choices once they have seen what happens to him.
- **The public demonstration.** A woman who survived years ago is paid to show prospective applicants what she can do. During a demonstration at the hangar, a relative comes to ask her to put the relative's whole name on the appointment list. The climactic decision belongs to the relative, with the demonstrator's living as the pressure she brings to it.
- **The last shuttle.** A coach driver takes a company crew to the Box on a paid day off and waits to take back whoever comes out. The driver has a place in the queue too, and has to decide where to stand when her passengers rally around her. The return trip changes a whole crew's working life.

The movers give the threshold scene two people with something different to lose from the same arrangement. They can both have the last word over their own body, and each decision changes the next day's work.

## Provisional direction

Dena and Bess move upright pianos with their friend Garth, who owns the van. A mover who can send an object across town without a truck has taken their regular dealer's work. Bess wants some of that astonishing freedom for herself. Dena has treated the three free appointments as an investment they can make together and has told herself that if somebody comes out with a power, the rest of them will be all right.

Garth volunteers first and burns. At the queue rail Dena asks Bess to leave with her. Bess wants Dena waiting for her but asks her to take the companion route out of the line. Dena abandons her own turn in front of the people she has been telling they are all going in. Bess goes ahead and survives. She discovers she can halt a moving object by looking at it and keep it still until she looks elsewhere. Garth's result gives no information about hers. The sisters have seen enough different powers at work to know she could have come out with something wholly different.

A week later, a new job offer is trying to make Bess's eyes everybody's safety net. On their first piano job since Garth's death, they learn what the power can do that hands alone could never manage, and what happens to a partnership when it becomes possible for one person to go elsewhere. Bess takes a paid trial for her new skill and helps finish the job she and Dena took together. Dena has to decide whether to quote their next customer for one worker or two.

## Craft plan

**Teller.** Dena can estimate the weight of an instrument from how a customer describes its stairs. At the hangar she applies the same confidence to things it can't tell her. Try immediate close third, hungry for measures and forces, and retrospective first person from several weeks later, with Dena testifying to the appeal of a choice she backed out of. Let her linger where her first impressions fail her, especially when Garth is taken through the service gate.

**Cast and speech.** Dena wants enough work to live on and wants Bess to think she has always been able to provide it. She knows their lost hours and believes a shared risk buys a shared future. She talks in sums and examples and corrects herself mid-course. Under pressure she'll give a whole account to prove a case. Bess wants a chance at something she has never been able to imagine doing. She knows Dena took the appointments out of genuine need and mistakes that for Dena's wanting the chance as fiercely as she does. Bess thinks aloud by proposing pictures, sometimes ridiculous ones. Her directness comes out when somebody makes a plan on her behalf. Garth wants his own reason to go understood before he does. He's tired of giving jobs out and then coming home with an empty book, and is embarrassed by how badly he wants to be somebody a stranger would cross the street to watch. He knows he's going first and thinks making light of the running order will make the sisters less afraid. He talks in stories with particular people and leaves others room to correct the way he tells them. No reference characters.

**Conversation owed.** At the hangar rail, with Garth gone, Dena has to ask Bess to come out with her, and Bess has to say why she still wants in and what she wants Dena to do if she goes. The sisters need enough room to argue over both the return trip and what they thought they'd promised one another. Dena wants an answer about who will be coming home with her. Bess wants Dena waiting on the far side of the rail of her own accord.

**Place.** Choose the closed maintenance hangar at North Sill Airport over the fenced sheds at Vicker Freight Depot and the old customs hall at Lamprey Ferry. The airport has room for one long line under cover, a separate apron-side service exit, and a hard physical division where an applicant can leave the queue and cross to the companions. Next door, tugs and wing walkers still bring planes into the working bays, and the old tow stripe survives on the concrete beneath the queue. It's late November, from sleet-dark afternoon into airport night. A row of clear roof panels goes green late in the day. Cold climbs up through boot soles, wet wool steams near the heaters, there's a rubber smell by the shut aircraft doors, and the approaching tug is a vibration before it is a sound. A leak over the purpose-built stanchions forces everyone into a bend where they can see who is moving forward. Dena can watch the Box and can't cross the open floor after Garth. On the last page, put the sisters on a narrow landing over Orchard Music, where both can reach the same instrument while standing at different heights.

## Scene briefs

1. **The queue, late afternoon, about 20 percent.** The three see an airport worker arrive at the service door with a case that sails behind her without wheels touching ground. The lost dealer and the contractor's power to move cargo become real through a job text Dena tries to finish accepting in the queue. Garth wants Dena to take his van key and call his sister herself if she has to. Dena thinks he is joking and tries to joke it back into his hand. Bess wants Dena to leave the phone in her pocket long enough to look at the far end with her. Garth's story about trading a perfectly good radio for his first van gives him a life apart from the next event. The key feels warm through Dena's pocket.
2. **The rail and Box, dusk, long scene, about 50 percent.** Staff check willingness one at a time at the threshold. Garth goes in under his own power. A voice, a flare at the closed door, and the crew taking his body through a gate Dena can't follow give the death its time and particularity without graphic description. While the floor is cleared, Dena asks Bess to leave and Bess explains her refusal. A place in the queue can be surrendered on the spot. The room pushes them to act, with staff reopening the gate and the people behind them making room to pass. Dena thinks Bess could still follow her, takes the gap anyway, and waits in the companion lane. After Bess's turn, a dropped paper cup arrests halfway to the floor. Give Bess fear, delighted experiment, and grief time to coexist. Dena learns that she can be grateful and furious at the same sight.
3. **Orchard Music, a week later, about 30 percent.** Their first job with a hired van takes an upright down a tight stair. Garth's sister has asked Dena to bring the key back because she has to sell his van. Dena wants Bess to guarantee the next two drives. Bess has a paid trial holding cargo for a freight company, who wants her on call immediately, and she wants Dena to help work out what hours she will actually sell. The talk has to continue around the physical work. When the piano moves too fast off a step, Bess brings it to a standstill above the tread. She can't look away until Dena has braced it, so the freight call goes to voicemail. Bess still means to take work with them on her own terms. Dena will send the next quote only after they have discussed whose hours she is offering.

Keep the Box's origin and the whole labor market outside the action. Carry the interval from the hangar to the music shop with the calls made after Garth's death and Bess's first practice at home.

## Ending

On the last step Dena has the instrument braced and Bess asks whether she can let go. Dena gets her hands into position and gives a real answer. Bess releases the hold, the weight comes down through the wood, and the two of them lower it the rest of the way. Earlier Dena assumed she could take Garth's key as a joke, tried to sign Bess up for a future in the queue, and watched Bess stop the paper cup. The upright gives the new power a working scale, and means some of their coming work really will be done together and some of it won't.
