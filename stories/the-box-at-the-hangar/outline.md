# The Way Back Down

## Promised experience

“Free opportunity” and “50/50” make the same door available to everyone and equally unforgiving once it closes. The later direction gives that door a physical address. There is only one Box, in a controlled site with room for a queue. A person who survives gets a random power. The user asks for “choice, the pressure to pick, and the everyday consequences,” with a rating that takes death seriously. I read that as the suspense of waiting among other people who have opinions about your gamble, then the shock of seeing a plain working day rearranged by the result. The queue gives the choice a public scale. No particular relationship, power, viewpoint, or verdict on entering is specified. Mature is the working intensity target.

## Takes considered

- **The movers.** Two sisters take their places in line as their livelihood contracts around them. Their fellow driver goes ahead of them. The central scene asks whether the sisters can still make separate choices once they have seen what happens to him.
- **The public demonstration.** A woman who survived years ago is paid to show prospective applicants what she can do. During a demonstration at the hangar, a relative comes to ask her to put the relative's whole name on the appointment list. The climactic decision belongs to the relative, with the demonstrator's living as the pressure she brings to it.
- **The last shuttle.** A coach driver takes a company crew to the Box on a paid day off and waits to take back whoever comes out. The driver has a place in the queue too, and has to decide where to stand when her passengers rally around her. The return trip changes a whole crew's working life.

The movers give the threshold scene two people with something different to lose from the same arrangement. They can both have the last word over their own body, and each decision changes the next day's work.

## Provisional direction

Dena and Bess move upright pianos with their friend Garth, who owns the van. A mover who can send an object across town without a truck has taken their regular dealer's work. Dena persuaded the other two to book alongside her. She treats the free appointments as an investment they can make together. Bess has been to see a room she wants to rent on her own, big enough to play an upright after work. She has seen powers earn people a regular wage and watched people do marvelous things that earn them nothing. She wants both a way to pay for that room and the chance to do something she would show Dena for the delight of it.

Garth volunteers first and burns. At the rail Bess initially takes hold of Dena's arm to go with her. Dena starts planning how the two of them will keep taking jobs with a rented van. Bess hears how much of her future Dena still expects to be able to give away. The sisters argue about whether either of them came for a chance of her own. Dena admits she wanted their work back and would sooner take a week's wages in her hands than risk seeing her own body go where Garth's went. Dena gives up her place while Bess can still come with her. After Dena has crossed, Bess brings up the kitchen window Garth imagined flying past to show his sister. Bess looks at the service door where he was taken out. She asks Dena to wait whether Bess comes out with a little, unmarketable wonder or the crew has to bring her out the way they brought Garth. Bess goes ahead and survives. She discovers she can halt a moving object by looking at it and keep it still until she looks elsewhere. The odds at each closing are even regardless of what went before. Anyone can come to the one Box for free, and Dena can return for a later turn.

A week later, a new job offer is trying to make Bess's eyes everybody's safety net. On their first piano job since Garth's death, they learn what the power can do that hands alone could never manage, and what happens to a partnership when it becomes possible for one person to go elsewhere. Bess takes a paid trial for her new skill and helps finish the job she and Dena took together. Dena has to decide whether to quote their next customer for one worker or two.

## Craft plan

**Teller.** Dena can estimate the weight of an instrument from how a customer describes its stairs. At the hangar she applies the same confidence to things it can't tell her. Leading choice is immediate close third, hungry for measures and forces. Try that against first person during the wait, which can let Dena make a more openly persuasive case to herself while keeping both outcomes in the future. Linger where her first impressions fail her, especially when Garth is taken through the service gate.

**Cast and speech.** Dena wants enough work to live on and wants Bess to think she has always been able to provide it. She knows their lost hours and believes a shared risk buys a shared future. She talks in sums and examples and corrects herself mid-course. Under pressure she'll give a whole account to prove a case. Bess knows the rent for the room she wants to move into, and hasn't told Dena which door she measured last week. She mistakes Dena's willingness to book alongside her for the same appetite for a gift. Bess thinks aloud by proposing pictures, sometimes ridiculous ones. Her directness comes out when somebody makes a plan on her behalf. Garth wants his own reason to go understood before he does. He's tired of giving jobs out and then coming home with an empty book, and is embarrassed by how badly he wants to be somebody a stranger would cross the street to watch. He knows he's going first and thinks making light of the running order will make the sisters less afraid. He talks in stories with particular people and leaves others room to correct the way he tells them. No reference characters.

**Conversation owed.** At the hangar rail, with Garth gone, Dena has to ask Bess to come out with her, and Bess has to say why she still wants in and what she wants Dena to do if she goes. The sisters need enough room to argue over both the return trip and what they thought they'd promised one another. Dena wants an answer about who will be coming home with her. Bess wants Dena waiting on the far side of the rail of her own accord.

**Place.** Choose the retired maintenance hangar at North Sill Airport over the fenced sheds at Vicker Freight Depot and the old customs hall at Lamprey Ferry. It has room for a double bend of queue under cover, a guarded apron-side service exit, and a rail dividing applicants from companions. The Box sits alone at the end of the old painted tow stripe. Its iron-colored upright walls come directly out of the floor, and the door has a raised lip people mind step over. Next door, tugs and wing walkers still bring planes into the working bays. It's late November, from sleet-dark afternoon into airport night. Clear roof panels go green late in the day. Cold climbs through boot soles, wet wool steams at the heaters, a stale rubber smell hangs by the aircraft doors, and the approaching tug is a vibration before it is a sound. A leak forces the line into a bend where everyone can see who takes the narrow way across the open floor. A short break while attendants move the stretcher out and clear the air gives the sisters their time to talk. On the last page, put them on a narrow landing over Orchard Music, where both can reach the same instrument while standing at different heights.

## Scene briefs

1. **The queue, late afternoon, about 20 percent.** Three dozen people wait for the single Box. A survivor makes a print of green leaves come alive on her coat, and Bess wants Dena to put down her phone and look. The lost dealer and the rival's ability to move cargo become real through a job text Dena tries to finish accepting on the spot. A man with his sister behind the rail asks which of the trio is going. Dena answers for all of them. Bess tells Dena there is a room she's considering, withholding that she has already asked when she can move. Garth wants Dena to take his van key and call his sister herself if she has to. Garth tells them about trading a perfectly good radio for his first van, and what he'd do if he got flight. His sister's kitchen is on the fifth floor and he'd go there to show her first. The key feels warm through Dena's pocket.
2. **The rail and Box, dusk, long scene, about 50 percent.** Staff check willingness one by one and wait for Garth's word to close the door. Spell out the equal odds before any result. A white line appears under the door before Dena hears Garth trying to get a word out. Then comes the smell of scorched wool, and the distinctive knock of a stretcher wheel over the old door track on the crew's way out. Dena holds Garth's key and has nowhere to go with it. While the gate is shut the sisters start to leave together and stop to have it out about the work, the room and what they wanted to risk their lives for. When the floor reopens, Dena's number is called. She steps across to the companion lane, giving up today's place in sight of the man who heard her speak for all three. Before using her separate turn, Bess remembers aloud what Garth hoped his sister would see. She looks at the track the stretcher crossed, and asks Dena to wait even if the crew has to take her through the same door. Dena watches Bess give her own word for the door. At the exit after Bess survives, a dropped paper cup arrests halfway to the floor. End the scene there and let the sight carry both elation and the immediate absence of the friend who could have seen it.
3. **Orchard Music, a week later, about 30 percent.** Their first job with a hired van takes an upright down a tight stair. Dena has had to tell Garth's sister what happened and return the key so she can sell the van. Dena wants Bess to guarantee the next two drives. Bess has a paid trial holding cargo for a freight company and wants Dena's help working out what hours to sell. Money for the room starts looking like another way to fill up her time for somebody else. When the piano moves too fast off a step, Bess brings it to a standstill above the tread. She can't look away until Dena has braced it, and the freight call goes to voicemail. Bess still means to take the trial. Dena will quote for the next move after Bess gives her the actual hours she has free.

Keep the Box's origin and the whole labor market outside the action. Carry the interval from the hangar to the music shop with the calls made after Garth's death and Bess's first practice at home.

## Ending

On the last step Bess asks if she can let go. Dena puts a block where she can bear the weight and calls to lower it onto the block, then asks Bess to come down and take the other handle. Bess releases the hold. The new weight comes down through the wood and both sisters are there for it. Earlier Dena took the key she had no way to get out to Garth, spoke for Bess in the queue, and watched Bess stop the paper cup. The upright gives the gift the scale the cup promised, and lets the sisters discover what this work will now take from each of them.
