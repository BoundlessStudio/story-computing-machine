# Opus review: Nobody Spends Her Name

Independent audit for a later editing pass. It is not a `review.md` verdict,
does not change canon state, and does not authorize edits by itself.

## Verdict

- Dialogue: REVISE — the traitor reveal, the rival call and the pool sit-down each turn on a line the listener or reader can't ground, and the narration keeps explaining lines right after they are spoken.
- Prose: PASS — hard, concrete noir with a few isolated misfires and a small tic of pointing at speeches nobody gives.
- Continuity and prompt: PASS — delivers the braided A/B structure, the hard-R tone and the length; the kidnapped son's timeline and a few staging details each need a clause.
- Edit scope: targeted-dialogue
- Existing review.md said: Verdict PASS, "Dialogue: PASS"; disagree — it missed Ground failures at the reveal, the entrance exchange and the evidence line.

The dialogue here is better than the collection's usual convergence. The ward captain talks in padded euphemism, the underboss in a paternal "we" with "sweetheart", the crew in doors, cars and wounds, and Elspeth's fatigue shows when she asks the same question twice. The problems sit at the load-bearing lines. The traitor reveal rests on "the old man", but the only old man the reader has met is "the older rival"; the lieutenant answers a phone call nothing shows he can hear; the driver objects to an entrance nobody proposed; and when Elspeth finally puts the evidence in front of the rival, she says the orders "came out of my room" instead of saying they were forged. Around those lines the narration keeps explaining what a line just meant, and the captain never answers Elspeth's ultimatum because a set-up and a zinger take his turn. The edit pass should make each of those lines say the plain, grounded thing and cut the glosses. It should leave the clipped rhythm, the two villain registers and the fatigue beats alone.

Do not drift toward a lesson about sleep, delegation or management. Length band is 2,500–4,000 words and the story is about 3,600, so fixes should replace lines rather than add beats. ## Dialogue findings

### D1 · Major · Traitor reveal hangs on "the old man"

- Where: L57–L77, the first-car phone call in Elspeth's room; also L317 on the pool deck.
- Text:
> "By him?"
> But the old man said your voice doesn't count if you call direct.
> He told all the floor leads that yesterday. Called it a continuity rule.
> The old man's attention was on Elspeth's crew
- Problem: Ground/referent at the story's key reveal. Up to L73 the only old man on the page is "the older rival", so "the old man said your voice doesn't count" first reads as the rival, which makes no sense. The underboss is not mentioned at all until the narration at L77 decodes the line. The lieutenant then replies with "He" and responds to words spoken into Elspeth's phone, and nothing shows he could hear them (Ground/knowledge). "By him?" has no referent for the man on the phone, and no look or gesture tells the reader she means the lieutenant. At L317 the narration again calls the underboss "the old man" while the rival stands on the same deck.
- Fix: Have the gunman name his source by the title the crew uses, or have Elspeth ask back (it fits her fatigue) and the lieutenant answer with the title. Put the call on speaker, or have Elspeth repeat the key phrase aloud, so the lieutenant's reply is grounded. Add a glance at the lieutenant before "By him?". At L317 write "the underboss". Keep "Called it a continuity rule." Illustrative only: "Which old man?" / "The underboss. He told all the floor leads yesterday."

### D2 · Major · The driver objects to an entrance nobody chose

- Where: L111–L125, in the car, Elspeth's call to the older rival.
- Text:
> Bring your daughter to the old athletic club. Basement pool.
> South entrance gives him the gallery.
> East service door
> You used that door last time.
- Problem: Ground/uptake. Elspeth names a building and a room, not a door, but the driver rejects a "south entrance" that no one proposed; the reader has to invent Elspeth's default plan to make the reply follow. "East service door" then doesn't say whose entrance it is. The reader learns only at L173 that it is Elspeth's, and that is what gives the rival's suspicious "You used that door last time" its point. The fatigue beat ("heard only *gives him*") sits on a line that already doesn't follow. Also: Elspeth tells the rival to bring the daughter her car has just hunted, and neither says why; he complies without a word about it.
- Fix: Let Elspeth state her default (the south doors) so the driver has something to object to, and make "East service door" explicitly her own way in. One clause of reason for bringing the daughter (proof she's alive, a witness) would ground his compliance. Keep "You used that door last time." and his refusal to trust any door. Illustrative only: "We'll come in the south doors." ... "East service door, then. That's ours."

### D3 · Major · The evidence line misstates what the papers are

- Where: L185, pool deck, as Elspeth lays the orders in front of the rival.
- Text:
> These came out of my room with a dead man.
- Problem: Ground. The papers came *into* her room with a dying man, and his body stayed there (L447). Read literally, the line says they left her room alongside a corpse. The rival would more likely hear "came out of my room" as "were issued from my office", which sounds like a confession. She doesn't say they were forged until L219 ("the man who spent my name"), so at the moment she needs the rival to believe her, her line doesn't make the claim. The daughter's reply sidesteps it, so nobody on the page registers what she means either.
- Fix: Replace it with the plain fact: a man died bringing them to her, they carry her marks, and she didn't write them. Keep it short and flat. Illustrative only: "A man bled out on my floor bringing me these. My marks. I didn't write them."

### D4 · Major · A set-up and a zinger take the captain's answer

- Where: L209–L215, pool deck, Elspeth's ultimatum to the ward captain.
- Text:
> My men are not a bargaining unit.
> Your men have always been merchandise.
- Problem: Punchline Patch. Elspeth has just given the captain an ultimatum: pull the raid, or be exposed and caught here. His reply uses "bargaining unit", a labour-union term for a group of employees, where he means a bargaining chip, and it exists mainly so the rival can land a line about bought police. The captain never gives his position on the raid, and the negotiation drops until gunfire forces it at L307. The zinger is also in the dry epigram register the collection overuses, not the rival's slow, precedent-minded voice.
- Fix: Let the captain answer the ultimatum in his own euphemism: stall, ask for guarantees, or claim he can't stop units already moving. That keeps the question open for L307–L319. Cut the rival's quip, or replace it with something slower that he knows from experience about this captain. Do not swap in a new comeback.

### D5 · Major · Narration explains lines right after they are spoken

- Where: L77 (reveal), L145–L147 (captain call), L195 (daughter exchange), L203 (captain at the pool), L275–L277 (underboss's shout).
- Text:
> She had mistaken the man who carried the message for the man who had written it.
> "Say it again."
> He mistook fatigue for invitation and began explaining.
> Kin had a different arithmetic.
> Under pressure he did not shorten. He poured more words into every hole.
> His people heard *our*.
- Problem: Each sentence tells the reader what a line meant or how it landed, leaving nothing to infer. Three do more damage than redundancy. The mode of "Say it again" can only be read through the gloss: noir readers hear a dare, the narration says fatigue, and "mistook ... for invitation" asks the reader to fault a captain for doing what he was literally asked. L195 ("a living child did not erase the road taken toward her") explains in advance exactly what the rival says at L413 ("Not the road your car took"), so his final ruling lands pre-explained. The L77 sentence is also inaccurate, because the lieutenant carried no message.
- Fix: Cut the L77 sentence and let the following sentences about the underboss do the work. At L145–L147 choose one mode for "Say it again": if she has lost the thread, show that (the wings, a phrase that slides past) and let him simply start again; if it is a dare, drop "fatigue". Keep at most one of the two sentences describing his voice there. Cut "Kin had a different arithmetic" and the sentence after it so L413 lands fresh. Cut the two L203 sentences, because his next line already shows the habit. "His people heard *our*" can go, since the split that follows shows it.

### D6 · Minor · Figurative references the listener has to guess at

- Where: L209 and L217 (pool deck); L227 (the underboss arrives).
- Text:
> You keep the years behind this paper.
> the next child with your seal learns my family can be hunted and I will attend a meeting afterward
> children in gun cars
- Problem: Ground/referent. "The years behind this paper" could be his career, his pension or years of payoffs, so the captain can't tell what he gets to keep. The rival's line has the right precedent logic, but it knots two clauses together, and "child" could be an insult to Elspeth or mean her young crew. "Children in gun cars" could mean the rival's hunted children or Elspeth's young gunmen.
- Fix: Name what the captain keeps. Untangle the rival's sentence into his slow voice and keep the precedent logic. Make the underboss's "children" clearly her young crew (or the rival's kids, if that is meant).

### D7 · Minor · The missed word in the corridor can't be tracked

- Where: L355–L361, machinery corridor, the decisive exchange with the underboss.
- Text:
> Gone. It didn't have to mean dead.
> She had missed a word.
> I said it didn't have to.
- Problem: Ground/referent. The reader can't tell which word she missed, and his repeat drops "mean dead", which changes the sense to "it didn't have to go this way". If that shift is deliberate, the reader has no way to see it.
- Fix: Either show what she heard (the negation drops out, so she hears it the other way round), or cut "She had missed a word" and let "What?" stand as fatigue. Keep "Gone" and the stall that lets her hear the rubber drag.

### D8 · Minor · The phone turns into a radio

- Where: L419–L431, aftermath with the ward captain.
- Text:
> "Your phone."
> That is official property.
> His next radio call contained provisional assessments
- Problem: Ground/object. Elspeth demands his phone and tells him to "use it", and he then makes a radio call. He has used the radio for every operational order (L307, L319).
- Fix: Make her demand the radio, or make his call a phone call. Keep the refusal followed by her order.

## Prose findings

### P1 · Minor · Isolated misfires and stock beats

- Where: L75, L81, L171, L195, L215; "three" at L13, L15, L41.
- Text:
> His relief was worse than his fear.
> Her pistol was still warm from the lieutenant's skin.
> the painted depth numbers on the tile looked like measurements beside a corpse
> The rival's jaw shifted.
> The captain's mouth tightened.
- Problem: A pistol doesn't get warm from striking someone. "His relief was worse than his fear" names an emotion and its meaning is unclear. The pool paragraph stacks two death similes ("opened grave", "corpse") on one set. The jaw and the mouth are stock beats. The opening counts in threes again and again (three hostile rooms, three men, three different rooms), which reads as a template.
- Fix: Make the gun warm from her hand or cut the detail. Replace the relief sentence with what his face or hands do. Keep one simile at the pool. Make the stock beats specific or cut them. Vary the counts.

### P2 · Minor · Narration keeps pointing at speeches nobody gives

- Where: L95, L303, L377.
- Text:
> No thanks. That helped.
> No speech about loyalty, no look waiting to be thanked.
> There was no last explanation in him.
- Problem: Three times the narration points at the speech the story is avoiding. Doing it once is a choice. Three times, it becomes the story's commentary on itself.
- Fix: Keep one (L95 is closest to Elspeth's own view) and trim the others to the action.

### P3 · Minor · The closing line names the B-story

- Where: L451, final line.
- Text:
> Men older than her moved. The lieutenant moved first.
- Problem: "Men older than her" states the B-story's point as a category in the last line, where the prompt wants the reader to infer it. It also leaves out the driver, a woman twice her age.
- Fix: Keep the compliance and "The lieutenant moved first." Replace the category with people already on the page (the driver, the gray-templed enforcers), or just show them moving.

### P4 · Minor · Pool-plant machinery as corridor texture (policy)

- Where: L345–L347 and L363, machinery corridor.
- Text:
> Somewhere behind the walls a neglected pump shuddered awake
> his back to a pressure tank
- Problem: A policy note for authorized revisions. The pump and pressure tank are the pool's water-circulation plant, which sits close to the style guide's "No default water infrastructure" rule, and the prompt didn't ask for it. The empty pool is fine as a venue.
- Fix: Only if the edit pass touches these paragraphs: a boiler, generator or ventilation plant gives the same noise cover and the same tank to hide behind.

## Continuity and prompt findings

### C1 · Minor · The son's whereabouts and the first car's casualty

- Where: L127 and L161–L165 (car), L181 (pool deck), L405 (aftermath).
- Text:
> One of ours from the first car has a chest wound.
> Two streets off the club. Nobody in front.
> Found in a car two streets over. Scared. Not touched.
- Problem: The second team's car is found two streets off the club before the meeting, with "Nobody in front", which is itself unclear: front seats, or in front of the car? The son turns up at the end "in a car two streets over". If that is the same car, Elspeth's side knew where it was an hour earlier and nobody looked in the back, and the story never says whose car he was found in. Separately, a man from the first car has a chest wound, and nothing says who shot him (the car turned after "it showed itself" to the daughter).
- Fix: Make "Nobody in front" explicit (front seats empty, nobody has checked the back). At L405 add one clause saying which car it was and why he was found so late. At L127 add a clause saying the daughter's people fired on the car. Keep "Scared. Not touched."

### C2 · Minor · Small staging contradictions

- Where: L13–L15 (opening), L287 and L303 (pool), L367 and L381 (corridor).
- Text:
> past three men who were supposed to stop a dying stranger before he reached her chair
> He hauled the captain behind the bench
> The lieutenant appeared at the bend.
- Problem: The runner carried Elspeth on his shoulders when she was small, so "stranger" misdescribes him. At L287 the lieutenant already has the captain on the floor behind the tiled bench, and at L303 he hauls him behind it. At L367 the lieutenant is already in the corridor behind Elspeth; at L381 he "appeared at the bend", which is ahead of her, where the underboss waited.
- Fix: Use "a dying man" (or "anyone"). Make it "left the captain down behind the bench". Have the lieutenant come up beside her instead of appearing at the bend.
