# Outline

## Story

- Premise and central promise: At twenty-three, mob boss Elspeth learns that her underboss has stolen her authority to order reciprocal assassinations, invite a corrupt official's raid, and replace her after the resulting war. Before dawn she must stop both killings, break their partnership, and end the coup, leaving her battered organization unmistakably hers.
- Focal pressure or attachment: Elspeth wants her territory and chosen crew without accepting an older man's regency. Three sleepless days fragment attention: she reads leverage but may miss soft speech or mistake fear for betrayal. Delay looks like collapse; indiscriminate punishment would create it.
- Counterforce or complication: The underboss wants old-generation rule; the official wants slaughter to justify a raid and erase his dealings; an older rival must protect his heir and cannot trust Elspeth without costly evidence. The traitor has enough loyalists to turn negotiation into a coup.
- POV, distance, and information limit: Close third-person past through Elspeth. Give every physical clue, but let her choices reveal the pattern. Fatigue causes one plausible wrong read that another person's behavior corrects. Others' motives remain external.
- Governing movement and time shape: One night moves from Elspeth's violated private room through a tense drive to a shuttered athletic club with a drained basement pool. Intercepted murder becomes negotiation, internal gunfight, then private reckoning. The B-story accumulates through what seasoned people refuse to risk around Elspeth and whom she protects under fire. End after war, raid, and coup are stopped—not with coronation or maxim.
- Speculative rule or ordinary-world constraint: An unnamed modern-like city with ordinary weapons, phones, patronage, and police power; no supernatural intervention. Keep criminal logistics impressionistic. Information travels imperfectly, and Elspeth cannot control simultaneous sites. Authority lasts while rivals credit her commitments and her crew trusts her to share danger and punish betrayal.

## Voice

- Narrative texture: Hard, intimate noir moving by Elspeth's triage of entrances, hands, absences, interrupted sounds, and who looks to whom before obeying. Fatigue fractures selection without making her dreamy or incompetent.
- Conversational texture: Mix clipped reports, mundane requests, unfinished confirmations, bargaining pauses, and occasional argument. Silence can change leverage; not every line needs menace or subtext.
- Rhetorical ownership: Elspeth reasons in choices and costs; the rival in kinship, territory, and precedent; the official in procedural euphemism; the underboss in paternal history and collective pronouns; enforcers in locations, injuries, and immediate needs.
- Pressure behavior: Elspeth loses connective language and asks twice; the official overexplains; the rival slows; the ignored underboss expands, then accuses; wounded crew conserve speech. Unequal listening failures matter.
- Anti-default: Avoid a chorus of polished threats, quotable confessions, and backstory speeches. Elspeth's command registers through decisions, shared danger, and consequences—not universal cleverness or a final definition of power.

## Beats

1. In her violated private room, Elspeth finds a dying loyal runner beside two authentic orders in her name: each targets a rival heir, while police notice reveals a raid timed for retaliation. Her enforcer reports one team moving and another missing. She stops the nearer killing, wrongly accuses a frightened lieutenant, then corrects when his behavior exposes the real access path.
2. Elspeth summons the older rival and compels the ward captain to neutral ground. During the drive, calls mix missing cars, wounded people, and uncertainty over orders. Her enforcer contradicts her; she uses the correction without softening command, showing why grown killers stay.
3. At the drained pool, the rival arrives with his heir and superior force. His precautions and refusal to patronize exhausted, outnumbered Elspeth imply history without recounting it. The official expects to arbitrate surrender. Their distinct negotiations expose needs, not biographies.
4. The underboss launches the second attack and approaching raid while offering himself as the adult alternative. Elspeth cannot prevent every injury. She pulls the rival heir from danger and fights beside her crew. Keep violence brutal, consequential, legible, and non-instructional. Her costly action prevents the rival from turning confusion into war.
5. As the club divides, Elspeth uses established evidence—the stolen authority, raid timing, and underboss's premature knowledge—to force immediate choices. Some defect from him, some stay, one freezes; no mass conversion. She judges conduct, then pursues him into the machinery corridor.
6. Privately, the underboss tries paternal familiarity, generational entitlement, and rescue rhetoric. Elspeth's impaired listening nearly misses a second threat, but an ordinary sound and the lieutenant's warning let her correct. She decisively kills him after he acts; hard-R consequence, no glamorized technique or dying confession.
7. Elspeth returns wounded. With both heirs alive, the rival stands down. Surviving witnesses and evidence of the unauthorized raid force the ward captain to withdraw it and accept diminished leverage; she neither reforms nor publicly exposes him. Their reactions complete the inference about her authority.
8. Before dawn, active traitors are dead, captured, or gone. Elspeth does not punish fear as treason. She gives one immediate command to those who stayed. Stop on compliance and human cost: the A-story complete, the criminal world still dark.
