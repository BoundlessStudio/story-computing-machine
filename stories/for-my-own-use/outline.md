# Provisional direction: For My Own Use

Neri, twenty-two, scans her battered personal grimoire before another move. Its loose leaves contain small useful workings, crossed-out failures, bodily observations, and sentences she never meant anybody else to read. She stores the scans on a website as a backup, behind an account screen she believes makes them private. A crawler reaches the page images; a spell-generating bot incorporates them. Neri discovers the breach when Lio, a young man she has never met, sends her a bright, shareable card made from her handwriting. His roommate Osa tried a personalized version and now cannot move. A private failed experiment has become a stillness shortcut attributed to Neri by name. Close third, grounded in her angry, frightened young-adult perspective, with room for the bot's cheerful absurdities and ordinary human humor.

The local working holds a bounded state through the caster's own living pulse. The caster must define what is held and leave the movement for release outside that boundary. A hand-only stillness trial can therefore work and end safely; extending it through the heart to the whole body can include the hands and jaw needed to let go. This is why Neri abandoned a whole-body trial after another practitioner had to release her. Her large strikethrough means "discard this experiment," not an operating safeguard. The actual safeguards are the bodily boundary and the deliberately free release. The bot fails on both levels: it treats the rejected draft as usable source material, then presents the missing boundaries as optional annotations and fills the gaps with pleasant inventions. Different personalized variants need different hands-on attention. People perform and supply these workings; the bot only generates instructions.

Neri deletes the exposed backup and reports the scrape. The files disappear there, while downloads, reposts, and generated variants remain reachable. She calls the practitioner who released her own failed trial; help is coming to Lio's studio, where he and Osa live, rather than conveniently absent from the world. Neri can reach it sooner. The initial messages and the studio scene reveal people already iterating on the leaked card. Some want quiet, some steadier hands, some a longer hold. The promise is attractive enough that Lio arranged a small shared quiet session, in person and on an existing online feed. He believed Osa was asleep when he began preparing it.

Lio wanted to help Osa, his bandmate and a night-shift worker who struggles to get a quiet hour and cannot pay for instruction. His own short, hand-only test really worked; the dangerous whole-body substitutions came afterward. Seeing Osa's condition makes him believe the danger quickly. His temptation is to preserve the promise by asking the bot for an automatic undo or a safe group version. That iteration happens in front of Neri: each reassuring answer evades the particular body that must be able to release it. Neri rejects the proposed undo because it would close Osa's remaining free boundary. Lio then has to cancel through his existing feed and moderator contacts, admit he distributed something he did not understand, and send already affected people toward practitioners local to them. Neri has no equivalent access or credibility in those channels. This can stop the reachable event, not recall the internet.

Neri could hand Osa over to the incoming practitioner and leave the public response to Lio. Staying costs something she can refuse: she chooses to appear on the feed, identify the rejected experiment, and admit her own failure while showing the bot's erased boundary beside the scanned page. The failed trial and the trapped movement demonstrate danger; authorship only demonstrates theft. She keeps adjoining private material covered and refuses a request for the rest of the book. She also teaches Lio a limited second person's part in receiving and releasing a hold. She cannot hold both sides herself. He must recover what Osa actually changed, and Osa's small remaining eye movement helps them locate what is still free. Their living cooperation begins a slow release before the more experienced help arrives; it does not turn physical contact or goodwill into an automatic cure. Remote affected casters need somebody present with them, while cancellation reaches people who have not yet begun.

Possible ending: Osa can move again, the experienced practitioner takes over continued care, and this particular gathering is stopped. Another personalized variant is still circulating. Lio asks for a copy of the corrected working; Neri refuses to have this night converted into another card. A more particular final contact or private act can emerge in the prose. It should answer the difference between the notebook she wrote for herself and the knowledge she deliberately chooses to share, without making her disclosure a clean victory or her future a public teaching appointment.
