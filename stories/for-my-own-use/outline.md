# Provisional direction: For My Own Use

Neri, twenty-two, scans her battered personal grimoire before another move. Its loose leaves contain small useful workings, crossed-out failures, bodily observations, and sentences she never meant anybody else to read. She stores the scans on a website as a backup, behind an account screen she believes makes them private. A crawler reaches the page images; a spell-generating bot then incorporates them. Neri discovers the breach when a stranger sends her a bright, shareable card made from her handwriting. A private failed experiment is now an anxiety/stillness shortcut, attributed to her by name. The proposed title refers to ownership, not a universal magic of names; "For My Own Use" may fit better than the working slug.

Close third, grounded in Neri's angry, frightened young-adult perspective. The central working is a local practice of holding a deliberately bounded state through the caster's own living pulse. A trained user defines what is held, what remains free, and how they will let go. Neri's abandoned stillness trial left her jaw and hands rigid until another practitioner could release her. Her scans include cancellations and personal shorthand rather than a teachable method. The bot turns a cancellation into an optional comment, changes her carefully limited bodily anchor to "your heart," and supplies invented substitutions for difficult gestures. People cast the generated instructions themselves. Neither website nor bot supplies magic.

The first report is already a failed attempt to move or speak. Deleting the backup stops access to those files, but reposted cards, downloads, and generated variants keep circulating. Neri follows the remix through people asking the bot to make it stronger, longer, and shareable with a group. One partial rescue or a failed rescue attempt will make the stakes bodily and establish the limits of what Neri can do; it will not become the whole plot. She cannot recall instructions or simply tell rigid casters to perform the release they have lost the movement to make.

Lio, another young adult, has promoted the shortcut after a brief personal test seemed to work. He wanted to help someone specific who could not afford ordinary instruction, and now people trust him. Responding to their requests, he has used the bot to assemble a small scheduled group working at his shared studio and online. That gathering grows from the same circulation Neri follows, rather than arriving as an unrelated public emergency. His attachment and the damage he has already helped cause put pressure on his willingness to believe Neri, cancel, and expose his own role. He is responsible for his choices without standing in for everyone who wants relief.

The central confrontation is over stopping the group attempt and reaching people who have copies. Neri has authenticating evidence in the original notebook, including the embarrassing record of her own trial; proving the warning risks giving strangers more access to her. She also knows why an attractive bot-generated "undo" is unsafe. The repair must require living cooperation, attention to individual boundaries, and actual work. A response from the bot or deletion of a source cannot reverse bodies or erase all copies. Lio's choices and the affected people's help must materially change what can be contained; Neri cannot become an effortless solo expert.

Possible ending: they stop the particular group working and get a useful, credible warning into the channels that carried it, while at least one affected person still needs patient help and the leak remains elsewhere. Neri's private mark and sentences have been copied beyond recall. The ending should find a concrete act of ownership or contact that is hers to choose, without granting the bot absolution, turning her into a public teacher, or making disclosure a clean victory. The final emotional form remains open for the prose to discover.
