## Verbatim writing prompt

> **[WP] Your friend got so excited when you said you're trans like them. You just figured out too late they meant transgender, not actually a shapeshifter like you.**
> - update the rate for this story to YA-14+
> - male vs female identity play

## Story controls

- Working title: Transitions in Common
- Target length: 2,500–4,000 words
- POV: Close first person from the shapeshifter's perspective.
- Tense: Past.
- Tone and genre: Contemporary-feeling speculative friendship comedy with
  escalating identity confusion, emotional sincerity, and a warm resolution.
- Audience/content rating: YA, ages 14+.
- Required elements: The central misunderstanding over the word `trans`; a
  transgender friend whose excitement is sincere and whose identity is never
  treated as the mistake; a literal shapeshifter; male-versus-female identity
  play through voluntary changes of body and presentation; a consequential
  reveal; an honest apology; mutual curiosity with explicit boundaries; and a
  complete repair or redefinition of the friendship.
- Prohibited elements: Deadnaming or deliberate misgendering as comedy;
  equating transgender identity with supernatural transformation; treating
  anatomy or magical shape as proof of gender; involuntary outing; graphic
  sexual content; fetishized transformation; or a resolution in which either
  friend must become less trans, less fluid, or less themselves to be accepted.

## Assumptions

- The two central characters are teens in the same broad age range; their bond
  is close friendship, not a required romance.
- The shapeshifter can voluntarily move among masculine, feminine, and mixed
  human forms, but the story distinguishes physical form, presentation,
  pronouns, and inward identity instead of collapsing them into one axis.
- The transgender friend knows the shapeshifter only through one ordinary
  human presentation when the misunderstanding begins.
- The shared universe permits a new, locally bounded shapeshifting mechanism;
  its rules and costs will be established in-story rather than generalized.
- Comedy comes from incompatible assumptions and social timing, never from the
  transgender friend's identity or from denying either character's personhood.
- The working title may change during planning or editing if a stronger title
  emerges.

## Completion tests

- The result is a complete short story, not a synopsis or chapter fragment.
- The central promise of the prompt is dramatized and resolved.
- The requested YA-14+ ceiling is respected: identity discussion, non-graphic
  transformation, embarrassment, and peril are allowed; explicit sex, graphic
  injury, hate-crime spectacle, and slur-driven abuse are not.
- Male-versus-female identity play materially shapes the plot, character arc,
  and dialogue rather than appearing as a cosmetic montage.
- The story clearly distinguishes transgender experience from literal
  shapeshifting while allowing the friends to find meaningful common ground.
- The shapeshifter's reveal creates a real decision or complication, not only
  an exposition scene, and the ending earns the friendship's repaired trust.
- The story passes canon and continuity review.
- Every character-facing name and alias is checked against `stories/NAMES.md`;
  any deliberate reuse has a documented narrative reason.
