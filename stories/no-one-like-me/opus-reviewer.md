This is one of the cases where the owner's complaint does not apply. I went through all 30-odd spoken lines in order. Every reply responds to what its listener actually heard, and every referent resolves. The mismatches that do occur are deliberate and marked by the listener's reply. The engine is a legible misread. Jannik asks for "the rest" of the tune. Sereva answers with more voices instead, and he says so plainly ("I meant the tune"). That plants her choice on stage, and the final wing exchange pays it off: he quotes her tune and gets the step wrong. Both speakers are terse, but they aren't interchangeable. Jannik talks about tune, words and practical risks, with colloquial ease. Sereva talks about technique, gets grand only through borrowed voices, and misreads the social moment ("You won, though."). Mutual correction runs through their scenes (L15–L19, L55, L63, L183–L191, L201), but each correction is about something concrete and carries the rivalry, so it isn't a tic. The edit pass should make small fixes only. It shouldn't add wit, speeches or extra corrections.

## Dialogue findings

### D1 · Minor · Host reads the ruling like a rulebook

- Where: L171, host's on-stage ruling after Sereva's song
- Text:
> “Under the final's imitation rule, that performance cannot be scored,” the host says.
> “Your final entry is disqualified.”
- Problem: The ruling is coherent and the beat is necessary. But "Under the final's imitation rule" is a mouthful for live television and sounds legalistic, and "cannot be scored" and "is disqualified" say the same thing twice. The outline gives the host "public polish". Here she sounds like an official reading a clause, the one place where the collection's legalistic house register shows up.
- Fix: Keep one clear ruling, spoken to the hall as a broadcast host would, and keep "She gives the words time to reach the hall." Cut the duplicate half. Don't add commentary on the song's worth. Illustrative only: “I'm sorry, Sereva. The judges can only score the songs you entered, so tonight's performance is disqualified.”

## Prose findings

### P1 · Minor · Muddled image of the remote audience

- Where: L39, corridor, the host's reminders about countries watching
- Text:
> people eating in front of me who will never once look toward this hall
- Problem: "In front of me" has to mean "in front of my image on their screens", and "will never once look toward this hall" contradicts the fact that they are watching it. On first read it's unclear where these people are and what they are looking at.
- Fix: Make it plain that they are far away, watching screens, and will never be in the room. Keep the next sentence ("I can imagine them so vividly…"). Illustrative only: "people eating in front of their screens who will never set foot in this hall".

### P2 · Minor · "Before remembering" beat used twice, blunting the payoff

- Where: L83, corridor, watching Jannik on the monitor; L161, Sereva's bow
- Text:
> I clap once before remembering he can't hear me.
> I bow before remembering the microphone.
- Problem: L161 is the payoff of Jannik's warning at L29 and should land fresh. L83 uses the same "I [gesture] before remembering [limit]" template eighty lines earlier, and L39's "I have to remind myself I cannot hear them" is a near variant. The repetition makes L161 feel like a formula.
- Fix: Leave L161 untouched. Rephrase L83 without "before remembering". For example, let the single clap stand alone, or give it a small consequence in the corridor. L39 can stay once L83 changes.

## Continuity and prompt findings

### C1 · Minor · "Where Jannik stopped listening" misdescribes the corridor beat

- Where: L85, walking to the wing
- Text:
> taking it past the place where Jannik stopped listening
> “I wanted to hear the rest. You've done the same bit louder.”
- Problem: In the corridor Jannik waits until she finishes (L53) and asks twice what comes next (L43, L55). He doesn't stop listening; the tune stops. The phrase reverses the cause, and his curiosity is what pushes her to take the tune further and later to sing it on stage. If it is meant as Sereva's sulky misreading, nothing marks it as one.
- Fix: Name the place as the point where the tune ran out for him. Illustrative only: "taking it past the place where Jannik asked what came next".
