# Outline

## Story

- Premise and central promise: Still Taking Runaways. Twenty years after Idrella promised her teddy bear a circus escape, she runs the ring and living Rumbit sells tickets. Velzie brings a wooden dragon, expecting transformation and a flight into adventure. His first destination surprises them.
- Focal pressure or attachment: Idrella cherishes their escape and imagines another inseparable pair. Rumbit wants to ride something that flies; Velzie intends to return home famous enough to silence her older brother's ridicule.
- Counterforce or complication: Velzie initially presents her departure as permanent. Idrella mistakes theatrical determination for a history like hers. Matchstick prefers a familiar destination to their planned adventure. Rumbit's eagerness turns the awakening into an impromptu expedition.
- POV, distance, and information limit: Close third past through Idrella. Her interpretations precede corrections; neither toy's thoughts are available.
- Governing movement and time shape: One afternoon between performances: arrival, attempted welcome, awakening, a chase out of the circus, homecoming. The twenty-year history enters briefly through disputed recollections. Target prose: 2,500–4,000 words.
- Speculative rule or ordinary-world constraint: Living circus performers awakened Rumbit during Idrella's first season. He now breathes, eats, sleeps, and acts independently. His own living warmth unexpectedly awakens Matchstick while he handles him. Neither can guarantee another awakening. Matchstick remains small and wooden, with flexible wing joints; he can carry the light stuffed bear, never a child.
- Dialogue promise: Grand childhood intentions meet ordinary questions, omissions, and a creature with unhelpful priorities.
- Dialogic medium: Speech, handling the dragon, Rumbit's preparations, and Matchstick's directed movements before his first words.
- Dialogue engine: Each answers a different imagined invitation. Corrections redirect who is leaving, who expects a ride, and where the dragon actually wants to go.

## Voice

- Narrative texture: Idrella notices entrances, obstructions, costume weight, and people preparing to do something foolish. Let her confident attention become hurried and less selective during pursuit.
- Conversational texture: Ticket talk, expansive welcomes, incomplete explanations, familiar disagreements, and excited instructions arriving too late. Allow mundane answers.
- Rhetorical ownership: Idrella owns theatrical exaggeration and misreading. Rumbit exaggerates his daring, knows their embarrassing history, and sometimes ignores perfectly clear instructions. Velzie argues from particular injuries and imagined victories. Matchstick communicates appetite and direction before language; nobody translates him infallibly.
- Pressure behavior: Idrella grows officious; Rumbit becomes distractingly cheerful; Velzie repeats her destination until frightened of losing sight of her dragon, then gives useful directions.
- Relationship movement: Idrella wants Rumbit to endorse her welcoming account; he wants her excitement without being managed. Velzie wants both adults to vindicate her plan but hides its childish provocation. The chase makes her their guide and Rumbit an adventurer with consequences.
- Anti-default: Avoid a polished discussion granting the newcomers permission to belong. Let mistaken plans, a ridiculous flight, and the girl's changing pursuit carry the emotional movement.
