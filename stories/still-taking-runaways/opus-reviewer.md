## Verdict

- Dialogue: REVISE — the voices are distinct and uptake mostly holds, but at the title-question pivot (L151–L153) Idrella answers the child with a tidy self-diagnosis, and there are six small hitches besides.
- Prose: PASS — concrete, varied and funny, with two small staging slips.
- Continuity and prompt: PASS — every prompt element lands (ringmaster, living ticket-selling bear, twenty-year promise, wooden dragon, the runaway question), the prose runs about 2,850 words, and nothing conflicts with canon.
- Edit scope: targeted-dialogue
- Existing review.md said: Verdict PASS, "Dialogue: PASS"; partly disagree: most exchanges work, but it missed a Major at the title pivot and several small Ground hitches.

This story's dialogue is better than the collection norm. It has three clearly separate voices: Idrella swings from showman grandeur to plain questions and then to shouting, Rumbit is brief, greedy and delighted, and Velzie argues from particular injuries in run-on child logic. Replies mostly respond to what the listener actually heard, and the chase is carried by short, broken shouts rather than composed speech. The failure is concentrated at one decisive moment. When Velzie says "You said you took runaways," Idrella answers with a neat account of her own procedural mistake, which the narration has already given. The rest are small hitches: an unshown ticket grab, an unattributed verdict, Velzie falling silent while the bear rides off on her dragon, narration taking the last word on two shouted lines, and a mother who is too composed about a miracle. The edit pass should repair those lines surgically and leave the voices, the comedy and the wordless ending alone.

## Dialogue findings

### D1 · Major · Title-question pivot answered with a self-diagnosis

- Where: L145–L157, wagon step, after Velzie explains the flying plan and just before the awakening
- Text:
> You said you took runaways.
> Yes. I should have asked more questions before I started welcoming you.
> Idrella wished she had answered the first question less splendidly.
- Problem: This is the only place where the title question comes back to Idrella, from a child holding her to her word. Instead of answering Velzie, she reviews her own procedure in the collection's tidy, faintly procedural register ("asked more questions before I started welcoming you"). The line repeats what the narration already said at L101 and what the ending shows at L297 ("For once she let the girl finish"), so it is a Philosopher line that speaks Idrella's lesson aloud. Said to a small child, it also withdraws the welcome, and we never see Velzie's reaction because the awakening interrupts. The pivot ends on a neat retraction instead of pressure. Because her sentence is complete, "Idrella stopped." (L157) has nothing to stop.
- Fix: Keep "You said you took runaways." exactly. Replace Idrella's reply with a short answer that admits the promise and breaks off, so that Rumbit's small sound (L155) cuts her off and "Idrella stopped." reads as her stopping mid-sentence. Do not add self-analysis or a retraction, and leave L101 as the only statement of her regret. The homecoming and "We came together." then answer the question without a speech. Illustrative only: "I did say that. I meant—"

### D2 · Minor · Opening ticket patter has an unshown grab and a loose referent

- Where: L13–L15, opening, Rumbit at the ticket window
- Text:
> Two for tonight. Keep them dry. The red end is yours.
> No, you've got the whole roll. Just those two.
- Problem: Ground/referent and staging. The narration says Rumbit pushed "the tickets" across the sill. His next line reveals that the customer is holding the whole roll, which we never saw him take. "The red end" has no referent (the stub? the roll?), and nothing explains "Keep them dry," since no rain is mentioned. The story's first dialogue makes the reader piece the sale together.
- Fix: Keep Rumbit's fussy clerk voice and his correction. Either have the narration say he pushed the end of the roll across and the customer took all of it, or make "the red end" concrete (the stub). Cut "Keep them dry" unless you give it a reason, such as threatening rain.

### D3 · Minor · Idrella's ruling in the biting joke

- Where: L113–L117, after the cloth-rabbit account
- Text:
> The biting wasn't part of the attempt.
> I was checking.
- Problem: A small case of correction ping-pong. Idrella hands down a clause-like ruling ("part of the attempt") that sets Rumbit up for the last word, while a child is waiting for an answer. Rumbit's "I bit it... Nothing." is funny and in character. Idrella's line is the collection's hyper-precise correcting voice in miniature.
- Fix: Keep Rumbit's two lines. Replace Idrella's spoken ruling with a look or gesture, so that "I was checking." answers her expression rather than a clause. Velzie's L119 then follows directly.

### D4 · Minor · "That was unkind." has no clear speaker

- Where: L139–L143, Velzie describes her brother and mother
- Text:
> Idrella kept her face still. Rumbit rubbed a paw along the wooden back.
> That was unkind.
- Problem: Attribution. The line has no tag and comes after beats for both Idrella and Rumbit and then a line from Velzie, so either of them could be speaking. If it is Idrella's, it contradicts "kept her face still," which shows her declining to judge the mother in front of the child.
- Fix: Tag it. It suits Rumbit better, because he is stroking the dragon that was held out of reach and he is the one who would blurt out a verdict. If the line stays with Idrella, drop or change "kept her face still."

### D5 · Minor · Narration takes the last word on shouted lines in the chase

- Where: L229–L239, the chase out of the circus
- Text:
> Still here!
> She could see that. She needed him to be somewhere else.
> We're meant to go away!
> They were going away. Idrella had no breath to point this out.
- Problem: A Punchline Patch moved into the narration. Velzie's "We're meant to go away!" does not say what she wants. She means she and Matchstick were supposed to leave together, but her words describe exactly what the dragon is already doing. The next paragraph then turns that ambiguity into a pun, so her shout reads as a setup for the narrator's comeback. The same move happens two paragraphs earlier with "Still here!" Two comebacks in six paragraphs turn Idrella's fear into a comic routine.
- Fix: Give Velzie a shout that says what she wants at that moment: the dragon is leaving without her, or he is going the wrong way. Cut "They were going away. Idrella had no breath to point this out." and keep the sentences about her boots and the curl. Keep "She could see that. She needed him to be somewhere else." because it carries Idrella's fear; one narrator comeback in the chase is enough. Illustrative only: "Bring him back! He's meant to go with me!"

### D6 · Minor · Velzie falls silent while the bear rides off on her dragon

- Where: L193–L211, Rumbit mounts Matchstick
- Text:
> He doesn't need one. I hold his horns.
> Plenty of room for me.
> He fitted one leg on either side of the dragon's back and left his feet on the ground.
- Problem: A missing relationship beat. Velzie's whole plan is to ride Matchstick ("I hold his horns," L45; "ride him to the circus," L137). Yet when the bear climbs on in front of her, she says nothing through the mounting, the squabble and the takeoff. Her stake drops out of the beat that starts the chase, so "Bring him back!" (L237) has no earlier claim behind it.
- Fix: Add one short claim or protest from Velzie between L199 and L207 in her own voice, self-centred and concrete, not a speech. Leave the Rumbit/Idrella squabble as it is. Illustrative only: "I'm riding him first!"

### D7 · Minor · Mother's deadpan in front of a miracle

- Where: L283–L289, kitchen, immediately after the crash
- Text:
> Her mother caught her by both shoulders.
> I can see. Where have you been?
- Problem: Uniform fluency under pressure. Her daughter's wooden toy has just flown through the window and skidded across her table, yet she answers "Matchstick's alive" with a dry "I can see." That is the collection's composed, economical register. Her other lines ("Are you hurt?", "On that?") and the shut eyes at L305 are right, but this one flattens the shock.
- Fix: Cut "I can see." Before "Where have you been?", give her a moment without words, such as a look at the table or her hands tightening. Keep her economical.

## Prose findings

### P1 · Minor · Two staging slips

- Where: L71 (the wing opening while Idrella kneels) and L239 (the chase)
- Text:
> Behind Idrella, Rumbit opened the dragon's left wing.
> a curl had shaken loose across her eyes. She spat it out of her mouth.
- Problem: At L71, Velzie is holding Matchstick in her arms, facing the kneeling Idrella (L55, L119), so a bear standing behind Idrella cannot reach the wing. At L239, the curl falls across her eyes and she then spits it out of her mouth.
- Fix: Put Rumbit beside Idrella or at Velzie's elbow. Make the curl fall across her mouth, or cut the spitting.
