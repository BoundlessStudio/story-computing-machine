# Before She Paints the Ending

## What the request promises

The explicit center is **“Capture the Princess before she paints the ending.”** A thousand incompatible armies should make that race spectacular, dangerous, and sustained. The CMYK Princess and three RGB Knights have centuries of shared defense behind them; the significance of pigment and light must affect their actions. The supplied original gives her a broad black hat, flowing CMYK hair and paint, a pale gown overtaken by color, and distinct armored red, green, and blue light protectors with weapons and shields. The later library direction requires a connected meta subplot in which the invading armies come out of its books. I infer wonder, urgency, and pleasure in the guardians' practiced trust; neither a particular sacrifice nor a benevolent ending was specified.

## Provisional story direction — invented choices

Close third person follows the Princess through the battle, interrupted by three short passages from an unnamed adult keeper of our story library. Red, Green, and Blue are the living knights' ordinary names. Their centuries together appear in familiar signals, old jokes under pressure, and each knowing how the others move. What she wants immediately is enough unbroken time to finish the war's ending without being carried into a conqueror's book.

The Princess can paint changes onto existing ground while fighting, but an ending needs a continuous new white ground, made by all three knights combining their light. Committing to that work stops them defending her separately. Her cyan, magenta, and yellow supply the image; black is the final key that makes its limits exact and closes it. An unfinished image can still be broken. These are this story's local rules, introduced by an interrupted attempt rather than a lesson.

The opening assault prevents that first attempt: pirate grapnels arrive inside a starship's tractor beam; mounted dinosaurs break through troops trying to steal the same prize; dragons attack the transport trying to lift her. Each knight exercises immense, distinct power, while the Princess paints her own escapes and counterattacks. Every change of ground is an attempted capture or its consequence. The armies' competition saves her briefly and then teaches them how to cooperate just enough to take her.

In the library, our keeper sees actual soldiers climb out of printed pages, leaving spaces in the sentences behind them. Familiar collection spines stand among the impossible books; titles are recognition, not retellings or changes to their source stories. Shutting a book cuts its reinforcement route but does not recall soldiers already outside. A robotic assault force tears free a printed leaf: its portable opening lets captors bypass the knights' outer defense. The keeper's effort to close the remaining books both limits reinforcements and makes the loose leaf the invaders' strongest remaining approach. The origin of the collapse remains unknown.

The robots bring a capture prism assembled from colored transparent walls. They have learned that cyan absorbs Red's light, magenta Green's, and yellow Blue's; its approach splits the protectors into the wrong passages. The Princess's first response fails, and the leaf-opening reaches her. Facing capture rather than an endlessly renewable escape, she changes the old defensive signal. The knights must follow her into the enemy's prize transport instead of preventing it from taking off. This is her gamble: the transport's armor and the rival armies attacking it may buy the time the open battlefield never gave her. Her paint is still alive inside the prison, but she has no white ground while the knights are excluded.

At the climax the capture race continues around the rising transport. The RGB Knights use the filters' actual transmission: each enters through a wall that passes its own color, abandoning the opponent's assigned lane. Their overlapping light makes white inside the prism. The Princess paints the battlefield's armies crossing back through the distinct openings that birthed them; her final black boundary closes the joined battlefield, not the futures of their individual worlds. Captors strike to interrupt her as the ship is torn apart by its competitors. All three knights must hold the white ground instead of raising their shields; she supplies the last defense with the painting itself and completes the key stroke. The ending has visibly immense consequences: starships fold into pages, dragon wings become moving lines, and the impossible front separates into a thousand homeward paths.

In the library, the loose leaf returns to its binding and the absent words return with it. A newly finished volume records this battle. The keeper finds a black thumbprint at its ending. On the Princess's restored ground, Red, Green, and Blue separate from white and resume their places around her. A small exchange carries the cost and relief of the just-survived capture; their centuries of defense continue. The final image belongs to her completed ending and the people who held its light, rather than an explanation of what fiction means.
