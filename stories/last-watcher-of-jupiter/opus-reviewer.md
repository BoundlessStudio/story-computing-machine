# Opus review: The Last Watcher of Jupiter

Independent audit for a later editing pass. It is not a `review.md` verdict,
does not change canon state, and does not authorize edits by itself.

## Verdict

- Dialogue: REVISE — the exchange about whether the organism and the signal are linked (L121–L131) has referents that don't resolve and a reply that doesn't answer what was asked; the other argument exchanges run on claim–correction–retort volleys, and the final exchange explains itself.
- Prose: PASS — concrete and well staged; only some reversal/aphorism closers, small staging slips and one flood-containment metaphor need polish.
- Continuity and prompt: PASS — the kneel, the exception and the resolution are all there, within the length band; there are minor gaps in the shutter power budget and in how Eight is freed after Nine goes dark.
- Edit scope: targeted-dialogue
- Existing review.md said: Verdict PASS; "Dialogue: PASS". Disagree: it is right that the operational talk is role-specific, but it misses the broken L121–L131 exchange, the retort volleys, and the final exchange that explains itself.

The operational dialogue is good and should be left alone: overlapping calls,
collided counts, pilots reporting what their joints and hands can do, and Idris
repeating "Sector occupied" until the tube is clear. The failures come when
people stop reporting and start interpreting or bargaining. Then everyone falls
into the collection's house rhythm: a claim, a precise correction, a dry retort
to close. Often narration follows to explain what the exchange meant (L121–L131,
L221–L233, L331–L339). In those volleys Varai and Idris are interchangeable, and
Romesh becomes a disclaimer attached to every answer. The last private exchange
with Nadja has real pressure (did she injure herself by pulling against load?),
but its lines state that pressure outright. The edit pass should make the argument
exchanges answer the words actually spoken, let Varai push with numbers and
requests rather than quips, and let Nadja's relief show without her explaining it.

## Edit guardrails

- Prompt: for thirty years the station's mecha have faced Jupiter to contain what lives beneath the storms. Every machine turns toward deep space and kneels except Varai's Cairn Nine. Keep it a complete story that resolves that promise: broadly accessible, 2,500–4,000 words (now about 3,380), close third person, past tense, on Varai. Active craft profile: prospective-2026-08-21 (the last `Craft profile:` line).
- Later scenes depend on these story mechanics:
  - Nine's eight-year-old analog integrator: installed after a micrometeor, it needs manual formation acquisition and can't authenticate three channels.
  - The three-channel signal: the station's own codeword, an optical sequence no longer in service, and a magnetic sweep.
  - Shutters block the optical channel, and Nine's crossing corrupts the magnetic baseline.
  - Service links are pulled only when a frame shows unloaded.
  - Nine emitters sustain the lattice and ten reshape it. The final state is ten working frames, with Five and Nine offline.
  - Payoffs: Naveen's "three and a half" shoulder-mount limit pays off at "The shoulders did not move.", and Nadja's cold left glove becomes the warm, swollen hand.
- Universe: no LOCKED or CANON entry names Jupiter, Jovian Station, the organism or the signal. Personhood protection (LOCKED) still applies. The organism stays alive, with its intelligence unknown, and is a hazard rather than an enemy. The source's makers and intent stay unresolved. Don't add water-infrastructure texture in any fix.
- Don't flatten what works: the overlaps and collided counts, roll calls, Naveen's dash-broken self-corrections in mid-line, Idris's repetition under pressure, Nadja's irritable reports about her body, and the understated procedural ending (L471–L481).

## Dialogue findings

### D1 · Blocking · Organism-versus-signal exchange does not resolve or follow

- Where: L121–L131, after the pulse test, before Naveen's three-channel analysis
- Text:
> “Response follows field arrival,” Romesh said. “Not whatever the machines are facing. Within measurement delay, the deep signal and its motion don't correlate.”
> “So it isn't calling them,” Idris said.
> “I did not say what the deep signal is doing.”
> “You said enough.”
> “I said the rise is a reaction to loss of containment.”
> The distinction mattered to Romesh. It mattered to Varai too.
- Problem:
  - Romesh treats "the deep signal" as a known quantity, but nobody has reported a signal yet. Naveen has only said "I'm pulling it apart" (L97), and his first analysis arrives at L133. The reader gets a definite referent with no antecedent (Ground/referent, information order).
  - Idris's "So it isn't calling them" can mean two things. The organism isn't summoning the machines, or the source isn't calling the organism up. "Them" most naturally means the machines, and the source plainly is calling those. Romesh replies about the signal, which doesn't answer the likelier reading (Ground/uptake).
  - "You said enough." / "I said…" is correction ping-pong. The narration then has to decode what was concluded ("not an enemy coordinating an attack").
  - This is the story's first interpretive beat, and the reader can't reconstruct it from the dialogue.
- Fix:
  - Introduce the signal before Romesh relies on it. For example, turn Naveen's L97 reply into a report that something is coming in from outside. Illustrative only: "Something's coming in from outside the formation. I'm pulling it apart."
  - Have Idris ask the fear outright: are the organism and whatever called the machines acting together? Romesh answers that question once, within the limit of the data.
  - Cut "You said enough." and the restatement that follows it. Reduce L131 to what Varai does with the answer, or cut it.
  - Illustrative only: Idris: "So the two aren't working together." Romesh: "It's rising because the field's gone. I can't tell you anything about the other thing yet."

### D2 · Major · Brownout bargain opens unclear and closes on a retort volley

- Where: L221–L233, Varai asks Idris for moving shadow windows
- Text:
> “Habitat shutters draw from the same reserve as traffic control.”
> “I know what you need.”
> “You don't know that.”
> “It's enough to find out.”
- Problem:
  - Varai's "Idris?" asks for shadow windows, and Idris answers with a constraint the reader can't parse. The moving leaves are freight-gallery shutters (L165, L235), not habitat shutters. Traffic below the inner ring was already halted (L77), so it's unclear why traffic control's reserve is the obstacle. The real cost (brownout, life support) only arrives in his next line.
  - The exchange then closes on a last-word volley. Covered tags wouldn't tell Idris from Varai, and her quip, not his authority, seems to settle the decision (Identical Voices, Punchline Patch).
  - The relationship does move (Idris commits habitats at a cost), but the volley buries it.
- Fix:
  - Make Idris's first reply the actual cost, in his own register, and cut "I know what you need."
  - Keep "I can brown out three habitat sections for forty-two seconds. Then life support circulation loses priority."
  - If Idris still objects, have Varai answer with what she'll do if one pass fails, not a quip. Let his confirmations at L235 carry the decision.
  - Illustrative only: "Moving the freight leaves means taking power from the habitats." and, for Varai, "If it's short, I come back and hold."

### D3 · Major · "We know what eight does" exists to be corrected

- Where: L331–L339, seven emitters inward, before Twelve is freed
- Text:
> “We know what eight does,” Varai said.
> “We know what nine sustains under normal conditions.”
> “Today isn't normal.”
- Problem:
  - Nobody has measured or said what eight emitters do. Varai's claim has no basis in anything she has heard and no legible mode (bluff, hope, mistake), so its only job is to set up Romesh's correction (Ground/knowledge, Punchline Patch).
  - "Today isn't normal." is a retort that undercuts her own claim as much as Romesh's.
  - All the real content (nine sustains; eight only slows; a reshape needs more) sits in Romesh's last line.
- Fix:
  - Make Varai's line a question or a request for Romesh's estimate, so that "Eight may slow the upper edge. It will not give us a safe reshape." answers her. Cut "Today isn't normal."
  - Keep the fact that nine sustains the lattice somewhere in Romesh's answer, because L435 relies on it.
  - Illustrative only: "What does eight buy me?"

### D4 · Major · Final exchange with Nadja states its own meaning

- Where: L451–L467, private channel during manual cooldown, before the formal handoff
- Text:
> “It's swollen.”
> “Less good.”
> “I know. I wanted someone else to say it.”
> “You waited for your window.”
- Problem:
  - This is the last meaningful exchange, and it runs as a strict alternation of short lines (Tennis Match). "Good." / "Less good." is a sitcom-style understatement in the house dry register.
  - Nadja was clipped and cursing under load. Here she explains her own motive in one tidy line.
  - Varai then restates the rule the whole release sequence already dramatized ("No service pull without an unloaded indication", "Knee clear. Hip clear. Pulling now."). The exchange explains what the action already showed (Philosopher).
  - The medical question is good pressure, since Nadja fears she caused her own injury, but the explanation removes it.
- Fix:
  - Keep "Four is stable. My hand's warm now.", the medical question and Varai's "You didn't."
  - Cut "I wanted someone else to say it" and "You waited for your window."
  - Let Nadja's reaction be a pause, a breath or a practical sign-off, so the reader infers she needed the confirmation. Replace "Less good." with an ordinary response, such as a question about the hand, or nothing.
  - Illustrative only: after "You didn't.", Nadja answers "Okay." after a beat and signs off to the lock.

### D5 · Minor · "Good. A disagreement." misdescribes what Varai heard

- Where: L109–L117, how long one frame can hold
- Text:
> “Good. A disagreement.”
- Problem: Romesh (four minutes until the field degrades) and Naveen (three and a half until the shoulder mounts soften) name two different failure limits. They aren't disagreeing. Varai's quip misdescribes the reports, and "Good" has no reason the reader can recover (Ground, epigram closer). Varai should also register the binding limit, because it pays off at L407.
- Fix: Have her take the shorter number and move on. Illustrative only: "Three and a half, then."

### D6 · Minor · Naveen's aborted "artifact" line cannot be recovered

- Where: L143, Naveen dates the three channels
- Text:
> The magnetic shape—hold on. No, that's an artifact. I can't date any of it.
- Problem: Right after "The codeword is ours" and "isn't in current service", "artifact" can read as an old object or relic rather than a processing error. The aborted claim about the magnetic shape then disappears. A reader may think Naveen identified something ancient and dropped it (Ground/referent).
- Fix: Make the self-correction plainly an analysis error, or cut the aborted clause. Keep "I can't date any of it."

### D7 · Minor · "Ten can hold the reshape" collides with frame numbers

- Where: L395–L403, Naveen tells Varai to break center phase
- Text:
> “Ten can hold the reshape. You are burning the stack.”
- Problem: Frames are called by number, and Frame Ten exists. As a count, "ten" is wrong: without Nine only nine emitters remain until Eight is freed ("Nine green planes held", L413). As a frame, it isn't what happens: Frame One takes center. The reader has to back-count (Ground/referent).
- Fix: Say what holds the field without Nine, in unambiguous terms. Illustrative only: "The other nine hold it till Eight's in."

### D8 · Minor · Two untagged lines at decision points

- Where: L145 (after "Whose recovery code?") and L301 (Seven's refusal)
- Text:
> “Can it hear us?”
> “Then close it.”
- Problem: "Can it hear us?" follows a run with Idris, Naveen and Nadja on channel, so it is probably Idris, but it isn't marked. "Then close it." could be Seven's pilot owning her refusal or Varai overruling Idris's count. These are different relationship beats, and the reader can't tell which one happened (Attribution).
- Fix: Tag both. For L301, the refusal is stronger if Seven's pilot says it.

### D9 · Minor · Narration labels or explains lines just spoken

- Where: L111, L159, L175, L191, L269, L303
- Text:
> Romesh hesitated just long enough to be honest.
> Now the same deficiency kept one machine facing Jupiter.
> Her breath hitched.
> When he spoke again, his voice was measured.
> “Four local,” Nadja said, each word clipped short.

- Problem: The tags and follow-up narration name the tone (honest, measured, clipped), use a stock beat (breath hitched) or spell out what a line meant (the irony of the repair, the moral of Seven's refusal) right after the dialogue has shown it.
- Fix: Cut these, or replace them with physical action or status data. Keep the surrounding sentences.

### D10 · Minor · Romesh appends disclaimers nobody asked for

- Where: L219, L387–L391
- Text:
> The ascent may accelerate. That's not a prediction of damage at the station.
> “Confirmed below minimum. The organism remains elevated relative to the start of watch.”
> “Not on current measurements.”
- Problem: Each hedge is in character, but together they make Romesh a disclaimer machine and feed the correction ping-pong. L219 heads off an inference no one voiced. L387–L391 is a three-beat qualification volley with Idris.
- Fix: Keep the hedges that answer a real overreach ("I have six seconds of it." at L73; "That is slowed, not contained." at L347). Cut the second sentence at L219. Let Romesh answer Idris once at L387–L391.

### D11 · Minor · Nadja's "pull" arrives before the service link exists

## Prose findings

### P1 · Minor · Reversal and aphorism closers in narration

- Where: L381 (reshape), L435 (next watch), L479 (penultimate paragraph)
- Text:
> The field did not drive the life downward. It bent the rising current sideways
> They had exactly enough, which was another way of saying they had nothing spare.
> She listened until their timing became a watch instead of an emergency.
- Problem: The "did not X. It Y." reversal and neat closing aphorisms recur at key moments. L435 and L479 restate the scarcity and the return to routine that the counts and the handoff already show. This isn't pervasive; the prose is mostly concrete.
- Fix: Cut the second clause of L435. Give L479 a plainer closing clause. Describe L381 positively. Keep the final line (L481) as it is.

### P2 · Minor · Small staging and referent slips

- Where: L17 (opening), L37 (seal check), L275 (Four's emitter)
- Text:
> Alive, Romesh Ival said. Intelligent, she did not.
> Someone laughed once, without opening a channel.
> Nadja released one hand from somewhere
- Problem:
  - "She" could mean Varai or Romesh. It is Romesh's only gender marker, and many readers will take the name as male.
  - A laugh "without opening a channel" couldn't be heard across separate cockpits.
  - "From somewhere" blurs a physical beat.
- Fix: Name the subject at L17, put the laugh on an open mic, and say what Nadja let go of.

### P3 · Minor · Flood-containment metaphor (water-infrastructure policy)

- Where: L19, opening exposition
- Text:
> Not a prison, Varai thought. More like holding a river out of a stairwell.
- Problem: `universe/style-guide.md` "No default water infrastructure" counts metaphor. Keeping a river out of a building is flood-control imagery, and the prompt didn't ask for it. The line is also a "Not X. More like Y." reversal at the opening.
- Fix: Replace it with an image from the watch's own material world, such as braced load or held weight. Don't swap in another public-works default.

## Continuity and prompt findings

### C1 · Minor · Shutter terms and power budget drift

- Where: L165, L223–L229, L289, L351
- Text:
> “I can brown out three habitat sections for forty-two seconds. Then life support circulation loses priority.”
> Idris held the shutter while station lights dimmed to embers.
> Habitats two through five have thirty seconds of shutter reserve.
- Problem:
  - The brownout is a hard limit, "enough for one pass". The release then runs through more than a dozen windows (Four; Two twice; Six; Seven twice; Three; Ten; Twelve on a third pass; One; Eleven; Eight) with no renewed cost.
  - The limit comes back later as a reserve for four sections, not three.
  - The dialogue calls them "habitat shutters", but the moving leaves are freight shutters.
- Fix:
  - Add one line after Four's release showing Idris renewing the brownout at a named cost.
  - Make "three sections" and "two through five" agree.
  - Call the moving leaves freight shutters throughout.

### C2 · Minor · Eight is freed without Nine's magnetic pass

- Where: L401–L413, after Nine goes dark
- Text:
> “Their shadow windows are next. You don't need to carry center.”
> Nine green planes held, then Eight's pilot pulled her service link in shadow and brought a tenth online.
- Problem: The story established that shadow alone restores concurrence in under a second (L173–L175) and that Nine's crossing was needed to break the magnetic channel. After Nine dies, Eight is freed "in shadow" with no reason given. Varai's objection at L401 raises exactly this problem, and Naveen's reply doesn't say why it no longer applies.
- Fix: Add one clause, ideally in Naveen's reply, drawing on the L203 premise: with only two frames left in the brace, the magnetic baseline no longer holds. Illustrative only: "Two frames can't hold a clean baseline. Shadow's enough now."

### C3 · Minor · "Each freed pilot" is walked through a reach already made

- Where: L313, Three and Ten
- Text:
> walked each freed pilot through the awkward reach under the harness
- Problem: The reach comes before the release, so a freed pilot has already made it.
- Fix: Change it to the next or waiting pilot.

### C4 · Minor · Nadja and Naveen are easy to confuse

- Where: throughout, worst in the Four release run (L245–L275)
- Text:
> “Nine, two degrees outward,” Naveen said.
> Nadja grunted. “Hip free. Service is behind my right thigh. I have to get under the harness.”
- Problem: Both names start with "Na-" and have two syllables, and the two characters alternate in the tightest action run, where tags are the reader's only anchor. The old review checked Nadja against other stories but not against Naveen in this one.
- Fix: Preferably rename one of them throughout, after a name check. At minimum, add role cues (engineering channel, Four's cabin) to the tags in the release run.
