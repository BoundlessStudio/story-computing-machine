# The Wheel That Fled — provisional direction

The promised pleasure is a vivid animal cosmology whose calendar behaves like a contested, living sovereignty. The explicit requirements are the Wheel of Ages overseeing the months and both ruling and being ruled by the Cat, Serpent, Fox, Boar, Frog, Wren, Bear, Salmon, Spider, Wolf, Hawk, and Stag. I infer a mythic register, room for wonder and grandeur, and satisfaction in discovering how that reciprocal authority actually works; a particular tone, equal scene time, assigned month names, and a cosmic emergency are not requirements. This direction takes the form of an intimate but adventurous myth, principally about the Wren and the living Wheel she has long served as herald. Its anticipated payoff is a dangerous reversal: to preserve the seasons she loves, the Wren must help hunt the sovereign she loves, while the sovereign must exercise its remaining power to make its own successor possible. This is a local seasonal order in an unspecified magic-active age, not a claim about all history.

The Wheel is alive: an enormous ring of warm, growing wood with an eye at its hub. Its twelve animals bring the powers of their months; it gives those powers their succession and limits. At the end of an age, they traditionally hunt the old Wheel, and a new living shoot in its heart becomes the next. They cannot manufacture that shoot or appoint an inert replacement. The Wren has sung the departure of countless months but has never lived through an age's ending. This time the Wheel asks her to hide its tracks. It fears being replaced by someone who will not remember her.

- Open in the last month, with the Stag lowering his antlers to receive the Wheel's heart-seed for the customary hunt. Instead the Wheel tears itself free of the horizon and rolls into the country below, carrying the next age with it. A season breaks loose in its wake: ripe orchards become blossom, then bare wood. The Wren follows, initially determined to save her friend from the hunters.
- The pursuit moves through months the Wheel summons out of order. The Cat can stalk the night it brings; the Serpent can shed a binding season; the Fox can mislead the Wheel through its own autumn paths. Their animal natures change what is possible, while the Wheel can end a hunter's advantage by calling the next month. The Wren's knowledge of its calls helps the fugitive until she sees the consequence: the country is losing its future, with young creatures and ancient trees alike caught between incompatible seasons.
- The Wren turns against the escape. The other animals do not simply overpower their ruler: Boar and Bear take the charge, Frog and Salmon open a route through the Wheel's summoned thaw, Spider binds the route behind it, and Wolf and Hawk drive it toward the Stag. These are actions in a hunt, with resistance and losses of ground, rather than twelve ceremonial donations. Their combined rule can bring the Wheel to bay but cannot force its living heart to release the shoot.
- At the edge of the last month, the Wheel tries to command the Wren into one more beginning. She instead flies into its moving spokes to carry its seed out, risking being caught with the ending age. The Wheel must choose between crushing her and using its final command to order all twelve animals to begin the new age together. It chooses the latter, expending its life to give her a way through. The animals then impose succession on that simultaneous, perilous spring, and their new Wheel imposes limits on them in return.
- End with the new sovereign's first independent command and the Wren's first refusal or correction of it: loss remains real, affection has not purchased exemption from time, and reciprocal rule is renewed through a concrete act. The exact ending, nature of the old Wheel's death, and distribution of the hunt remain open to what the prose earns.
