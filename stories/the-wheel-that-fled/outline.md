# The Wheel That Fled — provisional direction

The promised pleasure is a vivid animal cosmology whose calendar behaves like a contested, living sovereignty. The explicit requirements are the Wheel of Ages overseeing the months and both ruling and being ruled by the Cat, Serpent, Fox, Boar, Frog, Wren, Bear, Salmon, Spider, Wolf, Hawk, and Stag. I infer a mythic register, room for wonder and grandeur, and satisfaction in discovering how that reciprocal authority actually works; a particular tone, equal scene time, assigned month names, and a cosmic emergency are not requirements. This direction takes the form of an intimate but adventurous myth, principally about the Wren and the living Wheel she has long served as herald. Its anticipated payoff is a dangerous reversal: to preserve the seasons she loves, the Wren must help hunt the sovereign she loves, while the sovereign must exercise its remaining power to make its own successor possible. This is a local seasonal order in an unspecified magic-active age, not a claim about all history.

The Wheel is alive: an enormous ring of warm, growing wood with an eye at its hub. It chooses when a month begins and its maximum duration; an animal can yield unused days, and an extension needs both the Wheel's grant and the animal's consent. That month's animal determines what happens within it, and its laws bind even the Wheel. The Wren is also the herald who makes authorized calls audible across the country; the fugitive can make smaller, local calls without her. At an age's end the twelve hunt the old Wheel to rest; its living heart must open willingly to release the shoot that will become its successor. They cannot cut out an undamaged future. The Stag remembers the previous hunt; only the Wren is new to an age's ending. The old Wheel's heart is failing, and the growth of Wren's summer can keep its sap moving; show her thickets sheltering it before she withdraws that last refuge. Once cold reaches its exposed heart at bay, it will die whether or not it releases the shoot. The old Wheel fears extinction and asks her to sing deliberately unseasonal calls, promising that if they escape together she can keep her own summer forever. The successor will be another living sovereign, with no inherited memory of their friendship.

- Open with an ordinary, consequential handover before the age's last hunt: an animal insists on finishing its month's work; the Wheel holds it to its remaining days. Then the Stag lowers his antlers to receive the heart-shoot. The Wren knowingly sings a false beginning instead. The Wheel tears free of the horizon and rolls down into the country. Her complicity, rather than a subsequent decision to follow, starts the chase.
- Protect the hunt's changing contest. The Cat finds the Wheel in the night it has called; the Wheel escapes by calling the Serpent's thaw, but the Serpent can choose where the thaw runs. The Fox's false trail turns the Wheel back toward a hunter instead of merely delaying it. These become scenes with reversals, not twelve equal demonstrations. Other animals may hesitate or bargain according to what the interrupted months cost them; none needs to be an ideological faction.
- The Wren calls her own high summer to conceal the escape, waking the Bear and her cubs into warmth. The Wheel then calls the Hawk's clear frost to defeat pursuit. The cubs are stranded far from their den because of the Wren's call. She must abandon her place beside the Wheel to guide them home, then tell the Bear where the fugitive went. This is the concrete consequence that breaks her complicity. From here the Wheel can call only in its own smaller, cracking voice: her departure changes its practical options.
- The other animals help bring it to bay with differentiated actions: the Boar uproots a concealed path while the Bear refuses to let the Wheel pass her rescued cubs; the Wolf holds a trail from below while the Hawk can see the fugitive's true course from above. Frog, Salmon, and Spider alter the crossings and routes as the pursuit requires. The Wheel's powers remain dangerous, but every summoned month also gives its animal jurisdiction over the chase. The Stag finally catches the rim in his antlers. This can stop it; it cannot make the heart open.
- The Wheel offers the Wren her endless summer again. She governs her own month by singing its departure, surrendering that sanctuary and closing the Wheel's last escape through it. She then approaches the heart, prepared to carry the shoot if it opens, rather than somehow stealing a seed no one can force free. The Wheel must choose whether the future exists at all. It opens. Only then does she enter the hub and carry the living shoot to the Stag, while the old ring loses its life. Drop the unexplained simultaneous spring and miraculous rescue command: the heart's opening is the sovereign's final consequential exercise of power.
- Show the successor's lack of recognition without erasing the old friendship. In the first full cycle, the young Wheel wants to prolong the Wren's beautiful summer; she insists on its ending, brings on the next animal's month, and feels the concrete loss and renewal of that decision across the country. The new Wheel then sets that next month a real limit. Her sovereignty changes the world; its sovereignty binds her fellow rulers. The exact final image remains open to prose.
