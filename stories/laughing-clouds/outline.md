# Outline

## Story

- Premise and central promise: At her unnamed solarpunk city's annual
  useful-failures fair, painfully serious young engineer Zelka expects her
  compact condenser to put atmospheric water into a bottle. Instead it makes a
  handspan cloud that follows one person and rains whenever laughter sounds
  nearby. With only the afternoon repair window left before judging, Zelka
  reluctantly accepts help from Kavi, the rival whose delight she first takes
  for mockery. Resolve whether Zelka corrects the behavior away or enters the
  device as the useful failure the fair actually asks for; let usefulness mean
  bounded shade, cooling mist, and small-area watering rather than a claim that
  charm excuses every hazard.
- Intended reader experience: Begin with exacting anticipation punctured by a
  ridiculous, tactile surprise; build comic pleasure through wet notes,
  hovering clouds, and two engineers who disagree in method as much as mood.
  Preserve Zelka's seriousness as a strength while letting evidence alter what
  she thinks rigor requires. The resolution should feel affectionate and
  technically earned, not like a crowd teaching a joyless woman to laugh.
- Generating force:
  - Mode: Plot-led accumulation in one festival day. Zelka and Kavi branch the
    condenser into several modular corrections; each version solves the named
    defect while exposing a worse tradeoff, so the workbench gradually fills
    with mutually incompatible definitions of success.
  - Focal attachment or contradiction: Zelka treats fidelity to a specification
    as a form of honesty. She also loves the moment when a mechanism reveals a
    real pattern, and the original cloud keeps producing repeatable evidence
    that does not fit the purpose she promised.
  - Plot-led wants and useful limit, if applicable: Zelka immediately wants to
    restore bottle output before judging, protect her reputation, and beat Kavi
    with a device that does what she said it would. She also wants to submit
    work that is honestly characterized and safe enough for another person to
    use. Her useful limit is that she equates changing a purpose with evading a
    failure: this makes her a disciplined tester who will not hide risks, but
    it initially makes every unexpected result look like noise to eliminate.
- Counterforce or complication:
  - Source: A fixed judging time, limited spare modules, ambient humidity that
    cannot yield the promised bottleful at the device's size and power draw,
    and corrections whose gains conflict: grounding collapses the cloud but
    produces only a warm spoonful; filtering laughter lets charge accumulate
    until an unpredictable dump; stronger collection makes a larger cloud that
    chills its wearer and dries nearby plants.
  - Credible inside logic, if a person: Kavi genuinely thinks the first cloud
    is already perfect because portable shade and responsive mist have uses the
    bottle brief missed. They nevertheless give up preparation time for their
    own competing entry, perform Zelka's tests faithfully, and accept that
    delight is not a safety case. Kavi wants Zelka to enter because winning after
    her withdrawal would be hollow, but refuses to help destroy the last
    working original merely to make the failure less embarrassing.
  - Pressure or change created: Zelka needs Kavi's extra hands and readily
    reproducible laugh to gather data, while Kavi needs Zelka's discipline to
    turn a delightful accident into something responsibly usable. Neither can
    get the version they first demand without conceding something valuable.
- Narrative design:
  - POV, tense, distance, and limitation: Close third-person past through Zelka,
    intimate with measurement, embarrassment, damp fabric, and the private
    relief of a repeatable result. Kavi's motives remain available through
    speech, choices, and the care they take with Zelka's equipment; do not make
    their laughter an infallible sign of sincerity or give the nonliving
    condenser intention.
  - Time shape and compression: One linear day from the quiet morning test to
    Zelka's late-afternoon judging and her return to Kavi's stall before their
    later slot. Give the first failure, the dangerous high-output trial, the
    workbench decision, and the brief judging encounter full scenes; compress
    routine recalibration through the growing row of variant clouds and damp
    result cards.
  - Information and reveal strategy: Establish the fair's celebration of
    repurposed failures before Zelka's test, so her resistance comes from
    character rather than misunderstanding the event. Explain the condenser
    materially as the pair tests it: a hygroscopic collector gathers local
    moisture, static keeps droplets suspended around the nearest wearer, and a
    housing resonance in laughter's common pulse range triggers coalescence.
    Reveal no secret magical cause or late proof that Kavi was simply right.
    Let measured limits show why the bottle brief fails and why the original is
    useful only under named conditions.
  - Governing movement: Promise; condense; hover; laugh; rain; conceal
    embarrassment; accept help; branch corrections; improve the wrong metric;
    encounter harm; define the actual function; preserve the original; add
    safeguards; submit honestly; reciprocate.
- Speculative surplus: The cloud's electrostatic fringe gathers loose metallic
  repair dust into dark freckles along conductive seams. It fouls miniature
  hinges and makes nearby fabric prickle, so Kavi caps exposed bearings with
  ceramic sleeves and Zelka adds this nuisance to the use limits. The effect is
  incidental to the argument about failure and does not imply agency or a
  universal property of weather devices.
- Structural distinction: A cumulative workbench comedy in which three
  corrected variants remain physically present beside the original, turning
  an abstract disagreement into a crowded set of incompatible outcomes. The
  decisive choice happens in an almost empty repair bay before judging; the
  fair supplies texture and a deadline, not a public emergency, converted
  crowd, policy rewrite, or grand-prize vindication.
- Decisive turn, deepening, or recognition: After the boosted collector raises
  output but chills Kavi's arm and pulls moisture from a tray of nursery moss,
  Zelka stops treating bottle volume as the only honest measure. She lays the
  variants side by side and writes a fresh test rubric for what the original
  actually does: tether range, shade temperature, rain volume, false triggers,
  battery cost, safe humidity floor, and manual dispersal. She chooses the
  intact cloud, fits a waterproof collar and discharge tab instead of removing
  its defining behavior, and enters it plainly as a failed bottle condenser
  with a provisional wearable shade-and-mist use. Kavi still calls it perfect;
  Zelka does not, and their collaboration is stronger for the disagreement.
- Aftereffect or live uncertainty: During the short judging encounter, nearby
  laughter triggers a drizzle and Zelka demonstrates the discharge tab before
  it can wet another entry. She answers the judges' questions with measured
  benefits and hazards rather than a rehearsed redemption speech. The story
  need not reveal a prize result: the device has entered the fair under its
  true history, and Zelka uses the time Kavi gave up for her to help ready
  Kavi's own entry for its later judging. Longer-term questions of hygiene,
  noise discrimination, weather, and public tolerance remain open.
- Opening / ending relation: Open with Zelka choosing a sound-damped test booth
  because silence should make every variable obedient, then watching a cloud
  appear where her bottle remains dry. End at Kavi's cluttered stall before
  their judging: laughter from the aisle starts a brief rain, Zelka clips the
  waterproof hood over Kavi's exposed control board, and accepts the test load
  Kavi passes her. The opening isolates one engineer from every variable; the
  ending leaves two exacting rivals making room for one another and for an
  anomaly that remains inconvenient, useful, and unresolved.

## Beats

1. At the fair's morning setup, Zelka aligns her bottle, power cell, and result
   card inside a sound-damped booth. The reservoir stays dry while a tiny cloud
   rises from the collector and settles over her head.
2. Kavi arrives from their own stall, laughs in delighted surprise, and the
   cloud rains across Zelka's immaculate notes. Zelka claims the final repair bay
   before judging; when she cannot both operate and trigger the fault, she
   reluctantly accepts Kavi's offer to help.
3. At Kavi's insistence, they first characterize the unaltered device. The cloud
   follows one wearer, shades exposed skin, yields a small cool rain at common
   laugh pulses, consumes little power, and can also wet papers or bearings.
   Zelka records the facts while continuing to call them failure conditions.
4. Their grounded version sends moisture into the bottle, but only a warm
   spoonful and at excessive power cost. Zelka cannot reach the promised yield
   without enlarging the device beyond compact size.
5. Their acoustic-filtered version no longer rains at laughter. It stores
   charge until a dropped tool supplies the wrong vibration, then dumps its
   water without warning. Metallic dust stipples the exposed seams and jams a
   hinge, giving Zelka a separate nuisance to document.
6. Zelka boosts the collector for volume. The resulting cloud grows dense,
   chills Kavi's arm, and begins drying a nearby tray of nursery moss. They shut
   it down together; Kavi follows procedure, and Zelka admits that a corrected
   metric has made a worse device.
7. With spare modules nearly gone, Kavi refuses Zelka's plan to dismantle the
   intact original. Their argument exposes both wants: Kavi wants a worthy
   rival and a useful cloud preserved; Zelka refuses to disguise risk as charm.
   Kavi yields the bench but leaves the original in her hands.
8. Alone for a few minutes, Zelka compares the surviving variants and changes
   the question from whether the device meets its brief to whether its observed
   function can be used responsibly. She recalls Kavi, drafts the new rubric,
   and the pair fit the collar, ceramic bearing caps, and manual discharge tab.
9. At judging, Zelka states the failed purpose first, then demonstrates the
   bounded alternate use. Kavi's ill-timed laugh supplies rain; Zelka controls
   it without pretending the trigger is precise. The judges continue taking
   notes, and Kavi leaves to finish preparing their own entry rather than
   becoming part of Zelka's victory scene.
10. With her result still unknown, Zelka follows Kavi and returns the help she
    received. At Kavi's cluttered stall, aisle laughter starts another shower;
    Zelka shields the exposed control board, takes the test load Kavi passes
    her, and helps her rival reach their judging slot.

## Continuity

- Canon used: The story occurs in the one coherent deep-time world but needs no
  wider-history awareness. The culture may call the condenser technology; its
  physical local mechanism is what matters. The city can sustain a materially
  solarpunk civic fair without implying one universal architectural taxonomy.
- Boundaries and unknowns: The unnamed city is new and has no established
  connection to the unnamed city of eleven foundations, Qarsis, Ersene, any
  Crown/solar network, or another water system. The condenser and clouds are
  nonliving, originate no magic, create no water, and only collect and release
  local atmospheric moisture under bounded power and humidity limits. Do not
  establish an era, civil date, universal laughter rule, permanent municipal
  adoption, prize result, or recurring institution beyond this local annual
  fair.
