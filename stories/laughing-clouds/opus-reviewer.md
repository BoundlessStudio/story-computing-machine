# Opus review: A Little Weather Between Us

Independent audit for a later editing pass. It is not a `review.md` verdict,
does not change canon state, and does not authorize edits by itself.

## Verdict

- Dialogue: REVISE — one line cites a mat that isn't there yet, Rasim speaks in Ianthe's clipped corrective register, and the concession exchange is argued as a syllogism around a thesis word.
- Prose: PASS — concrete, varied and well staged; only minor staging slips and one theme stated in too many places.
- Continuity and prompt: REVISE — the "rival" exists only in the paratext, so nothing on the page makes Rasim a rival; also minor water-infrastructure texture and an assurance that happens off the page.
- Edit scope: heavy-dialogue
- Existing review.md said: Verdict PASS; "Dialogue: PASS", citing "the rivals' distinct observation and reasoning"; disagree. It missed the mat referent, the convergence of the two leads' voices, and the scripted consent line.

Ianthe's voice is the story's asset. She is painfully serious, numerical, and
over-specific when the judge asks the social question. The prompt earns all of
that, and the child, the father, the woman with the cane and the judge are each
distinct and brief. The problem is Rasim. He answers Ianthe in her own register
of short corrections, so the rival "who thinks the clouds are already perfect"
argues in radii, cycles and cleanup percentages. He never argues with delight,
overreach or a rival's needle, and he never laughs. As a result most of their
exchanges become volleys of equal-weight corrections (L77–L81, L97–L101,
L131–L141, L165–L167, L255–L261). The narration then gives him an "easy reach"
and ready answers that his lines never showed, so his loss of words at the tile
has nothing to break against. The edit pass should make Rasim fluent, warm and
a little too sure of himself in the first half, without turning him into a
quip machine. It should keep only the Ianthe rulings that land, fix the
Ground slips, and leave the accident, the judging and the quiet final test
mostly alone.

## Edit guardrails

- Prompt: a once-a-year solarpunk festival for failed inventions that became useful in other ways. A painfully serious young engineer enters a compact water condenser that makes tiny personal clouds, which rain when someone nearby laughs. She wants to correct the defect before judging and reluctantly accepts help from a rival who thinks the clouds are already perfect. The story must resolve that promise, be broadly accessible, and stay within 2,500–4,000 words (currently about 3,270). The active craft profile is prospective-2026-08-21 (the rewrite constraints); the rewrite request covers dialogue.
- Keep close third person on Ianthe, in past tense, over one festival afternoon.
- Mechanism facts later beats depend on: the acoustic ranger misreads laughter as moisture density; the targeting radius is four metres; blue light marks target acquisition and amber marks reserve; there are three descending battery tones; trapped accumulator pressure causes the court surge; there is a mechanical cutoff and a 0.8 m/s wind cutoff; trials are capped at one cloud; a steward checks the blue paddle before each cycle; the judge's ruling is "Unexpected-use class. Conditional demonstration only."; the final test is an optical opt-in with no pump. The quantities must stay consistent: 0.31 L, 0.26 L / 0.18 L, 63% yield, 18 minutes of calibration, 26 minutes to the second tone.
- No universe dependencies. The city and court are unnamed, and the device is ordinary non-living technology. No LOCKED or CANON fact is touched. No name collisions were found.
- What works and must not be flattened: Ianthe's formality and numbers (her thaw appears in actions: checking the father, taking the blame, turning the cloth); the child's literal questions; the unequal fluency after the fall; the judge's impatience; the sparse final exchange.
- The outline warns against "quip-banter between dour genius and whimsical truth-teller" and against joke sequences used as rain switches. A fix to Rasim should make him fluent and persuasive, not whimsical.

## Dialogue findings

### D1 · Blocking · Help negotiation cites a mat that is not in the scene

- Where: L75–L85, Ianthe's festival table, as she accepts Rasim's help
- Text:
> Your log won't show which foot leaves the mat.
> That is why you record distance.
> One variable per cycle.
- Problem: Ground/referent. There is no mat at the festival tables. The floor is cork (L17), and the only mats in the story are the root-fibre mats Rasim unrolls in the repair court at L189, so this line borrows a prop from a later scene. The reader can't find what "the mat" is, and Ianthe's reply ("feet") skips over it. This is the prompt's beat of reluctant acceptance, and it runs as correction ping-pong ("People move." / "That is why you record distance." / "They move because of the water."). Then "One variable per cycle", said right after she names three things to record, reads as a contradiction unless the reader already knows she means one changed test condition per cycle.
- Fix: Attach the foot observation to something present, such as the cork, the four-metre circle or the chalk Rasim is about to lay. Replace the "People move" / "That is why" exchange with Rasim making his point in his own looser way: the log will record positions but not how people react to the rain. Either clarify "One variable per cycle" (she changes one thing per cycle) or cut it. Illustrative only: "Your log gets the three metres. It won't get them jumping back into somebody's soldering."

### D2 · Major · Rasim speaks in Ianthe's clipped register; the rival never sounds fluent, delighted or rivalrous

- Where: Rasim's lines throughout, especially L77–L81 (festival table), L97–L101 (mapping), L165–L167 (proposing the court), L255–L261 (before judging) and L335–L341 (final test); narration at L213 and L235
- Text:
> It is where their hands go.
> They didn't ask for scheduled irrigation.
> No foot traffic until we lift them.
> He sat back on his heel. For once, no answer came ready.
> His voice had lost its easy reach.
- Problem: Identical Voices / correction ping-pong. Cover the tags on L97–L101, L255–L261 or L335–L341 and the two speakers are interchangeable: both trade short declaratives that correct or limit the other's claim. After "Leave it. It's perfect." Rasim argues the clouds' value only in Ianthe's terms (tests, radius, cleanup rules). He never voices delight, a too-broad generalization, persuasion or a competitor's jab, and in a story about laughing clouds he never laughs. The narration says he normally has an answer ready and an "easy reach" (L213, L235). His dialogue never showed that fluency, so the break it describes doesn't register. His lines in the second half ("No foot traffic until we lift them.", "Wind is lifting.") then turn him into a second Ianthe.
- Fix: In the first two scenes (L29–L171), give Rasim longer and looser sentences: what he saw people do, generalizations that go too far, attempts to sell her on the use, and a rival's needle about her entry or his abandoned one (see C1). Have him step around her corrections by ignoring them, laughing or changing the subject rather than countering them. Keep his attention on people and bodies, which is his best distinction. After the tile, sparse coordination is right, but give the rule-making back to Ianthe (for example, she says the no-traffic rule and he just does it). Do not make him whimsical. Illustrative only, near L39–L49: "Did you see her face? And the ferns came up. Every hot bench in this city could use one of these."

### D3 · Major · The concession exchange becomes a syllogism and a thesis word, then the narration explains it

- Where: L131–L145, after the woman with the cane leaves
- Text:
> That is not consent.
> You thought the child smiled, therefore the use was universal.
> Not about the machine, his tone implied. Not yet.
- Problem: This is Rasim's first concession and the first real shift between the two. It plays as seven evenly weighted alternating lines (Tennis Match), with Ianthe ruling on each. "That is not consent." states the story's theme as a one-word correction (Philosopher). The outline scripted "delight is not consent", and the prose delivers it almost word for word. "You thought the child smiled, therefore…" jumps from the woman Rasim has just mentioned ("I thought she smiled at first") to the child. On first reading it sounds as if the child's smile were imagined, and "therefore" is courtroom register. "I called it perfect." / "You had one wet child as evidence." make the same point again. L145 then tells the reader what his partial concession withholds.
- Fix: Have Ianthe respond to the woman concretely (her sleeve, her cane, that nobody warned her), ideally in a way that implicates her too, instead of naming the abstraction. Cut "therefore" and one of the two lines about evidence. Let Rasim's concession come out less tidily: he might defend the child's delight before giving up the radius. Keep "I was wrong about the radius." / "And the chair." and cut L145, so the reader infers "not about the machine" alone. Illustrative only: "Nobody warned her. We were both watching the chalk."

### D4 · Minor · The repair-court proposal rests on a request made off the page

- Where: L163, the end of the mapping scene
- Text:
> The repair court asked if we could test there.
- Problem: Ground/knowledge. Rasim has been at Ianthe's table through five cycles. No one from the court comes over on the page, so the request and its details (six degrees too warm, a dry wick) arrive without a source. "We" also assumes a partnership Ianthe hasn't agreed to. Minor, because an unseen visit is plausible.
- Fix: Add a one-line visit by a court worker during the cycles, or have Rasim say how he knows (for example, their steward came by while she was reading the log). Replace the clogged "west channel" (C3).

### D5 · Minor · Narration explains the subtext right after the line

- Where: L173 (end of the mapping scene) and L273 (judging); L145 is covered in D3
- Text:
> She had meant those facts as refusal. He had heard instructions.
> Ianthe heard, too late, the question underneath the technical one.
- Problem: "So tell them that." already shows Rasim turning her refusal into a to-do list. L173 explains it and closes the scene on a neat epigram. It also takes the place of Ianthe's actual decision to drop the patch, which is the goal the prompt gave her; the next scene simply opens with her at the court. L273 explains a question the reader has already felt.
- Fix: Cut L173, or replace it with a concrete beat that shows her choice, such as closing the firmware editor or picking up the chalk reel. Cut L273 or reduce it to a physical beat.

### D6 · Minor · Clipped callouts and shorthand whose referents arrive late

- Where: L49 (festival table), L199 (the accumulator surge), L335–L337 (final scene)
- Text:
> That gives you something to test.
> Inside edge
> Enough for two samples?
> One active scan. Three passive.
- Problem: Ground/referent. "Something to test" doesn't say what (the watering use?), so Ianthe's silent refusal has nothing definite to refuse. "Inside edge", called during the surge, doesn't say inside edge of what, or whether it's a warning or a report. "Two samples" of what becomes clear only from the narration at L343. "One active scan. Three passive." reads as a count rather than an either-or answer to his question.
- Fix: Give each line a concrete noun or verb. Name the use at L49. At L199 say where the cloud is, or give a warning. Move the opt-in intent from L343 ahead of L335, or have Rasim name the cloth test. Make it "One active, or three passive."

### D7 · Minor · Triplet fragments and a neat "not just" closer in dialogue

- Where: L239 (Rasim after the fall), L275 (Ianthe to the judge), L281 (Rasim to the judge)
- Text:
> No glass. No loose carts. No one standing with a foot across the seam.
> Not a drinking-water source. Not scheduled irrigation. Not general heat relief.
> The steward has to watch feet, not just faces.
- Problem: Both leads use the same "No A. No B. No C." cadence. At L239 it makes Rasim's shaken moment sound like a drafted policy clause, straight after the narration says his voice has lost its reach. "Feet, not just faces" is an epigram that closes the exchange.
- Fix: Let L239 come out as a halting partial count; the narration at L241 already has him counting under his breath, so one or two spoken items are enough. Keep "Not perfect," at L243, where the callback lands. Keep Ianthe's scope statement at L275 but break the triplet, for example by naming two exclusions in one sentence. Make L281 plain.

### D8 · Minor · The judging repeats the list of limitations from L169

- Where: L169 (festival table) and L277–L279 (judging)
- Text:
> Quiet people receive nothing.
> Anyone who does not laugh, anyone who cannot or does not wish to enter the rain field, and anyone beyond the range.
- Problem: Exposition. Ianthe gives much the same list of exclusions twice, and the second time the judge's "Who does it miss?" exists mainly to prompt it (an explanatory chorus).
- Fix: Keep one full list. Either trim L169 to the facts that support her refusal (battery, tile, wind) so the judging answer is the first full statement, or shorten L279 to what is new since the tile. Keep "What are you entering?" and "And if everyone is too hot to laugh?".

## Prose findings

- Text:

- Problem: The style guide warns against explaining a point again in dialogue, narration, policy and a closing symbol. Here the idea appears in all four places; the paddle and the final cloth test already carry it through action.

## Continuity and prompt findings

### C1 · Major · The rival is a rival only in the paratext

- Where: L29–L33 (introduction) and throughout; Rasim's entry disappears after L33
- Text:
> From the next worktable, Rasim said
> Rasim had abandoned his own entry, a folding shade frame whose westward hinge refused to fold.
- Problem: The prompt's central promise is that she "reluctantly accepts help from a rival." On the page Rasim is simply the exhibitor at the next table. There is no history, no shared category or standing, and no cost to Ianthe's pride in taking his help. Her reluctance comes only from his opinion of the clouds. His own entry is dropped at L33 and never mentioned again, even at judging. That also removes the pettiness and competitiveness the dialogue could use (D2).
- Fix: Establish the rivalry in one or two lines of dialogue: a past festival, the same category or apprentice cohort, a remark one of them once made about the other's work. Let the entry he abandoned register once as a cost (the judge passes it by, or he shrugs it off). Keep it light, since the outline says neither is an antagonist. Illustrative only: "Last year you called my hinge an anecdote in front of the judges. I'm going to enjoy this."

### C2 · Minor · Rasim's assurance about the drains is never shown

- Where: L235, the tile after the fall
- Text:
> exactly where he had assured her the drains would take it
- Problem: No such assurance appears during the court setup (L177–L191). His guilt here therefore depends on an off-page event, and the reference to drains is also water-infrastructure texture (C3).
- Fix: Either add one overconfident line from Rasim during setup at L179, which would also feed D2 and C1, or cut the clause and let the wet fan on the tile speak for itself.

### C3 · Minor · Water-infrastructure texture (policy)

- Where: L13, L37, L53, L163, L177, L235, L325, L331; also "scheduled irrigation" at L165, L167 and L275
- Text:

> the planting wick went dry when the west channel clogged

- Problem: The style guide's "No default water infrastructure" rule applies to authorized revisions. The condenser, the rain and the watered planter bed are all earned by the prompt. The irrigation line, the clogged channel, the drains, the drain edge, the planter channel and the desalination membrane are default texture.
- Fix: Cut or replace the ones the prompt didn't ask for: a different reclaimed-invention example at L53, a heat-dried wick at L163, and a mat seam or tool bag in place of the "drain edge" in the cleanup tease at L325. The disclaimers in Ianthe's scope ("scheduled irrigation") can become "watering schedule" or be dropped where D7 breaks the triplet.
