# Provisional outline

## Experience promised by the request

The explicit premise offers the bodily precision and splendor of royal blade dancing, a secret one-heartbeat freedom within a frozen world, the accumulating danger of repayment, and a silver-haired elven princess whose forbidden three-heartbeat movement ends seventeen years later. I infer an experience of dazzling control broken by an enormous, intimate loss of control: one beautiful gesture can swallow a life she expected to live. The court and supplied images support ceremonial magnificence, paired curved blades, purple and gold, and candlelight. The request specifies neither an interpersonal relationship nor a motive, scale beyond the court, aging versus disappearance, or an ending. The later direction concerns distinguishing document instructions from the request; it adds no plot requirement.

## Chosen direction, open to change

Close third person, centered on the adult princess, provisionally Asereth. She attempts the dance because she wants to make something no living dancer has achieved, rather than as an emergency rescue. Her younger sister, provisionally Tiriel, and her longtime human harpist are invented relationships. The principal conflict after the rupture is her desire to recover their interrupted artistic triumph, against the living changes in its participants. Her appetite for difficult, beautiful movement survives; the story should not punish artistry by teaching her to abandon it. The changed middle and climax replace the first outline's succession coup.

For this story, repayment can remove a dancer from the years she would otherwise have lived. Ordinary stolen beats accumulate unpredictably; joining three without returning to ordinary time calls the accumulated debt due. This is not a universal conversion rate of three beats to seventeen years. Asereth experiences three distinct heartbeat-length intervals, with the world frozen within them; she does not gain an indefinitely long interval. There is no journey backward, substitute debtor, or recovered seventeen years.

- **Before the dance:** Asereth tests a single stolen heartbeat against a falling petal and returns to the living rhythm. She keeps count of the hundreds of heartbeats borrowed in preparing this dance. An earlier repayment made her miss nine days, including her father's birthday; the knowledge is real, but an elven lifetime makes her future seem inexhaustible. The harpist knows the secret and has helped prepare the old score. The royal dancers forbid the joined sequence because failing to return between the beats can call the whole accumulation due. Neither knows a safe price. Tiriel sits beside their father. The court expects an approved dance. Asereth decides to attempt the forbidden completion.
- **The forbidden movement:** Give the dance room: blades crossing candlelight, feet and breath doing exact work, the first frozen interval, the choice to join the second, then the third. She completes the movement and lands. The musicians have changed; a memorial cloth bears her name. It is the seventeenth anniversary of her disappearance. The audience has been hearing a commemoration of the dance that stopped here.
- **The years she missed:** Tiriel now wears the crown. Asereth first mistakes the black ribbon on her sister's costume for part of the commemoration; it marks their father's death six years earlier. She is taken to his tomb, where the birthday gift she meant to give after the performance lies among his possessions. The human harpist has become an old man. He has lived a career, not merely waited seventeen years for her. Asereth finds that the court remembers the instant she vanished more vividly than the many dances she completed. She wants to finish the original performance before them and be present for its ending.
- **An attempted restoration:** At rehearsal the harpist tries the original opening at her insistence. His hands cannot sustain its uninterrupted fast runs. This is a physical failure between people who once knew exactly how to move together, not a speech about age. Tiriel offers a skilled younger player for a performance celebrating Asereth's return. Rehearsing with that player gives Asereth the old sound she wanted, but her longtime partner must vacate his place beside the dancing floor and join the audience. Their father is dead; this living participant would be displaced by her choice. In the harpist's teaching room she then hears a later piece she has never known: abrupt runs, unexpected rests, and striking shifts of rhythm. It is demanding music shaped by his present technique, not a gentler concession. Seventeen years have produced possibilities as well as losses.
- **The public choice and risk:** At the royal celebration, Asereth asks her old partner to play his later music instead. There is no prepared dance for it. She gives up a guaranteed reenactment of her former mastery to attempt a new achievement before the same court that has turned her disappearance into legend. She must listen and improvise with live blades; she cannot know the next phrase by remembering the old one. Tiriel authorizes the change but does not solve it. The younger musician is a capable artist, not a villain or an inferior replacement.
- **The new dance:** Give this performance its own ceremonial beauty and bodily danger. An unexpected musical turn catches Asereth committed to the wrong step; she makes a low, difficult recovery that becomes a new blade figure rather than hiding the error. The clash and ring of her curved swords begin to answer the harp. The harpist hears her answer and changes his next phrase. Their mutual improvisation builds a composition neither possessed beforehand, with fierce speed as well as stillness. Time-stopping could give her another prepared position but cannot tell her what this living musician will choose next. She does not steal another heartbeat here; this is a particular artistic discovery, not a vow to renounce her ability or a rule that ordinary art is morally better.
- **Payoff:** The performance finally has a witnessed ending, but it is not the original performance restored. Their father's chair stays empty, the harpist's hands stay changed, and the seventeen years remain unlived by Asereth. The new work is a real achievement she could not have made by returning the world to the instant she left. End on its completed movement and the harpist's answering final note, with her desire for difficult art alive. There is no recovered interval, canceled debt, political reward, or explanation that the cost was secretly a gift.
