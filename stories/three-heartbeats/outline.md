# Provisional outline

## Experience promised by the request

The explicit premise offers the bodily precision and splendor of royal blade dancing, a secret one-heartbeat freedom within a frozen world, the accumulating danger of repayment, and a silver-haired elven princess whose forbidden three-heartbeat movement ends seventeen years later. I infer an experience of dazzling control broken by an enormous, intimate loss of control: one beautiful gesture can swallow a life she expected to live. The court and supplied images support ceremonial magnificence, paired curved blades, purple and gold, and candlelight. The request specifies neither an interpersonal relationship nor a motive, scale beyond the court, aging versus disappearance, or an ending. The later direction concerns distinguishing document instructions from the request; it adds no plot requirement.

## Chosen direction, open to change

Close third person, centered on the adult princess, provisionally Asereth. She attempts the dance because she wants to make something no living dancer has achieved, rather than as an emergency rescue. Her younger sister, provisionally Tiriel, and an older human accompanist are invented relationships. The principal conflict after the rupture is whether Asereth will seize the old precedence offered by people who want to replace the reigning Tiriel, or act within a future she did not make. Her appetite for difficult, beautiful movement survives; the story should not punish artistry by teaching her to abandon it.

For this story, repayment can remove a dancer from the years she would otherwise have lived. Ordinary stolen beats accumulate unpredictably; joining three without returning to ordinary time calls the accumulated debt due. This is not a universal conversion rate of three beats to seventeen years. Asereth experiences three distinct heartbeat-length intervals, with the world frozen within them; she does not gain an indefinitely long interval. There is no journey backward, substitute debtor, or recovered seventeen years.

- **Before the dance:** Asereth tests a single stolen heartbeat against a falling petal and returns to the living rhythm. The accompanist knows the secret and has helped her prepare an incomplete old score. He warns her about the missing return between the third movement's beats. Tiriel, still the younger sister whose counsel Asereth can dismiss, sits beside their father. The court expects an approved dance. Asereth decides to attempt the forbidden completion.
- **The forbidden movement:** Give the dance room: blades crossing candlelight, feet and breath doing exact work, the first frozen interval, the choice to join the second, then the third. She completes the movement and lands. The musicians have changed; a memorial cloth bears her name. It is the seventeenth anniversary of her disappearance. The audience has been hearing a commemoration of the dance that stopped here.
- **The years she missed:** Tiriel now wears the crown; their father died six years earlier. The human accompanist has become an old man whose hands cannot play the original pace. Asereth's room and place were mourned, then used by living people. She discovers specific changes through encounters rather than receiving a history lecture. She cannot ask Tiriel to become her little sister again, or hear the applause she had been reaching for.
- **A dangerous return:** An old court faction offers its guards and Asereth's senior claim to the throne. Its leader turns her return into a supposed royal destiny. At the next public assembly, she refuses to serve as his claimant. He orders his men to take Tiriel into custody anyway, asserting the elder heir's rights. His captain is close enough to seize the queen before her own guards can reach her. Asereth's refusal must become action, not a declaration the room conveniently obeys.
- **A movement in the new world:** Asereth steals exactly one heartbeat. Within its small physical allowance she makes one decisive blade movement, freeing Tiriel from the captain's weapon and placing herself beside her. Tiriel can now direct her own guards; Asereth fights through the following ordinary seconds with her. She has incurred fresh debt knowingly, without receiving immunity or reimbursement. The sister she once dismissed is a ruler and an ally, not a vacant position to recover.
- **Payoff:** The coup fails through their linked actions. The seventeen years remain missing, including their father's death and the accompanist's changed hands. Asereth returns to him with the blades, still wanting to dance; he has seventeen years of music she has never heard. Their next performance will require a new score and movement that belongs to these bodies and this court. End in the beginning of that living movement, carrying both the irretrievable interval and her undiminished artistic desire.
