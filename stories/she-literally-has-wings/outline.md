## Story

- Premise and central promise: While three friends close an unnamed neighborhood supper hall, Julian calls Coralie an angel and discovers that Tomas, her husband, assumed she was an avian demi-human or hybrid. Coralie overhears. Fulfill the comic revelation without making marriage a punch line: she defines herself, Tomas's mistake is plausible, and his answer shows he married the person he knows while admitting the question he avoided.
- Focal pressure or attachment: Coralie values that Tomas never exoticizes her wings, but wants to be known without making her origin a lesson. His confident misclassification collides with both needs. Tomas wants their marriage to matter more than taxonomy; Julian wants to challenge astonishing inattention, yet both men risk turning Coralie into evidence.
- Counterforce or complication: Departing guests still need leftovers, lost belongings, and small favors, preventing an instant private reckoning. Each interruption undercuts Julian's claim that celestial force produces their smiles: Coralie remembers particular people. Embarrassment, competing forms of care, and her refusal to smooth the moment supply pressure without an antagonist.
- POV, distance, and information limit: Close third-person past through Coralie. She knows her angelhood and what she never explicitly told Tomas, but cannot initially distinguish indifference, courtesy, and fear of asking badly. Keep both men's motives exterior until choices clarify them. Her wing clearance and fatigue feel ordinary, never reverent spectacle.
- Governing movement and time shape: One closing hour circles through dining room, serving counter, coat hooks, and rear door while repeated entrances reveal why people smile. The disagreement contracts after the room empties; Julian leaves and the couple walk one block home. Resolve through altered attention and a bounded practical question, not public proof, polished reconciliation, or a thematic last line.
- Speculative rule or ordinary-world constraint: In this city, unrelated living peoples and transformed humans may have wings, so appearance does not settle taxonomy. Coralie calls herself an angel; her flight joins physical wings with her own living agency and has limits of space, weather, exertion, and recovery. She has no compulsory halo, moral infallibility, emotion control, healing authority, or universal rank. Her conduct—not magic—makes people smile.

## Voice

- Narrative texture: Warm close observation follows Coralie through doorways, chairs, trays, and other people's needs. Wit comes from concrete misreadings; when her identity becomes evidence, narration grows exact and refuses celestial lyricism.
- Conversational texture: Cleanup produces ordinary requests, overlap, and unfinished thoughts. Julian's disbelief comes in bursts, Tomas accumulates particulars, and Coralie speaks least before taking control of the dispute.
- Rhetorical ownership: Julian reasons from clues and categories; Tomas from routines and practical care; Coralie distinguishes self-knowledge from observation and turns abstraction toward consequence. Guests get single-purpose speech, not a chorus.
- Pressure behavior: Julian repeats and overstates. Tomas begins too fluent, then shortens as he listens. Coralie stops cushioning embarrassment, works through pauses, and becomes direct when either man speaks for her.
- Anti-default: Do not reproduce meme cadence, equalize everyone's wit, or let angel lore swallow the marriage. Avoid a taxonomy lecture, speech-length defense, apology protocol, or final exchange explaining what behavior shows.
