# Opus review: She Literally Has Wings

Independent audit for a later editing pass. It is not a `review.md` verdict,
does not change canon state, and does not authorize edits by itself.

## Verdict

- Dialogue: REVISE — the word-count exchange at the central reveal makes no sense, and the decisive exchange states its own moral while the narration explains every line.
- Prose: PASS — warm, concrete and well paragraphed; only small staging and wording slips.
- Continuity and prompt: PASS — all four prompt beats are delivered; two minor number/object inconsistencies.
- Edit scope: targeted-dialogue
- Existing review.md said: Verdict PASS, Dialogue PASS; disagree. It missed the incoherent L67–L69 volley and the confrontation's thesis close.

This dialogue is better than the collection's usual failure. The guests speak plainly and one line at a time. Coralie steers the room with chores instead of speeches, Julian's disbelief comes out in bursts, and the walk home is a good example of narrow, practical talk. The failures cluster where Tomas and Coralie argue about the word itself. First there is a word-count volley that doesn't match what Coralie actually said. Then Tomas gives the prompt's "bird demi-human" confession as tidy case notes, and the decisive exchange ends on a stated principle, with the narration explaining nearly every line right after it lands. The edit pass should repair the Ground break at L63–L71 and let Tomas's polish crack once he faces Coralie instead of Julian. It should also cut the second and third statements of the "you never asked" point and leave the silences to the reader, without touching the ordinary exchanges or the ending.

## Dialogue findings

### D1 · Blocking · Word-count volley does not match what was said

- Where: L63–L71, serving counter, right after Julian says "She just said she is."
- Text:
> "I heard four words."
> "Three, if she said what you think."
> Julian stared at him. "That's your objection?"
- Problem: Ground/referent and Punchline Patch. Coralie's actual line (L47, "I call myself an angel.") is five words. The only four-word line in the scene is the green-coat woman's "You are an angel." Tomas's "three" fits only an imagined "I'm an angel." The reader can't work out what either count refers to, and the setup exists only so Tomas can land a pedantic correction and Julian can react to it. This is the prompt's central confrontation, so the break is at the story's most important beat.
- Fix: Cut these three lines. Have Julian answer "You probably heard her wrong" with what he actually heard, her own words. Then Tomas's glance at Coralie (L73) follows from having no counter. Keep L73–L79 as written. Illustrative only: "I was standing right here. 'I call myself an angel.'"

### D2 · Major · Decisive exchange ends by stating its moral

- Where: L279–L283, the emptied hall, after Julian goes back to sweeping
- Text:
> "What do you need me to understand tonight?"
> The narrowness of it helped. Not everything. Not forever. Tonight.
> "That not making me explain myself isn't the same as leaving room for me to tell you."
- Problem: Philosopher, plus a setup built for a thesis. The point already landed at L253–L259 ("You didn't ask me anything." / "I thought that was respect." / "You could still have asked what I call myself."). Tomas's counselling-register question exists to tee up Coralie's maxim. The narration between them explains why the question helps, using a triplet-fragment tic. This is the third statement of the same distinction in 30 lines.
- Fix: Keep Tomas asking something narrow, but in plainer and clumsier words of his own. Have Coralie answer with a concrete request instead of a principle, ideally one that sets up his question on the walk home (L339). Cut L281. Keep "And don't say it doesn't matter." (L287), adjusting its "And" if needed. Illustrative only: "Ask me one of the things you guessed about. On the way home, not in front of Julian."

### D3 · Major · Tomas's bird-demi-human confession reads as case notes

- Where: L199–L209, hall, after Coralie asks "Something like what?"
- Text:
> "Avian demi-human was my first guess. Then you told me one of your parents didn't have wings, so I thought maybe hybrid, or a transformation that settled. I knew those weren't exact."
- Problem: Uniform fluency under pressure. This is the prompt's signature line ("to be honest I thought she was some bird demi human or hybrid or something"). It comes at the moment Tomas is caught in front of his wife, yet it arrives in order, qualified and composed. It ends in a self-audit ("I knew those weren't exact") that mainly exists for Julian to repeat. The story says Tomas's polish is a defense against Julian (L101); here he is facing Coralie. The prompt's sheepish comedy is lost.
- Fix: Keep the content (avian demi-human first, then hybrid after learning one parent was wingless), but let the polish break: a hesitation, a restart, a hedge, fewer clauses. Julian's repeat at L209 should then echo whatever hedge Tomas actually uses. Illustrative only: "Bird. Avian—demi-human, I thought. Then you said one of your parents didn't have wings, so maybe a hybrid. Or something."

### D4 · Major · Narration interprets the confrontation line by line

- Where: L255–L311, the decisive exchange; also L157
- Text:
> There it was: not an accusation, exactly. The first true question, arriving late and carrying all the others behind it.
> Tomas absorbed this without reaching for it. His arguments had run out; she could see the discomfort of empty hands.
> He did not reach for another defense. That mattered.
> It did not make his ignorance smaller. That was the trouble.
- Problem: Glossed subtext. Nearly every key line in the confrontation is followed by a sentence telling the reader what it meant or why it mattered, which leaves the dialogue and the silences nothing to do. "Would you have answered?", Coralie's confession and Tomas finally sitting down all land without this help.
- Fix: Cut these glosses, or shrink each to a physical beat (where he looks, what his hands do). Keep L101 (Coralie's reading of Tomas's polish) and L197 (the "crowded workbench" image), because they characterize him rather than grade a line. L281 is handled under D2.

### D5 · Minor · Correction volleys and a register slip

- Where: L133–L137 (kitchen, Julian and Coralie); L225–L231 (hall, start of the confrontation)
- Text:
> "That is not the same thing."
> "So you picked an explanation and kept it."
> "I picked a possibility."
- Problem: Correction ping-pong. At L135, Julian, who is bursty and uses contractions everywhere else, speaks in the collection's precise, uncontracted register. At L227–L229, Coralie turns Tomas's "explain" into "explanation" and Tomas corrects her noun, so the decisive scene opens with a word-choice duel. L227 is also untagged with three people present, and Julian's jab follows at once, so a reader can briefly assign it to Julian.
- Fix: Give Julian's L135 his own push. Illustrative only: "That's not the same thing and you know it." Cut Tomas's "I picked a possibility." so Julian's "For seven years?" answers Coralie directly (see C1). Add a beat that ties L227 to Coralie.

### D6 · Minor · Dialogue re-explains what the guests already showed

- Where: L185–L193 (Tomas's defense); L247 (Coralie, after she names herself)
- Text:
> They smile because she notices them.
> I don't make people happy. I don't know who deserves what. I can't heal anyone
- Problem: Philosopher and exposition. Tomas has just said "She does things for people." (L185) and listed the particulars (L189), so "They smile because she notices them" states the conclusion a second time. At L247, Coralie runs a checklist of angel limits (happiness, judgment, healing) that nobody in the room has raised. It reads as the author fencing off lore rather than Coralie answering what Julian actually claimed.
- Fix: Cut the first sentence of L193 and start Tomas's run at the decaf detail. At L247 keep "It doesn't prove whatever Julian thinks he has proved", "I don't make people happy" (which answers Julian's smile claim) and the rice-pans line. Cut "I don't know who deserves what. I can't heal anyone".

### D7 · Minor · Did Tomas hear her say it?

- Where: L47–L65 and L97–L99, serving counter
- Text:
> "Julian, take the carrots." Tomas was passing with two chairs braced against his chest.
> "You probably heard her wrong."
> "Yes, Julian. I was here."
- Problem: Ground/knowledge stance. Right after Coralie says "I call myself an angel," Tomas passes close enough to speak. He then says "Know what?" and "You probably heard her wrong" as if he missed it. Later he answers "She called herself an angel" with "I was here," which reads as having witnessed it. The reader can't tell what Tomas heard.
- Fix: Decide what he heard. Simplest: keep him out of earshot at L47 (he arrives from across the hall as Julian says "You call yourself—"), and change L99 to a complaint about Julian repeating himself rather than a claim to have been there. Illustrative only: "I heard you the first time."

### D8 · Minor · Stiff or unclear phrasing at key statements

- Where: L95 (Tomas's wing argument), L243 (Coralie names herself), L349 (walk home)
- Text:
> People grow them, inherit them, build them and end up keeping them.
> Only then did she say, "Angel is the truthful word for me."
> "I can't give you a useful answer yet."
- Problem: At L95, "People have wings" reads as a claim about everyone, and "build them and end up keeping them" never resolves: wings built and then made permanent, or kept out of habit? "The truthful word" and "a useful answer" put the collection's precise, slightly legal register into Coralie's two most personal statements. Everywhere else she speaks plainly.
- Fix: Make it "Lots of people have wings" or similar, and clarify or drop "build them and end up keeping them". Keep "Her feathers don't come with a certificate": L101 marks that polish as Tomas's deliberate defense. Give Coralie plain wording at L243 and L349. Illustrative only: "I am an angel. That part's true."

### D9 · Minor · The wet floor and "the rack" have no visible source

- Where: L103–L107 and L145–L149, the towel errand
- Text:
> "Do you want the floor wet all night?" she asked.
> "Those go under the rack."
- Problem: Ground/referent (staging). No water has been shown. The only rack so far is the carrots' cooling rack (L51), which wouldn't drip, and a drying rack only appears later (L259). The deflection works socially, but the reader can't picture what is wet or which rack is meant.
- Fix: Add one clause of staging (washed pans dripping on a drying rack, for example), or name the rack at L145.

> Julian stopped with the carrots halfway to the rack.

## Continuity and prompt findings

### C1 · Minor · Seven years or ten?

- Where: L29 (narration); L231 (Julian's jab)
- Text:
> Ten years ago he had learned her clearance
> "For seven years?" Julian said.
- Problem: Tomas has known Coralie for at least ten years, and his guesses date from early on (L207). "Seven years" can only mean the marriage, which the story never dates. A reader who noticed "ten" will read it as an error.
- Fix: Align the numbers, or make Julian's jab name the marriage (seven years married).

### C2 · Minor · Cupboard doors carry opposite traits

- Where: L155 (narration); L193 (Tomas's list)
- Text:
> He caught cupboard doors before Coralie walked into them
> She notices when I leave cupboard doors open
- Problem: The narration praises Tomas for catching cupboard doors before she walks into them. Forty lines later he says he leaves them open and she slams them shut. Both could be true, but the same object carries opposite traits close together and reads as a slip.
- Fix: Change one of the two objects (drawers, the bread bin, lights left on).
