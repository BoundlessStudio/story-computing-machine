## Premise and story promise

**Premise:** After surviving 617 days in Ravel, thirteen-year-old Nell Mercado
deliberately severs a collapsing Glass Gate to stop the Hollow Regent's last
identity-thread from following her home, only to return four months before her
companion Bash is taken; while everyone treats him as a trauma-born invention,
she must preserve enough private evidence for him to find her without surrendering
the secrecy that kept them alive.

**Story promise:** A wonder-rich but emotionally grounded portal-fantasy reunion
story in which scars, an unmatched half-token, a bad-tempered mule, and a disputed
moonrise become evidence that two children really shared an impossible life. The
story honors survival without pretending reunion cures trauma. Its title image
does three jobs: it marks Nell and Bash's intimacy, supplies the anonymous search
post that crosses their separated dates, and names the memory no impostor can
fully counterfeit.

**Central dramatic question:** Can Nell move from solitary, defensive certainty
to chosen trust before the mismatch between the children's Earth dates convinces
her that Bash died—or convinces everyone else that he never existed?

**Meaningful change:** Nell begins believing that survival means withholding,
counting, and making the necessary choice alone. She ends by freely completing
her identity for Bash after he proves himself, accepting that the unresolved
future of the Gate must be a shared decision rather than another burden one child
assigns to the other.

## Story controls

- **POV:** Close third person, entirely through Nell. Bash's separate return is
  learned through his message and later account, not shown from his viewpoint.
- **Tense:** Past.
- **Tone:** Taut, wonder-rich, intimate, and dryly funny at the edges. Concrete
  sensory echoes should carry backstory more often than explanatory summary.
- **Genre:** Speculative fantasy with portal, temporal-dislocation, and recovery
  elements.
- **Length budget:** 3,600 words, acceptable range 3,000–4,000.
- **Audience/content:** Teen. Non-graphic scars, coercion, death memories, panic,
  and trauma responses are allowed. No sexual content, gratuitous gore, or
  romantic framing of Nell and Bash; their bond is familial.
- **Scope choice:** This is a complete short story about separation, search, and
  reunion, not a synopsis of the forty-chapter source. The 617-day Ravel history
  appears through the opening crisis and a few authenticated memory shards.
- **Reader orientation:** Label the opening `Day 617`, then use sparse date cues
  (`February 18`, `June 7`, `One year after Nell returned`). The date mismatch
  must feel surprising but retrospectively fair.

## Character engine

### Nell Mercado — protagonist

- **Beginning state:** Thirteen in her restored modern-like body, but carrying
  617 days of scars, skills, and memories. She trusts counts, exits, and withheld
  facts more than adults or comfort.
- **Concrete desire:** Find Bash and prove he survived their separation.
- **External stakes:** If her search disappears before Bash's own departure date,
  he will return alone with no way to locate the only person who can authenticate
  Ravel. A public search also risks attracting opportunists who can exploit her.
- **Internal stakes:** If Bash is dead or invented, Nell loses the only witness to
  the person she became; if she gives strangers too much identifying information,
  she repeats the Regent's logic that a complete identity is a handle.
- **Blind spot:** She mistakes unilateral control for protection. Her Gate choice
  was necessary under pressure, but she generalizes that emergency logic into
  every relationship afterward.
- **Movement:** Guarded certainty -> disciplined hope -> consent-based trust. She
  never becomes carefree and never concedes that adults were right to use the
  children; she learns only that secrecy can be offered rather than merely kept.

### Sebastian “Bash” Wren — relational counterforce

- Twelve in his restored body, vain about competence, good with patterns, bad at
  reading Nell's defensive silences, and still inclined to believe enough study
  can repair a system.
- His desire is to find Nell and eventually preserve the possibility of helping
  people left in Ravel. This genuinely conflicts with Nell's wish to ensure no
  living power can use the Gate to take them again.
- He proves identity through useless, affectionate particulars rather than public
  biography. His answer about Saint and the scarf validates not just a fact but
  the way he and Nell remember one another.
- Their bond is explicitly sibling-like/familial, never romantic.

### Supporting cast and pressure

- **Celia:** Nell's mother. She believes Nell's distress and scars are real but
  cannot verify Ravel or Bash. Her pressure to close the dangerous blog comes
  from protective fear, not cruelty.
- **Trenn:** The deceased road guide who taught the children to offer one usable
  name and no more. His copper gaming token and flawed attempt at atonement survive
  him. This is the collision-safe replacement for the input guide called Oren.
- **Saint:** The gray mule whose attack on a customs priest supplies the private
  authentication answer. He remains in Ravel with Tavra.
- **Tavra:** The young Ravel ally entrusted with Saint when the children cross.
- **The Hollow Regent:** A living, unstable antagonist held together by stolen
  identities until the resistance breaks the primary ledger. Only his remnant
  identity-thread appears in the opening; the ledger and Gate never act as living
  minds.
- **Willa Corst:** A Ravel resident whose three-line identity is recited by a
  compelled house in one memory shard. This replaces the input example Mara Osset
  and demonstrates the system without preserving that collision.
- **Indexer Prax:** The Regent official remembered offering Nell passage home for
  Bash's missing identity lines. This replaces the input official Pell and makes
  Nell's refusal to publish all her clues emotionally specific.

## Causal arc

1. **Beginning state:** On Day 617, Nell and Bash have helped the living resistance
   break the Hollow Regent's ledger and reach a cracking Glass Gate. They share
   nearly everything except surnames, exact origins, and Earth departure dates.
2. **Inciting disruption:** A surviving thread of the Regent catches Bash as the
   Gate opens. Nell uses the severance phrase, knowingly sacrificing their joined
   crossing to prevent the remnant from riding his anchor. She returns alone.
3. **First turn:** The supermarket calendar still says February 18 and only eleven
   seconds have passed. No record of Bash matching her clues exists. Nell decides
   he is missing rather than dead and builds an anonymous search post.
4. **Escalation:** Public clues draw false claimants. An impostor knows searchable
   details but fails the private Marn's Bridge question. Celia urges Nell to close
   the post after harassment, while doctors frame Bash as a trauma construct.
5. **Midpoint reframe:** Nell realizes that complete disclosure would reproduce
   the Regent's weapon. She removes identifying clues, leaves only the title image
   and a private challenge, and chooses to preserve a narrow route for Bash rather
   than prove Ravel to everyone.
6. **Second turn:** On June 7, a message gives the impossible scarf answer. Bash
   reports that he disappeared that morning and found a four-month-old post. The
   Gate returned each child to a separate private temporal anchor.
7. **Crisis:** At 30th Street Station, proof of survival does not erase their
   conflict. Bash is furious that Nell severed the Gate without him; Nell fears he
   will turn their reunion into another mission. She can keep her final identity
   lines private and preserve control, or give them freely and make him a trusted
   equal again.
8. **Climax:** After matched copper halves, scars, and their tap signal establish
   who he is, Nell tells him `Nell Mercado` and names Philadelphia as the home she
   recognizes. Bash answers with `Sebastian Wren` and his own exact home. The
   exchange is mutual and voluntary; no Ravel mechanism is present to exploit it.
   They agree that if a route ever appears, neither will decide alone.
9. **Aftermath:** A year after Nell's return, the children enter aisle nine
   together. Trauma habits persist, therapy remains work, and their Gate dispute
   is unresolved, but Nell lets Bash share the exit count. They choose a frozen
   pizza, argue about pepperoni, and walk through ordinary automatic doors.

## Canon constraints to track

| Constraint | Planning application |
| --- | --- |
| Ravel and the modern-like setting are eras/cultural names for one physical world. | Never call Ravel a planet, plane, dimension, alternate universe, or separate world. Characters may say “home” and “Ravel”; narration avoids cosmological claims. |
| Ravel's chronological position is unresolved. | The prose gives no calendar conversion, ancient/future label, historical sequence, or implication that modern people descend from Ravel. |
| The Glass Gate is one local crossing system. | All crossing, body-restoration, object-transfer, and temporal-anchor behavior is explicitly Gate-local. No network or general portal taxonomy appears. |
| Identity binding is bounded, not universal. | The given-name/family-name/origin system belongs to the Regent's Ravel infrastructure. The children's voluntary station exchange has emotional meaning but no magical effect. |
| Only life uses magic. | The Hollow Regent and living resistance members exert magic. Travelers' living memories supply temporal anchors. The Gate, ledgers, houses, jars, and token only store, focus, transmit, or shape that input; narration never gives them independent intention. |

## Proposed inventions for the canon delta

These remain proposals until promotion. The final editor should record each
disposition separately rather than presenting the list as established lore.

### Reusable proposals

1. Ravel's sky has a small blue moon that rises before a larger gold moon on
   the witnessed nights; origin, fate, and chronological implications remain
   unknown.
2. The Glass Gate can bind a traveler to a private remembered departure and
   return different travelers to different modern-like dates from one shared
   Ravel crossing.
3. For this Gate, return reconstructs departure age, body size, and clothing
   while retaining healed Ravel scars and small objects held, worn, or bound at
   crossing.
4. The Hollow Regent's bounded identity infrastructure uses given name, family
   name, and recognized home-origin as three distinct lines. Living users can
   search with partial lines; all three enable deeper coercion.
5. Ravel resistance practice includes road names and deliberate withholding as
   a defense against that infrastructure.
6. The Gate can store and shape magic supplied by living travelers and living
   operators; it is not itself established as alive.

### Story-local proposals

1. Nell and Bash share 617 Ravel days, the Day 617 ledger assault, and the
   severed crossing described here.
2. Trenn's failed atonement, death, copper gaming token, and one-word safety rule.
3. Saint's border attack, Tavra's guardianship of him, Drosswall memories, the
   ash-hound scars, and the identity jars beneath Marn's Bridge.
4. The Hollow Regent, Indexer Prax, Willa Corst, the compelled houses, Gate Cant
   severance phrase, and resistance assault as this story's local people and
   events.
5. Nell's anonymous post, four-month search, 30th Street reunion, mutual identity
   exchange, and one-year supermarket aftermath.

## Failure modes to watch

1. **Novel-summary syndrome:** Do not enumerate all 617 days, six languages, or
   every Ravel location. Each memory must either authenticate Bash, explain
   Nell's search choice, or sharpen the reunion conflict.
2. **Passive protagonist:** The severance, narrowed post, mutual identity exchange,
   and supermarket entry must all be Nell's consequential choices.
3. **Mechanical Gate agency:** Avoid phrases such as “the Gate chose,” “the ledger
   wanted,” or “the house knew” without immediately locating living input. A
   compelled house recites a stored record because the Regent's living system
   drives it.
4. **System conflation:** Identity lines never determine return dates. Temporal
   anchors never grant identity coercion. The severance phrase cuts a path; it
   does not select February or June.
5. **Knowledge leakage:** Nell cannot search `Wren` or Bash's exact town before
   Scene 5; Bash cannot search `Mercado` or Philadelphia from Nell's disclosed
   clues. Neither child gives those lines in public messages.
6. **Chronology claim:** Do not label Ravel ancient, future, another planet, a
   dimension, or a separate world. The second moon cannot be used to date Ravel.
7. **Trauma as proof or cure:** Scars and habits support authentication but do not
   make suffering noble. Reunion and therapy do not eliminate hypervigilance,
   class tension, grief, or disagreement.
8. **Romantic drift:** Keep physical reassurance, arguments, and intimacy
   sibling-like. No attraction beats or romantic future implication.
9. **Unseeded climax power:** Nell already rehearsed the bounded Gate Cant severance
   phrase; it must not do more than sever. Her emotional climax is disclosure,
   not a new magical ability.
10. **Name regression:** Drafting must not reintroduce `Oren`, `Mara Osset`,
    `Mara`, or `Pell` for this story. Use only `Trenn`, `Willa Corst` / `Willa`,
    and `Indexer Prax` / `Prax`, after coordinator registration.
11. **Overstated canon:** Treat every proposed mechanic, moon, person, event, and
    place as local story material until the normal review and promotion workflow
    approves a delta disposition.
