# Outline

## Story

- Premise and central promise: On an autumn sheep drive, an unnamed young adult shepherd pauses at a spring where a fae unbinds their old name and coos the prompt's required words verbatim. Fog then separates the shepherd from their teenage sister, whose magical name-call now points toward the fae. The shepherd must find her without that convenience and freely choose the new working name **Sedge**. Make name magic overrated as an account of personhood, yet let the lost word matter as their dead father's ordinary gift.
- Focal pressure or attachment: The shepherd wants their sister and flock below wet scree before cold rain. They also want the stolen name back for its family use, not because it contains them. Having mocked name-calls as a crutch, they first chase the fae instead of trusting an imperfect whistle and their own landcraft.
- Counterforce or complication: The curious, careless fae treats names as replaceable sounds. While consciously carrying the detached binding, it moves through fog and redirects the sister's calls without understanding the route danger. Weather, scattered ewes, bad footing, and the shepherd's divided priorities provide pressure. The sister retains agency by staying with a ewe and lamb rather than following a misleading call.
- POV, distance, and information limit: Close first-person past, selecting wind, footing, wool, breath, tracks, and sound direction. Refer to the lost word as the old name without making its absence a riddle. The shepherd cannot know the fae's mind, the sister's exact position, or any universal rule.
- Governing movement and time shape: Two hours in a tightening circle around a dry-stone sheepfold. Three diverted calls tempt pursuit while ordinary traces narrow the sister's route elsewhere. Slow for the wet-scree crossing, then return to the fold for voluntary naming and a modest test. End during resumed flock sorting, not with recovery, public proof, or an identity maxim.
- Speculative rule or ordinary-world constraint: Locally, a living caller who knows someone may speak that person's active call-name to send a voice in the correct direction through fog or wind, within roughly a mile; the living recipient can answer. It cannot compel, pinpoint, judge identity, or cross unlimited distance. The living fae can detach and redirect one binding only while actively carrying it. Names and objects never act. A new binding forms when its chooser freely speaks before a living witness who knows them and that witness deliberately calls it back. Only this directional affordance changes; memory, recognition, kinship, personality, skills, records, property, and ordinary speech remain intact.

## Voice

- Narrative texture: First-person narration triangulates through weather, footing, wool, breath, and sound before interpretation. Keep wit dry and feeling embedded in use; the missing old name never forces coy syntax.
- Conversational texture: The fae coos and digresses through sensory distinctions. The shepherd asks measurable, action-linked questions. The sister's ordinary, breath-broken talk concerns animals, footing, and blame.
- Rhetorical ownership: The fae owns aesthetic comparison and slippery replaceability claims; the shepherd owns route logic and family use; the sister owns concrete risk judgments.
- Pressure behavior: The shepherd grows clipped, then listens better. The fluent fae elaborates when denied attention. Exertion breaks the sister's sentences without weakening her decisions.
- Anti-default: Avoid a clever bargain duel, naming bureaucracy, universal identity lecture, or polished quote-card exchange. Let pastoral work interrupt mischief and end the story without explaining it.

## Beats

1. At the spring, the fae detaches the call-binding and coos the prompt's required sentences verbatim. The shepherd remembers everything and expects inconvenience until their sister's distant call bends toward the fae.
2. Ordinary speech, a whistle, written marks, and another diverted call establish what remains. The fae proposes names for their sounds and textures; the shepherd demands the old one and pursues it around the fold.
3. Calls suggest the sister is uphill, but hoof marks, snagged wool, and the flock dog's attention point down. The shepherd recognizes that the moving fae—not the name itself—is redirecting her.
4. The fae explains only the local distinction between a call-handle and a person. A worn object and the shepherd's refusal to discard the old word carry the father's importance. A broken whistle below ends the argument.
5. The shepherd follows animal traces to the sister guarding a ewe and lamb near unstable scree. The sisters cross by rope, body position, the dog's training, and short whistles while the fae's fluent commentary goes unanswered.
6. At the fold, the shepherd chooses **Sedge** without calling it a truer self. The sister calls it back, creating the binding; the fae keeps the old one, leaving recovery outside this story. A practical warning during flock sorting modestly tests the new call before work continues.

## Continuity

- Canon used: Names are normally symbolic; literal name-power is a rare local binding, and only living agents use magic. No recurring people or places.
- Boundaries and unknowns: Keep the call and fae ability local; do not connect them to Ravel, Deepmarket, Calling the Name, souls, universal consent, or autonomous tokens. The fae's origin, wider powers, and possible restoration of the old binding remain unknown. Choosing Sedge changes no memory, relationship, identity, property, or responsibility.
