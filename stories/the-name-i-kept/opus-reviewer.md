# Opus review: The Name I Kept

Independent audit for a later editing pass. It is not a `review.md` verdict,
does not change canon state, and does not authorize edits by itself.

## Verdict

- Dialogue: REVISE — the voices are distinct, but three hinge exchanges (the kept name, the release condition, the early sparring) rest on loose logic or won-point closers.
- Prose: PASS — concrete and controlled; only minor triad cadence and two knotted sentences.
- Continuity and prompt: REVISE — the prompt is delivered, but the transfer log the climax depends on has no stated route into a buffer that sat in a suit lining, and "tonight" points two ways.
- Edit scope: targeted-dialogue
- Existing review.md said: Verdict PASS; "Dialogue: PASS"; disagree: it missed the loose hinges and the closer rhythm.

This dialogue works better than the collection's usual pattern. Auden and Mica sound different without tags. Auden is terse, practical and redirects through chores. Mica rushes, justifies, breaks off mid-sentence and says "Mother" and "Father". The parents speak in caption-ready soundbites, which suits them. There are two problems. In the first half, Auden wins nearly every exchange with a neat closer ("You just tried to peel one.", "That wasn't the question.", "The medic was right."), so a person the narration calls exhausted comes across as a quick-draw wit. And the lines that carry the story's biggest turns don't quite mean what they need to: the reason Auden kept the surname, Auden's condition for releasing the clip, and the evidence that the parents knew. The edit pass should cut about half of Auden's won points, let some exchanges end unfinished or on an action, and make each hinge literal and single-step. It must not sand down Auden's precision in the statement-writing scene, where that precision is the point.

Mica is fed up with the parents calling Auden a failure and a disgrace, and both words must stay in the parents' mouths (L14–L18, L48, L362) and in Mica's refusal (L392).

## Dialogue findings

### D1 · Major · Auden wins every early exchange

- Where: L24–L98 (dryer door, wrist check), L142 and L172 (bag argument), L216–L220 (cutting the clip)
- Text:
> "You just tried to peel one."
> "That wasn't the question."
> "The medic was right."
> "Then why is there a bag in your car?"
> "You said the whole buffer."
> "I meant the whole proof. Tell me where to stop it."
- Problem: Correction ping-pong and last-word closers are the default rhythm (Tennis Match, Punchline Patch). "I know how dryers work" exists to set up "peel", and "peel" does not fit opening a door. "It's locked during the cycle" makes Auden's physical catch unnecessary, and laundromat dryers don't lock anyway. "You sound like the medic" sets up "The medic was right." "You said the whole buffer" / "I meant the whole proof" is a quarrel over wording. The second bag line (L172) repeats the L142 trump instead of answering "You're not listening", and the buffer reveal that follows doesn't answer why a bag is packed. Taken together these make Auden sharp and winning when the narration calls them tired (L64). The outline's own anti-default warns against this.
- Fix: Cut or blunt these closers and leave each exchange on an action, a silence or an unanswered line. Give Mica's reach at L22 a clear aim (stopping the knock) and drop the "locked"/"peel" pair. Cut "The medic was right" and let "You sound like the medic" go unanswered while Auden tightens the knot. Cut L218–L220 and let Mica offer to cut the ending unprompted. Replace L172 with a question the buffer actually answers. Illustrative only: "Then what did you come here for?" Keep the pointed lines that carry new pressure: "The television is six feet away, Mica.", "So Crownfire wrapped it.", "Bold room.", "Pass the powder."

### D2 · Major · The kept-name answer does not reach the name

- Where: L312–L328, laundromat, after the release form
- Text:
> "You could change it."
> "I could change my boots. These pinch too."
> "My phone still sorted us together under Serrin. I left it that way."
- Problem: This beat explains the title, and its logic doesn't connect (Ground/referent). Mica asks why Auden keeps the surname. Auden answers with how a phone grouped contacts, but a contact sort has nothing to do with the name on Auden's paychecks. "us" and "it" (the contact, the phone, the name?) don't settle. The reader gets the feeling but has to invent the mechanism.
- Fix: Keep the boots deflection and Mica's "Auden." that pushes past it; that is a good turn. Replace the sorting line with one concrete, literal tie between the surname and Mica's six messages, then keep "You never called back." / "I listened." unchanged. Illustrative only: "You left six messages that week. Every one started 'Auden Serrin, pick up.' I saved them."

### D3 · Major · Release condition contradicts itself, then re-explains

- Where: L332–L338, end of the laundromat scene
- Text:
> "If you say your part. Not mine."
> "What does that mean?"
> "Don't call me innocent. Don't say I had no hand in it. Tell them where you were and what you saw."
> "Then write that. And write what I did."
- Problem: At L294 Auden made naming "what I did" the condition for using the recording, and Mica's broadcast statement does name it (L384). "Not mine" now appears to forbid exactly that. Mica's "What does that mean?" exists only to cue a three-part explanation (Explanatory chorus), and the explanation restates what the statement-writing already showed at L298–L304 (Philosopher). The scene's closing condition is therefore both muddled and redundant.
- Fix: Cut "What does that mean?" and the triad. Give Auden one plain condition consistent with L294 that points at the statement they just wrote, then go straight to Mica's "And after?". Illustrative only: "Say what's on the page. Don't make me sound better."

### D4 · Minor · "Before the whites" has no literal sense

- Where: L54–L58, laundromat, first report of the broadcast
- Text:
> "Blue towel in there?"
> "I need it before the whites."
- Problem: The towel is clean laundry from the sack, so "before the whites" means nothing (Ground). Deflection is right here, but the errand has to make literal sense, because it is what makes Mica dig and hurt the wrist.
- Fix: Give Auden a plain request that fits clean laundry. Illustrative only: "Dig it out, would you?"

### D5 · Minor · Referents the reader cannot fill

- Where: L124 (broadcast rundown), L150 (hearing), L280 (ambulance speech)
- Text:
> "They put the question in tonight's rundown," you say.
> I was concussed at the hearing.
> as if hearing me say that changes where you have to wake up
> It doesn't give you the hearing back.
- Problem: "the question" has no antecedent until L360. "concussed at the hearing" reads as being hurt at the hearing. "say that" doesn't point to anything Mica has said. "give you the hearing back" can be read as the sense of hearing, and even read as the board hearing it doesn't say what was lost (Ground/referent).
- Fix: Name the question ("the question about you"). Use "still concussed during the hearing". Point "that" at something specific, or cut the clause. Replace the hearing line with something concrete (reopening the finding, the crest, the team), or end the run one sentence earlier on "It doesn't—".

### D6 · Minor · Attribution slips in the bag argument

- Where: L138–L140 and L152–L154, laundromat
- Text:
> They turn the trousers and start on the other side.
> I didn't ask to move in with you.
> The toothbrush keeps moving. "I know how old you were."
> "And after?" they ask.
- Problem: An untagged line right after an Auden action beat reads first as Auden's (Attribution). Two consecutive Auden lines split into separate paragraphs look like a change of speaker until the tag corrects it.
- Fix: Put a Mica beat or tag on L140. Merge L152 and L154 into one Auden paragraph, or put a beat between them showing the pause.

### D7 · Minor · Auden asks what Auden already knows

- Where: L206–L210, laundromat, deciding about the clip
- Text:
> "How much afterward?" Auden asks.
> They know the suit pieces better than you do.
- Problem: Auden knows the buffer's six-second span better than Mica, so the question exists only to cue "Six seconds" for the reader (Ground/knowledge, setup-only line).
- Fix: Cut L206–L208 and let Auden ask "Does it show me beside him?" straight away; the later nod and L214 carry the rest.

## Prose findings

> Auden allows three seconds of video. No impact. No body. No ambulance.

### P2 · Minor · Two knotted sentences

- Where: L74 (family photographs), L182 (buffer exposition)
- Text:
> whose face strangers now cover with their thumbs
> Rampart used to check every latch before a family deployment because Palisade believed inspection looked nervous on camera.
- Problem: It's unclear who the strangers are or why they hold family photographs. The "because" clause reads as though Palisade's belief made Rampart inspect, and the missing step is that Palisade would not be seen inspecting.
- Fix: Say where the photographs are (posters, the team site) or cut the image. Add the missing step: Palisade wouldn't be seen checking, so Rampart did.

## Continuity and prompt findings

### C1 · Major · The log that convicts the parents has no provenance

- Where: L272–L274 (third viewing), L366–L370 (broadcast), L392
- Text:
> This is the local original.
> Two devices opened the full upload at three fourteen the next morning.
> They heard you warn him, and four hours later the board got the damaged copy.
> "You watched the buffer," you say.
- Problem: The ceramic square was shed into Mica's suit lining at impact and found six months later, yet it holds a log of who opened a separate upload the next morning. The story gives no route for that data to reach it. The log shows "opened", and Mica turns that into "saw", "heard", "watched" and "you knew", on live television in a story whose ethic is to state only what the evidence establishes (L298). The climax's accusation rests on both points. The LOCKED "Continuity and evidence discipline" entry puts provenance ahead of convenience.
- Fix: Add one clause giving the log a path, for example the suit docking at the family house that night and writing its relay receipts back into the buffer, or the log coming from the family account the phone reaches rather than from the ceramic. Make the log show playback ("played to the end") so "watched" is earned, or have Mica say "opened" on air.

### C2 · Minor · "Tonight" points two ways

- Where: L88, L110, L124, L156, L332; resolved only at L348–L356
- Text:
> We have the memorial broadcast tonight.
> "Tonight," you add. "At the house.
> Instead you ask, "If I say this tonight, will you release the clip?"
> You put everything back before dawn.
> At six that evening, your mother fastens the family crest
- Problem: In one conversation "tonight" means both the evening just past (L110, L156) and the broadcast still to come (L88, L124, L332). The reader first places the broadcast later the same night and has to recalibrate at L348.
- Fix: Anchor the laundromat hour once (a clock after midnight) and use different words for the two evenings, for example "after the taping" for the house and "tomorrow" or "at six" for the broadcast.

### C3 · Minor · Six seconds holds too much

- Where: L182, L206–L214, L254–L264
- Text:
> The ceramic square records six seconds on either side of a disabling impact
> The clip ends with Auden on their knees beside Shearpoint.
> whatever the forward lens can see
- Problem: After the carriage strikes the field, six seconds must hold the fourth pulse, the return, Shearpoint's fall, and Auden crossing to kneel at the far end of the rail while supporting the train. Auden is never placed physically, so the kneel isn't possible as staged. "forward lens" (L182) and "collar lens" (L298) name the same camera differently.
- Fix: Widen the window (for example twenty seconds), or place Auden on the rail near Shearpoint in one clause. Use one name for the lens.

### C4 · Minor · Hand staging

- Where: L288 (statement), L358–L400 (broadcast)
- Text:
> They cross it out with one hard stroke of your finger.
> On set, Palisade takes your uninjured hand. Crownfire takes the one bound in a proper brace.
> Your mother reaches for the crest. You cover it, and pain shoots through your wrist despite the brace.
- Problem: At L288 Auden strikes the line with Mica's finger, which reads as a slip. On set both of Mica's hands are held, yet Mica touches the wrist relay pad and covers the crest, and the father unfastens the clasp, all before the mother lets go at L400.
- Fix: At L288, write "their finger" or show Auden guiding Mica's hand. On set, free one hand on the page before L376 (Mica pulls the braced hand out of the mother's grip, or the father's grip breaks), and keep L400 consistent.

### C5 · Minor · Plumbing failure as texture

- Where: L44, laundry sack backstory
- Text:
> washed in the machines at the family house because the laundromat's cold-water line had failed
- Problem: This is incidental water-supply failure used as default texture, which the style guide's "No default water infrastructure" entry rules out for authorized revisions. The prompt doesn't call for it.
- Fix: Give a reason that isn't plumbing, such as Auden being short of quarters or Mica offering, or drop the explanation.
