# Outline

## Story

- Premise and central promise: Younger hero Ossia Vardel meets disowned older sibling Kerin above a repair shop. At midnight, their parents will disable and reclaim Kerin's cadence band; Kerin needs Ossia to remove its family controller. The work forces talk about Kerin killing Stillmark. Fulfill the promise when Ossia finishes the conversion, leaves Kerin in control, and joins its first safe test—not merely when she defends Kerin.
- Focal pressure or attachment: Kerin's only confidant, Ossia hates the parents' language but has kept their relationship secret. She wants Kerin safe without losing her family rescue role. Kerin wants a regulator and Ossia spared consequences; shame makes help feel harmful. Ossia must separate Kerin's tactical fault from the forced killing.
- Counterforce or complication: Kerin ignored a recall and pursued Stillmark alone, a genuine failure. They reject the invented nonlethal option yet would surrender the band to protect Ossia's standing. The live conversion, midnight lock, parent call, self-erasure, dependence, and unsafe calibration force choices without a family showdown.
- POV, distance, and information limit: Close first-person past through Ossia, attentive to cycles, tools, breath, and gaps between Kerin's words and body. She knows the record and saw the captives afterward but missed the killing; Kerin supplies its final seconds while working. The parents' future judgment remains unknown.
- Governing movement and time shape: One late evening moves from food and inspection through disassembly and calibration to a one-stop tram test, with midnight as deadline. Each stage changes the practical choice. End during the bounded test, family status unresolved and no speech defining heroism.
- Speculative rule or ordinary-world constraint: Vardels can arrest one perceived periodic process while holding one breath. Ossia best isolates mechanical and electrical cycles; Kerin isolates biological rhythms. Exhalation releases an intact process, but cannot repair it; a heart held beyond its restart window stays stopped. Stillmark linked eight captives to his heartbeat, making his next beat discharge lethal current through them. His barrier prevented contact, unconsciousness would not sever the link, and backup could not arrive in one beat. Kerin stopped his heart until the link died. The lone pursuit was avoidable; the terminal act was not. The cadence band offers an irregular tactile reflex target. Replacing its controller is possible, but a bad pattern distracts or exhausts the wearer.

## Voice

- Narrative texture: Ossia tracks cycles, tool contact, and Kerin's breathing; anger broadens her attention until hazard snaps it narrow. Technical noticing should expose divided loyalty without mechanizing every feeling.
- Conversational texture: Talk moves among repair directions, unfinished family business, and food or transit logistics. Machinery masks words, questions require repetition, and neither sibling answers every subject first raised.
- Rhetorical ownership: Ossia reasons through sequence and discrepancy, accumulating examples when angry. Kerin owns bodily timing and tactical counterfactuals, using precision responsibly and as self-punishment. The parent uses administrative categories and duty.
- Pressure behavior: Ossia begins rehearsed and overfull, then turns blunt. Kerin becomes procedural, misses emotional bids, and goes silent around need; dangerous calibration makes both literal. The parent grows formal at resistance.
- Anti-default: Avoid cape quips, balanced therapy confessions, a flawless martyr Kerin, or a polished final exchange making one forced death a universal definition of heroism.

## Beats

1. Ossia arrives with food, tools, and a concealed order to recover Kerin's band. Kerin has packed and treats the visit as a service call. Small logistics show intimacy and Ossia's refusal to use family labels instead of Kerin's name.
2. The lock will freeze the band at midnight. Kerin proposes surrender; Ossia starts conversion before admitting her order. Kerin withdraws cooperation, but exposed live contacts make withdrawal consequential.
3. Calibration requires Kerin to identify what their power caught during Stillmark's last seconds. Measurements fragment the account. Kerin owns the missed recall and pursuit, then gives the one-beat causality; Ossia tests alternatives against known limits rather than affection.
4. A parent calls to confirm recovery, invokes Ossia's future, and repeats failure and disgrace without Kerin's name. Ossia seeks no conversion: she states the equipment decision, ends the remote session, and arrests the controller oscillator while Kerin seats the independent component.
5. The first pattern fails: Kerin reflexively catches Ossia's heartbeat and releases it within one breath. Kerin wants to stop. Ossia recalibrates from the error, requiring trust without erasing danger or history.
6. On a one-stop tram test, Ossia misses family patrol while Kerin chooses the band's pulse over surrounding bodies. Stop after it survives a jolt and Kerin makes the next travel decision; the parents remain unmoved and Ossia's allegiance has material consequence.
