# Outline

## Story

- Central promise: In second-person present, you meet your disowned older sibling after your superhero parents again call them a failure and disgrace in public. The private conversation must make you stop treating secret sympathy as sufficient and choose a concrete form of loyalty that does not take your sibling's agency away.
- Character want and pressure: You want to be the one safe person Auden still has while keeping your place in the Serrin family. You witnessed Auden kill Shearpoint to prevent a passenger train from falling, and your parents' demand for family unity helped keep you silent; every new insult therefore turns comfort into complicity. Auden needs you, but refuses to become the excuse for you to destroy your own life.
- Turn: Auden makes you confront the pattern in which you bring private sympathy after each public injury, then insists on controlling what the recovered evidence may disclose. When the shared Serrin name surfaces as the quiet connection Auden kept, you stop offering dramatic private renunciation and choose accountable public witness instead.
- Ending intent: You correct your parents during their next live appearance, state the lethal constraint plainly, and refuse their words for Auden even when they strip you of the family crest. Rehabilitation is not instant and the killing retains moral weight. You return to the laundromat under the cameras' gaze and help Auden free the same trousers from the washer, restoring ordinary sibling action without pretending the family or public verdict is repaired.
- Drafting shape: 2,500–4,000 words; emotionally close second-person present; a mostly two-person conversation with a brief public coda; non-graphic violence recalled through concrete choices and aftermath.

## Voice

- Narrative texture: Close second-person present notices laundry mechanics, camera behavior, injuries, and the physical evidence of choices; emotion arrives through what Mica mishandles or cannot put down.
- Conversational texture: Practical laundry talk carries affection and avoidance. Mica rushes into explanations and misses questions under pressure; Auden answers late, redirects through work, or leaves gaps.
- Rhetorical ownership: Mica owns impulsive accumulations and the factual public testimony. Auden owns dry correction and the minimum moral precision needed to retain agency. The parents own polished brand language.
- Pressure behavior: Mica grows verbose and defensive; Auden grows quieter, works harder, and becomes direct only when consent or responsibility is threatened.
- Anti-default: Avoid balanced thesis-and-counterthesis, flawless sibling therapy, or making every dry reply a polished aphorism.

## Beats

1. After Crownfire and Palisade repeat “failure” and “disgrace” in a televised family interview, you bring Auden's clean laundry to the all-night laundromat where they will still meet you. Auden notices you are angrier than usual but tests whether you came to listen or merely to discharge guilt.
2. Small practical rituals establish the surviving bond: you fold badly, Auden checks an injury you conceal from your parents, and neither uses hero patter. You report the latest insult; Auden's exhausted lack of surprise hurts more than anger would.
3. You insist you defended Auden at home and offer to quit the family team tonight. Auden rejects the grand gesture because it would make them responsible for another exile, exposing how your private visits have comforted you without changing what they endure.
4. The argument returns to Shearpoint's death. In a tight, non-graphic reconstruction, Auden was sustaining the only barrier beneath a sabotaged passenger train while Shearpoint continued collapsing its support. Their sole reachable counterstroke redirected the force through him; releasing the barrier, delaying, or aiming away would have dropped the train. You confirm what you saw and that your parents opened the intact evidence before supporting the cleaner public lie that a “real hero” would have found another way.
5. Auden confronts the pattern of your locked-room defense without turning the exchange into absolution. They talk only to you because you remember the whole choice, yet your silence outside this room has allowed the family verdict to stand.
6. Asked why they still use Serrin, Auden reveals that their phone kept grouping the siblings under the shared name even after exile; they listened to the messages they could not answer. The connection turns your anger from punishing your parents toward public responsibility.
7. You reveal that your suit retained the incident timing and your own voice recording. Rather than broadcast it in anger, you accept Auden's authority over the release. Together you choose your firsthand statement and the decisive seconds of telemetry, with no spectacle of the body and no claim that killing was easy or clean.
8. In a short coda at the family's next live appearance, Crownfire again calls Auden a disgrace and you interrupt. You state the constrained choice, acknowledge its cost, and say Auden saved the train and saved you. Palisade removes your crest and cuts your feed, but the correction has already gone out.
9. Auden summons you back with a photograph of the jammed washer. You return in the uniform with two holes over the heart, leave the cameras outside, and help free the wet trousers. End on ordinary shared work performed in public view, not acquittal, family reconciliation, or a slogan.

## Continuity

- Canon used: Only the foundational shared-world allowance for independent entry points and the established importance of witnesses, material evidence, moral choice, and the fact that truth does not automatically produce justice. No recurring person, place, faction, power system, or historical event is used.
- Boundaries and unknowns: Keep the era and city unnamed. The Serrins' abilities, family brand, public hero culture, no-kill expectation, and Shearpoint incident are story-local; establish no universal inheritance rule, power taxonomy, hero authority, or connection to Solstice Evening Bell, the Northbridge trio, `ultrahuman` classification, or any other system. Names have ordinary social meaning only. Leave power origins, wider institutions, jurisdiction, and later public consequences unresolved.
- Moral boundary: “No choice” means no viable action in the witnessed seconds that would save the passengers and spare Shearpoint; it does not erase Auden's grief, responsibility, or the human cost of killing. Do not let the parents' branding dispute replace that consequence.
- Drafting authority: The prompt remains the acceptance authority; this outline is advisory scene guidance.
