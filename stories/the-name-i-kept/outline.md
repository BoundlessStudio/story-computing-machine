# Outline

## Story

- Central promise: In second-person present, you meet your disowned older sibling after your superhero parents again call them a failure and disgrace in public. The private conversation must make you stop treating secret sympathy as sufficient and choose a concrete form of loyalty that does not take your sibling's agency away.
- Character want and pressure: You want to be the one safe person Veyra still has while keeping your place in the Serrin family. You witnessed Veyra kill Shearpoint to prevent a passenger train from falling, and your parents' demand for family unity helped keep you silent; every new insult therefore turns comfort into complicity. Veyra needs you, but refuses to become the excuse for you to destroy your own life.
- Turn: Veyra tells you that being defended only in a locked room leaves them alone everywhere that costs something. When they admit they kept the Serrin name because it is also yours, you stop offering a dramatic private renunciation and instead offer the evidence and eyewitness statement you should have offered earlier, with Veyra deciding what may be disclosed.
- Ending intent: You correct your parents during their next live appearance, state the lethal constraint plainly, and refuse their words for Veyra even when they strip you of the family crest. Rehabilitation is not instant and the killing retains moral weight, but Veyra is no longer the family's hidden disgrace or your secret. You sign with the shared surname neither parent gets to grant or revoke, and Veyra answers your call.
- Drafting shape: 2,500–4,000 words; emotionally close second-person present; a mostly two-person conversation with a brief public coda; non-graphic violence recalled through concrete choices and aftermath.

## Beats

1. After Crownfire and Palisade repeat “failure” and “disgrace” in a televised family interview, you bring Veyra's clean laundry to the all-night laundromat where they will still meet you. Veyra notices you are angrier than usual but tests whether you came to listen or merely to discharge guilt.
2. Small practical rituals establish the surviving bond: you sort clothes the way Veyra taught you, they check an old injury you conceal from your parents, and neither uses hero patter. You report the latest insult; Veyra's exhausted lack of surprise hurts more than anger would.
3. You insist you defended Veyra at home and offer to quit the family team tonight. Veyra rejects the grand gesture because it would let you make them responsible for another exile, exposing how your private visits have comforted you without changing what they endure.
4. The argument returns to Shearpoint's death. In a tight, non-graphic reconstruction, Veyra was sustaining the only barrier beneath a sabotaged passenger train while Shearpoint continued collapsing its support. Their sole reachable counterstroke redirected the force through him; releasing the barrier, delaying, or aiming away would have dropped the train. You confirm what you saw and admit your parents knew the timing when they chose the cleaner public lie that a “real hero” would have found another way.
5. Veyra names the emotional wound: they can live with having killed one person to save many, but not with being told by their own family that the refusal to let you and the passengers die proves they never belonged. They talk only to you because you remember the whole choice, yet your silence outside this room has allowed the family verdict to stand.
6. Asked why they still use Serrin, Veyra says the name belonged to both siblings before it became a brand or a weapon; keeping it was a way to keep you. This turns your anger from an urge to punish your parents into a responsibility to stop making Veyra carry the truth alone.
7. You reveal that your suit retained the incident timing and your own voice recording. Rather than threaten an impulsive leak, you ask what Veyra can bear. Together you choose a narrow response: your firsthand statement and the decisive seconds of telemetry, with no spectacle of the body and no claim that killing was easy or clean.
8. In a short coda at the family's next live appearance, Crownfire again calls Veyra a disgrace and you interrupt. You state the constrained choice, acknowledge its cost, and say Veyra saved the train and saved you. Palisade removes your crest and cuts your feed, but the correction has already gone out.
9. Outside, stripped of the costume mark but still using your own name, you call Veyra. They answer after a long pause. End on the siblings beginning an ordinary conversation in public daylight: not repaired, acquitted by slogan, or reunited with the parents, but no longer hidden from each other or the world.

## Continuity

- Canon used: Only the foundational shared-world allowance for independent entry points and the established importance of witnesses, material evidence, moral choice, and the fact that truth does not automatically produce justice. No recurring person, place, faction, power system, or historical event is used.
- Boundaries and unknowns: Keep the era and city unnamed. The Serrins' abilities, family brand, public hero culture, no-kill expectation, and Shearpoint incident are story-local; establish no universal inheritance rule, power taxonomy, hero authority, or connection to Solstice Evening Bell, the Northbridge trio, `ultrahuman` classification, or any other system. Names have ordinary social meaning only. Leave power origins, wider institutions, jurisdiction, and later public consequences unresolved.
- Moral boundary: “No choice” means no viable action in the witnessed seconds that would save the passengers and spare Shearpoint; it does not erase Veyra's grief, responsibility, or the human cost of killing. Do not let the parents' branding dispute replace that consequence.
- Drafting authority: The prompt remains the acceptance authority; this outline is advisory scene guidance.
