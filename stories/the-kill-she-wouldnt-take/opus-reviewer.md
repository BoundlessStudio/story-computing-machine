- Existing review.md said: `Verdict: PASS`, `Dialogue: PASS`; I disagree on dialogue, because it missed the Blocking line at L71 and the three Major problems around the climax and ending.

This booth is better than most in the collection. The three speaking voices are really different: Caro calls sequence and numbers, Dax is a vulgar reader of bodies, and the producer speaks in clipped euphemism. The superstar communicates only through gesture, and in the decisive exchange Caro asks for Dax's basis and he honestly answers that it's only his eyes. The failures are local, and most of them sit at the edges of the climax. One of Dax's figures stops meaning anything. The line spoken as Caro hits the key has no owner. The first beat after the key is a gag built to reach back to the opening. The aftermath then explains the refusal twice after the replay has already proved it frame by frame. The edit pass should mostly cut: remove the dead figure and the callback, tag the climax line, and leave one editorial voice at the end instead of two. Do not sand down the profanity, the quips that do work, or the producer.

## Dialogue findings

### D1 · Blocking · Dead figure in Dax's mask-strike call

- Where: L71, Dax reacting to Nash's mask strike
- Text:
> “He made her blink. You ever try to move a corpse with a blink?”
- Problem: Ground/figurative. The question maps onto nothing. Nobody in the scene is a corpse being moved, and taken as a rhetorical question ("you can't move a corpse with a blink") it argues against Dax's own grin and the "he gets a chance" point that follows. Caro and the audience would hear noise, and nothing in the scene responds to it. The rest of the speech (death resets you, injury stays until someone ends it) does real work and needs no bridge.
- Fix: Cut the question. Keep "He made her blink." and the hurt-stays-hurt mechanics. If the editor wants a figure there, it has to map onto what just happened (Zindra flinching) and stay in Dax's vulgar register. Do not swap in an aphorism.

### D2 · Major · No speaker for the line cut off by the key

- Where: L199–L209, Caro fumbles the guard and hits the medical-integrity key
- Text:
> “I know how the fucking key works.”
> “Current evidence says—”
> The challenge tone booms from the arena before the producer can answer.
- Problem: Attribution at the decisive beat. The untagged line has three possible owners. Dax fits the alternation, but he has just said "Hit the key" and never talks like this. The producer fits the register ("Official assurance is valid"), but L209 says the producer has not answered yet. Caro could be starting an on-air justification before she stops talking and acts. Each reading changes what the key press overrides, and the reader has to stop at the story's most important second.
- Fix: Choose the speaker and tag the line. The producer is the strongest choice: a last institutional push that Caro's palm cuts off. If it is the producer, tag it and change "before the producer can answer" at L209 so it fits a voice that has already been cut off (for example "before the producer can finish"). Do not add lines to the countdown.

### D3 · Major · Forced zipper callback right after the challenge

- Where: L211–L217, the first exchange after the weapons lock
- Text:
> “Put it on my tab,” Dax says.
> “You do not have a tab.”
> “Then Caro was right about the zipper.”
- Problem: Punchline Patch. The producer's "You do not have a tab" exists only to set up a callback to the bathroom banter at L15–L19, and the callback does not follow. Having no tab makes Caro right about the zipper only if the reader builds a chain for it ("no tab" means "you've lost," which means "I lost to the zipper too"). It comes seconds after the weapons lock, with Nash's return still unverified and the marshals on the floor, and it turns the first beat after the challenge into a bookend gag. "Put it on my tab" on its own is good: Dax offering to take the blame for Caro's call is real movement in their relationship.
- Fix: Keep "Put it on my tab." Cut the producer's reply and the zipper line. Let the producer ignore him, or give a procedural cue in the producer's existing register, then go to Caro breathing through her mouth (L219). Leave the opening zipper banter (L15–L19) alone; it does not need a payoff.

### D4 · Major · The ending explains the hesitation twice after the replay proved it

- Where: L271–L273, the recap after the verdict
- Text:
> Quell controlled every lethal angle and then resisted the resolution rather than take the red-collar finish.
> “No-contest is paper,” Dax says.
- Problem: Philosopher, explained ending. By L271 the replay has already shown the finger coming off and the mask turning to the collar, frame by frame (L229–L233), and the final paragraph shows it once more with the telestrator. Caro's sentence then states the title's thesis outright, and Dax immediately restates the same meaning in his own voice. Two recaps in a row of the same point flatten the close. Caro's thesis sentence also fits badly with the pressure she is under, since the producer has just told her to lead with the safeguard and stop saying "stale".
- Fix: Cut Caro's middle sentence so her read keeps only its first and last sentences: the official sheet, then "alive and unverified." The quiet word "unverified" is her defiance. Keep Dax's "No-contest is paper" speech as the single editorial, because the producer's "Neutral analysis" and Dax's "mean version" depend on it. Do not write a replacement summary.

### D5 · Minor · "This" points at the wrong body part

- Where: L159, Dax correcting his earlier trauma-tracer guess
- Text:
> “That tracer sits under the shoulder.” Dax jabs two fingers into his own jacket seam. “This is the link ring.
- Problem: Ground/referent and staging. The only gesture shown is at his shoulder seam, which he has just assigned to the tracer, so "This is the link ring" points at the wrong place. "That tracer" also reaches back about 70 lines to his guess at L85. The key fact survives (red at the throat means no return), but on the beat that tells the reader the stakes, the demonstrative and the gesture disagree. It is also unclear whether this goes out on air, though the story tracks on-air status elsewhere (L95, L271).
- Fix: Give "This" its own gesture (Dax touching his own throat, or tapping the collar on the raw monitor), or name the thing ("the ring at his throat"). Mark whether he is on air.

### D6 · Minor · Words that miss the object on screen

- Where: L185 (Dax, as the marshals advance); L237–L239 (telestrator miss)
- Text:
> “Her trigger finger comes off Harl every time she sees his neck.
> Dax misses the next telestrator mark and circles the broken horn. “Shit.”
> “Wrong red,” Caro says.
- Problem: Ground, verb and object. A trigger finger comes off the trigger, not off Harl. The muzzle is what comes off him, and the replay later separates the two ("Finger off. Muzzle into the wall."). At L239 the horn is white (L39, L67), so "Wrong red" misdescribes what Dax circled. Both lines can be worked out, but they sit inside the chain of evidence, where precision is the point.
- Fix: L185: pick one, the finger off the trigger or the muzzle off Harl, not a mix of both. L237: have Dax circle something that is actually red (the mohawk) so "Wrong red" can stay, or keep the horn and change Caro's line to match it.

### D7 · Minor · Two untagged lines in three-voice runs

- Where: L133 (booth against producer during the replay cutaway); L287 (rematch odds)
- Text:
> “Then put the confidence figure back.”
> “You are not the bookmaker.”
- Problem: Attribution. L133 falls between two producer lines, right after Caro's warning "Dax." It is probably Dax, but if it is Caro, it is her first push against the producer and changes her arc. L287 falls between two Dax lines. Its uncontracted "You are not" matches the producer's register ("You do not have a tab"), but the alternation points to Caro. The reader has to guess in both places.
- Fix: Tag both. If L133 is Dax, an action beat is enough. If the editor wants Caro's first push to come here, give it to her on purpose and tag it.

### D8 · Minor · Producer cues with slipping referents

- Where: L97 (after Dax says on air that the collar is red); L261 (after the verdict)
- Text:
> “Correct: impact-spectrum contamination. Caro, clarify.”
> Do not say stale public status again.
- Problem: Ground/uptake. "Correct:" first reads as agreement with Dax, the opposite of what the producer means, and only "Caro, clarify" repairs it. At L261, "again" assumes someone said "stale" on air, but the word appears only in narration (L251), and no quoted line supplies it.
- Fix: L97: make it an unmistakable order to correct, not the adjective. L261: either give Caro the verdict read at L251 as quoted speech so "again" has something to point to, or drop "again".

## Continuity and prompt findings

### C1 · Minor · The opening countdown can't hold the opening

- Where: L11–L47, the introductions before the bout
- Text:
> Caro Blythe is already shouting when the red tally in the broadcast glass reaches ten.
> The tally hits zero. The producer shouts, “LIVE!” Zindra fires before Caro can say the word.
- Problem: A countdown that ends in "LIVE!" reads naturally as seconds, and ten seconds can't hold Caro's welcome, the required safeguard read, both walk-ons and several exchanges of commentary, which together run well over a minute. "LIVE!" also clashes with the booth already being on air (L31, "Dax says on air"). "The word" has no referent, because Caro's start call is never named.
- Fix: Make the unit explicit (minutes), or start the count when Zindra reaches her mark (L45). Give the producer a bout-start cue instead of "LIVE!". Name the word Caro was about to say, or cut "the word".

### C2 · Minor · The replay contradicts the live pin

- Where: L103 and L115 (the live pin) against L227–L233 (the replay of the same moment)
- Text:
> The muzzle stays fixed.
> “Finger off. Muzzle into the wall.
> Zindra has Nash pinned beneath the oversized firearm.

In the replay, Dax calls the muzzle going into the wall at that same moment, and the narration puts Nash "beneath" the gun instead of against the wall with the gun under his chin. The replay is the evidence for the upheld challenge, so the two versions should agree.
- Fix: Either let L115 show a small shift of the muzzle off his chin toward the wall, or change Dax's replay call to match L115. Bring L227 in line with the staging at L103.
