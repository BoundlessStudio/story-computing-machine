# Outline

## Story

- Premise and central promise: Twenty-three-year-old Leonor helps six younger divine siblings negotiate human company in Cusco. Deliver affectionate family comedy with appetite, embarrassment, and room for the eldest to be somebody's child.
- Focal pressure or attachment: After an early bakery shift, Leonor wants an adventure film with her human father, Eusebio, continuing a habit from before her mother's remarriage. She also enjoys being indispensable.
- Counterforce or complication: Leonor invites the children without consulting Eusebio, then tries to control every interruption. The siblings expect the help she offered. Her parents have let expertise become responsibility for everyone's complaints.
- POV, distance, and information limit: First-person past through Leonor's bodily fatigue and sometimes unfair interpretations. Disappointment emerges through behavior and replies; she cannot certify anyone's lesson learned.
- Governing movement and time shape: One Saturday, accumulating through breakfast, bus, ticket queue, and auditorium; contract into the foyer, then return to the unfinished film. The private interruption is the climax.
- Speculative rule or ordinary-world constraint: Mamaquilla, Leonor's mother, and Inti, her stepfather, retain worship, power, and recognized lunar/solar authority while living under human aliases. Their younger children scarcely need sleep and must contain their light and heat in public. Leonor inherits useful moonlit vision but ordinary fatigue. Powers cannot supply social understanding or replenish her sleep.
- Dialogue promise: Persistent sibling questions, competitive demonstrations, parental impatience, and mischief make help funny, practical, and fallible.
- Dialogic medium: Kitchen talk, walking conversations, failed whispering, touches along cinema seats, mouthed objections, and a foyer exchange.
- Dialogue engine: Each request recruits Leonor into another obligation. Her invitation makes refusal feel like betrayal. Correcting that expectation lets the parents assume care and changes who gets her attention.

## Voice

- Narrative texture: Bodily, affectionate, occasionally ungenerous; count interruptions more readily than favors. Notice greasy fingers, overheated sweets, sore feet, concealed light, and sitting down. Let conduct correct irritation's distortions.
- Conversational texture: Siblings revisit questions, interrupt, boast, and get distracted. Preserve requests for snacks and seats. Parents can be funny, competent, and bored by familiar explanations.
- Rhetorical ownership: Leonor owns practical demonstrations and self-serving interpretations. Braulio argues fairness when embarrassed; Zulema wants particulars and experiments before listening. Nélida claims parental authority; Celso uses physical affection and distractible humor. Eusebio has movie opinions and preferences of his own.
- Pressure behavior: Leonor's patient explanations become sharp commands. Braulio stops performing confidence and contests her account. Zulema persists because the answer still matters. Parents shorten negotiations when the youngest becomes restless.
- Relationship movement: Leonor wants Eusebio's undivided company without admitting possessiveness. He wants the daughter he expected. The siblings want her approval and access; they struggle to accept her unavailability. Nélida and Celso must reclaim care without treating Leonor as another infant. Changed seating makes those commitments concrete.
- Anti-default: Avoid an eldest-child wisdom seminar ending in a perfectly considerate family; retain squabbling, unfinished questions, and Leonor's appetite for importance.

## Beats

1. Braulio asks how to lose without patronizing school friends. Leonor sensibly suggests games where stamina offers no advantage, then undermines herself with theatrical losing. Zulema needs to practice sleeping before her first sleepover; questions about eyelids and breathing prevent Leonor's nap. Four more siblings create household congestion.
2. Establish that Nélida and human Eusebio divorced; Celso has helped raise Leonor since childhood. Both fathers remain loved. Enjoying her siblings' enthusiasm, Leonor expands the film invitation. Nélida and Celso accompany their children. Eusebio discovers the changed plan when they arrive.
3. Let the queue and film be enjoyable before they become too much: snack negotiations, Celso's inconvenient warmth, a child's absorbed delight. Leonor gets hungry while distributing everyone's food. Braulio wants discreet advice about an unanswered invitation; Zulema keeps practicing sleep against her. Eusebio cannot finish an ordinary anecdote. Leonor misses a favorite sequence while policing whispers.
4. In the foyer, Leonor snaps. Braulio holds her to the invitation instead of accepting another lecture. Eusebio acknowledges that he expected time with her; he does not adjudicate the household. Leonor admits fatigue and wanting him to herself without delivering a family indictment. The parents recognize the immediate job and handle the children's objections themselves.
5. They return with the parents among the younger children and Leonor beside Eusebio. Braulio's invitation remains unanswered; Zulema can practice with her mother. Give father and daughter a pleasurable disagreement about the film. Leonor settles against him and sleeps during something she meant to watch; nobody wakes her to explain the ending.
