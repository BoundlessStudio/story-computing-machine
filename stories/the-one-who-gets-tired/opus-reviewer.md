## Verdict

This story's dialogue is among the collection's better work, and the owner's usual complaint mostly does not apply here. The speech is domestic and ordinary. The children ask literal, experimental questions ("Do they stay pointed forward?"), a sixteen-year-old frets over an unanswered message, and a popcorn question cuts off Papa's fan story, which gets its payoff an hour later. The decisive lobby exchange is plain want ("Just us.") rather than an argued thesis. The house tics are largely absent: corrections are few and mostly earned, no one negotiates in clauses, no one states the lesson, and the final exchange stops instead of explaining. The main rhythm is short alternating lines plus parental conditions ("If your parents take you", "If everyone has lunch"). That suits a big family run by deputies, but edits should not add more of either. Keep the edit pass surgical: clarify a few referents, loosen one stiff echo, trim narration that re-explains lobby beats the dialogue already lands, and tidy the snack objects. Do not make the terse dialogue chatty or add explanation.

## Dialogue findings

### D1 · Minor · Opening "us"/"they" referents

- Where: L19–L23 and L35, Braulio on the stairs
- Text:
> “She's not coming running with us.” He waved our sister back upstairs. “They keep saying I'm letting them win.”
> “I waited halfway up yesterday.”
> to tell the twins they were wrong: he didn't have to stop running with his friends.
- Problem: Ground/referent. In the story's first exchange, "us", "they" and "halfway up" have no named group or route. Braulio is standing on the stairs, so "halfway up" briefly reads as the stairs. L35 then brings in a separate argument with the twins, which tempts the reader to reassign "they" to the twins. The gist is clear by L27, but the reader has to back-solve the opening.
- Fix: Name the running friends once in Braulio's L19 line and tie "halfway up" to a hill or route. Leave the rest of the exchange and L35 as they are. Illustrative only: "The boys keep saying I'm letting them win." / "I waited halfway up the hill yesterday."

### D2 · Minor · "take his place" points at the wrong man

- Where: L285, lobby, Mother's seating plan
- Text:
> “I'll put Celso in your seat. You sit here with your father until you're ready, then take his place.”
- Problem: Ground/referent. The nearest antecedent of "his" is "your father", so on first read Mother seems to send Leonor into Papa's seat. The intended swap (Leonor into Celso's empty seat beside Papa) is only confirmed at L315.
- Fix: Name the seat in Mother's line and keep her brisk tone. Illustrative only: "…until you're ready, then take Celso's seat."

### D3 · Minor · "Can't. Yes." stiffens the sibling repair

- Where: L297–L301, lobby, Leonor's apology to Braulio
- Text:
> “He didn't say he wouldn't come. He can't.”
> “Can't. Yes.”
- Problem: Braulio's correction is grounded and true to him. Leonor told Mother "his friend wouldn't come" (L245), and he defends his friend and his pride instead of accepting the apology. Leonor's reply echoes the corrected word and confirms it. That is the collection's clerk-like concession rhythm, and from a tired, sorry older sister it sounds like a ruling rather than an apology being accepted.
- Fix: Keep Braulio's line exactly. Replace Leonor's echo with a plain concession, or a beat that shows she accepts it (a nod, looking away), without repeating the word. Illustrative only: "I know."

## Prose findings

### P1 · Minor · Narration explains the lobby beats

- Where: L241, L247, L265, L275, L303, lobby
- Text:
> I'd brought him out here to spare the other people in the cinema his conversation, then given it to the lobby.
> although I was the one who had put four people between us.
> Now I wanted Papa all to myself and somebody else to do the disappointing.
> He wiped his nose with the back of his hand, annoyed.
- Problem: In the lobby scene (L219–L313), almost every lobby beat gets a neat self-diagnosis or an emotion label: L241's "a completeness I never managed for anything useful", L247, L265, L275, and "annoyed" at L303. Each line is good on its own. Together they explain what Braulio's step back and "Just us." already show.
- Fix: Keep at most two self-diagnoses. L275's last sentence is the strongest because it adds something new, and L265's "four people" callback is the other good one. Cut L247's gloss so "Braulio stepped back from me. I stopped." carries the moment, and drop "annoyed" at L303 so the nose-wipe stands on its own. Do not add new commentary in their place.

### P2 · Minor · Vague "that"/"something" in key narration

- Where: L37 (after the stairs), L109 (divorce backstory)
- Text:
> They argued for hours, then asked me, and something could happen.
> I still liked going places with Papa where nobody needed that explained.
- Problem: L109 is the reason time with Papa matters, but "that" has no clear antecedent: loving Celso, having two fathers, or the wedding explanation. L37's "something could happen" is too vague to show what Leonor enjoys about being asked, and that enjoyment is her flaw.
- Fix: Give each sentence a concrete referent without making it longer. At L109, say what never needs explaining with Papa (whose daughter she is, or why she has two fathers). At L37, say what her answer does (settles the argument, gets someone moving).

## Continuity and prompt findings

- Where: L155–L159, snack counter; also L163, L189, L315, L327
- Text:
> At the snack counter, Celso bought three tubs of popcorn
> He noticed my stare and passed the bars over before he could melt them completely.
> He folded it over his arm to carry them.
- Problem: Leonor takes the bars, then Celso is carrying them on his jacket again with no handback. Later, four separate tubs are in use against "three tubs": the twins' (L163, L327), the youngest's (L189, L255), the one Celso lifts from Leonor's old seat (L315), and the one Papa holds (L327). The slips are small, but the rest of the object tracking is careful (water bottles, the thumbprint on the jacket lining, chocolate on Leonor's fingers).
- Fix: Add a short handback (she lays the bars on his folded jacket), and either make it four tubs or drop the number. Keep the chocolate on Leonor's fingers (L279) and the thumbprint on the lining (L315).
