# Outline

## Story

- Premise and central promise: When the unnamed narrator brings soup and ordinary cold supplies to her best friend, Mallow Fenn, Mallow refuses to let her inside: she has a witch cold, and every sneeze casts a genuinely random spell she cannot select, aim, weaken, or suppress safely. The narrator enters anyway, convinced that enough padding, sorting, and distance can make caregiving predictable. Fulfill the prompt with a succession of cute, inconvenient, and frighteningly consequential spells, then resolve the immediate promise privately: the friends get Mallow through the fever peak without discovering control or a magical cure, the sneezing stops when the illness passes, and at least one transformation remains for them to deal with afterward.
- Intended reader experience: Warm domestic comedy with a tightening edge. Let the pleasure come from Mallow's unabashedly cute magical-girl aesthetic colliding with tissues, soup, fever hair, and absurd spell fallout; let affection remain visible even when the friends argue. Each sneeze should produce anticipation without establishing an escalation formula: harmless strangeness and real danger may arrive in either order. End relieved and amused, with the friendship intact, the apartment not intact, and randomness still random.
- Generating force:
  - Mode: Plot-led domestic ratchet. Each sneeze leaves a new physical condition that invalidates the narrator's latest safety arrangement and reduces the apartment's usable space. The accumulating aftermath moves the pair from threshold to kitchen to bathroom, then reverses the caregiving roles after the narrator is reduced to six inches tall.
  - Focal attachment or contradiction: The narrator expresses love by becoming useful and staying close; Mallow expresses love by trying to send the person she trusts most beyond the spell radius. Both are protecting the friendship, but their preferred forms of protection cannot coexist while Mallow needs help and has absolutely no control.
  - Plot-led wants and useful limit, if applicable: The narrator immediately wants to get fluids, food, ordinary symptom relief, and rest into Mallow before the fever peaks, while also wanting to prove she can stay without becoming another casualty. Her useful limit is fixer confidence: she is observant and quick with practical responses, but mistakes preparation for control, keeps adding clutter and rules after each spell, and initially treats Mallow's risk assessment as panic to manage. Mallow immediately wants the narrator safely outside, while competing exhaustion and loneliness make her want company within reach; shame about causing harm leads her to hide how long she has been holding back sneezes.
- Counterforce or complication:
  - Source: The cold's involuntary spellcasting has no readable sequence, target rule, emotional logic, or reliable radius. Suppressing a sneeze does not cancel its spell and leaves Mallow dizzy and short of breath; fever and fatigue also make ordinary falls, spills, and bad decisions more likely. Previous spell effects end on unrelated schedules, so a newly safe surface may change again.
  - Credible inside logic, if a person: Mallow is a countervoice, not an antagonist. She knows her own body and the absence of magical control, fears permanently altering her best friend, and continues to set conditions after the narrator refuses to leave. The narrator is right that Mallow should not weather a high fever alone; Mallow is right that no clever layout can make proximity safe.
  - Pressure or change created: Every precaution protects against the last spell rather than the next. Distance reduces magical exposure but increases the risk of leaving an ill person unattended; closeness permits care but makes the narrator part of whatever the next sneeze changes. Once the narrator is tiny and cannot leave independently, both must replace unilateral protection with explicit, revisable choices about risk.
- Narrative design:
  - POV, tense, distance, and limitation: Close first-person past through the unnamed narrator, comic and concrete rather than arch. The narrator knows Mallow intimately but cannot enter her thoughts or perceive a pattern in the spells. Mallow's fear, irritation, and relief emerge through her distinct blunt requests, evasions about symptoms, and practical actions after the role reversal.
  - Time shape and compression: One evening from arrival at the apartment door through the fever peak, followed by a short morning aftereffect. Give the threshold refusal, the first failed care plan, the argument about leaving, the shrinking sneeze, Mallow's dangerous suppression, their negotiated response, and the morning reversal full scene space. Compress a few later overnight sneezes into a brief accumulation that proves the agreement workable without turning the story into a spell catalogue.
  - Information and reveal strategy: Establish immediately that Mallow recognizes a local, temporary ailment but cannot know what any sneeze will cast. Demonstrate rather than explain the operative facts: spells vary wildly, suppression harms without granting control, and effects fade independently. Reveal only after the narrator has built an elaborate safe zone that Mallow has been swallowing sneezes to protect her. Do not reveal a hidden pattern, emotion-keyed spell, special friendship immunity, chosen-one cure, autonomous focus object, or connection to another magical-girl tradition.
  - Governing movement: Arrive with a plan; be refused; witness randomness cross the threshold; enter; feed; pad; sneeze; clean; classify; be disproved; bargain over leaving; retreat to a supposedly simpler room; shrink; reverse who is physically capable; suppress; stop; ask; choose conditions; sneeze openly; endure without decoding; sleep; wake changed but no longer casting.
- Speculative surplus: Every witch-cold discharge also makes nearby metal hum a different soft note and remain uncomfortably warm for several minutes. Keys, spoons, pipes, and the bathroom tap create a ridiculous shifting chord, so the friends set hot utensils on ceramic and avoid gripping fixtures after a sneeze. The resonance never predicts, powers, cancels, or interprets the random spell and carries no emotional message.
- Structural distinction: A one-apartment comedy of failed containment whose apparent safe area contracts rather than opening into pursuit, rescue, public proof, or a larger magical emergency. The decisive venue is an ordinary bathroom stripped of loose objects, and the decisive action is a small negotiation made after the narrator's competence has been physically reduced. No crowd converts, no institution learns a lesson, and the final spell is not stronger or more meaningful than the others.
- Decisive turn, deepening, or recognition: In the bathroom, a sneeze shrinks the narrator to six inches. Mallow lunges to keep her from falling off the sink, then begins choking back the next sneeze because the narrator is now more vulnerable than ever. From the soap dish, the narrator starts issuing another cascade of instructions, sees that her planning is worsening Mallow's immediate condition, and finally asks what Mallow needs. Mallow chooses a low phone, a clear line of sight, no running toward each other during a discharge, and permission to sneeze without apology; the narrator chooses a sheltered spot within speaking distance. The released sneeze turns the shower curtain into a flock of thumb-sized soap bubbles that keep asking the time. Its harmless absurdity is chance, not reward, but their chosen response lets Mallow drink, breathe, and rest.
- Aftereffect or live uncertainty: The fever breaks by morning and the witch-cold sneezes cease, but the narrator remains six inches tall, several spell effects are still unwinding, and the damp, humming apartment needs repair. Neither friend assumes another sneeze would reverse the transformation, and Mallow will seek bounded help once she has eaten and dressed. For now she is well enough to care for the tiny person who came to care for her; responsibility changes hands without making either friend blameworthy for an involuntary spell.
- Opening / ending relation: Open outside the apartment with the narrator balancing bags and insisting through a closed door that friendship means being let in. End inside with Mallow portioning cooled soup into a bottle cap, setting the phone where her tiny friend can use it, and asking which practical problem they want to tackle first. The opening offers one person's complete plan; the ending leaves two people choosing the next bounded action amid consequences no plan erased.

## Beats

1. At Mallow's door, the narrator meets a handwritten warning and Mallow's refusal from inside. A sneeze turns the sealed grocery bag inside out without tearing it, scattering supplies across the hall while the keys in the narrator's hand hum and heat. The effect proves reach and randomness; the narrator gathers everything and persuades Mallow to open the door anyway.
2. Inside, establish Mallow's cute magical-girl material life without glamour: star-bright hair clips beside used tissues, a frilled transformation mantle thrown over pajamas for warmth, and fever flushing beneath practiced embarrassment. The narrator sets food, water, phone, and a clear route while Mallow insists she should leave them and go.
3. A sneeze lifts every liquid in the kitchen into wobbling globes. The narrator saves some water, loses the soup to the ceiling, and answers with towels, padded corners, and a carefully marked safe zone. A later sneeze exchanges the shadows of every object in the room, doing no harm but defeating the narrator's proposed pattern; metal around them sounds another warm chord.
4. The narrator keeps classifying risks and moving possessions. Mallow grows terser, admits the cold should pass after the fever peak, and again asks her friend to go. When she sways, the narrator realizes Mallow has been suppressing sneezes since before the visit. Their argument exposes the incompatible goods: solitude is magically safer, company is physically safer, and neither can promise zero harm.
5. They compromise on the bathroom because its fixtures are anchored and it can be cleared quickly. The narrator brings only water, towels, phone, and a ceramic cup, treating the move as a final containment solution. Mallow warns that a bare room is still inside the spell radius.
6. The next sneeze shrinks the narrator to six inches on the sink edge. Mallow catches her in both hands before she falls, then tries to swallow another sneeze while holding the person she most feared changing. Her breathing catches; the narrator's first impulse is to invent more rules from the soap dish.
7. The narrator stops directing and asks what Mallow needs now. They negotiate conditions both can actually choose, relocate the narrator within sight, and let the sneeze happen. The shower curtain becomes time-asking soap bubbles. They handle the nuisance, drink what remains, and laugh without pretending the result was mercy or mastery.
8. Compress the rest of the fever night through two or three unrelated discharges and practical responses, with no rising power scale and no attempt to decode them. Mallow finally sleeps in the dry tub; the narrator dozes in the padded ceramic cup with the phone nearby while warm pipes hum after the last sneeze.
9. Morning brings no new discharge. Mallow wakes clear-headed enough to assess the lingering changes, refuses to test a forced sneeze, and takes over the practical work. She cools soup for the still-tiny narrator, places the phone within reach, and begins with food and a call for bounded assistance rather than promising an instant reversal.

## Continuity

- Canon used: The story occupies the coherent shared world and uses a local system commonly understood as magic. Mallow, a living person, supplies the magical agency; her clothing, phone, household objects, and other nonliving matter may be transformed or affected but never choose or cast. The independent entry-point policy permits a private modern-like setting whose characters do not discuss deep history.
- Boundaries and unknowns: Treat Mallow's transformation practice, witch cold, spell range, random effects, and recovery as story-local facts, not universal magical-girl taxonomy, universal illness rules, or evidence about every magic user. Establish no connection to Solstice, her historical circles, the Northbridge trio, their focus tokens, or any existing concealment/support practice. Do not name the narrator or apartment, identify a city or civil date, explain the cold's ultimate origin, give Mallow control over a sneeze spell, or let a nonliving focus act independently. The prompt governs: any draft variation must preserve uncontrollable random casting and resolve the friends' immediate night without converting involuntary harm into moral fault.
