# Opus review: Witch Cold

Independent audit for a later editing pass. It is not a `review.md` verdict,
does not change canon state, and does not authorize edits by itself.

## Verdict

- Dialogue: REVISE — two Ground failures (a stair instruction that contradicts itself, and a reply in the supply-point argument that answers nothing), a stair protocol played as clause-trading, and a healthy narrator who talks in the same fever fragments as her patient.
- Prose: REVISE — a rules-manual paragraph and repeated compliance footnotes turn the random-spell premise into a checklist, and evaluative narration explains most of Nuala's lines and gestures.
- Continuity and prompt: REVISE — the bloom working twice far exceeds its own "four times its volume" rule, and "rainwater" sits in the basin before any rain has fallen in the washroom.
- Edit scope: targeted-dialogue
- Existing review.md said: Verdict PASS, "Dialogue: PASS"; disagree — it missed both Ground failures, the contract-style stair exchange and the two continuity errors.

The story's non-spoken dialogue is its strength. Cards, finger counts, phone sketches, the typed capitals and Nuala's fever-broken fragments do real work, and the final exchange is wordless and earned. The spoken dialogue is where it goes wrong. Nuala has a reason to speak in two-word rulings; Imogen has none, yet she answers in the same telegraphic register ("Sit." / "Drink."; "Unfastened. Eligible." / "Still wheels."). As a result the fever loses its audible signature, and nearly every exchange runs the same loop: Imogen proposes, Nuala corrects, Imogen concedes. Around that loop the narration evaluates or decodes almost every line. The edit pass should fix the two Ground failures, let Imogen talk like a frightened well person (fuller, faster, over-planned), cut the glosses, and let one or two existing exchanges carry something between best friends other than protocol, without adding a friendship speech.

## Edit guardrails

Keep the no-control sentence at L53 in substance (she cannot stop, delay, aim, choose, or bias a working) and keep the random selection among the six workings with identical warnings.
- Length band 2,500–4,000 words. The prose is about 3,770 words now, so edits must be net-neutral or shorter; the P1 cuts are the place to find room for anything D6 adds.
- Active profile is `prospective-2026-08-21` (the last `Craft profile:` line). First-person past through Imogen; Nuala is an adult; broadly accessible rating.
- Universe: LOCKED "Only life can use magic" (`universe/rules.md`, Living agency and magic). Workings come from Nuala's living magic; the brooch, cards, chime, cold and objects never choose or cast. Do not tie Nuala to any canon magical-girl system (for example the Northbridge trio in `rules.md`).

## Dialogue findings

### D1 · Blocking · Stair order contradicts itself

- Where: L139, stair plan at the front door; contradicted by L181 and L189 on the stairs
- Text:
> You walk in front. I stay one stair below.
> Nuala's hand closed on the back of my jacket.
> I retrieved it. She watched my feet.
- Problem: Ground/staging. Going downstairs, "in front" means lower, and so does "one stair below"; both people cannot hold that position. The later action (Nuala grabbing the back of Imogen's jacket, watching her feet) puts Imogen below and ahead. The reader cannot picture the descent the line sets up.
- Fix: Make the instruction match the staging used later: Imogen leads, one stair below; Nuala follows with the rail. Illustrative only: "I go first, one stair down from you." Change nothing else in the line for this finding; D3 covers the exchange around it.

### D2 · Blocking · Supply-point reply answers nothing

- Where: L213–L229, washroom set-up, Imogen's single-case plan
- Text:
> “Everything here,” I said. “One supply point. I can reach you and the case from either door.”
> She pointed to the three-metre tape measure in my tool roll.
> “The case stays with me.”
> “If rain hits everything—” I began.
> She tapped the red card.
- Problem: Ground/uptake and staging. Nuala's gesture says "the case is inside my three-metre reach." Imogen's reply does not answer that, and it contradicts the staging: she has just set the case beside Nuala's wall and said she will work from the doors, so the case is not "with" her in any sense. The reader cannot tell whether she means to hold it, move it, or simply refuse. Then "If rain hits everything—" is an objection that applies to her own plan, not to Nuala's split, and the red card Nuala taps was laid "on top of the case" (L223) as a label, so the tap can mean "the case" or "stop." Imogen's "All right" follows either reading, so the argument has no content the reader can follow. This is the decisive disagreement about who designs the room.
- Fix: Give Imogen a wrong but coherent defense of the single case that answers the range objection, so Nuala's typed "ONE CASE = ONE WET CASE." has something specific to rebut. The best candidate uses facts already on the page: the case seals held in the stairwell rain (L193), and Imogen has just unlatched it (L213), which is exactly why Nuala's expression changes. Illustrative only: "It's sealed. It came down the stairs dry." Then cut "If rain hits everything—" and the red-card tap, or keep the tap only if red means a single thing at that moment. Keep "All right. But medicine outside means a handoff." and the two-fingers answer.

### D3 · Major · Stair protocol played as a contract

- Where: L131–L151, front door and fire door before the descent
- Text:
> “Wheels,” Nuala said.
> “Unfastened. Eligible.”
> “Still wheels.”
> “No grabbing you. If you ask, I support at the waist.”
> “Elbow.” She tapped it.
> Her mouth moved in a soundless rehearsal, then managed: “And wait.”
- Problem: Tennis Match plus the collection's negotiation routine: ten short turns of equal weight, each one a clause (wheels, eligibility, no grabbing, waist, elbow, wait), with Imogen echoing Nuala's terms back ("Elbow." / "Elbow."; "And wait." / "And wait."). "Unfastened. Eligible." is rulebook vocabulary spoken aloud to a sick friend, and "Still wheels." is correction ping-pong; cover the tags on L133–L137 and the speakers are interchangeable. The content matters (Nuala keeping authority over her own body, and "And wait" setting up "Imogen. Wait." at L183); the form turns best friends into parties to an agreement. The wheels debate also sets up a risk that comes true (the unheld case is swapped at L175) and the story never registers that.
- Fix: Keep the three things Nuala insists on (no grabbing her, elbow not waist, wait) and the "And wait" setup. Break the rhythm: let Imogen rattle out the whole plan in one over-long, too-fast run (the outline's own pressure behavior for her), and have Nuala cut in once, tapping her elbow, rather than negotiating term by term. Drop at least one of the echoes. Either cut the wheels debate to a gesture and a line of action, or, if kept, give Imogen an ordinary worry in place of "Unfastened. Eligible." and let the swap on the stairs register briefly that she was right, without an I-told-you-so line.

### D4 · Major · Thesis gloss after "YOU CAN WALK."

- Where: L239–L247, washroom, end of the supply-point argument
- Text:
> ONE CASE = ONE WET CASE.
> YOU CAN WALK.
> I had arranged the room around my reach, as if the only capable body in it belonged to me.
- Problem: Philosopher at the decisive exchange. The typed lines land on their own; the next sentence states the lesson. It also misreads the message it glosses: "YOU CAN WALK" is about Imogen's legs (she can fetch from two stations), but the gloss turns it into a statement that Imogen treated herself as the only capable person, which is a different point that belongs to the carrying and elbow beats.
- Fix: Cut L247. The next paragraph already shows Imogen taking the point by carrying water and medicine out through the two doors. If a bridge is needed, use a physical beat (she picks up the water), not a realization.

### D5 · Major · Healthy narrator speaks in fever fragments

- Where: L45–L49 arrival; L63 bathroom plan; L115 basement; L135 wheels; L303 repair list; against Nuala's lines throughout
- Text:
> “Sit.”
> “Drink.”
> “Bathroom,” I said. “No loose objects. Tile. Door.
> “Basement,” I said. “If you approve the route.”
> I heard myself say, “Splitting the kit doubles the retrieval points.”
- Problem: Identical Voices. Nuala's clipped speech is explained by fever and congestion (L39); Imogen's is explained by nothing, yet she talks in the same one-word imperatives and noun lists. At L45–L47 the two lines cannot be assigned without tags, and "Drink." reads as a counter-command in the same register as "Sit." (the reader only learns that Nuala is asking for water when she points at it). "If you approve the route" is procedure vocabulary where a friend would just ask. The story does know Imogen talks like a manual (L235–L237 is a good self-aware beat), but that beat only works if her other lines are not already like that.
- Fix: Sweep Imogen's spoken lines outside the 2–4 second warnings ("Brace", "Case shut" can stay clipped). Give her the fuller, faster sentences of a frightened well person: she over-explains, repeats herself, asks for confirmation. Make "Drink." unmistakably Nuala's request (a gesture toward the case or her mouth) or give Imogen a longer line to answer. Keep "Splitting the kit doubles the retrieval points." as the one lapse the narration mocks.

### D6 · Major · One relationship beat on repeat

- Where: L63–L85 bathroom; L117–L125 carrying; L131–L151 stairs; L163 fire door; L213–L247 washroom; L297–L301 basin guard
- Text:
> “We can pad the edges.”
> “With cloth.”
> “I wasn't going to carry you.”
- Problem: Negotiation/procedure routine and correction ping-pong as the only engine of speech. Six times Imogen over-plans, Nuala corrects in a word or a sketch, Imogen concedes. The later "Can I come in?" beats show some change, but each correction is the same size and the spoken dialogue between two best friends is almost entirely about hazards. Apart from "Good entrance.", the carrying joke and "Your... excellent guard.", nobody says anything about the friendship, the fear, the embarrassment of sneezing spells over your friend, or Nuala's work. The prompt promises a best friend and a cute magical girl; the speech delivers a safety briefing.
- Fix: Cut or compress one iteration of the loop (the wheels debate in D3 or the pad-the-edges beat are the lightest to lose). Then let one quiet interval (the eleven minutes at L257–L259 or the eighteen at L325–L327) carry a short exchange that is not a ruling. Ground it in something only these two share: the thirty metres of ribbon, the piping she sewed, the dark brooch "It was hers, and she wanted it there," a work night. Or let Imogen's fear leak as over-talk that Nuala tolerates or teases. Keep it under about 80 words, offset by the P1 cuts. No apology loop, no friendship speech, no line that states what the night meant.

### D7 · Major · Narration glosses the lines

- Where: L39, L163, L167, L185 (also L247 in D4, L301 in D10, L327 in P2)
- Text:
> The words were in there; fever and congestion had put a slow door in front of them.
> Even blurred by fever, the diagnosis was exact.
> The correction cost her less breath this time.
> She was right. One went nowhere alone.
- Problem: After almost every Nuala line or gesture, Imogen's narration grades it (exact, right, correction) or explains what it shows, so the reader never has to infer anything. "One went nowhere alone" is an aphorism closing a gesture that was already clear. "The correction" at L167 has no correction to refer to, since Imogen has not reached for the waist this time.
- Fix: Cut these evaluative sentences. Keep plain decodes of gestures the reader cannot see (for example "two of us, then the route" at L185, the finger counts, "not good, not worse"), because those translate rather than judge.

### D8 · Minor · "Forty" sign is unclear and redundant

- Where: L37–L39, apartment, first float
- Text:
> She held up four fingers, made a fist, then did it again: forty.
- Problem: Imogen has just said "Forty seconds?"; a nod would confirm it. "Then did it again" reads as a second four-and-zero, not as part of one sign, so the reader has to work out what was signed.
- Fix: Replace with a nod or one clean sign (four fingers, then a fist). Cut the gloss that follows (D7).

### D9 · Minor · "Prop" has no clear object

- Where: L163–L167, fire door, softened repair block
- Text:
> “Prop. Not push.”
> I jammed the metal doorstop under the lowest part without trying to straighten it.
- Problem: Ground/referent. Prop the door or the sagging block? "The lowest part" does not say of what. The later "second stop" at L345 suggests the first went under the block, but the reader has to reconstruct that.
- Fix: Name the object in the action line (under the sagging block, or under the door) so Nuala's two words can stay two words.

### D10 · Minor · Guard joke decoded only by narration

- Where: L295–L303, washroom, softened splash guard
- Text:
> “My guard,” I said.
> Nuala coughed into her sleeve. “Your... excellent guard.”
> It was not a quip so much as accurate ownership. I had added the thing after she approved the room because I could not leave a clear basin alone.
- Problem: A line whose meaning depends on the narration after it. The tease depends on Imogen having added the guard without asking, but the reader learns that only in the next sentence; the room prep at L211 never mentions it. The narration then denies that the line is a quip, when it plainly is, and flattens one of the story's few affectionate moments.
- Fix: Plant the guard in the prep paragraph at L211 (Imogen clipping it on after Nuala has approved the room, visibly against the plan). Then cut "It was not a quip so much as accurate ownership." and the explanatory sentence, so "Your... excellent guard." lands on its own. Fix C2 in the same passage.

## Prose findings

### P1 · Major · Rules manual and compliance footnotes

- Where: L53–L55 (the six-working specification), L59, L127, L161, L311
- Text:
> Her witch cold made every sneeze release one of six workings from her living magic.
> Loose nonliving objects under five kilograms might float for forty seconds.
> because an opaque barrier stopped the working from selecting what was beyond it
> It was a separate piece, less than a metre long.
> Neither object had been fastened; both had been in her direct sight, less than three metres away.
- Problem: Exposition. L55 sets out all six workings with weights, sizes and durations before the reader has seen more than one, which is exactly the manual the style guide warns against, and it takes the surprise out of every later sneeze. The durations are repeated anyway when each working arrives ("Thirty seconds," "Five minutes. Only the original would remain."), so Nuala's diagnoses tell the reader nothing new. Later sentences stop the scene to certify that a working obeyed the rules, as if answering a checker rather than a reader.
- Fix: Keep L53's no-control sentence (it is the prompt's promise). Cut L55 down to what the planning needs now: reach, sight, opaque barriers, and what the warning does and does not tell them. Let each effect's size and duration arrive with the effect, through Nuala's counts and icons. Delete the footnotes at L59 (the "because" clause), L161 (second sentence) and L311 (last sentence). This is also where the word budget for D6 comes from.

### P2 · Minor · Theme restated in narration and closing image

- Where: L327, L347
- Text:
> Light, card, screen, hand, and sound carried the parts of the work that speech could not.
> Nuala slept in her wet, brave, handmade gold piping while the coral lights stayed gone.
> close enough to see her phone change color and far enough to leave both ways clear
- Problem: L327 states the story's point about its own dialogic medium after the reader has watched it work for 3,000 words. The last paragraph closes on a balanced emblem that restates care-at-a-distance, and "brave" piping (echoing L13) gives the costume a feeling.
- Fix: Cut L327. End on the concrete action (Imogen between the open doors, watching the phone) without the close-enough/far-enough balance, or keep only one half. Drop "brave".

### P4 · Minor · Warning refrain as one-line paragraphs

- Where: L23–L31, L87–L89, L155, L171, L261–L267, L307, L329–L335
- Text:
> Coral pinlights pricked into view above the phone.
> The chime descended.
- Problem: The identical warning is thematically right (it tells them nothing), but it is written as the same run of one-sentence paragraphs (lights / chime / "She sneezed.") about ten times, so the prose settles into a metronome.
- Fix: Keep the sameness of the signal but vary how it is written. Fold some instances into the surrounding paragraph, especially at the fever peak, where compressing the refrain would enact the shortening intervals.

### P5 · Minor · Unclear physical staging

- Where: L95, L109, L159–L161, L177, L195, L211, L311
- Text:
> Half the tissues had torn along their perforations
> I let go. She sneezed.
> The case rolled through, dragging a deep curve into it.
> I dragged the bucket close and used it to push water toward the stair drain.
> I fastened the basin taps with cable ties so they were no longer loose.
> Water struck the floor from inside its lid, a hard splash but not a spill.
- Problem: Small physical claims that do not add up: pocket tissues have no perforations; the case rolls through the door after Imogen has let go of it, with nothing pushing it; the swapped case "struck the far wall" as if it arrived moving; a bucket is used as a squeegee; taps are fixed fittings and are not "loose"; water "struck the floor" in "not a spill". "Two rose buttons showing through" a wall of paper (L95) is hard to picture.
- Fix: Correct each in a few words (tear "along their folds"; say what moves the case through; tie the tap handles if they are removable; say whether the cup spilled or not).

### P6 · Minor · Drainage as default texture

- Where: L113, L127, L195, L269–L271, L295, L337
- Text:
> The disused washroom in the basement had a floor drain
> The small rubber drain guard made seven copies.
> poured away through the open grate
- Problem: Policy note (`universe/style-guide.md`, No default water infrastructure). The prompt does not ask for drainage, but a floor drain, a stair drain, a drain guard and a grate carry several beats. Edits made from these notes are authorized revisions, so the policy applies.
- Fix: Choose the washroom for its concrete floor, emptiness and two doors; let the rain run out under the doors (already at L337) and down the stairs. Make the copied object some other loose item under a kilogram already in the room, and drop "Rain ran down a drain" from L127. Keep the cause-and-effect of the rain beats intact.

## Continuity and prompt findings

### C1 · Blocking · Bloom far exceeds its stated scale

- Where: L55 (rule), L85, L95, L101, L279–L283
- Text:
> One paper or textile item might bloom to four times its volume for eight minutes.
> Nuala vanished behind a white wall with two rose buttons showing through it.
> The mass blocked the doorway and my sight of most of her body.
> the flowered cooling cloth beside her opened into a soft, monstrous acreage
> It climbed the basin and curtained the near door, cutting off my sight of her.
- Problem: At four times its volume, a pocket tissue packet would be about the size of a mug, and a cooling cloth would grow a hand's width or so. Neither could hide a seated adult, block a doorway, climb a basin or curtain a door. The story states the multiplier precisely (and repeats it at L85 and L95's "four layers' worth of space"), so the contradiction is visible exactly where the working matters most. The cloth hiding Nuala is load-bearing: it is why the second door exists (L281).
- Fix: Change the rule, not the scenes. Restate the bloom at L55 (or wherever it survives after P1), L85 and L95 at a scale that can hide a person and curtain a door. Keep the eight-minute duration and the "widening square" icon. Check that L101's "doorway" in the apartment is placed clearly once the scale is fixed.

### C2 · Blocking · Rainwater before any washroom rain

- Where: L295, washroom, softened splash guard
- Text:
> It folded under a shallow pool of rainwater left in the basin, drooping across the drain.
- Problem: The only rain so far fell in the stairwell (L191). The washroom rain comes later (L337), and the basin taps were tied off (L211), so the basin has no water in it. "We let the water pull it crooked" depends on water that isn't there.
- Fix: Give the water a real source. For example, have Imogen wring the stair water from her sleeves into the basin at L251 and call it that at L295, or let the guard sag under its own weight and drop the water.

### C3 · Minor · Props, phones and clothes drift

- Where: L249 vs L277 and L347; L109 vs L209; L13 vs L129, L337, L345; L223 vs L279; L289; L11; L321–L347
- Text:
> On my phone, I made four full-screen colors she could call with a tap
> Green shone on Nuala's phone.
> Then she put it in my palm, because she had no spare pocket now that the tissues had destroyed theirs.
> She made me take the loose cape from her shoulders, but kept her boots and dress on
> Inside the ring she drew only a mat, one covered cup, a small towel, and her repair roll.
- Problem: The color screens are set up on Imogen's phone but later shine on Nuala's. L109 has the packet split in the pocket while L209 says the pocket was destroyed. The outfit is an overskirt over shorts at L13 but a "dress" later, a cape appears at L129 that was never described, and a damaged "dress hem" goes on the repair list without being shown. The cooling cloth that blooms at L279 is not in Nuala's ring inventory. In the float at L289 only the tools rise, although the cup and towel in range should too. The unlaced boot at L11 goes down two flights of stairs unremarked. After the final rain Nuala sleeps soaked, having been shivering at L321, and the blanket's fate is not mentioned, which sits oddly with the nurse's "cool rather than chilled".
- Fix: Put the color screens on both phones at L249. Make the pocket damage consistent. Name the garments consistently and introduce or drop the cape. Add the cooling cloth to the ring (it can follow the nurse's advice) or change the bloom's target. Mention the cup and towel in the float or remove them from range. A half-line each for the boot and for a dry blanket that Nuala accepts or refuses.

### C4 · Minor · "Cute magical girl" is thin in the telling

- Where: L13, L73, L129; throughout the spoken dialogue
- Text:
> the gold piping she had sewn by hand made brave straight lines around a woman who looked ready to fold in half
- Problem: The prompt's literal requirements are met: best friend, magical girl, witch cold, random uncontrolled spells, and a resolved night. But the magical-girl identity lives only in costume description and hints ("work nights", "noisy jobs", the dark brooch), and the premise's play barely surfaces in a story told as a hazard drill.
