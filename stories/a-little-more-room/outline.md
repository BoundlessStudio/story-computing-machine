# Outline

## Story

- Premise and central promise: Robin mistakes his new neighbor's knocks for noise complaints. Suzette likes his records and eventually invites him to dance. Deliver affectionate neighbor comedy: a shy, perpetually blushing adult cyborg's conspicuous glamour and small railgun coexist with an ordinary appetite for company.
- Focal pressure or attachment: Robin wants her to like him but protects himself by becoming useful or amusing. Suzette wants someone to enjoy music with her; she keeps making invitations small enough to survive refusal. Neither wants an audience as much as a participant.
- Counterforce or complication: Their courteous evasions reinforce each other. Robin mistakes her sweetness for approval and her blush for distress or attraction. Her cognitive exclusions make some defensive humor inaccessible; kindness does not make his performance enjoyable.
- POV, distance, and information limit: First-person past through Robin, a thirty-year-old human. Her conduct corrects his confident explanations. No access to Suzette's thoughts, diagnostics, or hidden attraction; she explains her filters.
- Governing movement and time shape: Compress three hallway encounters across a week, then dwell in one evening next door. Sound through the wall becomes exchanged music, invitation, and shared movement. The awkward dance is the event, with no party or public performance.
- Speculative rule or ordinary-world constraint: Suzette, twenty-eight, has living and mechanical parts. Cognitive filters prevent malicious and sexual thoughts from forming, rather than censoring speech. She can recognize harm, dislike things, refuse, feel anger, and want affection. Impoliteness is not evil. Her small railgun is functional sporting equipment she habitually carries secured; it remains unfired. Technical rules apply locally, without magic.
- Dialogue promise: Warm, uneven adult sociability: mistaken courtesy, bad timing, mismatched jokes, small requests, and growing room for disagreement. Possible romantic interest stays unconfirmed; pleasant company is a sufficient outcome.
- Dialogic medium: Hallway speech, music heard through a wall, borrowed records, aborted invitations, gloved gestures, face-to-face talk, and tentative dancing. Neither music nor her blush transmits a reliably decipherable message.
- Dialogue engine: Each must distinguish participation from assistance or distance. Clarifying requests changes Robin's action; Suzette's specific preferences make company a choice rather than a reward for cleverness.

## Voice

- Narrative texture: Robin notices volume, cramped furniture, clothes, and where to put his hands. Initially he supplies motives too quickly; later he leaves Suzette time to answer. Favor embarrassed observation over retrospective wisdom.
- Conversational texture: Small talk can remain small. Allow missed openings, ordinary record questions, uneven pauses, and an unsuccessful joke without a string of punchlines.
- Rhetorical ownership: Robin owns self-deprecation and evasive cleverness; his jokes sometimes ask for reassurance. Suzette owns affectionate literal attention, unfashionable musical enthusiasm, and occasional innocent silliness. She has authority over her home and tastes, and listens without agreeing automatically.
- Pressure behavior: Robin becomes excessively helpful when exposed. Suzette grows quieter and blushes harder, but repeats requests more plainly. Neither acquires therapeutic eloquence. Distinguish embarrassment from filtering through uptake.
- Relationship movement: Robin wants welcome but cannot comfortably request it without earning it. Suzette wants his willing company but cannot readily ask him to stop performing. Exchanges turn presumed tolerance into understood preference and mutual participation.
- Anti-default: Avoid the worldly man teaching an innocent machine humanity, the perfect cyborg curing loneliness, and flirtation built from failed sexual jokes. Their common difficulty is ordinary self-consciousness.

## Beats

1. Robin lowers his music after Suzette's knock before hearing her question. Her geometric mod dress, cinch belt, long gloves, and go-go boots contrast with her shy delivery. Expressive eyes, emphatic lashes, and sharply shaped hair evoke 1990s anime. The small railgun complicates his assumptions; she wants the record's title.
2. Two shorter encounters establish reciprocity. She returns a borrowed record with one she likes, remembers his minor preference, and dislikes a track he expected her to admire. Her blush persists during ordinary conversation. Mechanical finger joints beneath a glove and heavy boots give embodiment practical texture.
3. Suzette invites him for music and dancing. Robin accepts the music and evades the dancing. In her sitting room, she secures the railgun before moving furniture. Her clothes and records express pleasure. Robin appoints himself record selector instead of joining her.
4. A joke proposing spiteful retaliation against an imagined nuisance fails. Suzette cannot form its malicious thought and briefly explains her filters, including their exclusion of sexual thoughts. He drops the joke; no testing or escalating innuendo. Her dislike of his next record prevents him from equating filtering with universal agreeableness.
5. Robin's helpfulness leaves her dancing alone. Her indirect invitation fails; she makes her wish for another dancer plain. He nearly leaves to spare her his incompetence. His self-mockery lands awkwardly instead of extracting reassurance. Her invitation concerns this evening, without confession or personhood argument.
6. He participates before feeling ready. Neither dances well; mistakes concern rhythm, cramped space, and embarrassment. Suzette laughs kindly, corrects a misstep, and chooses another move. End within their imperfect second song, after Robin expresses a musical preference and Suzette answers it. Do not certify romance, cure shyness, remove filters, or append tomorrow's routine.
