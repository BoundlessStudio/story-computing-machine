## Premise and promise

Carpenter and limeworker Agnes Wold spends nearly all her savings on the Hollow Cask, a roof-broken rural pub whose surviving carved gallery promises that it was once the district's grand common room. Each repair uncovers evidence that the gallery's impossible span was held by the Underbreath, an ancient, living cellar colony that earlier proprietors fed when prosperous and later drove with smoke when they wanted its labor cheaply. Agnes's faithful reconstruction reconnects that coercive system during a storm. To save the building and its resident, she must sacrifice the most spectacular piece of the old pub, give the colony an unforced way out, and rebuild grandeur as a smaller hall that stands on honest joinery and shared use.

The reader promise is rural gothic discovery through tactile work: rot, lime, oak pegs, drains, old color, and load paths yield a bounded supernatural secret rather than merely decorating it. The resolution answers what “bringing it back” means and completes both the structural emergency and Agnes's ownership arc.

## Story controls

- **POV and tense:** Close third person limited to Agnes; past tense. No access to Silas's or the Underbreath's private thoughts.
- **Genre and tone:** Rural gothic fantasy. Begin in workmanlike hope, accumulate unease through responsive materials and historical evidence, peak in clear non-graphic structural peril, and end with earned warmth rather than reassurance imposed on the secret.
- **Audience:** Teen and adult. Threat consists of falling timber, smoke, and a living colony drying under strain; no graphic injury or body horror.
- **Setting:** An unnamed rural district in an unresolved era of the one shared physical world. Do not name a kingdom, country, established era, or Galactic Cycle.
- **Restoration standard:** Work is materially legible and causal. Agnes may be wrong about meaning, but her knowledge of shoring, pegged framing, lime, ventilation, and drainage must remain credible.
- **In-world pub name:** `the Hollow Cask`. It is a nonliving place and is never shortened to `the Hollow`, personified as the magical actor, or connected to Ninth Hollow or the Hollow Regent.

## Character engine

### Agnes Wold — purchaser-restorer and viewpoint

- **Want:** Reopen the Hollow Cask by the harvest market as a faithful restoration of its grandest visible phase, proving that buying the ruin was a skilled choice rather than a lonely financial folly.
- **Need:** Accept that good restoration serves present lives and tells the truth about damage; a deed and sunk cost do not authorize her to reinstate another being's inherited labor.
- **Pressure:** Almost no reserve money, a storm approaching before the temporary roof is secure, a sagging gallery that embodies the pub's value, and Silas's skepticism. Every beautiful discovery makes it harder to surrender exact reconstruction.
- **Blind spot:** She treats “original fabric” and “original intention” as presumptively right, and first reads the Underbreath as one more rare system she can conserve and operate.
- **Arc:** Defiant proprietor -> evidence-led conservator still tempted by prestige -> frightened operator of the old system -> steward who lets a resident refuse and supports the building independently. Her success is not abandoning craft but using it more honestly.

### Silas Cray — local sawyer and reluctant witness

- **Function:** Practical foil, source of limited living testimony, and indispensable second pair of hands in the climax; he never supplies the whole mystery by exposition.
- **Want:** Have Agnes demolish the dangerous gallery and keep the cellar closed before the pub harms anyone again.
- **Private pressure:** A family maintenance book shows that a recent forebear knew the smoke-routing system consumed the colony. Silas has confused concealment with protection and fears both local blame and Agnes's determination.
- **Arc:** Warning without explanation -> partial, defensive disclosure -> full material evidence -> action in support of Agnes's adaptive repair. He is not rewarded as the wiser owner; his withholding helped create the danger.

### The Underbreath — ancient living resident

- A continuous, multispecies cellar colony of pale fungal cords and amber grain-mites occupying an ancient oak cask-shell and its linked hollow dowels. Individual organisms are born and die; the coordinated colony's continuity and exact degree of personhood remain locally unresolved, but it demonstrates preference, novel choice, memory of signals, refusal, and coordinated self-preservation. Agnes therefore treats it as a resident capable of refusing, not an asset conveyed by the deed.
- It does not speak or impersonate anyone. It communicates only through selective tapping, changes in tension, where fresh threads grow, and whether it accepts food or follows an opened route.

## Secret ontology, agency, mechanism, costs, and limits

### What is alive and what is not

- **Living magical agent:** The Underbreath. Positive evidence of life includes metabolizing watered malt, mite broods and molts, new fungal growth around a repaired damp channel, recoil from drying smoke, scar tissue around an old burn, and coordinated movement toward a newly opened air route. Its ability to make a novel three-choice response, rather than merely echo a stimulus, establishes bounded agency.
- **Nonliving foci:** The buried cask-shell, hollow oak dowels, mortised gallery frame, clay feed channels, copper breath grille, and flue bellows. They route moisture, vibration, smoke, or the colony's force. None acts, chooses, or casts magic on its own; the pub is not alive.
- **Ancient status:** The cask-shell lies beneath every later pub floor, carries tool marks unlike the successive frames spliced around it, and has been accommodated by several independently dated repair layers. This establishes that the mechanism and colony lineage are ancient relative to the inn without claiming an absolute date or known builder.

### Bounded capability

- Living threads can contract or relax the grain of old oak already colonized through the dowel network. In concert, they can reseat pegs, tune wall and floor resonance, and redistribute a modest load for minutes. This made the cantilevered gallery seem impossibly light and gave crowded music a physical warmth and resonance.
- The Underbreath cannot mend rot, move stone or metal, reach uncolonized new timber on demand, hold a grossly failed beam indefinitely, operate beyond the Hollow Cask, speak, read minds, summon or imitate the dead, preserve human memories, enforce bargains, or make a legal claim magically binding.

### Cost and failure conditions

- Contraction consumes stored malt sugars and moisture, raises the colony's heat, dries its living margins, and kills brood if sustained. Watered malt can replenish it slowly, but is not payment that purchases service.
- Open flame, bitter smoke, hard modern mortar, direct sun, and prolonged dryness injure it. Cold slows it. A clogged drain starves its damp habitat; too much water drowns sections.
- Earlier keepers used feed and music as cues during the pub's prosperous period, then installed bellows and bitter smoke to drive the colony away from its cask and into gallery members under load. Smoke can provoke a flight contraction but cannot guarantee obedience; panic produces erratic releases.
- Decay concealed the secret because blocked feed channels, cement patches, and the stopped copper grille isolated the cask. Repairing drainage revives its margins; clearing the grille restores air; faithfully reconnecting the flue and bellows recreates the dangerous forcing path.

## Story-specific style profile

- **Dominant voice:** Competent, materially curious, and stubbornly hopeful, with unease entering as things cease to fit Agnes's load calculations. Her mind names joints, failures, mixtures, and repair sequences exactly; she stays vague about debt, loneliness, and how much she needs the pub to vindicate her.
- **Narrative distance:** Close enough for free-indirect judgments and bodily work, but never a running emotional gloss. Move nearer at the first living response, the realization that the gallery rests on coerced labor, and the decision to cut historic fabric. Pull back only to compress repeated clearing, drying, and shoring.
- **Image sources:** Carpentry and anatomy only where Agnes would naturally compare them; breath, hollow things, grain, thirst, load, old color under dirt, and foam taking a vibration. Do not make the house “judge” her or give every silence physical weight.
- **Description:** Select damage that changes action: wet wall heads, powdering lime, beetle-soft sapwood, thrust at a mortise, smoke-black clay, and the smell shift from rot to warm malt. Former grandeur must be proven by converging fabric, wear, records, and testimony, not adjective stacks.
- **Dialogue pressure:** Agnes becomes exact and brisk when frightened; Silas circles danger with rural understatement until cornered. Their care is visible in handed tools, shoring, and unwelcome warnings, not polished ethical speeches.
- **Intentional departure:** The climax may use shorter syntax than the largely measured restoration prose, but spatial and causal clarity outrank gothic abstraction. The final scene becomes plain and warm; no epigram states the theme.

## Dialogue profiles

**Agnes Wold — dialogue profile:** Usually wants cooperation or a verifiable answer and begins with technical questions or a concrete bargain. Her clauses are quick, sequential, and exact about timber, money, and time; she refuses to say that she needs the reopening to prove she has not ruined herself. Resistance makes her cite evidence, then issue clipped instructions; with Silas she uses dry challenge rather than intimate confession. Under danger she repeats measurements, not feelings. She would not naturally deliver a polished statement about consent or restorative ethics.

**Silas Cray — dialogue profile:** Usually wants delay and safety without exposing his family's part, using proverbs, practical objections, and answers one question to the side. His pace is considered, his nouns exact for weather, sawing, and local people but vague about history. When Agnes defeats a practical excuse, he shifts from warning to withholding labor, then finally to the maintenance book and direct admission. Shame makes him overstate danger and mishear curiosity as accusation. He does not banter under peril or name his feelings cleanly.

**The Underbreath — interaction profile:** It seeks breathable damp habitat and freedom from smoke-driven load. It first withdraws or gives patterned taps; when those are mistaken for compliance, it changes tactic by refusing food, releasing a test peg, and growing a bright route toward the sealed grille. In crisis it cannot explain—only pull, release, or follow. Never render its signals as quoted words, a humanlike voice, ancestral memory, or proof of an all-knowing building mind.

## Causal arc, stakes, and reversals

1. Because Agnes voluntarily buys the ruin for its recoverable hall, she personally begins stabilizing it instead of accepting demolition.
2. Because stripping incompatible repairs reveals both grand finishes and an impossible historic load path, she follows the hollow dowels rather than covering them.
3. Because tracing damp leads beneath the dance floor, she discovers a living colony in an older cask-shell and recognizes that it—not the inert architecture—moves the oak.
4. Because repairing the colony's air and water routes lets it act again, Agnes can test its limits; those tests and Silas's book reveal the difference between earlier symbiosis and later smoke-driven exploitation.
5. Because Agnes has already restored the old flue geometry in pursuit of authenticity, the storm turns that geometry into a forcing system and the gallery begins to fail.
6. Because the clues have established the colony's route, limits, and refusal, Agnes can resolve the crisis only by opening its exit, severing the forcing path, and sacrificing the grand cantilever so independent shores take the load.
7. Because she chooses an honest, independently supported repair, the pub reopens at smaller capacity while the Underbreath remains by observable choice and owes no service.

**External stakes:** Agnes's life and savings; Silas's safety; the collapse of the hall; destruction or drying of the Underbreath; danger to future patrons if a hidden support remains unacknowledged.

**Internal stakes:** If Agnes restores the old grandeur exactly, she proves technical mastery by repeating an exploitation. If she cuts it away, she risks feeling that she bought and failed another abandoned thing.

**Primary reversals:** The sagging gallery is evidence of grandeur, then evidence that the design never stood honestly; the responsive oak seems like a magnificent recoverable feature, then reveals a living cost; Silas's obstruction seems like mere local pessimism, then partly protects a shameful truth; cutting historic work, initially Agnes's definition of failure, becomes the decisive act of conservation.

## Scene plan

### 1. Deed under a leaking roof — 400 words

- **Live pressure:** On the day the deed transfers, rain is coming through three roof breaches and one tile falls into the bar. Agnes could still board the place and resell the salvage, but she unloads her own tools and spends her first hour erecting a temporary shore.
- **Action and evidence:** Her survey establishes collapsed thatch/slates, a rotten wall plate, burst downpipe, bowed gallery, wet plaster, and a cellar door swollen shut. Under tar and flaking brown paint she finds a gilded vine bracket; across the hall, empty mortises show the gallery once wrapped three walls. Deep wear in the floor faces a broad hearth and speaking step, evidence of civic crowds rather than private luxury.
- **Choice/turn:** She pins the signed deed above the dry side of the bar and chalks `SAVE` on the gallery, personally committing money and labor to faithful restoration by harvest.
- **Dialogue:** None planned; the sale clerk and departing carter remain summarized and unnamed.

### 2. Beauty and an impossible span — 525 words

- **Restoration task:** Agnes clears roof drainage, removes hard cement from a damp wall, and strips one careful test patch rather than attacking the whole room.
- **Clue chain / former grandeur:** Beneath the cement are breathable lime, madder-and-gold procession painting, nail shadows from blue-glass lamps, and a tally of feast and meeting dates scratched by different hands. Silas identifies excellent old oak but calculates with Agnes that the surviving cantilever and shallow joists could never safely have carried the worn dance gallery. Hundreds of tiny polished bores run from each mortise downward; tapping one produces a delayed answer from below.
- **Conflict/turn:** Silas urges demolition. Agnes proposes shoring one bay and tracing the bores first. The answering taps alter the question from “how grand was it?” to “what held it?”
- **Dialogue aims and tactic change:** Agnes wants Silas's sawyer skill and assent to investigation; Silas wants her to authorize demolition. She starts with a paid technical bargain and switches to a limited evidence test when he refuses. He starts with load calculations and changes to withholding labor; the one-bay compromise ends the exchange with neither getting the whole aim.

### 3. The drain beneath the dance floor — 575 words

- **Restoration task:** A wet joist forces Agnes to lift numbered floorboards, expose the sprung elm dance floor, and clear a clay drain rather than simply replace surface boards.
- **Clue chain:** Different generations patched around the same hollow oak dowels. Under the floor lie shattered blue glazing, glazed tile borders, dropped dance tokens, and a copper feed gutter polished by repeated use—further converging evidence of a wealthy, heavily used common hall. The drain and feed gutter descend to a massive cask-shaped oak shell built into older stonework. Warm malt smell rises; fungal cords retract from Agnes's lamp and amber mites carry damp pulp toward pale brood.
- **Direct mechanism evidence:** A loose gallery peg shifts a hair's breadth only when fresh cords tighten in its linked dowel. When Agnes shades and waters one damaged edge, new growth begins; inert wood never acts by itself.
- **Conflict/turn:** Silas admits villagers once called the thing below the Underbreath but claims it was only a cellar pest. Agnes asks why a pest had a copper serving gutter; his inability to answer cleanly confirms withheld knowledge. She chooses not to fumigate and sets water, malt, and three alternative vent routes as a controlled observation.
- **Dialogue aims and tactic change:** Agnes wants Silas to identify the system and help open the cellar; Silas wants to keep the cellar sealed and his family's evidence private. She begins with neutral questions and shifts to a direct contradiction using the polished gutter. He begins by minimizing and changes to the partial local name plus a warning, withholding the maintenance book for now.

### 4. A resident, not a fitting — 575 words

- **Restoration task:** Agnes repairs the drain's grade, reopens the exterior copper grille, and temporarily sleeves—without sealing—the cracked cask-shell. She tests one colonized peg under a measured sandbag load.
- **Agency test:** The colony repeats Agnes's simple tap, but repetition alone proves little. Presented with three shaded channels—food, fresh air, and a smoke-stained gallery route—it refuses the food beside the smoke route, moves brood toward fresh air, and releases the sandbag peg when Agnes blocks that route. It then grows a narrow bright branch toward the still-sealed grille. The novel combination demonstrates choice and refusal without proving humanlike cognition.
- **Historical evidence:** Scraped flue tiles carry bitter soot only on the branches leading toward structural bays. A beam has healed fungal burn scars. Agnes finds early malt measures in a wall tally followed generations later by increased smoke purchases and fewer feed entries. She can support only the conclusion that practice changed from feeding/cueing to harmful driving; she cannot know the original builders or covenant.
- **Turn:** Agnes removes `SAVE` from her chalk plan and writes `SUPPORT WITHOUT IT`. She still hopes to preserve the gallery visually, but accepts that her deed contains no claim on the colony or its labor.
- **Dialogue aims and tactic change:** This is a nonverbal exchange between Agnes and the Underbreath. Agnes wants an answer about safe structural help; the colony wants the fresh-air path opened and the smoke path avoided. Agnes shifts from tap mimicry to consequential three-route choices. The colony shifts from withdrawal and echo to refusing feed, releasing the test load, and indicating the grille. No human words are assigned to it.

### 5. The restored mistake — 450 words

- **Restoration task and reversal:** Before understanding the soot, Agnes has already relaid the cracked flue throat and restored an antique bellows shutter because its profile matched the earliest visible plaster. A hard storm reverses the chimney draft. Bitter residue and hearth smoke are forced into the dowel network; the Underbreath flees upward, contracts the colonized gallery frame, and briefly lifts the sag—the seductive image of old grandeur returning.
- **Disclosure:** Silas arrives with his forebear's maintenance book. Its diagrams match the bellows routes; late entries record dead brood, shrinking feed, and orders to drive the “under breath” harder for festival crowds. It corroborates the observed mechanism but does not settle ancient origins or personhood.
- **Escalation/turn:** A smoke-driven contraction tears a rotten mortise. The gallery lurches over the hall; closing the bellows alone would leave the colony trapped in hot oak and the unsupported frame free to fall. Agnes's faithful repair has made the crisis, and only an adaptive change can solve it.
- **Dialogue aims and tactic change:** Agnes initially wants Silas to help preserve the restored system long enough to shore it; Silas wants her to abandon the gallery and confront what his family concealed. She begins defensively with calculations, then asks for the book and accepts its evidence. He begins with accusation and warning, then changes to full disclosure and practical aid when the frame tears.

### 6. Cut the grand room free — 700 words

- **Spatial problem:** Agnes and Silas stand below the bowed front bay. Independent shores are stacked along the wall; the main safe exit is behind them; the copper grille and cellar vent are opposite the smoke source. The colony is visibly drying through translucent cords while its contraction jerks the rotten frame.
- **Clue-powered plan:** Agnes uses every restoration clue rather than a new spell: she wedges the independent shores under the sound inner beam; Silas opens the cleared drain-side grille for cool damp air; Agnes rips out her reconstructed bellows shutter; and she saws through the decorated outer cantilever at the old scarf joint, isolating the rotten bay from the colonized dowel network.
- **Irreversible choice:** The finest gilded gallery front will fall onto the cleared, roped-off floor. Agnes orders Silas to knock the final wedge even though it destroys the feature that justified her purchase. She keeps the exit open for the Underbreath instead of recapping the cask to preserve it in place.
- **Supernatural/physical climax:** The colony first tightens against the familiar smoke command, then detects air and damp through the opened route. It releases the gallery peg and withdraws toward the cask as Agnes cuts. The outer bay crashes without injuring either human; independent shores receive the remaining load. The colony's retreat is its action, not Agnes's magical command.
- **Resolution turn:** After smoke clears, fresh cords occupy both the cask habitat and the open grille, while no cord returns to the now-separated gallery stump. Its choice to remain is evidence, not a permanent promise. Agnes pockets the deed rather than using it as an argument.
- **Dialogue aims and tactic change:** Agnes wants Silas to sacrifice the carved bay immediately; Silas initially wants to save both her investment and the structure by adding more shores. She shifts from clipped commands to showing the load path and naming what must be lost. He shifts from delay to trusting her calculation and taking the decisive wedge. The Underbreath wants escape from the smoke while Agnes needs it to release safely; it shifts from panicked contraction to withdrawal only after they change the physical choices available.

### 7. A hall that carries itself — 300 words

- **Compressed aftermath:** Weeks later, the rebuilt hall is smaller. New independent oak posts visibly carry the ceiling; surviving madder paint and one jagged gilded gallery end remain conserved rather than cosmetically completed. A public notice records the uncertain evidence and identifies the cellar as an occupied habitat excluded from ordinary storage and service. Agnes keeps the grille open and provides water and malt without conditioning them on labor. Silas has contributed timber and the maintenance book, not received absolution.
- **Ordinary resolution:** The harvest gathering fits without aristocratic spectacle: neighbors use the speaking step, musicians play on the floor, and Agnes serves the first round. The pub is financially open, structurally safe, and socially useful; the secret and ownership question are settled for this story even though ordinary upkeep continues.
- **Dialogue aims and tactic change:** Agnes wants Silas to acknowledge that the reduced room is enough; Silas wants assurance she will not turn the Underbreath into a novelty or hidden support again. She begins with a dry joke about lost seating and shifts to the concrete habitat notice and independent posts. He begins by inspecting rather than praising and changes to helping open the doors once he sees the terms embodied.
- **Ending image:** Music starts. The freestanding posts take the weight without a murmur; beneath the open copper grille, warm barley air rises and one small, voluntary tremor dimples the foam in Agnes's first poured mug.

## Canon constraints

- Keep every new fact local to the Hollow Cask and its unnamed district. No crossover or claim about all inns, fungi, hospitality, fermentation, ownership, or restoration.
- Only the living Underbreath performs magic. The pub, cask-shell, grille, deed, music, smoke, food, and joinery are nonliving materials, cues, routes, or resources.
- Do not equate responsiveness with proof that the whole building is alive. Establish the colony's life and bounded agency through metabolism, growth, reproduction, injury, preference, and novel response.
- The deed does not confer ownership of the Underbreath. Feed is care, not purchase; its final continued presence is voluntary and revisable.
- Do not make the colony a ghost, dead-person copy, memory store, promise tester, land guardian, dragon, universal spirit, or voice of ancient builders. It has no connection to the Morrow supper, Briar Host, magical wig, Hadrik's plot armor, hidden dragon collectives, Ninth Hollow, or the Hollow Regent.
- Historical claims must remain proportionate: fabric stratigraphy, observed operation, the local tally, Silas's book, and testimony establish use and harm, but not the builders' identity, absolute date, universal law, or perfectly recoverable original arrangement.
- Preserve teen-accessible non-graphic peril, tactile action, humane personhood treatment, and a complete ending rather than a sequel hook.

## Failure modes to watch

- Do not let Silas explain the secret before Agnes's work produces the evidence; his book should corroborate the mechanism only after the living and material clues exist.
- Do not let tapping alone prove life or personhood. Keep the metabolism, reproduction, healing, injury, novel choice, and refusal evidence on the page.
- Do not make the Underbreath solve the climax by sudden new power. Its only decisive act is the already seeded ability to release/withdraw from colonized oak when Agnes creates a viable route.
- Keep restoration procedures spatially clear: what bears weight, what is cut, where smoke travels, where the exit lies, and why closing the bellows alone is insufficient.
- Do not romanticize the old arrangement as consent purchased by ale, nor make Agnes's feeding demand a service in return.
- Do not erase financial consequence. The smaller hall, lost seating, salvaged material, and visible new posts should make her ethical choice costly but survivable.
- Avoid therapy, policy, or theme-certifying dialogue. Let the deed, open grille, independent posts, cut gallery, and habitat notice embody the resolution.
- Do not overstate history from the ledger, use real-world licensing or architectural claims without support, or treat grandeur as aristocratic luxury when the established evidence points to communal importance.
- End on the changed material relationship and voluntary tremor, without an ominous tease, universal moral, future monster, or promised sequel.
