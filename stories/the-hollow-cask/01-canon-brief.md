## Planning guidance

- Keep the setting independent and locally bounded unless a crossover is essential.
- Make restoration the discovery method: each major clue should emerge through a credible task such as opening a wall, lifting a floor, tracing damp, repairing a flue, clearing a drain, stabilizing a cellar, or stripping incompatible later work.
- Establish former grandeur through several converging evidence streams rather than one convenient legend.
- Define the secret’s ontology before plotting the resolution: alive, nonliving focus, continuation, collective practice, organism, or deliberately unresolved person-like phenomenon.
- Define who supplies magical agency.
- Define observable limits, costs, failure modes, and what the restoration changes.
- Establish why decay concealed or constrained the secret and why repair brings the conflict to a decision.
- Give the protagonist a real financial, emotional, or vocational investment without letting sunk cost justify coercion.
- Let local residents disagree about the pub’s history and future without converting every rumor into fact.
- Make “bringing it back” a concrete final choice: exact reconstruction, adaptive reuse, visible preservation of damage, shared stewardship, release, or deliberate refusal of the former arrangement.
- Resolve the secret and the protagonist’s ownership claim within the story even if the pub’s ordinary commercial future continues afterward.
