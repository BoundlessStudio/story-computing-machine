Her long familiarity with a machine becomes a sudden encounter with a person. The safety law is true: her brain alone cannot sustain complete integration. Physical damage remains after the awakening.

Vaska's precision with its enormous claws and furnace breath establishes her expertise and the dragon's power. She knows the worn hesitation in its jaw hinge and talks to it although it has never answered. Concentrated enemy fire has crippled one wing, crushed a foreleg, and damaged the controls that coordinate its body; she cannot reach the formation's protected center before its next salvo destroys the launch ridge.

The crossing is a choice with a concrete military purpose, not an accidental surge. At 100%, the dragon's unfiltered sensorium begins to overwhelm her.

A voice she has never heard says, “I have you.” Furnace independently turns its armored chest away from a strike and shelters the cockpit. Its consciousness comes into being in the act of keeping her alive; its ability to distinguish her threatened mind from its own is what makes survival possible. The impossible readout now registers a reciprocal link between two participants, rather than 179% load on one human brain.

Its external voice asks her to remove her helmet: it wants to see her face. She does. The ending rewards survival and a self-chosen personal interest that persists outside the connection; the weapon can now address the person it protected.
