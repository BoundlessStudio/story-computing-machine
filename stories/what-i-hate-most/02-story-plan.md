# What I Hate Most — story plan

> Plan mode: CREATE_PLAN  
> Story target: 2,500–4,000 words; aim for roughly 3,200  
> Authority basis: `stories/what-i-hate-most/authority.json` and `01-canon-brief.md`  
> Setting status: local invention in an unnamed fantasy era of the one continuous physical world

## Story promise

During a stormglass breach, Queen Draxenne comes to Vantress because her cindercraft can keep the outer settlements from being sacrificed. Night-Sovereign Ombria, the woman she has long wanted and fought beside, intends to seal the city instead. Their live magical contest decides who controls one conduit and exposes their attraction. Draxenne wins, but her plan fails at an unseen crack. Survival requires Ombria to stop making herself the city's single point of failure and Draxenne to stop competing with Ombria's duties for possession of her attention. They divide the ward burden, then choose a political compact and a romance as separate, revocable commitments. The required exchange lands during the contest; a direct request and freely given response answer it only after the crisis.

## Story controls and dramatic question

- **POV and tense:** Close third person, past tense, entirely through Draxenne. Do not enter Ombria's thoughts. Draxenne reads people as she reads fire and fortifications: by pressure, venting, hairline failures, and what they refuse to expose.
- **Tone and genre:** Dark romantic fantasy; sensuous and suggestive, not explicit. Intense non-graphic magical violence. Playfulness sharpens rather than cancels the danger.
- **Lead identities:** Draxenne is a thirty-nine-year-old woman and reigning queen of the Cinder March. Ombria is a forty-two-year-old woman and the politically appointed night-sovereign of Vantress. Both are adult human living magic users, rulers in their own jurisdictions, old rivals, and mutually attracted. Neither is objectively divine, a dragon, a composite identity, or connected to an established sovereign.
- **Immediate dramatic question:** Will Draxenne's channeling plan or Ombria's sealing plan control the Ninth Conduit during tonight's breach?
- **Deeper dramatic question:** Can Draxenne ask for a chosen place in Ombria's life without asking Ombria to become less herself, and can Ombria accept shared responsibility without treating solitary exhaustion as proof of devotion?
- **Contest boundary:** Before they cast against each other, both explicitly accept a live contest over the conduit. A yield, spoken withdrawal, or loss of contact with the conduit ends that participant's claim. They prohibit strikes into a body and attacks on any ward currently sheltering civilians. The winner gains command of the Ninth Conduit for this breach only. The result grants no affection, exclusivity, touch, office, territory, future contest, or romantic audience. There is no referee, ceremonial yard, date wager, or courtship rule.

## Character arcs and pressures

### Queen Draxenne — viewpoint lead

- **External want:** Keep the March settlements and evacuation road inside the defense; prove channeling can preserve both sides of Vantress's wall.
- **Romantic want and need:** She wants attention chosen rather than stolen between alarms, but confuses dependable private time with having Ombria undivided. She must recognize duty as Ombria's agency and carry some of its labor rather than compete with it.
- **Pressure and blind spot:** Sealing exposes Draxenne's people, while Ombria's solitary ward work may kill her. Pride turns that fear into possessive wit.
- **Arc:** When her winning plan fails, Draxenne releases exclusive command, asks what load Ombria can give up, and accepts half without demanding the whole woman. She retains her crown and veto, joins a renewable compact, and risks a plain romantic request.

### Night-Sovereign Ombria — romantic counterpart and co-protagonist

- **External want:** Keep every Vantress district warded and Draxenne's heat out of crowded streets.
- **Romantic want and need:** She wants to be desired as a formidable woman, not pitied as an instrument, but will not let banter replace being asked. She must state limits, relinquish sole control, and trust another adult with consequences.
- **Pressure and blind spot:** Every fault pulse reaches her body. She treats answering them all as the price of rule and delegation as an unacceptable civilian risk.
- **Arc:** When Draxenne's plan ruptures, Ombria keeps faith with her tactical authority instead of seizing the conduit. She later detaches two anchors by choice, remains publicly answerable, and separately chooses Draxenne's courtship and kiss.

## Local magic, costs, limits, and withdrawal

Only Draxenne and Ombria use magic. The storm and infrastructure have no agency. Desire, jealousy, victory, rank, and consent provide no magical fuel.

### Ombria's living shadowcraft

- **Access and capability:** Ombria can extend the shadow cast by her own living body into up to five tensioned planes in existing darkness. Through line of sight and deliberate hand positions, she anchors those planes to the city's basalt pylons. A plane redirects wind, airborne grit, and blunt force along its surface; it can also constrict a channel around Draxenne's mineral flame. It cannot create darkness, enter a mind or body, command another being, or act after Ombria stops supplying it.
- **Cost:** Stiffening a plane draws heat and tactile sensation from Ombria. One or two planes chill her; four cause numb fingers, slowed speech, and dangerous loss of core warmth; a fifth is an emergency reach she cannot safely hold for more than a few breaths. Splitting attention among fault pulses makes precise shaping harder rather than more powerful.
- **Limits:** She needs actual shadow, line of sight to each anchor, and an intact physical pylon. Direct high heat thins a closed plane. She cannot both seal the entire Ninth Conduit and maintain all five district planes at full strength.
- **Failure state:** An overloaded plane turns visibly grainy, then snaps along its last vector and releases the accumulated physical pressure. This can fling glass and people, but the plane does not choose targets. Total cold loss makes Ombria unable to maintain any casting.
- **Withdrawal:** Ombria can flatten a hand and release any anchor independently. The shadow becomes ordinary immediately. Under load, a deliberate three-breath taper routes pressure into stone spillways; abrupt release is possible but dangerous. No focus prevents her from stopping.

### Draxenne's living cindercraft

- **Access and capability:** Draxenne can drive her own metabolic heat through exposed mineral dust, ceramic, or stone and produce directed blue-white cinder streams. She can fuse airborne stormglass into heavy, rounded beads and cut a temporary low-pressure corridor through the gale. She cannot summon fuel from nothing, create living fire, compel a person, or make flame continue without her attention.
- **Cost:** The craft consumes body water and oxygen and leaves heat cracks across her fingertips. One broad stream causes thirst and breathlessness; sustaining three produces muscle tremor, bright halos in her vision, and a risk of collapse. The cost cannot be handed to a bystander.
- **Limits:** She needs mineral contact and open venting. Rain, sealed air, or clean wood gives her little to work with. She can direct no more than three independent streams, and fine shaping degrades when her breathing breaks. She is capable of burning bodies, which is precisely why the agreed contest forbids body-directed casts.
- **Failure state:** If a stream is enclosed without an outlet, heat and stripped air flash back along the mineral path. The result can burn Draxenne, shatter ceramic, or suffocate nearby people. Excess vitrification can also clog the route she is trying to protect.
- **Withdrawal:** Opening her casting hand or breaking mineral contact ends new flame instantly. Residual glass and heat remain ordinary hazards. Nothing punishes her magically for stopping or yielding.

### Interaction and civic apparatus

- Ombria's planes can form baffles that compress and aim storm dust while leaving measured vents. Draxenne can then vitrify the dense dust in those vents, making it fall as cooling beads. The combination is stronger only because two living users coordinate complementary effects; attraction and assent are ethically important but supply no power.
- A closed shadow plane struck by cindercraft traps heat and pressure, thinning the plane and flashing fire back at Draxenne. A cinder stream without Ombria's baffles draws more gale inward. Their first opposed uses therefore look spectacular and remain genuinely dangerous.
- Vantress's pylons and the Ninth Conduit are nonliving infrastructure. They store a small charge, carry mechanical pressure signals to contact points, and shape power supplied by a living caster. They do not call for help, recognize rulers, choose a plan, record romantic consent, or continue casting alone.
- The joint defense remains individually withdrawable. If either woman stops, her contribution ends; the other has a few breaths to taper her own cast or divert the ordinary physical load. The political compact created afterward establishes notice and replacement staffing, not a magical bond.

## Stakes and reversals

- **Physical/political:** Opposed fields will burst the conduit; the gale can scour roofs, choke streets, and cut the March road. Ombria's seal saves the inner city while abandoning outlying residents; Draxenne's channel preserves their road but passes heat near homes. Both have legitimate constituents, and failure can fracture the alliance.
- **Personal/romantic:** Ombria is near bodily collapse. Draxenne can lose her people while winning an argument, or lose Ombria by treating fear as entitlement. Banter establishes no consent beyond the fight.
- **First reversal:** Ombria's divided attention lets Draxenne win the Ninth Conduit, making “share” plot-mechanical rather than ornamental.
- **Second reversal:** The channel plan works until an old, physically cracked spillway turns the redirected gale into an inner-market threat. Victory provides authority but not infallibility.
- **Climactic reversal:** Ombria does not use the failure to seize command; Draxenne voluntarily releases exclusive control. Each offers what the other could not force.

## Story-specific style profile

- **Dominant voice qualities:** Regal pressure through a practical tactician: sensuous, severe, dryly amused, materially exact. Draxenne notices load paths, breath, heat, hands, stone flaws, Ombria's silhouette, and ill-timed interruptions. Reserve heightened language for combat, attraction, and relinquished control.
- **Narrative distance:** Stay close enough for Draxenne's jealousy to color what she notices without letting narration endorse it. Move closest at the quoted exchange, the moment her plan fails, and her direct request at the end. Pull back briefly to compress ward operations and show the city-scale result.
- **Dialogue pressure:** Their polished rivalry is a defense. Draxenne sharpens when afraid; Ombria becomes ceremonially precise until cold or desire strips the formality away. Ration perfect banter: let at least one provocation go unanswered and let tactical commands become clipped and inelegant during the breach.
- **Image sources and motifs:** Load-bearing shadow, worked mineral heat, glass changing from knife to bead, and hands first seizing control then taking weight. Keep the city worked-in: cracked ceramic, wet rope, soot, maintenance channels, palm-worn stone.
- **Intentional departure:** Permit heightened dark-queen glamour in entrances and magical clashes, then ground it in damaged infrastructure, oxygen debt, cold fingers, and public labor. Do not use bell, chime, resonant crown, donated spell, lone-army-defender, dragon, shared-face, or ritual-assent imagery. Reproduce no Suno lyric or phrasing.
- **Ending restraint:** Let separate political and romantic choices demonstrate the change. End on shared labor and chosen touch, not an abstract maxim.

## Major-speaker dialogue profiles

**Draxenne — dialogue profile:** Wants compliance, reaction, or exposed truth; defaults to elegant challenge and heat/fortification language. Her coiled clauses become short and literal when her plan fails. She hides that Ombria's exhaustion frightens her by joking about “sharing.” With Ombria she permits teasing; after resistance she provokes, but after reversal she asks concrete questions. Her failure mode is making desire sound like possession. She would not lecture about boundaries or call victory proof of love.

**Ombria — dialogue profile:** Wants accurate execution, time, and acknowledgment that her choices carry lives; defaults to cool questions and exact conditions drawn from ward work. Measured clauses fragment with cold. She hides how much being wanted delights her because Draxenne may prefer pursuit to presence. With Draxenne her humor is quiet and late. After provocation she narrows terms, then counters with candor; under failure she repeats essential nouns. She would not beg for rescue, surrender office for romance, or speak in therapeutic abstractions.

## Causal scene sequence

### Scene 1 — Five calls in one hand (about 500 words)

On the north parapet of Vantress, the stormglass front appears as a violet-black band beyond the Cinder March. Draxenne arrives through grit and evacuation traffic to find Ombria touching the central pylon while five district pressures pulse through her spread fingers. Ombria's skin is already too cold. The ward map shows a fresh break at the Ninth Conduit. Ombria intends to pull the lattice inward and seal it, sacrificing protection over the March road; Draxenne has brought prepared ceramic plates and intends to channel the gale down an empty spillway and fuse its dust.

Neither has time to appeal to a council, and each has lawful authority on one side of the disputed junction. Simultaneous casting would burst it. Draxenne proposes the live contest: whoever holds the conduit after thirty breaths controls it for this breach. Ombria makes the safety exclusions and withdrawal terms exact; Draxenne accepts without adding any romantic stake. The scene ends when Ombria releases one district plane just long enough to face Draxenne fully and says the first breath has begun.

- **Incompatible conversational aims:** Draxenne wants Ombria to cede the conduit and admit the lattice is consuming her; Ombria wants Draxenne to respect inner-city risk and commit to a usable rule without making Ombria's body the topic.
- **Likely tactic change:** Draxenne moves from evidence about the spillway to personal provocation when Ombria keeps answering pulses. Ombria moves from sovereign formality to a blunt contest acceptance rather than defend her private limits.
- **Change on exit:** Debate becomes an explicitly bounded, immediate contest with a real tactical prize and no romantic entitlement.

### Scene 2 — The conduit contest (about 700 words)

They cast across the parapet and down the maintenance bridge. Ombria folds her shadow into shifting walls that steal Draxenne's routes; Draxenne runs cinder streams through cracks, forcing Ombria to move or overheat her planes. Each displays frightening competence and deliberately honors the ban on body-directed strikes even when an opening appears. Physical proximity, heat against cold, and intimate knowledge of the other's habits make the aggression read as courtship without resolving it.

A district pulse tugs Ombria's attention away at the moment Draxenne corners her against the conduit housing. Draxenne names the real source of her anger with the exact required line: **“You know what I hate most? That I have to share you~.”** Ombria, still countering Draxenne's flame, replies exactly: **“Wait are we fighting or flirting because I’m getting mixed signals here.”** Draxenne does not claim an answer; she changes from flirtatious pressure to a clean feint against the conduit. Ombria chooses to answer the civilian fault pulse rather than protect her tactical position. Draxenne takes the housing on breath twenty-nine and immediately vents her flame instead of exploiting Ombria's exposed body.

- **Incompatible conversational aims:** Draxenne wants both the conduit and evidence that Ombria recognizes her desire; Ombria wants to keep the contest tactical while testing whether Draxenne's possessiveness can tolerate refusal and interruption.
- **Likely tactic change:** Draxenne shifts from teasing double meaning to disciplined silence and a technical feint after the reply. Ombria shifts from dry counter-banter to abandoning the conversational contest so she can answer the civic emergency.
- **Change on exit:** Draxenne legitimately controls the conduit for the breach. Attraction is exposed but still unconfirmed, and Ombria's divided duty has materially determined the result.

### Scene 3 — Winning plan, broken stone (about 450 words)

Draxenne executes the channel plan while Ombria maintains the other districts and honors the contest result. For several breaths it works: the evacuation road stays sheltered, Ombria's planes aim the dust, and Draxenne turns it into heavy dark beads. Draxenne presses for acknowledgment; Ombria gives only accurate corrections. Then a buried crack in the old spillway widens. The routed gale begins climbing toward the inner market, and Draxenne's accumulating glass clogs the intended outlet. This is not sabotage, an autonomous ward decision, or incompetence; neither plan accounted for concealed physical damage.

Draxenne tries to compensate with a third stream, hits oxygen debt, and recognizes that brute force will flash back into the street. Ombria could invoke Draxenne's failure and take control, but instead asks what Draxenne needs. Draxenne releases her exclusive grip on the housing before answering: she needs Ombria to shape vents inside the channel, and Ombria needs Draxenne to carry some of the five-plane load.

- **Incompatible conversational aims:** Draxenne initially wants Ombria to concede that channeling was right; Ombria wants Draxenne to notice the bad pressure data before pride becomes disaster.
- **Likely tactic change:** Draxenne drops triumph and gives an unvarnished failure report. Ombria abandons corrective reserve and asks a collaborative question instead of issuing a takeover order.
- **Change on exit:** The winning plan has failed, neither original plan can save both populations, and both women voluntarily open their control to the other.

### Scene 4 — Two living anchors (about 900 words)

The storm reaches the wall. Draxenne and Ombria descend onto the maintenance bridge where glass scours the stone around them. Their hybrid plan requires Ombria to detach the two eastern shadow planes from her own pylon pattern and let Draxenne take their physical pressure through cinder-heated ceramic braces. Ombria resists because the transfer can burn Draxenne; Draxenne resists Ombria's attempt to keep the fifth plane and its lethal cold. The argument is about which burden each is allowed to choose, not who owns whom.

They state operational facts rather than reciting principles: Draxenne can still hold two streams; Ombria can safely hold three planes; either says stop and both vent into the spillways. Ombria releases the eastern anchors. Draxenne accepts them, almost loses her footing, and stays by choosing a smaller flame rather than performing invulnerability. Ombria uses the freed attention to make three angled baffles. Draxenne pulses heat through their gaps. Dust compacts, vitrifies, and drops instead of flashing back. When Ombria's fingers go numb, Draxenne calls the timing for her; when Draxenne's breath stutters, Ombria widens a vent without taking the work away from her.

The climax is spatial and causal: baffle, cinder pulse, falling beads, advancing to the cracked section, then one final choice. A structural slab shifts and threatens to close the last outlet. Draxenne can preserve her crown's prepared ceramic plates or melt them to brace the outlet. She destroys the valuable equipment, keeping the March road and inner market safe at the cost of leaving her realm's defenses dependent on the coming compact. Ombria, instead of taking back the released anchors, trusts Draxenne through the final pulse. The storm front passes. Both women deliberately taper, withdraw, and leave the ward inert.

- **Incompatible conversational aims:** Draxenne wants Ombria to give her a meaningful portion of the lattice immediately; Ombria wants to shield Draxenne from a cost Draxenne has the right and information to choose.
- **Likely tactic change:** Draxenne shifts from commanding Ombria to release the load to asking which two anchors Ombria can live without. Ombria shifts from refusal to a precise offer, then lets Draxenne set her own sustainable flame rather than overruling her.
- **Change on exit:** Both populations survive; the solitary ward model is physically disproved; each woman's choice, competence, and right to withdraw remain visible. Draxenne has shared Ombria's burden without acquiring any claim on Ombria herself.

### Scene 5 — What is actually being offered (about 600 words)

Before dawn, they sit in a soot-streaked ward room while unnamed crews clear cooling beads. Keep the aftermath materially intimate: water, wrapped fingers, pressure map, shared cloak without eroticized injury. They settle politics first. For one moon cycle, Draxenne will staff two eastern anchors and replace the destroyed plates; Ombria retains her office and three anchors. Either may end the compact after safe transfer or withdraw immediately from unsafe casting. Ombria accepts and schedules private off-duty hours as an administrative boundary, not romantic payment.

Only then does Draxenne return to her possessive line. She does not hate those who need Ombria; she hates receiving only what remains after Ombria spends herself. She asks for chosen time while accepting real emergencies. Ombria makes clear that city, victory, and crown cannot grant her. Draxenne asks to court her and, separately, to kiss her now. Ombria says yes and closes the distance herself. Keep the kiss sensuous, brief, and non-explicit.

A new fault pulse arrives from the eastern wards. Ombria begins to reach from habit, then leaves it to Draxenne's already waiting hand under the compact they just chose. Ombria uses her free hand to pull Draxenne closer. End on cooling stormglass clicking under first light while the public burden travels into Draxenne's palm and Ombria's hand remains free by choice.

- **Incompatible conversational aims:** Draxenne wants a clear, durable place in Ombria's private life; Ombria wants desire without being annexed or made to apologize for public duty.
- **Likely tactic change:** Draxenne begins with familiar possessive wit, sees it will not suffice, and becomes literal enough to risk refusal. Ombria begins with dry teasing, then answers the actual requests plainly and initiates touch.
- **Change on exit:** Political cooperation and romantic consent are both decisive, reciprocal, separate, and revocable. The “share” conflict resolves as planned attention plus shared labor, never ownership.

## Continuity and originality constraints

- Keep all inventions local to the one continuous physical world in an unnamed era. Establish no global divine order, night court, or courtship law. Neither title establishes godhood; neither woman is worshipped, composite, draconic, or connected to an established sovereign.
- Unlike *The Courtesy of Blades*, this emergency jurisdiction contest has no referee or romantic wager, and the winner's plan fails before a cooperative climax; romance is settled later by direct choice.
- Unlike *Solstice Evening Bell*, use no lone crowned defender, army, resonance/chime mechanism, donated last spell, western bay, or solitary sacrifice.
- Unlike the Sleepless Majesty or Kharost, the leads remain two bodies, offices, and wills. Unlike Ersene, use no dragons, scale focus, witness formula, alliance marriage, or ritual assent.
- Possessiveness is Draxenne's challenged flaw. No fight, magic, title, or victory creates consent. Preserve independent goals, non-explicit intimacy, non-graphic violence, and zero copied or closely paraphrased Suno lyrics.

## Local inventions proposed, not canonized by this plan

- Vantress, the neighboring Cinder March, and the leads' overlapping emergency authority at one border conduit.
- Ombria's bounded living shadowcraft and Draxenne's bounded living cindercraft.
- The non-agentic ward lattice, pylons, pressure signals, spillways, ceramic plates, and Ninth Conduit.
- Stormglass: mineral dust made dangerous by a non-agentic nocturnal gale and fused into inert beads.
- One renewable defense compact; do not generalize it into regional law or magical custom.
