# Opus review: The Seraph of the Infinite Gear

Independent audit for a later editing pass. It is not a `review.md` verdict,
does not change canon state, and does not authorize edits by itself.

## Verdict

- Dialogue: PASS — the exchanges are grounded, the two voices are easy to tell apart, and every exchange shifts knowledge, leverage or trust; the findings are precision and beat polish.
- Prose: PASS — concrete and restrained, with no dash, reversal or fragment tics; two mechanism paragraphs lean toward a manual.
- Continuity and prompt: PASS — delivers the prompt's discovery at the final gear and a full visible rewind at about 3,270 words; two small knowledge and staging slips.
- Edit scope: polish
- Existing review.md said: Verdict PASS, Dialogue: PASS; agree. Its claim that every exchange "preserves grounded knowledge and uptake" slightly overstates things (L249, L257, L299), but nothing it missed is material.

This story's dialogue works better than most in the collection. The Seraph speaks almost only in commands and questions and holds back every pleasure the narration gives her. Sovekh talks in concrete, rueful, rehearsed anecdote. With the tags covered, their lines are still easy to tell apart, and neither of them trades corrections or aphorisms. The recollection test holds up, the pressed "What did I do?" run builds real pressure, and the story ends on a song rather than a lesson. The edit pass should fix two lines in the decisive exchange that claim more than the page shows, cut one bare cue question, replace one legalistic word and clean up a few repeated beats. It should not push either voice toward wit, explanation or closure.

## Dialogue findings

### D1 · Minor · Decisive exchange claims more than the page shows

- Where: L249–L257, at the great gear before the mirrors open ("I remembered you" through "You left out")
- Text:
> You could wake up and look at me as if there'd never been anything.
> You left out the times I told you to go.
- Problem: This is a Ground (staging and accuracy) slip at the story's pivot. (1) The story has just shown that after each rewind she woke in the cradle while he hid "Until you came down" (L139), so she never woke and looked at him. "Could" also reads as "was able to," which blurs his complaint. (2) He did tell her she sent him away, at L195 and again at L239. What he actually did was lead with the good part and answer "You were angry" until she asked twice. Read literally, her accusation is false, and that slightly weakens the refusal it motivates. Both lines can be understood, so this is polish, but it is the turn the ending rests on.
- Fix: Use the smallest possible edits. In L249, make it "You'd come down and look at me…" so it matches L139. In L257, make the charge match what he did ("tried to leave out"), or name the specific dodge. Keep "I can tell you" and her refusal as the turn, and add no explanation after it. Illustrative only: "You told me I was angry. I had to ask twice."

### D2 · Minor · One bare cue question in the testimony run

- Where: L175–L199, after the recollection test ("What did I say?" through "Did I tell you why?")
- Text:
> Then what happened?
- Problem: In this stretch all four of the Seraph's lines are short prompts. Her reactions (the white plains at L183, the urge to laugh at L193) appear only in narration. That withholding suits her, because she won't confirm his account aloud. "Then what happened?" is different: it has no stake of hers in it and only cues his next installment, which edges toward an explanatory chorus.
- Fix: Cut L189 and let Sovekh go on after a short narrated pause. He is eager to tell, and her silence shows she won't feed him. Keep "What did I say?", "Did I take you?" and "Did I tell you why?", since each asks about her own conduct. Do not give her a spoken line about the snow or the plains.

### D3 · Minor · Legalistic word in her demand for proof

- Where: L107, right after "We've spoken before. Here."
- Text:
> Show me something besides your account.
- Problem: He has said one sentence. Calling it "your account" makes her sound like a magistrate taking testimony, which is the register the owner objects to across the collection. Her other commands ("Try now." "Stay there.") are plain.
- Fix: Replace it with a plain demand that points to what she does next. At L113 she checks by touch. Illustrative only: "Show me something I can touch."

### D4 · Minor · Repeated and stock beats around his lines

- Where: L77 and L85 (the song scene), L171 (start of his testimony), L247 (after "I knew that by then"), L259 (before he sings)
- Text:
> He took his time, rubbing warmth into his throat
> Sovekh rubbed his throat.
> He made no attempt to soften it. Somehow that was worse than his first evasions.
> He opened his mouth, closed it, then began the invocation.
- Problem: He rubs his throat twice within eight lines. "He opened his mouth, closed it" and "He swallowed" (L171) are stock beats. L247 explains a line that already landed ("You have to sleep after the turning. I knew that by then.").
- Fix: Cut the second throat-rub at L85 or give him different business. At L247, cut "Somehow that was worse than his first evasions." and keep "He made no attempt to soften it." if a beat is needed. At L259, replace the open-and-close with something from his trade, such as the breath he takes before a phrase, or cut straight to the invocation. Leave "He nodded again, too quickly" (L153), which works.

## Prose findings

### P1 · Minor · Mechanism paragraphs read like a manual, and one clause raises an unasked question

- Where: L113–L115 (after the count in the spoke) and L125 (the little wheel)
- Text:
> He had climbed beyond its reach before the first enclosed dawn; there would never have been another Sovekh waiting on the ground.
> Its outer rim could wind that recollection back, as far as the first enclosed dawn. It kept no spare record of what was unwound.
- Problem: Two nearly consecutive rule paragraphs stack short statements of function. The style guide asks for operative limits to be clear "without turning prose into a manual." "Another Sovekh" brings in the idea of duplicates, which the story never uses. It also invites the reader to ask what a future return would do to him once she sets him on the ground at L311.
- Fix: Cut the "another Sovekh" clause. "The upper rings remained outside the return, keeping their occupants with them" already states the rule, and "Even the jars could only empty once" is good concrete proof. In L125, keep the facts the test depends on (it winds back only as far as the first enclosed dawn, keeps no record of what it unwinds, and leaves her body with its wear), but put them in fewer, less list-like sentences.

## Continuity and prompt findings

### C1 · Minor · Narration claims knowledge of her lost days

- Where: L299, the aftermath on the platform
- Text:
> She had never gone down to hear what they were saying.
- Problem: This is a Ground (knowledge) slip in close third. The story's premise is that she has lost twenty-one days and cannot know what she did in them. Sovekh reports only "We stayed here" for the first loop (L187). "Never" claims what she cannot know, in the paragraph that matters most for what she does next.
- Fix: Limit it to what she remembers. Illustrative only: "In the days she remembered, she had never gone down to hear what they were saying." Do not add a stated plan; the glance at the council house is enough.

### C2 · Minor · The seat he knew about doesn't match the seat she makes

- Where: L203 (his testimony) and L303 (before the descent)
- Text:
> You'd already told me where I'd sit between your wings
> At the edge of the platform she lowered one wing to make a seat against her side.
> He knew where to put his feet.
- Problem: He never rode with her ("Did I take you?" / "No."). He can only know where to put his feet from her old description of a seat between her wings. She now makes a different seat, against her side under one lowered wing, which blurs the payoff that her description was real.
- Fix: Make the two match. Either she makes the seat between her wings that she once described, or L203 names the side seat. Keep "She almost told him to move them, then found that they were correctly placed." as it is.
