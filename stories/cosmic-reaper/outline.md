# The Last Firmament

## Promised experience

After septillion years of taking the souls of civilizations, gods, and worlds, Death brings its scythe to the final city under dying galaxies. The required encounter is between Death and one immortal soul who refuses to surrender. Discovering whose soul it is must make Death understand why this ending is wrong. I read the dying galaxies, the single holdout, and the figure in the original as a promise of dread and wonder on the scale of a creation myth, concentrated in a face-to-face encounter. The last act should give the discovery a consequence at the scale of the sky. The soul's identity, its prior relation to Death, and the fate of the city and universe are open.

## Takes considered

- **The dying sky.** Death arrives to reap the universe's last soul and finds the universe itself alive beneath a spent firmament. The central confrontation lets the soul refuse the blade and obliges Death to find a different place to use it.
- **The predecessor.** The first Reaper waits to hand down the ending it once inflicted on another cosmos. The central scene is a contest over whether Death will accept its predecessor's account of the first harvest and repeat it.
- **The one born early.** The last inhabitant narrates her bid to be recognized as the first soul of a successor universe. The encounter turns on what she can show Death before the scythe comes down.

Choose the first. Death's experience with dying bodies becomes necessary to make sense of the refusal, and its choice determines the fate of the city and the universe.

## Provisional direction

Seva is the immortal soul of the universe. The universe should have shed its sky when its stars died, but the spires of Lethra are fused into the firmament. Refugees built there to catch the last heat. Now the retreating rim of the sky pulls against the spires and buckles down over the living layer. Death comes expecting to take Seva from a body it believes is spent, then return to the first landing, where a builder once made room for it to sit. Its scythe can part things at their join. The living universe can shed any number of skies. With this one still bearing down, the body could die. If Death took Seva's soul, the body would die and Seva would continue among the dead.

Seva refuses the offered hand and makes Death touch her wrist and the underside of the sky. Death recognizes a soul by where its pulse runs through its body. When the beat in Seva's wrist goes out through the depths behind the galaxies, Death sees whose life it was about to end. It pries up a loose scale and sees a star start to kindle in the gap. Seva begs it to spare their landing. Death wants it spared too and searches for a place to put the blade. The towers have fused with the sky above and share a footing below. Death must give up its resting place and cut the whole city from the ridge. The old sky carries Lethra with it as it peels away. The city's dead have gone on. Seva has room to grow a sky again.

## Craft plan

- **Voice.** Trial one will follow close to Death in past tense, in the temper of an exacting traveler who expects to finish a long journey. Trial two will give Death the first person as a teller who still recalls physically how it came to stand on the wrong side of its blade. Both keep Death's discovery as the turn.
- **Death** mistakes the dark for evidence that this body's time has come. It wants Seva's willing hand and, privately, an end to the walking. Years ago Neret, who drove the first spire, made Death sit beside her at the warm sill before she took its hand. It meant what it told her about coming back to that seat after its last crossing. Death gives people room to speak and speaks in complete, deliberate phrases. Under pressure its promises get specific.
- **Seva** believes Death can free her and leave her the city. She wants the Reaper to see what she and the refugees achieved, even after seeing what it cost. She talks in quick, warm runs, naming trades and people's habits. Let her be most expansive as she argues for the city she once offered others as shelter and now needs for herself.
- **The owed conversation.** In the cleft they have to say who Death has come for, why Seva will stay in her body, and what it will take to free her. Seva tells in order what happened when Neret and her people came looking for warmth and how she helped them. She is asking Death to save the landing and defending her own choice. Death owes her an answer about the landing and has its own promise to reckon with.
- **Reference casting.** Death faces forward, grave and assured, in a floor-length blue-black hood and cloak. It strides, plants, inclines, and draws. It takes the narrow way into the cleft with the confidence of the figure on the ridge. The upright scythe in its right hand first gives the traveler a stance, then becomes the lever bearing the sky and the instrument of the last stroke. Orange eyes in the dry skull bring their own light down to distinguish the two surfaces. The deep hem snags on a spire's exposed iron stay during the first slip and sweeps clear when the city falls. Keep the austere face and the force of the pose through the choice. The picture needs no change for the content target.
- **Place.** Lethra's Stair of Late Arrivals climbs between spires built on one course of stone above the bare ridge. The Brass Market steps could show how the refugees lived. The Far Lantern roof would show the galaxies. On the Stair the distant whorl of galaxies narrows to a rim one could put a hand under. The spires seem to lean into it from below while their bases pull at the same stone. At the old calendar's last winter dusk, the glazed steps have cobalt glare, dying spirals still blaze orange, the air tastes of cold iron, the stays drone in their sockets, and heat comes up through the broken nosing of a tread. Porters once carried refugees' goods up the Stair while lodgings rented out space at heated windows. The stair will leave the ridge. Its ascending line of red panes makes the city's passage into the sky visible from below.

## Scenes and ending

1. **Approach, about 15 percent.** At dusk on the burnt ridge, Death sees Neret's low red window and remembers taking her hand as her pulse left her body, with the window still full of light. It looks forward to keeping its promise once the last soul is gone. Above, the color across the sky streams upward at the edge while the spires hold it down. The climb takes Death beneath that flexing span. Summarize the breadth of the harvest in a few particulars here.
2. **The cleft, about 60 percent, the long scene.** Seva retreats into the cleft and makes Death reach in. The beat that would be confined to her wrist and chest in an ordinary reaping continues in the black depths of space. Death realizes she belongs to the whole living body and that the usual act would kill it. It raises the scythe to test the spent edge and the new heat opens before the scale crashes down. The sky catches on the spires and Seva has to stop for breath during her account. She points out the first landing to Death as the last thing she'd settle for keeping. It has yet to answer her.
3. **The stroke, about 25 percent.** They trace the join from the place where a stay rips a stone upward, down to where the first landing rests on the single reachable footing. Death tells Seva the blade must start beneath the window, and why it wished to keep the stair too. She wants to be close enough to see her city go and accepts Death's hand to step off the footing onto the ridge. It cuts from stone into the galaxies. As the spires lift, their windows blaze in the passing shell. Still alongside Death, Seva fills her lungs without bracing, and one new star catches, then others far beyond the cut. There will be roads to walk again. Death stays to see a thing it has always arrived too late for. The window, the star during the trial, and the journey Death expected to be its last prepare the ending.
