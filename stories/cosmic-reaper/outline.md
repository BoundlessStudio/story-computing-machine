# The Last Firmament

## Promised experience

After septillion years of taking the souls of civilizations, gods, and worlds, Death brings its scythe to the final city under dying galaxies. The required encounter is between Death and one immortal soul who refuses to surrender. Discovering whose soul it is must make Death understand why this ending is wrong. I read the dying galaxies, the single holdout, and the figure in the original as a promise of dread and wonder on the scale of a creation myth, concentrated in a face-to-face encounter. The last act should give the discovery a consequence at the scale of the sky. The soul's identity, its prior relation to Death, and the fate of the city and universe are open.

## Takes considered

- **The dying sky.** Death arrives to reap the universe's last soul and finds the universe itself alive beneath a spent firmament. The central confrontation lets the soul refuse the blade and obliges Death to find a different place to use it.
- **The predecessor.** The first Reaper waits to hand down the ending it once inflicted on another cosmos. The central scene is a contest over whether Death will accept its predecessor's account of the first harvest and repeat it.
- **The one born early.** The last inhabitant narrates her bid to be recognized as the first soul of a successor universe. The encounter turns on what she can show Death before the scythe comes down.

Choose the first. Death's experience with dying bodies becomes necessary to make sense of the refusal, and its choice determines the fate of the city and the universe.

## Provisional direction

Seva, the woman Death finds at Lethra, is the embodied soul of the universe. The universe grew a sky and should have shed it when its stars died. When the layer first began to loosen, refugees built Lethra with its spires driven into the firmament. They caught the last heat long enough to have a city. Now the spires pin the stiffening shell down and the shell is smothering Seva. Death comes expecting to separate a final soul from a body it takes for spent, leaving the city standing empty. Its blade can part things at their join. Putting an end to Seva's embodied life would take one stroke.

Seva refuses the offered hand and climbs into the narrowing cleft under the sky, where she can make Death touch both layers. The dead stars stain the outer surface. Under it Death feels Seva's pulse, beginning in her wrist and reaching across the dark between galaxies. Death pries up a loose scale with the scythe and sees the heat of a new star gather while it holds the gap open. The weight on the blade puts an end to the experiment. Seva wants Death to spare at least the first landing, where the refugees once slept with their feet in a warm window. Death works out that the spires can only come free with the shell if it cuts below their common footing. It lays out what will fall and chooses to take the old sky. The people's deaths stand and their souls have gone on. Lethra goes with the spent heavens and room opens for new stars.

## Craft plan

- **Voice.** Trial one will follow close to Death in past tense, in the temper of an exacting traveler who expects to finish a long journey. Trial two will give Death the first person as a teller who still recalls physically how it came to stand on the wrong side of its blade. Both keep Death's discovery as the turn.
- **Death** knows countless ways a body can end and mistakes the dark between stars for proof that this one has. It wants Seva's willing hand and the satisfaction of getting the last approach right. It usually gives people room to speak, then speaks in complete, deliberate phrases. Under pressure it becomes very specific about what it can promise, and it has enough of a memory to argue at length.
- **Seva** knows how Lethra came to pin the sky. She believes the city can be saved if she persuades Death to help lift it. She wants room to live and wants somebody else to remember the builders kindly. She talks in quick, warm runs, naming street trades and remembered acts, and becomes most expansive when someone might mistake an act of hospitality for folly.
- **The owed conversation.** At the cleft they have to say who Death has come for, why Seva will stay in her body, and what Death will have to destroy to free her. Seva tells how the first spire was planted because she wants Death to understand who sought shelter and why she gave it. Give her time to tell it in order and to argue for a part of the city she particularly wants to keep. Death has seen what has become of the builders and must answer her request to keep what they made.
- **Reference casting.** Death faces forward, grave and assured, in a floor-length blue-black hood and cloak. It strides, plants, inclines, and draws. It takes the narrow way into the cleft with the confidence of the figure on the ridge. The upright scythe in its right hand first gives the traveler a stance, then becomes the lever bearing the sky and the instrument of the last stroke. Orange eyes in the dry skull bring their own light down to distinguish the two surfaces. The deep hem snags on a spire's exposed iron stay during the first slip and sweeps clear when the city falls. Keep the austere face and the force of the pose through the choice. The picture needs no change for the content target.
- **Place.** Lethra's Stair of Late Arrivals is an exterior ascent between the dark spires of the last city. The Steps of the Brass Market could preserve evidence of its people, and the Far Lantern roof would show the whole sky. The Stair gives the sky somewhere to bear down on the characters and gives Death access to the seam beneath it. At the old calendar's last winter dusk, the glazed steps have cobalt glare, red light lies in the stacked windows, the air tastes of cold iron, loose stays drone in their sockets, and warmth comes up through the broken nosing of one tread. Porters once carried refugees' goods up the Stair while cheaper rooms beneath each landing rented out space at heated windows. The steps have buckled as the city has been pulled toward the shell. Look back down their slope at the end and there will be open depth where the spires stood.

## Scenes and ending

1. **Approach, about 15 percent.** At dusk on the burnt ridge below the Stair, Death anticipates the last surrender. A remembered reaping provides a physical measure for the final one. The city still shows red at the windows, but the light that gets in them belongs to the dead galaxies. The expected walk up becomes a climb under the descending edge of the sky.
2. **The cleft, about 60 percent, the long scene.** Seva refuses on the Stair's highest landing and forces Death to put its hand to what it meant to cut. Her identity, the evidence of the two layers, and Death's small trial with the blade change what is possible. Seva asks it to try to keep the city and tells her account as the sky keeps catching on the spires and dropping lower. Let the loosened scale strike the steps before the talk about loss. By the end Death has chosen which thing to part and Seva wants it to name the price aloud.
3. **The stroke, about 25 percent.** Still at the cleft, she wants Death to let her stay close enough to see the spires go. Death wants her to stand within reach so it can cut the footing and keep hold of the living edge. The choice gets a spoken answer and a difficult movement through the same collapsing place. Death draws the cut from the stone into the galaxies. As the spires and their red windows go up into the falling shell, Death holds its place and watches a first new star catch, then a reach of the sky well beyond where the blade touched. The light below the old shell and the light caught in the windows prepare this ending. Summarize the septillion years through a few particulars of the harvest, without a tour of past ages or a view of the next civilization.
