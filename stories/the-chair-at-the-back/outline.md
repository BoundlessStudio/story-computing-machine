# Outline

## Story

- Premise and central promise: **The Chair at the Back.** A tavern matron feeds a thirteen-year-old summoned hero while dismantling his dangerously complete faith in his recruiters. Her husband complicates the lesson: the adventure contains real wonders, but neither adult can deliver the child's promised homecoming.
- Focal pressure or attachment: Jemby wants to deserve his magnificent arrival and see something impossible. He also wants supper and his mother. Admitting the latter feels like disqualifying himself from the former.
- Counterforce or complication: Berrit wants him safely out of tomorrow's expedition. Pemric, himself summoned decades earlier, resents her habit of making the entire country sound like a confidence trick. His entertaining recollections can accidentally renew the invitation she is trying to interrupt.
- POV, distance, and information limit: Close third past through Jemby. Preserve his literal uptake, selective attention, and incomplete understanding. Adult history arrives through what they choose to tell him; no authoritative narration of summoning or royal motives.
- Governing movement and time shape: One evening entirely inside the tavern, around one chair and an interrupted meal. Other settings appear only in flashbacks or warnings. Expand the homecoming question; compress arrival recollections. Target 2,500–4,000 words.
- Speculative rule or ordinary-world constraint: Living summoners brought Jemby bodily here. Their recruitment promises exceed demonstrated powers. Pemric can lift a small morsel over his open palm while sustaining breath and attention; Jemby learns a tiny version. This supplies neither bodily flight nor protection from weapons. Returning home remains unverified.
- Dialogue promise: Exasperated hospitality meets a young person's appetite for glory, magic, and reassurance; useful teaching must survive boredom, interruptions, and unwelcome answers.
- Dialogic medium: Table talk, demonstrations, miscopied recruitment notes, food requests, and Jemby's attempts to reproduce a hand movement.
- Dialogue engine: Each remembered promise changes what the adults explain or withhold. Their disagreement gives Jemby room to ask the question his heroic performance was concealing and choose who accompanies him back to the summoners.

## Voice

- Narrative texture: Notice splendid and uncomfortable things together: embroidered cuffs dragging through gravy, an unfamiliar fruit's tough skin, the sword knocking against the chair. Stay close to Jemby's shifting attention without infantilizing him.
- Conversational texture: Uneven, interruptible supper talk. Jemby changes subjects when a marvel catches him; ordinary requests remain ordinary. Berrit's attempted orderly lesson repeatedly loses its place.
- Rhetorical ownership: Berrit owns observed injuries, prices, and blunt household judgments. Pemric owns anecdotes, pleasurable digressions, and awkward firsthand uncertainty. Jemby's humor comes from earnest priorities and occasional deliberate cheek, never stupidity. Neither adult knows everything.
- Pressure behavior: Jemby repeats impressive phrases, then becomes concrete about home. Berrit speaks too quickly when frightened. Pemric's storytelling contracts around what he cannot promise.
- Relationship movement: Jemby wants admiration and safe company but cannot initially request protection. Berrit wants him to stay without making herself another commander. Pemric wants his chosen life respected without defending the recruitment. Their exchanges turn an impersonal lesson into a specific commitment to accompany this child.
- Anti-default: Avoid a succession of polished rebuttals ending in universal distrust. Let Pemric be interesting, Berrit overstate something, and Jemby enjoy magic after recognizing manipulation.

## Beats

1. Jemby announces his heroic status while seeking his promised feast. Deliver the prompt's pity, Makers oath, ushering to the back chair, and shouted food-and-drink order. He mistakes the seat for recognition. Berrit recognizes the recruiters' borrowed finery and familiar promises.
2. Food precedes interrogation: savory food, an unfamiliar sweet, and a nonalcoholic drink. Jemby struggles with sword, sleeves, and appetite. Berrit explains that summoning has not taught him fighting or made him invulnerable; she has tended injured recruits. He insists his selection matters. Service interruptions disrupt her lesson.
3. Pemric's disagreement reveals his own summoning. He remembers frightening creatures and spectacular pleasures. Jemby pursues a delighted, irrelevant detail while Berrit tries to explain tomorrow's danger. Pemric demonstrates the lifting trick; Jemby's failure is funny without humiliating him. The tiny wonder remains real.
4. Berrit overreaches by treating Pemric's adventures as uniformly regrettable. He corrects that history without endorsing Jemby's departure. Their friction makes the boy realize a good life here still leaves someone waiting elsewhere. He asks about the promised return after victory. Pemric has found no route home; this neither proves return impossible nor validates the recruiters' assurance. Homesickness interrupts the meal without immediately becoming another lesson.
5. Jemby abandons tomorrow's expedition and asks for company when he questions the summoners. Berrit offers to accompany him; Pemric will contribute his account. This remains an intention, never an exterior scene. Establish tonight's bed without requiring labor or allegiance. Their commitment resolves his immediate exposure while return remains uncertain; no arriving villain or institutional confrontation.
6. Jemby's curiosity revives before his confidence. He initiates another lifting attempt. Berrit tolerates a mess; Pemric lets him concentrate. End within his delighted, precarious first success, with home still missed. The magic belongs to an evening he chose to stay for, not proof that recruitment was justified.
