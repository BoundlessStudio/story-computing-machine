# Opus review: The Chair at the Back

Independent audit for a later editing pass. It is not a `review.md` verdict,
does not change canon state, and does not authorize edits by itself.

## Verdict

- Dialogue: PASS — every exchange holds up in the scene, the three voices are distinct, and the exchanges shift what the characters know and owe each other; only four Minor fixes remain.
- Prose: PASS — clean, concrete close third with one em dash and no systemic tic; one retroactive plant and one small wording slip.
- Continuity and prompt: PASS — all present action stays in the tavern, the prompt's lines are delivered, and the promise resolves; the "Chair in the Back" location is only implied.
- Edit scope: polish
- Existing review.md said: Verdict PASS; Dialogue: PASS; agree, though it missed the small items below.

This is one of the stories where the dialogue works, and the edit pass should
not treat it as a rescue. The three speakers sound different because they want
different things and notice different things. Jemby asks eager, literal
questions and repeats the palace's phrases back ("To drive back the creatures",
"The gratitude of the nation"). Berrit runs a practical interrogation (food,
what he has eaten, what he has been tested on) with household sarcasm, and
speeds into logistics when frightened. Pemric tells comic anecdotes against
himself and hedges honestly about what he cannot promise. The exchanges change
things: the brass token, the girl's jaw, Pemric's reveal, "They knew mine,
too", the request for company. The ending does not explain itself. Keep the
edits surgical: give the decisive request a clear object, keep Pemric's claims
as careful as his character, break up the one repeated spousal correction
volley, and do not "improve" plain lines into wit.

## Edit guardrails

- Keep the prompt's required lines verbatim as they now stand: "Hero?"; "By the Makers. You believed every word they said. Come on."; and "Sit, sit. Time for a common sense lesson for the summoned child. HUSBAND! Bring food and drink. We got another one."
- All present-time action stays inside the tavern (the upstairs bed counts as inside). The palace, summoning room, northern road and Jemby's home appear only as memory, warning or intention. Do not add an exterior scene or bring the summoners on-page.
- Close third past on Jemby, who is thirteen. Broadly accessible comic fantasy with homesickness and non-graphic danger. The length band is 2,500–4,000 words; the story is about 3,170 now. The active profile is prospective-2026-08-23.
- Established facts to keep: six brass disks with a sun and a square punch; the summoned girl last spring with a broken jaw; Pemric summoned at fifteen with nine stolen sweets and a sister; seven summoners who knew Jemby's name; "home when finished" at the bottom of his list; Pemric has never seen anyone sent back; one bare foot and the other sock left at home; they will shut for the morning.
- Magic limits: lifting a crumb over an open palm takes breath and attention. Pemric cannot lift a person or teach flight. Only life can use magic (`universe/rules.md` "Living agency and magic", LOCKED), so the red stone in the sword's handle must stay ordinary. Return home stays unverified, with no universal summoning rule asserted (`rules.md` "Portals and crossing systems", CANON).
- Do not flatten Jemby's interruptions and childish priorities ("But can he?", the turnip, the moth's diet), Berrit's unfinished rush ending in "and—", Pemric's honest uncertainty, or the undeclared ending.

## Dialogue findings

### D1 · Minor · Decisive request has no stated object

- Where: L381–L387, after Jemby's crying, where he decides what he will do next
- Text:
> "I still want to ask them," he said.
> "I want them to show me. Before I go anywhere."
- Problem: Ground/referent at the decisive exchange. "Show me" has two plausible objects. It could mean show that they can send someone home, which follows Pemric's "I've never seen anyone sent back" (L335). It could also mean test or train his gifts first, which follows Berrit's "Which ought to happen before the dangerous part" (L221). Most readers will take the first, and the plot outcome is the same either way. Still, Jemby's own condition, the thing his agency rests on, is blurred at exactly the moment it should be sharpest.
- Fix: Name the object in Jemby's plain words and keep the rest of the exchange. The home reading is what the preceding scene built. The gifts question can ride on "the questions" Pemric will remember (L403). Illustrative only: "I want them to show me how I'd get home. Before I go anywhere."

### D2 · Minor · Pemric promises what he cannot know

- Where: L205–L211, after the burning-tent image, when Pemric first reassures Jemby
- Text:
> "You won't have to go with that company," he said.
> "Plenty of people come back from those expeditions. But you don't know how to travel with them yet, let alone fight."
> "I don't know if they can. I've never seen anyone sent back."
- Problem: Ground/knowledge stance. "Won't have to" is a prediction about the summoners' behaviour. Everywhere else, Pemric refuses to claim more than he has seen (L335). His own history ("there was always a reason to wait") suggests they apply pressure. This line is also the only place the story establishes that refusal is possible, and the resolution (L405–L409) relies on it without examining it. The concession in the middle sentence also reads slightly like a non sequitur after a flat promise.
- Fix: Make it a right, not a forecast ("don't have to"), or ground it in what Pemric knows, and let the concession read as a concession. Keep "I could learn." / "Yes. Over time. You're allowed time." as they are. Illustrative only: "You don't have to go with that company. Not tomorrow."

### D3 · Minor · Spousal correction volley used twice

- Where: L195–L201 (the tent fire) and L291–L303 (Berrit's "grand adventure" jab), with an adjacent echo at L137–L139 and L337–L339
- Text:
> "That was a different tent."
> "But on the same journey."
> "That wasn't the end."
> "It was the end of the soldiering."
> "Yes. But I wasn't miserable every day before that, either."
> He went on, still quietly,
> "Maybe. I'd want to find that out before something tried to bite me."
> "Maybe. I haven't met them."
- Problem: Correction ping-pong in miniature, used twice with the same template. Berrit states a cost. Pemric narrows its scope ("That was a different tent" / "That wasn't the end"). Berrit re-narrows ("But on the same journey" / "It was the end of the soldiering"). Pemric says "Yes." The first time it is a comic marital beat and it moves Jemby, so keep it. The second time it lands on the couple's one real disagreement and reads like a replay. The quibble over "end" delays Pemric's actual objection at L297–L299. "Still quietly" tags a quietness that was never shown. Separately, both adults answer Jemby's hopeful "Maybe…" with the same "Maybe. [flat reality]" shape, which slightly merges their voices.
- Fix: Keep the tent volley. In the second, cut "That wasn't the end." / "It was the end of the soldiering." and let Pemric answer her sarcasm directly with the substance of L297–L299, dropping the "Yes. But… either" scaffolding. Change "still quietly" to "quietly" or cut the adverb. Optionally drop Pemric's "Maybe." at L339 so that "I haven't met them." stands in his own register.

### D4 · Minor · Token's redemption place wobbles

- Where: L79–L83, when Jemby offers a coin
- Text:
> "That's for the royal kitchens."
> You'll get a meal with it at the barracks.
- Problem: Ground/referent. Berrit names two different places for the same token, the royal kitchens and the barracks. The reader can guess that the palace kitchens feed the barracks, but her next sentence ("find someone who wants a meal at the barracks") only works if the token belongs to the barracks.
- Fix: Use one place in both lines, or make the link explicit in her words. Illustrative only: "That's a barracks meal token."

## Prose findings

### P1 · Minor · Pemric's limp is planted after the fact

- Where: L305 (with his first entrance at L59), during the "limping in here" exchange
- Text:
> He had noticed the uneven walk without thinking much about it.
> Pemric came through the curtain carrying a tray.
- Problem: The uneven walk is first mentioned when it is already meant to be significant. Berrit's "I don't want him limping in here after six months" (L301) and the foot turned on its side lose force because the reader never saw the walk. The only earlier hint is "eased himself into it" (L157).
- Fix: Add one concrete physical detail of his gait at L59 or L157, without stock phrasing, so that L305 becomes recognition rather than a new fact.

### P2 · Minor · "Something to write on" answered with a pen

- Where: L215, the folded list of promises
- Text:
> He had asked for something to write on after the third speech. The man with the blue sash had laughed, but had given him a pen.
- Problem: He asks for paper and receives a pen, so the paper he is holding has no source in the sentence. It is a small verb-object slip.
- Fix: Have the man give him both (paper and a pen), or have Jemby ask for something to write with.

## Continuity and prompt findings

### C1 · Minor · "Chair in the Back" only implied

- Where: L25, Berrit towing Jemby across the room
- Text:
> she was already pulling out a broad chair beside the hearth.
- Problem: The prompt's key image, and the story's title, is a chair in the back. The prose places it beside the hearth and only implies "back" through the curtained passage to the kitchen (L41). The requirement is met in spirit, but readers looking for the prompt's image have to infer it.
- Fix: Add "at the back" to L25 (for example "a broad chair at the back, beside the hearth") and change nothing else.

## Recurring patterns

- Spousal scope-correction volley: 2 instances, L197–L201 and L293–L297. Keep the first and vary the second (D3).
- Adult "Maybe. [flat reality]" reply to Jemby's hopeful "Maybe…": 2 instances, L139 and L339. Differentiate Pemric's (D3).
- There is no other systemic tic. The story has one em dash (L359). Single-sentence paragraphs (about 15) serve as beats inside dialogue, not as a default pulse. There is no "not X but Y" habit and no closing emblem.

## Preserve

- The child's reading of pity: "That was the voice grown people used when he had misunderstood something expensive."
- "I'm thirteen," / "Then you'll want bread as well." This is a quick deflection into care, not a punchline.
- "You're welcome at this one," she said. "Put those away and eat."
- Berrit's "I cut her bread small for six weeks." The injury is told through a household detail.
- The turnip beat: "He ate it to show that turnips were not the problem."
- The sweets anecdote ("Asked where I could spit.") and the moth. Pemric is allowed to make the adventure attractive.
- "I know," Jemby said. "But can he?" Also the whole crumb-lifting lesson, including Pemric's admission "No, you're right. I haven't taught anybody in years."
- The home sequence from L307 to L355, especially:

> "There were seven."
> "They knew mine, too."
