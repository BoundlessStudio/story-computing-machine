# Editorial notes

## 2026-09-27 — first complete draft

- Before drafting, the writer rejected a damaged-recording premise after Claude challenged its borrowed grief object and loss of the prompt's playful energy. The committed first draft instead makes Kit, a stage magician, want to fool the one friend who always spots the move.
- Cold Codex reader: “The largest weakness is the story's handling of risk.” The story says a sneeze can affect a whole street, but Kit takes Tansy into a crowded audition with a seat by the door as the only precaution; the door plan visibly fails and both public spells are harmless. The reader valued Tansy's folded objects, Kit's need for her as audience, the chair rotation, and the backstage adaptation; detailed hand-position explanation slowed the trick.
- Cold Claude Opus 5.5: “The conflict is too soft for the premise.” It liked the spoon and tea opening, “To make me earn one,” “twenty-four backs,” “No, because I want to be wrong,” and the folded sugar packet. It found a concrete continuity break at “turn the sharpener over again” because no sharpener exists; “when I came downstairs” and “I had missed that expression” also assert unestablished geography or history. The smooth rebooking softens the cost of losing control.
- Coordinator's first reading: the story's friendship and stage craft are engaging, and the writer's rejection of a borrowed dark premise was wise. Both cold readers independently find low cost for the uncontrolled magic. The unexplained sharpener is a clear error. Discuss how much uncertainty a cozy story can carry without losing its warmth; a crisis is not required, but the stated street-scale possibility and the public choice need a believable consequence.

## Conversation before revision

- Writer's intent: a cozy contest between practiced, ordinary magic and uncontrolled wonder. The chair turn was meant to force Kit to perform without rehearsed staging. The writer accepts that broad danger and easy rebooking weaken the choice; they propose an audience check, a shaken spectator, help restoring the room, and a lost booking.
- Claude, after hearing the Codex reader, refined its diagnosis to a “contract break”: the story primes danger without showing why Kit takes the risk. Claude first suggested a low-severity pattern, then accepted that this would assert rather than demonstrate safety. On hearing the writer, Claude favored an audience check and a spectator's real reaction, with booking deferred rather than refused so the cost does not become three penalties for Tansy's illness.
- Codex reader, after hearing both, said the writer's accountability repair changes the reading more convincingly; a run of harmless spells does not make the next random spell safe. They cautioned that the booking response must feel like this booker's decision and that resetting chairs should not erase the accordion player's adaptation.
- Coordinator decision: show the public consequence and the friends' response, retain the warm magic contest, and let the booker's decision grow from the disruption without making Tansy's illness a moral sentence. The exact booking outcome is the writer's call. The committed draft still contains “sharpener” at the climax despite the writer's recollection; correct it and the other concrete continuity slips.

## Rereading and decision

- The writer revised the audition: Kit first checks the spectators, Tansy helps the dizzy woman, and laughter waits until she is steady. The booker offers another audition rather than an automatic booking because the full act was interrupted. The “sharpener” and early dialogue/continuity slips were removed. The story still closes on the private coin mystery and folded sugar packet.
- Codex reader, after rereading the complete revised prose: “The chair spell now has a consequence without swallowing the pleasure of the trick.” They found no remaining material failure; Kit's further responsibility is an emphasis preference.
- Claude Opus 5.5, after rereading the complete revised prose: “The cost now lands on the room and on Kit's plans, not on Tansy as a verdict.” It caught small physical/referent slips in the rotating chairs and an overstated range fix. The writer corrected those locally, clarified the unused silk, and retained the short aftermath beats because they show the room's response and carry the café payoff. The coordinator checked that final diff and agrees.
- The saved cover depicts the café challenge with the coin, tissue box, quilt and star clip. A first candidate falsely placed Kit onstage with chairs facing it and was rejected; the selected 864×1536 cover has a readable exact title and the story's intimate rather than spectacular tone.

The older story's procedural containment and Nuala's bodily control remain strengths to weigh against this version's stage desire and playfulness; neither version's virtues should be assumed transferable.
