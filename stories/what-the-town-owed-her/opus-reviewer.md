# Opus review: What the Town Owed Her

Independent audit for a later editing pass. It is not a `review.md` verdict,
does not change canon state, and does not authorize edits by itself.

## Verdict

- Dialogue: REVISE — the one scene that explains the magic (L72–L84) rests on a slogan, a line that only the next paragraph decodes, and a summarized rule, and Leonie asks nothing during it. Most other exchanges work.
- Prose: PASS — concrete and well staged. The edit pass has a few small staging slips, one over-explained line and a water-infrastructure rename.
- Continuity and prompt: PASS — delivers the youthful gothic neighbour and the town's debts, resolves both, runs about 3,300 words; one object slip (tea tin vs teacup).
- Edit scope: targeted-dialogue
- Existing review.md said: Verdict PASS, "Dialogue: PASS"; partly disagree. The rescue and dawn scenes pass, but the reveal does not.

The dialogue is better than the collection's usual standard. The bridge rescue has real unevenness: a frightened electrician repeats himself, an old woman answers literally ("Which one?"), and a carpenter just says sorry twice. Leonie and Evadne can be told apart by stance. Leonie accuses, checks and demands; Evadne deflects, admits and gives practical orders. The failure is concentrated where the story has to explain the promise-magic. There Evadne speaks in a balanced slogan and a regret line that contradicts itself until the narration explains it. The rule itself arrives as a summarized lecture ending in a vague "made it worse", and Leonie, the professional sceptic, doesn't question any of it. Elsewhere Evadne slips into ruling on who owes what and opening replies with "No.", a mild form of the collection's correction habit. The edit pass should rebuild the reveal so Evadne's plain, partial admissions answer Leonie's pressure. It should also clear three compressed referents and leave the rescue and dawn scenes essentially as they are.

## Dialogue findings

### D1 · Major · Mechanism reveal told by slogan and summary

- Where: L72–L84, Evadne's flat, after Leonie accuses her over the photographs
- Text:
> The promises keep me young. They also keep me here.
> “I'd only asked for blankets,” she said. “I should have left it at that.”
> Every promise since had made it worse.
- Problem: Three failures stack at the story's key explanation.
  - Evadne's first answer is a balanced two-sentence slogan (epigram), and "here" has no referent until L84.
  - Ground/referent: read literally, "I'd only asked for blankets… I should have left it at that" contradicts itself, because if she only asked for blankets she did leave it at that. The intended meaning (she took the blankets but never released the promise) can only be recovered from the narration paragraph that follows.
  - The rule then arrives as reported lecture ("she told me") and ends on "made it worse", where neither "it" nor "worse" can be recovered. She already cannot cross the boundary, and nothing later shows the hold getting worse.
  - Meanwhile Leonie, who has priced renovations for ten years and has just accused Evadne, accepts an extraordinary claim without one question. Her uptake is inert, and the scene turns into exposition for the reader.
- Fix:
  - Keep "No. I haven't taken anyone's years." and the gesture of leaving the photograph in Leonie's hand.
  - Break up the slogan. Give one plain half, then let Leonie's sceptic attention produce one short checking question, so the explanation answers her rather than the reader. Illustrative only: “Keep you here how?”
  - Make the blankets line say literally what she failed to do. Illustrative only: “I asked for blankets, and they brought them. I never said we were done.”
  - In L84, replace "Every promise since had made it worse" with a concrete consequence that fits the ending: each later promise she kept open was one more she would have to release before the boundary let her through. Or cut the sentence.
  - Keep L80's compressed backstory and the rule that a promise stays open until she releases it.

### D2 · Minor · "the chair" reads as the wrong chair

- Where: L50 and L62–L64, Evadne describing the grandmother on flood nights
- Text:
> She used to give them her bed. I could never persuade her that the chair was bad for her back.
> wool blankets stacked on a chair
- Problem: Ground/referent. The only chair on stage is the one in Evadne's flat with blankets on it, and Evadne has just glanced at those blankets, so "the chair" first reads as that one. Only Leonie's next paragraph shows it was the grandmother's own chair. "them" also depends on "someone crying" two sentences earlier.
- Fix: Tie the chair to the grandmother inside Evadne's line (her armchair, "that chair of hers"), so Leonie's memory confirms it instead of decoding it. Keep the warmth of the line; it is one of Evadne's best.

### D3 · Minor · Key relay compressed; "tell him" never paid off

- Where: L86–L92, notebook scene; L126–L128, foot of the stairs
- Text:
> “You need to tell him.”
> “The boy's gone up to hers. I told him to stop hunting in drawers.”
- Problem: Ground/referent. "hers" and "hunting in drawers" make the reader rebuild an off-page chain: the boy searched drawers, found the wardrobe key, the mother agreed, and the grandfather brought the long key. Evadne says nothing in reply. Separately, Leonie tells Evadne to tell the boy he owes nothing, gets "I know", and the story never pays it off. The grandfather's line half-does the job, but Evadne never acts on it.
- Fix: Make the grandfather's report plain enough that the key relay reads at once, and let Evadne answer with a message for the boy, which closes L90–L92. Illustrative only: “Tell him he doesn't owe me a thing.”

### D4 · Minor · Second no-debt ruling and a glossed nod

- Where: L112–L120, at the window as the evacuation begins
- Text:
> “You owe me nothing, Leonie. Not because of her. Not because you live next door.”
> She nodded, accepting the sharpness.
- Problem: This is the second time Evadne rules on liability in the same scene; L88 already says "I haven't bound them, Leonie. They weren't there to make the promises." The paired fragments give it a courtroom cadence. "you live next door" is also wrong on the facts: Leonie lives a train away and is here to sell, which Evadne knows (L32). "You can sell the apartment" and Leonie's "I know I can" make good friction, but the narration then explains it ("accepting the sharpness").
- Fix: Keep "You owe me nothing, Leonie." and "You can sell the apartment." Cut the two fragments, or replace them with one plain clause that does not say Leonie lives there. End the beat at "She nodded." so the reader supplies the sharpness. Keep Leonie's decision to fetch the blankets exactly as written.

### D5 · Minor · Evadne's "No." openers and a last-word tail

- Where: L58, L78, L104, L166, L226 (openers); L258, at the boundary post
- Text:
> “No. She didn't.”
> “No. I don't suppose she did.”
> I bruise my shoulder on the air. I have done it often enough.
- Problem: Five of Evadne's replies open with "No." followed by a short declarative, a mild form of the collection's correction rhythm. At L258 the second sentence drops the contractions Evadne otherwise uses ("I've told", "I haven't") to land a dry closing beat at a tense moment.
- Fix: Keep "No." at L78 (the denial) and L166 (the urgent answer); both are natural. Change the opening of L104 or L226, with a gesture or an answer that doesn't start with the negation. At L258 keep the concrete first sentence and either contract or cut the second.

### D6 · Minor · Grandmother's release happens in summary

- Where: L220–L228, the landing at dawn
- Text:
> Evadne spoke my grandmother's name and released her promise.
- Problem: Leonie demanded this at L94 ("Cross hers out. She's dead."). It is the personal payoff of her quarrel with Evadne, yet it happens in one reported sentence with no reaction from her. Saving the spoken "I release you" for the harder, never-answered entry is a sound choice. What is missing is Leonie hearing her grandmother's name.
- Fix: Keep the summary, and keep "I release you" first heard at L246. Add one concrete beat for Leonie as she hears the name (her hands, the basin, the open door), with no speech.

## Prose findings

### P1 · Minor · Over-explained release

- Where: L250, end of the release sequence
- Text:
> The paper did nothing but take the pencil marks. Evadne’s living voice did the work.
- Problem: A "not X but Y" closing explanation. Dialogue already states the rule at L96 ("It's only a record… I have to let the promise go myself"), and the action shows it as she speaks each release.
- Fix: Cut the second sentence, and the first too if the paragraph reads without it. The notebook must stay a nonliving record (LOCKED "Living agency and magic"); L96 and her spoken releases already establish that.

### P2 · Minor · Drainage channel as setting (policy)

- Where: L108, L110, L144, L150, L210, the east yard and the bridge
- Text:
> The drainage channel ran below the east yard, separating Rookglass House from the lower houses.
- Problem: Water infrastructure used as default setting texture; the prompt does not ask for it (style-guide "No default water infrastructure"). The flood and the river are allowed.
- Fix: Make it a natural watercourse (a stream or brook) and change each "channel" to match. Keep the footbridge, the flood, the falling tree and the geography.

### P3 · Minor · Small staging slips

- Where: L58, L74, L168, L200, L214
- Text:
> She smoothed the paper against her knee.
> Evadne took her hand off the page.
> Her fingers were already whitening against the wall.
> The electrician followed, his arm tight round her waist.
> We carried her upstairs in one of the blankets she had stacked by her door.
- Problem:
  - L58: Evadne is never shown sitting down.
  - L74: her hand was never on the page.
  - L168: "the wall" is a new name for the abutment she is pressing.
  - L200: "followed" conflicts with his arm round the woman's waist.
  - L214: her own blankets were on a chair in her flat (L50) and the grandmother's were over the yard chair (L142), not by her door.
- Fix: One-clause fixes. Seat her or use the table; put her hand on the notebook earlier or say "the notebook"; "against the stone"; "kept pace beside her"; "one of the blankets from the chair".
