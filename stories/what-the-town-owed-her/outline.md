# Outline

## Story

- Premise: In first-person past tense, Leonie Sayer returns to Mirehaven to empty her late grandmother's apartment in Rookglass House and finds that the unnervingly youthful witch next door, Evadne Trell, can name a different unpaid promise from nearly every family in town. Leonie's inherited instruction to open a sealed cellar wall reveals the central promise: why everyone owes Evadne, how those debts preserved her youth, and what she intends to collect.
- Character want and pressure: Leonie wants to sell the apartment, settle her grief, and leave the town whose inherited obligations made her grandmother secretive. A worsening storm, damage beneath Rookglass House, and her grandmother's precise unpaid promise force her to decide whether honoring a debt she never chose is submission, reciprocity, or the only way to keep the lower streets from flooding. Evadne wants the townspeople safe and herself released from more than a century of open promises, but she refuses to compel descendants who did not make them and may fail if they will not help freely.
- Turn: Leonie finds Evadne's ledger and old photographs and initially concludes that the witch has kept the town indebted to steal its years. Evadne admits the stranger truth: desperate people repeatedly promised her “anything” after she rescued them, and this local promise-working fixed her at the age of the first unanswered vow and tethered her to Mirehaven; it burdens Evadne, not the descendants, and grants her no power to compel repayment. She preserved the favors because the buried relief channel would someday fail again, but Leonie's grandmother's repair notes—not Evadne's claim—prove that day has arrived. Leonie shifts from exposing a predator to demanding an honest, voluntary settlement.
- Ending: Leonie opens the wall despite sacrificing the easy sale, and Evadne asks each household for one concrete contribution—tools, keys, labor, transport, or testimony—needed to reopen the channel while Evadne's living magic holds back the surge. Enough families choose to answer; the water is diverted, every ledger line is closed, and no obligation passes to another generation. At dawn Evadne crosses the town boundary for the first time in more than a century, with one new silver hair showing that ordinary aging has resumed; she returns only because Leonie freely offers tea. The town survives owing her gratitude rather than debt, and Leonie chooses her own next step without inheriting her grandmother's duty.

## Voice

- Narrative texture: Leonie notices saleable rooms, inherited tools, and water damage; grief interrupts her practical judgments. Preserve the rain-soaked domestic detail and her reluctance to stay.
- Conversational texture: Neighbors bring errands, family assumptions, and limits into brief exchanges. Questions arise from the immediate danger or Leonie's suspicion; avoid a continuous interrogation explaining the magic.
- Rhetorical ownership: Leonie owns building measurements and impatient skepticism; Evadne owns remembered rescues and guarded admissions. Neighbors know their own equipment and households, not the story's ethical argument.
- Pressure behavior: Leonie checks what she can verify, then gives urgent directions. Evadne loses explanatory fluency while bracing the arch. Listeners may need clarification without becoming foolish.
- Anti-default: Preserve Evadne's reserve without making every answer a cryptic retort. Do not turn voluntary assistance into repeated consent speeches or replace the flood rescue with another plot.

## Beats

1. Leonie arrives at rain-darkened Rookglass House to clear her grandmother's apartment. The immaculate young woman next door seems dressed for an older century, and a succession of nervous residents leave her mundane but peculiar offerings while referring to promises their parents or grandparents kept for them.
2. Her grandmother's note directs Leonie to the cellar wall and hidden masonry tools. Evadne warns that reaching the relief gate will end the sale. They inspect the worsening leak together.
3. Old photographs and recorded rescue promises make Leonie suspect Evadne takes people's years. Evadne explains that her own living working preserves her youth and binds her to Mirehaven; descendants inherit fear and practical arrangements, not magical liability.
4. Evadne retained the promises to preserve access and equipment for the neglected relief channel. Leonie challenges that choice. A worsening breach sends her upstairs for her grandmother's drawings and measurements.
5. Leonie doubts that convenient warning until her grandmother's hidden repair notes map the sealed wall onto a manual spillway beneath Rookglass House. A fresh collapse sends black water into the cellar. Opening the wall will ruin the apartment's value and may expose Leonie to the same inherited obedience she resents; leaving it shut will flood occupied lower streets.
6. They knock on individual doors for shores, a pump, yard access, and a winch. Residents agree, limit their involvement, or refuse. Concrete exchanges assemble help without a public declaration or conversion.
7. In the storm climax, Leonie breaks the east wall; households answer with the promised tools, access, vehicles, skilled hands, and witnesses. Evadne personally supplies the magic that braces the failing passage long enough for the nonliving gate and channel to be cleared and opened. A dangerous setback forces Leonie to finish her grandmother's task from inside the flooded passage rather than merely supervise it.
8. The reopened channel diverts the surge. Evadne strikes every fulfilled line and releases every declined one, ending the local working instead of preserving leverage. Morning shows the concrete consequences: damaged apartments, living residents, an empty ledger, Evadne's first silver hair, and her voluntary step beyond Mirehaven's boundary before Leonie's invitation brings the two neighbors back for tea.

## Continuity

- Canon used: The story is an undated modern-looking, magic-active independent entry point in the single deep-time world. Its existing publication placement is New-magic cycle 09, with internal ordering unresolved; no connection to another local system follows from that placement. Evadne personally supplies every magical effect; the ledger, building, tools, gate, and channel remain nonliving. The promise-working's costs and consent behavior are explicitly local.
- Boundaries and unknowns: Establish no connection to existing people, places, factions, artifacts, imagery, or magical systems. Do not generalize the premise into universal contract, debt, name, consent, cost, or longevity magic: descendants inherit only social knowledge, may refuse without supernatural punishment, and cannot be bound by an ancestor's consent. Evadne's exact origin, total lifespan, and the system's wider provenance remain unresolved, as does the era's exact universal date. The outline guides drafting but does not establish canon.
