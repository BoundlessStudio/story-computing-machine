# Outline

## Story

- Premise and central promise: **Before Lunch.** A practiced bluffer arrives at a hotel seeking casual work and accidentally acquires three incompatible reputations: legendary assassin, primordial elder, and prospective son-in-law to Satan. Deliver all three mistakes onstage, followed by a collision that requires deliberate improvisation.
- Focal pressure or attachment: You want a cash shift and a decent lunch. Being treated as important is an additional pleasure you keep mistaking for a practical advantage. Your useful skill is reading impatience; your dangerous habit is agreeing before discovering what agreement means.
- Counterforce or complication: Dott needs an unwelcome guest removed from his hotel's private dining room. The guest, Vhulcren, considers itself older than every authority that might expel it. Sathrie, waiting nearby for a family luncheon, takes your apparent disregard for both figures as unusual romantic promise. Each can physically stop your departure.
- POV, distance, and information limit: Second-person present, tightly attached to the bluffer's immediate calculations. Let readers understand each listener's interpretation just before you do. No secret supernatural ancestry or retrospective revelation that you arranged the morning.
- Governing movement and time shape: Continuous morning, roughly nine until noon, through adjoining hotel rooms. Each escape delivers you to the next listener; the fourth movement brings all three together. Expand the collisions, compress corridor transit. Target prose: 2,500–4,000 words.
- Speculative rule or ordinary-world constraint: Vhulcren and Sathrie are powerful living beings, physically present and capable of being mistaken. Neither can read minds or automatically detect age or lies. Belief grants you no powers. A proposal creates expectations, not magical compulsion or an accomplished marriage.
- Dialogue promise: Three listeners confidently hear different intentions in one frightened person's attempts to sound employable, unimpressed, and polite.
- Dialogic medium: Face-to-face speech, interrupted introductions, a returned ring, visible deference, and announcements overheard from the next room.
- Dialogue engine: Dott hears occupational euphemism; Vhulcren treats patronizing familiarity as ancient personal knowledge; Sathrie reads a kneeling ring offer alongside your recklessly flattering assurance. Their subsequent actions make those interpretations explicit and increasingly expensive.

## Voice

- Narrative texture: Immediate bodily discomfort and opportunistic attention: exits, hot collars, expensive food, whose hand has stopped moving. Let confidence swell within a paragraph before one concrete observation punctures it.
- Conversational texture: Uneven exchanges with mundane interruptions. Criminal caution, cosmic reminiscence, and social enthusiasm keep demanding incompatible kinds of answer. Permit unanswered questions when the listener visibly acts on an assumption.
- Rhetorical ownership: You improvise reassurance and borrowed professional language. Dott owns euphemism and suspicious follow-up. Vhulcren owns expansive historical grievance and wounded vanity. Sathrie owns teasing, direct appetite, and the ability to make private business public.
- Pressure behavior: You become more agreeable and less attentive. Dott repeats practical demands. Vhulcren overexplains evidence of its dignity. Sathrie becomes brisk when others impede her plans.
- Relationship movement: Dott wants obedience without exposing fear; Vhulcren wants recognition without admitting embarrassment; Sathrie wants an interesting suitor without having to advertise her boredom. Your successive replies give each leverage, then let their competing claims obstruct one another.
- Anti-default: Avoid a showman's rehearsed intimidation routine or a climactic explanation that makes everybody reasonable. Preserve partial understanding, petty decisions, and your continuing appetite for an audience.

## Beats

1. **The wrong appointment.** You enter a private interview intended for a discreet specialist while looking for hotel work. Dott asks about removing a difficult guest. Your inflated account of clearing troublesome people from previous events sounds like an assassin's carefully sanitized résumé. His staff already expect the face-unknown legend called the Velvet Goodbye; your casual treatment of their precautions confirms their expectation. Only after accepting an envelope do you discover what reputation you have acquired. Dott sends an escort with you, preventing a simple retreat.
2. **An older acquaintance.** Vhulcren initially presents a mostly human silhouette in the dim dining room. Mistaking its grandiose resistance for another wealthy eccentric's tantrum, you answer with familiar, belittling language about its infancy. Make the ordinary idiom and the creature's literal interpretation legible. It supplies the startling connection itself: its obscure larval swaddling and ancient humiliation. Its unfolding body establishes real danger; its sudden deference reveals that it thinks you witnessed its childhood. You exploit the discovery only after making it accidentally.
3. **The courteous recovery.** Escaping to the adjoining lounge, you find an adult woman whose dropped ring has rolled beneath your chair. A physical reason puts you on one knee. Your attempt to ingratiate yourself while returning it, with an extravagant assurance about her wishes, has the recognizable shape of a proposal. Sathrie genuinely accepts before your purpose becomes clear. Her introduction of her father as Satan completes the third realization. She has heard the other two defer to you and finds the apparent audacity attractive.
4. **Three claims at once.** Dott comes to demand the contracted killing; Vhulcren follows to retain the company of its supposed elder; Sathrie announces her engagement. Let their distinct reactions change the room before you intervene. Dott cannot casually order the death of his apparent superior's companion, and Vhulcren cannot attack someone Sathrie publicly claims. Their restraint is contingent and physical, not a supernatural prohibition.
5. **A useful departure.** You turn the engagement announcement into a demand for an undisturbed exit, return Dott's advance, and take Vhulcren out as an honored guest. This actually removes it from the hotel; Dott accepts the bloodless result because pursuing the matter now risks both formidable visitors. Outside, Vhulcren leaves rather than endure further familiarity about its infancy. Sathrie remains. Clarify the accidental proposal here without an ethical speech: she can be annoyed and amused, and choose to replace the supposed engagement with one lunch. You have a real opening to leave and instead accept, still susceptible to impressive company. End at the threshold of that knowingly chosen complication, before lunch begins.
