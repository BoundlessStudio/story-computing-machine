# Opus review: Before Lunch

Independent audit for a later editing pass. It is not a `review.md` verdict,
does not change canon state, and does not authorize edits by itself.

## Verdict

- Dialogue: PASS. Each misunderstanding rests on a legible double meaning, the replies follow what each listener actually heard, and the five speakers sound distinct. What remains is local: one unattributed line, a few pronoun and staging wobbles, and two muddy lines from Sathrie.
- Prose: PASS. The comic second-person prose is concrete and varied. The only notable lapse is a recap that explains the final joke.
- Continuity and prompt: PASS. All three required mistakes happen on the page before noon. There are two Minor internal slips, one about the cash and one about when Sathrie recognizes the ring.
- Edit scope: polish
- Existing review.md said: Verdict PASS; "Dialogue: PASS". I agree. It missed the unattributed pivot line (L311) and the cash contradiction (L257), both Minor.

This is one of the collection's better-dialogued stories, and the edit pass should not flatten it. The comedy works because four listeners each hear one bluffer in their own way. Dott hears euphemism ("Permanently." answered with "don't let him back in"). Vhulcren takes a waiter's put-down literally ("since before you had teeth"). Sathrie hears a proposal in a kneeling man holding out a ring. The escort hears legend in everything. The collection's house-voice failure (clipped correction ping-pong, epigram duels, everyone equally composed) is mostly absent. Most of the wit belongs to the protagonist, which the prompt earns. Dott is terse, Vhulcren is archaic and then childlike, and fright visibly breaks the protagonist's fluency ("I—", "No. No,", "Oh,"). The fixes are surgical: tag one line, make a few pronouns and stagings unambiguous, support one overheard fact, make two of Sathrie's lines literal, and cut the recap before the last line. Do not add quips.

## Dialogue findings

### D1 · Minor · Unattributed line at the pivot

- Where: L309–L315, lounge, just after Dott's "Congratulations"
- Text:
> “I hope the old gentleman will be pleased,” Vhulcren says. “He has always admired seniority.”
> “You can tell him so at lunch.”
> Dott hears this too.
- Problem: Attribution. This untagged line prompts Vhulcren's wish to leave, and that wish drives the whole resolution. Three readings compete. Elsewhere in the story, an untagged reply to another speaker is most often the protagonist's (L15, L23, L43, L127, L197, L203, L225, L237). Simple alternation would give the line to Dott, the last speaker before Vhulcren. Content points to Sathrie, whose lunch with her father this is. Each reading changes who engineered Vhulcren's exit. "Dott hears this too" only makes sense if Dott did not say it.
- Fix: Tag it as Sathrie's, with a plain "Sathrie says" or a short action beat for her. Change nothing else in the exchange.

### D2 · Minor · Sathrie cites an overheard line the text never lets her hear

- Where: L223–L229, lounge, first meeting; the source line is at L183
- Text:
> She looks up from the bag. Then at the door. “Was that Vhulcren calling you its elder?”
> “Stay,” Vhulcren says. “You will bring a fresh dish for my elder.”
> The voice is softer now, coming from several places.
- Problem: Ground/knowledge and referent. Sathrie was inside a washroom off a closed lounge when Vhulcren said "my elder" once, in the dining room, to the escort. The story has just described Vhulcren's voice as softer, not carrying. It never shows the line reaching her, so something she could have heard becomes something she did hear. "Was that" also fuses two referents: the scrape happening now (L223) and speech from minutes earlier.
- Fix: Separate the present sound from the earlier speech, and say how she heard it. Illustrative only: "That's Vhulcren. I heard it through the wall, calling someone its elder." The protagonist's "It gets carried away." and her "I've noticed." still follow unchanged.

### D3 · Minor · "It" means both the money and Vhulcren in the decisive refund

- Where: L339–L359, lounge, Dott aside and the invitation that follows
- Text:
> “All of it,” you say. “And it goes. Now.”
> “Don't bring it back.”
> “It was coming anyway,” you add.
> “I would not intrude upon a family occasion,” Vhulcren says quickly.
- Problem: Ground/referent. At the decisive beat, "All of it" means the money and "it goes" means Vhulcren, in adjacent sentences. Dott then pockets the envelope ("puts it inside his jacket") and says "Don't bring it back", which refers to Vhulcren again. A reader can sort this out, but stumbles at the moment that most needs to land. At L359, "coming" reads as circular, because the protagonist has just invited Vhulcren. What he means is that it was leaving anyway, as Vhulcren said at L313 ("I had not intended to remain").
- Fix: Take "it" out of the money half of L339 so that "it goes" can only mean Vhulcren. Illustrative only: "Every penny," you say. "And it goes. Now." Change L359 to "leaving" so the excuse points back to what Vhulcren said. Keep Dott's "Don't bring it back." It echoes L43.

### D4 · Minor · "Something cold" is an order nobody takes up

- Where: L317–L323, lounge, before Dott takes you aside
- Text:
> “I'll get you a drink.” She releases you and turns to Dott. “Something cold.”
> Sathrie has begun examining the bottles on a sideboard.
- Problem: Ground/uptake and staging. "I'll get you a drink" followed by "Something cold" said to Dott reads as an order to Dott. Dott ignores it, and she then serves herself without the text registering the snub, so the reader cannot tell who is fetching the drink. The drink never arrives.
- Fix: Keep her ordering the mob boss about like staff, which suits her, but register his refusal in the next sentence. Illustrative only: "Dott ignores her and beckons you a little way aside." Alternatively, delete "and turns to Dott" so that "Something cold" is addressed to you.

### D5 · Minor · Sathrie's decision line needs decoding

- Where: L435–L439, pavement, final exchange
- Text:
> “I know where you don't want to have lunch.”
> I can afford to be seen there with someone who isn't my fiancé.
- Problem: Ground/sense in the final meaningful exchange. Put plainly, the line says that at the café, being seen with a man who is not her fiancé costs her nothing. Everyone at the hotel believes he is her fiancé, though, and "afford" suggests money. The reader has to work out that the point is "nobody I know goes there". The line reads as a crafted quip where her choice should be clear. The protagonist's "I know where you don't want to have lunch." is earned: he is a professional charmer, and he is calling back something she told him.
- Fix: Keep "The chairs don't match and the bread's good." Replace the "afford" sentence with a literal reason. Illustrative only: "Nobody there will ask about the wedding."

## Prose findings

### P1 · Minor · Recap explains the final joke

- Where: L447–L451, pavement, closing lines
- Text:
> You have just talked your way out of a murder, escorted something older than houses across a hotel lobby, and broken off an engagement without being set on fire.
- Problem: An over-explained ending. The paragraph lists the prompt's three events back to the reader so that "Guest relations" cannot be missed. The reader has just lived through the morning, and the irony lands without the list.
- Fix: Cut L449, or shrink it to one concrete physical beat, such as the bag's weight or the phone. Let "What should I say you do?" run straight into "Guest relations". Keep the final line exactly.

## Continuity and prompt findings

### C1 · Minor · "Cash may be out of the question" while the envelope is in his pocket

- Where: L257, lounge, just after Sathrie's first "Yes"
- Text:
> Cash may be out of the question, but you have found an escort, possibly a lunch companion, and—
> The money is enough to pay your rent for a long time.
- Problem: Internal continuity. At this moment he is carrying more cash than he has ever held (L79). He gives it back only at L343. The sentence also uses "escort", the story's standing name for Dott's gunman, to mean "someone to leave with". That makes a reader look for the gunman.
- Fix: Point the clause at the waiting job, or at the fact that the envelope is now more trouble than it is worth, and replace "an escort". Illustrative only: "The waiting job may be gone, but you have found a way out, possibly a lunch companion, and—". Keep the em-dash break into L261.

### C2 · Minor · Sathrie studies her own ring and doesn't recognize it

- Where: L241–L267, lounge, the proposal
- Text:
> A ring lies against the back leg, set with a red stone almost the size of your thumbnail.
> Her gaze moves from your face to the red stone and back.
> Once it is in her hand she turns it over, sees the little nick on the back, and laughs in delight.
- Problem: Staging plausibility at the key comic beat. She has been searching her bag for this ring (L217, L239), and it has a distinctive stone. Yet she looks straight at it and only recognizes it after turning it over. The joke "You proposed with my own ring" is good, but the order in which she notices things undercuts it.
- Fix: Before "Yes", have her look at the gesture (your face, your bent knee) rather than at the stone, so she recognizes the ring only once it is in her hand. Keep the nick as confirmation, and keep "You proposed with my own ring."

## Recurring patterns

- Pronoun "it" in dialogue, meaning both Vhulcren and the money: L339, L345, L359 (D3). When revising, make sure every spoken "it" near the refund has one referent.
- Muddy or unsupported lines from Sathrie: L227, L321, L439 (D2, D4, D5). Her voice is right. The problem is that three of her lines need decoding or staging support.
- Explanatory narration at the close: L449 (P1). Elsewhere the narrator's asides add jokes rather than explain other characters' subtext. Keep those, and do not add more.

## Preserve

- The misreading at the center of the story and Vhulcren's literal uptake:
> “Yes, and I've been dealing with spoiled little monsters since before you had teeth. Come on. Up you get.”
> “When I was fed through the soft seams.” A long, jointed limb slides across the table. “You knew me then?”
- The protagonist blaming the venue out of service habit, and Vhulcren's relief (L201–L205):
> “These rooms are badly laid out.”
- Vhulcren's one sincere line, undercut by a practical answer. Keep the pairing, and do not add a second thematic statement anywhere:
> “I had forgotten,” Vhulcren says quietly, “what it was to be the younger.”
> “Watch that last step.”
- Bluffing through a stock phrase, and the escort's awe at it:
> “People talk too much,” you say, because a sentence is required
- Sathrie's dislike of her title, and her social steamrolling:
> “Sathrie,” she tells it. “We've discussed this.”
> “We've just become engaged,” Sathrie tells him. “Do congratulate us.”
- The protagonist handing back the money despite what it costs him (the rent, L337). This is the story's real turn.
- The uneven, non-epigrammatic apology beat:
> “And Mr. Dott.”
> “Yes, I remember.”
- The ending line, word for word:
> “Guest relations,” you say, and follow her round the corner.

## Suggested edit order

1. D1: one tag fixes who triggers Vhulcren's exit, which is what the resolution turns on.
2. D3: clear up the spoken "it"s in the decisive refund and the invitation that follows.
3. C1: remove the cash contradiction and the "escort" word collision in a single sentence.
4. D2: support Sathrie's overheard "elder" and separate it from the scrape.
5. C2: reorder what Sathrie looks at, so that recognizing the ring comes after the "Yes".
6. D4: register Dott ignoring "Something cold", or readdress the line.
7. D5: make Sathrie's café line literal.
8. P1: cut or shrink the recap so "Guest relations" lands without explanation.
