# Opus review: A Question for the Sky

Independent audit for a later editing pass. It is not a `review.md` verdict,
does not change canon state, and does not authorize edits by itself.

## Verdict

- Dialogue: REVISE — the two transmissions and the glyph messages work, but the talk between passes runs on correction ping-pong and closing maxims. There is also an unplanted "auto-center", unclear staging before the first pass, and a set-up punchline in the final exchange.
- Prose: PASS — a concrete, specific close third (hinge twelve, lentil pouch, label rubbings), with minor emotional glosses, emblem closers and negation reversals.
- Continuity and prompt: PASS — the story delivers the rewrite's visual correspondence, the child below, the eight-century lifeless-world doctrine and the service script in 3,083 words. The slips are minor: the scale of the reed display, the spelling and inversion errors, and a berry colour.
- Edit scope: heavy-dialogue
- Existing review.md said: Verdict PASS, "Dialogue: PASS", crediting "Orvek's threshold-bound accountability, Lassan's evidentiary restraint" as distinct voices. I disagree on dialogue. Orvek and Lassan converge into one corrective, maxim-closing register, and the review missed the auto-center, staging and packet-order slips.

The operational dialogue is this story's strength. During both transmissions, speech shrinks to numbers, orders and interruptions ("One confirmation mark." / "Open all."), and Piru's glyph messages are sparse, revised and properly foreign. Between transmissions, the three station voices all run one engine: someone makes a claim, someone narrows a word, the first objects, and the exchange closes on a maxim or a quip. Lassan does this to Sairu in almost every scene. Orvek does it with rulings and comebacks, and Sairu jabs back in the same clipped register, so whenever nobody is counting seconds, the talk becomes the collection's house voice. The edit should keep the procedural backbone, the hinge arc and every glyph, cut most of the closers, and give each person one line of their own stake: Lassan's pleasure or fear at seeing his dead script written by living hands, Orvek's care (the galley shows she has it), and Sairu's hands-first impatience. Do the three Ground repairs first: the auto-center, Orvek's position before the first pass, and the packet order.

## Dialogue findings

### D1 · Major · Lassan's corrections close on maxims

- Where: L101–L107 (archive, "preserved"); L203–L209 (radiator hub, RECEIVE vs SEE); L257–L263 (after the cloud)
- Text:
> "Or inherited something that shares its source. The oldest forms are closest. That's provenance, not an explanation."
> "I want the first true record we make to remain true tomorrow."
> "Language remains language at speed."
> "It is what the evidence can answer."
- Problem: Correction ping-pong and epigram closers, the same shape three times. Sairu makes a plain claim or asks a plain question, Lassan narrows one word, Sairu objects to the narrowing, and Lassan closes on a general maxim that nobody answers before the scene moves on. This turns a useful trait, his evidentiary care, into a verbal tic, and all three exchanges become interchangeable. L107 also states the story's ethic outright (Philosopher). L263 answers a frightened question with an aphorism: the literal uptake suits him, but the final line is written for the reader, not for Sairu.
- Fix: Keep each correction where it does plot work: "preserved" becoming "inherited", RECEIVE over SEE, and "No display visible." Cut the maxim each time and end the exchange some other way: Sairu dropping it, Lassan going back to the glyphs, Orvek cutting in (L211 already does this), or a concrete reason in his own terms. At least once, let Lassan voice his own stake, as an archivist who knows this script from coolant labels, instead of only restraint. At L375, drop the tag "corrected"; the line already shows the correction. Illustrative only (in place of L107, answering the jab by tapping the founding-hull scan): "Look at the hand stroke. Nobody up here has cut it that way since the founding hull."

### D2 · Minor · Orvek knows the frames before she is shown them

- Where: L111–L121, Orvek enters the archive
- Text:
> Her wrist slate already showed the access trail.
> "From twenty kilometers of orderly vegetation?"
> Sairu showed Orvek the changing message. She watched the supervisor's eyes pause on the heat traces
- Problem: Ground/knowledge. The narration gives Orvek only the access trail. The next beat stages Sairu showing her the frames, and Orvek's eyes stopping on the heat traces as if for the first time. Yet her retort already knows the display's length and that it is vegetation, which Sairu learned only by magnifying. "Could have opened the frames" has been promoted to "has studied them", and the knowledge exists to serve the comeback to "There was glare."
- Fix: Either add a clause at L111 saying she has already opened the frames, and make L121 about the sequence she hasn't seen, or limit the retort to what an access trail shows (a ground-facing hold and a visor lock under glare authority, at night). Keep "You used glare authority" / "There was glare."; the callback works.

### D3 · Major · The authorization runs as jurisdiction-trading and stacked quips

- Where: L131–L135 and L169–L181, archive, Orvek's ruling
- Text:
> "They used a maintenance channel."
> "They used light. The similarity doesn't grant you a channel."
> "Mechanic's choice. Supervisor's limit. Put the optical excursion in the maintenance log."
> "It will expose me. I'm authorizing the excursion."
> "Repair the hinge before you use it to talk to a planet."
- Problem: A negotiation/procedure routine plus an epigram cluster. Sairu's "maintenance channel" misnames what the ground used (a script, not a channel), and the line exists mainly so Orvek can correct the noun, which turns the argument into jurisdiction. The authorization then ends on three last words in eight lines: a parallel maxim, a correction-flip ("expose it" / "expose me") and an exit quip. Orvek putting her name on the excursion is the scene's real relationship movement, but it lands as a comeback rather than a cost, and Sairu never registers it. "The similarity doesn't grant you a channel" could just as well be Lassan's line.
- Fix: Keep Orvek's L167 ruling (numbers, conditions, the yellow abort) and the "We can do it in thirty-eight." / "Then remove three seconds of message." push. Terms from a supervisor fit the role. Replace L131–L135 with Orvek's actual objection in operational terms (heat, the log, who answers for it), and give Sairu a claim she would really make: the question was addressed to a worker, and she is one. Keep at most one of the three closers. Show Orvek's cost through the thumb signature and one plain word, and give Sairu a beat to take in what Orvek has just taken on. Illustrative only (answering "That will expose it."): "I know." and she signs.

### D4 · Minor · "That word" collides, and the question sets up a jab

- Where: L151–L155, Orvek questioning Lassan
- Text:
> "Do we know that word is Piru?"
> "Good. At least one of you still knows that word."
- Problem: Ground/referent and Punchline Patch. In Orvek's question, "that word" is the Piru glyph; two lines later it means "No", and a reader first parses it as the glyph again. The question mostly exists to get Lassan's "No" so Orvek can jab at Sairu's certainty.
- Fix: Cut L155, or rephrase so it doesn't reuse "that word". If Orvek needs to show she values Lassan's caution, let her act on it by turning straight to Sairu with her decision. Keep Lassan's "No," / "A name is the best fit."

### D5 · Minor · The contact tag is explained to Sairu, who already knows it

- Where: L159 (Orvek) and L329–L331 (Lassan); compare L51 and L349
- Text:
> A contact classification would dispatch the station's survey routines. She knew that.
> "And the standard contact tag centers every available ground optic on the source, writes coordinates into the survey ledger, and requests a communications assessment," Orvek said.
> "The form doesn't merely record our observation," he said. "It begins a search.
> "I knew what it did."
- Problem: Exposition ("As you know"). L51 establishes that Sairu knows what the tag does, and she says so at L349. Orvek explains it to her at L159 and Lassan explains it again at L331; both explanations are for the reader. The repetition also blunts Sairu's recognition in the corridor, because the mechanism has already been spelled out twice.
- Fix: Let L159 be the pointed question it wants to be ("Did you apply it?") with at most one clause of mechanism. Let L331 carry only what is new: the form starts a search of people who never asked for one. Keep the red square and the eight listed systems at L329; the image does the explaining.

### D6 · Major · Before the first pass: lost attribution, impossible glance, a quip

- Where: L189–L201, radiator hub, nine minutes before the surface angle
- Text:
> "Nine minutes to surface angle," Orvek said over the service channel.
> "It is the starting number."
> "Then we have achieved ordinary conversation."
> Lassan glanced between them.
- Problem: Attribution and staging. Orvek is placed on the service channel, so the untagged L195 and L199 could be hers or Lassan's; L195's uncontracted "It is" sounds like Lassan. Only "Lassan glanced between them" implies the lines were Orvek's, and that glance needs two people in the room when one of them is a voice on a channel. The reader has to back-count and still can't stage the scene. The exchange itself is a run of quips: Sairu reads a number, Orvek restates it, Sairu bristles, and Orvek closes on an arch line whose meaning is hard to recover. This is the one beat of nerves before first contact, and it is spent on a bit.
- Fix: Decide where Orvek is for this scene (in the hub, as she physically is at L461, or on the channel) and stage it that way. Tag L195 and L199. Replace the quip run with nerves each person shows in their own way (Sairu checking twelve again, Orvek repeating the number, Lassan pressing on his paper copy) rather than a closing line.

### D7 · Minor · The dialogue describes the packet wrongly

- Where: L149, L201, L207; compare the sequence sent at L217–L227 and the receipt at L361
- Text:
> The first block meant ABOVE. The second meant WORKER.
> "No. The old script gives us a defensible order for LIVING, ABOVE, RECEIVE.
> "The second block should remain RECEIVE, not SEE."
> "We have ninety-four seconds and four blocks."
- Problem: Ground/object. Lassan, the story's precision voice, names a "defensible order" of LIVING, ABOVE, RECEIVE. The packet actually goes out ABOVE, LIVING, WORKER, RECEIVE, and the ground's receipt repeats ABOVE, LIVING, RECEIVE. Since L21, "block" has meant a single glyph, so the second block is LIVING (or LIVING WORKER), not RECEIVE, and "four blocks" doesn't match the five or more glyphs sent. Sairu's "ninety-four seconds" is the angle window, not the thirty-five Orvek authorized.
- Fix: Make the plan match what is sent. Put L149 in the order ABOVE, LIVING, RECEIVE. At L201, name RECEIVE's real position or call it "the RECEIVE block". Make Sairu's time and count match the authorization and the packet. Keep "block" meaning one glyph throughout.

### D8 · Minor · Galley opening: unclear speaker, mirrored comebacks

### D9 · Major · The corridor turn is won by argument, not by what Sairu sees

- Where: L315–L339, outside the archive, the scene where Sairu's aim turns from answering to protecting
- Text:
> "We emitted a syntactically plausible sequence toward the observed grid."
> He stopped. "No. I'm trying not to finish their meaning for them."
> Sairu had no quick reply to that, which annoyed her more than the sentence deserved.
> "Yes. Not necessarily every eye we own."
- Problem: The story's most concentrated correction ping-pong, sitting on its moral turn. She makes a claim; he restates it in deflating jargon; she jabs; he answers with the theme in one sentence (Philosopher); the narration explains her reaction (a gloss); and the pair ends on four even one-liners in which he qualifies each of her statements (Tennis Match). Sairu turns by being out-argued. The stronger engine is the evidence she sees, the red-square form (L329) and the cooling scars where bodies knelt (L341), and it arrives only after the argument is already won.
- Fix: Cut L325's thesis and L327's gloss. Either cut L321 or give it a reaction that isn't a comeback. "Do you practice making everything sound dead?" can stay if his answer changes; the dead-world echo is good. Let the slate and the scars do the persuading: Lassan can hold out the form without the speech. Compress L333–L339 so her objection is answered once or not at all. Keep L345–L353 as written ("I knew what it did." / "Before or after your thumb stopped?" / she pushes off too hard). It is the one closing line in the story that changes something between two people.

### D10 · Major · "They saw the auto-center" refers to nothing the reader has seen

- Where: L405–L409, reading the ground's restriction; the payoff is at L465–L467
- Text:
> "They saw the auto-center," she said.
> "Or they know what centered observation means," Lassan replied.
> No contact tag, no locator request, no networked optics. Local heat-eye only.
> "Disable automatic centering on this optical set. Local manual acquisition only.
- Problem: Ground/referent at the key interpretive beat. The only centering the reader knows about is the contact tag's (L159, L329), which was never applied, and Orvek limited them to the local heat-eye (L167). Nothing shows the local optical set locking onto the grid in either pass, so "the auto-center" has no event to point to, and the reader learns it exists only at L465, after the fact. The line also asks the reader to accept that people on the ground could see a station optic re-aim, which nothing sets up. As written, Lassan's alternative is better supported than the line it answers.
- Fix: Plant it. During the first pass (near L213 or L255), show the heat-eye's reticle jumping onto the grid by itself and the coordinates appearing in temporary memory. Then Sairu's L407 can refer to that, framed as a guess, since she can't know what they saw. Keep Lassan's reading and his "all three revisions" point. Illustrative only: "The heat-eye jumped onto them by itself last pass. If they had anything looking back, they saw it swing."

### D11 · Minor · "Ours can be shorter" draws the moral

- Where: L425, composing the reply
- Text:
> "Twenty-nine seconds. Their last signal cost them. Ours can be shorter."
- Problem: Philosopher/epigram. The reasoning doesn't connect: the ground's cost is not why the station's message can be shorter. It is shorter because Lassan just cut words, and the thermal forecast sets the limit. The line states reciprocity, which L403 and L459 already show, and it puts Orvek in Lassan's aphoristic register.

### D12 · Major · The final exchange is a tee-up for a refrain

- Where: L461–L465, the last conversation; the refrain appears earlier at L179 and L299
- Text:

> "It has my name on it."

The first time (L179) it is a decision; by the third it is a catchphrase, and it ends the story's last conversation on Orvek describing herself instead of on the order she gives next.

## Continuity and prompt findings

### C1 · Minor · The reed display outpaces the people lighting it

- Where: L35 and L41 (first sighting), L371 (six or seven tenders), L389 (two people revise a warning)
- Text:
> Darkness remained for six breaths. Then the whole message returned, except that the middle block had changed.
> thousands of pale tips kindling like a field of stars only after a person reached them.
> On the surface, two people crossed the lit rows. The first warning vanished.
- Problem: The display's operating limit is unclear. The glyphs are kilometres long, and the reeds kindle "only after a person reached them", yet a handful of tenders blank and rewrite a twenty-kilometre message in six breaths, and two people revise a warning. The story never says how one touch reaches a whole row, so a reader who does the arithmetic finds the scale impossible. - Fix: Add one clause near L41 saying that a touch at the head of a row kindles or darkens the whole row, so a few people can drive the display. Keep "A mechanism could store and shape living work, never begin it."
