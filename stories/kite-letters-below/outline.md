# Outline

## Story

- Premise and central promise: After eight centuries of station teaching that
  the world below is dead, heat-radiator mechanic Naro frees a newly sewn cloth
  kite from a vane and uses obsolete maintenance labels to parse its question:
  `IS THERE A PERSON ABOVE THE RAIN?` She begins sending answers down inside
  supply capsules already condemned for disposal and receives the child
  Telu's replies on later kites. Resolve the question through a genuine,
  two-way relationship—not merely Naro's private conclusion that life exists—
  while keeping the contact small enough that neither correspondent becomes a
  prop in a public proof or reform campaign.
- Intended reader experience: Tactile maintenance work widening into wonder,
  then the patient pleasure and unease of building a shared language from
  labels, drawings, and corrections. Let ordinary particulars from below—wet
  cloth, a roof scorched by the first capsule, meals, weather, a child's
  impatience—undo the doctrine more powerfully than a technical dossier. End
  with the warmth of an answered question and the live risk of continued
  correspondence, not vindication.
- Generating force:
  - Mode: Recurrence and accumulation across six orbital return passes during
    one short season when charged storm columns can carry conductive-thread
    kites into the radiator wake; each exchange adds vocabulary, material
    knowledge, and reciprocal obligation.
  - Focal attachment or contradiction: Naro's care for things that must keep
    working makes her attend closely to the impossible kite, but the same
    maintenance culture has trained her to reduce anything unexplained to a
    fault class. She becomes attached first to solving the text and then to the
    particular questioner whose corrections refuse that reduction.
  - Plot-led wants and useful limit, if applicable: Naro immediately wants to
    clear the vane, close an overdue exterior-service ticket, and answer before
    the next disposal window passes. She also wants to preserve her safe place
    in the station's life-support workforce and avoid dropping dangerous mass
    on an inhabited surface. Her useful limit is a fault-code mind: she reads
    the old language's component nouns and imperative verbs but little grammar,
    and initially answers with impersonal system status because exact,
    auditable language feels safer than admitting uncertainty or desire.
- Counterforce or complication:
  - Source: Incomplete shared language, the station's disposal audit, changing
    ground tracks, the brief lift season, and the simple physical danger of
    deorbiting a capsule near people. The eight-century doctrine supplies
    Naro's habits and fear, but no official needs to arrive as an antagonist.
  - Credible inside logic, if a person: Telu is not passive evidence. After
    Naro's first capsule burns a furrow beside a roof, the child refuses another
    answer unless it descends slowly and supplies a landing map keyed to that
    first blackened footprint: a safe circle lies twelve furrow-lengths along
    the capsule's skip direction beside three pale boulders visible from above.
    Telu withholds the community's absolute coordinates, corrects
    Naro's vocabulary, and asks questions for personal reasons rather than to
    help Naro prove a thesis.
  - Pressure or change created: Every reply must be light, physically safe,
    useful in a tiny vocabulary, and timed to the right pass. Naro can preserve
    deniability by sending only anonymous status phrases, but that keeps
    treating Telu as a source signal; identifying herself makes the exchange
    unmistakably personal and easier to trace if either message is found.
- Narrative design:
  - POV, tense, distance, and limitation: Close third-person past through Naro,
    intimate with her bodily competence, translation guesses, and evasions.
    Telu is available only through handmade objects and written marks; do not
    enter the child's viewpoint or let Naro infer more than the replies support.
  - Time shape and compression: Open in one continuous exterior repair. Then
    compress roughly six weeks through six returns to the same radiator sector,
    giving the first unsafe drop, the naming exchange, and the final answer
    full scenes while carrying intervening waits in altered chores, capsule
    preparation, and a growing hand-built lexicon.
  - Information and reveal strategy: The reader knows the station doctrine at
    once but shares Naro's initial uncertainty about prank, relic, or living
    sender. Recent stitching, damp organic stains, changed handwriting pressure,
    Telu's account of the hot first capsule, and mutually useful corrections
    accumulate without becoming a courtroom chain of proof. Delay the exact
    grammar of the opening question: Naro first parses only `PERSON / ABOVE /
    FALLING WATER / QUERY`. A repeated mark she catalogs as a route code is
    eventually revealed, through a drawing and the old label for `PERSONNEL
    NAME`, to be `Telu`. The correspondence never discovers who instituted the
    lie or how much of the surface is inhabited.
  - Governing movement: Disentangle; classify; misread; answer mechanically;
    cause harm; accept correction; build a lexicon; exchange ordinary life;
    recognize a name; give a name; receive the next question.
- Speculative surplus: During strong radiator-dump weeks, charge leaking along
  the station's maintenance trunks kills yeast starter in nearby galley bays,
  so those corridors eat dense unleavened rounds and trade coveted risen loaves
  from the inner ring. Telu mistakes Naro's drawing of a ration round for a
  moon, creating one useful vocabulary correction; the effect remains a mundane
  consequence of station technology, not a metaphor or proof of life.
- Structural distinction: A private correspondence story built from recurring
  returns to an exterior worksite and asymmetric material media: cloth rises,
  discarded capsules descend. Its scale grows through attention rather than a
  widening cast or public venue. There is no infrastructure emergency, hearing,
  crowd conversion, official confession, institutional rewrite, or ceremonial
  object at the close; the station remains unaware and operating.
- Decisive turn, deepening, or recognition: On the penultimate kite, Telu's
  drawing makes Naro understand that the recurring `route code` is a child's
  name and that the original sentence asks whether a *person*, not a functioning
  system, exists above the rain. Naro sees that every reply so far—`ABOVE
  OPERATING`, `CAPSULE CORRECTED`, `BELOW SIGNAL RECEIVED`—has avoided the
  question. For the last safe disposal pass, she rigs a proven low-mass drogue,
  leaves the station's doctrine untouched, and writes the traceable first-person
  answer her labels never taught her: `PERSON ABOVE. NAME NARO. I HEAR TELU.`
  She does not delay a radiator cycle or endanger the station to do it.
- Aftereffect or live uncertainty: A final small kite confirms receipt by
  placing both names in the corrected grammar, then asks how people above drink
  when rain cannot fall indoors. The lift season closes before Naro can answer.
  A capsule-material discrepancy is waiting in her routine queue, and neither
  correspondent knows whether the contact can remain private or resume next
  season, but Telu has received an answer from a person and Naro can no longer
  turn the world below back into a maintenance category.
- Opening / ending relation: Open with Naro trying to finish a repair by
  removing a question she calls vane fouling. End on the same sector restored
  to ordinary duty while she measures the mass of a discarded condensation
  diagram and begins phrasing an answer to Telu's next, practical question.
  The first question is resolved; the ending's new question keeps the relation
  alive without converting it into a symbol or announcement.

## Beats

1. On an exterior shift, Naro finds colored cloth and hand knots snagged in a
   charged heat-radiator vane. The fibers are wet-stained and newly cut. Inside
   the kite is writing assembled from the old language still visible on access
   panels; she can parse `PERSON / ABOVE / FALLING WATER / QUERY` but not its
   grammar. She reports ordinary filament fouling, keeps the message, and just
   meets the next disposal preparation.
2. Naro marks `ABOVE OPERATING` inside an empty supply capsule and alters only
   its disposal timing. On the next lift pass, a second kite arrives: a drawing
   shows the capsule's hot landing beside a roof. It keys a safe circle to the
   still-black first-impact furrow with an arrow along the capsule's skip,
   twelve furrow-length marks, and a triangle of three pale boulders; a blunt
   sentence says no more answers unless they fall safely. Naro's improvised
   contact has had a consequence before it has become a comfort.
3. Naro tests a discarded drogue and a lighter message shell rather than risking
   another intact capsule. She matches the blackened furrow and boulder triangle
   in downward maintenance optics, ties them to the first capsule's logged
   disposal track, and uses Telu's relative bearing and scale to shift the next
   drop into the safe circle without deriving the settlement's coordinates. She
   asks how the kite climbs and sends pictograms of the vane and capsule. The
   next reply explains
   through pictures that conductive thread released into a seasonal charged
   storm column is caught by the moving light's wake; it also corrects Naro's
   word order. Keep the phenomenon bounded and partly unexplained.
4. Across two compressed passes, the shared lexicon grows. Naro learns of rain,
   roofs, seedlings, flatbread, and people who avoid old sky-fall scars; Telu
   learns of sealed water loops, exterior gloves, heat vanes, and yeastless
   purge-week food. Naro never requests coordinates or samples. She nevertheless
   keeps filing the repeating signature mark as a route code and continues
   answering in anonymous status language.
5. The penultimate kite pairs that mark with a self-portrait and the copied
   maintenance field `PERSONNEL NAME`, then points the field back at Naro. With
   the grammar corrections in place, Naro finally reads both `Telu` and the
   opening question exactly. She recognizes that proof of a sender is not the
   same as answering that sender.
6. With one safe disposal pass left before the lift season closes, Naro uses the
   tested drogue and sends her name, a direct answer, and no dangerous gift or
   promise of rescue. She accepts that the named message and altered capsule
   mass could connect the contact to her, but she does not compromise radiator
   duty or anyone else's safety.
7. The last kite carries both names in corrected grammar and Telu's new
   question about drinking without indoor rain. Naro returns the vane to routine
   service, sees the inventory discrepancy queued for review, and starts a
   low-mass condensation drawing for a season that may or may not bring another
   crossing.

## Continuity

- Canon used: The setting belongs to the one physical shared world that persists
  through deep time as civilizations rise, fall, inherit fragments, and
  misattribute their origins. The maintenance language's layered survival is
  consistent with that history, but does not identify its originating culture.
- Boundaries and unknowns: Leave this orbital era undated relative to every
  established era and do not assert literal real-world geography. The
  eight-century lifeless-surface teaching is local institutional doctrine, not
  universal truth. Establish only the living child, the inhabited particulars
  Telu reports, and the bounded kite/capsule corridor; do not infer a worldwide
  surface population, a complete history, or the doctrine's author.
- Boundaries and unknowns: Do not connect the charged lift, station systems,
  capsules, old language, or surface culture to any existing magical,
  technological, crossing, factional, or historical entry without new
  authority. Make the first capsule's hazard and Naro's later safe descent
  adjustment causally legible through its logged track, the visible impact
  footprint and landmarks, Telu's relative bearing and scale, and the tested
  drogue. Preserve Telu's identity, caution, and agency;
  no cosmic or technical explanation may reduce the child to evidence.
- Boundaries and unknowns: Naro and Telu are new names whose exact and close
  forms were checked against the frozen legacy baseline, current passing noun
  inventories, and relevant character authority. No named place is proposed.
  Prompt requirements control; these names, message phrasings, pass count, and
  mechanical particulars remain advisory drafting guidance.
