# Outline

## Story

- Proposed reader-facing title: *Coiled Together*.
- Premise and central promise: Adult caravan scout Kerric Alver wakes in the Hushvault, pinned from shoulders to ankles inside the immense warm coils of Sivareth, the serpentfolk queen humans call the Gilded Hunger. She says he entered her cave during a glass-rain storm, fell through a weakened shelf, and begged her not to let go while she kept him above the chasm. Kerric remembers only his caravan captain sending him ahead with a charm said to repel serpentfolk. Sivareth will release him if he clearly asks, but warns that the charm is actually a lure and that his companions are waiting for her to bring their bait back to the entrance. Resolve the captivity/consent promise when Kerric recovers enough concrete memory to know he did ask for her hold, discovers the betrayal without making Sivareth generically harmless, and—after she offers a real release at the point of danger—freely asks her to hold him again under explicit limits. She honors those limits, lets go when promised, and he chooses to remain for one meal and an honest conversation rather than becoming property or instantly declaring romance.
- Intended reader experience: Start with claustrophobic fear and dry, intimate friction. Let each physical adjustment of the coils change meaning—from restraint, to evidence, to shelter, to a consciously chosen form of trust—while preserving Sivareth's danger and Kerric's right to refuse. The ending should feel warm, wary, and romantically possible without treating coercion as courtship.
- Generating force:
  - Mode: Intimate containment moving through repeated requests: an alleged plea Kerric cannot remember, small awake permissions inside the chamber, and one decisive request he makes with full knowledge.
  - Focal attachment or contradiction: Kerric's body relaxes in the queen's warmth before his learned fear can accept her; Sivareth prizes promises but confuses being needed once with being entitled to keep protecting someone. Both want the other to acknowledge a truth without surrendering agency.
  - Plot-led wants and useful limit, if applicable: Kerric wants immediate release and reunion with his caravan, but also wants to survive the storm and understand his missing hour. His useful limit is trained certainty: he treats familiar warnings as evidence and every kindness from a monster as manipulation. Sivareth wants to keep him alive, expose the hunters using him, and retain the first human in years who asked for her rather than fled. Her limit is possessive literalism; she can defend a promise so fiercely that she stops asking whether it is still wanted.
- Counterforce or complication:
  - Source: Captain Halvek Prynn deliberately gave Kerric a heat-and-scent lure, then weakened the shelf above a chasm so Sivareth would expose herself rescuing him. Prynn's hunters wait near the cave mouth with barbed nets while glass-rain floods the lower passage. The lure also leaves Kerric feverish and his recent memories fractured.
  - Credible inside logic, if a person: Prynn wants the royal scale bounty and counts Kerric's survival as acceptable but unnecessary. Sivareth protects her hidden clutch-chamber and will use lethal force if the hunters reach it; she is neither tame nor obliged to forgive the trap. Kerric must decide whom to trust without receiving proof conveniently detached from risk.
  - Pressure or change created: Escape alone means walking toward the ambush while impaired; remaining passively means letting Sivareth decide his fate. Their only workable plan requires her to loosen every coil, return his knife, and accept terms before Kerric can choose her protection.
- Narrative design:
  - POV, tense, distance, and limitation: Close first-person past through Kerric. His sensory knowledge of routes and traps is reliable; his cultural conclusions and missing recent memory are not. Sivareth speaks with formal precision, predatory humor, and a dangerous tendency to make promises sound like ownership. Keep Prynn exterior until his shouted instructions and attack confirm the trap.
  - Time shape and compression: One stormbound dawn, about ninety minutes. Open at waking; restore the missing interval in short sensory shards triggered by the false charm, wet stone, and the queen's changing grip rather than a full flashback.
  - Information and reveal strategy: Establish immediately that Kerric is fully clothed, physically uninjured, and held by an adult queen whose power is real. Sivareth demonstrates the release rule early by freeing one arm when asked, then produces the cut lure and predicts the hunters' positions. Kerric tests each claim. His final memory confirms only his own plea and her rescue, while Prynn's attack confirms the betrayal; neither fact proves every serpentfolk legend false.
  - Governing movement: Wake in coils; hear the alleged plea; demand one arm free and receive it; test sensation, wounds, exits, and the cut charm; accuse Sivareth of luring him; hear her identify the lure's human manufacture; glimpse the flooded passage and barbed net through a heat-sensing mineral mirror; recover fragments of falling and being caught; learn Sivareth means to keep him until the danger passes and challenge the indefinite word; force a full release and receive knife and pack; approach the cave mouth alone; hear Prynn order the hidden bow line to wait for the queen; retreat by choice; negotiate a bounded hold—until they cross the flooded throat, stop means stop, no crushing, knife stays in his hand; let Sivareth carry/shield him through the ambush; use his routecraft to redirect the glass-rain runoff and strand the hunters; refuse Prynn's demand to come back; reach safety; say stop; have Sivareth release him instantly despite her desire; choose to stay for food and questions; close with a smaller, plainly requested coil around his cold feet.
- Speculative surplus: Serpentfolk read heat through thin mineral sheets; warmth blooms across the stone like muted gold weather. It helps Sivareth detect bodies and Kerric verify the ambush, but cannot identify motives, hear speech, or solve the confrontation.
- Structural distinction: A private consent negotiation whose action crosses one cave system and whose repeated physical motif changes through observable choices. No public vindication, species-wide reconciliation, institution, or lore lecture follows; one human betrayal does not settle every rumor about serpentfolk.
- Decisive turn, deepening, or recognition: Fully released and armed, Kerric reaches the entrance and hears Prynn instruct the hidden hunters to hold fire until “the gold one takes the bait.” He could still flee into the storm. Instead he returns to Sivareth and states a new request in awake, bounded language. She repeats each limit without embellishment and asks what happens when he says stop. Kerric answers, “You let go.” Sivareth does exactly that after the danger, proving the relationship's new basis through action.
- Aftereffect or live uncertainty: Prynn's party survives stranded on the far ledge and may spread whatever story protects them. Sivareth remains feared, territorial, and capable of killing intruders; Kerric has not pledged himself to her or disproved all warnings. He stays because curiosity, gratitude, attraction, and the need to decide what truth he will carry back are now his own.
- Opening / ending relation: Open with Kerric unable to move and hearing that he asked for this. End with him free to leave, asking for one modest coil because his boots are still wet, and Sivareth asking “How tight?” before touching him.

## Beats

1. Kerric wakes in Sivareth's coils, receives her account, and wins the first small release while checking his body and gear.
2. The cut lure, mineral heat-map, flood, and returning memory fragments make both the human ambush and Sivareth's overbroad idea of “keeping” credible dangers.
3. Kerric demands full release; Sivareth returns his weapon and lets him test the entrance, where Prynn exposes the betrayal in his own words.
4. Kerric returns voluntarily, negotiates precise limits, and combines Sivareth's protective strength with his routecraft to survive and foil the ambush.
5. Sivareth releases him at “stop.” Kerric chooses a finite stay and a smaller touch, turning the prompt's disputed plea into present-tense consent.

## Continuity

- Canon used: The story occupies an undated fantasy region within the shared world's broad tonal range and follows the binding personhood, consent, accessible-audience, prose, and continuity policies. All people, places, serpentfolk biology and customs, weather, and material practices are story-local and non-canon unless separately promoted.
- Boundaries and unknowns: Do not connect serpentfolk, Hushvault, glass-rain, heat-reading stone, the lure, or the caravan to an existing faction, magic system, location, era, or cosmology. One human hunting party neither proves nor disproves all stories about serpentfolk; Sivareth remains dangerous and accountable for possessive choices. Kerric and Sivareth are unambiguously adults, intimacy remains non-explicit, and the ending opens a possible relationship without establishing ownership, lifelong commitment, species-wide reconciliation, or a recurring identity.
