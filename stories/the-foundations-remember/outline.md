# Outline

## Story

- Premise and central promise: In an unnamed vertical neon city that teaches it is humanity's first civilization, junior memory-repossession technician Teren Vard must erase evicted transit worker Orla Dace's recollection of an unauthorized tunnel below the lowest train line. The recollection preserves the route to standing, testable remains of at least two cultures older than the city. Teren can keep an independent copy only by disguising it inside the licensed partition holding their already-collateralized childhood. The story must resolve whether Teren preserves the memory, make the risk to their childhood concrete, and deny an easy public rescue or cost-free copy.
- Intended reader experience: Claustrophobic procedural intimacy opening into archaeological vertigo, with the reader repeatedly unsure whether a sensory detail belongs to Teren or Orla. The ethical pressure should feel personal before it feels historical: preserving evidence means accepting that a creditor may seize the memories by which Teren knows their own beginning. End on a private, compromised success rather than exposure, vindication, or institutional reform.
- Generating force:
  - Mode: Plot-led real-time repossession, interrupted by three progressively deeper immersions in Orla's tunnel recollection and brief returns to Teren's collateralized childhood.
  - Focal attachment or contradiction: Teren entered repossession work to keep their own rented life solvent and treats clean memory boundaries as a survival discipline, yet the assignment asks them to destroy the only recoverable route to a history the city has buried.
  - Plot-led wants and useful limit, if applicable: Teren immediately wants to complete their first unsupervised repossession before the eviction handover, preserve their license and wages, and keep the lender from foreclosing on their childhood partition. Their competing want is to leave Orla's evidence somewhere the leaseholder corporation cannot edit, suppress, or resell. The rig permits only a creditor upload, verified deletion, or storage in Teren's licensed personal space; its raw buffer purges when the session closes. Teren's junior access cannot create a hidden partition, but their exacting habit of reconciling mislabeled fragments lets them disguise Orla's recollection as a repair to one collateralized childhood episode. That same tactic makes the entire childhood block contractually suspect and eligible for seizure.
- Counterforce or complication:
  - Source: The eviction clock, an automated end-of-session integrity audit, the rig's source-bound storage limits, and two incompatible goods: Orla's right not to be stripped of lived experience and Teren's right not to sacrifice their own past. No singular villain needs to enter the room.
  - Credible inside logic, if a person: Orla distrusts a technician employed by the same economy that took her home and wants to retain the tunnel memory, which includes years of work judgment as well as evidence. Once she learns what hiding it would endanger, she refuses to demand Teren's childhood as the price of saving hers; she instead asks that the recollection not become another edited corporate product. Her refusal to plead leaves the decision with Teren while preserving her agency and dignity.
  - Pressure or change created: Each playback temporarily weakens the rig's source labels, while the approaching purge removes every external hiding place. A clean deletion protects Teren and completes the eviction but leaves only a controllable corporate master. Concealment preserves an independent route to the physical evidence while giving the childhood lender a reason to lock, inspect, or seize both memories.
- Narrative design:
  - POV, tense, distance, and limitation: Close third-person past through Teren. During playback, keep the prose embodied and initially unlabeled; Teren can access Orla's senses but not her unspoken present thoughts, and Orla cannot see what Teren chooses inside the rig. The source confusion makes the cost intimate without implying that a bounded memory copy is a conscious duplicate of Orla.
  - Time shape and compression: Roughly fifty continuous minutes in Orla's stripped lower-tier apartment, braided with the remembered descent below the line and short fragments of Teren's childhood ascent through the city. The tunnel's deep time expands the scale while the eviction remains immediate.
  - Information and reveal strategy: Open with Teren performing the mandatory integrity check on one licensed childhood memory, establishing it as vivid, numbered, and collateralized before explaining the market. The first tunnel pass seems like prohibited infrastructure knowledge; the second reveals current foundation piles driven through an older rail concourse; the third reaches a masonry water chamber built from reused blocks bearing still older tool marks. Orla's issued assay tool dates incompatible materials, and the memory shows where she hid its ejected sample wafer, making the recollection a route to surviving physical evidence rather than self-authenticating proof. Interleave the childhood fragments more intrusively on each pass so the reader understands the hiding method and its cost before Teren acts.
  - Governing movement: Verify; inventory; enter; recoil; return deeper; distinguish evidence from claim; weigh whose past can be taken; misfile deliberately; close the session; discover what access remains.
- Speculative surplus: High-fidelity work memories carry short-lived motor residue. After sharing Orla's years of track sense, Teren braces before distant trains pass and steadies loose objects a moment before the building shivers; commercial memory users treat such afterhabits as an ordinary nuisance, not a revelation or moral signal.
- Structural distinction: A two-person, one-room repossession procedure whose largest setting exists inside one contested recollection. The city is never rallied, the tunnel is not revisited in present time, and no hearing, chase, infrastructure emergency, public proof, converted crowd, or legal rewrite completes the arc. Its decisive action is an almost invisible metadata choice followed by an administrative consequence.
- Decisive turn, deepening, or recognition: At final reconciliation, Teren sees that the rig will accept Orla's tunnel sequence as a sensory restoration to Teren's collateralized childhood elevator ride because both contain the same subsonic train cadence. Orla, seeing Teren hesitate, tells them to perform the clean deletion rather than trade one stolen childhood for another. Teren understands that obeying would not return Orla's past; it would only leave the corporation as sole editor and owner. They deliberately bind the full tunnel memory, including the hidden wafer's location, into their licensed block, then certify Orla's source copy erased.
- Aftereffect or live uncertainty: The repossession passes. Orla leaves without the tunnel recollection and with only the knowledge that something important has been taken; Teren does not pretend this is rescue. The rig immediately freezes Teren's childhood block for collateral revaluation, making both the childhood and the preserved evidence inaccessible pending audit. The tunnel survives in an independent human memory space, but whether Teren can recover either memory before foreclosure—and what responsible use of the evidence would look like—remains live.
- Opening / ending relation: Open on Teren successfully answering the rig's childhood integrity prompt and resenting that a lender can demand such intimacy. End when the same prompt can no longer retrieve the childhood view; instead Teren's body supplies Orla's practiced brace for a train several levels above. A plain audit appointment appears, and Teren records it before escorting Orla to the lift. The repeated check measures the literal consequence of the choice rather than closing on a symbol or announcement.

## Beats

1. Outside Orla's nearly empty apartment, Teren runs the required identity-and-license check: a childhood elevator ride through ascending neon tiers answers cleanly, while the interface notes that the whole early-life block secures Teren's housing debt. Teren enters determined to make their first solo repossession exact and uneventful.
2. Orla recognizes the unusually narrow deletion order and refuses the rig. Teren inventories the memory contract: the tunnel recollection sits inside a leased work-life tranche, the corporation will retain its master, and eviction lets it erase Orla's access. With the handover approaching, Orla agrees only if Teren first witnesses what is being taken.
3. In the first playback, Orla walks the deserted lowest platform, feels a wrong vibration below the rails, opens an unlisted maintenance panel, and descends beyond the official foundation depth. Teren returns carrying Orla's automatic train-bracing reflex and initially repeats the corporate explanation that the recollection is dangerous infrastructure data.
4. Orla explains that reporting the tunnel ended her transit shifts before it ended her tenancy. She wants her memory intact, not a heroic substitute, and tests whether Teren can distinguish concern for evidence from concern for her. The purge countdown and apartment handover continue without an onstage enforcer.
5. Teren enters the second and third passes. Current piles spear an older concourse with corroded rails and erased signage; below it, a water chamber uses blocks cut by a still earlier tool tradition. Orla's standard assay produces mutually incompatible ages, and she hides its sample wafer behind a precisely remembered drain grille before supervisors seal the route. Teren grasps that the memory is the only independent map to inspectable remains of multiple predecessor cultures.
6. Teren checks for lawful destinations and finds none outside the creditor vault. The rig's temporary crossfade has begun threading tunnel vibration through the childhood elevator memory. That apparent contamination suggests the only concealment: relabel the complete recollection as a repair inside Teren's licensed childhood block, which will invalidate its collateral warranty.
7. Orla sees the projected risk notice and tells Teren not to do it; if her memory must be taken, she will not authorize taking Teren's childhood too. Teren acknowledges the refusal, makes no promise of imminent exposure, and chooses preservation for their own reasons. They bind the tunnel recollection into the childhood block and certify deletion at Orla's source.
8. The corporate audit accepts the repossession and Orla experiences the immediate absence without being reduced to it. Teren's lender freezes the childhood partition for revaluation. When the integrity prompt returns tunnel balance instead of the childhood view, Teren logs the mandatory audit appointment and walks Orla to the lift, carrying evidence they cannot yet safely open.

## Continuity

- Canon used: The `LOCKED` deep-time world permits successive civilizations to become archaeology for later cultures; `LOCKED` memory-versus-myth continuity permits controlled, distorted, and only partially recoverable evidence without making truth automatically produce justice. The local memory technology follows the `CANON` requirement that different memory and copy mechanisms keep their own limits and moral consequences. The predecessor remains and assay are physical traces that can be interpreted or contested, not artifacts that confer automatic truth.
- Boundaries and unknowns: The unnamed city's era, geography, transit system, corporations, memory-licensing economy, neural partitions, and repossession tools are story-local and unconnected to existing settings or institutions. The bounded experiential copies do not create conscious people or establish a universal memory substance, and the leasing mechanism has no connection to Deepmarket's memory terms, Adrian Bellweather's severance cost, or any other established memory system. The remains establish at least two predecessor cultures older than the city's official founding but do not identify them, date the city within the Galactic Cycle, or connect them to any named civilization. The city's foundations and the title establish no relationship to the canon unnamed city of eleven foundations. Whether Teren later recovers, publishes, or acts on the evidence remains outside this story's resolved central choice.
