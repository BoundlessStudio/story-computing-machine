# Opus review: The Memory Under Line Zero

Independent audit for a later editing pass. It is not a `review.md` verdict,
does not change canon state, and does not authorize edits by itself.

## Verdict

- Dialogue: REVISE — two Ground failures (Ovik can hear the anchor conversation that the ending needs him to miss, and "That isn't what I said" objects to words Ovik never said), plus a first half in which Haru ends most exchanges on a correction or a quip.
- Prose: PASS — concrete, well staged and varied; only a policy swap of the drainage texture and a few "not X, not Y" closers to trim.
- Continuity and prompt: PASS — delivers the whole prompt promise at about 3,700 words; Minor nits only (the coat, thin neon and vertical texture). The audio-staging defect is filed as D1.
- Edit scope: targeted-dialogue
- Existing review.md said: Verdict PASS, "Dialogue: PASS"; disagree. It missed both Ground failures and the correction ping-pong that the outline's own anti-default warned against.

The dialogue problem here is concentrated, not pervasive. From the capacity check (L307) onward the exchanges work: Amel's fluency breaks under pressure, Ovik's refrains stop answering the question, and after the purge Haru speaks around the hole in his memory. The first half runs on the collection's house rhythm. Haru corrects Amel's category or closes on a neat line ("Seal isn't square.", "The train opens it.", "Somebody could fall off a diagram.", "Not the same as listening."), and Amel answers with matching crisp retorts, so for stretches the two share one voice. Separately, the story never settles whether Ovik can hear the booth, and the key private conversation takes place with the room pickup live. The edit pass should fix the audio staging and the two broken turns first. Then it should turn Haru's ripostes into plain warnings and instructions, leave him two or three dry jokes, and keep Amel's pushback in questions and procedure.

The story must be complete and resolve that promise, and stay broadly accessible. The active profile is `prospective-2026-08-21` (the last `Craft profile:` line, under Rewrite constraints). "First Foundation" is a local label; do not number or pluralise foundations (the CANON "city of eleven foundations" is unrelated). LOCKED personhood: the stored copy is "signed as foreign recall, source Haru Dent"; keep that.
- Internal figures that already cross-check. Countdown: 43 → 31 → 22 → 12 minutes. Space: 1:08 available, 4:41 target, a reserve change of 3:33 (4:41 − 1:08). ## Dialogue findings

### D1 · Blocking · Ovik can hear what the plot needs him to miss

- Where: L155–L167, L195, L259, L267, L319–L355, L471–L481 (setup of the supervisor call; the anchor debate; the farewell)
- Text:
> His voice arrived without a face, clean and level in Amel's earpiece.
> Haru watched her rather than the speaker grille.
> Amel restored the room pickup. "We're continuing."
> Amel unmuted Ovik.
> "You don't open personal in a repossession unless the job won't fit somewhere."
> "If you keep it," he said, "keep the route. Don't trim it down to the pretty part."
> "You left something measuring," she said.
- Problem: Ground/knowledge and staging. L155 puts Ovik in Amel's earpiece. L165 and L435 give the booth a speaker grille, and at L267–L271 Haru argues with Ovik directly, so Ovik must be audible in the room. Amel restores the room pickup at L195 and never mutes it again; "Amel unmuted Ovik" at L259 undoes a mute that never happened. As written, Ovik hears Haru ask "Anchor space?" and explain what opening personal space means. He hears "If you keep it... keep the route". Later he hears Amel tell Haru he "left something measuring". Yet Ovik learns of the anchor only from telemetry (L395, L401, L489). Amel's "No" at L437, and "Only the foreign cluster beneath her kitchen remained beyond the job system's sight", both depend on his not knowing. The reader cannot tell whether the central deception works or whether Ovik is quietly building a case, and that changes what the dawn audit means.
- Fix: Choose one audio arrangement and state it once: Ovik on the booth speaker, with Amel able to cut the outgoing feed. Change "earpiece" at L155 to match. Replace "Amel unmuted Ovik" (L259) with a beat consistent with L195. Right after "Yes. I heard." (L317), add a one-clause beat in which Amel cuts the feed, so L319–L355 is private. Reopen it before her question at L359; Ovik's orders at L357 can land on the muted line, and his trainee-patience beat already fits that. Cut the feed again before "You left something measuring" (L475). If the intended reading is that Ovik heard everything, his later lines must show it. Do not leave both readings open.

### D2 · Blocking · "That isn't what I said" objects to words Ovik never said

- Where: L169–L177, first supervisor call
- Text:
> "Then it may be fabricated."
> "Correct."
> "That isn't what I said."
- Problem: Ground/uptake plus correction ping-pong. Amel says the memory *may* be fabricated, and Ovik's "Correct." agrees with exactly those words. Her objection says he has misstated her, but he has not yet turned "may" into "is". That happens only in his next line ("It is the relevant classification"). The retort answers the author's intended meaning, not what she heard, and the exchange exists to produce a precision correction.
- Fix: Let Ovik's reply make the jump from "may" to "is" itself, so that any objection from Amel follows words on the page. Or cut her objection and let his classification land unanswered, which suits the power balance. Either way, cut "Correct." and "That isn't what I said." Keep "Then it may be fabricated." Illustrative only: Ovik: "Then log it as fabricated. The company cannot authenticate a historical claim through indebted neural property."

### D3 · Major · Opening scene runs on correction ping-pong and an unprompted question

- Where: L17–L41, sealing the booth and calibration
- Text:
> "That one never meets square," he said.
> "It passed its seal test."
> "Seal isn't square."
> "Are you refusing calibration?" she asked.
> "No. I'm telling you why your crown will drift left every time an eastbound brakes."
> "Then you'll account for the wrong thing."
- Problem: The story's first voice impression is Haru correcting Amel's category and Amel answering with a matching crisp line, which is the house register at its most visible (Identical Voices, correction ping-pong). Nothing prompts "Are you refusing calibration?". Haru has only commented on the door. The open restraints that might explain her question are described after it (L29), and nothing establishes calibration as a step a subject can refuse. The question exists so Haru can answer "No. I'm telling you…", and "Then you'll account for the wrong thing" adds a last-word jab on top (Punchline Patch).
- Fix: Keep the information: the booth racks under passing trains, eastbound braking walks the crown left, and she has never worked Line Zero. Haru should give it as an unasked-for warning from a man who knows this booth, not as a correction. Cut the seal-test/"square" volley and the refusal question. On her first solo job, Amel can react through procedure, for instance by checking the level or logging it, not with a retort. Keep at most one needle from Haru in this scene. "That one never meets square" can stay as his opener if it isn't answered with a counter-line. Illustrative only: "Eastbounds brake right over this booth. Your crown'll walk left."

### D4 · Major · Panel exchange: four corrections and a Punchline Patch

- Where: L95–L119, after the first playback pause
- Text:
> "You opened a locked panel by altering a drain coupling."
> "The coupling doesn't open it. It keeps the condensate line from pushing the panel shut. The train opens it."
> "New schematic or the one they actually built?"
> "Then it won't show the steel behind the skin."
> "Could somebody learn the route from a diagram?" she asked.

- Problem: Every Haru turn from L97 to L115 opens with or ends on a correction ("No." / "The train opens it." / "the one they actually built" / "Somebody could fall off a diagram."), traded with Amel in evenly sized one-liners (Tennis Match). The diagram question is a Punchline Patch. Narration at L111 has just said the memory holds "nothing that could be summarized into a neat instruction", so her question is redundant and only sets up the quip; she asks the real version later at L275 ("can you reconstruct the route from records?"). "New schematic or the one they actually built?" also offers a building as the alternative to a schematic.
- Fix: Keep the mechanism: the coupling holds the panel, the train's load lifts it, and weight on the shelf keeps it up. Keep "You need the loads. That's the route." Haru should give the mechanism as his own plain account, not open with "No.". Cut the diagram question, the quip and "Mr. Dent." (L113–L117). Cut the schematic volley down to one plain line from either speaker (the approved schematic predates what was actually built). Do this together with P1, because the coupling and condensate wording changes here.

### D5 · Major · The exchange before the anchor contradicts itself and closes on retorts

- Where: L343–L355, Haru and Amel just before she selects PERSONAL ANCHOR (with L267)
- Text:
> "I'm not asking for yours."
> "You're asking me to erase yours."
> "They're doing that already. I asked you to look at a wall."
> "Send a rail hand. Don't send her. Five minutes at the wall, that's all."
> "The stair isn't pretty."
- Problem: This is the decisive exchange before Amel's choice, and it has three faults. (1) Ground/referent: "I asked you to look at a wall" points to nothing on the page. The only time Haru asked for anyone at the wall (L267), he said "Don't send her." That line also answers a proposal nobody made. (2) "You're asking me to erase yours" turns his line back into a parallel comeback, and he caps it. The result is two retorts where the scene needs Amel's pressure to show; the outline itself has her losing fluency here. (3) "The stair isn't pretty." answers the literal word "pretty", not his point, which is to keep the working route and not just the striking evidence. The reply "Good." then has no recoverable meaning: is he glad the stair is ugly, or glad she understood?
- Fix: Keep "I'm not asking for yours." and "If you keep it," he said, "keep the route. Don't trim it down to the pretty part.". Replace Amel's mirror line with something her situation produces: a practical question, an unfinished sentence, or silence. Make Haru's wall line agree with L267, either by making it about someone checking the wall rather than her, or by changing L267. Cut "The stair isn't pretty." and "Good." or replace them with a plain acknowledgement. Illustrative only: "All I wanted was somebody at that wall."

## Prose findings

### P1 · Minor · Water infrastructure as default texture (policy)

- Where: L89–L101 (the access route), L131, L167, L211–L227, L331
- Text:
> crouched beside a drainage manifold hidden behind an advertising skin
> It keeps the condensate line from pushing the panel shut.
> Haru's hand passed over a drainage channel polished at the lip.
> "Dry channel. Wet air. There's a cracked return pipe behind the new pier."
- Problem: The prompt asks for a train line and a sealed tunnel, not drainage. Under `universe/style-guide.md` "No default water infrastructure" (CANON, applies to authorized revisions), several details count as default hydraulic texture: the drainage manifold, drain coupling and condensate line in the access mechanism; the ancient "drainage channel … made to carry water" offered as evidence; and the "return pipe" that threatens the gauge.
- Fix: Swap these consistently for rail or other non-hydraulic equivalents and keep the mechanics. The cold coupling can sit on a brake-air or signal-cable line that carries the opposite bore's pressure. The old feature should be plainly made by people, for example a groove or ramp worn by traffic, or a carved sill. Give the gauge a different local threat, such as a loose pier fixing. Update every mention: L89, L91, L95, L101, L131, L167 ("load-bearing channel"), L211, L215–L217, L227 ("the drain"), L331. Do not replace it with another public-works problem.
