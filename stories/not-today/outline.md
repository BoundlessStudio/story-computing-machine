# Provisional outline — Not today.

## The experience promised

The explicit premise grants one prevention per day, chosen in thought and enacted by saying “Not today.” The later request fixes the title and makes “what is the choice, why dose it matter? what dose it cost?” the dramatic questions. I infer a pleasure in enormous power made scarce by selection: the reader should want two incompatible uses, understand the chosen one, and feel what remains lost. The title should acquire weight through its use. No relationship, genre, scale, tone, or particular sacrifice is prescribed. My chosen direction is an urgent, unsentimental tragedy with a public rescue and a private loss; tragedy is a choice, not a requirement.

## Chosen direction

Meret, a retired train driver, has a permanently damaged heart. For months she has used her daily prevention on her own death. This gives her something valuable: continued life with her grown son, Tavin, and a future she still actively wants. She is helping him prepare for his wedding in six weeks and has plans beyond it. Her illness has not already finished her life in any meaningful sense.

The chosen interpretation is one occurrence prevented during the current day. One train crash can involve many people; that crash and Meret dying of her existing illness are two separate occurrences. The power has no surcharge and needs no object. Preventing death does not undo damage already sustained. Her heart can function without magic, precariously, so she is not mechanically killed by a clock or a missed incantation. She ordinarily chooses protection when she wakes. On this morning, the emergency reaches her before that familiar choice. She cannot know whether she would otherwise die today; she knows what she gives up is the certainty that she will not.

## Possible scenes

1. On the previous day, Meret says “Not today” while thinking of her own death, then goes out with Tavin to have his wedding suit fitted. She argues about the suit, embarrasses him, and makes a future appointment she means to keep. She wants another ordinary day and does not apologize for choosing herself.

2. Before her next morning's habitual words, she hears the unmistakable warning whistle of a train whose brakes cannot stop it. A rockfall has torn out a section of the high line visible from her window. The train is approaching the gap. Her working knowledge tells her what the approaching sound means; she has no second gift of prediction. Tavin, staying overnight, understands both available uses. He reaches for her before she speaks. He does not nobly volunteer her life.

3. Meret considers her own death, the thought she knows best. Then she changes what she is holding in mind to the approaching crash and says the words. The whole train stops with its front hanging beyond the broken track: an impossible, visible refusal of a disaster. Meret calls the railway so people can get out. This is the story's decisive action, not the discovery of a clever loophole.

4. The rescue occupies the morning. Passengers climb back along the carriages; one returns for someone who cannot walk. Meret watches particular lives continue. Meanwhile her symptoms worsen. She and Tavin seek ordinary medical help; nothing requires her to accept dying peacefully or to refuse treatment. When she reflexively tries “Not today” for herself, she has already made that day's choice. The words are only words.

5. She dies before the day ends. There is no fresh charge, cure, or recovered bargain. Tavin has to cancel the appointment she made. The final scene gives the rescued train's return to service and her absence room to coexist, without gratitude settling his loss. His wedding and the passengers' futures continue; hers does not. The ending rewards the prevention by showing what it saved and refuses to call its cost negligible.

## Open concern for discussion

Saving many at the price of a terminal person's life can become an automatic moral calculation. The test is whether Meret's desired future and Tavin's resistance make continued self-protection a credible choice, and whether the actual death remains a loss rather than a tidy completion of her life. The exact physical rescue and final image remain provisional.
