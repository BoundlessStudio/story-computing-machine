# Outline

## Story

- Premise and central promise: **The Sun in the Crowd.** Mial, a portraitist born with a Sprite Eye, sees one pale-haired stranger's hidden magic blaze amid faint sparks on a packed street. Mial pursues her as a subject; Auvet agrees to sit only for a likeness she can take away. Deliver the sight, one use of her power, and a portrait shaped by competing ways of seeing.
- Focal pressure or attachment: Mial draws faces for money but privately sketches sparks; visual judgments feel like intimacy. Auvet may vanish into the crowd, and Mial wants to preserve the radiance before asking permission becomes impossible.
- Counterforce or complication: Auvet has a performance to reach and wants a durable, recognizable portrait. Curious about Mial's claim, she still resists being handled as a marvel. She will pay and correct the image without disclosing its recipient. Passing bodies, bad light, and little time obstruct Mial.
- POV, distance, and information limit: Close first-person past through Mial. The left Sprite Eye overlays sparks on living people within sight; it cannot see through barriers or read thoughts, virtue, danger, identity, origin, or ability. The right eye sees ordinary faces. Closing the left removes glare. Distinguish Mial's guesses from observation; Auvet's knowledge enters through conduct and speech.
- Governing movement and time shape: One evening from sighting to departure. Spend the 2,500–4,000 words on pursuit, a negotiated sitting, two likenesses, and corrections.
- Speculative rule or ordinary-world constraint: Most people present as embers; Auvet has a gold-white center fringed pink and violet. Brightness proves neither goodness nor total power. Auvet draws and suspends visible colored light with her own living magic. It illuminates matter and can form moving figures, but is insubstantial, changes no minds, and vanishes when released. Paper and charcoal last.
- Dialogue promise: A portrait becomes a negotiation over how one person looks at another, with curiosity, salesmanship, impatience, play, and attention.
- Dialogic medium: Spoken bargaining amid market noise, poses, a sketch inspected and corrected by pointing, and Auvet's drawn light. Bystanders interrupt for ordinary reasons.
- Dialogue engine: Mial's request lets Auvet specify a useful image. Her corrections expose where Mial substitutes radiance for likeness. Mial must decide when to use the Eye, close it, or ask before adding private sight. Auvet may leave; neither owes a confession.

## Voice

- Narrative texture: Mial notices lifting paper, charcoal, occluded faces, and saturated light. Radiance startles without making every detail an omen; ordinary sight gains specificity.
- Conversational texture: Commercial exchanges, work silences, jokes about a bad drawing, interruptions, and direct questions without polished arguments.
- Rhetorical ownership: Mial reaches for visual comparisons and bargains for time. Auvet owns the purchase terms and is precise about her face and schedule; her humor deflates awe without lecturing.
- Pressure behavior: Mial stares and sketches too fast. Auvet changes pose and corrects details rather than embodying an insight. Time makes Mial quieter and Auvet more direct.
- Relationship movement: Mial wants the radiance but cannot comfortably admit a possessive impulse. Auvet wants a faithful likeness and control over what the stranger takes, yet asking to be seen apart from magic is awkward. Permission and attention change, without promised intimacy.
- Anti-default: Avoid a citywide emergency, power ranking, or explanatory partnership. Auvet's purchase and Mial's imperfect craft supply stakes.

## Beats

1. Folding the portrait stall at a crowded night market, Mial sees Auvet's gold-white blaze amid dark blue silhouettes. The original image guides this subjective contrast and Auvet's pale hair. Mial follows, repeatedly losing her behind taller bodies.
2. Mial catches Auvet where traffic thins and offers a portrait. She declines the extravagant pitch, sees the portfolio, then proposes a paid sitting with a firm departure time. They return to the stall. She wants a likeness she can keep.
3. Mial draws with both eyes open. Glare overwhelms contour; the radiant first page scarcely resembles Auvet. She inspects it, objects to a feature or expression, and asks why Mial looks past her. Mial explains the limited sight without claiming knowledge of her powers. She chooses to continue.
4. Auvet briefly shapes colored light in air to answer a reasonable question. Others see the figure, though not her inner spark. Its transience matters to her wish for paper. Mial's Eye flares harder.
5. Mial closes the Sprite Eye, surrendering the spectacle. Traffic, lamp angle, Auvet's restlessness, and Mial's habits complicate the ordinary likeness. Auvet corrects what she dislikes and may refuse magical embellishment.
6. The performance's starting sounds draw Auvet away. Mial offers the revision and asks whether any trace of the unusual light belongs on her page; her answer follows from the sitting, not a preset moral. She pays for what she accepts and leaves. Mial retains the rejected first sheet; bodies block the Eye from tracking her.
