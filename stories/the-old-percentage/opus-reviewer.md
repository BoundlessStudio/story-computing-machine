# Opus review: The Old Percentage

Independent audit for a later editing pass. It is not a `review.md` verdict,
does not change canon state, and does not authorize edits by itself.

## Verdict

- Dialogue: REVISE — mostly strong and well differentiated, but the decisive turn toward violence is an untagged mirrored stand-off, and the setup never shows how Elspeth hears the plainest theatre cues.
- Prose: PASS — concrete, varied and unsentimental; only a couple of small staging slips.
- Continuity and prompt: PASS — delivers the escape, the attempt to rebuild, the R+ register, the modern-world confusion and the continuation facts from *Nobody Spends Her Name*.
- Edit scope: targeted-dialogue
- Existing review.md said: Verdict PASS, "Dialogue: PASS"; partly disagree. Most exchanges do pass, but the decisive exchange and the misreading setup do not hold up line by line.

This story's dialogue is better than the collection's usual pattern. The speakers
are distinct: Elspeth is clipped, old-fashioned and in command, Corbin talks
performer trade, Dacre plays the host, and the guard sounds like a care-home
worker. The central engine (Elspeth hears tribute where Corbin hears an
appearance fee) is built from legible talk-past lines and pays off at the club.
The failures sit at the hinges. The decisive moment before the violence falls
back on a stock "I'm not playing" / "Neither am I" couplet with no clear speaker.
Elspeth's surprise at the club depends on her having heard "private group", "the
collection, the threats", "cast", "safety cue" and "prop weapon" without the text
ever showing how she read them. A few late pronoun slips also blur what is
happening. The edit pass should make small fixes at those points and leave
everything else alone, including the profanity, the misunderstandings and the
unequal fluency.

## Edit guardrails

- Prompt: adult R+ crime noir. Keep the hard profanity and the graphic, story-bearing violence with lasting consequences (the guard's throat wound and death, the tooth in the cream, the broken right hand, the bent fingers). Criminal methods stay non-instructional. There is no sexual content, and none should be added.
- Length band is 2,500–4,000 words. The prose now runs about 3,100 words, so the fixes should add or remove very little.
- Continuation facts from `stories/empire-within/story.md`: Elspeth was twenty-three, survived the underboss's coup, and took a wound above the left shoulder blade ("Her left arm hung badly, but it moved"). This story adds a capture nineteen days later, fifty-nine years inside, and her present age of eighty-two. Do not add reunions with characters from the source story.
- Elspeth already knows phones, cars and recordings. Only newer things should puzzle her: overlay glasses, bought faces, driverless cars and the recording bead. Do not have her fumble a phone. Do not turn the ending into better management or a moral lesson (the prompt's Avoid line).
- Injury continuity: her left forearm tears on the car latch (L21, dressed at L31, torn again at L241). Her right hand is broken at the table (L239–L241). She reaches out with her left arm at the end (L361). Keep this map intact.
- Mechanics that must survive: the "fifth" versus "twenty percent" misunderstanding (L101–L111, L185–L193); the "House lights" plant (L131) and its payoff (L251, L255); Shona's hidden agenda (Corbin's "Shona, you found her", the knee under the table, "I knew we'd get inside"); Corbin's recording (L293–L301) feeding the news (L331); and the callback from the ill-fitting coat (L11–L15) to Shona's new coat (L351–L357).

## Dialogue findings

### D1 · Major · Elspeth's uptake of the theatre cues is never shown

- Where: L113–L121 (dinner booking), L131–L135 (lobby safety briefing), L139 (door guard)
- Text:
> "He has a private group tomorrow. They want the collection, the threats, all of it. He's bored with the regular cast."
> Elspeth put down her fork. "What cast?"
> "The men he normally hires," Shona said.
> A guest gets upset, somebody feels ill, we stop.
> "How upset?"
> He asked Shona whether she'd brought a prop weapon.
- Problem: Ground/uptake. Read plainly, Corbin's L113 line says a group of people want to watch a staged collection. Elspeth has just seen him perform a racket for paying customers and seen her own face advertising *An Evening with the Mafia*. She queries only the one word "cast", accepts Shona's gloss, and the narration moves on to "an invitation from his employer". The same thing happens with the safety cue ("How upset?") and the guard's question about a prop weapon: she hears them and shows no reading of them. Then at L169–L175 she is honestly baffled ("What entrance?"). L157 ("You're recording a private meeting?") finally shows her frame, but too late. Until then the reader cannot tell whether she misreads these words or is choosing not to know, so the reveal feels like something the author held back rather than her mistake.
- Fix: Add one beat, not an explanation of the experience economy. At L115–L117, either let her ask about the group rather than only "cast" and let Shona's cover explain the guests in Elspeth's own terms, or make "How upset?" at L133 a question that shows she takes the guests to be Dacre's people. The alternative is to extend L121 by one sentence showing she half-suspects and goes anyway because she has nothing. In that case her anger at L185 is about being sold, which the line already says. Illustrative only: Shona: "Friends of his. He likes an audience when he does business." (This is a lie that is literally true.)

### D2 · Major · Decisive stand-off is an untagged, mirrored stock couplet

- Where: L223–L235, Elspeth's demand to Dacre just before the guard moves in
- Text:
> He stopped touching his phone.
> "I'm not playing now."
> "Neither am I."
> The guard moved between them. "All right. Up we get."
- Problem: Attribution plus a Punchline Patch at the story's decisive exchange. Neither line has a tag. By paragraph convention L231 belongs to Dacre, but Elspeth has just made the demand, so it reads naturally as hers too. The two readings give different escalations, and the reader has to stop and assign them. Either way the result is a stock film stand-off: a flat declaration and a mirror-image comeback that closes on a last word instead of a move. Dacre's "now" also has nothing fresh to point to, because he already dropped the act at L195 ("No one's going to make you perform"). The actual turn, Dacre setting the guard on her, happens with no line from Dacre at all.
- Fix: Cut the mirrored pair. Let Dacre make the real move in his host voice: a word to the guard, or to her, that has her removed, so that the guard's "Up we get" answers it. If Elspeth speaks, tag her, and give her a demand or a refusal, not an echo. Keep L227 and the guard's line. Illustrative only: Dacre, still smiling for the table: "Would you see the lady down to her car."

### D3 · Minor · "Eighty-two" arrives with no trigger and strains months of contact

- Where: L31–L37, the car after the escape
- Text:
> Now Shona wrapped a clean sock around the torn arm.
> "Eighty-two," she said. "You didn't tell me you were eighty-two."
> "I thought the dates were wrong."
- Problem: Ground/knowledge stance. Shona has spent months near Elspeth in a geriatric wing and asked about stories fifty-nine years old, yet the exact number lands as news, with nothing on the page that sets it off. "I thought the dates were wrong" legibly marks this as her mistake. But the line mainly works to tell the reader Elspeth's age, and what made Shona believe it now is left for the reader to guess.
- Fix: Tie the realization to something Shona sees or reads, such as the paper-thin skin under her hands or a clinic wristband with the birth date, so the number has a cause. Keep "You could have counted." and "Then we agree." Both are in character and show Elspeth brushing past what Shona has paid.

### D4 · Minor · "That's why I brought you" contradicts Elspeth's choice of address

- Where: L47 and L65–L69, arrival at the old bookmaker's address
- Text:
> By the following evening she had slept for eleven hours, pissed in a stranger's bed, and decided where to begin collecting.
> "Corbin can get us a meeting," Shona said. "That's why I brought you."
- Problem: Ground/continuity. The narration says Elspeth chose the address, then Shona claims she brought Elspeth here to meet Corbin, and Elspeth, who watches everyone for a skim, only looks at her. The reader can't tell whether Shona steered the choice, got lucky, or is taking credit. This is the first sign of Shona's own agenda, so it should be legible.
- Fix: Keep Elspeth's choice (it shows her old map of the city) and let Shona's line admit what she already knew about the place. Or add one clause of narration in which Elspeth registers that she was steered. Illustrative only: "I knew what this place was when you said the address. Corbin can get us a meeting."

### D5 · Minor · "He's opening again" points at the wrong man

- Where: L285–L287, Shona dragging Dacre to the office while the guard bleeds
- Text:
> "Don't empty him out. He's opening again."
- Problem: Ground/referent. "He" is meant to be Dacre, and "opening" means reopening for business. But the nearest bleeding "he" is the guard, and "opening" first reads as a wound. The idea is right: Elspeth wants a weekly payer, not a single robbery. The wording makes the reader parse it twice at a fast beat.
- Fix: Name the business or the time. Illustrative only: "Don't empty him out. I want him open next week."

### D6 · Minor · Shona's final report blurs its subjects and flattens her voice

- Where: L339–L347, Shona returns with the envelope and the new coat
- Text:
> "His collector brought it. The one who rang. I hired him. He collects the loans for us now. Dacre's been making him wait for his money too."
> "Downstairs. He's got somewhere for us tonight. Dacre told him he'd pay till you were caught. Reckons it won't be long."
- Problem: Ground/referent and voice. In "Dacre told him he'd pay till you were caught", "him" is the collector, "he'd" is Dacre, and "Reckons" has no subject (Dacre or the collector?). This comes at the story's final plot reveal. Across three turns Shona speaks in nine short declaratives, the same clipped register as Elspeth, just when she has taken control. The information is right and needed. Elspeth really doesn't know it, so this is not exposition.
- Fix: Give "Reckons" a subject and untangle the "him"/"he'd" pair. Let one sentence show how Shona feels about deciding without asking, whether pride, defensiveness or a sales pitch, so the power shift sounds like hers. Keep "I hired him.", "My money." and "You can buy your own now."

## Prose findings

### P1 · Minor · Small staging slips in narration

- Where: L147–L151 (the phone guest), L323–L325 (getting from the car to the room)
- Text:
> The guests turned; a woman adjusted the little lens pinned to her collar. Another raised a phone.
> The man grinned and lowered it to chest height, still pointed at her.
> They had to leave the car two streets away.
> By the time they reached the room Shona had borrowed, Elspeth had lost a shoe
- Problem: "Another", straight after "a woman", suggests a second woman, and then "The man" appears. Later, an eighty-two-year-old with a broken hand, weak legs and no wheelchair gets from an abandoned car to a borrowed room, and the text never says how.
- Fix: Make it "A man beside her raised a phone" (or similar). Add one clause for the journey, such as another car, a painful walk with Shona half-carrying her, or a lift from someone, without slowing the aftermath.
