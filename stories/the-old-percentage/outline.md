# Outline

## Story

- Premise and central promise: After fifty-nine years imprisoned, Elspeth escapes to establish a new mafia. Her first protection collection has secretly been sold as entertainment. Making it real extortion gives her a foothold among people intending to exploit her.
- Focal pressure or attachment: At eighty-two, she wants money, excellent food, and frightened attention. She mistakes being sought after for being obeyed. Rebuilding requires accomplices she cannot physically dominate.
- Counterforce or complication: Escape helper Shona wants a lucrative criminal business. Corbin wants fame staging danger without suffering it. Financier Dacre sells gangland entertainments and pays for performers' safety. He regards Elspeth as expensive material; his livelihood depends on insulating wealthy customers from consequences.
- POV, distance, and information limit: Close third past through Elspeth's fallible estimates of rank. Readers may suspect the performance first; disclose others' plans through their interventions.
- Governing movement and time shape: Compress capture and imprisonment before three days outside: escape, recruitment supper, private collection, immediate consequence. Target 2,500–4,000 words; expand humiliation and the moment make-believe ceases to protect anyone.
- Speculative rule or ordinary-world constraint: Ordinary aging; later technological modernity without magic or rejuvenation. Paid visual overlays disguise a decayed district; ubiquitous fabricated likenesses make her photograph poor identification. Crime has acquired unfamiliar purchasable performance conventions. Phones, money, and guns remain recognizable. Surveillance has limits; evidence and pursuit persist.
- Dialogue promise: Attempted recruitment encounters casting, pricing, flattery, interruption, and pity. Misunderstandings alter who can exploit whom; comedy exposes appetite and status before corrections become violent.
- Dialogic medium: Meals, whispers, booking messages read aloud, performance cues, and speech disrupted by injury.
- Dialogue engine: Elspeth hears tribute where Corbin means an appearance fee. Shona sustains the misunderstanding for access, then needs Elspeth to understand before Dacre dismisses them.

## Voice

- Narrative texture: Acquisitive narration notices seating, portions, teeth, expensive cloth, and effort concealed by dignified pauses. Mix longer recollections with sudden ugly particulars.
- Conversational texture: Recruitment mingles with food complaints, embarrassment over fees, impatient corrections, and disliked offers. Humor sometimes fails or wounds; explanations serve immediate decisions.
- Rhetorical ownership: Elspeth claims authority through personal questions and remembered obligations; she listens strategically. Shona punctures grandeur but flatters for access. Corbin describes excitement fluently and real harm badly. Dacre's solicitude becomes proprietary when contradicted.
- Pressure behavior: Elspeth loses breath before certainty. Shona stops cushioning bad news. Corbin repeats a failed safety cue. Dacre alternates demands with frightened appeals to fairness.
- Relationship movement: Elspeth wants obedience without requesting care. Shona wants terror without admitting she needs someone else to initiate it. Corbin wants approval without liability; Dacre wants authenticity without surrendering control. Their evasions become unequal, culpable dependence.
- Anti-default: Avoid a lovable elderly technophobe teaching loyalty, a competent crew exchanging polished percentages, or violence that conveniently heals loneliness.

## Beats

1. Establish capture weeks after the defeated coup and fifty-nine years served. Deaths, defections, and silence disperse her organization. Restricted communication and eventual geriatric confinement leave her aware of news but ignorant of ordinary participation. Shona physically assists escape during outside medical care; omit procedural mechanics. A skin tear and exhaustion accompany freedom.
2. Her old collection address now offers simulated gangland history. Her resemblance attracts Corbin, whom Shona already knows. At supper she attempts to hire them; they arrange to hire her. Her demand for a fifth of the take sounds like a performer's fee. Shona withholds her intended takeover.
3. A booking brings them to Dacre's club. Establish staging, security, customers, and recording before trouble. Elspeth expects an operator willing to hear protection terms. Asked to repeat her entrance, she becomes suspicious. Corbin's correction exposes the sale. Dacre offers payment and dismissal; she cannot afford his terms or the car home.
4. Shona privately asks Elspeth to make the threat real so they can take Dacre's business. Elspeth recognizes a prospective usurper and proceeds. Her demand now concerns continuing tribute. A guard understands and intervenes; her damaged arm and frailty make resistance disastrous. Show torn skin and a disabling hand injury without instructional choreography.
5. Shona shoots the guard rather than abandon her investment. His death is graphic, brief, and consequential. Corbin tries the agreed safety cue. Elspeth exploits his fear of implication to enlist him; establish what he actually witnessed and did. Refusal remains possible. Dacre survives, capable of bargaining and later retaliation.
6. Elspeth compels payment and an introduction to another operator, beginning a coercive organization. Shona claims the club's business while Elspeth claims a continuing share. Resolve the first collection through action; let the final exchange expose incompatible ideas of who commands whom. Medical need, witnesses, death, and police pursuit persist. Renewed criminal appetite supplies no redemption.
