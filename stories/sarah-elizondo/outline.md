# Provisional direction

The request promises a deep-time archaeological mystery that becomes an urgent family rescue: the pleasure of reconstructing fifteen consequential events, dread as Sarah's efforts to save humanity prove instrumental in its extinction, and the emotional reversal of discovering whose daughter she is. "Wandered through human history" calls, in my reading, for different centuries and human encounters, not merely dates attached to one laboratory. The ghost must matter, and the new signal must create an actionable last chance. The explicit relationship is the two researchers and their daughter; the researchers' species, the ghost's origin, the mechanics, and the outcome remain open. The later website-copy request does not change these story requirements.

## Chosen approach, open to challenge

Interleave fifteen short reconstructed diary passages, in Sarah's personal order, with the researchers' increasingly dangerous attempt to receive her. The passages are damaged objects: a tile, an overwritten prayer book, a photographic negative, the copper lining of a spacecraft. Their joins reveal causes and alter the extraction plan. Sarah remains physically twelve, but her handwriting and judgment change. She tries different strategies rather than repeating one mistake.

The researchers, provisionally Iru and Kesh, are nonhuman people who raised a human daughter from a recovered genome millions of years after the original human species died. She was their child, not a research assignment. A survey accident carried her into human history; the conscious survey guide folded into her temporal field became her "ghost." It cannot act bodily or dictate her choices. The parents have searched through innumerable renewed bodies for hundreds of thousands of years. Reveal their parenthood through recognized habits and finally a domestic memory, before the climax rather than as a last-line trick.

The human catastrophe is a failed temporal refuge. Sarah's original field lets one person survive passage between times. Humans gradually turn evidence of it into a species-wide escape system. Its final pulse instead destroys the people it addresses; no sleeping population or restorable backup survives. Sarah remains inside the ghost's original, independent field. This is one history: the diary does not describe abandoned timelines. The physical limits of the final pulse and extraction are provisional and need the outline discussion.

## Fifteen events and their consequences

1. **A city of bronze bells.** Having first witnessed the empty aftermath of the last human day, Sarah warns an astronomer of the white sky that precedes it. She draws the ghost's measured sky-pattern to make him believe her. His record preserves the signal by which later people will find temporal space.
2. **A library under siege, centuries later.** She tries to destroy that record. She cannot let the copying children burn with it, so sends them out with their working pages. What had been one vulnerable archive becomes nine independent traditions.
3. **The printing century.** She publishes a warning against the nine traditions, reproducing their different diagrams to expose their common dangerous origin. Printers bring the scattered pieces together for the first time; the missing joins become legible.
4. **A public demonstration in the age of engines.** To stop a fraudulent promise of immortality from attracting patrons, she submits to examination and explains why her own unaging condition is an accident. Her measured body supplies the proof the lecturer lacked. A seamstress who shelters her appears as a girl and then an old woman between entries, giving the intervals a human cost.
5. **A wartime hospital.** Sarah steals the first phase sample from a weapons laboratory and entrusts it to a doctor who promises never to use it for war. His successful treatment of injured children moves temporal research into medicine and makes destroying it politically impossible.
6. **The doctor's distant successor.** She shuts down a clinic's dangerous local clocks. Patients are dying during the interruption; she lets the ghost keep all their beds synchronized until they can be moved. The shared reference proves a single field can address many bodies.
7. **A border camp.** Having learned the future refuge excludes refugees, she supplies a universal human recognition pattern to prevent those people being left to die. The pattern becomes the system's species-wide address, including people who never consent to evacuation.
8. **The first departure city.** She persuades an expedition to settle beyond Earth, hoping some people will escape the impending terrestrial disaster. Its emigrants carry medical clocks. Their relay extends the address to the off-world settlements too.
9. **A hearing broadcast across the settlements.** She exposes the refuge's intended military owners. The resulting civilian compact gives every settlement its own control key; people now trust the system enough to connect everyone.
10. **The buried master clock.** Sarah destroys the single device she believes will initiate the catastrophe. The compact invokes its emergency design: the surviving local clocks elect one another. There is no longer one place she can break to stop them.
11. **An empty operating theatre.** She takes the ghost into the network to erase the universal pattern she supplied. The network recognizes the attempted deletion as the extinction warning it was built to answer. It begins evacuation preparations, centuries before the planners intended.
12. **The engineer's final journey.** To prevent that premature launch, she carries its designer to the last human day and shows him the outcome. He returns with the exact timing of the lethal pulse and builds an automatic rescue to fire immediately before it. The apocalypse acquires its recorded date.
13. **The closing stations.** Sarah intercepts the rescue command. People already inside failing transfer chambers will die unless she gives them somewhere else to go. She supplies her own remembered home coordinates, believing her parents' civilization can receive them. The human network adopts the distant address without the ghost's independent protection.
14. **The last human evening.** The ghost finally reconstructs the whole chain and identifies the refuge itself as the cause. Sarah orders it to tear out the shared reference. The people already entering the field begin to disappear unevenly; the emergency system binds every human clock together to keep families from being separated. Her sabotage has removed the last isolation between them.
15. **The white sky.** Sarah orders the ghost to catch the people falling out of time. It opens its own field to reach them. That last act completes the circuit, and the refuge's pulse consumes the people it was meant to save. The ghost closes around Sarah only after the irreversible failure. Her final entry names a child whose hand she could not hold, rather than reducing humanity to a total. The dying pulse finally carries her original signal across millions of years to the parents.

## Present rescue and ending

At first the researchers intend to lock onto the strongest signal. Reconstructed events 6–9 show that it is now the address of a whole extinct species, not Sarah alone. Events 13–15 prove that repeating the automatic rescue would expose her to the lethal pulse again. They must reach her original protected field instead, while the ghost is still holding it closed. Their last finite aperture lasts only as long as its collapse; it cannot be reopened later.

They speak to Sarah through the diary's original signal pattern. She wants another jump to undo the last evening. They give her the reconstructed chain, not an unexplained declaration that history cannot change. She recognizes her own missing pages and chooses the reachable future while still naming what happened. The ghost chooses to stay at the human end, holding the failed circuit away from her as the parents dismantle their receiver behind her crossing. Sarah must walk out of its protection into the parents' arms; they must destroy the only route by which they could ever search again. The extinction is neither reversed nor declared worthwhile. The rescued girl can finally age. Her last unwritten page belongs to the world after the catastrophe, and her parents are there to hear it.
