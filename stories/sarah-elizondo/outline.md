# Provisional direction

The request promises a deep-time archaeological mystery that becomes an urgent family rescue: the pleasure of reconstructing fifteen consequential events, dread as Sarah's efforts to save humanity prove instrumental in its extinction, and the emotional reversal of discovering whose daughter she is. "Wandered through human history" calls, in my reading, for different centuries and human encounters, not merely dates attached to one laboratory. The ghost must matter, and the new signal must create an actionable last chance. The explicit relationship is the two researchers and their daughter; the researchers' species, the ghost's origin, the mechanics, and the outcome remain open. The later website-copy request does not change these story requirements.

## Chosen approach, open to challenge

Interleave fifteen short reconstructed diary passages, in Sarah's personal order, with the researchers' increasingly dangerous attempt to receive her. Fourteen have been assembled over the search from damaged objects: a tile, an overwritten prayer book, a photographic negative, the copper lining of a spacecraft. The fifteenth is arriving through the newly live signal as the ghost transmits Sarah's last written page; it is not an object she somehow deposited after becoming trapped. Its joins with the earlier fragments reveal causes and alter the extraction plan. Sarah remains physically twelve, but her handwriting and judgment change. She tries different strategies rather than repeating one mistake.

The researchers, provisionally Iru and Kesh, are nonhuman people who raised a human daughter from a recovered genome millions of years after the original human species died. She is a new person with her own memories, not the restoration of anyone killed. A survey accident carried her into human history; the conscious survey guide folded into her temporal field became her "ghost." It cannot act bodily or dictate her choices. The parents have searched through innumerable renewed bodies for hundreds of thousands of years. Reveal their parenthood before the climax through a diary drawing of Kesh's particular hand and a repair seam in Sarah's old nursery; the researchers recognize what strangers mistook for religious symbols. Their present disagreement over interrupting a child at work returns under deadly pressure when they must call her away.

The human catastrophe is a failed temporal refuge. A closed field can protect its contents. An attached outer contact permits signals and identifies a field; opening the field through that contact joins its contents to the shared circuit and exposes them to any failure. Demonstrate both states through the clinic in event 6. Humans gradually turn evidence of Sarah's field into a species-wide escape system. Its final pulse destroys the people it addresses; no sleeping population or restorable backup survives. The ghost closes its original field around Sarah after the failure, but its outer contact remains attached to the dying network. This is one history, not abandoned timelines. Early failed calls establish that knowing home coordinates does not create a route: the ghost can follow historical traces of its signal, but cannot reach the distant parents without a live receiving link. The extinction pulse is the first transmission strong enough to reach their waiting apparatus. Sarah spends several subjective hours in the sealed field after event 15; she has neither a safe receiving endpoint nor a way to step into the lethal exterior before the parents connect. Detaching earlier would only discard the unanswered lifeline. The pulse is consuming its transmitter; its disappearance will end the only live route. The parents must receive the closed field and cut that outside connection before opening it; opening it in their present ends the suspension of Sarah's biological age.

## Fifteen events and their consequences

1. **A city of bronze bells.** Having first witnessed the empty aftermath of the last human day, Sarah warns an astronomer of the white sky that precedes it. She draws the ghost's measured sky-pattern to make him believe her. His record preserves the signal by which later people will find temporal space. Her attempt to call home reaches silence; in the present her parents have preserved countless unanswered calls in the opposite direction.
2. **A library under siege, centuries later.** She tries to destroy that record. She cannot let the copying children burn with it. She sees the pages in their arms and chooses to let them leave rather than fight them in the fire. What had been one vulnerable archive becomes nine independent traditions.
3. **The printing century.** She knowingly risks reproducing the nine traditions' diagrams in a public warning: secret suppression has failed, so she wants their readers to argue against them. Printers bring the scattered pieces together for the first time; the missing joins become legible. Her printer refuses to remove the evidence from her argument.
4. **A public demonstration in the age of engines.** To stop a fraudulent promise of immortality from attracting patrons, she submits to examination and explains why her own unaging condition is an accident. Her measured body supplies the proof the lecturer lacked. A seamstress who shelters her appears as a girl and then an old woman between entries, giving the intervals a human cost.
5. **A wartime hospital.** Sarah steals the first phase sample from a weapons laboratory and entrusts it to a doctor who promises never to use it for war. His successful treatment of injured children moves temporal research into medicine and makes destroying it politically impossible.
6. **The doctor's distant successor.** She shuts down a clinic's dangerous local clocks. Patients are dying during the interruption; she lets the ghost keep their beds synchronized until they can be moved. An old woman begs not to wake alone among strangers. Sarah learns to detach a closed patient field before opening it; a joined empty bed is destroyed when another clock fails. The shared reference nevertheless proves one field can address many bodies.
7. **A border camp.** A refugee is denied the protection given to a citizen in the next bed. Sarah supplies a universal human recognition pattern so the future refuge cannot exclude him or his children. That pattern becomes its species-wide address, including people who never consent to evacuation. She draws her nonhuman parents for the boy, who has asked who would be allowed to rescue them.
8. **The first departure city.** She persuades an expedition to settle beyond Earth. On a later visit, the pilot's children have grown past the marks on the ship's wall while Sarah remains the same height. The emigrants' medical clocks have become their settlements' relay: the attempted escape has extended the fatal address beyond Earth.
9. **A hearing broadcast across the settlements.** With a clock worker's widow, Sarah exposes the refuge's intended military owners. The resulting civilian compact gives every settlement its own control key; people now trust the system enough to connect everyone. The widow has won the public protection she asked for, not a knowingly malign bargain.
10. **The buried master clock.** A retired worker helps Sarah destroy the device he built, believing her account of the last human day. They hear the remaining clocks resume one by one: the civilian compact's emergency design lets them elect one another. There is no longer one place she can break to stop them.
11. **An empty operating theatre.** She takes the ghost into the network to erase the universal pattern she supplied. The network recognizes the attempted deletion as the extinction warning it was built to answer. It begins evacuation preparations, centuries before the planners intended.
12. **The engineer's final journey.** To prevent that premature launch, she carries its designer to the last human day and shows him the outcome. He returns with the exact timing of the lethal pulse and builds an automatic rescue to fire immediately before it. The apocalypse acquires its recorded date.
13. **The closing stations.** Sarah intercepts the rescue command. People already inside failing transfer chambers will die unless she gives them somewhere else to go. She supplies the home coordinates that have never answered her calls, gambling that her parents will make room if the network can finally reach them. The human system adopts the distant destination without the ghost's independent protection. Reading this, the parents understand she was trying to send them the people she could not save herself.
14. **The last human evening.** The ghost reconstructs the chain and identifies the refuge itself as the cause. Sarah orders it to tear out the shared reference, knowing this may strand people mid-transfer but believing it preferable to the pulse. The emergency system binds every human clock together to keep families from being separated. Her sabotage has removed the last isolation between them.
15. **The white sky.** Sarah orders the ghost to catch the people falling out of time. It states that opening its isolated field exposes it too, then chooses to try. Sarah makes the physical connection. It completes the lethal circuit, and the refuge's pulse consumes the people it was meant to save. The ghost closes around Sarah only after the irreversible failure. Her final entry names a child whose hand she could not hold. The dying pulse finally carries her original signal across millions of years to the parents.

## Present rescue and ending

At first the researchers intend to lock onto the strongest signal. Reconstructed events 6–9 show that it is now the address of a whole extinct species, not Sarah alone. Events 13–15 prove that opening her field at the human end would expose her to the lethal pulse again. They must transfer her original field intact, still closed. Their aperture lasts only while the failing human transmitter does.

They call Sarah through the individual signal in the first tile. She wants another jump and refuses to disconnect while the human address still calls for help. They supply her missing pages and the death record: the voice is an automatic request, not survivors they are abandoning. Sarah recognizes the apparatus omitted from the damaged diary. She must physically detach the contact she made in event 15, using what she learned at the clinic, while the ghost keeps their field sealed. Neither parent can do this from outside. Iru disables the receiver's automatic species lock; Kesh takes hold of the isolated field at their end. They sever the receiver from the terminal system before opening the field, carrying Sarah into their arms as the route vanishes. The ghost's continuous mind can remain in the receiver, but its links to human history end. No sacrificial death displaces the parents' reunion. Extinction is neither reversed nor declared worthwhile. Sarah can finally age; her next page belongs to the world after the catastrophe, with her parents there to hear it.
