# Outline

## Story

- Premise and central promise: The First Winter. On the morning an unnamed twenty-two-year-old decides to leave, Neshka, the witch who raised her, admits that the entire outside world has repeated one day since her birth. The searcher is her birth mother, still seeking a newborn. Deliver the release of time and a departure complicated by two incompatible experiences of motherhood.
- Focal pressure or attachment: The narrator wants winter, strangers, and an ordinary future; she also expects Neshka to remain the person she can tell everything. Discovering Orlaith makes her hungry for an alternative childhood she cannot actually obtain.
- Counterforce or complication: During a dangerous evacuation, Orlaith entrusted her newborn to Neshka overnight. Neshka feared returning an infant to that road; a temporary refuge became twenty-two years of possession. She concealed the living mother. Her care was real, her fear partly justified, and her continued confinement deliberate.
- POV, distance, and information limit: Close first-person past through the daughter. No access to either mother's thoughts or distant events; Neshka's admission explicitly establishes the worldwide scope. The narrator's guesses about maternal feelings can fail.
- Governing movement and time shape: Three successive valley days, each meeting the same exterior day, followed by a brief first-winter coda. Revisit the pass with different conversational intentions. Target 2,500–4,000 words.
- Speculative rule or ordinary-world constraint: Living Neshka continuously sustains the working. Valley lives and possessions age, but its season remains autumn; the rest of the physical world resets at valley dawn, including bodies, events, and memory. The boundary turns crossing travelers back, while sight and speech pass through. Nobody crosses it during the repeats. Release ends both effects: outside time advances from the held day; valley inhabitants retain their years. No doubled people, accumulated exterior aging, or restoration of lost childhood. Autumn crops still need tending, clothes wear out, and no new goods arrive.
- Dialogue promise: A grown child tries to meet a mother who has not had time to become the mother of a grown child, while confronting the guardian who did.
- Dialogic medium: Spoken encounters across the pass, private household argument, habitual gestures, and one ordinary letter after release.
- Dialogue engine: Only the narrator remembers successive meetings with Orlaith. Improved questions retrieve facts but cannot manufacture shared years. Neshka initially treats another reset as an opportunity to obtain a less painful response.

## Voice

- Narrative texture: Attend to familiar things becoming temporally strange: a worn blue cuff beside an unworn baby blanket. Curious, bodily narration admits envy and petty disappointment without supplying an ethical commentary.
- Conversational texture: Domestic familiarity with Neshka includes interruption and irritated affection. Orlaith has urgent, repetitive questions and scant interest in a stranger's elaborate hypotheticals. Allow plain answers and failed starts.
- Rhetorical ownership: The narrator experiments, jokes defensively, and overexplains. Neshka names concrete dangers, evades through remembered care, and expects old permissions. Orlaith owns details of childbirth and evacuation; she can be distracted, impatient, and kind without becoming the narrator's counselor.
- Pressure behavior: The daughter becomes more fluent through rehearsal while Orlaith always arrives unprepared. Neshka loses her usual ability to finish the daughter's sentences.
- Relationship movement: The daughter wants both recognition and departure; Neshka wants continued attachment without confessing its coercion. Orlaith wants her infant returned. Release changes what they may ask of each other without making either mother replace the other.
- Anti-default: Do not turn the repeated meetings into a perfected reunion speech, or make understanding Neshka's fear restore trust.

## Beats

1. Packing at breakfast, the daughter mistakes Neshka's hesitation for ordinary parental fuss. The confession changes the meaning of every autumn she remembers. Establish the search, worldwide repetition, and boundary before she attempts departure. The inspected image informs Neshka's dark hair, broad pointed blue hat, layered blue clothes, quiet profile, and brilliant gold leaves against deep shadow.
2. At the pass, Orlaith arrives carrying the newborn's blanket and searching for Neshka. The daughter asks indirect questions about the evacuation and her birth. Orlaith answers what seems useful to finding her baby, then resumes that purpose. The daughter notices their near equality in apparent age. Establish the particular repeated arrival and its timing.
3. Back home, familiar clothing and childhood objects substantiate Neshka's years of care. They also expose the duration of her concealment. She could not make the road safe by denying it another day; she nevertheless kept choosing their household. Give her affection ordinary physical expression without letting it settle the argument.
4. On the second valley day, Orlaith arrives exactly as before. The daughter attempts a clearer disclosure, using yesterday's intimate information as evidence. Orlaith's response combines disbelief, alarm, and grief; whatever she begins to accept, she still wants the baby she held yesterday. Let one particular response hurt more than the daughter expected. She cannot cite yesterday as an experience Orlaith remembers.
5. Neshka offers another attempt tomorrow. The daughter recognizes her own temptation to rehearse Orlaith into giving the desired welcome. At the third meeting she abandons that project, identifies herself without demanding immediate maternal feeling, and gives Orlaith space to ask her own questions. This encounter remains unfinished when evening separates them.
6. The decisive private exchange concerns release and actual departure. Neshka may ask the daughter to stay or promise a return; she cannot secure either before choosing. The daughter waits with her packed bag at the pass instead of resuming household life. Neshka releases the working. At the next dawn, Orlaith retains the previous meeting and can continue it. Crossing becomes possible; the daughter leaves, with destination and companionship chosen in scene. Preserve estrangement from Neshka and awkward beginnings with Orlaith.
7. Weeks later, the daughter experiences real winter. She sends Neshka a plain firsthand account of snow while withholding her address. The letter carries an attachment she still has and the distance she still needs; avoid an explanatory final maxim.
