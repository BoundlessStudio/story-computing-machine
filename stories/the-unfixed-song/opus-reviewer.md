# Opus review: The Unfixed Song

Independent audit for a later editing pass. It is not a `review.md` verdict,
does not change canon state, and does not authorize edits by itself.

## Verdict

- Dialogue: REVISE — the argument scenes run on correction ping-pong and closing maxims, and the decisive authorization exchange is built for a last word.
- Prose: PASS — clean, concrete, zero em dashes; only minor thesis narration, glosses and one policy nit.
- Continuity and prompt: REVISE — the relay cloth code changes colour at the climax (small fix); the prompt's promise is otherwise delivered.
- Edit scope: targeted-dialogue
- Existing review.md said: Verdict PASS, "Dialogue: PASS"; disagree — it missed the argument-scene ping-pong, the "ingest" exchange and the cloth-code flip.

The action dialogue is the story's strength. Under wind and a clock, Brannic's load calls, Mazhira's relay correction, the "Take them" exchange and the three-way standoff with Heket holding her wrist are practical and distinct, and they change what happens. The failures come where people argue. At the intake, the sounding report and the history dispute, Heket, Zulmec, Kuvesh and sometimes Mazhira trade clipped, uncontracted corrections and closing maxims ("Water moves. The order doesn't.", "Repeating a depth is not taking one.", "It's the number."). As a result, the disputed history reads like a volley about rules of evidence, not two people defending their dead. Both bookends that matter most are also built for last words: the opening exchange exists to deliver Heket's maxim, and the authorization ends on "I won't ingest it." / "You don't have to. I do." The edit pass should break the symmetry of the argument runs (let lines go unanswered, let someone dominate or deflect), give Heket and Zulmec one concrete personal stake each in speech, and state the bargain earlier so Kuvesh isn't briefing the lead watch. The climax and the teaching coda should stay mostly as they are.

The rewrite request covers dialogue only; the plot and the choice stay. Keep 2,500–4,000 words (now about 3,160), broadly accessible, close third on Mazhira. - Mazhira's youth is shown only by contrast with "the two elders" and her mother's teaching. Do not lose that contrast. One light marker is optional.
- Causal spine that must survive: the archive cannot hold two routes as one voyage; a partial form is rejected because spoken attribution is an observation field; other variants may survive only as non-authoritative audio, never as taught route authority. Soundings confirm the south shelf; present water favours the lower fork; Mazhira authorizes Zulmec's full form, accusation included; the 38 percent sail figure saves the glasshouses; Heket's "wait for the rear" correction is right; Mazhira teaches the unprovable line whole.
- The old history must stay unresolved. No edit may prove either the "chose the lee" or the "forward knives" version.
- Universe: no LOCKED/CANON entry names this city, archive or people. The archive stays a local, nonliving, non-magical system (LOCKED "Living agency and magic"). The unresolved witness dispute fits LOCKED "Memory, myth, and recoverable truth". Content is well inside the LOCKED audience boundary.

## Dialogue findings

### D1 · Major · Opening exchange exists to land Heket's maxim

- Where: L11–L19, opening rehearsal on the lead deck
- Text:
> The pepper gardens were still three towlines short when Heket stopped the song.
> No. Hold that note over the pale water.
> The pale water has moved,
> Water moves. The order doesn't.
- Problem: Punchline Patch plus Ground/referent. The reader is never told who was singing when Heket "stopped the song", so "that note" has no owner. Mazhira's objection is never developed: it exists so Heket can answer with a maxim. That maxim also dismisses the landmark Heket used one line earlier ("over the pale water"), and nobody notices. This is the reader's first dialogue in the story, and it sets up the correction-and-epigram rhythm that follows.
- Fix: Make it clear in L11 or a beat that Mazhira is rehearsing. Turn her objection into a concrete observation that matters now (where the pale water lies against the fork), and let Heket answer with the sequence, not the maxim. Cut "Water moves. The order doesn't." Illustrative only: Heket: "Then hold it on the count. Third seam, hold high, wait for the rear to cast clear."

### D2 · Major · "The wind didn't" has no recoverable frame

- Where: L39–L47, Mazhira asks Zulmec for his version
- Text:
> Zulmec's drum answered from below the last note.
> "Again?" Mazhira asked him.
> The wind didn't.
> That brought his head up.
- Problem: Ground/figurative plus Punchline Patch. "Again?" asks him to repeat a song he hasn't sung yet: so far he has only said "Four under" and struck the drum. "You heard." / "The wind didn't." depends on a belief the story never sets up (that a song counts only when sung aloud, or to the wind). The comeback still moves Zulmec ("brought his head up"), so it works by the author's logic, not the characters'.
- Fix: Have Mazhira ask plainly for his full form with words, for a practical reason (she needs his words beside Heket's), or let him start unasked. Keep "You heard." if useful as reluctance. Illustrative only: "I heard the drum. Sing me the words with it."

### D3 · Major · Correction ping-pong at the intake and the sounding report

- Where: L95–L105, the archive intake after "VOYAGE COUNT CONTRADICTION"; L135–L151, Zulmec's report after the skiffs return
- Text:
> Then the machine is not ready.
> The mesh is ready," Kuvesh said. "The corpus isn't authorized.
> Repeating a depth is not taking one.
> Brannic, passing with a bucket, said, "That is not a number."
> That's a bad number.
> It's the number.
- Problem: Tennis Match / Identical Voices. Both runs are six perfectly alternating one-liners, and each line corrects the one before. Both close on a last word ("Repeating a depth is not taking one." / "It's the number."). Heket, Kuvesh, Zulmec and Brannic all use the same uncontracted, word-precise register here, and Brannic's "That is not a number" is Heket's voice, not his. In the first run, "mesh" and "corpus" bring in authorization as jargon before the reader knows the bargain. In the second, "I have the sounding." / "Then use it." is another comeback.
- Fix: At the intake, keep Kuvesh's point in plain words (the machine works; the city hasn't given it one form) and keep Zulmec's "Authorize the route that has a shelf." Turn Heket's doubt into a factual question (when was the shelf last sounded?) and cut "Repeating a depth is not taking one." so that Mazhira's "We sound both approaches" answers an open question, not a maxim. At the report, keep the facts (a span and a half at the workshop keels, only if sail comes down before the lip). Cut "Then use it." and "It's the number.", and let Brannic answer the bad number with what it costs him. Illustrative only: Brannic: "Span and a half with the kilns loaded? Then I want the roofs down before the lip, not at it."

### D4 · Major · The history dispute plays as an evidentiary tennis match

- Where: L153–L177, Mazhira asks whether the rear gardens cast loose
- Text:
> Did the rear gardens cast loose?
> What they could know was the release signal came before the knife order.
> Your teacher was a child.
> Old enough to count cut rope.
> Not old enough to see the lead signal.
> From the forward record.
> The only record made during the turn.
> By the hands on the knives.
- Problem: Tennis Match / Negotiation-procedure register at the story's central history exchange. Mazhira's question and "That wasn't my question." work well. After Heket's evasion, though, the exchange becomes nine alternating one-liners of equal weight, argued as admissibility (what could be seen, who was old enough, whose record counts), with parallel comebacks ("Old enough..." / "Not old enough..."). Neither elder's stake reaches speech as anything but procedure: Zulmec's teacher was on a cut garden, and Heket's sequence was kept by the accused crews (L233). The last three lines are untagged, so the reader has to count back to know who speaks. Heket's admission that a "knife order" existed goes by with no reaction from anyone.
- Fix: Keep L153–L159 and the drum strike at L169. Then break the symmetry. Give Zulmec one concrete remembered detail from his teacher in place of the "Old enough" comeback. Let Heket refuse the point or turn to the strips instead of volleying "Not old enough to see the lead signal." Cut one of the three closing "record" lines, or tag them. Optionally let Mazhira register the knife-order admission with a look or a half-question, without resolving the history. Illustrative only: Zulmec: "He counted them going past him. Seven ropes, and every end was cut."

### D5 · Major · The bargain's terms are recited to the lead watch mid-crisis

- Where: L203–L217, after "PARTIAL FORM INCOMPLETE"
- Text:
> The climate archive had been speaking for three days.
> People also set the service condition. All variants recorded. One authorized form. Future route reports must use it.
- Problem: Exposition / "As you know". The archive has been working for three days, and Mazhira is the officer who must authorize it, yet Kuvesh turns to her and recites the service condition as a list of clauses. This is also the first time the reader learns the prompt's central bargain, about two-thirds of the way through, delivered as a briefing during an emergency. The loophole questions that follow (L209–L215) are earned: a clause-by-clause register suits a contract with a fixed machine.
- Fix: State the price once, early: one clause in the L73–L77 narration, or folded into Kuvesh's line at L97. At L207, have Mazhira name the condition herself as the thing she is trying to get around, with Kuvesh only confirming, or answer her specific proposal. Keep "Can a navigator use it?" / "Not in a report. Not in instruction that becomes a report." Keep L201–L205 ("Of course it mistakes blame for weather." and Kuvesh's cause-field answer); trim the "People made that field." / "People also set..." pivot if the recital goes.

### D6 · Major · Decisive exchange ends on a patched last word

- Where: L241–L253, Mazhira authorizes Zulmec's full form
- Text:
> At the accusatory line, Heket stepped away from the cabinet.
> Heket said, "I won't ingest it."
> You don't have to." Mazhira's thumb shook against the cold metal. "I do.
- Problem: Ground/verb-fit plus Punchline Patch at the story's decisive moment. "Ingest" appears nowhere else in the story. It is the machine's verb, and here it comes from the character most opposed to the machine. Nobody has asked Heket to do anything: Kuvesh's hand is at the key, and Mazhira has already spoken the authorization. The reader has to translate "ingest" into "swallow" or "accept" to follow Mazhira's reply, and "I do" then says Mazhira must ingest it. The exchange is shaped for its closing line. Heket's step back at L241 and Mazhira's shaking thumb already carry the moment.
- Fix: Either cut both lines and let the step back, the thumb and the key carry it, or give Heket a refusal that fits what is physically happening (she won't witness, won't stand at the cabinet, won't hear it played back), in her own vocabulary of records and sequence. In that case Mazhira does not reply with a comeback; she turns the key. Illustrative only: Heket: "Don't ask me to stand here for it."

### D7 · Major · "Thirty-eight now" is an unclear referent at the climax

- Where: L297–L303, Heket holds Mazhira's wrist before the turn
- Text:
> We have eleven minutes.
> Thirty-eight now. If you turn, you draw them across the corner.
- Problem: Ground/referent at a key beat. Heket's case for waiting depends on the sail already being down to the archive's 38 percent, so the gust is no longer the main danger. But "thirty-eight" answers a line about minutes, and the sail figure was last mentioned about forty lines earlier. Readers can easily take it as minutes, hulls or sections before working it out.
- Fix: Name the unit, and keep Heket's second sentence, which carries the argument. Illustrative only: "We're already down to thirty-eight percent. Turn now and you drag them across the corner."

### D8 · Minor · Small Ground slips in single lines

- Where: L53, Brannic asks for a course; L191, Kuvesh runs choices; L229, Heket objects to "Full form"
- Text:
> If the history has finished moving the pepper raft, I need a course.
> Record both, mark one historical,
> Then don't bind them.
- Problem: At L53 the history didn't move the raft; Brannic's crew did. The sarcasm has a subject and verb that don't fit together. At L191, "mark one historical" never says which one, yet Kuvesh keys it in without asking. At L229, "them" could mean the stone and the knife, the directions and the blame, or the city's crews.
- Fix: L53: make the sarcasm fit what his crew actually did (illustrative only: "If the history's done, the pepper raft's in line and I need a course."). L191: name the form marked historical, or have Kuvesh ask. L229: make "them" explicit in a few words.

### D9 · Minor · The haggle's "Nine" lacks a reason

- Where: L109–L121, Brannic and Mazhira bargain over skiff crews
- Text:
> Ten, and you keep the other two you were about to hide from me.
> Those two are fixing lunch.
> "Nine."
- Problem: The play suits Brannic and is worth keeping. But once he explains the two hidden hands, Mazhira drops her ask from ten to nine for no reason the reader can see. The reader has to do the arithmetic and still can't follow it, and the scene stalls while the cold stripe is due in twenty minutes.
- Fix: Keep "With which spare city?" and "Those two are fixing lunch." Either make the concession readable (she gives him one of the lunch pair back) or close at ten.

### D10 · Minor · Zulmec's refusal closes on a pun

- Where: L357–L359, morning teaching, Mazhira offers the stick
- Text:
> He shook his head. "You signed it. Keep the beat."
- Problem: This is the final exchange with either elder. "You signed it." is plain and puts the responsibility on Mazhira; "Keep the beat." adds a tidy double-meaning last word on top.
- Fix: Keep the head shake and "You signed it." Cut "Keep the beat.", or replace it with an action (he leaves the stick where it is, or looks toward the water).

## Continuity and prompt findings

### C1 · Blocking · The relay cloth code flips colour at the climax

- Where: L59, the code is set up; L321–L339, the wait for the far square and the turn
- Text:
> white for joined, yellow for waiting, red at the far rear where the seed gardens still drifted on temporary tethers.
> Yellow flickered white, then yellow again.
> At last the far square turned black: joined and clear.
> The last relay turned white.
- Problem: The climax turns on one yellow square. L59 establishes white as "joined", and L321's white flicker is called a misread, which fits that code. Then the square turns black, a colour the cloth code does not contain, and the narration has to explain it as "joined and clear". After the turn, the last relay turns white, which reads as a second, redundant "joined". Red also drifts from "on temporary tethers" (L59) to "in the backwash" (L125, L185).
- Fix: Smallest option: make L327 "turned white", keep the L321 flicker as the false start, and change or cut L339 (for example, point it at the route shutters). Alternatively, add a distinct "clear of the lip" colour to the L59 code. If convenient, let L59's red mean "in trouble" so its later uses match.

### C2 · Minor · Heket's "transmitted sequence" doesn't match what she sang

- Where: L37, Heket sings; L171, she recites
- Text:
> Cold rain. Third seam. Hold over. Rear casts clear.
> Third seam. Hold high. Rear casts clear. Four down. That is the transmitted sequence.
- Problem: For the character whose authority is exact transmission, singing "Hold over" and then reciting "Hold high" as the exact sequence looks like an error, not a deliberate variation.
- Fix: Use the same words in both places, or make the difference deliberate and noticed.

### C3 · Minor · Archive units and intake path drift

- Where: L63, calibration trial; L181, decision window; L79 and L239, song intake
- Text:
> ARRIVAL: FORTY-TWO BREATHS. DURATION: ELEVEN BREATHS.
> WARM-CORRIDOR DECISION WINDOW: FOURTEEN MINUTES.
> Kuvesh laid two waxed song strips side by side beneath the intake needle.
> Zulmec sang into the horn.
- Problem: A fixed machine whose purpose is standardization reports in breaths once and in minutes and hours afterwards. Songs go in through waxed strips and the intake needle, but at the decisive moment Zulmec sings into the horn, which until then has only been the archive's output.
- Fix: Use one unit throughout, or have Kuvesh convert once. Either have Zulmec sing onto a strip or at the intake, or mention once that the horn also takes sound in.
