# Opus review: Sakura-Hearted

Independent audit for a later editing pass. It is not a `review.md` verdict,
does not change canon state, and does not authorize edits by itself.

## Verdict

- Dialogue: REVISE — the banter is warm and the two voices are distinct, but one exchange invents a misreading to set up a punchline, one beat is out of order, and the decisive memory exchange shifts time frame partway through.
- Prose: PASS — concrete, restrained and well paced; only a spelling-consistency sweep and one repeated touch in the last paragraph.
- Continuity and prompt: PASS — every prompt element is delivered and ages and intervals agree; one small wobble in the tram-stop memory.
- Edit scope: targeted-dialogue
- Existing review.md said: Verdict PASS; "Dialogue: PASS — Exchanges preserve shared reality and listener uptake"; I disagree on uptake: three exchanges break it.

For this collection, the dialogue here is in good shape. The spouses sound different, most exchanges change something between them (the trays, the peaches, the tram-stop kiss, "I liked you so much"), and the ending doesn't explain itself. The failures are local and come from one habit: lines written backwards from a payoff, or placed after the beat they are meant to answer. The vase exchange makes him misread her so she can say "In the sky". The glasses-cupboard dialogue comes after the obstacle is already gone and relies on "stands" and "the rest", which the reader never sees. The decisive memory exchange opens in tonight's time frame and answers from the first date. The repeated pattern of him asking and her answering in one short, exact line suits a quiet genius and should stay. The edit pass should make small, precise repairs: pin down the referents, reorder the beats, cut the setups that exist only for a punchline, and keep the warmth and his mock-formal humour.

## Edit guardrails

- Prompt requirements: a retro-futuristic dress (L47), white elbow-length opera gloves, a white circular-buckled cinch belt (L39, L47, L389), white knee-high go-go boots, and a sakura pink raygun held in one hand (L23) must all stay on the page. Her quietness, sweetness, genius and active love are central. Keep first-person past tense through the unnamed husband, warm non-explicit adult romance, and a length of 2,500–4,000 words (currently about 3,150).
- Both spouses must stay unambiguously adult: keep "I was thirty-eight, she was thirty-six" (L125) or an equivalent.
- Internal facts the story depends on: eight years married, and ten years since the first-date ride at twenty-eight; one planet region per year of marriage (eight regions, L219, L249); a ninety-second coin timer; the raygun's limits (three kilos per disc, about kitchen range, discs hover about twenty minutes and need a surface beneath); the ride stays on the floor; scenery resets to its starting positions at L313 and L379 without showing the moon's face.
- Universe: technology only, with no magic (the Long Dark backbone in `universe/timeline.md`, LOCKED). The story establishes no shared canon and names no places. Don't add water infrastructure while revising.
- What works and must not be flattened: his mock-formal, self-mocking humour; her understated mischief through literal truth; the unhurried physical affection; and the ending, where the moon's face stays unseen because she chooses the kiss.

## Dialogue findings

### D1 · Blocking · Vase riddle built for "In the sky"

- Where: L283–L291, kitchen, just after he fills the vase
- Text:
> She found a pair of scissors and trimmed the flowers. I filled their vase.
> “May I put these in?” she asked.
> “In the ride?”
> “In the sky.”
- Problem: Ground/uptake plus Punchline Patch. The last thing we see is him filling the vase, so "put these in" can only mean "in the vase", and he would answer yes. His "In the ride?" doesn't respond to anything he heard. He guesses a destination nobody mentioned only so that she can land "In the sky." The exchange is written backwards from its last line, and it is the one place Celestine talks in riddles rather than in her usual plain, understated way.
- Fix: Cut L285–L289 and let the action be the surprise: the disc clipped under the vase, the trigger, the water trembling (L291). "Will they stay up?" (L293) already gives him a natural reaction. If a line is wanted before she lifts the vase, make it plain. Illustrative only: "Don't put them down yet."

### D2 · Major · Glasses-cupboard beat out of order

- Where: L275–L281, kitchen, ringed planet in front of the glasses cupboard
- Text:
> The ringed planet blocked the cupboard where we kept the glasses. She considered it, then put the raygun down and moved it by hand.
> “There's a button for all of them to return to their stands,” she said. “But then you'd see the rest.”
> “We could drink straight from the bottle.”
> She glanced at me over her shoulder. “You may. I'd like a glass.”
- Problem: Ground/timing and referent. The narration clears the obstacle ("moved it by hand") before either line is spoken, so his bottle suggestion answers a problem that no longer exists, and her "You may" becomes a comeback with nothing to push against. No bottle has been mentioned. Nobody asked why she didn't take a shortcut, so her button line comes out of nowhere. "Stands" were never shown, since the models hang or hover, and "the rest" can't be pinned down: the models are already in plain view, so the reader can't tell what returning them would reveal. "Moved it" also points grammatically at the raygun.
- Fix: Put any talk while the planet still blocks the cupboard, then have her move it, and write "moved the planet". The cleanest option is to cut the button line, because moving the planet by hand already shows her care for the surprise. If it stays, make "the rest" concrete without contradicting the later resets at L313 and L379. If the bottle joke stays, it must come before she clears the cupboard, with the bottle visibly in his hand.

### D3 · Major · "The first ride" points to tonight; the reply answers ten years ago

- Where: L339–L359, after the second ride stops
- Text:
> “I wanted that on the first ride,” she said.
> “A planet in the face?”
> “No.” She rubbed her cheek against mine. “A kiss. Before we got out.”
> “You kissed me at the tram stop.”
> I drew back enough to see her. “I thought you were being polite all evening.”
- Problem: Ground/referent and uptake at the story's decisive exchange. The narration has just called this circuit "the second ride" (L311) and shown the couple climbing out after the first (L219), so "the first ride… Before we got out" reads as tonight's first circuit. His own "A planet in the face?" takes it that way. His next line, "You kissed me at the tram stop", suddenly answers from the first date ten years ago. The reader has to go back and reread, and "polite all evening" leaves the frame unclear until the narration at L359 explains it. This is the reveal that she wanted him from the start, and it shouldn't depend on a double take.
- Fix: Fix the time frame in her reply to his joke so that his tram-stop line follows. Keep his planet joke as a misreading the reader can see. Illustrative only: "No." She rubbed her cheek against mine. "A kiss. The first time, before we got out." Leave "I got tired of waiting", "There was room" and "I liked you so much" untouched.

### D4 · Minor · Raygun explanation: unclear referents and a spec sheet

- Where: L111–L123, at the ride's nose, about the chrome bands; also L293–L295, the flower timer
- Text:
> The old emitter wouldn't fit. It wasted most of its energy keeping the field steady, so I made it send a sequence of smaller pulses instead.
> “Three kilos on one disc. Less if I want it moving for long. It reaches about as far as our kitchen.”
> Then the disc will lower them. There needs to be something beneath it.
- Problem: Ground/referent plus light exposition. "Wouldn't fit" has no object (the gun? the ride?). "It" shifts from the old emitter to the gun within one sentence. The answer never says what the chrome rings do, which is what he asked. The "How much can it lift?" run that follows is a spec sheet that sets limits for the reader, the only stretch where quiet Celestine lectures. At L295, "beneath it" is also clipped. This is Minor because the limits pay off later: the vase, the ride staying on the floor.
- Fix: Give "wouldn't fit" an object and have her say in one clause what the rings are for. Drop or shrink the field-and-pulses sentence. Keep "Three kilos on one disc", the kitchen range, and "We stay on the floor". "Less if I want it moving for long" is never used and can go. At L295, name the surface ("something to land on"). Illustrative only: "The old emitter was the size of a toaster. The rings do its job in little pulses."

### D5 · Minor · She borrows his "may", and the exchanges close on tidy rejoinders

- Where: "may" at L213, L281, L285. Neat reframing rejoinders at L55, L95, L175, L209, L225, L253, L281, L289, L357
- Text:
> “May we go again?”
> “You may. I'd like a glass.”
- Problem: Identical Voices, mildly. The mock-formal "may" is the husband's nervous register (L213), but Celestine uses it twice ("You may", "May I put these in?"), which blurs one of the clearest differences between them. Separately, many exchanges close on a short line followed by a tidy reframe ("There were." / "I know." / "There was room."). Most of these are earned and among the best moments, but the density makes the talk feel composed.
- Fix: Give the "may" register back to him and let her answer in her own plainer words. D1 and D2 already remove the two weakest rejoinders (L281, L289); after those fixes, don't add new neat comebacks in their place. Let at least one of the remaining exchange endings be laughter, a touch, or a plain yes. Keep his mock-formal quips at L191 and L331: the narration establishes them as his.

## Prose findings

### P1 · Minor · Mixed US and UK spelling

- Where: L219 and L237 against L153 and L397, the planet regions and the final moon
- Text:
> divided into eight irregular colored regions
> travelling across the dark glass
- Problem: Copyedit inconsistency. "colored" and "color" (US) sit beside "TRAVELLERS" and "travelling" (UK), in a text whose vocabulary is otherwise mostly British ("skirting board", "sitting room", "tram", "fizzy orange", "kilos").
- Fix: Pick one convention and apply it throughout. Given the vocabulary, "coloured" and "colour" are the smallest change.

### P2 · Minor · Final paragraph repeats a finished touch

- Where: L393 and L397, the third ride and the last paragraph
- Text:
> “Later.” She took my hand and drew it a little farther around her.
> She shifted her hand into mine and settled still closer as the ride turned us toward the window.
- Problem: Staging. Her hand is already in his from L393, so "shifted her hand into mine" in the last paragraph repeats a completed action and blurs the closing image.
- Fix: Make the second touch different (fingers lacing, her hand moving to his chest) or cut it. Keep the moon coming into view, the half-seen painted face, and the kiss as the last beat.

## Continuity and prompt findings

### C1 · Minor · The tram-stop kiss sits oddly with her fear

- Where: L347–L349 and L365–L369, the first-date memory after the second ride
- Text:
> “I got tired of waiting.”
> “You were already saying goodnight. I was afraid you'd go home and think I hadn't enjoyed it.”
> I remembered that too: her holding the tram door with one elbow to ask whether I was free the next evening
- Problem: Internal logic. As written, she kissed him at the tram stop, then boarded, then asked him out from the door because she feared he'd think she hadn't enjoyed the evening. Right after she had kissed him, that fear is hard to believe. The story also never says whether the kiss happened that night or on a later date.
- Fix: Either make clear that the kiss belongs to a later evening, or change her fear to something a kiss wouldn't settle. Illustrative only: "I was afraid you were too polite to ask."
