# The Bait Remembers — provisional direction

First person, close to a single afternoon. The narrator has been placed in a busy post office because psychics searching strangers' thoughts will mistake them for one more person in line. Their handlers have built a snare around the narrator's real traumatic memory. At one particular remembered cue, they trained the narrator to press a thumbnail into their palm and recite an assigned number, cutting their attention away from the memory; a reader who followed them in is left circling that severed moment. Before the conditioning, the memory sometimes ran to its painful end and returned the narrator to live senses. The injury predates the weapon; the handlers' achievement was learning how to use it.

The narrator has brought an unstamped birthday card for a sister they have been kept from contacting. Buying a stamp and mailing it is a small, unauthorized purpose of their own, worth pursuing even if no psychic comes. A psychic catches a genuine, unbidden thought of distress from the narrator, then deliberately sweeps the line to find its source. The impulse to help is real; so is the intrusion into strangers' private thoughts. The psychic reaches the narrator and the trap closes. The handler, close in the queue, needs the reader held still long enough to pick them out.

The narrator may be able to refuse the palm-and-number response and let the memory continue past the cut, giving the psychic the older route through its actual aftermath and back to the post office. This would mean enduring that continuation, not conquering the trauma; the handler can see the trained response fail and will act. The card is not a key to the trap. Its practical fate should follow from the same confrontation: can the narrator still reach the counter and mail it, or does helping the stranger cost them this contact with their sister? The prose should discover the consequence and exact final action.
