# The Bait Remembers — provisional direction

First person, close to a single afternoon. The narrator has been placed in a busy post office because psychics searching strangers' thoughts will mistake them for one more person in line. Their handlers have built a snare around the narrator's real traumatic memory: a reader who enters it becomes caught in its repetitions long enough to be identified and taken. Each activation leaves the narrator in that memory too. The injury predates the weapon; the handlers' achievement was learning how to use it.

The narrator has brought a birthday card for a sister they have been kept from contacting. Mailing it is a small, unauthorized purpose of their own, worth pursuing even if no psychic comes. While they inch toward the counter, a psychic sweeps the line in search of someone who may be in immediate danger. The search may be understandable, but it takes private thoughts without asking. The psychic reaches the narrator and the trap closes. In the overlap, the narrator can feel the stranger rifling through both the terrible memory and the ordinary, unsent words in the card. The handler is moving in.

The narrator may be able to lead the psychic back toward the present by resisting the trained habit of leaving their own mind when the memory begins. Doing so means remaining with what happened, while the handler can see their disobedience. The possible ending is a narrow act of control rather than a cure or a clean defeat of the operation: the narrator decides whether to help this particular trespasser out, and what to do with the card once the trap has failed or fired. The prose should discover the cost and the exact final action.
