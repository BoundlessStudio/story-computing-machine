# Outline

## Story

- Premise and central promise: An apprentice repairs an inherited traveling door before appraisal. Four siblings expect their mother's house, using that route to validate repair and ownership. Instead, each opens and crosses to a different present home. Prove all four routes mechanically valid and turn the diagnosis into an estate decision.
- Focal pressure or attachment: The apprentice needs a first independently certified repair to pass inspection but shares the family's assumption that one lock has one correct set. The siblings must clear inherited goods before the house sale and want the destination to settle ownership.
- Counterforce or complication: The late owner's repeated route left one deep wear position, mistaken for a permanent address. Deadline, embarrassment, interruptions, competing hands, and attempts to aim for an approved result corrupt testing without making anyone dishonest.
- POV, distance, and information limit: Close third-person past through the apprentice, attending to hands, resistance, filings, sounds, and witness marks before emotion. They can prove engagement and interference, but cannot feel recognition, interpret a destination's meaning, or know family history beyond partial remarks and routines.
- Governing movement and time shape: One workshop afternoon: repair, four identical trials with accumulating contradiction, controlled failures, reopened mechanism, diagnosis, and immediate estate use before collection. Keep the turn at the bench, ending after revised sale instructions, routed boxes, and an ordinary transport pin.
- Speculative rule or ordinary-world constraint: When the latch clears its keeper, a floating home cam samples the primary living user holding bare brass and intending to cross. It follows that person's currently active, practiced recognition of a dwelling as a place of return—not love, virtue, title, childhood, or spoken claim—and uses a familiar usable threshold within it. Multiple homes are possible; the active complete recognition governs. A flush witness pin, full bolt throw, and square contact band mark a valid set. Competing hands or unsettled attention make the cam hunt and the pin remain proud; gloves, no stable recognition, or an unsafe receiving threshold block travel. Closing resets the route, and no remote return exists afterward. The inert door retains wear, not its dead user's identity.

## Voice

- Narrative texture: Tactile close third moves from resistance to comparison, measurement, and revised inference. Let wear and handling habits carry family history; delay interpretation until the mechanism supports it.
- Conversational texture: Workshop talk mixes test instructions, estate logistics, tool requests, private corrections, and disputes about touch. Let threshold action and pauses replace destination speeches.
- Rhetorical ownership: The apprentice owns material questions and conditional claims; the executor deadlines, shares, and documents; the brother routes, loads, and maintenance; the sister household sequences and obligations; the youngest sparse access and tenancy corrections.
- Pressure behavior: The apprentice becomes exact rather than reassuring. The executor repeats schedules and stops listening; the brother answers amid practical distractions; the sister redirects abstraction to tasks; the scrutinized youngest loses fluency, then acts cleanly under one instruction.
- Anti-default: Avoid four polished testimonies and an apprentice's lesson about plural homes. Differentiate through procedure, unequal disclosure, threshold action, and the estate choice enabled by evidence.

## Beats

1. Four siblings bring the removed door in a test frame. Their inherited house closes next day, an appraiser collects the door that afternoon, and boxes remain unsent. Their late mother always operated it, so they expect her house and mean to assign the door with that property.
2. The apprentice finds a snapped return spring, burr on the floating fence, and excess spindle play. Preserve the circular wear track, replace and tension the spring, stone the burr, shim the spindle, and verify deadbolt and hold-open brace. An inert jig proves movement, not routing.
3. Set one protocol: full reset, one bare-handed user, no named target, steady pressure, and a marked test weight carried across and back. The executor opens to the nearly sold house's pantry and retrieves an appraisal folder. A flush pin and gauged bolt throw appear to confirm the family assumption.
4. The brother follows the protocol but reaches his cramped cabin on a dry-docked canal barge. He crosses for a needed work item and returns before closure. The family calls the repair unstable; the apprentice records another full pin drop, equal throw, and clean reset.
5. The third sibling reaches her apartment kitchen; a household interruption cuts across questions while she crosses. The youngest reaches a rented print-shop loft after one terse access correction. Distinct geometry, sound, light, and use establish real destinations; both yield full sets.
6. Naming the inherited house, rehearsing childhood details, and adding a second hand produce a chattering cam, proud pin, and blocked pawl. The apprentice stops the argument, applies marking blue, and repeats neutral checks with each user controlling the latch alone.
7. Inside the case, four fresh arcs end at different angles but meet the fence squarely; none bears a false set's edge smear. The mother's deep track records repetition, not a master notch. The apprentice confines the conclusion to evidence: four sound sets, with no mechanical ranking or property verdict.
8. The siblings keep equal estate shares, confirm the house sale, list the removable door separately as functional, and route each allotted box to its owner's home. The apprentice records one-user, hold-open, blocked-threshold, and return-risk rules, then seats the transport pin for collection. No route receives priority or repairs the family.
