# Opus review: The Body Called One

Independent audit for a later editing pass. It is not a `review.md` verdict,
does not change canon state, and does not authorize edits by itself.

## Verdict

- Dialogue: REVISE — Mursa's decisive reversal turns on a twitch she cannot see, and the identity argument moves by swapping nouns in one clipped register both speakers share.
- Prose: PASS — concrete, embodied and well paced; one Minor staging problem at the blink clue.
- Continuity and prompt: REVISE — the prompt's promise is fully delivered, but the shaft-junction choice does not follow from the stated six-degree bias, and there are three small mechanical slips.
- Edit scope: targeted-dialogue
- Existing review.md said: Verdict PASS; "Dialogue: PASS", crediting broken audio, glyphs and practical exchanges with keeping Loxan and Mursa distinct; I agree about the medium but disagree about the identity and vault exchanges.

The dialogue is strongest where the medium does the work: a bone link that drops out, a molar tap for yes, Mursa's glyph arrows and paper sums, and silence as uptake ("did not answer for eleven rungs"). The operational exchanges on the approach, the descent and the escape are clean and should be left alone. The trouble is in the exchanges about what Unit 01 is. That argument moves by relabeling ("Unit 02 carried"/"I carried", "Body."/"Not body. Not yet.", "The seed is her body."/"It's a biometric response set.") and by one-word scorekeeping. Loxan's voice never changes, so the cradle scene sounds like the approach checklist, and Mursa's turn away from the seed has no cause she could perceive. The edit pass should keep the brevity. Each speaker should argue from what they own: Loxan from bodily memory, Mursa from her theory and her numbers. Loxan's speech should change in front of the cradle, and Mursa's reversal needs a visible cause.

## Dialogue findings

### D1 · Blocking · Mursa reverses on a twitch she cannot see

- Where: L257–L269, preservation vault, the seed argument
- Text:
> “Run one challenge and I can trace—”
> The woman's hand twitched again. No tone this time. Perhaps the muscles were settling. Perhaps not.
> Mursa stopped. “Don't run it.”
- Problem: Ground/knowledge and uptake. Mursa is remote and has no view of the cradle. At L239 she has to ask "What happened?", and at L243 "Response quality?". Yet she breaks off and reverses the instant the hand twitches, and Loxan says nothing. Readers will take the twitch as the cause, but Mursa cannot perceive it. The other possible reading, that she stops because she hears what she is asking for, is not on the page either. This is the story's decisive turn: Mursa gives up the seed she has argued for since L41, and on the page nothing she could perceive causes it.
- Fix: Keep the reversal and give Mursa a cause she can perceive. There are two options. Loxan can report the twitch in plain words, which also gives her a line outside the telemetry register. Or Mursa's challenge-response table, which she is reading at L257, can log a response with no challenge attached, and the narration can show her seeing it. Keep "Run one challenge and I can trace—" breaking off, and keep "Don't run it." Illustrative only: “She moved again. Nothing's testing her.”

### D2 · Major · The identity argument advances by relabeling

- Where: L117–L121 (horizon bias), L149–L157 (maintenance-room matches), L257–L263 (the seed)
- Text:
> “Six-degree correction,” Mursa continued. “Same bias Unit 02 carried.”
> “I carried,” she said.
> “Not body. Not yet.”
> “Four,” she said.
> “The seed is her body.”
> “It's a biometric response set.”
- Problem: Correction ping-pong and Tennis Match. Each step of the central disagreement (was Unit 01 a machine or a body?) is a swap of nouns: Unit 02 becomes I, Body becomes Not body, the seed becomes a biometric response set. There is also a spoken scoreboard ("Three matches" answered by "Four"). Both speakers use the same clipped declaratives, so the argument reads as a word game, not as two people with different evidence and stakes. Loxan has bodily memory: the salt-planet spin at L147 and the shoulder that catches at L155. Mursa has a real alternative theory (pilot capture, L113) and a real stake (a seed for the next pilot). Neither brings any of this into the exchange; the labels stand in for it. "I carried" works once: it is short, it silences Mursa, and it is Loxan's first claim on the bias. The repeats wear it out.
- Fix: Keep "I carried" and Mursa's silence after it. At L149–L157, replace "Body." / "Not body. Not yet." / "Four," with the evidence each speaker owns. Loxan says what she once did with that sequence (the narration at L147 already has it). Mursa answers with her pilot-capture theory in her own terms, so they disagree about what the evidence means. Let the shoulder catch and the plot line stopping (L155) land as action, without a spoken score. At L259–L261, cut the relabel pair so that Mursa's "coupled to the challenge-response table" line leads straight into "Taken how?", which is the best line in the scene. If Mursa's framing is kept, give Loxan a question for it to answer, not a thesis. Illustrative only: “I flew that. Flat spin over the salt planet, left side dead.”

### D3 · Major · The vault test exchange stays in telemetry register

- Where: L239–L251, preservation vault, right after the first challenge
- Text:
> “What happened?” Mursa asked.
> “Response quality?”
> Loxan watched the fingers loosen. “She moved.”
> “Voluntary?”
> “No data.”
- Problem: Tennis Match and uniform fluency under pressure. Loxan has just watched her first body flinch at a test her own palm started. The exchange runs six one-liners of equal weight in strict alternation. It has one real voice difference: Loxan says "her" and "she", Mursa says "response quality" and "voluntary". "No data." erases it by putting Loxan in Mursa's register and closing the exchange on a clipped last word. Loxan here sounds exactly as she did on the approach ("Clear." "Balance test."), so the scene's pressure never reaches her voice; all of it is in the narration ("jerked her hand away").
- Fix: Keep Mursa's clinical questions, which set up her silence and her later reversal, and keep Loxan's pronouns. Replace "No data." with an answer that comes from what Loxan saw and knows from inside, such as the right eye or the six-degree lag. It must stay honest that she cannot tell whether the movement was voluntary. Break the strict alternation: let one of Mursa's questions go unanswered for a beat, or let Loxan answer something Mursa did not ask. Keep "Mursa did not offer a theory." Illustrative only: “Her right eye flinched. Same side as mine.”

### D4 · Major · Mursa re-explains the reveal the display just made

- Where: L221–L229, preservation vault, immediately after the cradle display
- Text:
> BIOLOGICAL ROOT: LOXAN VYTHE.
> “I have the production table. One and two aren't vehicle numbers. They're embodiment sequence.”
- Problem: Philosopher and explanatory redundancy at the prompt's key moment. The display has already made the reveal, and the narration has registered it ("By then the name did not surprise her."). Mursa's line restates the reveal as a tidy "aren't X. They're Y." reversal, so the story's thesis ends up in her mouth. The only new work the line does is Mursa's concession after "Not body. Not yet.", and the explanation buries it. The grammar ("They're embodiment sequence") also reads as a slip, not as jargon.
- Fix: Keep "I have the production table." Make the rest a concession in Mursa's own idiom that adds something the display did not. Alternatively, cut the rest and let her silence and her next line ("What happened?") carry the concession. Do not restate the vehicle/embodiment distinction. Illustrative only: “I have the production table. I had it filed under hardware.”

### D5 · Minor · Decision readback blurs who is speaking

- Where: L279–L289, preservation vault, choosing the index
- Text:
> “No full table. No seed.”
> “We leave with addresses and no key.”
- Problem: Attribution and identical voices. The untagged readback after "“Confirm,” Mursa said" sounds like Mursa reading back the selection, even though the paragraphing makes it Loxan's. The next line could belong to either speaker. The fragment pair repeats the clipped listing habit. Otherwise the exchange works: Mursa has already given up the seed at L269, and "Index first. Release on my mark." shows her committing to the body under fire.
- Fix: Tag both untagged lines or give them action beats. Let Loxan's confirmation be an action or a single word. Make Mursa's cost line unmistakably hers, ideally tied to her paper or her numbers. Keep "Two minutes." and "Index first. Release on my mark."

### D6 · Minor · The opening self-correction answers a confusion no one voiced

- Where: L17–L25, stolen courier, the opening exchange
- Text:
> “That was yours,” Mursa said through the bone link.
> “I mean the bank,” Mursa added. “Courier controls show no fault.”
> “Then I can stop checking the courier and return to checking the retrieval craft.
- Problem: Ground/referent and correction rhythm. The bank has just happened, so "That was yours" has a clear referent. But an unexplained green bracket then closes around the mast, and Mursa clarifies a misreading that Loxan never expressed. The story's first exchange is therefore a line correcting itself. Mursa's follow-up is a long, over-complete procedural sentence that sets a legalistic register from the start.
- Fix: Either fold the bank point into a single line, or make Loxan's acknowledgment visibly misfire so the clarification answers something. Say who drew the bracket, the courier or Mursa. Loosen the L25 sentence into what Mursa would say while working, and keep the seven-hour figure.

### D7 · Minor · Untagged pack readout

- Where: L75–L81, docking lock
- Text:
> Loxan showed the status icon to the link.
> “Eight fifty-six. It is counting worse.”
- Problem: Attribution. Loxan shows the icon so that Mursa can read it, which makes the readout plausibly Mursa's, while its echo of "count honestly" (L27) suggests Loxan. "Noted." belongs to whoever did not read it out, so the reader has to guess both lines. "It is counting worse", without a contraction, is stiffer than either speaker is elsewhere.
- Fix: Tag the readout. The most natural version has Loxan reading it and Mursa answering "Noted."

### D8 · Minor · "That's why we're here" tells both speakers what they already know

- Where: L57–L61, debris approach after the guidance-light turn
- Text:
> “Inherited calibration,” Mursa said. “That's why we're here.”
- Problem: As-you-know tail. "Inherited calibration" is good: it is Mursa's machine reading of Loxan's flinch, which the story later overturns. "That's why we're here" restates a mission both of them already know.
- Fix: Keep "Inherited calibration." Cut the tail, or turn it into Mursa's practical next step, such as marking the route or logging the latency.

## Prose findings

### P1 · Minor · The blink clue is staged ambiguously

- Where: L99–L107, first contact with the annex
- Text:
> For that fraction of darkness, the diagram's horizon disappeared too.
> Loxan blinked again. The recorded field pinched downward beneath a fringe of black curves.
- Problem: "too" reads as her own eyelids hiding the overlay, which is no clue at all. The second sentence makes it look as if the archive mirrors her blink. The reader has to work out the intended fact (the recording contains a blink) backwards from Loxan's line at L107.
- Fix: Stage it so the recorded field blinks on its own while Loxan's eyes are open, or out of step with her own blink. Keep the lashes image and the L107 line.

## Continuity and prompt findings

### C1 · Major · The junction choice does not follow from a six-degree bias

- Where: L181–L187, ventilation shaft junction
- Text:
> Harrowstar drew horizontal reference bars across both branches; the bar on the left sat just above Mursa's arrow.
> Mursa had mapped the instruction for a level maintenance route onto the archive's uncorrected frame.
- Problem: Causality and staging at a key beat. A six-degree roll bias cannot turn a right-hand branch into a left-hand one. It could only matter if the branches differ in slope and Mursa was choosing "the level one". The geometry of the bar sitting above the arrow cannot be pictured. This is the one moment where Loxan's bodily knowledge overrules Mursa's data and earns her "You were right," so the reader has to be able to follow it.
- Fix: Make the branches differ by slope, one level and one slightly climbing or dropping. Have Mursa's instruction be explicitly "take the level branch", and let the archive's tilted bars make the wrong branch look level. Loxan knows the tilt from inside and takes the other one. Cut the bar-above-arrow detail. Keep "Loxan went left." and Mursa's "You were right," and check that "Found the construction plane" still names what Mursa found.

### C2 · Minor · The muscle-plot colour changes

- Where: L145, L155, L301
- Text:

> The plot's yellow line stopped at the same angle.
> the left shoulder stop where the archive's orange muscle line had stopped
- Problem: The shoulder trace is orange, then yellow, then orange again. The yellow may be meant to echo the yellow left extension at L99, but it reads as a slip.
- Fix: Choose one colour for the left-shoulder trace and use it at all three points.

### C3 · Minor · The second grip is an unexplained inference

- Where: L301–L303, preservation vault, the manual release
- Text:
> The lever wanted the range that first body had never possessed.
> So the maintenance crews had built a second grip lower down.
- Problem: "So" makes the lower grip a consequence of the first body's limited shoulder. That only follows if the first body once had to work this lever, and nothing says it did.
- Fix: Drop "So" and have Loxan simply find the lower grip, or give the grip a reason she can see, such as worn paint at the lower height. Keep the shoulder stopping at the archive's line.

### C4 · Minor · "Three options" are followed by a fourth

- Where: L275–L279, preservation vault, the extraction choices
- Text:
> Three options lit on the cradle controls.
> The record index—the locations of other calibration vaults, without their keys—needed thirty-eight seconds.
- Problem: Three options are listed, and then Loxan selects a fourth that was not among them. The reader cannot tell whether the index is one of the lit options.
- Fix: Make it four options, or say that the index is a separate control. Keep all the timings.
