# Opus review: A Crown in the Bargain

Independent audit for a later editing pass. It is not a `review.md` verdict,
does not change canon state, and does not authorize edits by itself.

## Verdict

- Dialogue: REVISE — one exchange is incoherent and unattributed, the two-forms knowledge chain is broken, and a steady cross-examination-and-aphorism register makes Tareya sound nothing like her prompt line.
- Prose: REVISE — the narration keeps explaining the subtext and states the "records placement, not intent" theme three times.
- Continuity and prompt: REVISE — the prompt is met (verbatim line, roles, resolution, length), but the rooftop staging contradicts itself at the climax.
- Edit scope: heavy-dialogue
- Existing review.md said: Verdict PASS, "Dialogue: PASS"; disagree. It missed the incoherent bridge exchange, the aphorism trading the story's own outline ruled out, and the contradictions in the rooftop staging.

The couple spend their wedding night in something like a deposition. After the prompt's wry, teasing line, Tareya speaks almost entirely in one-line questions, one-word echoes, orders and folk aphorisms, and Osvan answers with terms, counts and "the law calls it" corrections. Readers tell the two apart by role, asker or answerer, more than by voice. The three long question-and-answer runs repeat one another, while the real haggling is summarized in narration. Beneath this sit real Ground failures: a bridge quip nobody can parse, a reveal whose key terms ("stronger", "parallel") arrive early or with no setup, and a muddled first answer to the prompt's own question. The strengths are real too: the attendant and the lamp tender sound like themselves, Tareya's interruptions build to the decisive "Osvan." beat, and "I would like the chance to learn which door you use" is the right size for the moment. The edit pass should fix the Ground problems, give Tareya back the prompt's irony and some unevenness, cut the aphorism rebuttals and the repeated checklist, and let one piece of bargaining happen aloud. Apart from staging fixes, it should leave the decisive and final exchanges alone.

## Dialogue findings

### D1 · Blocking · Bridge minutes quip is incoherent and unattributed

- Where: L221–L227, lantern bridge, just after the third grate
- Text:
> "Still forty-two minutes?" she called.
> "That isn't how minutes work," the attendant muttered.
> "We spent eight of them."
- Problem: Ground/uptake, Punchline Patch and Attribution. Tareya heard "forty-two minutes" and has since dressed and crossed half the bridge, so "Still forty-two?" is not a real question; it only sets up the attendant's quip. The quip then fits nothing. It arrives after the chamberlain has already said "Thirty-four," and if it disputes that figure the words are wrong, because counting down is exactly how minutes work. The last line has no tag and no recoverable speaker (Tareya, Osvan and the chamberlain are all possible), and all it does is restate the arithmetic.
- Fix: Have Tareya ask plainly (Illustrative only: "How long?") and keep the chamberlain's "Thirty-four." Cut L225–L227. If the attendant keeps a line here, give it something real to attend to (the wet articles, Tareya's footing), tag it, and let nobody correct it.

### D2 · Major · Tareya drops the prompt's voice and becomes a cross-examiner

- Where: from L29 on; clearest at L29–L33, L43, L53–L65, L123 (the first reply after the prompt line, and the chamber questioning)
- Text:
> Clearly, you did not put a ring on me because you were smitten with me.
> "That is a schedule."
> "I asked for your plan."
> "Who decides whether a washed bridge is urgent?"
- Problem: Identical Voices, house-voice convergence. The required prompt line gives the Baroness a wry, conversational, teasing register ("So...", "mind telling me", "husband?", "smitten"). Her very next line is a terse legal correction, and she never sounds like the prompt again. Nearly everything she says afterwards is a one-line question, a one-word echo ("Beneath." L43, "In Lumespire." L65, "My reserve." L123), an order or an aphorism. She uses no contractions, never teases, never hesitates, and wins every exchange. That puts her in Osvan's precise, procedural register, so in the chamber the reader can tell them apart only by who asks and who answers. The prompt line reads as if a different woman had said it.
- Fix: Do a voice pass on Tareya's lines only, keeping their content (roads, reserve, keys, rooms). Carry the prompt's irony into her first few replies, so that she needles and teases where she now corrects (Illustrative only: "You're reading me the bell schedule. On our wedding night."). Let some lines run longer and looser when she is angry, and let her be wrong-footed once, for example after "unlikely to ask me to love you" or after "which door you use", instead of always having the next line ready. Keep her concrete attention to land and access. Drop most of the one-word echoes. Leave her formal only where she chooses formality, as with the chamberlain.

### D3 · Major · Correction-and-aphorism pairs close exchanges

- Where: L43–L47 (the fox "beneath" the crown); L79–L83 (the seventh sheet, before the knock); L267–L271 (bridge, the rooms)
- Text:
> "In sequence, not in rank. The law calls the relation supportive."
> "My stewards call a road supportive when it remains under the cart. Who holds the road?"
> "The law calls it no-fault dissolution until dawn."
> "People have mouths even where law has definitions."
> Tomorrow comes whether we file or not.
- Problem: Punchline Patch and correction ping-pong. This is the collection's house tic, and the story's own outline ruled it out ("Avoid ... aphorism trading"). The same template runs twice: Osvan corrects Tareya's word with what "the law calls" it, and she answers with a folk aphorism that ends the exchange. At L269–L271 she answers his figurative "tomorrow" literally before she gets to the real point. Nobody replies to any of these rebuttals, so the exchanges become point-scoring instead of pressure. "Who holds the road?" also only makes sense through the metaphor built one sentence earlier.
- Fix: At L79–L83, cut L81 and L83 and let the knock land on Tareya's L79 line, which already works. At L43–L47, have Osvan hedge or fumble instead of correcting her (L49 says his faltering starts here). Replace the stewards' aphorism with a direct question about who controls what in the March; the next answer, about countersigned appointments, can stay. At L271, cut the first sentence and keep "I need to know who chooses the keys." Allow Tareya one earned sharp line: keep L141 ("grateful for neglect").

### D4 · Major · Deposition runs repeat themselves and hide the real bargaining

- Where: L51–L67 (chamber); L293–L309 (strong-form demonstration); L359–L381 (parallel form), with L385 summarizing the haggling in narration
- Text:
> "Who releases the storm reserve?"
> "The crown treasury, after review."
> "Reserve?"
> "Crown review."
> Osvan objected to five years of toll exemption; she reduced it to two and required crown wagons to carry March salt on the return journey.
- Problem: Tennis Match and negotiation routine. There are three long runs of equal-weight one-liners, question then answer, with no interruption, overlap or change of pace. The rooftop run (L293–L307) asks again what L51–L67 already settled (appointments countersigned, reserve under crown review), while the array is already showing the fox pass under the hawk. Only "My assent?", "Legible separately?" and his silence are new. Meanwhile the one place where the two could argue, trade, and get petty or funny (tolls, carts on village greens, salt wagons) is summarized in narration at L385. L49 also says Osvan has lost his fluency ("as though an answer might have crawled under them"), but his next eight answers are crisp.
- Fix: At L51–L67, keep the content but break the metronome. Let Tareya stack two questions before he answers, or let him give one long, badly organized answer that she cuts off, so the reader sees the faltering L49 promises. At L293–L309, cut the repeated appointments, reserve and customs lines. Keep "My assent?", his answer, a plainer version of "Legible separately?" and the silence. At L359–L381, keep only the new lines: her wardens direct the crews, who pays, and the debt clause. Then move one haggle out of L385 into speech (the toll exemption is the best candidate), so their first bargain as partners can be heard as uneven give-and-take instead of rulings.

### D5 · Major · The two-forms information arrives out of order

- Where: L41 (chamber, before the seventh sheet), L73, L169 (the chamberlain at the door)
- Text:
> "The stronger filing registers it beneath the royal crown."
> "I prepared both forms."
> "And the parallel form," Tareya said. "Bring it."
- Problem: Ground/knowledge and Ground/referent. At L41 Osvan says "the stronger filing" before Tareya finds the seventh sheet, which gives away the reveal at L69–L73. Tareya seizes on "Beneath." at once but lets "stronger" pass, which a negotiator promised "protection without absorption" would not do. At L73 "both forms" points to a second form the reader has never been told about. At L169 Tareya asks for "the parallel form", a term nobody has used, and the reader has to work out backwards that it means the six-sheet arrangement she negotiated.
- Fix: At L41, drop "stronger" (Illustrative only: "The filing registers it beneath the royal crown."). At L73, have Osvan name the other form so that Tareya's "parallel" at L169 and L179 has something to point back to, for example by saying he had a parallel form drawn as well. Keep "You gave me one." and the chamberlain's knowing reply at L173.

### D6 · Major · Osvan's answer to the prompt's question is muddled and contradicts his later account

- Where: L35, chamber; compare L133, L137, L181
- Text:
> Your herald would place the Solvane vote outside the regency council's bloc.
> The general's proposed match then lacks the houses required to compel a second betrothal hearing.
> If I refuse without another registered marriage, they can call a fitness hearing.
- Problem: Ground/referent at the very beat that answers the prompt. The Solvane vote is already outside the bloc (L137: "your vote has never sat in a council bloc"), so "place ... outside the bloc" describes no change. The actual mechanism, given at L133, is that her herald becomes his eighth. The threat has three names: "second betrothal hearing" (L35), "fitness hearing" (L133) and "accession hearing" (L181, L337, L381). The reader cannot tell whether these are one hearing or three.
- Fix: Keep L35 short, since L133 is the full account after "Say all of it", but have it describe the same mechanism: with her herald counted beside his, the council's match for him loses its point. Use "fitness hearing" at both L35 and L133. Keep "accession hearing" only if one clause makes clear it is the later hearing on his claim. Osvan can stay procedural here, since that is how he evades; he just has to make sense.

### D7 · Major · Bridge relay exchange states the theme and changes nothing

- Where: L233–L251, middle landing, at the dead relay pane
- Text:
> "So the light is evidence of placement. Not the placement itself."
> "And no light means nothing except that no light arrived."
> The answer came easily because this was machinery, not marriage.
- Problem: The Philosopher, plus an explanatory chorus. Tareya asks so that Osvan can explain, then turns the explanation into two parallel aphorisms for the reader, and Osvan is left saying "Correct." twice. Nothing changes between them, and the point is never used: when she takes off the ring at L313, nothing connects that act to this lesson. The narration then makes the same point twice more (L333, L421; see P1), and L251 spells out the contrast the scene has just shown.
- Fix: Either cut L243–L249 and the first two sentences of L251, keeping the dead pane, the tender's wick and "The clerk reads the signed vellum"; or make Tareya's question personal and practical, so that what she learns serves her later choice and settles C2 (Illustrative only: "If my light goes dark before the roof, am I still your wife?"). Let Osvan answer in his own procedural way, and don't let either of them sum up.

### D8 · Minor · "Separate households" collides with the filing vocabulary

- Where: L143, chamber, Osvan's defense after "grateful for neglect"
- Text:
> "I thought separate households would be a courtesy."
- Problem: Ground/referent. "Household" is a legal term in this story: the strong form is a "unified household" filing (L181, L307) and the outcome is a "Parallel household" (L417). Osvan means living apart, but the line reads as if he had meant to offer the parallel arrangement, which the seventh sheet contradicts.
- Fix: Make the wording domestic (rooms, apartments, leaving her alone), not legal.

### D9 · Minor · Garbled debt clause

- Where: L381, rooftop, the answer to "Who pays?"
- Text:
> the debt remains with the royal treasury, not my successor's right to your title.
- Problem: A debt cannot "remain with" a right, so the one clause Tareya orders written ("Write that last part.") never says what it protects. L391 shows that the written clause is about the debt not *attaching*.
- Fix: Say it plainly in a way that sets up her edit at L391: the debt is owed to the treasury and may not attach to her title, whoever follows him.

### D10 · Minor · "Bridge clothes" is ambiguous

- Where: L105, chamber, after the weather slip
- Text:
> "Set my bridge clothes out," Tareya said.
- Problem: Ground/referent. The line follows a slip about Solvane's roads and the washed-out bridge worry, and the lantern bridge is not introduced until L199. A reader can take it to mean Tareya is getting ready to ride home to her own bridges.
- Fix: Name the rain gear, or say "the upper walk" to echo what the attendant has just said.

## Prose findings

### P1 · Major · Narration explains the subtext and restates the theme

- Where: L37, L139, L177, L251, L275, L333, L421
- Text:
> Not a lover's answer, but at least it belonged to the room.
> It was not quite cruel. That made it worse.
> She had begun the night with broad questions and received broad answers. The rain had narrowed both.
> She noticed and did not reward him for it.
> It offered no opinion on whether he was brave, desperate, honest, or merely out of alternatives.
> The array had done its work and no more.
- Problem: After many key lines the narrator tells the reader how to take them, or repeats the thesis that the array records placement, not intent. That thesis appears in dialogue at L243–L247, again at L333, and again at L421, in the story's closing narration. L177 also misdescribes the scene: from L53 on, Tareya's questions and Osvan's answers were narrow, not broad, and "The rain had narrowed both" is an epigram placed in the narration.
- Fix: Cut the glossing sentences or reduce each to its concrete observation. State the array's limit once at most (the first two sentences of L333 are enough), and let the ending rest on the ring back on her finger and the lights crossing. Cut the last two sentences of L177.

### P2 · Minor · "Not X, but Y" reversals

- Where: L37, L139, L219, L251, L273, L413
- Text:
> Osvan took the outside rail, not gallantly but because he was taller
> Not laughter, though it had passed near it.
> She did not place it beside his.
- Problem: The narration uses the negate-then-assert template often enough that it becomes a rhythm. Most instances are also glosses (P1).
- Fix: Keep one or two where the negation carries information (L413 does, because it marks the parallel placement), and state the others positively or cut them.

### P3 · Minor · Water-infrastructure texture

- Where: L91 (a gutter backing into the wardrobe); L99, L385 and L409 (the farm culvert)
- Text:
> "The west gutter has backed into the lower wardrobe," she said.
> "Northern farm culvert gone.
> Tareya added culvert stone, bridge chain, and seed grain to the list.
- Problem: A policy point for authorized revisions (style guide, "No default water infrastructure"). Drainage works appear as incidental texture, and the prompt did not ask for them. The storm, the rain, the roads and the washed bridges are fine.
- Fix: Give the wet boots another cause, such as a leaking window. Replace the culvert with road or bridge damage (a farm road washed out, a farm bridge down), consistently at L99, L385 and L409.

### P4 · Minor · Small wording slips

- Where: L85 (the attendant's knock), L89 (her entrance)
- Text:
> A soft knock interrupted him before he could make a count out of that too.
> the cuffs of her blue gown were wet to the elbows
- Problem: Osvan is not speaking at L85, so there is nothing to interrupt, and "make a count out of that" is unclear: a legal count, or one of his lists? At L89, cuffs sit at the wrist and cannot be wet to the elbows.
- Fix: Change L85 to "before he could answer" (or adjust it to fit the D3 cut), and "cuffs" to "sleeves" at L89.

## Continuity and prompt findings

### C1 · Major · Rooftop staging contradicts itself at the climax

- Where: L283–L291, L331, L355–L357, L373, L405–L413, the rooftop succession room
- Text:
> Then he fitted his signet into the northern cup and nodded toward her hand.
> He placed his own ring in the northern cup.
> Inside lay two forms and the royal emergency seal.
> Osvan shifted the demonstration blanks into two cups opposite each other.
> She slid her ring onto her finger.
> She set it into the western cup assigned by the parallel form.
- Problem: Four staging errors. (1) At L287 Osvan puts his real signet into the northern cup. He then fits two blanks "into the cups" without taking it out, and at L331 he "placed his own ring in the northern cup" as if for the first time. Under the logic of L333–L335 a real placement registers and is witnessed, so L287 would already have filed him. (2) After the real hawk has "filled the floor alone" (L333), the blanks go into "two cups opposite each other" (L357) and a second hawk appears. North (his cup) and west (her cup, L413) are not opposite, and the blanks are never removed before Tareya places her ring. (3) The case holds only "two forms and the royal emergency seal" (L283), and Osvan's list at L189 implies that no compact exists yet. Yet at L355 the chamberlain opens "the relief compact", Osvan opens it again at L373, and its terms are already drafted. (4) Tareya puts the ring on at L411 and sets it into the cup at L413 without taking it off.
- Fix: At L287, have him hold the signet over the cup or set it on the table. Remove the blanks before L331, or run the parallel demonstration with blanks in cups that really are opposite each other before anyone places a real ring, and make the western cup at L413 fit that layout. Add the relief compact to the L283 inventory as a draft or blank form (Osvan's L189 answer can then list what it still lacks), and have only one person open it. At L411–L413, either set the ring straight into the cup or show her drawing it off again.

### C2 · Minor · The effect of taking off the ring is unclear

- Where: L79–L81 (chamber), L313–L315 and L343 (rooftop)
- Text:
> "The law calls it no-fault dissolution until dawn."
> Across the palace, red relay light ceased in window after window
> "Do you want me to stay married to you?"
- Problem: L79–L81 treat taking off the ring as the act of refusal ("no-fault dissolution"). When she does it, the palace sees her light go out and the chamberlain reaches for the bell, yet "stay married" and the ring going back on show that the marriage is still open. L17 supports that reading (the ceremony lapses only if the last bar goes dark without both placements), but nothing at the climax reminds the reader, so some will think she has already dissolved the marriage.
- Fix: Add a half-line, either through D7's reworked bridge question or in the narration at L315, making clear that taking off the ring is a refusal that becomes final only when the last bar goes dark.

### C3 · Minor · Bread handoff

- Where: L111, L117, L131, chamber
- Text:
> Tareya broke the bread in half and gave him one piece.
> He did not take the bread.
- Problem: She gives him the bread, then he does not take it, then she "pushed the bread into his hand."
- Fix: At L111, change "gave him" to "held out".
