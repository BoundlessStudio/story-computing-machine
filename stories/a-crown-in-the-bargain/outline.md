## Beats

1. Vaela inventories a suite arranged from secondhand reports: maps, court clothes, two desks, and luggage routed toward the royal wing. She opens with the exact prompt. Renald's avoidance confirms calculation, but his avoidance of imperatives suggests more.
2. Vaela makes him identify the ring's function and tests immunity with a harmless refusal. He wants an uncommanded spouse and chose her because her barony is independent and she once opposed his profitable policy. Neither fact supplies consent.
3. She inspects the settlement: toll cancellation already dispatched, separate property, and an annulment she alone can file. These establish an exit and explain why he waited for her immunity before seeking cooperation, without making concealment harmless.
4. Renald wants Vaela beside him at coronation, able to identify coercion and interrupt his certainty. She exposes the missing power: she cannot release others, undo commands, prevent retaliation, or observe every coerced choice. Making her the sole exception would isolate her and privatize blame.
5. An exhausted page enters with the summons and hot wax. Renald reflexively tells him to wait; he freezes as wax spills toward his fingers. Vaela removes the danger but cannot release him. Renald must retract the command and face the frightened person. Keep harm minor and shame active.
6. Vaela declines the role and dismantles the staged household: clothes return to cases, her desk remains unassigned, and her name leaves the seating plan. Renald uses neither tolls nor scandal as leverage and prepares to be crowned without her presence as proof of safety.
7. Vaela does not file the annulment before dawn. She keeps it among her papers and remains married long enough to judge what Renald does when refusal inconveniences him, while declining to attend coronation as his magical safeguard. This is a bounded choice, not reconciliation or a promise of love.
8. As bells call Renald away alone, Vaela sends the toll decree toward her barony and dismantles the itinerary prepared in her name. Crown-voice, backlash, and marriage remain live; she now helps decide the next plan.
