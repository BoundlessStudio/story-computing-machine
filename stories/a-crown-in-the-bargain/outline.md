# Outline

## Story

- Premise and central promise: On their first married night, Baroness Tareya Solvane sees her ring cast a succession pattern and asks Crown Prince Osvan Rhel what he plans to do with a wife he did not marry for love. Before dawn, they must ratify a marriage using her independent herald to support his contested claim or invoke a refusal that ends it without transferring her title. Resolve both the bargain and Tareya's choice.
- Focal pressure or attachment: Tareya wants crown protection for the storm-damaged Solvane March, not absorption as an ornament or borrowed vote. Osvan wants to block the regency council's preferred military match. He mistook separate households and no demand for affection for generosity, while never asking what Tareya would choose.
- Counterforce or complication: The strongest arrangement lights Tareya's herald beneath Osvan's crown; a weaker parallel arrangement preserves her title but leaves his claim challengeable. Bell signals shorten the night. Law, Osvan's fear, the March's need, and his nondisclosure supply pressure without requiring a villain.
- POV, distance, and information limit: Close third-person past through Tareya, attentive to projected color, weather, and legal omissions. She knows Osvan only through faltering explanations, prepared documents, and choices under cost. The array reports structure, never intention.
- Governing movement and time shape: Over several hours, move from the hushglass marriage chamber across a rain-swept lantern bridge to a private rooftop succession room. Each location strips away a protection. Climax through separate signet actions, not public debate; leave the throne and romance open while settling the marriage and immediate political work.
- Speculative rule or ordinary-world constraint: Avarnth's nonliving herald lanterns turn registered signet positions into animal-shaped colored light and relay civil records between towers. Marriage rings are portable signets. During a first-night interval, either spouse may remove one and end the marriage without fault; continuation requires each living holder's separate placement. The system cannot read truth or feeling, bind anyone, or choose terms. Weather may disrupt signals but cannot change a decision.

## Voice

- Narrative texture: Follow Tareya's practical reading of light, legal gaps, weather, and room geometry; attach politics to objects she can move, refuse, cross, or sign.
- Conversational texture: Mix court formality with blunt domestic questions, failed starts, bell codes, and single-purpose staff remarks about gloves, rain, time, or food.
- Rhetorical ownership: Tareya reasons through land, access, and obligation; Osvan through counts, contingencies, and legal sequence. The chamberlain owns protocol and minutes; her attendant bodies and weather.
- Pressure behavior: Osvan's policy fluency fractures around tomorrow's household and Tareya's choice. Her polished address narrows into concrete questions, silence, and physical refusal.
- Anti-default: Avoid flawless negotiators, aphorism trading, a static confession, instant romance, or consent-certified magic. Change leverage through movement and signet actions; do not explain the ending twice.

## Beats

1. In the hushglass chamber, Tareya sees her ring project a crown-shaped countdown and asks the prompt's question. Osvan answers with succession strategy; she keeps returning him to her title, household, and daily freedom.
2. Bells announce an earlier dawn count. A weather slip from her attendant and the chamberlain's clipped schedule add ordinary work. Osvan reveals his plan: borrow her herald, protect the March, then maintain distance. Tareya identifies distance as another unilateral decision.
3. Tareya carries the articles across the wet lantern bridge to the rooftop array. Wind turns reading and shielding vellum into shared action. She knows the March's tolerances; Osvan knows the machinery but loses fluency when policy becomes personal.
4. The lenses show the safe default absorbing her herald and a riskier parallel braid preserving it. Osvan admits fear of losing the throne led him to prepare the default despite his promise of independence.
5. Tareya removes her ring, extinguishing her herald. Osvan neither stops her nor answers the chamberlain's urgent signal; he destroys the default article and places his crown signet alone, accepting a challengeable claim.
6. Tareya tests the parallel arrangement against the March's needs and chooses terms that keep her title and assent separately legible while committing both spouses to frontier relief. Osvan accepts the reduced claim without bargaining her toward absorption.
7. Tareya places her signet after his. Two distinct heralds cross the towers without merging; the device records order, not love or approval. Below, she requests two storm-road ledgers and chooses where morning work begins. Their political marriage is settled; intimacy remains unpromised.

## Continuity

- Canon used: Only the universe's personhood, consent, accessible-content, evidence-discipline, and prospective craft boundaries. No recurring person, place, monarchy, or magical system is used.
- Boundaries and unknowns: Herald lanterns are local, nonliving record-and-signal devices, not truth readers, agents, universal oath magic, or a form of the Crown of Noon, Solstice's crown, the Glass-Sea oath-seal, or Ersene succession law.
