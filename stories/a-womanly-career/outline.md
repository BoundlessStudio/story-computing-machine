# A Womanly Career

*Provisional direction; the draft can find a better route.*

Lady Cressida Varn has finished an officers' education and is expected to take a commission. She wants a nursery apprenticeship instead. In this culture children need a man's gentleness, while a highborn woman should have the steadiness for heavy weapons. Her father voices the prompt's scandalized accusation. Mother, an accomplished .90-calibre sniper, hears a daughter giving up reliable pay, regimental quarters, and a pension for poorly paid work. Grandmother, who served as a tank gunner and radio operator, remembers the power of earning her own living and is no automatic rebel ally. Cressida has investigated the school and saved a cushion; that does not make their practical objections imaginary. Her vocation does not require that she be bad at shooting.

Close third person with Cressida; probably a family breakfast, a nursery trial day, and a family encounter afterward. The comedy is the straight-faced conviction of this whole society, sustained beyond the first accusation. Father can consider teaching valuable men's work and still find his daughter's choice alarming. He helps prepare an overambitious child-facing activity despite his objection. Her trial day comes before she accepts either offer: something must actually be chosen after it.

Cressida's positive appetite is for children's absorbed and peculiar attention, which the ordinary trial day will make specific. The children have wants that do not exist to validate her. Hoping to bring home an impressive account, she overinvests in getting them to use the prepared activity properly; their engrossment takes another direction. She causes an ordinary, recoverable teaching error and must choose to follow their actual work instead of arranging a successful demonstration. An experienced male teacher has real authority and gives correction she needs. Let this be funny and difficult as well as rewarding; neither a classroom emergency nor combat applied to teaching is required.

The nursery experience changes the family encounter. Cressida can spin the day as vindication and keep seeking permission, or give a frank and funny account, accept the apprenticeship, and allow Mother to remain unconvinced. Grandmother's economic concern need not evaporate: the wages are still the wages. A possible closing movement is Cressida asking Mother for company after work, rather than an inspection or a verdict. Mother appears at the school gate among the fathers, conspicuous in her highborn martial clothes. The social crossing costs Mother something small and real; asking costs Cressida some armor. No converting speech or performance test. The exact activity, family conversation, and last image remain discoveries for prose.
