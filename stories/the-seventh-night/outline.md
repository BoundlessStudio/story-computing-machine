# Outline

## Story

- Premise and central promise: Over one private dinner, adult elven adventurer Thalenna recounts to Galten, the adult man across from her, the crawl whose seventh night exposed her youth-stealing curse and ended in a consensual four-adult orgy. The crawl and sex remain retrospective and offstage. Deliver a bawdy two-hander about ridiculous aftermath, heroic reputation, and whether Galten was desired or merely necessary.
- Focal pressure or attachment: Thalenna wants to protect the heroine celebrated in songs and keep Galten close without admitting his opinion matters. She guards beauty, privacy, and the distinction between choosing him and needing sex. Galten wants his choice and affection remembered, not edited into emergency aid.
- Counterforce or complication: Thalenna edits humiliating chronology; Galten recalls inconvenient particulars and grows jealous over two unnamed adult companions. A broadside invents demonic blood and seduction. Dinner serves incompatible purposes: reputation cleanup versus relationship reckoning. No villain is required.
- POV, distance, and information limit: Close third-person past through Thalenna, sharing tactical notice, vanity, appetite, and omissions but never Galten's thoughts. No objective flashback: the dungeon exists only in table talk, the broadside, and surviving gear.
- Governing movement and time shape: One continuous dinner. Each course strips polish: heroic summary, visible aging, voluntary negotiations, hard fade, aftermath, rumor, then Galten's significance. Both remain across the table; revelation, not public proof, supplies the turn.
- Speculative rule or ordinary-world constraint: Prolonged abstinence rapidly steals Thalenna's apparent youth, beauty, and physical ease. Consensual sex resets her to her normal adult baseline but does not heal ordinary wounds or halt normal aging. There is no cure, substitute, timing trick, or loophole; no demonic blood or seductive power; and the curse never alters desire, compels consent, or obligates anyone else.
- Dialogue promise: Frank, affectionate, jealous supper talk where heroic euphemism meets bawdy recollection, petty correction, silence, and mishap-specific jokes. Comedy comes from aftermath and unequal willingness to name desire, never from aging or consent.
- Dialogic medium: Face-to-face speech, eating, eye contact, wine, and gear handled as shields; a broadside and mismatched gauntlet provide silent counterevidence. No other present or remembered voice appears.
- Dialogue engine: Thalenna needs Galten's help containing rumor; he needs her to stop erasing his agency and feeling. Each correction reallocates embarrassment, desire, blame, or credit, pitting clean legend against private truth.

## Voice

- Narrative texture: Track Thalenna's elegant self-editing through tactile interruptions: stiff armor, cooling meat, wine near gold fittings, a disputed gauntlet. Her attention shifts from room control to Galten's reactions and her omissions.
- Conversational texture: Familiar adults circle one mortifying memory through teasing, correction, pauses, and eating. Crude reference coexists with tenderness; the act stays undescribed.
- Rhetorical ownership: Thalenna owns heroic sequence, euphemism, vanity, and social authority. Galten owns earthy particulars, wounded humor, jealous misreading, and better listening.
- Pressure behavior: Cornered, Thalenna becomes ceremonious, then loses sequence. Galten jokes too quickly, chews instead of answering, and turns direct when erasure matters.
- Relationship movement: She wants discretion and intimacy but cannot ask him to believe he was desired. He wants acknowledgment and a future place but fears sounding possessive. Their account shifts narrative control and trust.
- Anti-default: Avoid polished adventurers solving scandal through a consent debrief. Keep vanity, jealousy, appetite, bad logistics, poor explanation, physical comedy, and uneven affection.

## Beats

1. Open after food and wine are served in a private timber room, with only Thalenna and Galten across the table. Use her reference-led golden hair, green eyes, pointed ears, dark green-black armor, gold fittings, and practiced poise against candlelight and a substantial meal. A broadside beside his plate sets the agenda.
2. Thalenna gives the heroic account. Galten interrupts with route mistakes, hunger, a lost boot, and her claim that tight gauntlets meant swelling. Their corrections reveal her expectation of gossip control and his of honesty.
3. Assemble the decline solely through conversation: hair paling, joints stiffening, beauty vanishing by the hour, and tomb-dust excuses. Her disclosure states the curse's limits without explaining its origin.
4. Distinguish urgency from obligation. Thalenna asked; Galten and two unnamed, unambiguously adult companions could refuse, set boundaries, and choose from existing attraction. Stop at the transition; return after the act without choreography.
5. Give aftermath the largest share: Thalenna restored but bruised and exhausted; hair caught in mail; cloak reversed; belts, boots, bedrolls, and gauntlet misassigned; privacy rope repurposed for escape; watch order confused; four adults rebuilding dignity before daylight. His corrections expose erasure; hers, jealous embellishment.
6. Mixed gear and exhaustion fed a rumor; the broadside added demonic seduction. Both reject a version deleting free choice, though Thalenna fears ruin. Keep the response private.
7. At final course, surface whether Galten mattered beyond the reset and why she invited him. Resolve by behavior: she drops blanket denial, keeps the curse private, and stops editing him out; he drops jealous use of absent companions. They stay after the agenda ends, with no reset forcing either.
