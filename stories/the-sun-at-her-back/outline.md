# The Sun at Her Back

## Promised experience

For “ten thousand years” a colossal woman has sat motionless against the sun, worshipped as the creator of a civilization that has forgotten she was an ordinary girl. Its great libraries were built to preserve the memories she sacrificed to keep the heavens alive. A young librarian finds the forgotten book with her true name and must decide whether to wake her at the cost of everything she dreamed into existence. The distance between the figures in the image and the intimacy of what he learns suggest awe, tenderness and dread. The discovery should make both her release and the destruction of an inhabited world feel possible. The later user direction favors Iven’s refusal. How they meet, how he arrives at that decision and who tells the story are open.

## Takes considered

- **The open window.** The librarian speaks to the woman through her dream and can call her awake even after he sees the first piece of the city vanish. This puts the remembered girl, the creator and the cost of the name in one live scene.
- **Her last ordinary evening.** Alternate the girl’s decision to save the sky with the librarian finding her name ten thousand years later. Cut from her giving up her name to his first chance to say it.
- **The city that fell.** A survivor remembers the young librarian who called her awake. The woman stands up while the dream city vanishes beneath the witnesses. The librarian’s decision would be seen at a remove.

## Provisional direction

At Tall Sill, one of the great libraries of Lask, Iven finds a forgotten copybook. Its first owner wrote *Bess Gault* in careful capitals. The girl’s account of a pail song matches a passage in a volume he once mended. An early keeper’s record links the books and libraries to the girl who gave up her waking memories to keep the failing heavens alive. Lask grew out of her dream.

Iven addresses the memory to Bess. She hears him and dreams a voice into the room. She can shape the things she dreams, and her actual body remains still in sleep. Her own name is among the memories she surrendered. Someone else must call it to wake her, and waking ends the dream that sustains Lask. She wants to stand up and feel an ordinary weight in her hand again. Iven wants to give her the chance. When he rises to call her, Bess recognizes her own haste in him. She agreed to give up her memories before she could imagine the loss of even one evening. She wants him to wake her and wants him to see what that would cost. She lets go of the great bell tower. At the window he holds the action she wants to finish in mind while looking at the father and child on the occupied stair beside the gap. He refuses to call her and leaves her name intact. Bess hears his answer and chooses to dream a dusk of her own. The celestial light changes, and her hand stays open.

## Craft plan

- **Teller.** Try close third present with a young librarian used to turning what he sees into what he can prove, and first-person past with Iven speaking plainly in the wake of a decision people will want to call inevitable.
- **Iven.** The image gives him dark curls, a pale shirt, a folded-up posture on a low chair and a book. I read a young man able to sit with a difficult thing. He props, peers, burrows and finally stands. He qualifies claims, corrects terms and wants Bess to be glad he found her. Wind makes him trap the page with his sleeve. He pushes himself out of the low chair when he means to call her, and its vacancy matters when she asks what it is like to get up. The light that catches his curls at the window goes with the sun.
- **Bess.** Her lowered face rests on a hand, her other hand hangs open, and sunlight comes through the blue of her curls and draped gown. She would settle into a room and take it in. She listens, weighs, lets go and makes. Her speech has the practical detail and digressions of the girl who banged a pail until her palm stung, then picked it up to follow her sister. She wants to take such a weight in her own hand again. Her loose fingers were always Lask’s blessing. The tower vanishes below them and stars are visible through them at dusk. The image needs no change in intensity.
- **Place.** The open arch of Tall Sill gives Iven the book, the face to address and sight of the lives at risk. The Choir Steps would put the crowd between them. Ash Library’s closed stacks would hide the consequences. At summer closing time the high room smells of wheat paste and hot cedar. A shutter taps in the wind, the stone cools his forearm and white towers with gold-lit windows rise through cloud. Mending and lending share the cluttered window room, with a plant on the sill taking the light meant for the readers. Shutters can make the only evening the room has known. The closing bell times worship and the lending counter, and its loss interrupts both.

## Scene briefs

About 3,200 to 3,600 words.

1. **The first closing stroke, about a quarter.** When it sounds, people set down what they are holding and offer an open palm in thanks to the Maker. A father wants his daughter to finish signing her first reader’s card before the pair of strokes that closes the library. Iven is drawn into the careful capitals of the forgotten book, recognizes the pail passage and follows the brief record of its keeping. The readers clear in advance of the final strokes, leaving him the open arch. He reads from the mended volume to Bess.
2. **The answer, about half.** The voice in the room wants him to hear about the pail. Bess wants to lift something for herself, even a dented piece of tin, and to take the step that goes with it. Iven rises with the name ready. The first of the two closing strokes sounds. She lets the tower go, and the bell finishes that note with no tower underneath it. The second stroke never comes. Beside the gap the father and daughter are still on the stair. Let Iven hold them in the same sightline as Bess’s hand and remember the weight her hand used to bear. He refuses. Stay with him as she takes in his answer.
3. **The first dusk, about a quarter.** Bess makes the evening from her own lost one. The city stays at its open shutters past the missing stroke. The girl takes her father’s hand as the lights come on below. Bess’s hand remains open above the permanent gap, visible against the stars. The hand that thanked her at closing, the remembered sting of the pail and Iven’s easy rise load that final sight. Iven stays at the window. Keep the sister’s tune with Bess and leave out the later history of Lask and the origin of the failing heavens.
