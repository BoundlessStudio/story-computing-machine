# The Sun at Her Back

## Promised experience

For “ten thousand years” a colossal woman has sat motionless against the sun, worshipped as the creator of a civilization that has forgotten she was an ordinary girl. Its great libraries were built to preserve the memories she sacrificed to keep the heavens alive. A young librarian finds the forgotten book with her true name and must decide whether to wake her at the cost of everything she dreamed into existence. I read awe, tenderness and dread in the distance between the two figures in the image and the intimacy of the knowledge the librarian acquires. The name should make both the girl’s release and the destruction of a whole inhabited world feel possible. The request specifies their positions in that decision and leaves the nature of their contact, the outcome and the telling open.

## Takes considered

- **The open window.** The librarian finds the book on a working day and speaks to the woman through her dream. When the city’s oldest bell falls out of the sky, he can still call her awake. This brings both people and the price of the name into the same scene.
- **Her last ordinary evening.** Alternate the girl’s decision to save the sky with the librarian’s discovery ten thousand years later. The great scene would cut from her giving up her name to his first chance to say it. The symmetry gives the original sacrifice space and makes it harder to bring them into a live encounter.
- **The city that fell.** A survivor remembers the young librarian who called her awake. The great scene is the woman standing up while the dream city vanishes beneath the witnesses. It gives the collapse its full scale and keeps the discovering librarian’s struggle at a remove.

## Provisional direction

Iven finds a girl’s copybook among the oldest bindings at Tall Sill, one of the great libraries of Lask. Her true name, Bess Gault, is on it in a hand that still takes pains over its capitals. As the old heavens failed, Bess gave up her waking memories to keep them alive and dreamed a city under the sun. The first librarians built their libraries to keep those relinquished memories. Iven has been tending her childhood without knowing it.

Bess can hear him reading from her memories and can dream her voice into the room. A call in her true name would wake her and end the dream sustaining Lask. She wants him to do it, and she wants him to understand what he is about to choose. She lets go of the great freestanding bell tower, the city’s clock and a place no one lives. The fall leaves a permanent gap in the skyline. Iven listens to the city below him trying to go on and refuses to call her. He leaves her name intact. Bess decides what to do with the light once she knows his answer. She dreams the first dusk anyone in Lask has seen and brings out the stars while her vast body holds its place. Her continued power makes the refusal bearable for the city and leaves her own request unanswered.

## Craft plan

- **Teller.** Try close third present, close to a clever apprentice who is used to turning what he sees into what he can prove, and first-person past, with Iven speaking plainly in the wake of a decision he knows people will want to call inevitable. Give the encounter room for long attention and the stop after the bell a different rhythm.
- **Iven.** The image gives him dark curls, a pale shirt with creased sleeves, a folded-up posture on a low chair and an open book. I read a young man able to sit with a difficult thing. He peers, props, burrows and finally has to get on his feet at the sill. In speech he corrects his terms, uses the language of evidence and asks more questions than he answers. He wants to be the person who uncovers the truth and wants Bess to be glad he found her. His chair keeps him low beneath her in the opening and is left vacant at the turn. Wind makes him trap the page with his forearm. He keeps that same hand open on the book when he refuses. His hair catches the light at the window, then loses that familiar rim at dusk.
- **Bess.** The blue silhouette has loose wind-streamed curls, a draped gown, a chin resting on one hand and an easy, open hand over her knee, with a whole sun behind her. She looks thoughtful, self-possessed and capable of waiting. She would settle into a room before she took it in. Here she listens, weighs, lets go and makes. The memory page has a girl improvising a song on a battered tin pail with her sister after sunset. Bess talks in the hands-on language of that girl, with ample breath for a digression and the directness to ask for what she wants. She dreams the clock tower away below the loose fingers the city has always painted as a blessing. The change in sunlight makes the outline of hair, dress and unmoving hand strange to Iven again. The image needs no change in intensity. Her past and the meaning of her stillness are open for the story to discover.
- **Place.** Tall Sill, the highest library in the clouded stair city of Lask, gives Iven the book, a face to address and sight of the ordinary lives at risk. The Choir Steps would give the crowd the encounter, and the sealed stacks of Ash Library would hide its consequences. On a warm, windy summer closing shift, the room smells of wheat paste and hot cedar. A loose shutter taps, stone cools Iven’s forearms at the sill, and pale towers with gold-lit windows rise through cloud. The tall arch’s shutters can make the only dark this room has ever known. The open arch makes concealment difficult and makes it possible to hear what a vanished bell leaves behind. The room will have its first use for a lamp after closing.

## Scene briefs

1. **The closing bell, about a quarter.** Iven reads near the sill and wants to finish before a waiting girl and her father need him to take their card. The copybook links the ordinary girl to the colossal woman, and the library records join up around her. The careful capitals and a child’s complaint from the door keep the discovery human-sized.
2. **The answer, about half.** Iven clears the high reading room and wants to hear what Bess would choose. She wants him to call her and chooses to show him the price. She takes time over the remembered evening. The wind works the shutters while he strains to understand her voice, and the bell sounds a last time with no tower left under it. Iven can see the waiting pair down on the stair with the other readers. He weighs what it would mean to break the rest of Lask and refuses. Give the refusal and his wait for her reply as much attention as the fall.
3. **The first dusk, about a quarter.** Still at the open window, Iven waits for Bess to speak. She chooses to bring on an evening. Real lamplight and stars take the place of the familiar halo while the shape of her hand stays suspended above the city. The opening’s artificial dark behind the shutters, the girl’s night song and the sight of the bell’s absence load that last image. Summarize the libraries’ change of use through what Iven can recognize in his own room. Leave out a tour of the other branches and the origins of the failing heavens.
