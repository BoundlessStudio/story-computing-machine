# The Last Courtesy

## Promised experience

The request gives us the youngest Imperial Geisha, a thousand years of peace between great corporate houses, and beauty and grace that conceal the deadliest engineered assassins. She refuses to kill an innocent child heir. Her own sisters are sent after her, and she must decide alone in a city of neon temples and mechanical samurai whether to risk a civilization-wide war for the child. I read “her sisters” and “beauty and grace concealing” as an invitation to make the pursuit elegant, lethal, and painfully familiar. The peace should be real enough to miss. I expect the refusal to begin the trouble and a later, informed act to decide it. The prompt leaves the outcome and the sisters’ personal histories open.

## Takes considered

- **The public bow.** Follow the youngest across a capital closing its gates, then into the public accession she was meant to prevent. The central scene is a formal duet in which the two sisters contend, in full view, over whether the heir will live to be recognized. This gives the hunt and the choice a common physical language. Chosen.
- **The sister who found her.** Follow the eldest sent to hunt the youngest through the temples they once played in. Her discovery of the youngest rehearsing a real goodbye with the child gives her a decision of her own, with the central choice seen from the outside.
- **The heir remembers.** The grown child recalls one night spent out of sight during the hunt, suspecting the woman who came for her. Its strongest scene would be hearing an assassin speak her name through a screen, followed at once by the first salvo. The retrospect would make the youngest’s judgment less immediate.

## Provisional direction

Tonight the late emperor’s throne will pass to Iori if the Imperial Geisha present her at the Open Lotus. From their bow onward, the neutral mechanical samurai will guard her and take orders from her mother’s corporate house, Kase, until she comes of age. The rival houses have brought their own armies to strike before Kase can turn the imperial guard on them. They have a compromise ready if the emperor’s line has ended. A hidden heir could delay the succession and would leave Kase a claim. The order is to make the girl’s death appear natural before she can be presented.

Naho, the youngest, refuses the order and tries to get Iori out of the city without a public claim. Her sisters hunt her and shut the exits. Tsune, her most trusted sister, can still preserve the peace by killing Iori in private and asks Naho to yield her. Naho protects the girl by speaking the formal introduction in reach of the imperial guard. The nearest samurai put their bodies between Iori and the hunters as they bow. A rival house begins its attack while the two sisters can still see each other across the stage.

## Craft plan

**Teller.** Try first-person past in Naho’s cultivated speaking voice, told close to the moment and defensively precise about what she notices, and close-third present in the slightly vain, highly physical attention of a woman accustomed to directing where a room looks. Either voice moves inward at the choice.

**Cast and speech.** Naho wants Iori safe and wants Tsune to release her from having to oppose her. She knows the houses’ terms and overestimates the refuge sisterhood still offers. She tends to offer precise courtesies about the person or thing in front of her and reserve her bluntest speech for the moment permission fails. Tsune wants the city to hold and her sister to come home. She knows the hunt order and which gates have been closed. She mistakes Naho’s refusal for a horror she can bear on Naho’s behalf. Her practised warmth anticipates the listener’s objections and talks them through a difficulty. Under pressure she gives one plain, angry account of what it has cost her to keep people alive. Iori is nine. She wants Naho to tell her what is happening and let her stay awake through the vigil. She thinks the ways strangers handle her belong to grief for her father. She links observations in the order they occur to her and says what she dislikes by describing exactly what an adult did. Other sisters hunt in concert, with Tsune their only named voice.

**Casting.** The original’s young adult woman meets the eye without smiling. The poised fold of her legs on wood, white segmented plates over dark joints and red wiring, blue light low in her chest, and black updo pinned with pink and blue flowers suggest a performer so still she can decide when the room moves. Naho steadies, listens, pivots, and holds. In rehearsal her folded stillness gives Iori space to talk, and the girl watches her chest light when she tries to hold the pose herself. In the hunt a joint ticks and gives her away to women who know her sound. Tsune adjusts a loosened flower before the duet as she did before Naho’s first performance. Keep the exposed wiring, a seam of visible skin between fitted thigh plates, and the unarmored fingers of the hand Iori takes in the smoke.

**Place.** Choose the Open Lotus over the enclosed Black Mirror Theatre and the freight lifts at Spindle Gate. This temple in Karusen faces the Fold Street terraces and lets thousands see the same movement. On the first cold night of the year, flower sellers trim silk offerings and Kase’s tram signs promise the next departure across the light of neighboring temple roofs. A bell opens the huge pink neon lotus. Until then its closed petals hide the passage’s mouth and the wooden stage. Resin smells stronger inside, incense catches in the throat, and two close rows of neutral samurai hum underfoot at the stage lip. The terraces have nowhere to hide from the adjacent roofs where the rival troops are gathering. Their open sightlines eventually let Naho name the child to the world and see where the first shots land.

## Scene briefs

1. **Behind the lotus, at dusk (20%).** Naho shows Iori the samurai’s folded bow, taking the floor at her eye level. Iori asks if the samurai will kneel for her, too. Naho promises they will guard her once she is presented and answers who can command them while Iori is too little to do it. Iori bargains to stay awake through her father’s bells. Out of her hearing Tsune orders a quiet death. Naho refuses. Tsune sends their sisters after her as Naho uses the public opening bow to slip away with Iori. Remember the child trying to hold her breath as long as Naho holds still.
2. **Fold Street, before the lotus opens (30%).** Naho leaves Iori in the enclosed passage below the stage to try the tram exit. Iori wants a time when she will come back. Alone among the temple stalls and guards, Naho discovers that her sisters have had the trams stopped and the exits sealed. A Kase timetable turns into a notice of suspended service while she watches. The neutral samurai let the sisters through to the precinct as officiants, then resume their watch at the gates. The sisters recognize Naho by her ticking joint. She evades them through the passing procession and gets back to Iori. The stage is the only place left where Naho can put the guard between Iori and her sisters.
3. **The stage and its wings, at the opening of the petals (50%, long scene).** The sisters reach Iori from opposite ends of the passage. Tsune wants Naho to let her take the girl away and kill her while the petals are still shut. Naho wants Tsune to let the girl walk, and Iori wants to know why either woman would take her out of sight again. Here the sisters have to say plainly who they are asking the other to let die. Tsune answers Naho’s appeal to let Iori go by telling her that Kase’s claim would follow the girl wherever she goes. Tsune tells Naho whom she expects to be caught in the houses’ first attack. Naho considers yielding while that choice can still save the peace. The bell opens the petals on their formal duet. Tsune closes in on Iori, and Naho uses their shared measure to hold Tsune clear. Iori tells Naho when she is ready to step out. Naho names the child to the city. The nearest pair of samurai interpose themselves as they kneel, closing Tsune’s path from the stage. The other ranks turn, and the houses see that Kase has the guard.

**Ending.** The imperial samurai fold exactly as Iori saw Naho fold in rehearsal. A rival house’s first assault reaches Fold Street before the far ranks rise. Tsune leaves the stage for the part of the terraces where she had named people in the crowd. Under the open flower Naho watches where the audience is caught, and Iori puts her hand in the one that held Tsune clear of her. Compress the order’s history into the details Naho recalls under pressure. Leave the campaign beyond this first night outside the story.
