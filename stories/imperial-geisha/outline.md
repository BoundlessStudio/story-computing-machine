# The Last Courtesy

## Promised experience

The youngest of the engineered Imperial Geisha refuses to kill an innocent heir. Her sisters hunt her through the neon temple city they kept at peace for a thousand years. She alone can decide whether to keep this child alive at the cost of war between the great corporate houses. “Beauty and grace concealing their true purpose” makes elegance part of the pursuit and the killing. The later request calls for a darker telling at the highest rating and art that reflects its darkness. I read that as a choice whose damage reaches the next morning as well as a more physically explicit account of the adults who bear it. The request leaves the outcome open.

## Takes considered

- Naho surrenders Iori after seeing what the houses are willing to do to the city. The last scene would show her maintaining the official story of the child’s death. This makes the lost child the lasting cost and leaves the threatened war imagined.
- Naho crowns Iori while leaving Tsune alive in the ruins. They meet among the wounded after the assault and both try to save people on the same terrace. This gives the war more time on the page and risks making their shared work a reconciliation.
- Naho saves Iori by killing Tsune in the public bow. A morning accession portrait puts Naho’s old skills back at the service of a state now at war. The same ceremony that saves the child begins teaching her how little of the throne belongs to her. Chosen.

## Provisional direction

Recognition at the Open Lotus gives Iori the imperial guard’s protection and gives her mother’s corporate house, Kase, command during her minority. The rivals will strike before Kase can turn the guard on them. All have agreed to leave the guard neutral if the line ends. Naho tries to get Iori out before the vigil ends. The hunt closes that route. Tsune can still take the girl away and deliver the peace she negotiated. Naho chooses to put Iori on the apron even if she has to go through her own teacher. In the bow she sees that her injured arm cannot hold Tsune back and deliberately makes the struggle lethal. She speaks the child’s name while people can still see Tsune dying.

The first attack lands where Tsune said it would. By dawn the fighting has spread beyond Karusen. Naho stays with the girl and sees Kase put the crown to work. The moral center remains the rescue and its price. The last scene shows what survival costs the child and what Naho finds herself doing to stay beside her.

## Craft plan

Keep Naho’s first-person past, close to the night and its first morning. She guards her face and watches how much space people expect before they move. She speaks in precise corrections until Tsune makes her ask at length for what she wants. Tsune knows whose deaths her bargain can prevent and coaxes where an order would fail. Iori wants the last bell, an answer in her own hearing, and a guard that will listen. She asks with the concrete unfairness of somebody who still has to get a shoe onto a sore foot. Sae, who once caught Naho whenever her knee gave in rehearsal, wants her to come back alive without the houses seeing the order split. Even the threat sounds like she is sorting out a rehearsal problem.

The supplied woman is Naho. Keep the guarded gaze, white plates over red wiring, dark floral updo, bare fingers, and blue chest light. The stage imitates the image’s living pink bloom. Tsune sets the flower in Naho’s hair, the pin hurts in the fatal turn, and the flower remains in the morning portrait. Treat the adult Geisha’s metal and living tissue as parts of one body. The violence comes close to Naho’s injury in the hunt, Tsune’s fatal wound, and what the fire leaves of known adults. At dawn Naho braces a hand on the boards in the low posture of the original.

The shutters of the Open Lotus control who can see the sisters. The Twenty Vows projects one enormous line of neon script at a time across the Fold Street tenements and mourners. Each change brings the two women in white into and out of view. Warm resin in the stage, singed plum sugar and tram heat outside, and the lacquer samurai humming at the closed lattice make the two places physical. For the art on this version, use deep blue-black shadow with pink and cyan confined to actual light, white enamel catching only parts of it. Keep the soft form of the flower, with damaged architecture and ash-gray dawn as the counterweight.

## Scene briefs

1. **Open Lotus, rehearsal and first bell.** Fusa fits the mourning and coronation shoes. Naho remembers wearing Fusa’s shoes in the photograph where the man Tsune killed at her debut still looks asleep. She shows Iori the mark and promises her the last bell. On the stair, Tsune shows Naho the houses’ seals on the neutral-guard bargain and points out the rivals taking the roofs above the terraces full of mourners. She orders the child’s death. Naho refuses and takes Iori down below the stage. Tsune sends their sisters after her.
2. **Fold Street, before the last bell.** Naho hides Iori behind the hanging curtains until she calls her name, then tries the tram at a speed the child cannot match. She sees the unmarked guard shut the lattice while the departure board cancels the cars. Two sisters clear the gate and hear her knee. Sae catches her at the terrace and offers her a way to come back quietly. When Naho keeps Iori’s hiding place to herself, Sae chooses to hurt her under cover of the greeting. Naho leaves a piece of her armor behind and escapes with the turning vows and the public bow. Sae follows with the plate and, on Tsune’s order, shuts and holds the street door while Tsune comes down from the stage.
3. **The passage and the open petals, long scene.** Tsune can still take Iori away unseen. She tells the story of Fusa holding her son’s coat, and of the same family sitting where the rivals will fire. Naho weighs that offer, tells Iori the truth and waits while she finishes putting on her shoe. In the bow Naho tries to turn Tsune as they practiced. Tsune keeps coming for the girl through the space the turn should open. Naho’s damaged arm will only hold her back for a beat. Naho uses lethal force while she can still yield the child. Stay with Tsune dying and Iori waiting to go past her. The samurai protect Iori at her name and the Kase mark lights on their collars. The rivals fire on Fusa’s terrace.
4. **The same stage, first light.** Naho recognizes Fusa among the dead being carried out. A Kase officer wants a portrait of the child and the woman who brought her out, for cities where the fighting has begun. Iori orders the samurai to stop. They bow to her and march at the officer’s word. He tells the people in earshot that the sovereign has ordered them into battle. Iori asks Naho to tell him what she really said. The officer has a samurai ready to escort Naho out if she has contrary testimony. She braces herself beside Iori for the portrait, on the same boards where Tsune died. The flower Tsune set is still in her hair while the guard moves on.
