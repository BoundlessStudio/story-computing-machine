# The Last Courtesy

## Promised experience

The request gives us the youngest Imperial Geisha, a thousand years of peace between great corporate houses, and beauty and grace that conceal the deadliest engineered assassins. She refuses to kill an innocent child heir. Her own sisters are sent after her, and she must decide alone in a city of neon temples and mechanical samurai whether to risk a civilization-wide war for the child. I read “her sisters” and “beauty and grace concealing” as an invitation to make the pursuit elegant, lethal, and painfully familiar. The peace should be real enough to miss. I expect the refusal to begin the trouble and a later, informed act to decide it. The prompt leaves the outcome and the sisters’ personal histories open.

## Takes considered

- **The public bow.** Follow the youngest across a capital closing its gates, then into the public accession she was meant to prevent. The central scene is a formal duet in which the two sisters contend, in full view, over whether the heir will live to be recognized. This gives the hunt and the choice a common physical language. Chosen.
- **The sister who found her.** Follow the eldest sent to hunt the youngest through the temples they once played in. Her discovery of the youngest rehearsing a real goodbye with the child gives her a decision of her own, with the central choice seen from the outside.
- **The heir remembers.** The grown child recalls one night spent out of sight during the hunt, suspecting the woman who came for her. Its strongest scene would be hearing an assassin speak her name through a screen, followed at once by the first salvo. The retrospect would make the youngest’s judgment less immediate.

## Provisional direction

Tonight the Imperial Geisha will publicly present the late emperor’s legitimate heir at the Open Lotus. The presentation puts his neutral mechanical samurai under the command of the girl’s maternal house, Kase, as regent. The other great houses have brought their own armies and intend to strike before Kase can turn the imperial guard on them. An empty throne leaves the guard neutral, and the houses have a compromise ready if the emperor’s line has ended. The order is to make the death appear natural before the girl can be presented.

Naho, the youngest, refuses the order and tries to get Iori out of the city without a public claim. The sisters hunt her and cut off that first plan. Tsune, her most trusted sister, offers a last chance to preserve the peace by letting Iori die privately. Naho protects the girl by bringing her into the light and recognizing her aloud, so the samurai assert the regent’s authority. A rival house begins its attack while the two sisters can still see each other across the stage.

## Craft plan

**Teller.** Try first-person past in Naho’s cultivated speaking voice, defensively precise about what she saw that night, and close-third present in the slightly vain, highly physical attention of a woman accustomed to directing where a room looks. Either voice moves inward at the choice and leaves room for the life Naho risks beyond the stage.

**Cast and speech.** Naho wants Iori safe and wants Tsune to release her from having to oppose her. She knows the houses’ terms and overestimates the refuge sisterhood still offers. She tends to offer specific courtesies and reserve her most direct speech for the moment permission fails. Tsune wants the city to hold and her sister to come home. She knows the hunt order and which gates have been closed. She mistakes Naho’s refusal for a horror she can bear on Naho’s behalf. Her practised warmth makes room for one plain, angry account of what it has cost her to keep people alive. Iori is nine. She wants Naho to tell her what is happening and let her stay awake through the vigil. She thinks the ways strangers handle her belong to grief for her father. She links observations in the order they occur to her and says what she dislikes by describing exactly what an adult did. Other sisters hunt in concert, with Tsune their only named voice.

**Casting.** The original’s young, mature-faced woman meets the eye without smiling. The poised fold of her legs on wood, white segmented plates over dark joints, blue light low in her chest, and close black updo pinned with pink and blue flowers suggest a performer so still she can decide when the room moves. Naho steadies, listens, pivots, and holds. In the rehearsal her folded stillness gives Iori space to talk. In the hunt one joint ticks and gives away her path to women who know her sound. At the turn Tsune adjusts a loosened flower in the way she did before Naho’s first performance. The face Naho has trained to be easy for an audience to watch remains lifted when the fighting starts. Keep the white-and-dark mechanical silhouette and bare joints, with fitted paneling across the thighs in the public costume.

**Place.** Choose the Open Lotus over the enclosed Black Mirror Theatre and the freight lifts at Spindle Gate. This temple in Karusen faces the Fold Street terraces and lets thousands see the same movement. In the first cold night of the year, flower sellers trim silk offerings below its wooden stage and secondhand temple signs color the surrounding steps. The petals of the enormous pink stage lotus open to reveal the performer. Warm wood smells of resin, incense catches in the throat, and the waiting samurai make a low hum underfoot. The wings are shallow enough to hear words onstage, and the terraces expose anyone crossing them. By the end the open sightlines that let Naho protect Iori let her watch the first return fire.

## Scene briefs

1. **Behind the lotus, at dusk (25%).** Iori asks Naho to promise she can stay awake until the last bell. She knows how many bells she is meant to hear for her father. Naho knows why she has been asked to take the girl somewhere quiet. Tsune gives the order out of Iori’s hearing, assuming an obedient sister. Naho says she will take Iori out alive, then uses the first public bow and its sightline to leave with her. Remember the nine-year-old trying to hold her breath as long as Naho can keep still.
2. **Fold Street, before the stage flowers open (25%).** Naho leaves Iori in the enclosed performers’ passage under the stage while she tries the last open route out. Iori bargains for a time when Naho will come back. Alone among the temple stalls and unmoving samurai, Naho discovers her sisters closing the route with the same courteous gestures used onstage. They recognize her by the sound of a joint and cut through the line of imperial samurai to reach her. She uses her rehearsal knowledge of the steps and the passing procession to get back to Iori. The city belongs to the hunters. The live public stage offers the only protection she can reach in time.
3. **The stage and its wings, at the opening of the petals (50%, long scene).** The sisters reach Iori from opposite ends of the passage as it opens onto the stage. Tsune wants Naho’s help drawing the audience’s attention so she can get the girl out of sight to kill her. Naho wants Tsune to hear her choice and let the girl walk, while Iori wants to know why either woman would take her out of sight again. Here the sisters have to say plainly who they are asking the other to let die. Tsune tells Naho what the first attack will do to the filled terraces, where she knows people by name. Naho considers yielding. Tsune closes in during the formal duet and Naho uses their shared measure to hold her clear of the child. Iori tells Naho when she is ready to step out. Naho gives the formal introduction herself. The guard bows to the new sovereign while both sisters are still in full view.

**Ending.** The imperial samurai fold exactly as Iori saw Naho fold in rehearsal. The rival house’s first assault reaches Fold Street before they rise. Under the open flower, Naho can see where the audience is caught, and Iori puts her hand in the hand that was holding Tsune clear of her. Compress the order’s history into the details Naho recalls under pressure. Leave the campaign beyond this first night outside the story.
