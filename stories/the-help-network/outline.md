# Outline

## Story

- Premise and central promise: After the Northbridge incident, Cassia, Drita, and Bryn make an alias-only contact card for magical girls across their unnamed town. Copies acquire translations and a name, becoming global. A distant requester who refuses identity or power proof tests whether it can help without demanding exposure. Resolve the “somehow” through reciprocal handoffs: the Magical Girl's Help Network becomes an association once no founder holds every identity, answer, or decision.
- Focal pressure or attachment: Cassia wants nobody left alone and wants to remain indispensable. Answering everything makes her the weakest center. Her friends want to share the burden without extinguishing her openness.
- Counterforce or complication: Safety conflicts with usefulness. Messages can be copied, delayed, impersonated, mistranslated, or lost; systems and needs differ. School, sleep, family, and bad assumptions prevent constant availability. There is no antagonist or perfect authentication.
- POV, distance, and information limit: Close third past through Cassia. The globe exists only in received messages, altered cards, partial translations, and friends' reports—not a map, count, or proof of senders.
- Governing movement and time shape: Five returns across a school term, from a school stairwell to Cassia's bedroom. Each finds the card more altered and Cassia less available. A private handoff is the climax; time-zone changes imply global scale.
- Speculative rule or ordinary-world constraint: Ordinary phones, radio, paper, translation, and voluntary relays carry the network. It has no magic channel, identity registry, command, compulsory disclosure, or reliable membership test. Experiences remain system-specific; Northbridge rules do not generalize.
- Dialogue promise: Messy teenage contact becomes cross-cultural aid through unfinished texts, practical questions, failed jokes, correction, ambiguous silences, and ordinary chatter.
- Dialogic medium: Face-to-face speech, texts, voice notes, translated fragments, paper cards, radio, reactions, silence, failed deliveries, and refusal to provide proof.
- Dialogue engine: Cassia accompanies a distant requester without controlling her; Drita reduces disclosure while Bryn arranges a handoff. Exchanges shift knowledge, responsibility, and whether help depends on proof or Cassia.

## Voice

- Narrative texture: Return to the warming phone and altered card; notice lag, crossed-out details, clutter, and who answers instead of surveying the globe.
- Conversational texture: Use clipped logistics, failed jokes, homework interruptions, repetition, small talk, and plain requests. Translation creates embarrassment and repair, not exoticized voices.
- Rhetorical ownership: Cassia reads tone associatively, invites with humor, and overvalues listening. Drita owns blunt risk and practical affection, mistaking control for care. Bryn maps failures, evades feeling with specifics, and accepts correction. Remote voices vary in fluency and authority.
- Pressure behavior: Cassia overpromises; Drita shortens into commands she reconsiders; Bryn floods the channel, then falls silent. The requester grows briefer, making them stop filling gaps.
- Relationship movement: Cassia wants shared responsibility but cannot ask for relief. Drita wants her safe without claiming authority; Bryn wants boundaries without implying disbelief. The requester wants company without proof. Their handoff shifts trust, burden, and the right to define help.
- Anti-default: Do not stage a polished youth committee launching a flawless platform. Keep pettiness about the name, missed context, tired errors, bad translation, and partial help; association grows from fallible reciprocity, not managerial competence.

## Beats

1. After two mission-free nights, local magical girls ask how the trio's channel failed. Cassia proposes backup contacts. Drita removes civilian identifiers; Bryn records offered help, boundaries, and forwarding permission. Cassia volunteers as hub.
2. The card crosses town. Requests include accompaniment, a hazard warning, an unanswered question, and a check that becomes gossip. Replies label firsthand experience. Cassia answers through homework and dinner; Drita notices the denied strain.
3. Relationships and travel carry copies outward. Versions return with omissions and the name Magical Girl's Help Network before anyone approves it. Pettiness about the name cannot erase its use. Regional clusters relay independently, but Cassia still treats every route as hers.
4. Months later, a distant requester reports a demand to transform on a contact she distrusts. She withholds identity, location, system, and proof. Cassia asks too much; Drita challenges disclosure; Bryn overloads the channel with contingencies. Delay and misunderstanding raise pressure without combat.
5. They share their compromise as one bounded experience, honor refusal, and ask what help is acceptable. Members take intervals, repair a translation, and connect her to a trusted local person. She controls confirmation; no identity reveal or magical display rewards the founders.
6. Cassia rejects a central rulebook. Clusters choose contacts, limits, and availability under the shared name. Later, a request is already acknowledged across two time zones. Cassia leaves it unanswered: the network is present without her.

## Continuity

- Canon used: The incident ended without public identity confirmation. The trio retain their voluntary, system-specific powers, veil, ordinary relay, compromise check, darkness protocol, and anonymous adult support. The prompt orders this after that incident and before the western-bay invasion.
- Boundaries and unknowns: Do not reveal the adult helper's knowledge, recruit him, resolve the Rollcaller, or prove magic publicly. The association is independent of the later monarch, crown, circles, bureaus, and invasion; it creates no subjects, universal taxonomy, or shared mechanism. Keep locations, dates, and interval unnamed. Do not foreshadow or aid the invasion; its defender remains alone.
