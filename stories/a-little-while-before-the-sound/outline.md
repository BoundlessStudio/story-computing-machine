# A Little While Before the Sound

## Promised experience

The phone sounds an emergency alert. Its owner ignores it because they assume it is an AMBER alert, then discovers what the same warning was actually for. “It wasn’t until later” makes the intervening time part of the pleasure. An action taken in ordinary confidence can look different once the true danger is known. I read the ellipsis as an invitation to suspense and a discovery with consequences. Genre, scale, relationships, viewpoint and outcome are open.

## Takes considered

- **The ferry queue.** Play it straight on a regional scale. A driver descends from high ground after dismissing the alert. The water retreats from the ferry slips, the driver opens the tsunami warning, and the queue now blocks the way they drove in.
- **The council recording.** Tell it from the next morning as a witness's untidy account. The narrator ignored the notice that the valley had one hour to get its animals out before the first hard freeze of a freak cold front. On the playback, a neighbour was asking for a lift while the narrator was insisting on finishing one item of council business.
- **The silent flash.** A woman keeps her sister on the ice to work on an audition video. A light fills their rink and goes out in silence. She finds out from the ignored alert that an airburst can be over and its blast can still be coming. Choose this for the scene in which the first delay puts her sister in the path of the glass and the second gives her a chance to reach her.

## Provisional direction

Jory has promised to film her younger sister Mieke's audition for a touring ice show after public hours at Hale Arena. Jory hears the emergency tone and leaves the phone in her jacket, sure it's another AMBER alert. She talks Mieke into taking the sequence from the far end of the rink, where the gallery windows give her better light. The extra minutes put Mieke under the windows and take her across the ice from the players' tunnel.

A brilliant flash comes through the windows. Jory checks the overhead fixtures, which are steady. The light came from the street side and threw hard shadows across the blue line. The fans are still humming. Mieke heads toward the gallery to look out. Jory remembers answering for an alert she never read and takes out her phone. The original warning is for a predicted meteor airburst. It says a blast wave may arrive after the flash. She leaves the shelter within reach of the bench to get her sister's attention on the ice. They get behind the concrete turn before the blast breaks the gallery windows across the ice where she asked Mieke to stand.

## Craft plan

**Teller.** Try Jory in close third, alert to feet and sightlines and quick to correct, and in first person from the following morning, still arguing her case to her sister in her head. Both can slow at the flash as she tries to place what she's seen.

**Jory.** Works the arena's front desk and used to compete here. She wants to send her sister away with footage she can vouch for and likes being the person Mieke needs to ask. She speaks in practical sequences and supplies the reason for every correction, including the ones nobody asked for. When frightened she first tries to give an instruction and has to see whether anyone can follow it. She knows the regular emergency tone from AMBER alerts and assumes she can tell by sound what this one is. She knows where the tunnel opens off the bench.

**Mieke.** Wants the ice-show job and the chance to be cast on the strengths she chose to show. Knows the run she brought and how to get off the ice by the public gate. She hears the noise through the boards and takes Jory's “AMBER” on trust. Her talk starts with a specific example and often detours into a little story, particularly when she wants time to say something direct. On the ice she's comfortable making a choice at speed. Her own phone is with her gear in the dressing room.

**Place.** Hale Arena, in Raincross, a standalone town in a modern-like era. Chosen over the Alder Cove ferry approach, where people can scatter, and the Tern Fair conservatory, where footing and speed would be roughly equal. Cold clear November afternoon going into dusk. Learn-to-skate and minor hockey trade the ice on ordinary days. Ground it in detergent on the rubber mats, blades hissing on fresh ice, a low fan thrum, contact cold from the steel gate, and window mullions reflected all the way to the blue line. The public gate opens onto the glazed gallery, a later addition at the street end. The bench gate is midway along a solid side wall, with a concrete passage behind it. The same ice that separates Mieke from that passage gives her a faster way to reach it than Jory has in shoes.

**The exchange owed.** At the flash, Mieke thinks the disturbance has passed. Jory has to say what is still coming and give her a direction she can use from the ice. Mieke picks her own line to get there. In the wait behind the concrete turn, Jory has to admit that this was the warning she'd waved off.

## Scenes and ending

1. **Rink, late afternoon (35%).** Jory wants a clean take in one continuous shot. Mieke wants Jory to watch the finish she worked out before asking for a retake. They have time to talk about what each sees in the run. Jory waves off the tone, then coaxes Mieke toward the squares of window light at the street end. Establish the two exits and the broad hand cue they use when Mieke needs to stop across the ice.
2. **Rink and players' tunnel, minutes later (65%, long scene).** The silent flash interrupts the setup. Jory sees where it came from, reads the original alert and calls from behind the boards. Mieke hears the shout without its words and keeps toward the glass. Jory goes six paces diagonally out from the bench to clear the boards and get into Mieke's side view. She gives their stop cue. Mieke sees her, swings toward her and comes within earshot. Jory tells her what the flash was and what's still coming. Mieke skates for the bench and passes Jory, who has to retrace those steps in shoes. Stay with the wait behind the concrete turn, including Jory's admission, and let the sound arrive in full. When the pieces settle, end on the glass across the bright part of the ice where Jory lined up the shot, with Mieke's skates audible beside her on the tunnel floor. The light that justified one more take and the quiet behind the flash load that view.

Fold their history on this ice into the first scene. Leave the meteor's discovery, regional casualty reports and the audition's outcome outside the story.
