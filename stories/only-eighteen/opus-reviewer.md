This story avoids the collection's house-voice failure. Only Ceveth has the formal, uncontracted, literal register, and the story earns it as characterisation (an elf who "liked a table"). The twins answer her in fragments, demands, deflections, bad jokes and silence. Nearly every exchange shifts leverage from Ceveth to one twin. At the burn he has a life she was not there for. At "Thursday" she offers retreat and he turns it down himself. In the passage she admits she wants them to stay, and Romy sets terms ("Not in ten years."). At the wall Denzil asks for a way back and she hides how much she wants him to use it. The ending runs on callbacks (the bell, the ears, the pins, the early coach), states no lesson, and leaves Denzil's last unheard words unheard. The edit pass should be surgical. Fix the coat "arrive" stumble, the brush-search pronoun and two contraction slips, and leave everything else alone, including the short alternating runs.

## Dialogue findings

### D1 · Minor · Coat plan: "arrive" seems to contradict "taking it off"

- Where: L137–L151, front passage, Romy in the green coat before the coach
- Text:
> “I'm taking it off on the coach. I want to arrive in it.”
> “Then you would be better to carry it now.”
- Problem: Ground/referent. "Arrive" has no stated destination, so on first read Romy seems to say both that she will take the coat off and that she will arrive in it. Ceveth's reply only follows if "arrive" means reaching the troupe after putting the coat back on, and the reader has to work that out backwards from her answer. The beat itself is good and must stay: Ceveth's practical logic misses that Romy wants to be looked at now.
- Fix: Give "arrive" a destination in Romy's line so that "carry it now" follows on first read. Keep the strangled noise, "Look at it." and "That's what I was doing." unchanged. Illustrative only: “I'm taking it off on the coach. I want to turn up at the wagons in it.”

### D2 · Minor · Ceveth's uncontracted register slips without pressure

- Where: L183 (kitchen, fussing at Denzil) and L289 (the wall at the coach stop); compare L83 and L359
- Text:
> “You mustn't worry about finding the house.”
> “I don't believe I mentioned it.”
- Problem: Voice consistency. Ceveth's uncontracted speech ("They do not." "It is a very bright day." "That is a dreadful joke.") is the clearest thing separating her from the twins, who contract everything. Her contractions at L83 ("Why didn't you tell me?", discovering the burn) and L359 ("I don't know what I'm looking like.", the farewell) read as pressure cracking her composure and are worth keeping. L183 and L289 are calm, so they blur the marker and weaken the L359 slip.
- Fix: Uncontract L183 and L289 ("You must not worry…", "I do not believe I mentioned it."). Leave L83 and L359 as they are.

## Prose findings

### P1 · Minor · Ambiguous "she" in the brush search

- Where: L187, kitchen, while Romy searches upstairs
- Text:
> Ceveth could hear Romy opening drawers overhead, though she had been certain the brush was on the washstand.
- Problem: Because Ceveth is the grammatical subject, "she had been certain" first reads as Ceveth's certainty. Ceveth claimed nothing about the brush; the sentence means Romy's confidence has cracked after "Why would I do that?". It is the only signal of that, so the pronoun matters.
- Fix: Name Romy in the second clause or recast the sentence so the doubt is plainly hers. Illustrative only: Ceveth could hear drawers opening overhead; Romy had been so certain about the washstand.
