# Outline

## Story

- Title: Only Eighteen
- Premise and central promise: Elf caretaker Ceveth raises human twins Romy and Denzil from infancy at a residential children's home. Eighteen years scarcely suffice to learn them before they choose adult lives elsewhere. Deliver care's daily absurdity across unequal lifespans and a difficult farewell to living young adults.
- Focal pressure or attachment: Ceveth loves knowing each child's needs. She understands human growth but experiences change before she has finished enjoying what preceded it. On departure day she disguises her desire for another ordinary day together as assistance.
- Counterforce or complication: Romy wants an exciting departure; Denzil wants to leave despite fear. Neither wants infantilization or responsibility for Ceveth's loneliness. Their different responses defeat her habit of caring for them as a pair. No eviction or villain is needed.
- POV, distance, and information limit: Close third past through Ceveth, compressing passing years. She remembers bodily habits more readily than dates. Reveal the twins through conduct and imperfect talk, never certifying their futures.
- Governing movement and time shape: Uneven childhood episodes accumulate, occupying less room than departure day. Each return makes familiar care unsuitable. Keep the final movement continuous; omit future reunion and deathbed codas.
- Speculative rule or ordinary-world constraint: Ceveth has lived for centuries and scarcely changes visibly across eighteen years; the humans grow normally. Her memory remains fallible. Durable belongings become the wrong size; children tire of her stories before the plot starts. Both choose supported placements and lodging; eighteen marks their transition, not a universal removal rule. They remain welcome.
- Dialogue promise: Intimate household comedy changing into embarrassing separation: childish interruption, sibling disagreement, affectionate irritation, familiar instructions that suddenly land badly, and affection no one can deliver neatly.
- Dialogic medium: Face-to-face speech across the years, interrupted storytelling, imitation, affectionate touch, refused help, and the conspicuous absence of habitual replies.
- Dialogue engine: Ceveth must discover which help each twin still wants. Her attempts to delay or anticipate make their intentions harder to say. Their resistance forces her to relinquish authority without pretending that the attachment has ended.

## Voice

- Narrative texture: Let years disappear inside remembered bodies and habits, then make departure's minutes stubbornly long. Notice sweat, uneven haircuts, noise, and uncomfortable closeness before picturesque elven beauty.
- Conversational texture: Familiar speech wastes words, interrupts stories, and occasionally concerns only an irritating practical detail. Twins disagree rather than finishing each other's thoughts.
- Rhetorical ownership: Ceveth owns patient elaboration, dry amusement, and evasive fussing; Romy owns mockery, impatient optimism, and mistaken confidence about her brother; Denzil jokes badly, withholds fear, and listens selectively. Affection belongs to all, expressed differently.
- Pressure behavior: Ceveth slips into a nursery cadence and repeats answered questions. Romy's excitement becomes sharpness; Denzil's indirect requests become nearly inaudible. None acquires therapeutic fluency at the farewell.
- Relationship movement: Ceveth wants to remain necessary but cannot ask them to stay. Romy wants shared excitement without asking permission to enjoy leaving. Denzil wants comfort without seeming to cancel adulthood. Exchanges separate welcome from supervision and make each twin individually legible to Ceveth.
- Anti-default: Avoid the timeless wise elf dispensing bittersweet lessons to grateful children. Ceveth is experienced, funny, possessive in small ways, and emotionally late to something she understood perfectly well.

## Beats

1. **The short appointment.** Begin near the twins' arrival: Ceveth is competent at infant care but unprepared for human childhood's speed. Establish paid work inside a caring household. Give the infants inconveniently different appetites or sleeping rhythms.
2. **Learning them too slowly.** Select two or three ages. One child interrupts a sprawling bedtime tale; the other demands its continuation after claiming to be too old. Clothes outgrow a careful repair. Ceveth is irritated, outwitted, and pleased. Let settled understandings expire between episodes.
3. **The day already arranged.** At eighteen, Romy joins a travelling troupe; Denzil accepts a bakery apprenticeship elsewhere. They share the first coach, then separate. Ceveth helped arrange both opportunities. Their packing exposes her unnecessary checks and their uneven eagerness.
4. **Two different departures.** Ceveth's description of their age as only eighteen lands as belittlement for Romy and tempting permission to retreat for Denzil. Avoid a balanced reckoning. Romy wrongly answers for her brother; he resists her as well as Ceveth. The caretaker learns that treating them separately now matters more than performing one perfect goodbye.
5. **Care changes hands.** Let each initiate or decline a small piece of help. Ceveth accepts the difference even when the request seems inconsistent with independence. Give her one selfish impulse she does not act on. They can still laugh at something embarrassingly familiar without laughter cancelling hurt.
6. **Departure.** Allow a messy farewell at the roadside coach. One twin's composure may fail after the other's. They leave because they want those lives. Stay with Ceveth's bodily aftermath, allowing grief without assigning it a useful task. End before resumed duties, replacement children, keepsake arrangement, or a final maxim.
