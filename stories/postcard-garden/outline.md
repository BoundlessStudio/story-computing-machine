# Outline

## Story

- Premise and central promise: **The Address of Here** (advisory title). Centuries after humans disappeared from their civilization, a young gardening android follows a human postcard through flourishing garden cities to a former departure ground. Discover where a traceable group went, establish that they arrived, and encounter the ordinary lives inside the disappearance.
- Focal pressure or attachment: Zinnet wants to stand in the garden pictured on the card. She loves growing things with surprising habits and has never taken a journey without knowing what she would bring back. The anonymous human's wish gives her an appetite that seed catalogues cannot satisfy.
- Counterforce or complication: The pictured landscape resembles several surviving gardens. Zinnet initially trusts plants more than dates; cultivated varieties travel. Her neighbor Ovolo enthusiastically supplies destinations that would benefit his festival postcard stall. Historical evidence survives unevenly, while nobody in their immediate circle remembers a human.
- POV, distance, and information limit: Close third past through Zinnet's hands, botanical attention, and increasing excitement. Human experience enters through photographs and bounded surviving communications; distinguish what these prove from what she imagines. No human flashback presented as omniscient fact.
- Governing movement and time shape: A roughly weeklong outward journey, with two substantial stops and travel compressed between them. Historical messages widen the timescale without interrupting chronology. Target prose: 2,500–4,000 words.
- Speculative rule or ordinary-world constraint: Ordinary technological androids cultivate cities for their own pleasure. Solar collection requires exposure and changes travel habits; onboard reserves allow nights and shade. Humans gradually emigrated aboard sublight garden ships. One archived arrival transmission proves a settlement existed when sent, not that its descendants remain there now. Messages cannot outrun light. Keep dates and travel times mutually possible.
- Dialogue promise: An intimate, unanswered human message draws a sociable android beyond familiar botanical quarrels; contemporary friendship and delayed human communication keep revising her idea of the journey.
- Dialogic medium: The postcard's exact “Wish you were here,” spoken exchanges, Ovolo's intermittent illustrated travel notes, historical photographs, and a short archived human arrival message.
- Dialogue engine: Suggestions make Zinnet change destinations; her reports expose Ovolo's unsuitable recommendations. Historical communications supply new evidence and human appetites absent from the androids' theories, determining where she looks next and whom she can address.

## Voice

- Narrative texture: Sunlit, tactile, curious. Notice leaf undersides, warm gold joints, paper grain, and awkward baggage. Let travel widen paragraphs; slow around photographs without making every object a symbol.
- Conversational texture: Tomato disagreements can ramble; travel advice may be affectionate and wrong. Preserve ordinary requests, tangents, disappointed expectations, and amusement. Historical fragments should sound like people sending news, not explaining humanity to posterity.
- Rhetorical ownership: Zinnet notices physical inconsistencies but guesses badly about human habits. Ovolo owns promotional exaggeration and visual jokes; his affection includes listening badly, then helping. Agata mixes homesickness with delight in an unglamorous new home.
- Pressure behavior: Excitement makes Zinnet skip questions; disappointment sharpens her requests. Ovolo initially decorates uncertainty with confident detail, then sends an unembellished correction. Neither becomes a universal interpreter.
- Relationship movement: Zinnet wants help without admitting how personally she takes the card. Ovolo wants news worth sharing but cannot ask whether his sheltered life looks small. Their exchanges produce help without ownership of its result. Agata wanted distant people to share her new place; Zinnet gains enough evidence to attempt a response.
- Anti-default: Avoid a panel of competent professionals agreeing on the correct interpretation, followed by a communal repair and a return to gardening.

## Beats

1. During preparations for a neighborhood festival, Zinnet opens a dry, sealed seed tin recovered with an old planting cabinet. Inside lies the postcard. Surround it with rooftop farms, serious tomato disagreements, and the repair café's social bustle. The card's only handwritten message is the required phrase; its photograph shows a garden through a distinctive curved window.
2. Ovolo offers an attractive nearby match, partly because he wants pictures for his stall. Zinnet takes the journey. The surviving garden rewards her with unfamiliar growing customs, but the supposed identifying tree proves to be a widely distributed cultivar. Give the detour pleasure and a real correction. A resident recognizes the photographed window as one displayed at the former departure ground.
3. On the onward leg, solar exposure shapes pauses and fellow travelers' sociability. Ovolo sends a correction and a useful reproduced visitor image after checking his claim. His picture identifies the garden ship Farleaf. Zinnet realizes she cannot reach the pictured place simply by walking farther.
4. At the departure ground, now an android botanical collection with a modest human-history display, the matching photograph is credited to passenger Agata, whose portrait survives beside it. Preserved departure material shows decades of ordinary emigration, not one miraculous vanishing. Some stayed longer, some followed relatives, and reasons differed. This explains the local disappearance without claiming to catalogue every human.
5. Ask beyond the departure display. Its accessible archive contains a later arrival packet from Farleaf: navigational identification of its destination and photographed people growing a first garden there. Agata appears older and sends small personal news. Make recognition rest on corroborating provenance, dates, and a recognizable person, not a botanical hunch alone. The humans arrived; their current fate remains outside the packet's evidence.
6. Let the last movement arise from having an actual destination and an unexpectedly ordinary person to answer. Zinnet can send a contemporary image of the android-grown cities toward that settlement, taking the transmission delay seriously. No immediate human reply, obligatory homecoming, or public consensus is needed. Preserve her excitement about a place she has not visited.
