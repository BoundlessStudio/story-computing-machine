# Outline

## Story

- Premise and central promise: **Mother of Monsters**. The kingdom's host breaches a ruined sanctuary to kill Halwen, the cursed woman it says commands the creatures there. Sapper Lieutenant Chask discovers an evacuation: goblins, beasts, and dark-crawled people are refugees who use Halwen's signals but choose for themselves. Her routes, gates, and willingness to stand between them and the host are their only protection. Resolve their immediate escape, not the kingdom's prejudice or their lasting safety.
- Focal pressure or attachment: Chask must open the inner chainhouse before the dawn assault crosses a failing approach; completing that task and keeping her section alive become incompatible. Halwen wants everyone out but hoards route decisions, distrusts all humans, and delays surrendering the sanctuary and its winter stores.
- Counterforce or complication: The first breach cracks the main escape culvert and starts a fire. The captain treats coordinated withdrawal as obedience and orders a second blast. Halwen's broad route is failing; Nacre's crawlway excludes larger bodies. Loads, injuries, smoke, incomplete translation, and the advancing host supply pressure.
- POV, distance, and information limit: Close third past through Chask, limited to sightlines, loads, fuse time, formation habits, and fragments of trade speech. Military categories distort her first reading. She cannot fully understand Halwen or Nacre, know refugee histories, or see beyond the ravine.
- Governing movement and time shape: One predawn hour moving inward with the breach, then outward with evacuation: approach, blast, mistaken pursuit, recognition through work, structural deadline, redirected charge, withdrawal. Keep the decision inside the smoke-bound chainhouse; end after escape and forced delay, without conversion, treaty, martyrdom, or symbolic closure.
- Speculative rule or ordinary-world constraint: When Halwen voices breath, her local curse splits speech into rasp, low vibration, and hard clicks. It never translates or alters anyone's will. Silence stops it; use swells her throat, unbalances her, and eventually removes her voice. Refugees voluntarily assigned a few emergency meanings to its components but keep their own signals. The curse and ordinary sanctuary mechanisms are non-sentient and establish no universal species, command, consent, or magic rule.

## Voice

- Narrative texture: Track load paths, smoke, fuse burn, footing, and bodies needing different clearances, then the choices those measurements expose. Stay inside Chask's changing classifications without essaying her realization.
- Conversational texture: Orders, counts, route checks, refusals, and incomplete translations overlap work. Trade speech has unequal fluency; taps, light changes, posture, and animal sounds remain only partly readable.
- Rhetorical ownership: Chask owns structural contingencies; the captain, doctrine and timetable; Halwen, sanctuary logistics and boundaries; Kritt, stores and loads; Nacre, immediate hazards through signals.
- Pressure behavior: The captain repeats simplified orders. Chask becomes conditional and listens longer. Halwen spends her failing voice, then yields control. Kritt interrupts with quantities; Nacre accelerates and simplifies signals.
- Anti-default: Avoid polished prejudice speeches, witty competence, a grateful chorus, apology loops, or final explanation; distinction comes through incompatible tasks, partial understanding, and costly coordination.

## Beats

1. Before dawn, Chask crosses a broken causeway with her sappers. Refugees move rubble, water, medicine, and wounded in coordinated lanes after Halwen's warning. Chask and the captain read obedience and place the first charge before the approach shifts.
2. The blast opens the wall but damages the escape culvert and ignites timber. Inside, Kritt reallocates carriers and disputes abandoning grain; an unnamed beast refuses an overloaded frame; Nacre taps and flashes an incompletely translated ceiling warning. Others tend injuries, retreat, or form a rearguard without Halwen's instruction.
3. Halwen keeps the broad route open for bodies that cannot fit Nacre's crawlway. Her warning injures her; Kritt rejects her load order. Chask reads fresh cracks: the ordered chainhouse blast will bury the escape roof. The captain's horn sets a short deadline as soldiers enter.
4. A falling lintel blocks easy retreat. Chask refuses the planted charge and orders it moved to the outer switchback, giving only structural reasons. One sapper leaves with the cut fuse; others stay to shift and brace it. Neither response represents the whole host.
5. Kritt abandons winter stores, Nacre guides a smaller group and marks bad stone, and the beast chooses the exposed ravine. Halwen releases the counterweight she meant to preserve, ruining the doors and her control of the sequence. Refugees and sappers share lifting and timing without fluent trust.
6. The redirected blast drops the switchback after the accessible groups clear it. The army halts to rebuild, unconvinced. Halwen leaves injured, temporarily voiceless, and furious; refugees divide by body and priority instead of following one column. Chask returns with surviving sappers and an accurate engineering account. Stop with escape achieved, pursuit delayed, and consequences pending.
