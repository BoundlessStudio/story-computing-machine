# Outline

## Story

- Premise and central promise: Rennic leads the first human file through a breach in the Roofless House, certain that killing Iskava—the cursed woman called the Mother of Monsters—will free the beasts, goblins, and stranger beings withdrawing before him. Each captured room proves less military than the last: barracks become sleeping wards, cages become infirmary braces, and ordered retreat becomes evacuation. At a clean killing stroke, Rennic understands that Iskava's death would expose rather than free the refugees. He seals the breach from inside, buying their evacuation one night without converting the army or ending the kingdom's hunt.
- Intended reader experience: Begin inside the hard clarity of an assault—counts, angles, signals, silhouettes reduced to units—then make every success more disorienting. Particularize the refugees through needs, arguments, fear, and mutual aid, not a claim that all are innocent. End with compromised relief: the immediate slaughter is interrupted, but discovery does not guarantee justice.
- Generating force:
  - Mode: Plot-led inward unmaking. Every chamber brings Rennic closer to his target while removing one premise of his mission: rank becomes a carrying line, restraint becomes treatment, and a throne becomes support for a failing refuge path.
  - Focal attachment or contradiction: Rennic protects his small file and needs the frontier's harms to have one directing will behind them, because then one finite mission can answer the people he has lost. The refuge forces him to hold two facts together: humans have genuinely been harmed, and exterminating these people will not repair that harm.
  - Plot-led wants and useful limit, if applicable: He wants to kill Iskava before the main force widens the breach, recover a missing advance soldier, and bring his file home. He also refuses to cut down anyone unarmed or surrendered, a rule he has considered irrelevant to “monsters.” His useful flaw is command-shaped attention: he reads coordination as hierarchy and mistakes Iskava's position amid many requests for control over those making them.
- Counterforce or complication:
  - Source: The outer army steadily enlarges the breach while refugees bottleneck at Iskava's last thresholds. Her local curse lets a living being she has welcomed pass through certain broken arches into another chamber of the Roofless House when fleeing deliberate pursuit. The ruin only channels her living magic. She cannot compel a crossing, send anyone outside, open a path for an attacker, or sustain one unconscious or dead. Repeated use leaves her cold, numb, and unable to stand. The army can still enter through ordinary broken masonry, so the curse creates time, not safety.
  - Credible inside logic, if a person: Iskava shelters the helpless, people who fought back, dangerous cornered creatures, and some who stole food or killed pursuers; refuge is not acquittal. She refuses Rennic's demand to “release” people who act for themselves and continues the evacuation while he chooses. The soldiers have real bereavements and years of official claims that synchronized monster flight proves a hidden army. After seeing otherwise, one retreats to report, one still insists on the kill, and others remain uncertain.
  - Pressure or change created: Killing Iskava strands the refugees; withdrawing leaves the breach open. Sealing it from inside means visible disobedience, self-entrapment among people who distrust Rennic, and only the time needed for the army to move its tools.
- Narrative design:
  - POV, tense, distance, and limitation: Close third past through Rennic. Early narration shares his tactical compression of bodies into types and threats without endorsing it. Keep Iskava and the refugees exterior; refusals, competing routes, terse speech, and material evidence reveal their agency. As his model fails, his attention widens without granting certainty about anyone's innocence.
  - Time shape and compression: One late-afternoon assault through full dark, with the inward passage under an hour and a brief aftermath before work begins on another wall. Render the breach, recovery of the missing soldier, the apparent throne, and Rennic's choice in scene. Let campaign phrases and familiar wounds imply the past; use no explanatory flashback.
  - Information and reveal strategy: Begin with details Rennic can misclassify—body-sized bedding as barracks, splint frames as cages, lookouts passing signs—then add facts the military reading cannot hold: refugees choosing different routes, a fearful goblin treating the missing human, human brands and cut collars, young and eggs carried ahead of food, and Iskava being contradicted without consequence. Establish the curse through crossings Rennic cannot follow and Iskava's mounting physical cost. Supply no link to an established magic, creature, sanctuary, or kingdom.
  - Governing movement: Count supposed ranks; enter the breach; pursue retreat; find domestic remains; recover the wounded soldier; watch “units” divide by need; reach Iskava; demand release; see refusal without command; identify the failing path; hear the breach signal; turn from the offered kill; let the file divide by individual choice; drop the cracked arch; complete the last crossings; hear the siege resume.
- Speculative surplus: Opening a refuge threshold fuses loose salt nearby into a hard translucent clump without changing its weight or taste. Residents keep salt in waxed packets and chip fused pieces with small hammers. The effect neither identifies refugees nor judges, powers, or explains the paths.
- Structural distinction: A one-way negative conquest through successive rooms. The enemy center loses military meaning with every gain, and the climax is Rennic's self-entrapment at a low exterior breach, not combat at Iskava's throne-like seat. There is no public proof, crowd conversion, institutional rewrite, or reconciliation emblem.
- Decisive turn, deepening, or recognition: Rennic finds Iskava seated against carved stone and reads the scene as the promised throne. Up close, her body is bracing a fractured arch while refugees interrupt, contradict, and pass her according to their own judgment. His bandaged soldier confirms that a goblin rescued him. When the assault horn announces the widening breach, Iskava cannot move without dropping the last path. Rennic warns his file, pulls the sappers' retaining pin, and drops an already-cracked section of masonry into the opening. One soldier escapes outward, one opposes him, and the rest choose separately. The fall blocks this entry long enough for evacuation but leaves Rennic inside and Iskava too depleted to protect him.
- Aftereffect or live uncertainty: The refugees reach deeper chambers from which they can scatter after dark, but the Roofless House is not safe. The army has tools, witnesses, legitimate grief, and another wall to test. Iskava is unconscious, Rennic's comrade still needs care, and the refugees have not granted Rennic trust or pardon. He has interrupted one massacre and lost his command; earlier raids and deaths remain unsettled.
- Opening / ending relation: Open with Rennic counting shadows behind broken arches as companies and waiting for the order to advance. End below the wall he dropped, picks starting elsewhere while the goblin healer tells him to hold a bandage taut for his own soldier; the evacuation continues without Iskava's orders, and Rennic has no command large enough for what he now sees.

## Beats

1. At the siege line, Rennic turns varied silhouettes and hand signs into an enemy order of battle. His file is ordered through a new breach to kill the Mother of Monsters and recover a missing soldier.
2. The first “barracks” contains body-sized bedding, feeding bowls, slings, and travel bundles. Rennic calls the weaponless room evidence of an efficient withdrawal while wedges widen the breach behind him.
3. A frightened goblin healer is splinting the missing human beneath a frame Rennic mistakes for a cage. The soldier says falling stone broke his leg and the goblin dragged him clear. Rennic stops a panicked attack and takes the injured man onward.
4. Refugees cross the next chamber in conflicting streams: a burrower refuses a low passage, goblins argue over bundles, and a horned beast doubles back for its young. A group vanishes through a broken arch backed by stone; nearby spilled salt fuses as the path opens.
5. An infirmary and stores hold human brands, cut collars, old arrows, stolen grain, and trophies from hunters—evidence of persecution and morally mixed survival. Rennic decides the curse, rather than ordinary command, must enforce obedience.
6. In the inner hall, Iskava appears enthroned among waiting creatures. Rennic advances and discovers the seat is a brace: she is holding a fractured threshold open while cold and numbness overtake her. Refugees refuse routes and cross only by choice.
7. Iskava tells Rennic she opens shelter and owns no one, then returns to the bottleneck rather than pleading. His rescued soldier confirms the goblin asked nothing of him. The outer horn signals that the breach is nearly open for the full column.
8. Rennic warns his file and drops the cracked arch into the breach. His soldiers respond differently; the collapse costs him his route back but does not defeat the army.
9. Iskava completes the last crossings and loses consciousness. Rennic helps the goblin stabilize his soldier while refugees keep moving. Picks strike another wall, and the story stops inside the time he bought.

## Continuity

- Canon used: The story occupies the one physical deep-time world in an otherwise unplaced era. Magic is one local real system; living Iskava uses it, and the nonliving Roofless House only channels it. The kingdom's account is fallible. Humans, goblins, beasts, and stranger living beings retain consequential agency, grief, and moral choice.
- Boundaries and unknowns: Keep the kingdom, era, species origins, sanctuary builders, curse origin, and geography unnamed and unconnected. The thresholds establish no universal sanctuary, pursuit, consent, portal, beast-empathy, or command rules and are unrelated to Phoebe Bellweather's beast affinity, Garran Bellweather's command-bonds, or the Glass Gate. `Monster` is the kingdom's fearful category, not objective taxonomy. Do not imply universal refugee innocence, human agreement, automatic reform, or settled responsibility. Keep violence non-graphic and broadly accessible.
