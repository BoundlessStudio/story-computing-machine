# Story plan

## Premise and promise

**Premise:** Hadrik, a legendary smith whose identity rests on being able to fix
anything, must decide whether repairing a young hero's damaged "plot armor"
means restoring the protection that saves its wearer by conscripting other
people, or remaking it into an honest, limited tool that obeys its wearer's
choices before a flooded reservoir destroys the valley town below.

**Story promise:** The comic audacity of bringing plot armor to a blacksmith
will open into a concrete magical diagnosis, a hands-on redesign, and three
increasingly dangerous proofs of the new rules. The climax will be won by
chosen, costly action rather than invulnerability, coincidence, fate, or an
external author.

**Central dramatic question:** Can Hadrik save the town and remain the smith who
"fixes anything" without restoring a device that treats the hero and everyone
near them as parts in somebody else's survival mechanism?

**Meaningful change:** Hadrik begins by treating "fixed" as an object's return
to intended function and the owner as another condition of the job. Hadrik ends
by accepting that a worthy repair can reject the maker's intention, expose its
costs, require the user's consent, and be spent in service of a choice; this
costs Hadrik the boast on which the legend was built.

## Story controls

- **POV and tense:** First person, past tense, narrated by Hadrik. The narration
  can sound retrospective but must stay inside what Hadrik knew in each scene.
- **Tone and genre:** Wry, humane fantasy. Begin with confident craft comedy,
  darken through the diagnosis without becoming grim, and deliver a physical,
  adventurous spillgate climax with a sincere aftermath.
- **Target length:** Approximately 3,300 words, within the 2,500–4,000-word
  contract.
- **Audience/content:** Teen / PG-13. Flood danger, a prior off-page broken leg,
  bruising, a strained shoulder, and a burned palm may appear without graphic
  detail. No gore.
- **Era and scale:** An unspecified high-magic fantasy era in the one persistent
  physical world. Keep the setting to Hadrik's forge, the uphill spillway, and
  the valley town at risk. Do not name or connect it to Earth, Ravel, the
  Glass-Sea era, or any established character, faction, artifact, or place.
- **Narrative emphasis:** Craft action and causal decisions over lore
  exposition. Metafiction stays in the local nickname "plot armor" and
  Hadrik's jokes; no literal author, narrative cosmos, or awareness that the
  world is fiction.
- **Résumé requirement:** In the opening page, Hadrik must naturally establish
  having repaired talking swords, obsidian breastplates, crystal staves, and
  dragon-scale daggers. Present them as distinct jobs with no claim of shared
  origin or mechanism. Do not name the objects, dragons, or former clients.

## Character engine

### Hadrik — protagonist and narrator

- **Story-facing identity:** Hadrik; a famous but mortal blacksmith whose
  observation, practice, tools, and judgment earned the legend.
- **Concrete desire:** Diagnose and repair Aven's harness before the upper
  reservoir overtops, preserving both the town and Hadrik's public claim to fix
  anything.
- **Pressure:** Water is already rising; the relief gate is jammed; Aven's first
  attempt injured their guide; and the adults who assigned the task expect the
  supposedly protected hero to try again alone.
- **Blind spot:** Hadrik believes technical competence makes the smith the final
  authority on what "fixed" means. Pride initially reduces Aven's ethical fear
  to a damaged component and regards the original maker's design as the default
  specification.
- **Strengths:** Precise observation, physical courage, knowledge of the old
  spillgate's ironwork, dry humor, and willingness to revise a diagnosis when
  evidence contradicts pride.
- **Stakes:** Failure can drown the lower town and kill Hadrik and Aven. A quick
  conventional repair would preserve Hadrik's reputation but perpetuate
  coerced rescue. An honest redesign risks failure and ultimately makes the
  old "anything" promise untenable.
- **Internal movement:** "I restore what was made" -> "I can improve the
  mechanism without questioning my authority" -> "the owner must define the
  acceptable function" -> "my own share of the risk must also be chosen" ->
  "truthful limits are part of good craft."

### Aven — young hero and moral co-driver

- **Story-facing identity:** Aven; a young, capable hero, old enough to accept
  danger but habituated to adults assigning solitary risks because the armor
  supposedly guarantees survival. Use they/them pronouns.
- **Concrete desire:** Open the jammed relief gate and save the town without the
  armor making another person pay for Aven's survival.
- **Wound/tension:** A guide's leg was broken when the damaged harness dragged
  the guide into the path of a falling gate bolt during the first attempt.
  Aven fears both being used as an expendable symbol and discovering that their
  past victories belonged to the harness rather than to them.
- **Strengths:** Knows the spillway route from the failed attempt, learns rules
  quickly, owns prior reliance without surrendering moral responsibility, and
  chooses to return even after invulnerability is removed.
- **Arc:** Assigned hero who equates survival with obligation -> client who
  names the unacceptable cost -> learner who experiences honest vulnerability
  -> person who chooses one dangerous action for a reason they endorse ->
  survivor who will demand crews, plans, and the right to refuse future work.

### Supporting presence

- Keep the wounded guide, court chroniclers, town adults, and people below
  unnamed and offstage or collective. They provide pressure and consequence,
  not subplots. The story's active relationship is Hadrik and Aven learning to
  distinguish coerced rescue from voluntarily shared risk.

## Repair mechanism and rules

The prose should reveal these rules through observation and tests, not deliver
them as one uninterrupted lecture.

### What the "plot armor" is

- It is a nonliving, close-fitted under-harness of ordinary plates connected by
  locally made **response wire**, with a central **choice rivet** and outward
  **witness hooks**. "Plot armor" is Aven's and the court chroniclers' wry name
  for equipment intended to keep the designated hero alive and therefore
  available for the next celebrated deed. It is not a universal category.
- The response wire detects actual physical strain after contact begins; it
  does not predict futures, alter probability, read a cosmic plot, or originate
  magic.
- In the original design, Aven's living alarm and the protective intention of
  nearby living people supply the usable current. The nonliving harness stores,
  focuses, and routes that current through its wire and hooks.
- When sound, its choice rivet is supposed to admit only a nearby protector who
  actively joins the circuit. The first spillgate attempt split that rivet.
  With the assent check stuck open, the witness hooks seize the smallest
  protective reflex—reaching, flinching, wanting Aven safe—and turn it into a
  bodily rescue pull. The armor preserves Aven by moving another person into an
  intercepting path or redirecting the load through them. It cannot create a
  lucky miss; it physically spends a protector.

### Diagnostic proof

1. Off Aven's body, the harness remains cold and inert, proving it is not an
   autonomous magical user.
2. While Aven wears it, Hadrik marks the wire junctions with lampblack and
   swings a padded test weight toward Aven. Hadrik intends to keep Aven safe
   but deliberately holds still. The split rivet heats, soot jumps along an
   outward hook, and Hadrik's arm is wrenched between Aven and the weight before
   Hadrik stops the swing. This reproduces the guide's accident at safe scale.
3. Hadrik blocks the witness hooks and repeats the threat. The harness tightens
   but cannot finish its circuit, confirming that it shapes living protective
   intent rather than acting independently or changing chance.

### Hadrik and Aven's redesign

- The quick restoration would be to replace the split choice rivet and reconnect
  the original outward hooks. Hadrik initially calls that the proper repair.
  Aven rejects any device whose normal purpose remains spending protectors,
  even with an assent catch that can crack again. This forces the ethical
  decision before the physical rebuild.
- Hadrik cuts away the outward hooks, folds the response braid inward, drills
  out the split rivet, and forges a **will-catch** keyed by contact and tension to
  Aven's sternum and working hand. Aven remains present and supplies living
  intent while Hadrik tempers the fitted circuit; the forge and harness only
  shape that agency.
- Hadrik adds three heat-blued **shear rivets**. Each is a visible, single-use
  overload sink: it breaks before the whole harness can silently redirect
  excess force. One controlled forge test, one real approach hazard, and the
  climax consume them in order, creating a legible countdown.
- Hadrik retains one outward copper loop as a **consent ring**, mechanically
  isolated unless a second living person grips it with bare skin and gives an
  immediate, explicit yes. It cannot preserve standing permission, seize a
  reflex, or stay engaged after release. A light forge test establishes this
  before the climax.

### Operating rules after repair

1. **Actual threat only:** The mesh warms and draws tight only under real
   contact or strain. It offers no prophecy or advance warning.
2. **A chosen motion:** Aven must be conscious, perceive the situation, press
   the will-catch, and speak one active verb such as "stand," "catch," or
   "hold." The armor reinforces only that chosen vector for roughly one breath.
   It cannot decide, dodge, or fight for them.
3. **No erased force:** It spreads load through the harness and Aven's body.
   Aven can still be knocked down, bruised, strained, or killed by excess or by
   force arriving from a different direction.
4. **Visible expenditure:** A hard use shears one of the three blue rivets. Once
   all three are gone, the response circuit is spent and the harness becomes
   damaged ordinary armor rather than finding a hidden reserve.
5. **Current consent for shared load:** A second person can add their living
   intention only by taking the consent ring and saying yes for that activation.
   Shared power also means shared heat and strain. This is a feature of this
   local circuit, not a universal claim that consent powers all magic.

## Causal arc

### Beginning state

Hadrik opens by defining the legend through the absurd breadth of past repairs:
talking swords, obsidian breastplates, crystal staves, and dragon-scale daggers.
The boast that nothing leaves the forge unfixed protects Hadrik from admitting
uncertainty, age, or moral responsibility beyond workmanship.

### Inciting disruption

Aven arrives soaked from the uphill spillway, places the scorched under-harness
on the bench, and says explicitly, **"I need you to fix my plot armor."** The
line lands as a joke until Aven explains the guide's broken leg and the rising
reservoir. Aven has disobeyed the instruction to make another solitary attempt
because they will not wear it again without understanding what it does.

### Escalating turns

1. Hadrik expects a broken protective charm and promises a repair on the
   strength of reputation. The controlled pendulum instead makes the armor use
   Hadrik's own protective reflex against Hadrik, establishing both the danger
   and the living-agency mechanism.
2. Hadrik finds the split choice rivet and offers the fast, orthodox repair.
   Aven refuses because restoration would still make their survival depend on
   converting people into shields. The reservoir deadline makes redesign the
   riskier choice; Hadrik accepts it rather than lose the client, then gradually
   understands the refusal.
3. The inward redesign succeeds in a controlled test: Aven first dodges a
   padded blow without activating anything, then deliberately says "stand."
   The harness braces that one line, the blow still hurts, the first blue rivet
   shears, and Hadrik is not pulled. The consent ring remains cold when Hadrik
   merely touches it and warms only after Hadrik explicitly agrees to the light
   test.
4. Hadrik accompanies Aven because a redesigned safety system should not be
   fielded without its maker and because Hadrik once serviced the ordinary
   spillgate ironwork. On the rain-slick approach, a catwalk support tears
   free. Aven initially freezes, waiting for the old automatic rescue that never
   comes, then chooses "catch" and hauls Hadrik back from the gap. The second
   rivet shears; Aven's shoulder is strained. The repair works, but its honest
   cost and the danger of learned reliance are now undeniable.

### Crisis

At the jammed relief gate, water pressure starts tearing the final retaining
bracket loose. They can retreat and probably survive while the lower town
floods, or Aven can stand beneath the release bar and use the last rivet to hold
one direction for one breath while Hadrik drives the locking wedge. Hadrik can
also reconnect the bypass hook and let the old circuit seize whoever is nearest,
restoring the kind of survival the legend was asked to guarantee. Aven asks for
an honest assessment, not reassurance. Hadrik says the plan can work but cannot
promise either of them will leave unhurt.

### Climax

- Aven chooses the danger for a reason they own—not because the court named a
  hero—and says "hold" while pressing the will-catch. The last rivet begins to
  shear as the harness braces Aven against the release bar.
- Hadrik attacks the rusted locking wedge with practiced blows. The gate shifts
  sideways, outside Aven's protected vector, and the one-breath interval is not
  enough. The old bypass is within reach, offering automatic rescue at the
  price of restoring coercion.
- Hadrik instead drops the bypass, takes the consent ring with a bare hand, and
  gives a clear, present-tense yes. The added living intent does not cancel the
  load: it splits the heat and strain, burning Hadrik's palm while Aven's
  shoulder takes the remaining force. The extra moment lets Hadrik drive the
  wedge.
- The relief gate opens. Water takes the safe channel. The third rivet shears
  and the response wire fuses exactly as the established overload rule
  predicts. Aven and Hadrik fall clear through their own positioning and work,
  injured but alive; nobody is compelled to intercept for them.

### Resonant aftermath

Back at the forge, Hadrik initially calls the fused harness a failed repair.
Aven answers that it did precisely what they chose, stopped when its material
limit was reached, and charged its cost to no unwilling person. Aven declines a
replacement, choosing ordinary mail and promising to demand a crew, a plan,
and the right to refuse before accepting another heroic assignment.

Hadrik's palm will heal, but the old sales promise does not. Hadrik removes
"anything" from the forge sign and replaces it with an offer to explain what a
repair can do and what it will cost before taking the job. The plot armor is
the last thing Hadrik ever claims to be able to fix alone. The ending answers
the title and prompt without ending Hadrik's craft: the legend becomes smaller,
truer, and more useful.

## Canon constraints

- Treat every setting detail as part of one unspecified era of the persistent
  physical world, not a separate plane or planet. Do not claim a chronological
  link to an established era.
- "Plot armor" is a local cultural nickname for one harness. Never convert it
  into objective cosmology, universal narrative law, fate, probability
  alteration, or evidence of an external author.
- The harness, response wire, forge tools, spillgate, and prior résumé artifacts
  are nonliving unless prose explicitly establishes otherwise. They may store,
  focus, transmit, or shape magic but cannot originate it, decide, or exercise
  agency. All operative current in this story comes from Hadrik, Aven, or the
  nearby protector whom the broken design exploits.
- Do not make speech proof that one of the résumé's talking swords is alive.
  The résumé need not resolve whether any sword was living, echoing, or using a
  separate speech mechanism, because no such distinction drives this plot.
- Do not imply that talking swords, obsidian breastplates, crystal staves,
  dragon-scale daggers, and the plot armor share a maker, primordial source,
  magical system, or crossover. They demonstrate Hadrik's varied experience
  only.
- Consent, stated verbs, living intent, shear rivets, and shared load are rules
  of this redesigned local circuit. Do not generalize them into a law that all
  magic requires consent, cost, sacrifice, breath, or desire.
- Aven's personhood and responsibility remain intact. The armor can have
  influenced past survival without making every past victory fake or absolving
  the adults who assigned risk, Hadrik's choices, or Aven's choices.
- The climax must follow the diagnosis and five operating rules exactly. No
  prophecy, lucky structural failure, surprise capacity, unseeded power, or
  retrospective exception may save anyone.
- Keep Hadrik famous but local, mortal, fallible, and dependent on tools,
  knowledge, Aven's participation, and Hadrik's own costly decision.
- Keep injuries non-graphic and the aftermath resolved. The final state of the
  town, Aven, Hadrik, and the harness must all be clear.

## Failure modes to watch

- **Sketch instead of story:** Do not let the opening joke or repair résumé take
  over. Aven's arrival must cause diagnosis, redesign, field proof, climax,
  choice, and aftermath within this story.
- **Literal metafiction:** Do not mention authors, readers, chapters, genres,
  narrative necessity, or a universe that knows it is fiction. Only the local
  phrase "plot armor" carries the joke.
- **Résumé omission or false linkage:** Include all four required artifact
  types early, but do not explain them through one system, name them, or imply
  crossovers.
- **Autonomous object magic:** Avoid prose in which the armor "decides,"
  "wants," "chooses," or casts power of its own. Describe wire tension and the
  routing of living intent. Metaphor must not blur the causal account.
- **Diagnostic hand-wave:** Show the off-body inert test, lampblack/padded-weight
  reproduction, outward trace, and blocked-hook confirmation. The split choice
  rivet must explain the guide's injury and Hadrik's test injury alike.
- **Rule drift:** Track every blue rivet. The armor must require perception,
  the will-catch, a chosen verb/vector, one breath, bodily load, and present
  consent for sharing every time. No automatic warning before contact, fourth
  activation, omnidirectional shield, or painless absorption.
- **Unseeded climax solution:** Establish Hadrik's spillgate knowledge, the
  locking wedge, the consent ring, the sideways-load weakness, and the three
  rivets before the final gate sequence.
- **Agency reduced to a password:** Speaking a verb does not itself create
  virtue or safety. Aven must understand options and consequences; Hadrik's yes
  must be freely given and physically costly. Show that either could refuse.
- **Heroism confused with solitary sacrifice:** Aven's growth is not choosing
  to suffer alone. The climax validates preparation and voluntary cooperation,
  and the ending commits to crews and refusals.
- **Narrator displaced:** Aven drives the ethical specification, but Hadrik
  remains the protagonist. Hadrik diagnoses, redesigns, accompanies the test,
  rejects the bypass, takes the consent ring, drives the wedge, and changes the
  public promise.
- **Restoration presented as the only repair:** Make the quick orthodox option
  plausible, then let evidence and Aven's refusal redefine the job. Do not have
  Hadrik arrive at the redesign instantly.
- **Ambiguous resolution:** State that the gate opened, the town was spared,
  both characters survived with finite injuries, the third rivet sheared, the
  mesh fused, Aven refused a replacement, and Hadrik amended the sign.
- **Costless moral victory:** Preserve Aven's shoulder strain, Hadrik's burned
  palm, the destroyed harness, the lost absolute guarantee, and Hadrik's
  reputational change.
- **Canon overreach:** Keep the circuit, court custom, and terminology local.
  Proposed invention is not established lore, and omission elsewhere is not
  proof of a fact.
- **Name leakage:** Do not improvise names for the guide, former clients,
  artifacts, dragons, town, court, gate, tools, or groups. If prose introduces
  any new character-facing form or capitalizes a role as a name, it must be
  checked against `stories/NAMES.md` before use.
- **Tone/content breach:** Keep the forge wit alive after the diagnosis, avoid
  sermons, and render all injury non-graphically within the Teen / PG-13 ceiling.
