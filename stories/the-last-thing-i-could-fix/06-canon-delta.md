# Proposed canon delta

This file proposes reusable shared-universe facts. It is not canon until the
user explicitly approves promotion and the facts are added to `universe/`.

## New characters or character facts

- **Hadrik (proposed):** Hadrik is a famous but mortal mononymous blacksmith in
  an unnamed valley. His varied repair career includes separate jobs involving
  talking swords, obsidian breastplates, crystal staves, and dragon-scale
  daggers, with no proposed shared origin among them. He had also serviced the
  valley reservoir's ordinary spillgate ironwork. During the flood crisis,
  Hadrik redesigns Aven's protective harness, rejects its coercive bypass, and
  voluntarily shares its final load. The consent ring burns his palm, but the
  injury is expected to heal. He afterward removes the claim that he can fix
  “anything” from his forge sign and promises instead to explain a repair's
  capabilities and costs before accepting a job.
- **Aven (proposed):** Aven is a young mononymous hero who uses they/them
  pronouns. Adults associated with an unnamed court have repeatedly assigned
  them dangerous work because a protective harness was believed to guarantee
  their survival. Their own knowledge, choices, and actions remain responsible
  for their achievements even though the harness affected who paid for failed
  encounters. After learning that the damaged harness had involuntarily spent
  nearby protectors, Aven refuses restoration of that function and helps
  specify an agency-preserving redesign. They strain a shoulder during the
  spillgate crisis, decline a replacement harness, and choose ordinary mail
  plus a crew, a plan, and an explicit right to refuse future heroic
  assignments.
- Unnamed guide (proposed): Before the story's main action, Aven's unnamed guide
  survives a broken leg when the damaged harness pulls them beneath a falling
  gate bolt that would otherwise have struck Aven.

## New locations

- **Proposal — unnamed valley town:** An unnamed slate-roofed town lies below
  an uphill reservoir and river in an otherwise unspecified fantasy era. A
  relief channel and old iron spillgate can divert reservoir water away from
  the town.
- **Proposal — Hadrik's unnamed forge:** Hadrik operates an unnamed valley
  forge whose sign initially promises that he can fix anything. The workshop
  contains the ordinary smithing and diagnostic equipment used to test and
  redesign Aven's harness.

## New factions or cultural facts

- **Proposal — unnamed court practice:** Chroniclers attached to an unnamed
  court use the lower-case nickname “plot armor” for Aven's protective harness
  because it preserves the designated hero for another celebrated deed. Adults
  relying on that protection have assigned Aven solitary dangers they would
  not accept themselves.
- **Proposal — local reservoir warnings:** The valley uses an uphill reservoir
  bell to signal worsening water pressure and, after the spillgate opens, a
  long steady toll to signal falling water.

## New rules, capabilities, or costs

- **Proposal — one bounded protective circuit:** Aven's “plot armor” is a
  nonliving close-fitted under-harness of plates and locally made response wire.
  It neither predicts danger nor originates magic. It remains cold and inert
  off Aven and reacts only after real physical contact or strain, using living
  alarm or protective intent as current that its materials store, focus, and
  route. These are proposed properties of this one local device, not universal
  laws of magic.
- **Proposal — original failure mode:** The original circuit combines a central
  choice rivet with outward witness hooks. When sound, its assent gate admits a
  nearby protector only through active participation. After the choice rivet
  splits and jams open, the hooks can seize a nearby living person's smallest
  protective reflex and turn it into an involuntary bodily interception. The
  harness redirects physical load through a protector; it does not alter
  chance or create a lucky miss.
- **Proposal — will-catch redesign:** Hadrik cuts away the witness hooks, folds
  the response braid inward, and replaces the split choice rivet with a
  two-contact will-catch keyed to Aven's sternum and working palm. Under actual
  contact, conscious Aven must perceive the force, engage both contacts, and
  speak one active verb. The harness then reinforces only the chosen direction
  for roughly one breath; it cannot choose, dodge, or fight for Aven.
- **Proposal — bodily and directional cost:** The redesigned harness spreads
  rather than erases force. Aven can still be bruised, strained, knocked down,
  or killed by excess load, and force outside the chosen vector remains fully
  dangerous.
- **Proposal — three shear rivets:** Three heat-blued, single-use shear rivets
  make overload visible and prevent the inward response braid from silently
  seeking another body. Each hard activation consumes exactly one rivet. After
  the third shears, the response braid can fuse and the harness becomes inert,
  damaged ordinary armor with no hidden reserve.
- **Proposal — immediate-consent ring:** One mechanically isolated copper loop
  permits a second living person to share an activation only while gripping it
  with bare skin and giving an immediate explicit yes. Release ends the join;
  permission cannot be stored, and shared reinforcement also shares heat and
  strain. This is a feature of Hadrik and Aven's local circuit, not a proposal
  that consent universally powers magic.

## Timeline events

- **Proposal — unnamed flood crisis:** During a thunderstorm in an unspecified
  fantasy era, the valley reservoir's relief gate jams and water approaches the
  rim. Aven's first attempt leaves their guide with a broken leg, after which
  Aven brings the scorched harness to Hadrik rather than obeying instructions
  to attempt the gate again alone.
- **Proposal — exact three-rivet sequence:** Hadrik's controlled “Stand” test
  shears the first blue rivet without pulling him into the impact. On the
  spillway approach, Aven's chosen “Catch” activation shears the second while
  they save Hadrik from a collapsing catwalk and strain their shoulder. At the
  spillgate, Aven's chosen “Hold” activation shears the third after Hadrik gives
  immediate consent to share the load.
- **Proposal — crisis outcome:** Hadrik rejects the old bypass, drives the
  locking wedge, and opens the relief gate while Aven holds the release bar.
  Water enters the relief channel, the lower town survives, both workers fall
  clear according to their plan, and the response braid fuses. Aven refuses a
  replacement harness, and Hadrik amends the forge sign the following morning.

## New glossary terms or aliases

- **Proposal — `plot armor`:** A lower-case local nickname used by Aven and
  court chroniclers for Aven's nonliving protective under-harness; not a
  universal cosmological category or person-like identity.
- **Proposal — `response wire`:** The harness material that stores, focuses,
  and routes current supplied through living agency.
- **Proposal — `choice rivet`:** The original circuit's central assent-gating
  component.
- **Proposal — `witness hooks`:** The original outward paths that receive a
  nearby protector's living intent and, when the choice rivet is damaged, can
  convert reflex into involuntary movement.
- **Proposal — `will-catch`:** Hadrik's two-contact, bearer-operated replacement
  for the original assent mechanism.
- **Proposal — `shear rivet`:** One of three heat-blued, single-use overload
  sinks in the redesigned harness.
- **Proposal — `consent ring`:** The isolated copper loop through which a
  second living person may explicitly and immediately share one activation's
  load.
