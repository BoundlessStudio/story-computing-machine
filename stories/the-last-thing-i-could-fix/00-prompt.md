# Prompt contract

## Verbatim writing prompt

> **[WP] You are a legendary blacksmith, sought out for your ability to fix anything. Talking swords, obsidian breastplates, staves made of crystal, daggers forged from dragon scales, you've done it all. The real challenge today is a young hero asking you to fix what they call their 'plot armor'.**

## Story controls

- Working title: The Last Thing I Could Fix
- Target length: 2,500–4,000 words
- POV: First person, from the legendary blacksmith's perspective
- Tense: Past
- Tone and genre: Wry, humane fantasy with an adventurous climax and a sincere emotional core
- Audience/content rating: Teen / PG-13; fantasy peril and non-graphic injury
- Required elements: A legendary blacksmith whose established repair work includes talking swords, obsidian armor, crystal staves, and dragon-scale daggers; a young hero who explicitly calls the damaged object or condition "plot armor"; a concrete diagnostic and repair process; escalating consequences that test the repair; a resolved central choice about survival, heroism, and personal agency
- Prohibited elements: A story that remains only a joke or sketch; effortless invulnerability as the solution; an unexplained authorial rescue; graphic gore; an unresolved ending; inventions presented as established shared-universe canon without evidence

## Assumptions

- "Plot armor" can manifest as a real, repairable magical protection inside the story world, but its operation must have observable rules, costs, and consequences.
- The blacksmith is famous but local and mortal; their skill does not establish a new universal law of magic.
- The young hero is old enough to choose dangerous work but young enough that adults' expectations have helped define the role for them.
- The smith's greatest challenge is ethical as well as technical: repairing protection must not mean restoring the hero to consequence-free use by others.
- The story will favor earned causality and shared responsibility over destiny, while still delivering the prompt's playful metafictional premise.

## Completion tests

- The result is a complete short story, not a synopsis or chapter fragment.
- The central promise of the prompt is dramatized and resolved.
- The story passes canon and continuity review.
- Every character-facing name and alias is checked against `stories/NAMES.md`;
  any deliberate reuse has a documented narrative reason.
- The repair process changes how the plot armor works in a way the reader can understand before the climax tests it.
- The climax requires the young hero and the blacksmith to act rather than survive by coincidence.
- The ending answers whether the blacksmith truly fixed the plot armor and what that repair costs.
