# Canon brief

## Hard constraints

- The story must take place somewhere in the one persistent physical world of
  the shared universe. It may use a previously undescribed fantasy culture and
  era, but it must not present that setting as a separate planet, plane, or
  incompatible cosmology merely because it is not modern-like Earth, Ravel, or
  the Glass-Sea era. (`universe/premise.md` — `## The deep-time shared world`;
  `universe/locations.md` — `## The world across eras`)
- “Magic,” technology, and divinity are cultural descriptions of real systems,
  not objective universal categories, and the universe contains many distinct
  systems. The smith and hero may call the protection magic or “plot armor,”
  but the story must give this instance a coherent local mechanism rather than
  establish one metaphysical law behind all protective artifacts.
  (`universe/rules.md` — `## System categories`; `universe/glossary.md` —
  `## Magic, technology, and divinity`)
- Only life can use magic. Nonliving armor, tools, weapons, and workshop
  apparatus may store, focus, transmit, or shape magic supplied through living
  agency. If the plot armor or a talking sword independently originates or
  exercises magic as a user, the story must establish that it is alive rather
  than assume that status from speech or enchantment. (`universe/rules.md` —
  `## Living agency and magic`)
- The prompt requires observable rules, costs, and consequences for this
  repair. That is a story-specific requirement, not evidence that all magic is
  powered by sacrifice, desire, consent, pain, or equivalent cost. Any such
  relationship must belong to this local system and remain internally
  consistent. (`stories/the-last-thing-i-could-fix/00-prompt.md` —
  `## Assumptions`, `## Completion tests`; `universe/rules.md` —
  `## Costs, desire, and local mechanisms`)
- The repair cannot erase the young hero's personhood, consent, relationships,
  grief, or moral responsibility by reducing them to a defective mechanism.
  The central choice must remain meaningfully theirs even if the protection
  has influenced their past survival. (`universe/style-guide.md` —
  `## Personhood and moral consequence`)
- Provenance and causal consequence outrank a convenient metafictional rescue.
  “The plot needed it” cannot be the unexplained cause of survival; prediction,
  fate-like behavior, altered probability, or records of likely outcomes must
  be established through a defined, causally coherent local system.
  (`universe/rules.md` — `## Records, falsehood, and historical traces`;
  `universe/style-guide.md` — `## Continuity and evidence discipline`)
- Similar materials, imagery, or capabilities do not prove common origin. The
  talking swords, obsidian breastplates, crystal staves, dragon-scale daggers,
  and plot armor in the smith's history must not silently become products of
  one primordial artifact tradition merely because the smith repaired all of
  them. (`universe/rules.md` — `## Artifacts, motifs, and folklore`)
- The prompt contract fixes a first-person, past-tense smith viewpoint; a wry,
  humane fantasy tone; a 2,500–4,000-word target; and a Teen/PG-13 ceiling with
  non-graphic peril and injury. It also requires the four-item repair résumé,
  the hero's explicit phrase “plot armor,” a concrete diagnosis and repair,
  escalating consequences, and a resolved choice about survival, heroism, and
  agency. (`stories/the-last-thing-i-could-fix/00-prompt.md` —
  `## Story controls`, `## Completion tests`; `universe/style-guide.md` —
  `## Audience and content boundaries`)

## Useful established context

- Civilizations repeatedly rise, exchange or steal systems, collapse, and
  leave misattributed remnants. This supports a legendary repairer encountering
  unrelated objects from different traditions, provided the story does not
  claim a shared origin without evidence. (`universe/premise.md` —
  `## The deep-time shared world`; `universe/rules.md` —
  `## Artifacts, motifs, and folklore`)
- Cultures may understand real phenomena incompletely or incorrectly. “Plot
  armor” can therefore be the hero's or culture's name for a mechanism whose
  operation the smith diagnoses more precisely; the label does not need to be
  the universe's objective taxonomy. (`universe/premise.md` —
  `## Knowledge and connection`; `universe/glossary.md` —
  `## Magic, technology, and divinity`)
- A new story need not advertise the whole deep-time cosmology or connect to an
  existing character, faction, place, portal, or flagship narrative. Multiple
  independent entry points and broad fantasy tonal ranges are established
  production practice. (`universe/premise.md` — `## Memory, myth, and
  recoverable truth`, `## Knowledge and connection`;
  `universe/style-guide.md` — `## Tonal range, scale, and connection`)
- Different systems need not be active in every era. An unspecified high-magic
  fantasy era is compatible with canon. If the story instead identifies itself
  with the modern-like Nell and Bash era, it must respect that era's status as
  a low-magic trough in which ordinary life treats magic as myth or legend.
  (`universe/rules.md` — `## System categories`; `universe/timeline.md` —
  `## Established era order`)

## Conflicts or ambiguity

- **No authoritative-source conflict found; no canon ruling is required.** No
  story in `stories/INDEX.md` is marked canon `yes`, so no finalized story prose
  has authority for this research pass. The prompt's proposed local magical
  protection is compatible with the topical canon if the constraints above are
  observed. (`universe/README.md` — `## Authority order`;
  `stories/INDEX.md` — `# Story index`)
- A talking sword is not automatically either alive or nonliving. Speech,
  memory, decision-making, and magical agency are separate questions under the
  canon; the draft must answer only the distinctions on which its events rely.
  This is ambiguity, not contradiction. (`universe/rules.md` —
  `## Living agency and magic`, `## Memory, transformation, copies, and
  branches`; `universe/style-guide.md` — `## Personhood and moral
  consequence`)
- “Plot armor” is not an established glossary term or cosmological force. A
  literal external author, universal narrative law, or claim that the world is
  fiction would be a major new cosmological assertion and is not licensed by
  the prompt assumption alone. A bounded in-world protection bearing that name
  is compatible. (`universe/premise.md` — `## Knowledge and connection`;
  `universe/rules.md` — `## System categories`; `universe/glossary.md` —
  `# Glossary`)

## Unknowns

- **Unknown:** the story's era, settlement, culture, calendar, and relationship
  to the named modern-like, Ravel, and Glass-Sea eras. No placement should be
  stated as established canon. (`universe/timeline.md` — `## Universal
  chronology`, `## Established era order`; `universe/locations.md` —
  `## The world across eras`)
- **Unknown:** the smith's and hero's identities, ages beyond the prompt's
  functional guidance, histories, relationships, gender, and any recurring
  status. The authoritative recurring-character file establishes none for this
  prompt. (`universe/characters.md` — `# Recurring characters`)
- **Unknown:** any guild, heroic order, court, village, army, patron, prophecy,
  or other institution involved. The authoritative factions file supplies no
  applicable organization or culture. (`universe/factions.md` — `# Factions
  and cultures`)
- **Unknown:** whether plot armor is a worn object, an attached condition, a
  living entity, a ward, or a cultural misdescription; who made it; why it is
  damaged; what powers it; how it chooses or detects threats; what it can and
  cannot prevent; and what repair costs its bearer or smith.
- **Unknown:** whether the previously repaired talking swords are alive or
  merely speech-capable; how obsidian, crystal, or dragon scales function; and
  whether any donor dragon was living, dead, consenting, hostile, or purely
  legendary. The prompt requires the repair résumé, not answers to all of these
  questions.
- **Unknown:** the immediate danger that will test the repair and the adults or
  institutions, if any, whose expectations helped define the hero's role.
- **Unknown:** the exact meaning of “fixed.” Restoration, ethical redesign,
  removal, transfer, limitation, or a negotiated change could each fulfill the
  premise if the mechanism and ending make the answer unambiguous.

## Safe invention space

The following are **permitted local inventions, not established lore**. Any
reusable facts actually introduced belong in the later canon delta.

- A new workshop, settlement, nearby hazard, craft tradition, hero-making
  custom, and small supporting cast situated in an otherwise unspecified
  fantasy era.
- One bounded protection system called “plot armor” by its bearer or culture,
  with concrete inputs, diagnostic signs, limits, failure modes, and repair
  steps. It may redirect, absorb, anticipate, or redistribute danger if the
  draft establishes how living agency supplies or uses its magic and avoids
  turning the behavior into universal narrative law.
- A repair that replaces consequence-free survival with a narrower protection
  tied to the hero's informed choices, cooperation, preparation, or another
  clearly defined condition. This is safe only as a property of the local
  system, not a claim that consent, cost, desire, bravery, or teamwork powers
  all magic.
- Distinct local mechanisms for the smith's earlier talking swords, obsidian
  armor, crystal staves, and dragon-scale daggers—or concise résumé references
  that make no claim about their mechanisms. If a talking artifact is treated
  as a person, preserve its agency and ethical stakes; if it uses magic, resolve
  the living-agency question.
- A famous but mortal local smith whose expertise comes from observation,
  tools, practice, relationships, and fallible judgment. Their fame need not
  imply awareness of the world's deep history or authority over every magical
  system.

## Name constraints

- The prompt supplies no character-facing proper name or alias. `Blacksmith`,
  `smith`, `hero`, and `plot armor` have no exact reserved-form match in the
  current registry, so there is no prompt-required collision or close-match
  ruling at this stage. If any of those common nouns becomes a capitalized or
  recurring title used as a name, it must be treated as a character-facing form
  and registered. (`stories/NAMES.md` — `## Rules`)
- Every planned personal name, mononym, nickname, alias, username, title used
  as a name, named animal, talking sword, named armor, construct, or other
  person-like entity must be checked case-insensitively against all `Reserved
  forms`, including legacy, abandoned, candidate, and in-progress rows. A
  speaking object does not escape the name check merely because it is an
  artifact. (`stories/NAMES.md` — `## Rules`)
- Do not introduce another `Lena`, `Mara`, `Nisha`, `Pell`, or `Voss`. Those are
  unresolved legacy collisions already shared by distinct identities; adding a
  third use would compound the defect. (`stories/NAMES.md` —
  `## Known unresolved legacy collisions`)
- If the résumé or climax names a dragon, avoid the already reserved
  dragon-associated forms `Kharost`, `Queen Orvessa IV`, `Queen Orvessa`,
  `Orvessa`, `Larkspur`, `Mossback`, `Nyrzath`, `Nyr`, `the Grief-Clad`,
  `Grief-Clad`, `Nyrzath the Grief-Clad`, `Nyrzath, the Grief-Clad`, `the
  Sleepless Majesty`, `Sleepless Majesty`, `Majesty`, `Your Majesty`, `Urumet`,
  and `Varkesh`. This is production-memory guidance, not evidence those dragons
  are canon. (`stories/NAMES.md` — `## Rules`, registry rows for those
  identities)
- `Captain` is already reserved as a named dog, and `Saint` as a named mule;
  do not use either as a standalone character-facing name or alias for the
  smith, hero, or an artifact. Generic lowercase occupational or religious use
  is not a new identity label. (`stories/NAMES.md` — registry rows `Captain`
  and `Saint`)
- Any intentional reuse requires the plan and registry to state whether the
  identities are the same or distinct, why the reuse matters, and how readers
  will distinguish them. No reuse is required or supported by this prompt.
  (`stories/NAMES.md` — `## Rules`; `universe/style-guide.md` —
  `## Prose defaults`)

## Required checks after drafting

- Search the prose for every stated rule of the plot armor and verify that the
  diagnosis, repair, escalating tests, climax, cost, and ending obey the same
  local mechanism. Survival must follow actions and established rules, not
  coincidence, retrospective exception, external authorship, or an unexplained
  prophecy.
- Trace every magical action by armor, weapon, tool, or workshop apparatus to
  living agency or to the permitted storage/focusing/shaping behavior of a
  nonliving device. If an artifact originates or exercises magic as a user,
  verify that the story establishes it as alive.
- Check that explaining the armor does not negate the hero's personhood or
  absolve adults, institutions, the smith, or the hero of meaningful choices
  and consequences. Confirm that the resolved repair gives the hero real
  agency rather than merely changing the form of coercion.
- Check that the four résumé artifact types appear, but that their shared
  repairer does not create an unsupported common origin, universal magic rule,
  or crossover with an existing artifact, dragon, place, or era.
- Check era language. If the story names Earth, Ravel, the Glass-Sea world, the
  Glass Gate, or a modern-like setting, re-audit the associated chronology and
  terminology; otherwise do not add a gratuitous connection.
- Check violence and injury for the Teen/PG-13, non-graphic treatment required
  by the prompt and the universe content ceiling.
- Extract every final character-facing form—including surnames used alone,
  epithets, capitalized role labels, talking artifacts, animals, constructs,
  alternate identities, and aliases—and reconcile it against
  `stories/NAMES.md`. The primary coordinator must register planned names after
  the plan, run the story-scoped name-validation script, then reconcile and
  rerun it after final review; this canon-research stage must not edit the
  registry.
