# Canon brief

## Hard constraints

- **Authority boundary:** No story in `stories/INDEX.md` is currently marked
  canon `yes`, so no finalized story prose establishes this city. The city, its
  districts, and its crisis must be treated as new story material rather than
  recovered canon unless later approved and promoted. Authoritative setting
  facts come from `LOCKED` or `CANON` entries in `universe/`
  (`universe/README.md` — `## Authority order`).
- **One-world continuity:** If the city belongs to the established setting, it
  must occupy some place and era of the one physical world that persists
  through deep time. Earth, Ravel, and the Glass-Sea world are era-bound labels
  for that same world, not three planets or planes. Do not identify the city as
  one of those settings, or place it relative to them, without establishing a
  compatible era (`universe/premise.md` — `## The deep-time shared world`;
  `universe/locations.md` — `## The world across eras`).
- **System language is viewpoint-bound:** Magic, technology, and divinity are
  cultural descriptions of real systems, not objective universal categories.
  Distinct systems may interact or hybridize, but the coexistence of eleven
  design languages does not establish one origin or mechanism
  (`universe/rules.md` — `## System categories`;
  `universe/glossary.md` — `## Magic, technology, and divinity`).
- **Living agency limits magic:** Only life can use magic. Nonliving buildings,
  machines, foundations, and utility networks may store, focus, transmit, or
  shape magic supplied through living agency, but they cannot independently
  act as magic users unless the story establishes that they qualify as alive
  (`universe/rules.md` — `## Living agency and magic`).
- **No connection by resemblance alone:** Repeated materials, motifs, styles,
  portals, or artifacts do not prove shared origin. Any causal link among
  districts must be established as a local physical or systemic connection,
  not inferred from visual similarity (`universe/rules.md` —
  `## Artifacts, motifs, and folklore`; `universe/style-guide.md` —
  `## Continuity and evidence discipline`).
- **Personhood remains consequential:** If the city, a district, a living
  building, or constructed infrastructure becomes literally aware or
  person-like, a technical explanation cannot reduce its identity, consent,
  loss, or choices to a disposable machine error (`universe/style-guide.md` —
  `## Personhood and moral consequence`).
- **Prompt contract:** The story must remain city-scale and architectural, use
  at least eleven legible structural systems (including every required design
  language), keep humanoid protagonists unnamed, avoid cultural caricature,
  and express a complete causal crisis and resolution through the built
  environment. It must remain suitable for a general/PG audience
  (`stories/the-city-of-eleven-foundations/00-prompt.md` —
  `## Story controls`, `## Assumptions`, and `## Completion tests`;
  `universe/style-guide.md` — `## Audience and content boundaries`).

## Useful established context

- The setting is built for civilizations, technologies, and material remains
  to accumulate in layers over immense spans. Architectural borrowing,
  rebuilding, buried foundations, and partially recoverable provenance are
  compatible with canon, but none is required for this story
  (`universe/premise.md` — `## The deep-time shared world`).
- Evidence can outlast its interpretation: later societies may mistake
  machines for temples, catastrophes for creation stories, or survival
  measures for sacred law. This supports architecture as a carrier of partial
  history without making any building or inscription automatically truthful
  (`universe/premise.md` — `## Memory, myth, and recoverable truth`;
  `universe/rules.md` — `## Records, falsehood, and historical traces`).
- Cultures can possess radically incomplete models of the wider world. A local
  city can function as an independent audience entry point without explaining
  the shared cosmology or connecting itself to a recurring institution
  (`universe/premise.md` — `## Knowledge and connection`;
  `universe/style-guide.md` — `## Tonal range, scale, and connection`).
- Hybrid architecture is compatible with canon because distinct systems can
  substitute for, reproduce, or combine with one another. Each recurring
  mechanism must nevertheless remain internally consistent and cannot be
  generalized into a rule for all eras (`universe/rules.md` —
  `## System categories`; `## Costs, desire, and local mechanisms`).
- The established modern-like era is a low-magic trough after earlier ages of
  technology and magic and before later divine ages. Other eras may operate
  differently; choosing an era would affect what kinds of infrastructure can
  be ordinary rather than anomalous (`universe/timeline.md` —
  `## Established era order`; `universe/glossary.md` —
  `## Modern-like era`).
- Canon permits wide tonal, genre, and narrative-scale variation. An
  omniscient, city-focalized architectural story does not need a recurring
  character, major crossover, or overt supernatural clue
  (`universe/style-guide.md` — `## Tonal range, scale, and connection`).

## Conflicts or ambiguity

- **No blocking canon conflict found.** No authoritative entry establishes this
  city, its districts, or an incompatible architectural geography, and there
  are no canon-`yes` stories to consult. The prompt can proceed without a canon
  ruling.
- **Real-world labels are ambiguous in-world.** The setting's modern-like
  “Earth” is not necessarily literal real-world Earth. Canon does not establish
  historical China, Egypt, Greece, or the real-world movements named by
  “Gothic,” “steampunk,” “cyberpunk,” “dieselpunk,” “raypunk,” or “solarpunk.”
  Those terms are safe as reader-facing architectural descriptors, but stating
  that their literal real-world source cultures and histories existed here
  would add unsupported global history (`universe/premise.md` —
  `## The deep-time shared world`; `universe/locations.md` —
  `## The world across eras`).
- **Architectural agency is unresolved, not contradictory.** Metaphorical
  city-scale focalization does not require sentience. Literal autonomous magic,
  biological awareness, or constructed personhood would trigger the
  living-agency and personhood constraints above
  (`universe/rules.md` — `## Living agency and magic`;
  `universe/style-guide.md` — `## Personhood and moral consequence`).
- **Architectural focus and shared-universe ethics do not conflict.** Anonymous
  traces of occupancy, evacuation, maintenance, and return can preserve the
  consequences of the crisis without shifting the center to a named humanoid.
  If person-like architecture is introduced, however, its stakes cannot be
  erased merely to preserve the city-scale viewpoint
  (`universe/style-guide.md` — `## Personhood and moral consequence`;
  `## Tonal range, scale, and connection`).

## Unknowns

- **Unknown:** The city's existence, proper name, location, era, climate,
  geology, age, builders, and relationship to any known setting.
- **Unknown:** Whether the eleven foundations are literal foundations,
  infrastructural zones, an in-world classification, or only the working
  title's organizing image.
- **Unknown:** The exact additional architectural district beyond the ten
  required prompt examples, and whether there are exactly eleven districts or
  more than eleven.
- **Unknown:** The in-world origins and names of every architectural tradition.
  Canon does not establish literal Chinese, Egyptian, Greek, Gothic, or
  twentieth- and twenty-first-century “punk” movements in this world's history.
- **Unknown:** The mechanisms, energy sources, costs, maintenance requirements,
  and failure modes behind the steampunk, cyberpunk, biopunk, dieselpunk,
  raypunk, solarpunk, or fantastical systems.
- **Unknown:** Whether any district, organism, network, building, foundation,
  or the city as a whole is alive, aware, magical, divine, or merely described
  through architectural personification.
- **Unknown:** The cause, chronology, physical scope, and long-term
  consequences of the citywide crisis.
- **Unknown:** Whether the city has any relation to Ravel, the Glass-Sea era,
  the modern-like era, the Glass Gate, a portal system, recurring faction, or
  known character. Absence of evidence is not a connection.
- **Unknown:** Whether eleven has local symbolic importance. Canon assigns it
  no universal cosmological, magical, or chronological meaning.

## Safe invention space

- A new city in an otherwise unspecified place and era of the shared world is
  safe when it remains local and does not establish a new planet, plane,
  universal authority, or master chronology.
- District-specific palettes, materials, structural loads, street sections,
  drainage, ventilation, water, power, food-growing systems, transit, repair
  practices, and physical interdependencies are safe local inventions.
- A citywide structural or infrastructural hazard, along with a causal
  resolution produced by interactions among several districts, is safe if its
  mechanism and consequences are defined within this city rather than asserted
  as a universal law.
- Local combinations of mechanical, computational, biological, solar, or
  apparently magical infrastructure are safe. If the story calls a mechanism
  magical, it must identify living agency or limit nonliving structures to
  storing, focusing, transmitting, or shaping supplied magic.
- Reader-facing analogues to real architectural traditions are safe when
  rendered through specific construction principles and materials. Literal
  claims about real-world cultural ancestry should be avoided unless the story
  deliberately establishes and later records that much larger addition.
- Anonymous inhabitants, maintenance traces, evacuation patterns, wear,
  occupancy loads, and return are safe ways to show scale and consequence
  while preserving architecture as the subject.
- Local records, strata, reused masonry, and misunderstood structural remnants
  are safe, but none should automatically reveal objective history or prove a
  relationship to an existing artifact, portal, civilization, or era.
- A metaphorically focalized city is safe. Literal sentience is also possible,
  but it would be a substantive personhood and system invention that must be
  defined consistently and captured in the later canon delta.

## Name constraints

- The working title and required architectural labels have **no exact matches**
  in `stories/NAMES.md`. Because they describe a place and architectural
  categories rather than characters, they do not currently create a
  character-name collision.
- The prohibition on named humanoid protagonists means no humanoid
  character-facing names are expected. Ordinary role descriptions such as
  occupants, inspectors, builders, or maintenance crews are not aliases unless
  the prose consistently capitalizes or uses one as an individual's identity.
- A proper name or title given to an aware city, district, building,
  biological colony, machine, companion, or person-like infrastructure **does**
  count as character-facing and must be checked and registered. Architectural
  personification cannot bypass the registry (`stories/NAMES.md` —
  `## Rules`).
- Generic-looking forms already reserved as identities or aliases and
  especially relevant to personified architecture include `Captain`, `Saint`,
  `Mother`, `Time`, `Regent`, `Hollow Regent`, `Keeper Below`, `Court Ghost`,
  `Goddess`, `Sun Queen`, `Burning Queen`, `Green Coat`, `Red Scarf`, and
  `Tea Girl`. Do not reuse them as person-like district or infrastructure names
  without an intentional, documented rationale.
- `Rae` is reserved, and a person-like entity called “Ray” would be a close
  spoken match; the ordinary architectural adjective **raypunk** is not itself
  a collision. Likewise, solarpunk imagery is not a name collision, but
  person-like titles such as `Sun Queen` or `Goddess of the Sun` are reserved
  aliases.
- The unresolved source-name collisions `Lena`, `Mara`, `Nisha`, `Pell`, and `Voss`
  must not be introduced again. None is required by this prompt
  (`stories/NAMES.md` — `## Known unresolved name collisions`).
- No deliberate reuse is justified or required by the prompt. If drafting
  unexpectedly introduces any character-facing form, the coordinator must
  check exact matches, aliases, close spellings, reversals, and confusing forms
  before prose is finalized.

## Required checks after drafting

- Confirm that all eleven or more design languages are expressed through
  materials, structure, space, and infrastructure rather than facade-only
  decoration, and that the Chinese-derived district uses specific building
  techniques rather than generalized cultural ornament
  (`stories/the-city-of-eleven-foundations/00-prompt.md` —
  `## Story controls` and `## Completion tests`).
- Verify that architecture remains the focal subject of every major scene and
  that changes in one district have causal physical consequences in at least
  three others.
- Search for unsupported statements that make the city a separate world, put it
  in a known era, establish literal real-world cultural history, or connect it
  to Ravel, the Glass-Sea era, the modern-like era, the Glass Gate, or a
  recurring system.
- Audit every apparently autonomous building or network: if it uses magic,
  establish living agency; if it is nonliving, confine it to storing, focusing,
  transmitting, or shaping magic supplied by life.
- Distinguish metaphorical architectural focalization from literal
  consciousness. If any structure is a person or constructed mind, check that
  its identity and moral stakes are preserved and record that reusable fact in
  the canon delta.
- Ensure the citywide crisis has a defined local cause, propagated effects,
  structural consequences, and a resolution that follows from established
  interdependencies rather than unexplained coincidence or a universal rule.
- Check that shared colors, motifs, materials, and forms are not treated as
  automatic proof of common origin, portal linkage, divinity, or global
  historical truth.
- Run the scoped name-validation workflow even if no humanoid is named. Inspect
  the city, districts, buildings, organisms, machines, titles, and constructs
  for person-like labels; reconcile any such form with `stories/NAMES.md`.
- Keep the final treatment broadly accessible and non-explicit, and use precise
  concrete architectural prose rather than lore-display exposition
  (`universe/style-guide.md` — `## Audience and content boundaries`;
  `## Prose defaults`).
