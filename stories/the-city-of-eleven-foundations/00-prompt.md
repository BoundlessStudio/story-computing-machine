# Prompt contract

## Verbatim writing prompt

> [WP] There is a city that is architecturally colorful. It is divided by districts, and each district has its own style, like Gothic, Chinese, Egyptian, Greek, steampunk, cyberpunk, biopunk, dieselpunk, raypunk, solarpunk, and more.
>
> Notes: The story should focus on the city architecturally not the people or culture;

## Story controls

- Working title: The City of Eleven Foundations
- Target length: 3,000–3,800 words
- POV: Third-person omniscient with city-scale, architectural focalization
- Tense: Past
- Tone and genre: Lyrical speculative architectural fantasy with civic suspense
- Audience/content rating: General audience / PG
- Required elements: A visibly colorful city divided into architecturally distinct
  districts; Gothic, Chinese, Egyptian, Greek, steampunk, cyberpunk, biopunk,
  dieselpunk, raypunk, and solarpunk design languages; at least one additional
  architectural district; material, structural, spatial, and infrastructural
  detail; a complete dramatic change whose cause and resolution are expressed
  through the built environment.
- Prohibited elements: Named humanoid protagonists; people or culture becoming
  the emotional or thematic center; ethnic caricature or flattening a living
  architectural tradition into costume; a static travelogue with no causal
  arc; unexplained contradiction of established shared-universe canon.

## Assumptions

- The prompt's architectural focus permits people to appear only as anonymous
  traces of scale, occupancy, maintenance, evacuation, and return.
- "Chinese" is interpreted through specific architectural techniques—such as
  timber frames, dougong brackets, courtyards, screens, roof profiles, and
  glazed tile—rather than through generalized cultural decoration.
- The city is a local, story-scale invention in an unspecified place and era
  unless canon research establishes a suitable setting.
- The eleven design languages form genuinely different structural and
  infrastructural systems, not merely differently decorated façades.
- The central dramatic question concerns whether the divided built systems can
  survive a citywide physical crisis without erasing their distinctions.

## Completion tests

- The result is a complete short story, not a synopsis or chapter fragment.
- The central promise of an architecturally colorful district-city is
  dramatized through at least eleven legible architectural systems and resolved
  through a physical transformation of the city.
- Architecture—not an individual or a culture—remains the story's primary
  subject in every major scene.
- The story avoids an inventory-like tour by giving changes in one district
  consequences for at least three others.
- The Chinese architectural district is rendered in material and structural
  terms without stereotype.
- The story passes canon and continuity review.
- Every character-facing name and alias is checked against `stories/NAMES.md`;
  any deliberate reuse has a documented narrative reason.
