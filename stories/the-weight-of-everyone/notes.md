## Outline first readings

Codex keeper: “Its strongest possibility is the opening distinction between measurable value and a seller's title becoming the very instrument of the climax.” Its central concern: “Why can the Crown create an effective claim able to collect souls if its ownership weighs nothing? Why would this exchange, serving a Crown that signed the fraud, rescind it?” The assay needs a discoverable boundary between actual authorization and counterfeit title, and the officers need earned motives and authority.

Claude: “The image of a birth tipping both pans at once is the story's best single moment of wonder.” It identifies conflicting incentives in buying the scales versus requiring an assay; the unshown deduction from census balance to soul pledge; and the officers taking over the climax. “The climax should consist of moving pans, not clauses.” The changed gem, the reach of release, and any loss of savings need demonstrated causes. Writer independently raised the same warranty/cost contradiction and Halden's divided motive before receiving these findings. Coordinator shares the concern that ownership cannot become a moral oracle and that the initial offer must have a coherent reason.

In the outline exchange, Claude answered the keeper's force objection with institutional enforcement and proposed bribing a bonded assayer. Writer chose a leaner alternative: Halden sells the whole local soul claim for the portable unique scales; no signature requirement. “This is missing transfer, not a universal soul-ownership ban.” The deed offers what the Crown owns; Halden substitutes those it rules. Living officials operate seals that can enforce a false claim, and the earlier blessing establishes measured value separately from actual transfer. The keeper accepted this distinction while insisting the threat cannot depend on magically valid ownership. It challenged Claude's claim that officers acting necessarily displaces Merryn: “Her proof, leverage and immediate risk can be the indispensable cause.” Coordinator agrees, and also accepts planted contractual cleverness as part of this prompt's pleasure. Revised direction folds the mother and officer into one person, shows the newborn/census and name-token deductions, plants cancellation, removes arbitrary savings loss and unsupported instant reform, and gives Merryn the cancellation action with the officer's indispensable aid.

Both readers tested the revised direction. Keeper asked which transaction cancellation voids; Claude asked why collection would force an unaccepted purchase and whether any officer could cancel on suspicion. Writer's settlement: the original Crown pledge remains pending; its indivisible title warranty gives Merryn standing as the owner of one falsely pledged soul, and her assay supplies the demonstrated defect. The officer releases a guarded seal; Merryn cancels that original pledge. Halden knows the fraud and rushes collection to close the cancellation window. Coordinator accepts this over Claude's optional magical title-lock: physical enforcement can act on false title, while evidence changes a human official's knowledge and legal grounds. A magical moral lock would weaken that distinction. Drafting authorized with no unmet explicit requirement; warmth, tactile investigation, and human risk must carry the rules.

Claude's response to the settlement: “The guarded seal resolves my ‘decoration’ worry better than my magical-lock option did,” withdrawing that proposed lock. Keeper also found no material mismatch. Both will be judged against the actual prose, not treated as approval of a story not yet written.
