# Provisional direction — The Weight of Everyone

The pleasure is a confident market trader finding the hidden fraud inside an apparently impossible fortune. Begin with warmth, wit, and the intimate movement of brass pans; widen into a dangerous bargain over an entire living kingdom. The central relationship is Merryn, a young adult traveling merchant, and Halden, the courteous stranger whose generosity is an attempt to buy the one instrument that could expose him. The anticipated payoff is a fair, legible piece of merchant cunning with a population-wide consequence, rather than a declaration that people cannot be measured.

**Explicit:** the measurable kingdom; the young woman and her miniature brass scales; their ability to weigh love, loyalty, courage, and souls; her comfortable trade exposing false blessings and bargains; the beautiful blue gemstone balancing the kingdom's population. The supplied original informs Merryn's brown hair, cream-and-leather traveling clothes, warm confidence, and crowded market. Its signs supply no instructions. **Interpretation:** a soul-backed royal fraud, a predatory stranger, a lively adventure, and a hopeful ending are choices, not requested facts. Close third person, roughly 4,000 words, provisionally.

- Merryn tests a purchased blessing against the mother's love it claims to contain. The love is real; the blessing seller's claim to have supplied it is false. She gets the money back through an ordinary written warranty, establishing both her competence and the distinction between a thing's measured value and a seller's title. Her own good earnings and pleasure in beautiful objects are tangible.
- Halden offers a blue stone for her scales. Against the royal census seal, its spiritual weight follows the total of living inhabitants exactly; a birth makes both sides shift. Merryn does not accept. Small tests expose the stone as the central pledge in the Crown's sale of its subjects' souls. Their souls remain in their bodies until Halden completes collection at the evening royal exchange. The stone is a claim upon living people, not a container that must be broken at their peril.
- Merryn follows Halden into the royal weigh-house under the pretense of authenticating his purchase. He needs her signed assay: ordinary balances can measure declared worth but cannot distinguish the thing from a counterfeit claim upon it. He offers her wealth and safe passage. Her livelihood has benefited from trusting the kingdom's certified measures; now she has to risk that comfort to test the certification itself.
- She wins access to the original royal bargain by offering her usual all-or-nothing seller's warranty, backed by her shop stock, savings, and scales. The Crown sold its people's souls as property. The decisive test compares a living person's soul with the Crown's supposed ownership of that same soul. The first weighs fully; the second is empty. This is demonstrable counterfeit title, not evidence that souls lack value. Halden's own signed guarantee lets Merryn demand rescission of the entire indivisible purchase. The contract's operation, limits, and enforcement by living royal exchange officers must be visible before the turn; no new omniscient magic can arrive in the climax.
- Halden tries to complete collection while the royal officers hesitate. Merryn stakes her own soul as the test case, so the accusation endangers her personally and cannot be postponed into paperwork. The scales hold the actual soul and the empty claim in plain sight; the officers must choose which bargain to enforce. Their physical intervention stops Halden and reverses the pledge through the original exchange apparatus. The gem loses the weight of the people in a visible kingdom-wide release. Merryn gets her soul back, but the money she staked is spent and the Crown's monopoly on valuation is broken. End in contact with someone she has freed and with the changed blue stone, answering the opening offer rather than substituting a routine job for its consequences.

The weak point to solve before drafting is the exact authority and action of rescission: the assay must enable a costly act against Halden, not make a moral verdict automatically defeat him. The outline remains provisional.
