# Provisional direction — The Weight of Everyone

The pleasure is a confident market trader finding the hidden fraud inside an apparently impossible fortune. Begin with warmth, wit, and the intimate movement of brass pans; widen into a dangerous bargain over an entire living kingdom. The central relationship is Merryn, a young adult traveling merchant, and Halden, a courteous financier trying to exchange a dangerous royal claim for her unique instrument. The anticipated payoff is fair, legible merchant cunning with a population-wide consequence. People remain measurable; a measure of value does not establish ownership.

**Explicit:** the measurable kingdom; the young woman and her miniature brass scales; their ability to weigh love, loyalty, courage, and souls; her comfortable trade exposing false blessings and bargains; the beautiful blue gemstone balancing the kingdom's population. The supplied original informs Merryn's brown hair, cream-and-leather traveling clothes, warm confidence, and crowded market. Its signs supply no instructions. **Interpretation:** a soul-backed royal fraud, a predatory stranger, a lively adventure, and a hopeful ending are choices, not requested facts. Close third person, roughly 4,000 words, provisionally.

- Merryn tests a purchased blessing against the mother's love it claims to contain. The love is real; the seller's claimed contribution is empty. A sale may transfer a real intangible, but this mother never sold or gave him hers. Merryn secures a refund by a standard title warranty: an indivisible lot may be rejected if one part is not the seller's. The mother is a royal weigh-house officer carrying her newborn; she knows the commercial rule but needed Merryn's unusual instrument to prove this fraud. Keep her ordinary affection and irritation, not a lecture about law.
- Halden offers a blue stone for Merryn's scales. Its weight follows the current soul census exactly; the mother's newborn, not yet entered in the paper register, accounts for an apparent discrepancy. Touching Merryn's own name-token to the opposite pan subtracts precisely her soul from the stone's measure. He admits the stone is the collection key for a Crown pledge. It can draw the souls it has been attuned to; ordinary hands carry it lightly. It does not yet contain them.
- Halden wants to unload the defective claim before its evening collection and leave the kingdom with her unique scales, useful for business far beyond this one kingdom. He does not need her assay. He knowingly substituted the population for royal property, expects her to admire its aggregate value rather than inspect herself, and relies on the royal seal's prestige. Merryn demands the ordinary buyer's inspection, including the original deed. Eager for the exchange, he takes her to the weigh-house. The officer from the market operates the apparatus; its copper links follow the census seals, not consent or ownership. A false claim can therefore inflict real harm. The original Crown pledge has the same indivisible-lot title warranty as the opening sale: a proven actual owner can reclaim an unowned part and void the whole pending levy. The officer guards its red cancellation seal and demonstrates its control and settlement deadline. Suspicion alone does not authorize her to break a royal pledge.
- The deed sold **what the Crown owns**, which Halden has substituted with **everybody the Crown rules**. Merryn tests one indivisible part: herself. She has pledged taxes and sworn a merchant's obedience, but never transferred her soul. Her soul weighs fully against her name; the Crown's purported ownership of it weighs nothing. This is a discoverable missing transfer, not a universal rule that souls cannot be sold. The officer sees the same substitution has pledged her baby.
- Halden starts collection early to close the cancellation window. As actual owner of a falsely pledged soul, Merryn has now proven her standing to challenge the original Crown levy; her prospective purchase only gave her access. To keep her soul from being drawn with everyone else's, she holds it on the little brass pan, physically occupying both her hands. She must free a hand long enough to pull the red title seal while Halden reaches for the scales. The officer holds him off and keeps the apparatus from settling; Merryn performs the cancellation of the original pledge. The links go dark across the kingdom's census map, the blue stone's immense pull ends, and the baby's mother feels her child's soul settle back. No crowd conversion or automatic moral judgment supplies the victory.
- Merryn loses no arbitrary savings for winning her own warranty. The danger she accepted was real; she retains the scales and rejects the fraudulent claim. The stone is now only a beautiful blue gemstone, with an ordinary measured weight. Close with the mother and child and Merryn's decision about that actual jewel, echoing the opening offer. The royal seal is exposed as fallible, but no instant governmental reform is claimed.

The action must stay clearer than the paperwork: value, actual transfer, and the red cancellation seal are the only commercial rules the climax needs. The soul-holding risk must be demonstrated before the final struggle. Length can grow if the human scenes need it; this remains provisional.
