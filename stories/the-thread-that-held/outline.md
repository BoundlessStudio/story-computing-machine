# Outline

## Story

- Premise and central promise: **The Thread That Held.** Four gods bring a divided proposal to end an Era through deliberate collapse. The female Tapissier, who remembers no existence before tending the world's loom, must cast the deciding vote. Her secret identity as the Astral Valkyrie makes anticipated survivors painfully particular.
- Focal pressure or attachment: She loves following families beyond celebrated moments. Generations of banner makers supply private pleasure: altered recipes, inherited impatience, children abandoning trades. She wants their continuity, although royal commissions sustain an oppressive dynasty.
- Counterforce or complication: Irthess and Dembrel favor rupture because petitions and successive revolts have failed. Vaulenne remembers recovery measured in generations without reliable seed; Orsivet protects both sworn obligations and his dynasty's privileges. Continuation preserves real oppression. Neither side can promise the better future it imagines.
- POV, distance, and information limit: First-person present through the Tapissier's hands and selective attention. She sees outward lives through chosen threads, not every mind. Reader-only addresses explicitly recognize someone reading this story among other stories of the same world; the gods hear none of these communications.
- Governing movement and time shape: One gathering with tactile visits to generations already woven. Deliberation accumulates incompatible testimony. Target 2,500–4,000 words. Cast and enact the vote onstage, then glimpse its immediate human consequence.
- Speculative rule or ordinary-world constraint: Her living hands weave actual seasons, settlements, and connections from living influences; people supply their own actions. The loom cannot reverse completed history or command choices. These gods propose jointly withdrawing this civilization's sustaining protections. Their agreed vote governs that specific intervention, not every civilization or anomaly. Continuing protection cannot ensure peace or freedom.
- Dialogue promise: Gods petition a craftswoman who attends to lives they overlook; she must hear histories her attachment makes unwelcome.
- Dialogic medium: Face-to-face argument, petition objects, thread-borne glimpses of outward action, and private fourth-wall address. The reader cannot answer or supply a vote.
- Dialogue engine: Each attempted justification exposes a different obligation. Her pauses toward the reader make the gods demand her own commitment; private cosmic awareness offers no escape from answering them.

## Voice

- Narrative texture: Cloth close enough to snag skin beside immense intervals. Follow grain, fading dye, body heat, and changes within a repeated household scene. Fourth-wall intimacy briefly changes the addressee without dissolving the depicted room.
- Conversational texture: Uneven deliberation: testimony, interruption, impatient questions, failed recollection, divine vanity. Allow long accounts when another participant needs them.
- Rhetorical ownership: Irthess reasons from named failures without ornamental death metaphors; Vaulenne remembers physical shortages but loses dates; Orsivet hears promises as obligations and enjoys precedence; Dembrel tests claims against particular rejected petitioners. The Tapissier owns textile comparisons and dry reader-directed humor.
- Pressure behavior: The Tapissier's private fluency exceeds her spoken certainty. Irthess grows more specific when challenged; Vaulenne stops correcting him once memory hurts. Orsivet interrupts; Dembrel leaves an unanswered question unanswered.
- Relationship movement: The gods want authorization without assuming one another's burdens. She wants their judgment without revealing her other work. Testimony removes that shelter; her vote binds their proposed act while leaving disagreement and independent agency intact.
- Anti-default: Do not turn the gods into chastened pupils, translate their argument into friendly banter, or reward the vote with companionship and a reassuring shared meal.

## Beats

1. Begin following a banner maker's adult daughter through a household quarrel while easing gold thread across the loom. Flower-pinned dark hair, an embroidered wrap, brass fittings, and family silhouettes against stars draw from the inspected reference. The gods interrupt attention already invested in this family.
2. Establish the exact proposal and two votes on each side before expanding their reasons. They would withdraw their combined protection and let the old order's accumulated violence break it. Their powers, present worship, and recognized authority appear through offered objects and small deliberate acts. Their breathing and bodily discomfort establish living participants.
3. Irthess brings executed rebels' pleas; Dembrel brings an exile's worn footwear and repeated refusals. Vaulenne recalls recovery through vanished crops and households. Orsivet remembers predation before enforceable oaths, while defending arrangements enriching his worship. Disagreements change the evidence under discussion without arranging a winner.
4. The daughter reappears, cutting a royal commission into insurgents' coverings. Preserving the Era cannot preserve this household unchanged. Privately address the actual reader's access to separate stories and freedom to leave the page; neither privilege belongs to those living here.
5. Reveal to the reader alone her Astral Valkyrie identity: hidden gold wings, pinned indigo hair, concealed broken violet-crystal halo. She imagines whom she could carry through catastrophe, but sheltering one living survivor cannot save civilization or retrieve its dead. She refuses to let that later duty predetermine this vote. The gods interpret her silence as deliberation only.
6. She votes against deliberately inducing collapse. The agreed continuation is enacted through the intact weave and renewed divine contributions, with no secret repair that removes oppression. Irthess accepts the binding decision while declaring his intention to support living revolt by other means. Their final substantial exchange establishes that she cannot require a gentle struggle in return for his compliance.
7. The city and season continue; the daughter leaves home for the insurgents. The Tapissier follows an unfamiliar direction in the family thread. Survival remains possible; the family future she wanted is already departing. End within that experience, without forecasting victory or supplying a maxim.
