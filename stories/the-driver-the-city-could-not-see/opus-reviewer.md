# Opus review: The Driver the City Could Not See

Independent audit for a later editing pass. It is not a `review.md` verdict,
does not change canon state, and does not authorize edits by itself.

## Verdict

- Dialogue: REVISE — one line has no recoverable referent at the first warm beat (L145), and the Android, who communicates only in pictures, writes words in the first scene.
- Prose: PASS — concrete and well staged; a minor "not X, Y" habit and a few small slips.
- Continuity and prompt: PASS — the prompt is fulfilled; minor gaps in the timeline and in what triggers the scan, plus a water-infrastructure policy note.
- Edit scope: targeted-dialogue
- Existing review.md said: Verdict PASS, "Dialogue: PASS"; I disagree on dialogue. It missed the broken "compliment" line and the slip where Arovi writes words instead of drawing.

This is one of the stronger dialogue designs in the collection. Arovi cannot speak, so her side of every exchange is pictures, taps and touch, and when Kaith misreads them (the lost lower lane, the "six" with no unit, the early arrow) the plot changes course. Kaith's clipped speech suits a working driver under surveillance, and her longer, grumbling cover speeches to the city give her a second register. The failures are local: one line whose referent can't be recovered at the first moment of warmth, Arovi writing words in a story built on her not writing, a few opening lines that misfire, and narration that labels Arovi's pictures with "X, not Y" glosses. The edit pass should fix those exchanges and let Kaith's voice bend twice, once with warmth and once in fright. It should leave the wordless decisive exchange and the practical ending alone.

## Edit guardrails

- Prompt: the last human driver in a city run by autonomous machines is hired to carry an Android that must stay invisible to the system. The story must be complete and resolve that promise, stay within 2,500–4,000 words (now about 3,300) and remain broadly accessible. The active craft profile is `prospective-2026-08-21` (the last `Craft profile:` line, from the rewrite constraints), and the rewrite request targets dialogue.
- POV is close third person, past tense, locked to Kaith. She never enters Arovi's head, and the city has no interior life.
- Arovi never speaks aloud or transmits. Her voice or radio would expose her (L43–L45), so her side of every exchange stays pictures, taps and touch. Kaith can talk because the city ignores speech that isn't addressed to it (L25).
- Plot facts to keep:
  - The fare comes through the coin slot and prints a paper route strip.
  - The occupancy bar shows one person.
  - Arovi is registered DEMOLISHED, and capture means memory rollback.
  - The weigh strip leads to the verification bay and a temporary acceptance.
  - The missed lower lane leads to the tunnel scan and the transponder fuse, which ends the cab's legal life.
  - The gate refuses them, they take the grade under the wall, and they catch the ferry.
  - Arovi shows four onward roads, and Kaith draws fuel, south, east on the back of the route strip.
- Universe: no LOCKED or CANON entry names these people, this city or its traffic technology, so keep them local. The outline rules out any link to the western-bay adaptive mesh or other crossing systems. The LOCKED personhood-protection rule is met because Arovi chooses the trip and Kaith refuses to give her up. Keep the choice Arovi's.
- Do not flatten Kaith's economy under pressure, the wordless decisive exchange (L199–L215) or the practical ending that explains nothing.

## Dialogue findings

### D1 · Blocking · "That was a compliment" has no referent

- Where: L143–L149, driving away after the verification strip accepts the variance
- Text:
> "That was a compliment," Kaith said.
> Arovi projected the open hand again. This time it rotated palm down over a drawing of the road. Attention ahead.
- Problem: Ground/referent. Kaith's last words to Arovi were an instruction ("Do that too early and they'll see the suspension change. On the line.", L133). Nothing since has been praise, so neither Arovi nor the reader can tell what "that" is. The only other candidate is the city's accepting chord at L137, and nothing on the page presents it as praise. Arovi's palm-down "attention ahead" reply and Kaith's snort depend on a compliment having been paid. The exchange therefore collapses at the first moment the relationship warms.
- Fix: Give Kaith a real compliment about Arovi's timing on the strip, plain and a little grudging. Let Arovi's palm-down sign answer it, and keep the snort and the tourist line. There are two ways to do this. Replace the line with the compliment itself. Or keep "That was a compliment" only after a real compliment that Arovi visibly ignores. If the city's chord was the intended referent, Kaith has to say so. Illustrative only: "You timed that better than I would've."

### D2 · Major · Arovi writes words, undercutting her picture-and-tap language

- Where: L41–L55 (first projection, her name, the registry record); the effects show at L97–L103 and L193–L199
- Text:
> Soft blue light appeared across the dead navigation glass.
> A single word replaced them, square white letters reflected faintly in the windshield.
> A line struck through the number. DEMOLISHED appeared below it.
> Distance meant nothing without a unit. Six seconds? Six light bars?
- Problem: The story's conversation runs on one limit: Arovi can only draw and tap, so Kaith has to read her and sometimes misreads. That limit drives the cargo picture, the lost lower lane, the "six" with no unit and the early arrow. But in the first scene Arovi projects written words twice. After seeing AROVI and DEMOLISHED, a reader will ask at every later picture puzzle why she doesn't just write "6 SEC" or "MOVE JACK", and the signal language she and Kaith build looks self-imposed. The first projection also doesn't say where the light comes from. A reader can take it as the dead navigation unit waking over a wireless link, which the no-radio rule forbids.
- Fix: Keep Arovi wordless. At L41, establish that the light comes from Arovi (the later "cupped hands" at L199 suggests her palm) and falls on the dead glass. Make both words stored images rather than composed text. Her name can be a counter nameplate or service badge that she shows or reproduces. DEMOLISHED can be a registry stamp over the struck chassis number. One clause saying she draws the city's counter pictograms, the kind a service Android shows the public, would also explain why she draws instead of writing. The tap-only passages in the tunnel (L171–L195) are already justified because she can't use light there. Leave them.

### D3 · Minor · Name exchange: one line misfires and the narration contradicts the reply

- Where: L53–L55, after Arovi shows her name and the ferry
- Text:
> "Kaith," she said, tapping her chest once. "You know the destination. Show me why you have to stay invisible."
> Arovi did not answer that question.
- Problem: Ground/uptake. "You know the destination" tells Arovi something she obviously knows. The likely intent is "I've got where we're going, now show me why", but the words don't say it. The narration then says Arovi "did not answer that question" and immediately shows her answering it: she is registered demolished, and being found means rollback. The reader is told a reply failed when it succeeded. Also, "she said" comes straight after two paragraphs in which "she" is Arovi. Her name and the chest tap fix it, but only after a stumble.
- Fix: Replace "You know the destination" with a plain acknowledgment of the ferry picture, or cut it. Cut "Arovi did not answer that question," or change it to what actually happens: she answers with a record, not a story. If the pronoun still wobbles, open the paragraph with Kaith's name in the narration.

### D4 · Minor · "They can hear your make in the signal" carries a key rule unclearly

- Where: L45, Kaith reading Arovi's first picture
- Text:
> "Right. They can hear your make in the signal."
- Problem: Ground/referent. This line carries a rule the whole drive depends on. The city recognizes Arovi by the factory signature in her voice or radio, which is why Kaith can talk freely in a monitored cab and Arovi can't. The picture shows only that the link, the mouth and the mast all flare red. "Hear your make in the signal" is an odd phrase: "make" means manufacturer, and it's unclear which signal is meant. A reader can pass it without taking in the rule.
- Fix: Keep the idea, and keep it as something Kaith already knows. Phrase it so the reader gets "your voice carries a factory code; mine doesn't." Illustrative only: "Right. They'd hear the factory in your voice."

### D5 · Minor · The weigh-strip exchange skips two inferences

- Where: L103–L125, from the first weigh strip to the verification bay
- Text:
> Kaith saw the service bay forty meters beyond the strip. Too late to redistribute anything before the first reading. "Brace."
> Two taps, pause, one. The indicator for the bay changed from red to amber as its entrance approached.
> Kaith understood.
- Problem: "Brace." can mean "do the lean you just drew" or "hold on". The story never shows whether Arovi leaned, so the reader can't tell why the first reading failed when the same move works later (L135). At L125, "Kaith understood" hides the step the reader needs: the stop in the bay is the chance to move the load under a "manual cargo correction" excuse. The red-to-amber indicator doesn't point there.
- Fix: Make "Brace." clearly mean the lean, and show Arovi doing it while the strip still flags them (a better load shape, but not good enough). Replace "Kaith understood" with a clause stating what she realizes. Keep "I know how cargo works" and "I can't move it while driving." Both are good, prickly responses to the pictures.

### D6 · Minor · "Tell me before the split" doesn't say what signal or why

- Where: L165–L177, approaching the glass tunnel
- Text:
> "Fine. Tell me before the split."
> Kaith had expected two and one for the lower turn.
- Problem: Ground/setup. This exchange causes the story's crisis: Kaith misses the lower lane while waiting for a tap she then can't read. But the setup line doesn't say what Arovi should tell her. Kaith then times the cut herself using the freight carrier's cover (L169), so a reader may wonder why she didn't just turn. The go signal is only named after it fails.
- Fix: Make the setup line hand Arovi the go/no-go and name the signal. Ground it in what Arovi can see and Kaith can't: Arovi watches the exterior cameras (L91, L153). Illustrative only: "Fine. You watch the cameras behind us. Two and one when they're off."

### D7 · Minor · A maxim at the near miss

- Where: L237–L241, the first arrows after the fuse is pulled
- Text:
> "Not where the gap is," Kaith said. "Where it's going to be."
- Problem: Uniform fluency under pressure, and an epigram. A van has just passed close enough to comb rain off her mirror and the rear has broken loose. Kaith answers with a balanced antithesis that sounds like coaching advice. The content is needed, because it makes Arovi change her signals at L247. The polish isn't.
- Fix: Let the near miss reach her voice first (a breath, a swear, a fragment). Then give the instruction as a plain complaint about what went wrong, not as a rule. Illustrative only: "Early! That one was already full. Show me before it opens."

### D8 · Minor · Narration labels Arovi's pictures

- Where: L65, L263, L305 (the ferry roads, the grade, the final route)
- Text:
> Choosing, not promising.
> Arovi added the open hand—not certainty. Choice.
> A sequence, not an argument.
- Problem: Three of Arovi's picture replies are followed by a narrator's "X, not Y" label. The label settles the meaning before Kaith reacts, so the reader has nothing left to infer and Kaith's response becomes redundant. In each case the next beat already carries the meaning: "Your choice?" at L67, Kaith rolling onto the grade at L267, the redrawn route strip at L309.
- Fix: Cut the three labels. If a picture then feels too opaque, add a concrete detail to the picture itself, not an interpretation. Keep the lines where Kaith decodes taps (L273, L277). They are her response to Arovi and they work.

## Prose findings

### P1 · Minor · "Not X, Y" reversals in the narration

- Where: L103, L135, L189, L231, L279
- Text:
> The weigh strip did not only total mass. It compared load shape against the declared category.
> not as six centimeters this time but as a smooth transfer
> Not a trap. A safety envelope around a discrepancy.
> The choir did not stop. It changed around her
- Problem: Counting D7 and D8, the story uses the negate-then-assert move about ten times in 3,300 words, twice in one paragraph at L189. Most instances carry information on their own. Together they become the narrator's default way of making a point.
- Fix: Keep the two or three that carry real information (L189's "safety envelope" is the best). Turn the rest into straight statements.

### P2 · Minor · Small staging and wording slips

- Where: L29, L33, L129, L247
- Text:
> She wore a rain coat with the hood drawn forward.
> "You paid." Kaith took the strip.
> To any mass sensor, frantic incompetence. To the axles, a different story.
> Kaith answered by shifting once for understood, twice for repeat.
- Problem: Four small slips:
  - "Rain coat" should be one word.
  - Kaith already has the route strip from the slot at L21, so "took the strip" at L33 either repeats that or confuses the staging.
  - A mass sensor can't perceive "frantic incompetence". The watching cameras can, and the axle reading is itself a mass reading.
  - In dense traffic, "shifting" reads as changing gear, which would change speed.
- Fix: Write "raincoat". At L33, give Kaith a different gesture (she pockets or lifts the strip) or cut it. Change "mass sensor" to "camera". Say exactly what Kaith does to acknowledge a signal (her grip on the wheel, a knock on the shifter).

## Continuity and prompt findings

### C1 · Minor · Midnight to dawn in what reads as one short drive

- Where: L11, L249, L307
- Text:
> At twelve minutes past midnight, the road outside the manual depot changed key.
> Ahead, the floodwall rose black against a whitening sky.
> Dawn put a thin copper edge on the water.
- Problem: The fare asks for departure "now", the ferry is already waiting (L51), and the continuous drive reads as well under an hour. Yet the sky is whitening at the wall and dawn arrives during the crossing. Nothing accounts for four or five hours.
- Fix: Move the opening time to late in the night, or drop the dawn cues. If the time moves, keep the copper light on the water.

### C2 · Minor · Why the accepted variance is suddenly "unresolved"

- Where: L139 and L187, entering the tunnel
- Text:
> "Temporary commercial variance accepted. Proceed to quay verification."
> "Unresolved mass variance. Dynamic cabin reconciliation scheduled. Maintain route."
- Problem: The city accepted the variance pending checks at the quay. Inside the tunnel it calls the same variance unresolved and brings the interior scan forward. Nothing on the page triggers the change. The likely cause is the cab's aborted cut toward the closed, non-commercial lane (L169–L175), but the story never connects the two. The crisis reads as the city simply changing its mind.
- Fix: Add one clause that ties the escalation to the aborted cut. For example, the city sounds a tone when the containment rail rises, or its reconciliation announcement mentions a route deviation. Keep the city's flat voice.

### C3 · Minor · Water infrastructure as setting texture

- Where: L21, L157, L249, L257, L283, L289
- Text:
> Its ink showed the western floodwall freight quay, departure requested now.
> To the left, concrete dropped toward a drainage grade scarcely wider than the cab.
> At the bottom, an old service channel passed beneath the floodwall. Rainwater ran down it in a bright sheet.
- Problem: Policy note for authorized revisions under the style guide's CANON rule "No default water infrastructure". The prompt doesn't ask for flood defenses or drainage. Even so, the destination wall is a floodwall (six mentions), and the climax bypass is a drainage grade and a service channel under it.
- Fix: Rename without changing the staging. The wall can be a quay wall or freight wall. The bypass can be a disused loading grade or a cart cut under it. Keep the steepness, the width, the washout, the low clearance and the rain sheeting down (weather is ordinary). Change all six wall mentions and the three channel mentions in one pass.

## Recurring patterns

- "Not X, Y" reversals and glosses: about 10, at L65, L103, L135, L189 (twice), L231, L241, L263, L279, L305. Covered by D7, D8 and P1. Sweep them together and keep only those that carry information.

- Water infrastructure: "floodwall" ×6, "drainage grade" ×1, "service channel" ×3. See C3.
