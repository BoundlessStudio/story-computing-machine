# Room to Move — provisional direction

Anja, thirty-three, is an audio editor who likes exact timing, competitive squash, and the fuller figure she has maintained for most of a year. She enjoys choosing how she looks; this is not a journey toward discovering an authentic fixed body. The story follows one ordinary working day in close third person, from getting ready to the late-night aftermath of her first proper date with Kit, her thirty-six-year-old squash rival and longstanding friend.

In the morning she deliberately reduces breast volume and weight for the match. The physiological relief, fluid redistribution, warm-up, clothing adjustment, and effect on her game make the adaptation practical. Kit has returned from touring sound work and asked her out. Attraction interrupts her sporting suppression and costs a point; consciously readjusting helps her recover her play. They already enjoy each other, but his unilateral changes of plan have previously irritated her. A brief squash-court joke about men's less cooperative public growth can season this scene; it is not a second plot.

At work Anja needs to finish a recording to deadline. Her chosen fullness returns as the fast sporting reduction wears off; her long-settled hips do not instantly shrink with her chest. Adaptive workwear makes most changes ordinary, until a small fastening fault consumes time she meant to spend on the edit. The deadline is real, and she mutes messages to finish. Her work should also show appetite and judgment: she can preserve an untidy human sound when it improves the recording. She is not rigid in every domain waiting for a man to teach her spontaneity.

She collects a beautiful evening garment fitted for the fuller shape she deliberately wants tonight. Adjustable panels have their own aesthetic compromises; she loves a precise old-fashioned line and knowingly accepts its smaller margin, expecting a seated dinner. The fitting gives fashion its own pleasure rather than treating clothes as camouflage. She goes to the date dressed to enjoy both herself and Kit's attention.

They agreed on a seven-o'clock restaurant table. Kit buys seven-o'clock music seats and moves their existing reservation to nine before sending his afternoon notice, presuming she will enjoy the surprise. He has not asked for an answer; the breach exists whether she reads his message or not. Their released seven-o'clock table has been reallocated by the time she answers. He drops the concert and meets her at the originally agreed time, but they must find a walk-in meal. She plainly requires him to ask before changing a shared plan. Giving up the seats does not cancel that requirement.

Walking and standing for the fallback meal, and attraction to Kit, push her chosen full figure beyond the close-cut garment's margin. He reflexively suggests draining as she did for squash. She wants to enjoy this body and this attraction, not spend the date holding a sporting reduction. Her fashion gamble is her own separate pleasure and cost, not a share of blame for his booking change. She releases or repairs the garment and chooses movement over the perfect line. Neither has become perfectly compatible. She deliberately invites him home. Desired intimacy is present, with clear adult choice, then fades to black.

The final movement stays with that night's aftermath. Anja enjoys being desired, keeps the shape she likes, and accepts a specific further piece of time with the inconvenient man she has chosen: breakfast at eight at her flat, before she leaves for a recording session. He asks instead of announcing it. End in their physical, ordinary company; do not turn the garment into an explained relationship metaphor or claim hormones have identified her true match. The provisional title may change.
