# Room to Move — provisional direction

Anja, thirty-three, is an audio editor who likes exact timing, competitive squash, and the fuller figure she has maintained for most of a year. She enjoys choosing how she looks; this is not a journey toward discovering an authentic fixed body. The story follows one ordinary working day in close third person, from getting ready to the late-night aftermath of her first proper date with Kit, her thirty-six-year-old squash rival and longstanding friend.

In the morning she deliberately reduces breast volume and weight for the match. The physiological relief, fluid redistribution, warm-up, clothing adjustment, and effect on her game make the adaptation practical. Kit has returned from touring work and asked her out. They already enjoy each other, but his improvisation and unreliable scheduling have previously irritated her; attraction does not dissolve that friction. Their match allows teasing, competitiveness, and a shared joke about men's less cooperative public growth and emergency cover.

At work Anja needs to finish a recording to deadline. Her chosen fullness returns as the fast sporting reduction wears off; her long-settled hips do not instantly shrink with her chest. Adaptive workwear makes most changes ordinary, until a small fastening fault consumes time she meant to spend on the edit. An attractive recorded voice or a minor flirtation can cause a bodily response without becoming a romantic revelation. She and a colleague treat wardrobe failures as the familiar nuisance men and women share, not a new scandal. She mutes Kit's distracting messages to finish the job.

She collects a beautiful evening garment fitted for the fuller shape she deliberately wants tonight. Adjustable panels have their own aesthetic compromises; she is tempted by a precise old-fashioned line and accepts its smaller margin. The fitting gives fashion its own pleasure rather than treating clothes as camouflage. She goes to the date dressed to enjoy both herself and Kit's attention.

The evening exceeds the garment's allowance and the plan's allowance. Both adults suffer small, funny failures of composure or clothing; their existing competitiveness helps and hurts. Kit must answer for an actual scheduling failure instead of winning her by tactful compliments. Anja can be bodily drawn to him and still annoyed. Once they repair the human misunderstanding, she deliberately invites him home. Desired intimacy is present, with clear adult choice, then fades to black.

The final movement stays with that night's aftermath. Anja enjoys being desired, keeps the shape she likes, and negotiates something specific with the inconvenient man she has chosen rather than accepting a promise of perfect compatibility. A small alteration to tomorrow's plan carries the cost and pleasure of making room for another person. End in their physical, ordinary company, not an explanation of the biology or a claim that hormones have identified her true match.
