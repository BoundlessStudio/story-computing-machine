The writer's opening possibility: "the power she has prepared to inherit is also a place to live." An early arrival steals expected years on earth and brings a predecessor who needs relief. The coordinator asked the writer to preserve trained competence, make the predecessor a person, and test whether the heroine can genuinely decline rather than being pushed into a decorative sacrifice. The provisional outline and subsequent reader conversations follow below as they occur.

Independent outline effects, before discussion: Codex wanted the encounter with the hungry predecessor and the unfinished robe, but found that the outline establishes "the availability of refusal more clearly than the reason for choosing inheritance." It asked what makes taking the moon worth the years to this particular woman, and objected that "a journey with Veya cannot be the journey Nimare wanted with her mother." Claude independently protected "the robe fitted with room for thirty years," Veya's hunger, and survivable refusal. Its principal concern was "two wants, not a want against a duty"; Veya also lacked a costly choice, Odel's actions were all service, and the proposed letting-out of a garment already cut for an older body needed physical clarification. Claude called the impact's arbitrariness mostly taste and warned against briefing-shaped exposition. These are concerns about a provisional direction, not judgments of prose that does not yet exist.

The outline exchange sharpened rather than voted on these concerns. Codex challenged Claude's suggestion that inward tension or sacrifice itself weakens agency: "A temporary interval in which two people honestly permit refusal could be charged and distinctive." Claude challenged the language of learning a reason to accept: "I'd rather the reason come from what her hands do." The writer agreed the hinge was missing and proposed a long-standing bodily fascination with lunar terrain, made experiential through the spindle, so "acceptance then means choosing one desired life at the cost of another." Veya would initially prefer dying in the familiar lunar chamber to returning to an earth where her people are gone; accepting an offered exit would relinquish both her home and control over continuation. The substitute journey was cut. The coordinator accepted intelligible limits on the spindle hold while declining to demand a mechanical rule inventory or an artificial countdown. The unfinished earthly journey remains a loss.

Both readers tested the changed direction. Codex found that Veya's prior descent and Nimare's decision now belonged to separate people; it warned against lunar exhilaration making earth seem small. Claude withdrew its earlier opposition between sacrifice and authored choice and its appetite misreading, then raised a concrete staging question: "where is Odel during the interval?" The writer accepted it and put Odel on the terrace from the climb, bringing sewing kit and fruit. Codex agreed this made wish and touch physically available while the choice remained open. The robe now has generous allowances basted to Nimare's present shape; final sewing, not unexplained letting-out, makes it ready tonight. The coordinator authorized drafting because the remaining risk is the execution of two living desires, with neither a forced blessing nor a trick erasing the loss.
