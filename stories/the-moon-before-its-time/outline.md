# Provisional direction

Close third person, compact and intimate, following Nimare, an adult Moon Priestess. Her mother, Odel, is fitting the blue and teal inheritance robe with room for the thirty years Nimare still expects to live on earth. Nimare is good at the work she has trained for and wants it; she also wants those years, including the journey she and her mother have never taken.

The living bearer within the moon, Veya, has been hurt by a celestial impact. She brings the moon down thirty years before the thousand-year return because she cannot maintain its living light much longer. At the jungle temple's upper terrace, Nimare meets a person rather than an immaculate office: hungry, frightened, and reluctant to admit that she wants to come home. The luminous spindle is a tool Nimare has practiced with all her life; her trained hands let her steady Veya long enough for an actual decision.

There is a legitimate refusal. Nimare can use the departure working to release Veya without replacing her. The moon will rise as ordinary stone, its local magic and living light ending; people will continue living, and Veya can spend her remaining mortal days below. This is a real loss, visible in the moon-dependent night flowers closing, not an apocalypse or a threat to punish Nimare. Veya will help with either working. She must have a desire and a recognizable choice of her own, not exist only to deliver the emergency.

Nimare chooses inheritance, knowing she could stop, and requires Veya to come down to earth first. Odel will receive the returning woman; their planned journey can still happen, with a different companion. This does not compensate Nimare for her own stolen years. Mother and daughter make their hurried farewell while letting out the unfinished robe. Nimare ascends carrying her ordinary appetite and attachment, rather than becoming serene or discovering that she never needed a mortal life. The ending should give the first strange physical sensation of being the moon and one earthly detail she cannot take with her. The exact ending, and the balance between acceptance and refusal, remain open to the outline conversation.

Protect: trained competence, lunar awe, a predecessor allowed to want release, and grief for unspent life. Test: whether choosing inheritance is still too foreclosed, whether the mother becomes merely generous permission, and whether the alternate journey makes an overly consoling substitution.
