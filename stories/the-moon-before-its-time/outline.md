# Provisional direction

Close third person, compact and intimate, following Nimare, an adult Moon Priestess. Odel, her mother, is fitting the blue and teal inheritance robe: generous seam allowances basted to Nimare's present body, with thirty years of alterations expected before the final sewing. They share fruit while discussing their long-postponed journey beyond the jungle. Nimare also wants the moon. She has mapped its terrain by charcoal and practiced the spindle movements since childhood, anticipating the day she can feel that enormous country answer her hands.

The living bearer within the moon, Veya, has been hurt by an offstage celestial impact. She descends thirty years early. Odel climbs with Nimare to the temple's upper terrace, carrying her sewing kit and their remaining fruit; the fitting, wish to stay, and bodily contact continue there while the choice remains open. Nimare's practiced winding establishes a temporary hold: the living light enters her hands and she steadies its vast motion, an exhilarating skilled act rather than a lesson about her calling. While she consciously maintains the pattern, she can hold it for hours, long enough to consider either future; this cannot become a permanent earthbound watch because she must eventually rest. She knows both endings from her training. Unwinding releases the moon as ordinary stone, ending its local magic and living light without threatening ordinary human life. Closing the working binds her to the moon until the next descent. No later loophole reverses this.

Veya has prepared to die in the familiar lunar chamber during the handover. She wants to taste food and stand below again but fears an earth where everyone she knew is gone. Nimare offers to hold the working while Veya comes down first. Veya chooses to step off the moon and surrender her control over whether its living light continues; she will need help on earth and cannot take the mantle back. Odel responds to this returning woman with resentment and practical care of her own choosing. The moon sustains its bearer, but ordinary food and touch have been absent for a thousand years: Veya's appetite preserves personhood and also makes the deprivation plain.

Odel wants Nimare to stay and says so. Their journey remains unspent, with no replacement companion or blessing that resolves the loss. Nimare chooses the wanted lunar life at the cost of the wanted earthly one, after experiencing the work and knowing she could end it. Odel finishes the robe to her daughter's present shape; finishing it is care, not agreement. Their farewell should keep both women's wishes alive. The ending will discover the physical strangeness of Nimare's first ascent while returning to the opening's earthly appetite, without promising her lost years back. The precise last image remains open to the prose.
