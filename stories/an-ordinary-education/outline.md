# Outline

## Story

- Premise and central promise: A nonmagical, laboratory-created, rabbit-eared teenager discovers that admitting she has never skated feels harder than announcing her origins at ordinary school. Deliver the prompt's dry surprise through friendship accommodating her history and her irritating behavior.
- Focal pressure or attachment: Sixteen-year-old Delsie wants a place in Bettina's Friday outing. Her fluent account of amoral scientists repels curiosity; admitting ordinary ignorance feels like auditioning to remain somebody's friend.
- Counterforce or complication: Bettina wants company and a chance to show off, not somebody to hold upright. Delsie's bluff and embarrassment make her friend's clumsy assistance feel like another assessment.
- POV, distance, and information limit: Close third-person past through Delsie. Notice school habits, occupied space, and desired attention. Keep Bettina's intentions observable and occasionally misread. Reveal the laboratory through brief recollections, never a flashback or dossier.
- Governing movement and time shape: One school week: Monday's striking disclosure, compressed lunchtime acquaintance, then an expanded Friday skating session. Move from practiced self-presentation into untidy participation; end during the outing without public demonstration.
- Speculative rule or ordinary-world constraint: In this local society, naturally born demi-humans possess innate magic; Delsie's creators reproduced a living body without that faculty. She grows, tires, and heals ordinarily. Her rabbit ears complicate school headphones but grant neither lie detection nor special balance. No dormant gift arrives.
- Dialogue promise: Blunt teenage curiosity meets overprepared deadpan, then runs into ordinary chatter, showing off, poor explanation, and genuine annoyance. The spectacular answer begins acquaintance rather than finishing a punchline.
- Dialogic medium: Spoken school and rink conversation, interruptions from noise, impatient demonstration, an offered arm, and the difference between being left alone and being given room.
- Dialogue engine: Bettina's invitation elicits Delsie's small false claim of skating experience. Subsequent questions preserve that claim until incompatible expectations threaten their enjoyment.

## Voice

- Narrative texture: Stay close to Delsie's hungry appraisal of ordinary school life. Let confident observations expose what she misunderstands. Use bodily inconvenience and adolescent priorities; avoid a permanent laboratory-metaphor filter.
- Conversational texture: Lunch talk wanders and sometimes goes nowhere. Rink talk is louder, repetitive, badly timed, and interrupted by movement. Neither girl turns every irritation into a polished joke.
- Rhetorical ownership: Delsie owns dramatic understatement and defensive exaggeration, but not social omniscience. Bettina owns enthusiasm, careless familiarity, teasing, and the authority of having been here before. Her affection is impulsive; she sometimes listens badly and sometimes notices what Delsie misses.
- Pressure behavior: Delsie's rehearsed origin story remains fluent while basic requests become evasive. Bettina demonstrates faster when misunderstood, then loses patience. Noise and embarrassment prevent a comprehensive discussion.
- Relationship movement: Delsie wants Bettina's company without pity and cannot ask to be taught. Bettina wants shared enjoyment and cannot easily admit that she wants time for herself. Exchanges replace assumed competence with friendship capable of annoyance and limited help.
- Anti-default: Do not make either girl a precocious counselor, a scientific prodigy, or a uniformly witty survivor. Preserve silly interests, selfish moments, and unequal understanding.

## Beats

1. **Monday, ordinary lunch.** Use the supplied exchange near the opening, with Bettina asking and Delsie delivering the laboratory answer. Establish visible demi-human traits first. Bettina is startled but has other conversational interests. Her skating invitation catches Delsie less prepared than the origin question; Delsie claims prior experience.
2. **Lunchtime acquaintance.** Delsie is unimpressive at science, Bettina loses things, each finds the other funny for reasons unrelated to the laboratory. Delsie extracts rink information indirectly. Establish that she now lives safely with an unnamed guardian; nobody is hunting or reclaiming her.
3. **Friday exposes the bluff.** Rental skates and a busy rink make Delsie's imagined competence useless. Bettina mistakes her hesitation at the barrier for fooling around, then offers help. Delsie accepts an arm while continuing to deny that she needs beginner instruction. Let physical mishaps be awkward and comic, not dangerous.
4. **Assistance becomes irritating.** Bettina wants a circuit with older pupils; Delsie wants her to stay but dismisses each correction. Repeated encouragement recalls being watched and scored in the laboratory. One concrete memory establishes the harm without making the whole rink threatening. Delsie sends Bettina away, then resents her for going. Bettina has reasons to be cross and remains free to enjoy herself.
5. **The private cost becomes visible.** Alone at the barrier, Delsie must choose between preserving her claim and joining an activity she actually wants. Bettina's failed attempt to impress the older pupils punctures Delsie's assumption that her friend is effortlessly at home here. Their next contact permits an admission of inexperience; leave its language and uneven reception to the scene, without reciprocal confessions.
6. **Stay inside the evening.** Another attempt is possible without proving that Delsie is exceptional or declaring her healed. Bettina remains a poor teacher and Delsie remains impatient. Finish amid a harmless tangle and an excessively serious disagreement over whose feet caused it: two participants with an unfinished evening, not an audience applauding inclusion.

## Continuity

- Canon used: Living agency, personhood protection, system plurality, and recurring civilizational forms. Set this society in an unspecified old-magic modernity before the extinction, never the Long Dark.
- Boundaries and unknowns: Establish no connection to existing schools, characters, laboratories, or biological systems. The local birth distinction does not classify all constructed life. The creators' cruelty was real, not a misunderstanding; ordinary pleasure neither excuses it nor cures its effects. No institutional reform, literal godhood, or shared-canon expansion is needed.
