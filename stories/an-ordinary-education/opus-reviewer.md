# Opus review: An Ordinary Education

Independent audit for a later editing pass. It is not a `review.md` verdict,
does not change canon state, and does not authorize edits by itself.

## Verdict

- Dialogue: REVISE. The Ground holds throughout, but the first half of the rink scene falls into claim, denial and topper, and Bettina borrows Delsie's dry register to deliver the toppers.
- Prose: PASS. Concrete and funny with a controlled close third; only a few narration caps and small wording slips.
- Continuity and prompt: PASS. The prompt exchange is verbatim, the lab harm stays non-graphic and the story completes its promise. There is one small staging slip.
- Edit scope: targeted-dialogue
- Existing review.md said: Verdict PASS, "Dialogue: PASS". I agree that no line is incoherent. I disagree that the dialogue needs no work.

This is one of the stronger dialogue stories. I found no incoherent line, no knowledge leak and no reply that fails to follow what the listener heard. The lunch scene handles the prompt's over-articulate line well: the narration explains that Delsie rehearsed it, and Bettina answers with a yogurt pot instead of a speech. The weakness is the shape of the exchanges, not their sense. Nearly every exchange runs as claim, denial and a short corrective last word. Early at the rink, Bettina closes several of these with Delsie-style precision ("You're looking at the turn.", "Well, that was one of them.") and loses the impulsive, digressive voice she has at lunch. The lyric and feet squabbles at the end use the same shape to show a new, playful footing, so the earlier toppers blunt that change. The edit pass should vary three or four early exchanges. Let Bettina gush, explain badly or get distracted, and let some of Delsie's denials go unanswered. Leave the bickering itself, the bench scene and the ending alone.

## Edit guardrails

- Keep the four prompt lines at L19–L25 verbatim and in order. That includes "Wait" with no comma and the word "visit". Bettina asks and Delsie answers. Keep L27 ("She had practiced the sentence...") because it is what makes Delsie's fluency believable.
- Keep close third person on Delsie, past tense, and the British school idiom (trainers, fringe, cross, year above, mark, pupils). The prompt band is 2,500–4,000 words and the prose is about 3,170 now, so edits should stay roughly length-neutral.
- Keep the lab material non-graphic, as the prompt requires: the bell, the broken sleep, "uncooperative" on the chart, the ear scar. Do not expand it or add a second memory.
- Facts to keep: Delsie has no magic and no dormant gift. The lab is closed and two scientists are in prison. She lives with a guardian above a pharmacy and takes two buses to school. She has been out of the lab two years and at this school four days by Thursday. Bettina is sixteen. Delsie's two lies are "Yes" and "Not anymore". Bettina has white skates with red wheels and Delsie wears brown rentals, a green T-shirt and a grip on a yellow sleeve. The song is "stay awake" and Delsie heard it nine times. There are three older girls, one in a blue skirt. The horned boy with green lights shows this is a magic-active modern society, before the extinction per `universe/premise.md` (LOCKED). Nothing in the story conflicts with "Only life can use magic" (`universe/rules.md`, LOCKED). Do not add taxonomy for demi-humans or the magic school.
- The bickering is the friendship. Do not smooth it into supportive or therapeutic talk, make Bettina a counselor, or add any line that states what the evening meant. The story must end on the feet argument and Bettina not listening.

## Dialogue findings

### D1 · Major · Early rink exchanges end on a topper by default

- Where: L105–L183, first half of the rink scene. Mainly the video exchange at L167–L175 and the turn exchange at L179–L183. Optionally the arrival at L105–L109.
- Text:
> “You showed me lots of things.”
> “Well, that was one of them.”
> “I am turning.”
> “You're looking at the turn.”
- Problem: These are last-word lines and correction ping-pong. Each mini-exchange is a Tennis Match of equal one-liners in which Bettina tops Delsie's denial with a neat correction. The Ground holds: "I am turning" is a legible defensive fib. But the turn exchange changes nothing, since they simply get round the bend afterwards. The video exchange spends Bettina's first mention of the girl in blue on a comeback when it could show her admiration for that girl, which is the want that takes her away at L249 and makes her failure at L265 matter. Together with L109 ("Yes, but I meant to be.") and the narration cap at L163 (see P1), the scene sets a formula. The ending's lyric and feet squabbles then repeat that formula when they should mark a change.
- Fix: Rework the video and turn exchanges so they do not end on Bettina scoring a point. In the video exchange, let Bettina run on about the girl in her lunch-table voice, admiring and over-detailed, and let Delsie's "You showed me lots of things" go unanswered or get talked over. Illustrative only: “She does it going backwards as well. Backwards! I'll find it again in a bit.” In the turn exchange, keep "You can't just stop" and "I am turning". Replace the topper with a bad explanation or a demonstration that does not win the argument. Illustrative only: “You sort of lean and your feet just go with it. Watch mine. No, watch mine.” L109 is low priority and can stay. Keep the knees exchange (L153–L161), the walking frame and sleeve (L187–L197) and "Announce it" (L201–L209) as they are. They end on action or on real strain. Do not swap one topper for another.

### D2 · Minor · Two word-corrections by Bettina on the bench

- Where: L343–L347 and L357–L363, bench scene after the confession
- Text:
> “The frames are yellow. The helmet was purple.”
> “You don't have to say obviously. I know now.”
- Problem: Within twenty lines Bettina twice answers Delsie by correcting her wording. That is correction ping-pong inside the reconciliation, and the second is meta-commentary in the house register. Each works alone. The yellow/purple correction is petty and funny, and Delsie's "Whatever." deflates it well. Together they make the renegotiation run on the rhythm this pass is trying to thin out.
- Fix: Keep the yellow/purple correction. Keep the meaning of the second line (Bettina now knows and will not be snapped at) but drop the framing that polices Delsie's word. Illustrative only: “I know. I'm not thick.”

### D3 · Minor · Untagged "need" line reads as Bettina's grievance

- Where: L297–L305, bench, "You could've waved"
- Text:
> “I thought you didn't need me for anything.”
> Bettina frowned. “It was a wave.”
- Problem: Attribution. The line stands in its own paragraph after Delsie's lace beat. Its content belongs more naturally to Bettina, since she was the one told she was not needed at L229–L243. So the reader has to count back to see that Delsie is saying it, as a sulky reason for not waving. Once attributed, the logic works: Delsie projects her own stance onto Bettina, and "It was a wave" deflates it.
- Fix: Attach the line to Delsie's lace paragraph or give it a plain "Delsie said". Keep the wording and Bettina's reply.

### D4 · Minor · "Wait" in the prompt exchange has a thin trigger

- Where: L13–L19, lunch, just before the prompt exchange
- Text:
> Bettina tried it. Delsie laughed with a mouthful of sandwich and got bread on her sleeve.
> Wait why do you visit a normal school instead of a magic school?
- Problem: The prompt's "Wait" is a realization, but the beat before it is Delsie's laugh. Nothing puts Bettina's attention on the ears or on Delsie being a demi-human. The line is also untagged directly after a paragraph about Delsie. Content resolves the speaker within a clause, but this is the story's headline moment and it arrives without a beat.
- Fix: Add one short beat before L19 that turns Bettina's attention to the ears, such as her looking at them after the headphone joke. Leave the four prompt lines untouched. Do not duplicate L29's business with her fingers under her chin.

## Prose findings

### P1 · Minor · Narration caps and glosses after exchanges

- Where: L113 (rink carpet), L163 (after the knees exchange), L355 (bench, shoulder)
- Text:
> On carpet, they were heavy shoes. Heavy shoes were manageable.
> Delsie had seen. Seeing was not the problem.
> Delsie felt an unpleasant pinch of guilt. She had not meant to hurt her.
- Problem: The narrator has its own last-word habit: an aphoristic second sentence that turns the first one around. It also appears at L69 and L251. L163 doubles the topper effect at the end of the knees exchange (D1). L355 labels a feeling that Bettina rubbing her shoulder and Delsie's nod already show.
- Fix: Cut "Seeing was not the problem." and keep "Delsie had seen." Cut "She had not meant to hurt her." and consider trimming the guilt label to the physical beat. L113 is optional. Keep L69, which is the reader's only confirmation that "Yes" was a lie, and keep L251.

### P2 · Minor · Small wording and referent slips

- Where: L211 (the song), L281 (the phone), L399 (the fall)
- Text:
> a singer insisting that tonight was the night. She wished it would be whatever night he wanted so he could stop asking.
> Delsie opened their messages, then closed them.
> She had never heard anyone laugh like that at school.
- Problem: At L211 the singer is "insisting", then the next sentence has him "asking". At L281 "their" has no plural antecedent, and a reader can take it as the thread with Bettina rather than with the guardian. At L399 "at school" sits oddly because the scene is at the rink. The echo of L17 is recoverable but reads like a location slip. Also, at L393, "inconveniently slow" is optional polish ("slowly").
- Fix: Make the verbs agree, either "asking whether tonight was the night" or "stop going on about it". Write "her guardian's messages". Write "from anyone at school" or equivalent.

## Continuity and prompt findings

### C1 · Minor · Bettina squats while Delsie grips her forearm

- Where: L149–L161, first assisted stretch at the barrier
- Text:
> Delsie left her right hand on the rail and took the arm with her left.
> Bettina dropped into a squat and rose again, all while moving.
- Problem: The staging is impossible as written. Delsie is still holding Bettina's forearm, and she holds her sleeve again at L195. A full squat on the move would drag Delsie down with it. Nothing says she let go when her foot veered at L151.
- Fix: Add a clause in which Delsie lets go or Bettina frees her arm for the demonstration, or make the demonstration a deep knee bend with the arm held steady.

2. P1: cut the L163 cap while in the same passage. Then trim the L355 gloss.
3. D2: soften the "obviously" correction on the bench.
4. D3: attribute L301.
5. D4: add the anchoring beat before the prompt line, without touching its wording.
6. C1 and P2: fix the squat staging and sweep the three wording slips.
