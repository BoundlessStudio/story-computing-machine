# Opus review: The City Keeps Score

Independent audit for a later editing pass. It is not a `review.md` verdict,
does not change canon state, and does not authorize edits by itself.

## Verdict

- Dialogue: REVISE — the plan exchange and the hand signal the climax depends on fail Ground, and Raska and Kero argue in the same legalistic register.
- Prose: REVISE — the climax uses a release, rail support and bin that are never set up, so a reader cannot follow the decisive action; the rest is vivid and mostly clean.
- Continuity and prompt: PASS — the prompt is fully delivered and internal facts hold, with two Minor nits (a canal, a stale "three weeks").
- Edit scope: targeted-dialogue
- Existing review.md said: `Verdict: PASS`, `Dialogue: PASS` ("misunderstood signs... have supported uptake"); I disagree. The signal misreading cannot be reconstructed, and Kero's plan line refers to mesh that is not in the scene.

The dialogue is better than the collection's usual run. Sevi has a real voice (rulings in fragments, deflecting jokes about sauce, "you idiot" when it counts), and several exchanges land: "I stopped." / "Yes.", the mark refusal ending in "Correct.", "Full point." / "To my hair." The failures are concentrated. When Raska and Kero argue, both litigate in clipped counter-rulings ("That is not the rule." / "It is exactly the rule.", "You just did." / "I set mine.", "Your mesh missed a stationary wall." / "The wall moved."), so the cover-the-tags test fails between them. At the two moments the climax depends on, the plan and the hand signal, the characters trade quips and corrections instead of carrying information, and the narration contradicts itself about what Kero understood. The game premise does earn score-law pedantry, so the edit pass should leave rulings with Sevi and pride-driven rule-lawyering with Raska. It should give Kero teasing and demonstration instead of counter-rulings, and make the plan and the signal legible before the three notes start.

## Edit guardrails

- Prompt: dystopian police drones that harass, surveil and incapacitate; teens competing in daring drone takedowns. Keep 2,500–4,000 words (currently about 3,080), broadly accessible, stun effects non-graphic, and takedowns vivid but non-instructional: make the climax legible as story staging, not a how-to.
- Craft profile `prospective-2026-08-23` (active). Sevi is fourteen and uses they/them throughout, including in any new lines.
- Internal facts the ending depends on: the three-note warning and blue ring; Raska stunned three weeks before the board scene; board scores KERO 12 1/2, RASKA 11 plus a half; the witness-and-mark rule is Raska's own (L413–L415), and "No mark, no fall" decides the ending; the association counts in order (12/11/18, Sevi to 19, Raska to 13, then 20/14/12); the retrieval timer; the red service handle.
- Setups with payoffs that must survive: the hair lines (L99–L103) pay off when Kero loses a strip of hair (L381, L391); the sauce deflection (L195–L199) pays off at L287; "city furniture" (L47) pays off at L291; the hook dispute (L209–L215) pays off at L445–L451; "Melon assisted" closes the story.
- No universe canon is used; city, state and police institution stay unnamed. Do not add names or institutions.
- Keep the story's structure: three rounds over ten days, most of the length on the last night, ending on an unsettled score rather than a speech.

## Dialogue findings

### D1 · Blocking · Climax hand signal cannot be reconstructed

- Where: L337–L375, south market, Raska under the rail trolley through the second note
- Text:
> She raised both hands and backed beneath the rail trolley.
> Raska showed him two fingers low by her hip: wait.
> Kero read it as two sides. He crossed early.
> but he stayed at the rail support, waiting for the signal she had given him.
> Sevi shouted, "Not two sides! Two beats, you idiot!"
- Problem: Ground/knowledge and continuity at the decisive exchange. The narration glosses the sign as "wait", but Sevi later says it meant "two beats", so the reader is told two different meanings. L351 says Kero misread the sign and acted on it, while L367 has him "waiting for the signal she had given him". That signal was already given and misread, so what is he waiting for? Raska, the POV character, states Kero's private reading ("two sides") as fact, and Sevi somehow knows that exact misreading, though no "two sides" sign has been shown. It is also unclear whether Raska's falling hand (L375) is the go signal or just her freeze breaking. Staging: both hands are raised at L337, yet she signals "low by her hip" with no beat lowering one.
- Fix: Decide what the sign means once, for example "move on the second note". That fits Raska setting the go at the count she can still reach. Gloss it accurately at L347, or not at all, and add a half-clause lowering one hand. At L351, make Kero's misreading an inference from his movement, using a sign the reader can believe exists. Illustrative only: "Kero must have taken it for the flanking sign; he crossed early." At L367, keep "Kero did not come for her", but have him hold at the support watching her hand, not "waiting for the signal she had given him". Then keep Sevi's shout as written: once the sign is legible it works as both correction and go-call. Make L375 read clearly as the go.

### D2 · Blocking · Plan exchange has no plan and a phantom mesh

- Where: L301–L323, south market, after Sevi's confession and before Raska steps into the crossing
- Text:
> "There is no later if that strap goes," Kero said.
> "I mean argue later."
> "Right means my mesh can turn it."
> "Your mesh missed a stationary wall."
> "Walls love you."
- Problem: Ground/referent at an important beat. No mesh exists in this scene. Kero's mesh was at the flyover (L131), and the climax never uses one. Kero is hauling at a shutter while talking about a mesh the reader cannot locate. "Missed a stationary wall" also misdescribes the flyover, where the mesh "missed the drone" (L145), and "Walls love you" has no recoverable meaning beyond sarcasm. The run is a Tennis Match of quips while a drone strains at its straps, and it never tells the reader who will do what with the trolley, the support or the red handle, which is why the climax (P1, D1) is so hard to follow. "Later" / "There is no later..." / "I mean argue later" is correction ping-pong: Kero's reply already shows he understood "argue later", so Raska's clarification corrects a misunderstanding that never happened, and Kero's line is a last-word epigram.
- Fix: Cut Kero's L303 and Raska's clarification; "Later," then pointing at the shutter and trolley is enough. Keep Sevi's correction and hedge ("It climbed right before." / "Twice. Mostly right." / "That would have been useful." / "You were busy asking what I did."). That is the scene's best evidence of Sevi catching what the older two missed. Replace L317–L323 with Kero reacting to "right" by using objects actually present, so roles are assigned: Sevi on the red handle, Kero at the trolley's support, Raska drawing it under. Let Kero demonstrate rather than argue. Illustrative only: Kero slaps the rail support: "Then it comes up under this." If Raska needs a jab, make it about something that happened. Illustrative only: "Don't drop anything on me this time."

### D3 · Major · Opening rules spat is correction ping-pong over a false line

- Where: L37–L45, bread queue, just after the melon drop
- Text:
> He glanced at the awning above the queue, then at Raska.
> Kero arrived without hurrying. "That was an awning drop."
> "There wasn't an awning."
> "That is not the rule."
> "It is exactly the rule."
- Problem: Ground plus Identical Voices. "There wasn't an awning" is literally false: L19 put an awning above the queue. Raska means she didn't use it, but the line misdescribes the scene. The last two lines are pure correction ping-pong about a "rule" the reader has not been given, and either speaker could say either line. This is the first time the reader hears Raska and Kero, and it sets them up as litigators rather than a needling rival and the girl he has been managing. Kero's jab ties to his "Too obvious" look (L19), which is good and should survive.
- Fix: Change Raska's line so it matches the scene (she didn't touch the awning; it was loose canvas). Cut "That is not the rule." / "It is exactly the rule." and let the dispute pass to Sevi's "Half", which already carries the scoring logic. If Kero needs one more beat, make it a tease, not a ruling. Illustrative only: "Looked like one from over there."

### D4 · Major · Challenge exchange answers words nobody said

- Where: L111–L121, the hidden board, Raska announcing her challenge
- Text:
> "You don't set challenges."
> "You just did."
> "I set mine."
- Problem: Ground/uptake and correction ping-pong at a key relationship beat. Raska means that Kero doesn't get to set or veto her challenge. "You just did" only follows if Kero hears her "you" as generic ("people don't set challenges") and catches her in a contradiction, and that reading isn't legible. The reader has to back-parse three clipped lines to find out who can challenge whom. Raska and Kero are again interchangeable.
- Fix: Smallest edit: cut "You don't set challenges." and "You just did." so Kero's "No." is answered by Raska's "I set mine." Then Sevi's "Then find another witness" follows directly: if it's only hers, she can find her own witness. Keep Raska looking at the smudge where Sevi's zero was, and "Fine."; that subtext works.

### D5 · Major · Rack exchange is a Punchline Patch under an armed drone

- Where: L227–L233, south market, Sevi on the delivery rack holding the red handle, ring blue
- Text:
> "Then get off and let go."
> "You always make things sound like one action when they are two."
- Problem: Punchline Patch and uniform fluency under pressure. Kero's reversed instruction exists mainly to set up Sevi's generalising comeback. A fourteen-year-old holding a live incapacitation unit by one handle, with the ring already blue, delivers a composed aphorism about Kero's habits of speech. It is the house voice, and it flattens the fear the scene needs (Sevi's "I can't" at L271 shows they are frightened).
- Fix: Keep Kero's first command and Sevi's "If I let go, it rises." Cut L231–L233. If Sevi says more, let it be over-talk that shows fear and pride, not a ruling on Kero. Illustrative only: "If I let go it goes up and the straps go and I had it lower, I had it—"

### D6 · Minor · Untagged line makes "Then why do we have you?" undecodable

- Where: L77–L87, the hidden board, after Sevi marks the half point
- Text:
> "You don't get style as a number."
> "Then why do we have you?"
- Problem: Attribution plus Ground/referent. By alternation L83 reads as Kero's, which makes Raska's retort to Kero meaningless. The joke only works if Sevi, the judge, says L83 and Raska asks what a judge is for, which also explains Sevi's snort. The reader can't tell.
- Fix: Tag L83 as Sevi's, and point Raska's L81 at Sevi with a glance or turn so "you" clearly means the judge.

### D7 · Minor · "We have one" reads as offering Sevi as bait

- Where: L329–L335, south market, Raska stepping into the crossing
- Text:
> "It needs a nearer subject."
> "We have one." He jerked his chin toward Sevi.
- Problem: Ground/uptake. Read plainly, Kero proposes leaving the drone locked on a fourteen-year-old moments after he "looked sick" about Sevi's nineteen. If he means that Sevi is safely covered behind the post, the line doesn't say so. "Needs" also depends on the unstated plan (D2).
- Fix: Decide what Kero means. If he is choosing Raska's safety over Sevi's, which is a real and ugly version of his calculation, let Raska register it before "Not anymore." If not, have him say Sevi is covered. Once D2 states the plan, "It needs a nearer subject" will make sense.

### D8 · Minor · Narration muddles whose hook is whose

- Where: L203–L215, hidden board and south market arrival
- Text:
> Raska recognized Kero's old challenge sign, though he had drawn the hook badly.
> "That was your hook."
- Problem: Ground/referent. L203 calls the whole sign Kero's, so when Kero says "That was your hook" the reader can't tell whether the sign belongs to Kero, Raska or both. That blurs the mutual misattribution the ending pays off ("That hook looked like his." / "It looked like hers.").
- Fix: At L203, make the triangle-and-hook their shared challenge sign and say the bad hook looked like Kero's. Keep "My hook closes." / "Your hook looks like an injured spoon." as they are.

### D9 · Minor · Narration glosses the subtext right after the line

- Where: L105 (board, hair joke); L185 (flyover, "Yes."); L343 (south market, sleeve)
- Text:
> He meant it as a joke. Raska heard the warning underneath and hated him for putting it there.
> It was the truth, and the bluntness of it hurt worse than pity.
> Then she wanted to hit him for knowing that.
- Problem: Each gloss explains a line that already landed. "I stopped." / "Yes." is the strongest exchange in the story and needs no label. Kero's hair line is his throwaway humour and sets up the payoff; the reader can hear the warning without being told.
- Fix: Trim each gloss to the minimum or cut it. At L105, keep at most Raska's resentment in a few words. At L185, cut the sentence or reduce it to a physical beat. At L343, cut "for knowing that" or the whole sentence; "He did neither." then needs a clearer referent (see P3).

### D10 · Minor · "My first drop" has no stable referent

- Fix: Name the drop. Illustrative only: "The melon one wasn't derivative." Contracting "was not" also takes the line out of the formal register.

## Prose findings

### P1 · Major · Climax machinery is never set up

- Where: L275, L305, L337, L353, L377, south market climax
- Text:
> Raska grabbed a loose parcel bin and rammed it under the rack.
> Kero's shoulder struck the rail support instead of the release.
> Kero threw his weight against the support.
- Problem: Confusing staging at the key beat. "The release" first appears in the sentence where Kero misses it; the reader never learns it exists, where it is, or what freeing the trolley is meant to do to the straps. L353 says that because the drone "snapped right", Kero hit the support instead, but that causal link isn't shown. At L377 his weight on that same support works after all, so it's unclear why the release mattered. The bin at L275 is "rammed under the rack" for no stated reason, and it becomes Sevi's step only by inference. "None of it happened in the right order" is a good choice, but it needs a right order the reader has seen.
- Fix: When Raska surveys the crossing (L235 or L305), add one or two clauses planting the trolley on its rusted rail, the support and whatever holds it, at story level and not as a manual. Pair this with the D2 fix so roles are spoken. At L353, show the drone's swing forcing Kero off his line. At L275, make the bin a step for Sevi in a half-clause.
