# Opus review: All the Ways They Knew Her

Independent audit for a later editing pass. It is not a `review.md` verdict,
does not change canon state, and does not authorize edits by itself.

## Verdict

- Dialogue: REVISE — at the halfway stop a speaker is lost and the steward's "That part." has no recoverable referent. Around that, the protagonist, the steward and the niece all trade the same clipped corrections, and the steward narrates the recognition mechanism as it happens.
- Prose: REVISE — the narrator restates the recognition mechanism (L155) and the reason for the decision (L187) after the scene has already shown both, and closes each with an aphorism.
- Continuity and prompt: PASS — the prompt's promise is delivered and resolved inside the length band. Only small knowledge and staging slips remain.
- Edit scope: targeted-dialogue
- Existing review.md said: Verdict PASS, "Dialogue: PASS"; disagree. It missed the lost speaker and unrecoverable referent at L141–L145, and the retort rhythm at the halfway stop.

Most of this story's talk is work talk: radio calls, warnings, route instructions, and that mostly suits a festival run by stewards. The radio voice, the jar vendor, and the children's scraps of logic ("She has wheels." / "She's with staff.") are good and should stay. The trouble is in the face-to-face exchanges: the halfway stop, the side gap, and the niece's harness. There the late, harried protagonist sounds as composed as the steward. The two of them swap small corrections and comebacks ("The gate was open." / "It was closing."), and a child at a drum lineup talks like a marshal. The halfway stop also plays as a guided tour. The steward lists the three ways people know her, a bystander and two children then show each one on cue, he annotates them, and the narrator sums it all up again. The edit should cut the annotations and retorts, let the protagonist sound hurried rather than exact, and let the niece sound like a child, without adding speeches or thanks.

## Edit guardrails

- Prompt requirements: the city closes the old park road for a summer street festival; she crosses it on skates (rollerblades) every summer; halfway down the avenue she realizes everyone seems to recognize her; the story resolves that promise. The length band is 2,500–4,000 words and the prose is about 3,250 now. Keep it broadly accessible. The active profile is `prospective-2026-08-21`, the last Craft profile line.
- The explanation must stay ordinary and uneven: a radio relay of her clothes and direction, a few people who remember her from past summers, and bystanders copying staff behavior. No fame, no surveillance, no named source. The protagonist, niece, sibling and all places are unnamed, and review.md records no proper nouns. Keep it that way. Adding a name would need an inventory update.
- The plot chain the ending depends on: she enters as the gate narrows after an early noon reset; the road closes section by section behind her; she stops at the halfway post; she refuses the escort because children would copy her; she leaves on foot through a side gap; she arrives late and the niece's drum is on an improvised cord; she fits the clasp; she takes the stall from her sibling without telling anyone what happened. No universe entries bind this story (no universe hits for its terms), and the edit should add none.
- What works: the visual reveal of the road closing in her wake (L111), the fact that the steward's radio jargon is procedural rather than witty ("Held at half." / "Final wheeled traveler is off route at half."), the unexplained "No escort", and the unacknowledged cost at the end (the niece's "You're late.", the sibling's unanswered "What happened?"). Do not add a thank-you, a reconciliation, or a line in which anyone recognizes what she gave up.

## Dialogue findings

### D1 · Blocking · Stroller beat: lost speaker and an unrecoverable "That part."

- Where: L139–L145, halfway stop, the woman with the folded stroller
- Text:
> The woman retreated, tugging the stroller sideways. Her companion asked, "Is that her?"
> "Must be," she said.
> The steward touched his radio. "That part."
- Problem: Attribution and Ground/referent. In this story "she" means the protagonist in almost every sentence, so "'Must be,' she said." reads first as the skater answering a question about herself. That makes no sense, unless it is a wry joke nothing else supports. Only the narration at L155 ("The adult beside the stroller had read the workers.") tells the reader it was the stroller woman. The steward's "That part." has no referent a listener can recover. He means "that's the 'other people see us make room' part of what I just said," but nothing in the words says so. Touching his radio as he says it makes it look like radio talk. The line can only be decoded from the narration that follows, and it is his "see?" last word.
- Fix: Tag the answer to the stroller woman ("the woman said"). Cut "That part." entirely and let the steward go back to his work. The protagonist can register the exchange with a glance or a beat of discomfort, with no spoken gloss.

### D2 · Major · Halfway stop runs on retorts and corrections

- Where: L117–L133, first talk with the steward at the route post
- Text:
> He kept his palm raised toward the crossing while two more people hustled through. "Give me a second."
> "They've had several sections."
> "And I've got this one."
> "Then you entered at the worst possible minute."
> "The gate was open."
> "It was closing. The noon reset went early."
- Problem: Punchline Patch, Ground/referent, and Identical Voices. "They've had several sections." is a clipped retort whose referent ("they", "had") only resolves after the steward's comeback. It exists to set up "And I've got this one." A few lines later the pattern repeats: she protests, he corrects ("The gate was open." / "It was closing."). "The gate was open." also contradicts her own "Last one." (L17) as she slid through the narrowing gate. That can read as defensiveness, but as written it reads as a line built to be corrected. Cover the tags on L119–L133 and the two speakers are interchangeable, both dry, exact and quick. That is wrong for a woman who is late, anxious and baffled.
- Fix: Cut the "several sections" / "I've got this one" pair. Let her press in plain, grounded terms, and let his radio work interrupt instead of his wit. Drop "The gate was open." / "It was closing." or make her protest clearly defensive and have him not bother to argue. Keep "Give me a second.", "I need the east lineup." and the needed explanation that follows. Illustrative only: "They've been saying my jacket on the radio since the gate. What did I do?"

### D3 · Major · The steward explains the recognition, then annotates each example

- Where: L137–L153, halfway stop, after "So everyone was told to look for me."
- Text:
> "The crews were told blue jacket, black shorts, red bag, eastbound. Vendors hear the radios. Other people see us make room."
> The steward glanced toward them. "Some remember you."
- Problem: Explanatory chorus and Philosopher. The steward lays out a neat three-part account of how the crowd knows her. A stroller companion and two children then show up on cue to act out those ways, and he labels them ("That part.", "Some remember you."). The narrator then sums up the taxonomy again at L155 (P1). A steward holding back a crowd with one hand and listening to his earpiece would not stop to tell a stranger that some people remember her. The line is there to finish the story's list. The children's exchange lands better with no comment at all.
- Fix: Keep the first sentence of his answer, the crews' description, because she needs it. Cut "Other people see us make room.", since the stroller beat shows it. Cut "Some remember you." and let the "summer skater" children stand alone, with at most her own brief physical reaction. Coordinate with D1 and P1 so the mechanism is given once in speech and shown once, not explained three times.

### D4 · Major · Correction and last-word rhythm is the story's default small talk

- Where: throughout. L17–L19 (west gate), L79–L81 (cup vendor), L195–L197 (side gap), L283–L289 (niece's strap). D2 covers the halfway instances.
- Text:
> "Last one."
> "Yes. Exactly." He turned his shoulder to the barrier.
> "Wasn't her."
> "Was still six."
> "If you're carrying the skates."
> "That's what getting off means."
- Problem: Identical Voices and Punchline Patch as a pattern. Across the story, exchange after exchange closes with a correction or a capping line. The gate volunteer's "Yes. Exactly." winks at the "last wheels" reveal instead of reacting to a skater slipping past him. The volunteer's "Wasn't her." claims knowledge he has no basis for, and it exists to set up the vendor's tidy "Was still six." At the decision point, "That's what getting off means." corrects the steward's reasonable condition instead of just agreeing. The niece does it too (D5). Separately, each is a small stiffness. Together they give the gate volunteer, the protagonist, the rack volunteer, the vendor, the steward and the child one shared rhythm.
- Fix: Sweep these instances. Where a retort only caps the exchange, delete it or turn it into a plain answer or action: the volunteer just radios; the vendor gets the last grumble with no reply; she says "Yes" or simply takes the skates off. Keep two that reveal character and aren't built as setups: "I know my buckle." (L219, paid off by her doing exactly what he said) and the niece's "I am still." while the drum rocks (L265). Do not replace the cut lines with new quips.

### D5 · Major · The niece talks like a marshal; the last relationship exchange is a run of instructions

- Where: L257–L305, east lineup, fitting the clasp
- Text:
> "We move after this hold."
> "Short webbing," her niece said.
> "Not the long one."
> "Hold the drum level."
> Her niece moved with the others, stopped after two steps, and looked back. "Your shift."
- Problem: Uniform fluency under pressure, Tennis Match, and Identical Voices. A child about to drum in a procession, whose strap is jammed and whose aunt is late, speaks in the adults' clipped jargon ("this hold", "Short webbing") and swaps two- and three-word instructions with her aunt in even alternation (L283–L289). The aunt says only instructions ("Then keep still.", "I need it loose now.", "Hold the drum level.", "Again."). This is the story's last exchange between people who love each other. The unacknowledged cost ("You're late.") works, but nothing else in the scene sounds like this child or this relationship. The outline's "frustration and relief" never reaches the dialogue.
- Fix: Give the niece a child's words and pressure: her own names for the strap, a repeated worry, a flash of blame or excitement, maybe cutting across her aunt once. Give the aunt one line that isn't an instruction, such as a short apology, a reassurance or a joke meant to steady her. No speeches and no thanks. Keep "They tied it wrong.", "I am still." and "You're late." Illustrative only: "The little strap! Not that one, the little one!"

### D6 · Minor · Two women called "she" in the same exchange

- Where: L203 (produce vendor at the side gap) and L291–L293 (testing the clasp)
- Text:
> "Not through my stock." She braced both hands on a tower of shallow produce crates.
> Her niece rolled one shoulder, testing the height.
> "Again," she said.
- Problem: Attribution. The protagonist is "she" throughout, so "She braced" briefly reads as the skater bracing on the crates, until the next line resolves it. "'Again,' she said." directly follows a sentence whose subject is the niece, so it reads as the niece's line. The next sentence ("The child bounced carefully.") shows it was the aunt's.
- Fix: At L203 write "The vendor braced". At L293 name the speaker or give the line an action beat for the aunt.

### D7 · Minor · "Stopped" doesn't fit moving through a gap

- Where: L199, side-gap exit
- Text:
> "I need you stopped until you're through it."
- Problem: Ground/verb fit. She is already stationary, and she has to move to get through the gap. He means "off your wheels" or "not rolling," but "stopped ... until you're through" contradicts itself.
- Fix: Make the instruction literal. Illustrative only: "Walk it. No rolling till you're past the barrier."

### D8 · Minor · "Not while you're pulling" promises a cut that never happens

- Where: L273–L281, loosening the knot
- Text:
> Her niece pulled the drum toward herself. "Can you cut it?"
> "Not while you're pulling."
- Problem: Ground/uptake. The reply implies she has a blade and will cut once the child lets go. She then works the knot loose with the flat of the clasp and never cuts. The listener, and the reader, would expect a knife.
- Fix: Make the reply a plain no that also stops the pulling (she has nothing to cut with), or have her actually cut the cord.

### D9 · Minor · Stall handover is coded past clarity

- Where: L319, family stall
- Text:
> "Small change left tin. Ice under the towels. Don't open the last bottle crate until the front row is gone."
- Problem: "Small change left tin." reads like a missing word or comma rather than hurried speech. "The front row" could mean the front row of bottles or the front row of drummers the sibling is rushing to see.
- Fix: "Small change is in the left tin." Name the front row as bottles, or rephrase so it can't be read as the procession.

## Prose findings

### P1 · Major · Narration explains the mechanism and the decision after the scene has shown them

- Where: L135 (after "last wheels"), L155 (after the children), L187 (before "No escort")
- Text:
> Last wheels. Not a name. A dependency.
> The road offered no single way of knowing.
> The jar vendor had recognized her passage from other summers, perhaps only by the draft under the tablecloth.
> The worker with the cart had matched four plain details to a radio call.
> The clasp in her bag was urgent to her family. It did not grant the road a different sequence.
- Problem: Philosopher in narration. L135 turns the steward's phrase into a fragment aphorism. L155 is a full paragraph restating the taxonomy the steward just gave and the crowd just acted out. Its guesses are stated as facts in close third ("had matched", "had read") when she can only guess. Its timing is off: the vendor spoke before the draft lifted the cloth, so the draft can't be how she knew her. And the cart worker was cued by "Blue jacket," not by four details. L187's reasoning about the children is needed, because she never explains "No escort" aloud, but it ends on two aphoristic sentences that turn a concrete risk into a maxim.
- Fix: At L135, cut "Not a name. A dependency." At L155, cut the paragraph or reduce it to one sentence of what she sees, with guesses marked as guesses. At L187, keep the concrete picture of children copying her and following her gaps, and cut the final two sentences.

### P2 · Minor · "Not X" fragments and glossing tags

- Where: L89 (clothing inventory), L187 (escort), L277 (niece lets go)
- Text:
> Nothing remarkable. Nothing that explained the boy beside a stack of folded chairs
> The escort would not hide her. It would put a bright vest beside her
> That was how her niece handled pressure: not gracefully, not after discussion, but completely once the next task was clear.
- Problem: A small cluster of "not X / Y" constructions and paired fragments. L277 also glosses a line right after it lands: the child letting go already shows her character.
- Fix: Cut L277 or shorten it to the action. Vary the other two. This is low priority, after P1.

### P3 · Minor · Staging and skate slips

- Where: L13 (west gate), L105 (the stop), L171 (panel), L255 and L281 (clasp)
- Text:
> One volunteer had a hand on a waist-high barrier and the other cupped over an earpiece. Beyond him,
> She turned both toes inward. The brake blocks caught.
> People crowded toward the unclosed half.
> The repaired clasp had been replaced by a length of cord tied twice and pulled hard.
> worried the cord backward with the flat end of the new clasp
- Problem: L13 first reads as two volunteers ("one ... the other"), then "him" makes it one. On inline skates a toes-in plow stop works through wheel friction; the heel brake engages by lifting the toe, so "brake blocks caught" is wrong. At L171 "half" collides with "half", the steward's name for the halfway post (L123, L199, L233). L255 says the clasp "had been replaced" when it was never on the drum; the cord is standing in for it. L281 calls it the "new" clasp when everywhere else it is the repaired one.
- Fix: At L13 say "one hand ... the other hand". At L105 describe a plow stop without brake blocks, or a heel-brake stop. At L171 say "the open side". At L255 say "In place of the clasp, a length of cord...". At L281 say "repaired".

## Continuity and prompt findings

### C1 · Minor · Her puzzlement ignores the radio calls she has already heard

- Where: L19–L21, L33, L45, L69 (calls she hears), L89 (her inventory)
- Text:
> The description followed her down the hill, flattened by static.
> "Blue jacket coming," a radio voice crackled from somewhere inside a fabric stall.
> She checked what they could see. Blue jacket despite the heat, because its inner pocket zipped.
- Problem: Ground/knowledge. She hears her description at the gate, "Blue jacket" at the cart, and "Blue jacket coming" on a radio. She even guesses at L45 that the radio call made people careful. Yet at L89 she lists "Blue jacket ... Nothing remarkable" as if the calls never registered, and the reader is well ahead of her. The real mystery the story wants, vendors and children expecting her, gets blurred with one she should already have half solved.
- Fix: At L89, let her acknowledge that the calls explain the crews but not the boy calling "She's nearly here", the vendors or the children, so the puzzle narrows to them. This also sets up a plainer question at the halfway stop (see D2).
