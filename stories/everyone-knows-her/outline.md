# Outline

## Story

- Premise and central promise: Maris Calder has spent fourteen summer festival days skating the closed park road from end to end, valuing the rare pleasure of moving alone through a crowd. This year strangers wave, point, make room, and call her “the skater from the program.” Halfway down the avenue, she brakes beneath the festival’s community-photo exhibit and finds her current face on the program cover, assembled from unrelated residents’ snapshots in which she has appeared year after year. Resolve why apparently everyone recognizes her, how an anonymous private ritual became public material, and what she does now that neither the printed programs nor other people’s memories can simply be recalled.
- Intended reader experience: Begin with speed, sun, music, and the comic pleasure of a practiced skater reading gaps in a dense crowd. Let isolated greetings accumulate into social unease before the reader and Maris can identify their common source. The reveal should feel plausible, intimate, and stranger than a magical answer: many people have retained pieces of a person who never noticed them. End with bounded agency rather than either grateful celebrity or total erasure; Maris can stop the exhibit’s present use of her image, but not make herself unseen in every past photograph.
- Generating force:
  - Mode: Plot-led accumulation and deceleration. Each block supplies a more specific recognition and one repeated clue—the same blue program—while Maris’s uninterrupted run grows harder to preserve. At the midpoint the street mosaic contracts into a quiet, stationary exchange behind the photo awning, where individual images replace the crowd’s apparent single gaze.
  - Focal attachment or contradiction: Maris loves the festival because she can be solitary without being isolated: the road is smooth, traffic-free, and crowded enough that no encounter has to hold her. Repeating that supposedly anonymous route has made her one of its most visible continuities.
  - Plot-led wants and useful limit, if applicable: She immediately wants to complete her annual end-to-end skate without putting a foot down. She also wants to learn why strangers know her and regain some choice over her face, while curiosity about the missing years in her own memory draws her toward the photographs. Her useful limit is momentum: she is superb at reading bodies as openings and hazards, but reflexively treats speech and attention as things to pass. That habit makes her dismiss the first greetings, miss the shared program in people’s hands, and skate deeper into attention she does not understand.
- Counterforce or complication:
  - Source: Thousands of cover programs have already been handed out, and the exhibit contains photographs submitted by many unrelated households. The volunteer coordinator can cover the large panels and halt new distribution, but cannot retrieve copies, alter private albums, or promise that people will stop recognizing Maris. Festival noise and moving bodies also make it difficult for her to ask one stranger a complete question while skating.
  - Credible inside logic, if a person: The unnamed archive volunteer is a countervoice, not an antagonist. The project began as a display of recurring background details in fourteen years of community snapshots; several volunteers chose Maris’s repeated figure for the cover and unsuccessfully tried local skate groups and public posts to identify her. The coordinator believed an unnamed person photographed on a public street could remain a harmless motif, wants to preserve the contributors’ larger histories, and must face that a beautiful pattern can still impose a role on its subject. After Maris objects, the volunteer acts rather than arguing her into consent.
  - Pressure or change created: Maris can leave quickly and recover anonymity off the route, but then the exhibit keeps speaking for her; staying means missing the run she came to make and enduring further recognition while she decides only today’s use. The volunteer can remove her centrality without destroying the many family photographs in which she happens to appear.
- Narrative design:
  - POV, tense, distance, and limitation: Close third person past through Maris. Early narration shares her fast, functional attention—hands, wheels, shoulders, openings—and lets faces become individual only when recognition makes them obstructive. Do not enter strangers’ or the volunteer’s minds; their intentions remain available through words, gestures, submitted captions, and what the volunteer is willing to change.
  - Time shape and compression: One festival afternoon. Render the launch and first two ambiguous greetings in scene, compress several blocks into a tightening sequence of recognitions, then give the midpoint brake, exhibit discovery, and conversation over the contact sheets full scene space. End within the same hour; use photographs for glimpses of prior summers, not dramatized flashbacks.
  - Information and reveal strategy: Let the reader initially share Maris’s explanations: mistaken identity, someone behind her, a familiar vendor, a joke she has missed. Repeat the blue program without showing its cover clearly until a child holds it beside her face. At the exhibit, reveal the ordinary chain completely: residents submitted pictures; the archive team noticed the same skater; last year’s sharp image supplied the cover; today’s audience has just seen it. The source sheets then complicate, rather than reverse, the answer by showing that there was no single watcher and no complete portrait. Supply no supernatural recognition, secret fame, hidden organization, or link to the Rollcaller.
  - Governing movement: Lace; launch; thread gaps; accept one greeting; dismiss another; notice people yielding before she signals; hear the repeated phrase; chase a blue program; overshoot; circle back; brake at the midpoint; meet her printed face; trace fourteen peripheral appearances; challenge the caption; hear the project’s incomplete search; request immediate limits; help cover the central panels; remain to separate today’s decision from any future permission.
- Speculative surplus: none. The recognition, photographs, distribution, and memory are entirely ordinary; the story’s strangeness comes from accumulated social evidence rather than a hidden power.
- Structural distinction: A moving social mosaic that abruptly sheds speed. The first half advances by short encounters across successive street blocks; the second stays behind one canvas awning and enlarges still photographs one by one. The decisive action is an interrupted personal ritual and a bounded removal, not public proof, crowd conversion, pursuit, institutional rewrite, or a speech that changes the festival audience.
- Decisive turn, deepening, or recognition: The program caption describes Maris as the rider who “never stops.” In the uncropped submissions, she finds herself stopped often: retightening a wheel, eating on a curb, waiting for a fallen child to stand, laughing at something outside the frame. She remembers some moments and not others, and none yields the heroic continuity the cover invented. She recognizes that the archive has evidence about her without possessing her experience. Instead of either validating its story or demanding that every contributor erase a family photograph, she asks for the cover stack to stop circulating, the large crops to be covered, and the source contacts to be given to her without a release attached. The volunteer closes the display front and brings out tape and packing paper.
- Aftereffect or live uncertainty: Printed programs remain in festivalgoers’ hands, private photographs remain with their owners, and some strangers will recognize Maris after the panels are covered. She has not consented to next year’s exhibit, agreed to become its subject, decided whether she wants copies, or settled what public photography ought to permit in general. She has missed the uninterrupted run for the first time in fourteen summers and chooses to spend the next hour checking captions and contact information rather than trying to recover it.
- Opening / ending relation: Open at the northern barricade with Maris setting both wheels down at once and promising herself that she will not put a foot on pavement until the southern gate. End behind the covered midpoint display after she has deliberately removed both skates because the awning floor is hot and uneven; when the volunteer offers a release form with the contact sheets, she crosses out the permission language, writes only where the archive can reach her, and turns to the first disputed caption. The opening treats stopping as failure; the ending makes stopping the practical condition for a decision she will not let momentum make.

## Beats

1. At the northern barricade, Maris begins her fourteenth annual crossing with the familiar private rule that she will not put a foot down before the far gate. Her attention renders the festival as music, food smoke, shoulders, wheels, and usable gaps.
2. A vendor greets her as if expected. A pair of teenagers wave. Maris answers automatically and assumes each recognition belongs to somebody else, but people begin clearing a path before she signals and several hold the same folded blue program.
3. Successive blocks make the attention specific: someone repeats “the skater from the program”; a child compares the cover to Maris’s face; a stranger asks how many years she has done this. She overshoots the questioner, circles back for the first time, and sees arrows toward the community-photo exhibit at the exact halfway point.
4. Maris brakes under the exhibit awning. The cover enlarges last year’s face and layers it over smaller images of her crossing the same road at different ages. She meets the archive volunteer and learns how unrelated submissions produced the pattern and why today’s crowd recognizes her.
5. Anger first fixes on the idea of one person tracking her. The source sheets remove that simpler target: different families photographed their own children, meals, performances, and reunions, with Maris recurring at the edge. The volunteer admits the team turned that recurrence into a public identity without reaching her.
6. Maris finds the “never stops” caption contradicted by uncropped moments of rest, maintenance, minor help, boredom, and laughter. She separates what the photographs truly show from the story the exhibit assigned, and separates stopping today’s use from trying to own every stranger’s memory.
7. She requests three immediate actions: halt new program distribution, cover the large crops, and provide source contacts without treating her presence as consent. The volunteer complies and retains agency by proposing how to preserve the larger family images while the feature closes.
8. Maris takes off her skates behind the covered display, lets the southern-gate goal go, and begins checking the first caption. Recognition continues outside the awning, but the story stops with her making one bounded decision in her own name rather than resolving the whole public question.
