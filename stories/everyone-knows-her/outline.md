## Beats

1. She enters at the west gate late, the repaired clasp zipped away. As crews receive the early reset call, a marshal sees she is already inside and radios a plain description of the last wheeled traveler heading east.
2. A worker holds a cart, a vendor withdraws fragile jars, and adults draw children inward. The adjustments improve her line but cost others room. She reads the looks as reactions to skates or ordinary familiarity; each section closes behind her, removing easy retreat.
3. Recognition diverges but still drives the same clearance. A vendor remembers her annual draft and alerts the next crew. Children recognize the recurring skater and edge forward; parents know only to restrain imitators. Workers match clothing and direction to radio traffic. Interruptions and narrowing gaps cost her time.
4. Halfway, an incomplete family warning and a safety signal make her brake at the route post. She sees the reset advancing behind her and hears her current description on the radio. The steward gives the bounded explanation: she entered after the reset call; sections held until she passed; performers are already entering ahead. Some people remembered her, most recognized the relay’s moving target, and others copied the response.
5. The steward proposes an escort through the remaining sections. She wants the fastest route, but a conspicuous skate would encourage children to follow and carry her into the tightening procession. The clasp does not justify shifting that risk. She refuses both the traditional finish and a full-procession wait.
6. Through a staff-opened side gap, she dismounts and carries the skates along the rough service verge. The steward reports the wheeled route clear; barriers close without spectacle. Gravel and foot traffic make her late even after the mystery is solved.
7. Her niece has started with an improvised knot. At a stationary hold, the woman installs the clasp amid task-focused frustration and relief. The procession moves, the annual skate stays unfinished, and she goes directly to relieve her sibling at the stall instead of seeking anyone who “knows” her.
