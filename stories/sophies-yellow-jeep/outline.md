# Provisional outline

## Promised experience

Three weeks into Sophie's first solo road trip across Canada, her Jeep breaks down on a quiet country road. She has her toolbox and less mechanical know-how than she counted on. A retired farmer offers her somewhere to work. Her two-day estimate turns into several weeks of friendships, small-town routines, and conversations she didn't know she needed. As the Jeep comes close to running, she has to decide if she's ready to resume her original journey. The gap between “two-day repair” and “several weeks” suggests the pleasure of a town becoming familiar by degrees. I read the tone as warm, observant, and open to embarrassment and comedy. The scale moves between a first crossing of Canada and the close attention of a few weeks in one place. The farmer is a specified connection. The other friendships, her reasons for the trip, and her decision are open.

## Takes considered

- **Straight through.** Begin with the breakdown and follow Sophie into the farmyard and the weekly market. On the afternoon the Jeep can finally run, the two friends she's made want different things from her, and she finds she wants something from them she can't pay back in work.
- **A neighbour watches the yard.** A baker who has watched the retired farmer close his barn wants to see it used again. When the Jeep starts, the baker realizes she wants to see Sophie again and has to decide what to say before the engine warms up.
- **Begin with the seat empty.** Open on the morning of Sophie's departure and work backward through three Saturdays at a bread stall. The surprise is the first Saturday, when the bread seller is the person who asks Sophie for help.

The first take gives the weeks a chance to accumulate in Sophie's own time. Its strongest scene puts the last of the repair, the threat of losing the ordinary company she now wants, and the returning appetite for the coast in the same afternoon.

## Provisional direction

Sophie has driven out of Halifax in an old yellow Jeep, bound for Chesterman Beach on Vancouver Island. She letters signs at home and wants to see some of the names at full size. She's been looking forward to driving along Superior almost as much as reaching the coast. On a side road near Desbarats the Jeep overheats. She reckons on two days and a hose. Vern, a retired dairy farmer, offers his shed and room to pitch her tent. She narrows the cooling fault to a stuck thermostat. She forces a seized bolt on its housing, then snaps the hardened extractor in the bolt she's trying to remove. The machine shop wants the cylinder head on its bench and has a wait. Three weeks pass between the breakdown, the shop queue, and the work of taking the engine apart and putting it back together. Sophie and Vern do the work they can together. While the head is out, Sophie starts spending Saturdays at the market with Marla, who sells bread. Vern's morning coffee and the market start dividing her weeks. At the first start, the same joint she broke needs watching. She can imagine the Jeep healthy and herself still here. Vern makes that a real offer. Sophie misses the open road too. She chooses to head west the next morning, with Pancake Bay as her first stop, and asks outright if she can camp at Vern's on the way home.

## Craft plan

**Teller and voice.** Try close third past, with Sophie's sign-maker's eye for the big bright thing everyone else can see from far away and her habit of counting things when she wants to appear sure. Try a first-person Sophie looking back after the trip, with a more expansive comic temper and enough distance to admit what she enjoyed at the time.

**People.** Sophie wants to get herself to the Pacific and hates being watched while she finds out what she can't do. Her quick, exact accounts get longer and less convincing under pressure. Vern wants someone to use the shed well and wants company for the first cup of coffee, even when nobody has a job in there. He can tell a winding story about the price of something thirty years ago. He's generous with his place and easily offended when somebody says a job is beyond him. Marla likes selling bread and wants an hour in which nobody calls for a dozen anything. She talks in brisk quantities and questions until somebody makes her forget the till. She likes Sophie enough to try asking outright for her company and can mistake a stalling answer for a refusal.

**Sophie in the original.** Short black hair falling forward, a mustard tank and pale cutoffs, a woman folded low over iron in grass, a ring spanner raised, an open red toolbox and a doorless yellow Jeep big enough to eat the frame. She braces, leans in, tries the fit, and persists. In a room she would look for where to put the thing she's carrying before saying hello. She pushes her hair back with the clean part of her wrist when caught at the roadside. The tray and spanner come out under the maple as the parts start to spread, and she stays absorbed enough in the work to keep going well past the first mistake. Bring her bent, intent posture back when she sets the spanner down to look for a leak. She can see the roadside grass through the open doorway on her way out. The summer clothes and grease fit the story as pictured.

**Place.** Sussex, New Brunswick, would put the stop too close to Sophie's point of departure. In Minnedosa, Manitoba, the prairie road would exert a different pull. Desbarats puts her in North Channel farm country with the run of Highway 17 beyond the Sault and the lake shore just ahead. The fictional farm has a shallow gravel turnout, a former dairy shed with one good door, and a maple big enough to work under. One of the old milking stalls is where Vern puts things he means to give away. In August, hay cuttings smell sweet at the end of the day, loose gravel rolls underfoot, the shed stays cool until the sun reaches its sill, spanners clack on the steel tray, and the maple shadow moves off the Jeep's yellow wheel. The Saturday market nearby has a covered pavilion and shuts at two. It's a place for Sophie to go when Vern's shed starts to feel smaller than her problem. At first she measures the farm by distance to the highway. By the end she knows who is likely to be awake when she passes. ## Scenes

1. **The turnout, late afternoon, day one.** Sophie wants a little time to make her guess look right. Vern wants the Jeep moved off the curve where the hay wagons come through. A passing tractor buries a dropped socket in road dust. She finally tells him what the gauge did and takes his offer of a place to work. The first close look at the hot yellow Jeep and what was marked for tonight at Pancake Bay. About 17 percent.
2. **Under the maple and at the shed door, day two.** Sophie snaps the thermostat housing bolt and then the extractor, trying to finish while the repair still looks like the job she promised. She is bent over the hole when Vern asks about coffee. He wants her to step away long enough to look at the damage. She shows him the little broken spiral and makes the call to the shop herself. When the shop asks for the head, she needs Vern and his hoist. She marks the pushrods' places on a scrap of cardboard and makes room to work beside him. About 23 percent.
3. **Johnson Farmers' Market, near closing on the second Saturday.** Sophie came with Vern the previous week and brought Marla a hand-lettered price card. This time she sees Marla trying to grab a bite between customers and steps up to bag loaves without waiting to be asked. Then the pavilion empties and Marla still wants Sophie there. She splits a heel with her and tells her about where she goes when she has a free afternoon and wants to see the water without seeing anyone she knows. Sophie finds herself counting how many times they might get to do this. About 15 percent.
4. **The shed and maple, late afternoon after three weeks. Long scene.** Sophie sees a trace of coolant at the housing where she broke the bolt. The prospect of a fresh fault is almost welcome. She nearly reaches for the spanner, then wipes the seam and asks Vern to watch and listen with her. The seam stays dry as the Jeep warms. On a brief test loop the needle holds steady over the same rise that stopped her, and she has to make herself turn back at the next junction. Marla drops by after market and asks about breakfast the following Saturday. Vern brings two chairs into the patch where the Jeep has been and offers to leave Sophie's tent where it is for another week. He tells her how he carried his first cup out to the empty barn after the cows went. Give Sophie time to see an ordinary Saturday she could stay and have. She can still feel the pull of the Jeep over the rise when she tells Marla what she will miss and asks where she would stop for her first look at Superior. Her last ask is for a place to pitch the tent on her way back. About 45 percent, including a brief departure the next morning.

Bridge the scenes with the shop queue, the first market morning and Vern coming out with a second cup before either of them has a job in the shed. Leave the pre-trip weeks largely to Sophie's brief memories of driving and the rest of the crossing beyond the last page. On departure the yellow Jeep takes the western turn from the gravel she could barely stand to wait on. The open doorway brings back the sound and air of the road she wanted. Let her enjoy the motion.
