# Provisional outline

## Promised experience

Three weeks into Sophie's first solo road trip across Canada, her Jeep breaks down on a quiet country road. She has her toolbox and less mechanical know-how than she counted on. A retired farmer offers her somewhere to work. Her two-day estimate turns into several weeks of friendships, small-town routines, and conversations she didn't know she needed. As the Jeep comes close to running, she has to decide if she's ready to resume her original journey. The gap between “two-day repair” and “several weeks” suggests the pleasure of a town becoming familiar by degrees. I read the tone as warm, observant, and open to embarrassment and comedy. The scale moves between a first crossing of Canada and the close attention of a few weeks in one place. The farmer is a specified connection. The other friendships, her reasons for the trip, and her decision are open.

## Takes considered

- **Straight through.** Begin with the breakdown and follow Sophie into the farmyard and the weekly market. On the afternoon the Jeep can finally run, the two friends she's made want different things from her, and she finds she wants something from them she can't pay back in work.
- **A neighbour watches the yard.** A baker who has watched the retired farmer close his barn wants to see it used again. When the Jeep starts, the baker realizes she wants to see Sophie again and has to decide what to say before the engine warms up.
- **Begin with the seat empty.** Open on the morning of Sophie's departure and work backward through three Saturdays at a bread stall. The surprise is the first Saturday, when the bread seller is the person who asks Sophie for help.

The first take gives the weeks a chance to accumulate in Sophie's own time. Its strongest scene puts the last of the repair, the threat of losing the ordinary company she now wants, and the returning appetite for the coast in the same afternoon.

## Provisional direction

Sophie has driven out of Halifax in an old yellow Jeep, bound for Chesterman Beach on Vancouver Island. She works in a sign shop at home and has spent years lettering names of places she has never seen. She's been looking forward to driving along Superior almost as much as reaching the coast. On a side road near Desbarats the Jeep overheats. She reckons on two days and a hose. Vern, a retired dairy farmer, offers his shed and room to pitch her tent. A machine shop finds the cylinder head cracked. The repair takes three weeks between waiting for a replacement head and putting things back together. Sophie does the work she can, with Vern as a fallible second pair of hands. While the head is out, a ride to the Saturday market draws Sophie into a friendship with Mavis, who sells bread. Vern's morning coffee and the market start dividing her weeks. She can stay when the Jeep is fixed, and for a while she can imagine how pleasant that would be. Sophie misses the open road too. She chooses to head west the next morning, with Pancake Bay as her first stop, and asks outright if she can come back through Desbarats on the way home.

## Craft plan

**Teller and voice.** Try close third past, with Sophie's sign-maker's eye for the big bright thing everyone else can see from far away and her habit of counting things when she wants to appear sure. Try a first-person Sophie looking back after the trip, with a more expansive comic temper and enough distance to admit what she enjoyed at the time.

**People.** Sophie wants to get herself to the Pacific and hates being watched while she finds out what she can't do. Her quick, exact accounts get longer and less convincing under pressure. Vern wants someone to use the shed well and wants to be asked along for coffee even when the repair gives him no reason to be there. He can tell a winding story about the price of something thirty years ago. He is generous with his place and easily offended when somebody else says a job is beyond him. Mavis likes selling bread and wants an hour in which nobody calls for a dozen anything. She talks in brisk quantities and questions, until somebody says something that makes her forget the till. She asks for company directly and can mistake a stalling answer for a refusal.

**Sophie in the original.** Short black hair falling forward, a mustard tank and pale cutoffs, a woman folded low over iron in grass, a ring spanner raised, an open red toolbox and a doorless yellow Jeep big enough to eat the frame. She braces, leans in, tries the fit, and persists. In a room she would look for where to put the thing she's carrying before saying hello. Keep her absorbed and stubborn even at her most embarrassed. She pushes her hair back with the clean part of her wrist when caught at the roadside. Put the tray and spanner in her hands when she spreads out under the maple while the Jeep is apart. Bring her bent, intent posture back for the first start as she holds still long enough to listen. She can see the roadside grass through the open doorway on her way out. The summer clothes and grease fit the story as pictured.

**Place.** Sussex, New Brunswick, would put the stop too close to this Sophie's point of departure. In Minnedosa, Manitoba, the prairie road would exert a different pull. Desbarats gives her the marked change from North Channel farm country into the run of Highway 17 beyond the Sault and the lake shore she has been waiting to see. The fictional farm has a shallow gravel turnout, a former dairy shed with one good door, and a maple big enough to work under by late afternoon. One of the old milking stalls is where Vern puts things he means to give away. In August, hay cuttings smell sweet at the end of the day, loose gravel rolls underfoot, the shed stays cold until the sun reaches its sill, spanners clack on the steel tray, and the maple shadow moves off the Jeep's yellow wheel. Nearby, the Saturday market at the community centre keeps its own hours, quite apart from what Sophie needs. At first she measures the farm by distance to the highway. By the end she knows who is likely to be awake when she passes. ## Scenes

1. **The turnout, late afternoon, day one.** Sophie wants a little time to make her guess look right. Vern wants the Jeep moved off the curve where the hay wagons come through. When a passing tractor buries a dropped socket in road dust, she asks him to stop a minute and tells him what the gauge did. His offer of a place to work gives her a way to stay on her own terms. The first close look at the hot yellow Jeep and what was marked for tonight at Pancake Bay. About 19 percent.
2. **Johnson Farmers' Market, closing time on the first full Saturday.** Sophie is waiting for word from the shop and tries to give Mavis a price card she has lettered to settle a minor favour. Mavis asks for her help through the last few customers, then wants her to sit and eat the unsellably crooked loaf with her. The sellers pack up around them while Mavis keeps talking long after the question about the Jeep has run out. A slice with half the crust missing between the two chairs. About 21 percent.
3. **The shed and maple, late afternoon of the third week. Long scene.** Sophie is nearly ready to turn the key. She wants Vern to tell her she can trust the Jeep. Vern starts talking about how, after the cows went, he kept carrying his first cup of coffee out to the empty barn. Mavis drops by after market, asks Sophie to breakfast the following Saturday, and takes Sophie's postponements for a polite brush-off. Sophie briefly wishes for a fresh fault to make the choice for her. She asks Vern to listen with her through the first start and takes the short local test drive. Back in the yard she tells Mavis what she will actually miss. With the Jeep still ticking, Sophie asks where Mavis would pull off for the first good look at Superior. Let the three have time to misunderstand one another and to want a little more from the conversation. About 47 percent.
4. **Farm lane to Highway 17, next morning.** The Jeep has cooled and Sophie is ready to drive it. She asks if there'll be room to pitch her tent on the way back, and means to come if there is. She takes the western turn and hears the road through the open doorway again. About 13 percent.

Bridge the scenes with the machine-shop delay, the rides, the second market morning and Vern coming out with a second cup before either of them has a job in the shed. Leave the pre-trip weeks largely to Sophie's brief memories of driving and leave the rest of the crossing beyond the last page. The ending returns to the gravel she could barely stand to wait on and the stretch of Superior she was sorry to miss. Give her room to enjoy the motion before the cut.
