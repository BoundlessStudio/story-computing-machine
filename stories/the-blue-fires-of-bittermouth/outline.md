# The Blue Fires of Bittermouth

## Promised experience

“I don’t see the future, I live it” promises the shock of apparent clairvoyance turning into firsthand knowledge bought with real deaths. The speaker has lived today for three hundred years and needs the listener to understand why they are telling them now. The later directions call for a clean restart, death itself rewinding the world to the previous morning, and repeated deaths experienced on the page deeply enough to feel their accumulated cost. The requested Mature or Explicit intensity comes from the dying body and what the speaker has to do with those memories. “Make the characters young and relatable; make the setting exotic” adds young people with ordinary desires whose home is unlike the reader's. The prompt's exchange suggests urgency with room for a disbelieving laugh. The characters' relationship, the stakes beyond the speaker and whether they get to tomorrow are open. Proposed slug `the-blue-fires-of-bittermouth`.

## Takes considered

- **Rescue in a volcano.** A supposed seer spends three centuries climbing down into the fires below his town, trying to bring his brother home. The central scene is the next morning's confession to his sister once his own deaths have shown him whom he can bring out.
- **The night's last witness.** A trainee court reporter in a city built in a petrified forest hears a condemned man her age testify to two mutually exclusive things she herself did. He dies in both versions, and she has to choose which testimony to get out of the court.
- **Odds on tomorrow.** A young carnival bettor throws a fight he has won in a thousand versions of the night. His best friend wants to know why they've lost their ticket out of a desert fair lit by passing meteor showers, and the answer opens onto the lives the bettor has spent to keep winning.

Bittermouth gives a young man accustomed to making himself useful and impressive a reason to tell his sister what the people around him have never seen. It gives each death a practical consequence in the place where it happens.

## Provisional direction

Tallis, 22, grew up bringing baskets of yellow sulfur out of Bittermouth Crater with his older brother Rian. Merrin, 20, works the freight cage above them and wants their household to stop relying on Rian to settle every argument. A slip before the Tuesday morning Tallis keeps waking into took out the crater's main stair. Rian is pinned below it, out of reach from above. In three hundred years Tallis has gotten most of the crew out in some attempts. He keeps going back down after Rian until the crater kills him. Every death returns him, the people and the world to Tuesday at 6:12 a.m. Tallis keeps only what he can remember living through.

In the last attempts Tallis follows a hammer ringing through the crater wall, falls far enough to hear the crew dismissed as lost and reaches Rian from below at last. The slab pinning Rian is holding up a broken section of the stair. Before he dies, he directs Tallis to the three workers he sheltered under an old weighing shelf, visible only from below. Their way out depends on getting the cage to that shelf from the rim before the gases Tallis died in cut off its approach. At the next wake-up Tallis needs Merrin to believe him enough to move the cage and the team. Giving her the information means telling her exactly what he has been doing, what he saw happen to Rian, and why he wants to bring them all back up alive this time. He goes down past Rian's shelf and takes the others out, using what he saw and felt while dying. He stays alive into the next morning.

## Craft plan

**Teller and voice.** Try Tallis in first person, telling his first death with the fast talk of someone defending an embarrassing decision, and in immediate close third, following his attention as his body fails and he tries to hear his brother. Reserve short sentences for events his mind can't get back ahead of.

**Cast.** Tallis wants to be someone his siblings can brag about. He and Rian had planned to leave the crater for good as soon as they had each got enough put aside, and in three centuries Tallis has learned exactly how their wages run out by supper. He performs confidence through absurd precision and gets sweary and direct when it fails. He thinks getting closer to Rian means he can still save him. Merrin wants to rent a room in town with a door she can lock and to be trusted with choices her brothers make over her head. She thinks Tallis gets his calls from a knack that costs him very little. She calls out a boast by telling the related embarrassing story in full, and becomes very careful with names when frightened. Rian, 24, likes being needed and is sick of it. In his last conversation he wants Tallis to finish a job without him and says it in the language of two people who have borrowed lunch money and carried each other's loads for years. No supplied or established characters.

**Owed conversation.** In the final morning's cage house, Tallis needs Merrin to take the abandoned shelf seriously and let him lead the way to it. She needs him to treat her as Rian's sister too, and hear how her brother will die before she puts workers down there. Let him tell her the order in which he heard the hammer, found Rian and heard the trapped workers answer. The death and reset exchange comes when his usual line about being able to see it won't persuade her.

**Place.** Bittermouth is a town on a volcanic crater's rim, chosen over the iron cliffs of Scald Bank and the open salt flats of the Red Shelf. Ore comes up a wall too steep for ordinary roads, and people have to wait for the freight cage to stop to pass their lunch down to the shelf. On this autumn night, sulfur fires burn blue between yellow crusts. Steam beads on eyelashes, cage wheels squeal overhead, brimstone catches in the throat, and gritty sulfur stains clothes that come back clean in the reset. A baked-rock pocket shields the three from the wind. The ordinary basket stair is gone, and the suspended cage is the only way to lift injured people from the surviving shelf. Its old loading position is visible from below when flame shows through the gap in the rock. Come dawn, the light hides the fires and reveals the true heights between ledges. Bittermouth is invented. Volcanic sulfur mining and the blue fire have physical analogues. ## Scenes and ending

1. **The remnant of the main stair, Wednesday 1:17 a.m. (18%).** Tallis wants Rian to answer. Falling rock crushes his chest against the crater wall. He keeps trying to breathe past broken ribs while a worker underneath pounds something metal, and dies with that sound still going. At 6:12 a.m. Tuesday his uninjured chest rises and Merrin splashes coffee onto the clean shift roster. Her first words are the question he has heard for three hundred years about where he put her charger. She remembers the collapse that came before he woke.
2. **Below the stair, subsequent attempts (23%).** Following the ringing, Tallis falls at 2:54 a.m. and bleeds to death on a ledge, close enough to hear the people he hadn't known were alive. Next time he avoids that fall and reaches Rian from below at 4:09 a.m. Still trying to move the stone, he has to hear Rian out about the three workers. He sees Rian die and turns to see their helmet lights over the weighing shelf. When the wind brings fumes through the gap, Tallis's last breaths burn, and his body fights him for each one. He dies there. His feet remember being unsupported and he flinches at the first draft through the cage house when he wakes again. Compress the intervening centuries into things he realizes he can do from practice, and mistakes he hates knowing he will still make.
3. **The cage house, final Tuesday at 6:12 a.m. (long scene, 35%).** Merrin is used to Tallis talking himself into the center of things. He has walked the routes she can't see from above. He tells her what has been happening and lets her hear the part he wants to rush, Rian trying to get him to leave. The cage motor starts and the first crews below want their supplies. They need to decide whether she will send a crew with him via the old loading shelf. She agrees and takes responsibility for backing his story to the people he needs on the rim.
4. **The working face, Tuesday night into Wednesday daylight (24%).** Tallis needs Merrin to hold their agreed position while he crosses the shelf. She needs him to climb back into the cage when it comes for the others. The fire below lights the shelf he first saw as he died. Tallis hears his brother beyond the fallen stone and passes that turn while Rian is still alive. He reaches the three workers in time, carries the injured into the cage and climbs in with them, slicing a hand on the rock as he comes out. He stays awake past midnight and his last remembered hour at Bittermouth. At 6:13 Wednesday, the rescued names remain marked out and new blood comes through his bandage. Dawn washes the blue fire out of his view. He can see how far down he went and see Merrin coming toward him with a morning's worth of things they both remember.
