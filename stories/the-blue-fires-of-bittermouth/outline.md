# The Blue Fires of Bittermouth

## Promised experience

“I don’t see the future, I live it” promises the shock of apparent clairvoyance turning into firsthand knowledge bought with real deaths. The speaker has lived today for three hundred years and needs the listener to understand why they are telling them now. The later directions call for a clean restart, death itself rewinding the world to the previous morning, and repeated deaths experienced on the page deeply enough to feel their accumulated cost. The requested Mature or Explicit intensity comes from the dying body and what the speaker has to do with those memories. “Make the characters young and relatable; make the setting exotic” adds young people with ordinary desires whose home is unlike the reader's. The prompt's exchange suggests urgency with room for a disbelieving laugh. The characters' relationship, the stakes beyond the speaker and whether they get to tomorrow are open. Proposed slug `the-blue-fires-of-bittermouth`.

## Takes considered

- **Rescue in a volcano.** A supposed seer spends three centuries climbing down into the fires below his town, trying to bring his brother home. The central scene is the next morning's confession to his sister once his own deaths have shown him whom he can bring out.
- **The night's last witness.** A trainee court reporter in a city built in a petrified forest hears a condemned man her age testify to two mutually exclusive things she herself did. He dies in both versions, and she has to choose which testimony to get out of the court.
- **Odds on tomorrow.** A young carnival bettor throws a fight he has won in a thousand versions of the night. His best friend wants to know why they've lost their ticket out of a desert fair lit by passing meteor showers, and the answer opens onto the lives the bettor has spent to keep winning.

Bittermouth gives a young man accustomed to making himself useful and impressive a reason to tell his sister what the people around him have never seen. It gives each death a practical consequence in the place where it happens.

## Provisional direction

Tallis, 22, grew up bringing baskets of yellow sulfur out of Bittermouth Crater with his older brother Rian. Merrin, 20, works the freight cage above them and wants their household to stop relying on Rian to settle every argument. A slip before 6:12 a.m. Tuesday took out the main stair and pinned Rian below it. Every time Tallis dies, he and the rest of the world return to 6:12 a.m. Tuesday. Tallis alone remembers what he lived through and how he died.

At the fork below the break, two hammer blows are Rian's old signal that he needs a hand. Tallis has tried to reach the fork from both sides. He has saved other workers in some attempts, but for three centuries he has let the hammer send him up toward the blind alcove where Rian is pinned. The slab holds up the broken stair. Tallis has seen the stair come down on Rian when he tried to shift the slab. It would crush a rescue party on the shelf below. A fatal fall takes Tallis past his usual turn and lets him hear other workers answering the hammer. On the next attempt he asks Rian about the voices. Before dying, Rian points Tallis toward three workers he sent to shelter under the old weighing shelf. By the light of the blue fire, Tallis sees the bent arm of the old scale at their hiding place. He dies in the fumes before reaching them.

Tallis needs Merrin to get a search crew and cage to the shelf as soon as he wakes. The price of her help is an honest account of how he knows where to go and what passing Rian will cost. On the final descent the same two blows are still coming from the upper branch. He turns down toward the other workers and brings them out using what he lived through. He lets the day continue.

## Craft plan

**Teller and voice.** Try Tallis in first person, telling his first death with the fast talk of someone defending an embarrassing decision, and in immediate close third, following his attention as his body fails and he tries to hear his brother. Reserve short sentences for events his mind can't get back ahead of.

**Cast.** Tallis wants to be someone his siblings can brag about. He and Rian had planned to leave the crater for good as soon as they had each got enough put aside, and in three centuries Tallis has learned exactly how their wages run out by supper. He performs confidence through absurd precision and gets sweary and direct when it fails. He thinks getting closer to Rian means he can still save him. Merrin wants to rent a room in town with a door she can lock and to be trusted with choices her brothers make over her head. She thinks Tallis gets his calls from a knack that costs him very little. She calls out a boast by telling the related embarrassing story in full, and becomes very careful with names when frightened. Rian, 24, likes being needed and is sick of it. In his last conversation he wants Tallis to finish a job without him and says it in the language of two people who have borrowed lunch money and carried each other's loads for years. No supplied or established characters.

**Owed conversation.** In the final morning's cage house, Tallis needs Merrin to take the abandoned shelf seriously and let him lead the way to it. She needs him to treat her as Rian's sister too. She hears his exact forecast of a radio call and still needs reasons to risk sending workers past where Rian is knocking. His usual line about being able to see it gives way to the full exchange from the prompt, with “Because I’ve been stuck on today for three hundred years!” in exasperation. Let him tell her in order how he fell past Rian, heard the answer below, stayed with Rian and died after seeing the shelf.

**Place.** Bittermouth is a town on a volcanic crater's rim, chosen over the iron cliffs of Scald Bank and the open salt flats of the Red Shelf. Ore comes up a wall too steep for ordinary roads, and people have to wait for the freight cage to stop to pass their lunch down to the shelf. In the autumn darkness, sulfur fires burn blue between yellow crusts. Steam beads on eyelashes, cage wheels squeal overhead, brimstone catches in the throat, and gritty sulfur stains clothes that come back clean in the reset. An overhang shields the trapped workers and hides them from the rim. Below the fork, a bent scale arm marks the old weighing shelf. The main stair is gone, and the suspended cage is the only way to lift the injured workers. Dawn hides the fires and reveals how far apart the ledges are. Bittermouth is invented. Volcanic sulfur mining and the blue fire have physical analogues. [Smithsonian Global Volcanism Program, Ijen](https://volcano.si.edu/volcano.cfm?vn=263350) and [field study at Ijen crater](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11780170/)

## Scenes and ending

1. **The broken stair, Wednesday 1:17 a.m. (17%).** At the fork Tallis hears two blows and turns upward as he always has. Falling rock crushes his chest against the wall. He keeps trying to breathe past broken ribs and dies with Rian still knocking. At 6:12 a.m. Tuesday his uninjured chest rises and Merrin splashes coffee onto the clean shift roster. She remembers the collapse that came before he woke. She asks for the charger he took so she can keep her phone going in case Rian calls, and sets three breakfast rolls where they always eat.
2. **Below the stair, subsequent attempts (28%).** At 2:54 a.m., trying to reach Rian up a different path, Tallis falls past his usual turn. He bleeds to death hearing voices below answering the hammer. Next time he takes a known way around the fall and reaches Rian at 4:09 a.m. Let the brothers have a full conversation while Tallis wrestles with the impulse to keep pulling at the rock. For once he asks about the voices and gives Rian time to tell him where he sent them. He sees Rian die and then, by firelight, sees the bent scale arm and three helmet lights below it. The wind brings fumes through the gap and Tallis's last breaths burn. He dies there and wakes flinching at the clean air through the cage house. Compress the other deaths and the years between these attempts. Give one accumulation its particulars. He spent years of mornings on the rim learning card tricks from a worker who offers to teach him the very first shuffle every Tuesday.
3. **The cage house, final Tuesday at 6:12 a.m. (long scene, 32%).** Before the radio sounds, Tallis tells Merrin the upper landing will send up two workers and one has lost a boot. The call comes exactly as he said. Merrin takes his supposed clairvoyance seriously, as she always has, and won't send a search crew past their brother on that claim alone. Tallis tells her what happened, including the part he wants to rush, Rian asking him to leave. The morning search team is waiting to go down to the upper landing, and the cage motor interrupts them as it comes up. Tallis gets Merrin to let him lead this team straight to the lower shelf within the hour. She backs the changed route with the people on the rim and works the cage herself.
4. **The face and the rim, Tuesday morning into Wednesday daylight (23%).** Tallis needs Merrin to hold their agreed cage position. She needs him to climb into it when it comes for the others. At the fork, Rian is still alive and knocking. This time Tallis takes the lower branch. He recognizes the bent scale arm in daylight and reaches the three workers hours before the fumes he died in. He carries the injured into the cage and climbs in with them, slicing a hand on the rock. Carry Tallis to the rim and compress the intervening hours as he stays there while Rian's hammer stops. He stays awake past midnight and his last remembered hour. At 6:13 Wednesday, the rescued names remain marked out and dried blood stains his bandage. Merrin brings three breakfast rolls out of habit, and has to decide what to do with Rian's. The daylight reveals how far down Tallis went for the workers as he looks over the rim for the first time in this new day.
