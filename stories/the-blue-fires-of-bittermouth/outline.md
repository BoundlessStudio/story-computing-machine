# The Blue Fires of Bittermouth

## Promised experience

“I don’t see the future, I live it” promises the shock of apparent clairvoyance turning into firsthand knowledge bought with real deaths. The speaker has lived today for three hundred years and needs the listener to understand why they are telling them now. The user clarified “make him die even when he tries to leave” and wants to see him “trying different ways out and dying over and over.” The place must physically defeat genuine attempts to escape. Each death sends the world back to the previous morning, with the cost felt on the page at the requested Mature or Explicit intensity. Young, relatable characters and an exotic setting are required. The quoted exchange suggests urgency with room for a disbelieving laugh. The relationship, the kind of place and the fate of the others remain choices.

## Takes considered

- **Escape from a volcano.** A supposed seer is trapped inside a crater with his siblings. He dies trying ways out alone and has to tell his sister what happened when he finds a way through the rock their camp sits on.
- **The night's last witness.** A trainee court reporter in a city built in a petrified forest hears a condemned man her age testify to two mutually exclusive things she herself did. He dies in both versions, and she has to choose which testimony to get out of the court.
- **Odds on tomorrow.** A young carnival bettor throws a fight he has won in a thousand versions of the night. His best friend wants to know why they've lost their ticket out of a desert fair lit by passing meteor showers, and the answer opens onto the lives the bettor has spent to keep winning.

Bittermouth makes the remembered deaths discoveries about where and when escape is possible. They change what Tallis risks, what he asks of his sister and what he can do for his brother.

## Provisional direction

Tallis, 22, lives in a sulfur camp on a terrace inside Bittermouth Crater. His sister Merrin, 20, works the freight cage. Before 6:12 a.m. Tuesday an earthquake breaks the road through the saddle, buries the far landing across the crater lake and brings the main stair down on their older brother Rian. Fresh cracks vent gas that eventually reaches the camp. At every death Tallis and everyone else return to that Tuesday morning with the damage already done. Tallis alone remembers each attempt and how he died. He has tried leaving and staying, with help and alone. There have been years when he went down to Rian because every way out he knew was gone anyway.

In one attempt to leave on foot, Tallis tries a shelf below the ruined saddle road after failing to get around it from above. The shelf breaks and drops him beyond the rock that hid the outer mouth of a long-closed freight bore. He sees the uncovered arch before he dies. In another attempt he takes a workboat around the inner bank of a narrow inlet, hoping to find a way onto the old path beyond the buried landing. After what he saw from the road he also wants to see what the inner wall hides. Following the bank brings him within earshot of people below the overhang, answering Rian's familiar two hammer blows. The hull breaks on submerged rock and he drowns. He has tried leaving at different hours, climbing above the cage and waiting for help. He has died in fumes in the cage house, the highest inhabited room inside the bowl.

At the fork beneath the stair, the hammer usually sends Tallis up to Rian's blind alcove. This time he asks Rian about the people he heard. Rian points down to the old weighing shelf where he sent three workers. Two can't walk and the other won't leave them. The slab pinning Rian supports the broken stair. Tallis remembers seeing it come down on Rian when he tried to move it. Tallis stays while Rian dies, then follows his pointing hand. In the blue light he recognizes, beneath the shelf's bent scale arm, the inner door of the freight bore torn open by the quake. He dies in the fumes before reaching it. His view of the outside opening on the road attempt tells him where the passage leads. His boat attempt and the fatal visit to Rian tell him where to get to it from inside.

On the next morning Tallis needs Merrin to bring people down toward the fire while the approach is still clear. She needs to hear what happened, including why her two brothers will be on opposite sides of the fork. He tells the whole story and she gets two searchers and litters into the cage with him. At the fork he takes the low branch while Rian still knocks. With the hands Merrin sent and one worker who can walk, they get the injured through the open bore and put the crater wall between themselves and the gathering fumes. Tallis lives through the night on the outer slope and sees Wednesday morning for the first time.

## Craft plan

**Teller and voice.** Try the opening escape in first person, with Tallis trying to sell the reader on the good sense of his route, and in immediate close third, following his attention as a way out comes into view below him while his footing gives out. Let him solve immediate problems, remember too much and make jokes of the things he hopes are still ordinary at twenty-two.

**Cast.** Tallis wants to get out. Before the quake he and Rian were already saving to leave, and Tallis daydreams about ordering food without counting his coins in the queue. He performs confidence through absurd precision and gets sweary and direct when it fails. Merrin wants a room with a door she can lock and to be trusted with choices her brothers make over her head. She thinks Tallis gets his calls from a knack that costs him very little. She calls out a boast by telling the related embarrassing story in full and becomes very careful with names when frightened. Rian, 24, likes being needed and is sick of it. When Tallis comes back for him he wants Tallis to finish a job without him. He says it in the language of two people who have borrowed lunch money and carried each other's loads for years. No supplied or established characters.

**Owed conversation.** Merrin needs evidence before putting anyone on a cage headed down the bowl. She needs Tallis to treat her as Rian's sister too and admit that in some attempts he meant to leave them both. Tallis wants her to believe that the freight bore is open at both ends and that he knows when they have to move. Use the full quoted exchange here, ending in his exasperated “Because I’ve been stuck on today for three hundred years!” Let him tell the story of the two openings in order, with room for her to ask how Rian dies.

**Place.** Bittermouth is a small camp on an inside terrace above a turquoise volcanic lake, chosen over the sheer iron cliffs of Scald Bank and the open Red Shelf. The broken road is above camp, the ruined landing is across the inlet, and the old freight bore runs through the crater wall under the road. The wall is sheer between the two usual ways out. The cage runs from the working shelves past the upper landing to the cage house at camp, short of the rim. The upper landing is a working stop below camp where the searchers can call the cage up or down. In the autumn darkness sulfur burns blue over yellow crusts. Cage wheels squeal, mineral grit rasps between teeth, steam beads on eyelashes, and heavy air bites when the fresh vents send gas into the bowl. The scale's bent arm is visible by firelight at night and from the lower branch in daylight. At the bore's far end the slope falls away outside the crater. Getting to it means descending while everyone wants to put height between themselves and the lake. Bittermouth is invented. ## Scenes and ending

1. **Two attempts to get out, Wednesday 1:17 and 2:54 a.m. (24%).** Tallis's bag is packed. He's tried climbing over the road's break before. This time he tries the shelf underneath it, hoping it reaches intact ground around the bend. Rock gives and drops him to where he can see the arch from the side. The fall leaves him unable to shift his own weight. Stay with the panic of losing even the parts of his body that can still respond. He dies and wakes whole at 6:12 a.m. Tuesday as Merrin splashes coffee onto the clean shift roster and asks for the charger he took. In the next attempt he takes the workboat along the wall so he can try for the bank beyond the ruined landing. This brings the answering voices within earshot. The boat overturns, leaving him to fight his body's demand to draw breath under the hull. He drowns, and Tuesday morning returns again. A brief lived memory of suffocating beside Merrin high in the cage house establishes what staying in camp costs. Give the years one particular accumulation. Tallis learned all a worker's card tricks while the man still offers him a first lesson every Tuesday.
2. **Rian's fork, Wednesday 4:09 a.m. (24%).** Tallis climbs to Rian at the two blows. He's spent centuries using the knock as a reason to keep going back. Rian wants Tallis to listen long enough to know where the answering workers are. Give them room to talk through Tallis's repeated impulse to move the slab. Tallis hears what Rian needs done with his savings and his lunch money. He sees him die. Looking down where Rian pointed, he can see the old scale and a breached door, from which the trapped workers call. The fumes interrupt his attempt to reach them. Stay with his body as he dies, and with the return to clean air and Rian's untouched breakfast roll.
3. **The cage house, final Tuesday after 6:12 a.m. (long scene, 32%).** Tallis predicts an upper-landing radio call with the implausible detail that one of the two recovered workers lost a boot. It gets Merrin to listen. She wants enough testimony to risk evacuating further down into the crater. Tallis has to tell her how he got his knowledge and that the three hundred years include days he tried to leave without her. The cage motor and waiting search crew press on the exact exchange and the account that follows it. He gets Merrin to send word round the camp and put two people with litters in the first cage with him. They leave within the hour.
4. **The lower shelf and the outer slope, Tuesday morning into Wednesday dawn (20%).** The same two blows come from Rian's alcove. Tallis wants to turn up and hear his brother talk once more. Merrin needs him to take the lower branch and bring the workers to the bore while she brings the following cages down. He recognizes the bent scale arm in daylight. The searchers and the one worker who can walk help him carry the injured through. He cuts a hand helping lift someone through the outer arch. They get clear on the outer slope, with the ridge between them and the fumes collecting in the bowl. They stay alive past midnight and see the haze erase the far saddle. At 6:13 on Wednesday the blood is still dry on Tallis's dressing and Merrin is still carrying the rolls she packed the morning before. There is a third one for the brother who never came out. It is the first Wednesday sunrise Tallis has lived to see.
