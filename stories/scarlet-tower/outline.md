# Provisional outline

## Promised experience

The explicit situation is collective, open rebellion: Scarlet Tower is the first to defy the crown; Lavinia refuses its ultimatum, Dorian summons the Order's battle mages, Ysolith prepares spells unused for centuries, and an encircling army brings siege engines. The final warning makes villages suspected of sheltering a mage answerable for the Tower's resistance. The distinctive pleasure I infer is formidable people making their defiance real under intolerable pressure, with ancient magic answering an army at consequential scale. The threat to the villages should change their choices, rather than decorate the siege. A grave, gathering, ultimately exhilarating register fits that promise and the commanding scarlet figures in the originals. The request fixes no personal relationship, magical system, viewpoint, length, or outcome. General is the initial intensity target, not a limit on the battle's ambition.

## Three takes considered

- **Straight siege, chosen:** Lavinia's night from public refusal to dawn counterstroke. Its central scene is a gate under attack that opens onto the threatened villages, turning the enemy's hostage strategy into a battle on fronts the Tower chooses. All three named figures determine the result.
- **The herald's return:** The royal messenger tells the king how a seemingly compliant Tower made him witness the mobilization and ancient spells. The audience with the king could be powerful, but retrospective testimony would put the promised confrontation at a distance and transfer attention to an invented person.
- **The last company out:** Dorian leads battle mages through the encirclement toward a village while Lavinia and Ysolith's impossible counterstroke unfolds behind him. The field rescue offers bodily immediacy, but the two women risk becoming distant powers supplying his adventure.

## Provisional direction

Choose the straight take because the strongest scene needs the three leaders visibly committing their different powers to the same refusal. They are longstanding colleagues, not relatives or an invented romance. Lavinia has ended the Order's obedience; Dorian wants a defensible military plan; Ysolith wants permission to attempt magic whose old authors were less cautious than their successors.

Before sunset Lavinia refuses the first ultimatum in hearing of both armies. Dorian's raven carries the war summons; resident battle mages muster, while answers come from companies outside the closing ring. Ysolith begins the old spells of the Many Doors, initially intended to bring those companies into the besieged Tower. **One rule:** these spells make the Tower's one open gateway open in several places, and anyone can pass either way.

The messenger's later warning changes the object of their resistance. He brings the army's list of villages to be burned, including places accused on suspicion alone. Lavinia cannot answer by protecting only actual mage refuges. Dorian wants to concentrate the Order before opening further fronts; Ysolith's first impulse is to strike the besiegers so decisively that the king will lose his means of carrying out the threat. Lavinia makes them confront the detachments already posted beyond the siege: victory here would come too late elsewhere. They choose to open the gate in every named village and send the summoned companies to defend them. The Tower becomes the exposed center of their counterattack; an enemy reaching a village doorway can enter it too.

At dawn the king's forces advance both at the Tower and toward village houses. The spell makes those fronts physically present at one gate. Dorian commits his companies instead of keeping a reserve around Lavinia. Ysolith sustains the vast opening with the fierce delight visible in her reference, including when armed men come through it. Lavinia refuses to close an endangered village's doorway to save herself, then uses her own formidable battle magic against the siege engines while Dorian's returning companies take their crews from behind. The attack is broken and the burning parties driven off. No promise that the whole kingdom is won: the first siege and the threatened burnings are defeated on the page. The open ways remain, with armed mages standing among the people the crown had meant to isolate.

## Craft plan

**Teller and trial.** Intended voice: close third through Lavinia, past tense, ceremonious but bodily and impatient with ceremonial excuses. Her thoughts should work on actual people, distances, and orders, not pronounce the moral of rebellion. After the gate, trial this against Dorian's first-person account told shortly after the battle: irritated, partisan, precise about what commanders conceal from their own soldiers. Neither trial will reduce Lavinia to a symbol.

**Lavinia — third original.** The elder woman's level gaze, clasped hands, silver-streaked dark hair, immense gold-edged scarlet mantle and red gems suggest someone who occupies office without needing to advertise strength. She enters, appraises, waits, and commands. Use her clasped hands to hold the herald to his full message; her heavy sculpted shoulders force others to make space at the gate; her gem catches the red light when she steps into the bombardment; disordered silver hair returns after the counterstroke without changing her composure. Her speech is patient and complete, capable of a substantial refusal that names what the king is asking her to do. She wants her colleagues' honest commitment, and privately wants them to stop treating her survival as the Order's purpose. She knows the crown's cruelty but initially overestimates what winning the siege would prevent. Her contradiction is protective authority that still expects to choose other people's dangers.

**Dorian — second original.** The weathered, unsmiling man, severe red coat, gloves, medallion, beads, scroll and hooded raven express practiced, irritated readiness. He measures, sorts, restrains, then releases. The raven's red hood comes off for the summons and its ribbons flash through the dawn fight; beads count the answering companies until he abandons counting to command them; his gloved hand pins the burn list where everyone must see it; his immaculate coat becomes recognizable among the returning fighters. He talks in practical, sometimes long barracks sentences, naming people and objections before orders; nervousness makes him offensively specific. He wants Lavinia to entrust deployment to him and Ysolith to state danger plainly. He knows the roads and available companies; he is wrong that defending the Order requires concentrating it. He will not call a company expendable, though he may have to send it where he cannot follow.

**Ysolith — first original.** Long fair hair, lifted face, closed eyes, open book, broad red sleeves and the great embroidered eye express absorption and exultation, not a timid archivist. She listens, inhales, opens, and dares. Her hair stirs before the first ancient word; sleeves drag across the preparations until she flings her arms wide in the casting; the book's leaves rise in the wind of the opening; the embroidered eye faces the charging soldiers while her own eyes stay shut. She speaks expansively and sensuously about what she has heard in the old language, then abruptly and plainly about danger. She wants Lavinia's leave to use her scholarship at its full reach and Dorian's belief that she can do it; she dislikes being handled as a fragile specialist. She alone knows the paired old spells, but initially mistakes a magnificent victory at the Tower for a sufficient answer to the king. No revealing or violent image details need translation; the casting assignments are editorial inference, not names supplied by the images.

**The conversation owed.** In scene two they must answer, aloud: will they still refuse when the king makes villagers pay, and what will they actually do for those villagers before dawn? Lavinia must ask Dorian to spend the force he has assembled, Dorian must demand genuine command rather than ceremonial consent, and Ysolith must distinguish what she longs to try from what she can promise. The herald gives a continuous, intelligible account of where the burning detachments are and when they move; he is a credible opposing presence, not a fourth principal with a competing redemption story.

**Place.** Scarlet Tower stands on Red Tor above Thornsett, a sheep-market village: invented locations, without shared-universe claims. Choose its broad gate court and adjoining vaulted gate hall over the summit observatory, which separates the leaders from the soldiers, or the buried spell chamber, which turns this into an occult problem. Late autumn, dry cold, afternoon to dawn. Mages still teach apprentices and couriers still arrive while the royal cordon closes. Five anchors: red grit under the door, sheep grease in cloaks brought up from market, the raven's claws clicking on stone, breath catching in cold air, siege wheels complaining below the tor. The western arch has a deep old crack. The steep approach delays engines but leaves the neighboring villages exposed; the great arch allows the decisive transformation into many fronts. By dawn the once-exclusive entrance frames village lanes and soldiers coming home together.

## Scene briefs and ending

1. **The gate court, late afternoon; about 900 words.** Lavinia refuses the original ultimatum before its escort and her Order. Dorian wants permission to mobilize without waiting for unanimity; she gives it and asks for his judgment, not his reassurance. Ysolith wants access to the old gate inscriptions and starts preparing the spells. Only she knows their full reach; the reader learns their simple two-way nature through the initial reinforcement plan. Let people assemble, dispute space, and hear the first engine arrive. Turn: refusal becomes a deployed force. Remember the raven's hood in Dorian's glove.

2. **The gate hall, after dusk; about 1,500 words.** The messenger delivers the final warning while the mobilized mages wait within hearing. Lavinia first needs his exact meaning; then he wants her answer, Dorian wants concentration, and Ysolith wants authorization for a decisive attack. His list and account reveal why breaking the immediate siege is insufficient. Give the three allies room to press their claims and concede what they have been avoiding. Turn: they commit the Many Doors to all threatened villages, accepting a penetrable Tower. Remember Dorian pinning the list under his glove while the book's leaves begin to lift.

3. **The same gate at dawn; the long scene, about 2,100 words.** The named villages appear beyond the arch, including Thornsett's market lane. Dorian demands and receives deployment authority; Ysolith needs the soldiers to hold her physical ground, not praise her feat; Lavinia needs both to leave her unguarded when the village attack intensifies. Give one endangered doorway a sustained, intelligible fight, with other fronts heard and glimpsed rather than toured. Enemy entry makes the open gate dangerous; Lavinia's refusal to close it forces the counterstroke. Dorian's companies move through the village ways and return against the siege train, while Lavinia stops its bombardment and Ysolith keeps the old spells fully alive. Turn: the encirclement and burn offensive break. Allow aftermath within this scene rather than a victory montage.

Summarize the hours of preparation and arriving responses between the first two scenes; leave out court politics, the king's viewpoint, and a history of the ancient war. The ending is the still-open gateway at sunrise: mages in scarlet stand in the threatened villages, and village people cross freely into the Tower while its enemy withdraws. The first closed gate, Dorian's protective concentration, and the king's attempt to turn hospitality into a liability have loaded that image. Lavinia remains at the open entrance to receive the returning companies, visibly the same commanding woman who refused the crown.
