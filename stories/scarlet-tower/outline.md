# Provisional outline

## Promised experience

The request explicitly gives open rebellion, Lavinia's refusal, Dorian summoning battle mages, Ysolith preparing centuries-unused spells, an encircling army with siege engines, and the dawn ultimatum against every village *suspected* of sheltering a mage. I infer the pleasure of formidable people making defiance consequential through ancient magic and military action under intolerable pressure. The village threat must change their choices. A grave, gathering, ultimately exhilarating register fits the words and commanding scarlet figures. No personal relationship, viewpoint, length, magical system, defeat, or victory is prescribed. General limits intensity, not scale.

## Three takes considered

- **Straight siege, chosen:** Lavinia's night from public refusal to a counterstroke fought through village doorways. Its strongest scene makes all three leaders answer the hostage strategy together.
- **The herald's return:** The messenger explains the Tower's impossible resistance to the king afterward. A strong audience scene, but the requested confrontation becomes testimony centered on an invented man.
- **The last company out:** Dorian leads mages through the encirclement to a village while Lavinia and Ysolith act behind him. Immediate field adventure, but the women risk becoming distant powers supplying his story.

## Provisional direction

The three are longstanding colleagues: Lavinia commits the institution, Dorian deploys its force, Ysolith attempts the old Many Doors. **One rule:** these spells open passages between the gate hall's separate blind arch bays and named destinations; anyone can pass either way. Former generations stopped using them because they undo fortified separation. Initially Ysolith prepares to bring reinforcements inside.

Lavinia publicly refuses the first demand; Dorian's raven carries the summons and mages muster. The outer doors are barred, trapping a Thornsett carter who insists on going home.

The final warning changes their objective. The herald names accused villages to intimidate; the carter hears Thornsett. He supplies no deployment chart. Dorian has seen departing detachments and knows the roads: breaking this siege cannot recall their orders. Lavinia genuinely weighs surrendering the Tower and disarming the Order; the herald credibly confirms this cancels today's burnings. Dorian argues that disarmament leaves those same villages without the Order's protection against the next accusation. Lavinia accepts that today's reprieve is real. She chooses to contest the crown's power to burn on suspicion by putting mages in the villages now, acknowledging that she is risking their immediate safety. The carter insists on hearing how she can reach her own lane, without consenting for anyone else. They choose a dangerous defense of every named village, including false suspects. Lavinia grants Dorian deployment authority here.

At dawn the arches make those fronts reachable. Ysolith's transported concentration sustains the ways; Dorian sends defenders through them. Lavinia shields the court while the reserve emerges in Thornsett outside the cordon and advances uphill behind the siege train. Its departure leaves fewer mages holding the lane; the royal attack presses them back and forces the bay. Lavinia refuses closure while villagers and defenders remain beyond it. She redirects her power to their defense and drives the intruders back. The unshielded outer doors are smashed before Dorian's flank silences the engines. The siege and burning offensive are defeated on the page; the wider war remains. Keeping the village ways open afterward is voluntary.

## Craft plan

**Teller and trial.** Close third through Lavinia, past tense: ceremonious, bodily, impatient with ceremonial excuses. Her mind works on people and orders rather than conclusions about rebellion. Test this against Dorian's first-person account shortly after the battle, irritated, partisan, precise about commanders' omissions. Choose after the readers hear both.

**Lavinia — third original.** The elder woman's level gaze, clasped hands, silver-streaked hair, gold-sculpted scarlet mantle and red gems express composed office. She appraises, waits, commands. Her folded hands keep the herald speaking; the immense shoulders make people yield space; the gem catches battle light; silver hair comes loose without undoing her composure. She speaks patiently and completely, with room for a substantial refusal. She wants Dorian's honest judgment and genuine commitment, and Ysolith's distinction between possibility and promise. Protective but controlling, she initially overestimates what victory at the Tower would prevent. She governs the collective choice and fights; her personal rescue is no emotional destination.

**Dorian — second original.** Weathered severity, red coat, gloves, medallion, beads, scroll and hooded raven suggest practiced readiness. He measures, sorts, releases. Unhooding the raven carries the summons; beads count answering companies; his gloved hand pins the burn list; the red coat remains visible directing the counterstroke. His sometimes long barracks sentences name objections before orders; anxiety makes him offensively specific. He wants Lavinia's real trust in deployment and plain danger from Ysolith. He knows roads and companies, but mistakes concentration for the only defense of the Order. He refuses to call troops expendable, though he must commit them where he cannot follow.

**Ysolith — first original.** Fair hair, pointed ear, lifted face, closed eyes, broad sleeves, open book and embroidered eye express devotional rapture and audacity. She listens, inhales, opens, dares. Hair blows back from her ear at casting; sleeves spread with her arms; the book's leaves lift; the embroidered eye faces attackers while her eyes stay shut. Her delight belongs to the old language, not hurting soldiers; her attention still answers the endangered people. Expansive, sensuous speech becomes abruptly plain about danger. She wants Lavinia's authorization at full reach and Dorian's belief, resenting treatment as a fragile scholar. She knows the paired spells, but initially confuses a magnificent local victory with a sufficient answer. No intensity translation is needed. These casting assignments are editorial inference; the images supply no names.

**The conversation owed.** Scene two must address whether to refuse when villagers will pay, what institutional surrender actually offers, and why Lavinia chooses a dangerous defense despite its credible reprieve. Her concern for future protection does not prove her morally flawless. Dorian wants authority, Ysolith permission and trust, Lavinia their unvarnished commitment. The herald has no redemption subplot. The carter wants home, then acts for her neighbors rather than supplying gratitude or collective consent.

**Place.** Invented Scarlet Tower stands on Red Tor above sheep-market Thornsett, without shared-universe claims. Choose the gate court and vaulted hall over the summit observatory, which removes leaders from troops, or the buried spell chamber, which centers an occult problem. Late autumn, dry cold, afternoon to dawn. Teaching and deliveries continue until the cordon closes. Anchors: red grit underfoot, sheep grease in market cloaks, raven claws clicking, cold breath catching, siege wheels complaining uphill. The hall's blind bays face the court; the ordinary outer doors face the approach. Thornsett lies at its foot outside the cordon, with engines between village and Tower. The steep slope delays engines and exposes a flanking advance; the bays permit simultaneous fronts. By dawn the ordinary doors lie smashed; the magical village connections remain open by choice. Cut the unused old crack.

## Scene briefs and ending

1. **Gate court and hall, late afternoon; about 750 words.** Lavinia refuses before the escort and Order. Dorian wants permission to mobilize without waiting for other houses; Ysolith wants access to the gate inscriptions. Lavinia asks for judgment rather than reassurance. Mages assemble and the first engines arrive. Establish the separate bays before a reinforcement preparation demonstrates two-way passage; only Ysolith knows the spells' full reach. The carter's insistence on returning to Thornsett makes barring the outer doors an interruption of someone's life. Turn: defiance becomes mobilization. Remember the raven's hood in Dorian's glove.

2. **Gate hall, after dusk; about 1,700 words.** The herald names targets within the waiting mages' hearing, including the carter when Thornsett is read. His sealed warrant confirms that timely institutional surrender cancels the burnings; Lavinia seriously considers it. Dorian wants concentration and command; Ysolith initially wants a decisive assault. His observed departing columns show why local victory is insufficient. Dorian answers what disarming would leave the villages next time suspicion is enough to threaten them. Ysolith herself commits to reaching every named village and plainly states that the passages admit enemies as readily as defenders: she promises reach, not victory. Lavinia faces today's reprieve and takes responsibility for resistance, without claiming the carter's endorsement. Her insistence on reaching her own lane keeps a particular person present. Turn: Lavinia refuses again, commits the ways to all threatened villages and gives Dorian command. Let the argument inhabit people and choices rather than a sermon or magic lesson. Remember his glove on the list and lifting book leaves.

3. **Same hall and court at dawn; long scene, about 2,100 words.** Establish Thornsett's lane through one recognizable bay; glimpse other fronts without touring them. The carter knows individual neighbors and lane obstacles; Dorian knows the wider roads and deployment. She clears the crossing and guides the company. His deployment tests authority already granted. Lavinia's shielding enables the reserve's exposed uphill flank from Thornsett. That commitment weakens the lane's remaining defense; royal soldiers press the defenders back toward the bay while villagers are still crossing. Ysolith needs defended casting ground, not praise. Lavinia refuses closure and moves her defense to the endangered people, directly fighting the incursion. The outer doors fall before the flank silences the engines. Each leader's power changes what the others can accomplish. Turn: encirclement and burning offensive break. Stay for the immediate aftermath.

Summarize intervening preparations, answering companies and other houses' messages asking what Scarlet has answered while none has openly countermanded the crown. Omit court politics, the king's viewpoint and ancient-war history. At sunrise Ysolith could close the magical bays; the leaders deliberately keep them open. Scarlet defenders stand among the threatened villages, the recognizable carter guides people through, and Lavinia receives returning companies beside the smashed ordinary outer doors. The doors are what was lost; the village ways continue by choice. The initial barring, Dorian's concentration, credible surrender and the king's criminalization of hospitality load that image. Complete this confrontation without declaring the kingdom won.


## Provisional voice trial — after the outline gate

### Opening A

Lavinia had come down in the gold mantle because the king's men must have no excuse to mistake her answer for an outburst by one of the younger scholars. Now its weight pressed the tendons at the base of her neck, and the herald was still reading out her titles.

His breath whitened over the parchment. Behind his escort, the road dropped toward Thornsett between walls of red stone; four teams of oxen were dragging the first siege engine around the lower bend. The wheels complained every time the driver checked them. Above that noise, she could hear the apprentices breathing in the gate hall at her back.

The herald reached the demand for the Order's surrender and looked up. He had a prepared dignity for this moment, his chin raised, the parchment held at the height of his breast. Lavinia kept her hands folded until he lowered it. He was entitled to finish.

“I will not surrender this house or deliver its people to the king's officers,” she said. “Carry my answer as plainly as you carried his command. Scarlet Tower refuses. We will keep our arms, our books, and everyone who has come here for shelter.”

A hand moved behind her. One of the apprentices had begun to applaud. She turned her head far enough to see him stop, his palms still apart, ashamed. She wished he had not been ashamed. He had scarcely been here a month.

Dorian drew close on her right, the raven shifting its claws on his red shoulder.

“The hill companies have an hour of light left. If I wait for the other houses to answer, we'll be bringing them up under the king's bowmen. I want them called now.”

“Yes. Call them in my hearing.”

She wanted the king's escort to hear, too. Dorian unwound the raven's hood strings from his gloved finger. Down on the bend, a wheel struck stone hard enough to jar the whole wooden engine, and the oxen stopped.

### Opening B

The accounts already reaching us have Lavinia alone on the steps, with the royal army spread beneath her. I was beside her. My raven had one foot on her gold mantle, and she pushed his toes off without interrupting the herald. I don't particularly require a place in the accounts, but I object to the deletion of a useful bird. He carried the summons.

There were also six apprentices in the doorway, two battle companies waiting for an order, and a woman from Thornsett who wanted somebody to move the escort's horses so she could get her cart downhill. She had brought fleeces. I remember the smell because my new coat went on smelling of sheep all night.

Below us the king's bowmen were taking the path on the eastern face of Red Tor. They had perhaps forty paces left before the path narrowed. I kept my thumb on the third bead of my command string and watched their captain trying to hurry them without running himself. He knew we could see him.

The herald demanded the surrender of Scarlet Tower and everyone within it.

Lavinia let him lower the parchment.

“We will not surrender the Tower,” she said. “We will not disarm the Order. You may tell the king that everyone in our keeping remains in our keeping, and that I have given you this answer before witnesses.”

One apprentice tried to applaud. The others stared at his hands, which put an end to that. I had picked out the adults who would help carry the ladders when the shooting started; he was not among them.

I told Lavinia I wanted the hill companies called before the bowmen reached the narrows.

“Call them now, Dorian. Let the herald hear.”

I took the raven's hood off. He shook himself, driving both claws through the seam of my shoulder, and cried the Order's war summons into the cold air.

Every window of the Tower answered.
