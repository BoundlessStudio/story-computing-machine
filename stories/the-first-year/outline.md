# The First Year — provisional outline

The promised pleasure is a calendar myth in which twelve animal sovereigns and a living Wheel make a year through their actual, conflicting powers. The tone will be numinous, sharp, and sometimes funny; the scale is a whole country's seasons. The central relationship is the Wheel's contested sovereignty over the twelve, and theirs over it. The anticipated payoff is the first complete circuit: recognizable months made possible by reciprocal rule, with a year that carries the consequences of its earlier months into its later ones.

Explicit requirements: the Wheel of Ages oversees the months, and it both rules and is ruled by the Cat, the Serpent, the Fox, the Boar, the Frog, the Wren, the Bear, the Salmon, the Spider, the Wolf, the Hawk, and the Stag. The user rejected the previous attempt as unaligned and requested a fresh replacement. Inferences: these names suggest distinct, formidable animal powers, a mythic voice, and a meaningful seasonal order; neither a particular order nor a creation story is mandated. This will be the first ordered year of this country, not a claim about the physical world's beginning. There are no supplied images or requested audience; General is the starting intensity target.

Before the months, the twelve have kingdoms of permanent seasons. Their borders are a continual contest: the Frog's rain crosses the Bear's berry country, the Wolf's frost stops the Salmon's return, and the Hawk takes the evening the Fox claims. A living Wheel arrives to end this struggle by making sovereignty successive. Each shall rule the whole country, for a month, and then yield. The twelve consent to a trial circuit because each wants a turn with no rival. The Wheel makes the Cat first; when the Cat tries to keep the longest night, the Wheel rolls and the Serpent brings thaw. Its command is consequential from the start.

The trial circuit develops through several substantial scenes, not twelve separate visits. The Wheel protects each successor by sweeping away the outgoing ruler's work. At first this seems just: the Serpent cannot keep the ground hot in the Fox's month; the Boar cannot leave his uprooting to obstruct the Frog. But the Wren cannot finish nesting in a country stripped of the Boar's branches, and the Bear's feasting has no berries when the Wren's blossoms are swept away. Their appetites and bargains become opposition. The Fox has illicitly carried something from one month into the next; the Spider learns that her thread can preserve a route between them. Their transgressions demonstrate an alternative before anyone argues for it.

The later months turn the opposition into a coalition. The Salmon needs the beginning of his journey to survive into its return; the Wolf finds that a sovereign hunting season cannot feed itself; the Hawk can see the losses left behind the Wheel. The Stag inherits a bare country and refuses to be king of the Wheel's erasures. He drives his antlers through its living rim and stops it. This is an act of rule over the Wheel, with a real cost: no next month can come while he holds it. The Wheel offers to punish the Stag by abolishing his month. The others refuse that victory as well.

The twelve compel a different circuit together. The Spider's spanning thread and the Fox's smuggled survival make a path; the others use their seasonal powers to carry what their successors need through it. The Wheel must accept these inheritances and bear their accumulated weight. In return it holds its indispensable authority: every ruler must yield, including the Stag. They cannot make an endless season; it cannot make an empty succession. The final movement enacts their compact in a flourishing, dangerous, irreversible year. The Cat returns to a country altered by all eleven others. The Wheel bears its first growth ring. Recurrence has made an age possible, not restored the opening.

The specific powers, surviving gifts, and emphasis among the twelve remain discoveries for the prose and outline discussion. Protect the calendar as the story's action; do not let an incidental private crisis or a closing explanation replace the lived first cycle.
