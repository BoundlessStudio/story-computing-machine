# The First Year — stone-table revision direction

The promised pleasure is a dark creation myth in which the familiar year becomes a recurring captivity. The captor is an actual stone table; “Wheel” names the circle of time its exploitation creates. The tone is numinous and cruel, the scale a country's seasons and coming ages, the central relationship enslaver against imprisoned sovereigns. The Fox's private refuge and its sacrifice can carry attachment through the ensemble. The anticipated payoff is Cat's return: resistance wins succession without making the captor benevolent or freeing its prisoners.

Explicit requirements: the Wheel of Ages oversees the months and both rules and is ruled by the Cat, Serpent, Fox, Boar, Frog, Wren, Bear, Salmon, Spider, Wolf, Hawk, and Stag. The user rejected the former story as unaligned and requested a fresh replacement. After endorsing a creation myth, the user directed: “go hard on this and make it evil where the wheel traps this sprit animals creating the 12 months of year”; “i think the wheel should be stone table.”; and “also makes the wheel more metaphorical in circle of time created.” These govern the revision. A stationary circular table, living stone, shadow-roots, unresolved captivity, and particular seasonal domains are interpretations. No audience, graphic gore, or prescribed escape outcome was requested. The complete request and selected contexts remain in prompt.md.

Before months, the twelve living spirits hold neighboring countries of permanent weather and growth. Their shadows connect them to those countries. A living stone table exploits their competing desires, reflecting the kingdoms they want before drawing their shadows into its surface. The captive part becomes each spirit's root. The table breaks Stag's antlers when he tries to bear its weight during the ambush. Its roots remain embedded even when the pressure used to extract a season slackens.

Each forced reign lasts a moon and changes what its successor inherits. Cat's long nights and Serpent's thaw establish duration. Fox's treacherous evenings accompany his failed escape and discovery of a cold blind refuge beneath the edge. Boar splits stone, but biting his exposed root thins his own leg and kills shoots outside. Frog's rain reveals a path beneath the slab; it gives the captives access to one another without providing an exit.

Wren's flowering and coerced song reveal that resonance can briefly lift extracting pressure. Bear's fruiting heat follows, but her attempt to send sleep into the living stone brings retaliation: the table forces Wolf's frost alongside her reign and freezes ripe fruit. Salmon inherits that ice. His upstream return exposes the roots and reverses the pull into their captor.

Spider's dew reveals the roots and mist hides her joining thread. Wolf holds the fissure apart while she works; Hawk finds its missing route beneath the edge. Fox carries the thread through his refuge to Cat, letting living warmth enter the dead crack and costing him his protected place. Shared sensation makes coordinated resistance possible.

At the end of Stag's winter the table refuses succession because the killing cold pleases it. Wren, Bear, and Salmon act together; Serpent's heat meets Stag's inward-directed cold in the damaged stone. Boar's old fissure opens at a renewed bodily cost. Stag's regrown crown and the others bear the broken edge above their shadows. The extracting pressure lifts; winter stops deepening; Stag remains bound. Cat sends night from her own place, making the first return against the captor's will.

The table regrows its grip around Cat's root. Collective resistance restores dawn; it hurts her in reprisal and demands “Again.” The circle is a rhythm wrested from continuing oppression.

This short direction supersedes the literal turning-body conception after the user's later steering. It records the agreed stone-table proposal; the prose remains free to refine its images and contacts. Earlier outlines and their spatial repairs survive in Git and editorial memory, not as instructions for this form.
