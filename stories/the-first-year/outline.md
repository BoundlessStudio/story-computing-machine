# The First Year — replacement provisional outline

The promised pleasure is a dark creation myth: the familiar year revealed as the captivity of twelve formidable spirit animals. The tone is numinous and cruel, the scale a country's seasons and coming ages. The central relationship is enslaver against imprisoned sovereigns. An ensemble narrator can follow the Fox's attempted escape, with the Cat's first and returning reigns bookending it. The anticipated payoff is a completed year whose beauty cannot absolve its making. Resistance wins leverage over the captor without making captivity an agreement.

Explicit requirements: the Wheel of Ages oversees the months and both rules and is ruled by the Cat, Serpent, Fox, Boar, Frog, Wren, Bear, Salmon, Spider, Wolf, Hawk, and Stag. The user rejected the previous story as unaligned, requested a fresh replacement, endorsed a creation myth, and directed: “go hard on this and make it evil where the wheel traps this sprit animals creating the 12 months of year”. The evil Wheel must trap the spirits, and that imprisonment must create the twelve months. Numinous horror, unresolved captivity, the particular seasonal order, and a local rather than universal origin are interpretations. No audience, explicit gore, or particular escape outcome was requested; the complete original words remain in prompt.md.

Provisional direction: before months, the spirits hold neighboring countries of permanent weather, growth, and darkness. Their shadows are living roots connecting them to those countries. The Wheel exploits their competing desires, appearing as an arbiter before closing a circular trap. It swallows their shadows as spokes and breaks the Stag's antlers when he tries to stop it. This is a deliberate ambush, not consent or a well-intentioned reform.

The first forced moon must happen on the page. Cat's long nights cover the whole country while she remains unable to touch it; Serpent's thaw follows. These successive coerced reigns create months. Fox's deceptive evenings allow him to find a blind refuge. Boar opens a wound in the captor, but biting his exposed root thins his own body and kills shoots outside. Frog's rain reveals a circular path between compartments. The failed escapes establish captivity and new possibilities through consequences.

Wren's flowering and forced song can reveal that power sent along a root affects the captor. Bear's fruiting heat follows, but her attempt to send sleep into the Wheel brings retaliation: it pulls Wolf forward beside her and freezes the ripe fruit. Salmon inherits that ice during his returning rivers and spawning; his upstream struggle exposes the buried root junctions. Each month changes what its successor receives.

Spider's dew and mist disclose and conceal a joining thread. Wolf holds the closing wound apart while she works; Hawk's stripping winds accompany the view that reveals the missing span. Fox must carry the thread through his refuge, exposing it and losing his only protected place. Stag's regrown crown can then brace the roots apart. The combined powers compel a turn; the crown prevents another doubled extraction. Their leverage is specific and continuing, not ownership of the Wheel's will.

Cat's return makes the year recur. The Wheel should remain actively evil: it attempts to extend her killing night; collective resistance restores dawn; it hurts her in reprisal. Outside, people name the months and celebrate, among losses the seasons have left behind. Inside, all twelve remain captive. Preserve the Fox's cost and the continuing demand to perform.

This is a condensed record of the proposed direction, not a retrospective prediction. Drafting and reader discussion later refined the single lower gap, normal turning versus abusive hauling, the crown's fastening, and the resisted monthly handoff. Those spatial repairs belong to the finished prose and editorial memory; the outline remains provisional.
