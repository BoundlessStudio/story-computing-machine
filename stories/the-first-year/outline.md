# The First Year — replacement provisional outline

The promised pleasure is now a dark creation myth: the apparently natural year revealed as an evil creature's captivity of twelve formidable spirit animals. The scale is a country's seasons and the coming ages. The central relationship is enslaver against imprisoned sovereigns. An omniscient mythic narrator follows their developing resistance, with the Fox's attempted escape a continuing thread and the Cat's first and returning reigns bookending it. The payoff is the first completed year, whose beauty and regularity remain inseparable from a monstrous act. Resistance wins the captives specific powers over their captor, but does not turn the Wheel benevolent or make imprisonment an agreement.

Explicit requirements: the Wheel of Ages oversees the months and both rules and is ruled by the Cat, Serpent, Fox, Boar, Frog, Wren, Bear, Salmon, Spider, Wolf, Hawk, and Stag. The user rejected the earlier story for misalignment, requested replacement, endorsed a creation-myth direction, and then said: “go hard on this and make it evil where the wheel traps this sprit animals creating the 12 months of year”. The evil Wheel, trapping, and causal creation of twelve months now govern the plot. Inferences: numinous horror, coercive power, and an ending that leaves the calendar morally dreadful. Explicit gore, a particular escape outcome, and a universal origin of physical time are not required.

Before months, the twelve living spirits hold neighboring countries of weather, growth, and darkness. Their shadows are living roots of their forms, reaching into the countries they inhabit. The Wheel covets their power and, more than any one season, ownership of whatever comes next. It is a living predator, warm under its black hide, with an eye at its hub. It enters their border disputes as a supposedly powerless arbiter. Each spirit wants sovereignty over the others' country; the Wheel offers each exactly that. While showing the twelve the circular boundary of their promised kingdom, it closes the boundary around them and draws their shadow-roots into its flesh as twelve spokes. The Stag tries to wedge the closing boundary with his antlers; the Wheel breaks them and shuts him in. The trap is the Wheel's ambush, not the spirits assenting to captivity.

The first month is made on the page. The Wheel pulls the Cat to the opening in its rim and forces her long nights across the whole country; every dusk is hers, but the free Cat is nowhere in it. Days and dawns still happen within her reign. The Wheel holds her through a waxing and waning moon, then pulls the Serpent to the same opening. Heat reaches the ground and the Cat is shut away. These forced reigns, one moon for each of twelve captives, are the months. The Wheel keeps its sources alive to use again. Its reigns produce distinct consequences: Cat's long nights, Serpent's thaw, Fox's deceptive dusk and shifting weather, Boar's broken earth and shoots, Frog's rain, Wren's flowering, Bear's heavy fruit and heat, Salmon's returning rivers and spawning, Spider's dew and mist, Wolf's frost, Hawk's stripping winds, Stag's bare boughs and deep cold. These unfold around the escape plot rather than in twelve equal chapters.

Escape attempts establish the limitation before the climax. The Serpent sheds skin but cannot shed her living root. The Boar tears open the Wheel and bites his exposed spoke: his own foreleg begins to thin, and the new shoots outside blacken. He must let go. Breaking the flesh around a root does not free it; cutting the root mutilates its owner. The Frog's boundary leap reveals that the compartments connect around the rim, a path the Fox follows to a blind recess where the Wheel cannot see or squeeze him. The Wren sings to the hurt Boar; vibration follows his root into the Wheel, whose eye shuts against its will. The Bear sends sleep down the same living connection and stalls it. Their powers can affect the captor through contact, not issue arbitrary commands to it. The Wheel retaliates by wrenching winter across the Bear's fruiting country; fruit freezes on the boughs. The reprisal is visible outside as well as felt inside.

The Salmon's effort to return upstream reverses the flow through his root, exposing the buried root-junctions before the Wheel can close them. The Hawk sees the route from above; the Spider follows it with thread so the prisoners can feel one another's timing. The Wolf holds the closing wound apart; if he lets go the thread will be severed inside it. The Fox's blind refuge lies across the missing link. He carries the thread through it to the Cat, exposing and losing his only place beyond the Wheel's reach. Now they can coordinate the powers already demonstrated. The Cat darkens the hub so the Wheel cannot see which spoke to pull; Bear's sleep arrests it; Salmon reverses its motion. The Stag, whose month has finally come, grows his new antlers through the exposed junctions and braces the twelve roots apart so the Wheel cannot pull two into the opening at once. His first broken attempt and a full circuit's regrowth earn this leverage.

These acts force the Wheel's first obedient turn. The prisoners cannot extract their buried roots or leave their cells; stopping, blinding, and reversing a living captor are specific powers, not possession of its will. They can keep their roots braced for succession and turn their domains against it when it tries to skip a captive or hold a season beyond its moon. The Wheel still chooses cruelty within those limits. When the Cat's opening returns, it squeezes her root to prolong a killing night. The Wren carries the count through the roots; the others hold the Wheel while the Cat puts her darkness into its eye instead of the sky. Dawn reaches the hills. The Wheel crushes her against the cell wall in reprisal. Outside, frozen fruit remains on the boughs and people light fires to greet their first New Year. Inside, the Fox can feel the Cat's resistance and knows his exposed refuge is gone. Twelve months have become a recurring year; none of the twelve has been released.

The images and scene proportions remain provisional. Protect the capture as an irreversible event, months as the forced occupation of the opening, and mutual rule as an active struggle with power and cost on both sides. Do not replace the creatures' actual attempts with a technical lecture or a closing stipulation about what they cannot do.
