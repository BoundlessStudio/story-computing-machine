# The Dedication

## Promised experience

The Black Dahlia, “the most dangerous woman alive,” sings jazz in a city run by mobsters and crooked politicians. The pleasure is watching her turn years of secrets men gave her in intimacy into a death she can order with a glance or a tiny movement, under crimson velvet and in full voice. The image suggests sensual confidence and amusement. The city's rulers make the stakes wider than the room. “up the rating for this story.” calls for greater intensity. Aim for Mature through adult desire, reciprocal corruption, and the immediate effects of a killing. Relationships, motives, means, and outcome are open.

## Takes considered

- **The succession set, chosen.** On election night two men expect the Dahlia to finish a deal she helped make. Her dedication claims a greater share of the city and costs her the man she wanted to share it with. She improvises as a singer and power in the room at once.
- **The night of the request.** A crooked judge realizes his lover has decided to kill him and tries to buy one last song. His dread at the foot of the stage would carry the story.
- **The voices on shellac.** A pianist hears the dead men's bedroom confidences on the last surviving master of a show. He must decide whom to protect, with the Dahlia's performance at one remove.

## Provisional direction

In Merrow, the election party at the Oriole Room is to double as a transfer of power. The Dahlia has agreed to remove club boss Emil Corbett during her set and let mayor-elect Vernon Saye inherit his protection business. She expects to take charge of the city's clubs. Saye, her lover and ally, offers her the Oriole outright. He has already promised the rest of the rooms will close to clear their blocks for his backers. He thinks that gives her the best room and the two of them a private life.

The Dahlia already has the authority to order a death. Years ago she used it from this very stage on the alderman who stood between Saye and his first office. Now she uses what Corbett told her in bed, that he bought his clubs with money entrusted to him by his partners, to offer him a new bargain. She can keep his theft private and his partners earning. He agrees while the band plays to put his men and their paid police at her disposal. Saye mistakes what's happening for tribute. She lets him ask for their private song, and sings it to him after they talk through his offer and what she wanted. With a small movement she gives the order for his death. Afterward, Corbett defers to her before his men. She keeps the clubs open and claims their protection payments.

## Craft plan

**Teller.** Try close third, alive to everything a singer must do to own the room, against the Dahlia looking back in an expansive, seductive first person, exacting about what others claim happened. Give her time to want her lover while she decides.

**Cast.** The Dahlia wants her share of the city and wants Saye to know what the music means to her. She knows he's enjoyed listening and mistakes that for understanding. She prolongs a sentence when she likes watching someone wait, then speaks plainly when she wants an answer. Saye knows who expects him to close the clubs and mistakes exclusivity for generosity. He wants loyalty and a private life with the woman who has heard him at his worst. A campaign speaker, he paints the happy future and fusses over a domestic particular at the point he fears she'll refuse. Corbett wants his place and his partners' trust. He knows what he stole and mistakes discretion for loyalty. He talks to an adversary like a paying guest and counts what she owes him down to who paid to fix the piano.

**Casting from the original.** Long black hair, wine-dark lips, opera gloves, heels, and a high slit in a gleaming black gown. She offers, listens, holds, and dares. Her left glove rests near the ribbed microphone. The open right hand looks as easy as the lifted face. Saye moves her hair aside in the wing. She sweeps it clear herself to lean toward him later. The lit line of her leg makes men at the front lean toward the stage, and the hand they take for a generous invitation makes the final choice. The band behind her watches and plays to her beneath the crimson curtain.

**The Oriole Room.** Lake Crown Hotel would put the politicians on official ground. Chaffin's Cellar would lose the ceremonial visibility. At the Oriole, politicians and racketeers come to the singer's apron to speak privately within sight of everyone they want to impress. It is almost midnight in a cold Merrow November. Wet wool and fried onions crowd the air, carpet dies at the raised stage, ice ticks on glass, and lamps leave narrow pools of light. Waiters know whose supper is a standing order and where a chair leg will snag the carpet. From the apron a whisper during a piano break goes to the person a foot away. The rest of the room gets what she chooses to give the microphone. ## Scene briefs

1. **At the curtain, one fifth.** They are close enough to touch as the last plates go. She lets Saye clear her hair away. They trade the opening of their private song, his answer low against her skin. He invokes the alderman whose death she ordered and whose office first gave him a claim to power. He wants her to give them that life again and tells her why he promised to clear the blocks. She wanted the clubs. He lifts the velvet for her as she goes on with their bargain coming apart.
2. **The set, the long scene.** The Dahlia's first number carries the room while she decides what to do. At the first break Corbett climbs to the apron. She speaks aside, away from the microphone, about the theft he confessed in bed. She wants his word now and he wants his secret kept. He gives her his word and stays on the stage, visible to his people. Saye steps from the front table to request their song. Here is the owed conversation about what they promised each other in private and what they were willing to do for it. His version is worth wanting while she makes her choice. He draws breath to give her his usual answer, then catches the movement of her glove and tries to leave. A waiter ends up holding his whole weight by the shoulders of his good suit. Corbett, who was poised to go, sees what she chose and brings Saye's chair over himself. She sings through it, and the nearest table has to make its own decision about when to stop smiling.
3. **The return, the last bars.** Corbett defers to her in public. The police chief, who came expecting the closing orders, gets his direction from the microphone. The clubs open and their protection payments come to her. Give the old confidences a memory in the set. Keep the killing's means and the campaign outside the frame.

**Ending.** Saye's lamp burns as the others turn their chairs toward the stage. In the reprise she leaves the space where he gave her that answer at the curtain. The offer of a last room to sing in, the dedication he wanted, and the way he really listened all return while the people who took his place wait for her to fill it.
