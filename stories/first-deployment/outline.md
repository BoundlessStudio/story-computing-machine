# Room on the Train — provisional outline

The explicit request promises an old combat robot's interview about its first deployment against the infected, roughly twenty years after the Zombie war ended. I infer the distinctive pleasure of a machine veteran's firsthand battle account: perceptions and capabilities a human soldier would not have, rookie assumptions tested in combat, and a later voice able to distinguish what it recorded from what it understood. The interview supplies a present listener and the distance of surviving the war. Tone, scale, interviewer identity, any intimate relationship, and a moral or trauma arc are unspecified. My proposed tone is dry, candid, increasingly urgent in the remembered action, with an earned warmth at the close; that is a choice, not a requirement.

My invented direction: BR-41, now called Forty-One, gives an oral-history interview for the twentieth anniversary of the war's end. Its first deployment was a counterattack meant to reopen an evacuation railway at Bellwether. A rookie combat machine, it possessed judgment and attachments before combat; this is not the day a program becomes a person. Its mistake was believing immunity to infection made it exceptionally safe. It can destroy infected at a rate humans cannot, but mass, damaged joints, ammunition, and heat still matter. Its governing relationship is with the people it is deployed beside and trying to get out, rather than a secret connection to the interviewer.

- The interview opens on the familiar cropped photograph of Forty-One outside Bellwether station, captioned as its arrival. It was taken near the end of the operation. The interviewer asks what came before it. The old robot answers with the loud, absurdly confident first minutes: a deployment crate opening, soldiers cheering the machines, and its first attempt to distinguish weapons fire from railway noise.
- In the station approaches, Forty-One and its section fight infected at close range. Their immunity allows them to advance through places human troops cannot hold. It briefly braces a platform gate against a rush while evacuees pass within its reach; a passenger repeats the reassuring claim that the machines cannot catch the infection, which Forty-One also takes as evidence of safety. A human soldier's practical warning about being tipped over is vindicated when mass and close combat damage Forty-One's arm. Its individual voice and concern for others are present before any invitation.
- The infected orient toward distant noise but pursue visible living prey at close range: early gunfire turns a streetful toward the section, while an infected already at the gate keeps reaching past the robot for people. A last evacuation train is trapped while the station's amplified announcements draw more infected toward its passengers. Forty-One recognizes that the enemy's indifference to a silent machine can be used. It shuts down the station amplifier itself, takes a portable horn loudspeaker, powers it, and broadcasts a continuous siren from the adjacent freight yard. The sustained, street-facing horn becomes the nearest continuous signal for the approaching mass; intermittent gunfire at the platform is farther away, partly screened by the loading sheds. Its section still fights infected already too close to the train to divert.
- The tracks run parallel: passenger line on one side, a freight lane between loading sheds on the other. The approach has already shown a gantry leaning on damaged supports. Forty-One draws the pursuit down that lane, away from the train, then brings the gantry down across the narrow route behind it with deliberate gunfire and a physical shove. It switches off and drops the horn there before escaping; no signal leads the infected back to the train. Fighting, traction, failing arm movement, and the necessary interval govern the run. The obstruction delays pursuit; a service opening at the far end lets the now-silent machine circle back to the outbound passenger track, where its section is withdrawing. This makes both the train's departure and the robot's survival consequences of its judgment and combat power.
- The photograph catches it reaching the clear track as the last car passes. In the moment the official account calls triumph, a passenger is struggling to force an enormous suitcase out through the carriage door to make space for the robot. The offer cannot realistically fit Forty-One, but the passenger keeps trying. Forty-One had already chosen to protect them; the surprise is being wanted aboard. It survives and rejoins its section, with the broken arm becoming one of the old repairs visible in the interview.
- Back in the room, the interviewer revises the photograph's caption rather than extracting a speech about war. Forty-One asks them to use the uncropped photograph, keeping the suitcase at its edge. Its selection grows from the picture they have been discussing, without a question planted solely to cue a closing line. The final image stays with the machine remembering a stranger's invitation after twenty years of peace; the completed combat escape gives that memory its weight.

The exact railway geography, supporting names, and balance of transcript with continuous recollection remain open to discovery in prose. The principal risk is sentiment or a generic lesson overwhelming the distinctive combat testimony; the sustained deployment action and Forty-One's tactical agency must earn the final memory.
