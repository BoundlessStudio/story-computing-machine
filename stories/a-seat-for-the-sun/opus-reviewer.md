## Verdict

- Dialogue: PASS. Every exchange holds up in the scene and changes status or alliance. One untagged line (D1) is the only dialogue fix.
- Prose: PASS. Concrete, varied and clearly staged, with two small polish items (P1, P2).
- Continuity and prompt: PASS. The invitation line is verbatim, the party arc completes, the chair counts hold and nothing conflicts with canon. There is one Minor identity handoff (C1).
- Edit scope: polish
- Existing review.md said: Verdict PASS, "Dialogue: PASS"; agree. Here the old PASS holds up under exchange-by-exchange checking.

This story avoids the collection's usual dialogue failure. Each speaker talks from what they want in the game, not from a shared register. Tebbit stacks questions and pleads "But it's my birthday." His mother cuts everything down to hosting logistics. The cousin barely speaks and keeps winning. Nika alone speaks in formal, uncontracted sentences, and that is the joke, because what she says in them is vain and petty ("I had intended to eat the chocolate."). The one run built on corrections (L141–L151) is the rules dispute the plot turns on, and a hovering goddess running into a child's rule about bottoms on seats earns it. The exchanges change things: Nika is ruled out, the two losers bond over grievance and the rocket, they hatch the rematch plan, the cousin exposes their signal, and Nika turns the chairs. Neither the decisive exchange nor the final one states a lesson, and the story ends on a shout and a tipped chair. The edit pass should fix the four Minor items and otherwise leave the dialogue alone. In particular, it should not explain why Nika faces away or add a closing line.

The tone stays playful and broadly accessible, with non-graphic mishaps. The length band is 2,500–4,000 words (the story is now about 3,190). The active profile is prospective-2026-08-23.

## Dialogue findings

### D1 · Minor · Untagged line in the prize exchange

- Where: L113–L121, where Tebbit shows the chocolate sun
- Text:
> Nika took a closer look.
> “That's the prize,” Tebbit said. His cousin reached for it, and he twisted away. “You have to win it.”
> “I'm looking.”
> “You look with your eyes.”
- Problem: Attribution. "I'm looking." has three plausible speakers. Nika "took a closer look" two paragraphs earlier and has just asked what she wins, so a reader can hear the line as Nika answering "You have to win it." Only the next line, a parent's hands-off rule, shows that the speaker must be the cousin who reached for the chocolate. The reader has to go back and re-read. This is also the cousin's first line as Tebbit's rival, so it should land cleanly.
- Fix: Tag "I'm looking." to the cousin, or give it a beat. Leave "You look with your eyes." untagged as Tebbit's reply, and change nothing else. Illustrative only: "I'm looking," said his cousin, her hand still out.

## Prose findings

### P1 · Minor · Paragraphs opening on "Nika" plus a verb

- Where: 26 paragraphs open with "Nika". The densest clusters are L123–L149 (the first game) and L317–L339 (the ending).
- Text:
> Nika counted. The deficiency was deliberate, then.
> Nika folded herself gracefully above the chair with the sun on it
> Nika rose to avoid a collision.
> Nika looked down at the little girl, whose bottom was unquestionably on it.
- Problem: A repeated sentence template. In close third this is natural, but in the clusters the openings start to read like a camera log: Nika counted, Nika had expected, Nika began, Nika folded, Nika rose, Nika looked. It is not systemic, and the prose is otherwise varied.
- Fix: In the two clusters, recast three or four openings to lead with what Nika sees or with someone else's action, or fold a short Nika beat into the paragraph before it. Leave the openings that tag dialogue (L81, L293, L327), because they carry attribution.

### P2 · Minor · Nika and the chair move to the table without a beat

- Where: L157–L163, after Nika is ruled out
- Text:
> The goddess lowered her feet to the grass.
> Tebbit's mother carried the chalk sun back to the table and placed it beside Nika.
- Problem: Staging. Nika was last seen at the ring of chairs, yet the chair is carried "back to the table" and set "beside Nika", so she has moved off-page. "The chalk sun" is also the first time the chair gets that nickname, thirty lines after the chocolate, "the little golden sun", "watched from the table". The reader briefly juggles two suns at the table until "Nika sat on it."
- Fix: Add half a sentence that moves Nika to the table; she can withdraw with some dignity. On first use, call the chair something like "the chair with the chalk sun". The later uses at L309 and L323 can keep "chalk sun".

## Continuity and prompt findings

### C1 · Minor · "His cousin" before anyone says she is one

- Where: L67–L83, the fetching of plates
- Text:
> The tall girl followed, announcing that she knew where the good plates were.
> His cousin came behind him carrying three, which she set down with considerable ceremony.
- Problem: An identity handoff and a small point-of-view knowledge gap. The tall girl goes indoors for plates, and "his cousin" comes out with plates. The reader must infer that they are the same person until L83 ("The tall cousin"). Nothing has told Nika, whose close third this is, that the girl is Tebbit's cousin. The first spoken hint comes at L215 ("my other cousin's").
- Fix: Join the two labels once at L77 (for example, "The tall girl, his cousin, came behind him..."). If it can be done in an existing line, let Tebbit or his mother say "cousin" early. Do not add a name.
