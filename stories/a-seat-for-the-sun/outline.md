# Outline

## Story

- Premise and central promise: A child's homemade rocket interrupts Sun Goddess Nika's cloud nap with “Please come to my birthday.” She attends an already lively garden party and becomes fiercely invested in musical chairs. Deliver divine splendor meeting the pleasures, disappointments, and untidy hospitality of an eighth birthday.
- Focal pressure or attachment: Nika wants an entertaining afternoon and enjoys being admired. Tebbit wants his rocket returned, his ambitious invitation vindicated, and the chocolate sun awarded to the game's winner. Inviting her was exuberance, not a concealed rescue request. Both discover that they particularly dislike losing while the other watches.
- Counterforce or complication: Adult deference and Nika's conspicuous arrival disrupt Tebbit's plans. His brisk explanations leave things out; her assumptions about what counts as sitting prove inconvenient. His mother needs guests fed and the party moving. Nobody supplies a villain or an emergency.
- POV, distance, and information limit: Close third past through Nika. Stay intimate with her bodily ease, curiosity, and ridiculous competitive investment. Tebbit's motives emerge through requests, interrupted explanations, and behavior; Nika cannot inspect his thoughts.
- Governing movement and time shape: One afternoon: invitation, arrival, a complete game, cake, and the beginning of a freely chosen rematch. Expand the first game and the two losers' time together; compress introductions. Intended prose: 2,500–4,000 words.
- Speculative rule or ordinary-world constraint: Living Nika supports herself on clouds or in air and shapes nearby light and radiant warmth within sight. Her splendor makes icing soften until she dims it. Worship and acknowledged solar authority exist independently of this party. The rocket is an ordinary propelled model that reaches her low cloud; its invitation includes a recognizable sketch of the garden below.
- Dialogue promise: An exuberant child and an initially assured goddess must become intelligible playmates without acquiring equal fluency or perfect manners.
- Dialogic medium: Handwriting and drawing, spoken explanation and protest, pointing, party chatter, and shared singing. Keep the required invitation exact.
- Dialogue engine: Incomplete rules produce consequential misunderstandings; corrections change play and status. Losing gives Nika and Tebbit something immediate to discuss besides her divinity, and their competing complaints develop into a joint desire for another round.

## Voice

- Narrative texture: Spacious, sun-drowsy observation tightens into physical comedy around chair legs, trailing ruffles, and coveted chocolate. Nika notices beauty and affronts before practical inconvenience.
- Conversational texture: Unequal turns, food interruptions, unfinished explanations, ordinary requests, and delighted shouting. Allow stretches of simple talk without concealed agendas.
- Rhetorical ownership: Nika owns expansive comparisons and mildly wounded dignity. Tebbit owns game particulars, abrupt subject changes, and self-serving amendments. His mother has hosting authority without an appetite for divine debate; other children care about their own turns and prizes. Humor belongs to actions and conflicting attention.
- Pressure behavior: Nika becomes overprecise when challenged. Tebbit repeats the part that favors him, then goes quiet after losing. His mother shortens requests while carrying food.
- Relationship movement: Nika wants Tebbit to keep including her but dislikes asking after defeat. He wants her admiration and participation, yet cannot comfortably admit how badly he wanted the prize. His mother wants cooperation without publicly embarrassing either. Sharing the losers' seats gives them room to enjoy one another without fixing those flaws.
- Anti-default: Avoid a lonely immortal receiving a child's polished lesson about ordinary life. Let both enjoy attention, lose badly for a moment, and find a specific way to keep playing.

## Beats

1. The rocket punches a ragged hole through Nika's cloud and catches in her ruffles. Inspect its taped construction and handmade invitation through her surprise. The original reference informs her adult appearance: long white hair, rainbow-reflective round sunglasses, pale lilac and white ruffles, gold accents, and relaxed blue-sky light. She identifies the sketched garden directly below and brings the rocket down herself.
2. Tebbit has saved a little chair with a drawn sun on it. His first urgent concern is whether the rocket survived; admiration and questions arrive out of order. Friends are already there. Adults briefly defer, while his mother finds another plate. Nika offers a brief, beautiful play of sunlight that attracts everyone and softens the cake's icing. Tebbit wants the scheduled game started; Nika adjusts her brightness when the mother identifies the problem.
3. Establish the chairs, music, player count, elimination rule, and chocolate prize through actual preparation. Tebbit explains while distracted by the returned rocket and a cousin eyeing the chocolate. Nika misses the bodily requirement of sitting and hovers proudly over an available chair; another child sits beneath her. Her protest follows that real misunderstanding. The mother rules her out, and the game continues while she takes a removed chair beside the table.
4. Tebbit survives several rounds, then loses just before the final. His attempted birthday exception meets the rule he insisted on earlier. He joins Nika with a view of the remaining players. Allow an awkward pause, competing grievances, questions about the rocket's puncture, and interest in which cake portion each wants. Neither offers a lesson or manufactures consolation. Their shared attention turns toward the cousin still playing.
5. Finish the game and award its prize honestly. Tebbit remains briefly disappointed; Nika keeps watching the emptied playing area. Cake interrupts before either can dominate the afternoon with a demand. Over eating and inspecting the rocket, they discover that both want another round. Let the request and response arise plainly from the completed game, without a confession or promise about eternity.
6. After cake, they take over the music together, intent on catching the quick cousin out. Nika starts with the stately rhythm she knows; Tebbit supplies an unsuitable, infectious birthday tune and has to teach its shape. Children insist on a usable beat and start circling again. End with the former losers enjoying the renewed scramble from their side-by-side chairs. The saved seat has become a place she wants to occupy.
