# Opus review: A Knock in Someone Else's Sleep

Independent audit for a later editing pass. It is not a `review.md` verdict,
does not change canon state, and does not authorize edits by itself.

## Verdict

- Dialogue: REVISE — the talk is strong, but at L145 the Entity asks why Garnet "never came back" when the story has shown it knows why.
- Prose: PASS — concrete, varied, no em dashes or reversal tics; only a few loose pronouns.
- Continuity and prompt: PASS — every prompt clause is delivered at ~2,960 words; three Minor staging/wording slips (the who-knows-what slip is filed as D1).
- Edit scope: targeted-dialogue
- Existing review.md said: Verdict PASS, Dialogue PASS; I disagree narrowly. The dialogue mostly earns its PASS, but the review missed the L145 knowledge contradiction.

This is one of the better dialogue stories in the collection, and the owner's usual complaint mostly does not apply here. The two speakers never merge. Cleo is nine and it shows: she bargains, forgets things, answers a question meant for someone else ("I said towels"), blames the Entity, and won't be an endless courier. The Entity talks plainly and briefly, and its pressure shows through dream objects instead: the roof comes off, the houses flood in. Garnet exists only in Cleo's partial, sometimes wrong retellings, which is exactly the medium the prompt needs. Every exchange changes something: recognition, a test question, a grievance, a refusal, a choice, and then play resumes. The ending stops mid-game, with no lesson stated. The one real failure is the Entity's question at L145, which undoes its side of the central misunderstanding. Fix that line and the few small staging and pronoun slips, and do not push the rest toward more articulate or more explanatory speech.

## Dialogue findings

### D1 · Blocking · Entity asks what it already knows

- Where: L145, first return from Garnet's door (cupboard/sea exchange); contradicts L77–L79 and L189
- Text:
> “Tell her it's the sea. She made it salt. Ask why she never came back.”
> The Entity approached as faintly as it knew how, and felt her strain against even that. It left her alone.
> Other children had changed in the same way; being Garnet had not protected her.
> “Tell her I stopped because I was hurting her.”
- Problem: Ground/knowledge. "Ask why she never came back" is a sincere question, and it assumes Garnet could have come back and chose not to. But the narration has already shown that the Entity knows she couldn't: her mind closed the way other children's had, and it was the Entity that withdrew and left her alone. At L189 it says exactly that. Nothing marks L145 as rhetorical or bitter, so the reader takes it at face value and trips over it. The slip also removes the Entity's half of the mutual misunderstanding. Garnet's reply (L155), "she tried for ages, but you wouldn't open up," is the real revelation, and it lands best if the Entity is asking about something it genuinely doesn't know.
- Fix: Change only the last sentence of L145 so the Entity asks about something it doesn't know. It felt her strain, but it never knew whether she wanted to reach it. Keep "Tell her it's the sea. She made it salt." Keep Garnet's reply at L155 and the realization at L165. Both still follow, because the new question invites "she did come back." Illustrative only: "Ask if she ever tried to come back." Do not fix this by rewriting L79 so the Entity thinks she could have chosen to return, because L189 would then contradict it.

### D2 · Minor · "Lift it." reads as an order

- Where: L235–L239, after Cleo brings back the toy bus (egg memory)
- Text:
> “She told me about the egg, too. You made a handle so she could drink it.”
> “Lift it. I thought it would be hot.”
- Problem: Ground/referent. Standing alone, "Lift it." reads as an instruction to Cleo, who has her hand on the bus bell at that moment, and not as the Entity's correction "(the handle was so she could) lift it." The next sentence recovers the meaning, but the reader has to back up. The exchange itself is good: a memory disagreement that the Entity then lets stand.
- Fix: Add the missing words so the line reads as a correction. Keep "Well, she drank it." and "The Entity let that stand." Illustrative only: "It was for lifting. I thought it would be hot."

## Prose findings

### P1 · Minor · Loose pronouns in narration

- Where: L109, the backstory of how the relay was discovered; L263, Cleo returning with the whale
- Text:
> through a boy who wanted his absent brother to see a thing they had made
> then climbed onto its head. It pressed its enormous face against the bus window
- Problem: At L109, "they" could mean the boy and his brother (which makes no sense, since the brother would already have seen it) or the boy and the Entity (the intended meaning). At L263, the nearest antecedent for "its head" is "the garage", so the reader briefly puts Cleo on the garage before "enormous face" makes it clear she is on the whale.
- Fix: At L109, name the makers ("a thing he and the Entity had made"). At L263, say "the whale's head". Change nothing else in either sentence.

## Continuity and prompt findings

### C1 · Minor · Who carried the boxes

- Where: L81, how the Entity learned that Garnet had moved away
- Text:
> A child who lived in her old room dreamed of helping carry out the last boxes.
- Problem: It is unclear who this child is. If it was Garnet's room when the boxes went out, another child living "in her old room" means a sibling, and nothing sets one up. A later occupant would be carrying boxes in, not out. The reader can't tell how the Entity got the news.
- Fix: Name the child's relation in a few words, for example a younger sibling or a neighbour's child, or drop "her old room". Keep the fact that the Entity learned of the move second-hand through a child's dream.

### C2 · Minor · "Unentered" room that Garnet had entered

- Where: L159, the Entity floods Cleo with houses; compare L81 and L209
- Text:
> The same unentered room appeared in every window.
> It remembered her standing ankle-deep in blue tiles, furious that the rest would not change.
- Problem: The room kept ready is the one whose floor Garnet "had not yet managed to escape" (L81), and L209 shows her standing in it. "Unentered" contradicts both. The intended sense is probably "unfinished" or "not re-entered".
- Fix: Replace the one word, for example with "unfinished" or "blue-tiled", so all three mentions agree.

### C3 · Minor · Garage staging in the final game

- Where: L263–L275, closing garage/whale moves
- Text:
> She folded one wall of the garage down
> There was no space to enlarge the seat without enlarging the garage as well.
> Beyond the plaster there would have to be a road
- Problem: The game only works if the garage still pins the bus: one change is allowed, and the walls are pressed to the windows. But Cleo folds a wall down at L263, and the reader may ask why the bus can't simply leave through that side. L271 then treats the garage as fully enclosing again. "The seat" at L271 is also unclear: the driver's seat, where the Entity's weight sits, or the passenger seats that become a sea at L273?
- Fix: Make the lowered wall serve only the whale's face. Either fold it back up after the whale pushes through the window, or cut the wall-folding and let the whale reach the window directly. At L271, say "the passenger seats". Keep the whale, the bell, the sea and the final request to Cleo.
