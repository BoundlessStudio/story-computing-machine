# Outline

## Story

- Premise and central promise: A living Entity imprisoned beneath a quarry settlement recruits sleeping children to find Garnet, its childhood friend who lost access sixty years ago. One visitor recognizes the elderly woman. Deliver an actual, imperfectly mediated reunion through dreams, with childhood and direct contact irretrievable.
- Focal pressure or attachment: The Entity has preserved every game Garnet invented. It wants her to know it kept waiting, but also wants the particular girl whose next visit once seemed certain. Its fidelity has become a demand that memory remain unchanged.
- Counterforce or complication: Nine-year-old Cleo wants fantastic dreams of her own and leaves the settlement tomorrow. Garnet, now seventy-three and living elsewhere, remembers their friendship differently. Neither can supply the missing sixty years in one night's messages.
- POV, distance, and information limit: Close third past through the Entity. Render its nonhuman senses concretely; human sights arrive through dreamers. Garnet's present life and responses reach it only through Cleo's retellings, never an independent adult POV or certified view inside another mind.
- Governing movement and time shape: One interrupted dream, with two concentrated backward movements into first contact and Garnet's visits, then increasing urgency within the present night. The action contracts to departures and returns across a dream threshold, ending during play.
- Speculative rule or ordinary-world constraint: The Entity cannot move its living body from the sealed cavity. Its telepathy reaches nearby minds; only sleeping children tolerate its uncompressed awareness. Development closes that capacity gradually, regardless of affection. Within contact, a child can make a dream route to someone personally known who is also asleep. Only the child's remade images and remembered words can cross safely to an adult; the Entity cannot accompany, observe, or search beyond the child. Waking ends the route. This bounded relay is enabled by living participants, not a universal property of dreams.
- Dialogue promise: Alien loneliness meets a child's distractions and an absent friend's changed humor. Failed explanation, play, petty objections, and inaccurate reporting should carry affection before anyone can explain it.
- Dialogic medium: Shared dream objects and spatial transformations, child speech, attempted thought transfers, departures, and returned reports of an adult's dream speech. Ordinary waking conversation remains possible but cannot give the Entity a simultaneous exchange.
- Dialogue engine: Cleo must understand enough to remake a message rather than merely carry its shape. Her omissions expose the Entity's mistaken expectations; Garnet's unexpected replies make a present relationship possible.

## Voice

- Narrative texture: Attend first to pressure, enclosure, duration, and the mutable size of dream things. Let remembered games acquire human specificity. Compress geological solitude; linger over the small delays before Cleo returns.
- Conversational texture: Dream actions interrupt speech. Cleo forgets clauses, pursues amusing details, and sometimes delivers a report solely to get it over with. Garnet's relayed remarks mix interest, teasing, and mundane corrections.
- Rhetorical ownership: The Entity understands simultaneity but struggles with human sequence and exaggeration. Cleo owns practical skepticism, embarrassment, and mischief. Garnet owns her remembered childhood, including its unflattering details; nobody supplies universal explanations of growing up.
- Pressure behavior: The Entity repeats or enlarges an image when misunderstood, making explanation worse. Cleo becomes blunt and stops cooperating with exact reproduction. Garnet asks specific questions instead of offering instant reassurance.
- Relationship movement: The Entity wants Garnet back but cannot comfortably ask whether she missed it. Cleo wants interesting company without being everyone's messenger. Garnet wants an answer to the vanished friendship, then room to surprise its survivor. Retelling changes all three relationships.
- Anti-default: Avoid the wise child who translates everyone into therapeutic fluency, and the ancient being who learns one tidy lesson about letting go.

## Beats

1. Cleo catches the Entity replacing part of her chosen dream with its familiar question about Garnet. She has been visiting during a holiday stay near the quarry; this is her final night. Her annoyance forces it to make its request intelligible. Establish the sealed cavity and the difference between the Entity's reach and freedom.
2. Enter the first remembered arrival of humans above it. Its attempted welcome overwhelmed most recipients: show lasting disorientation and interrupted lives without treating illness as monstrosity. A sleeping child was the first mind whose dream could give its thoughts temporary shapes. Later, Garnet stayed to play. Give their invented impossible-house game a lived pleasure, including her habit of cheating, before showing her visits thinning as dream tolerance closed. She moved away after adolescence; later children knew no one who matched its request.
3. Cleo recognizes the game's deliberately wrong rule: Garnet, the elderly neighbor who minds her after school at home, taught her the same trick. The Entity initially rejects that age. Cleo constructs a route from her own familiarity; her first return brings an adult's specific question about their old game. Recognition earns excitement without recovering direct contact.
4. Repeated crossings accumulate errors and useful surprises. Garnet thought the disappearance meant the Entity had tired of her; the Entity assumed she could simply choose to come back. It attempts to give Cleo the entirety of its waiting, which she cannot remember or retell. Her refusal is concrete and irritated. The Entity must select what it needs Garnet to know, and let Cleo express it in her own human dream language. Garnet's reply includes an ordinary piece of her later life that the Entity has never imagined.
5. Turn the final relay into a few moves of their old game. Garnet changes a rule rather than performing a remembered childhood move; Cleo alters another on her own behalf. Let the Entity actually respond to these new partners. Stop amid their awkward, funny, relayed play, with both old friends knowing contact has resumed. No restored adult faculty, physical release, future service arrangement, or concluding explanation.
