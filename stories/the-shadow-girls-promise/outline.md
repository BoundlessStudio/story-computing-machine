# Provisional direction

## The request and its likely pleasure

The request explicitly gives a childhood friendship beginning at six, years of play, the shadow girl's unexplained disappearance, and her return twenty years afterward as a beautiful, terrifying adult with the same bow and mischievous recognition. Her quoted claim that she has done her half of their promise to rule the world must have substance. The human's gender, adult occupation, relationship beyond friendship, exact division of the promise, conflict, and ending remain open.

I infer a pleasure in affectionate recognition surviving an enormous change of scale: the monster is still the old friend, and the old game has become alarmingly real. I want warmth, comedy, adventure, and the opportunity to enjoy her power. The bow and her delighted greeting suggest attachment; neither a romance nor a moral rejection of conquest is required. This direction chooses an enduring friendship with possible adult attraction and an enacted partnership in actual world rule.

## The story I want to write

First-person narration, probably around 4,000 words. The human is now a small passenger-airship pilot who loves flying, has a competent and satisfying adult life, and still wants to see the enormous places beyond the scheduled routes. Recognition and appetite, rather than dissatisfaction with ordinary adulthood, make the invitation irresistible. At six the narrator befriended the living shadow girl; they played for several years, particularly pirates, with the human at an imaginary wheel and the girl becoming smoky sails. Their bargain was: she would take the world, and the human would come with her. She disappeared at eleven. The present reunion happens twenty years later, when the narrator is thirty-one.

- **The bedroom opens outward.** The returned woman gives the prompt's greeting. Familiar mischief, a remembered physical habit, and the bow establish the old friend inside the terrifying beauty. The narrator learns that she has spent the missing years defeating the powers beneath the kingdoms and then their human allies. Her darkness is living, tangible, and hers to shape; she can travel through shadows, lift great weights, and enfold or seize physical things. It does not read minds or make every technical skill hers. No new vulnerability to light appears at the climax.
- **The claim is demonstrated.** She has completed the conquest before returning: recognized rulers have surrendered, their arsenals have been seized, her forces hold the capitals, and public broadcasts confirm it. A walk through a bedroom shadow brings the pair onto a captured palace roof overlooking a whole capital beneath their crooked childhood flag. Further openings show other capitals, not merely an invisible parallel realm. Frightened ministers and disarmed soldiers have their own recognizable concerns. Her authority and capacity are real; they are not all magically persuaded to love her. The narrator says yes to coming with her, with the pleasure and fear of stepping into an immense life.
- **Someone tries to take the victory back.** After surrender, a former ruler attempts a countercoup from a vast flying crownship with its escort. He threatens the coronation and demands that the human disavow the shadow sovereign. This is an attack on an accomplished conquest, not a last territory she secretly failed to take. She answers by rising over the capital as a colossal living darkness and stripping the escort's weapons, while catching falling people and machinery. She could destroy the flagship. She is not waiting for the human to rescue or morally correct her.
- **Their first joint prize.** Seeing a magnificent vessel he or she has only dreamed of flying, the narrator chooses to take the crownship intact as their own ship and moving palace. The shadow woman approves and lifts the pilot to its outer bridge deck, then holds and disarms the surrounding fleet. On the bridge the human's established skill matters: its frightened professional crew can fly, but the failed countercoup has left them under conflicting orders and with their commander trying to force a final attack. The narrator identifies as the other promised ruler, takes the physical helm, and works with the crew to turn the ship away from the capital. This is a dangerous, chosen boarding and an expert maneuver, not an unearned new magical faculty or a conversion speech.
- **Pirates again, at the scale they once imagined.** The girl becomes immense black sails and a supporting hand around the captured crownship. The pilot steers it free of the escort while she brings the disarmed ships down and carries their prize clear. Their old call-and-response returns naturally inside actual flight. The childhood game gives them shared trust and an image for coordinating already established abilities. Neither has become the other's parent or therapist; both discover what it feels like to do something enormous together as adults.

The possible ending is a coronation aboard the captured flying palace, visibly accepting joint rule rather than rejecting the world-sized gift. Their old flag goes up over a real horizon, and the narrator reaches to straighten the familiar bow before taking the helm. The woman who conquered the world is still delighted to have her fellow pirate back. They possess the world, its consequences, and their chosen adventure; the final pleasure belongs to their partnership and her astonishing achievement, not to administrative repair.

This is provisional. In prose I may simplify the countercoup or alter the coronation if discovery, action, and recognition produce a stronger whole, while preserving the complete request's reunion and the substance of her accomplished half.
