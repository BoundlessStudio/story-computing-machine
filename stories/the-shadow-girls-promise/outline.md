# Provisional direction

## The request and its likely pleasure

The request explicitly gives a childhood friendship beginning at six, years of play, the shadow girl's unexplained disappearance, and her return twenty years afterward as a beautiful, terrifying adult with the same bow and mischievous recognition. Her quoted claim that she has done her half of their promise to rule the world must have substance. The human's gender, adult occupation, relationship beyond friendship, exact division of the promise, conflict, and ending remain open.

I infer a pleasure in affectionate recognition surviving an enormous change of scale: the monster is still the old friend, and the old game has become alarmingly real. I want warmth, comedy, adventure, and the opportunity to enjoy her power. The bow and her delighted greeting suggest attachment; neither a romance nor a moral rejection of conquest is required. This direction chooses an enduring friendship with possible adult attraction and an enacted partnership in actual world rule.

## The story I want to write

First-person narration, probably around 4,000 words. The human is now a small passenger-airship pilot who loves flying, has a competent and satisfying adult life, and still wants to see the enormous places beyond the scheduled routes. Recognition and appetite, rather than dissatisfaction with ordinary adulthood, make the invitation irresistible. At six the narrator befriended the living shadow girl; they played for several years, particularly pirates, with the human at an imaginary wheel and the girl becoming smoky sails. Their bargain was: she would take the world, and the human would be her other captain. A little signal flashed with a torch meant that the captain was aboard and ready. She disappeared at eleven. The present reunion happens twenty years later, when the narrator is thirty-one.

- **The bedroom opens outward.** The returned woman gives the prompt's greeting. The narrator first feels the beautiful stranger's terrifying physical presence, then recognizes familiar mischief, a remembered habit, and the bow. After their last childhood expedition, while the human slept, the ruler who claimed the girl dragged her back out of sight beneath the bed. The narrator woke one morning to find her gone, searched, and carried that unanswered loss into a full adult life; what happened is disclosed only at the reunion. She spent the first years confined, then won allies and took the underworld thrones controlling her routes home. Those defeated route-holders can no longer follow her through the shadows into the bedroom. Winning freedom and safety mattered, but she went on to take the surface world because she meant to keep their promise and wanted her other captain beside her. Their reunion includes this history without turning into a trial of her absence. Her darkness is living, tangible, and hers to shape; she can travel through shadows, lift great weights, and enfold or seize physical things. It does not read minds or make every technical skill hers. No new vulnerability to light appears at the climax.
- **The claim is demonstrated.** She has completed the conquest before returning: recognized rulers have surrendered, the major arsenals have been seized, her forces hold the capitals, and public broadcasts confirm it. This does not mean every individual weapon has vanished or every surrendered unit is already physically disarmed. A royal flying squadron remains moored pending handover. A walk through a bedroom shadow brings the pair onto a captured palace roof overlooking a whole capital beneath their crooked childhood flag. Further openings show other capitals, not merely an invisible parallel realm. Frightened ministers and disarmed soldiers have their own recognizable concerns. Her authority and capacity are real; they are not all magically persuaded to love her. The narrator says yes to coming with her, with the pleasure and fear of stepping into an immense life.
- **Someone tries to take the victory back.** After surrender, a former human ruler breaches its terms by taking the moored flying crownship and its escort into a countercoup. He has physical weapons, not the defeated underworld rulers' access to the bedroom. This is an attack on an accomplished conquest, not a last territory she secretly failed to take. She leaves the narrator on the palace terrace and rises over the capital as a colossal living darkness, stripping the escort's weapons while catching falling people and machinery. She could destroy the flagship. She is not waiting for the human to rescue or morally correct her. The unsupported demand that the human disavow her is removed.
- **The other captain comes aboard.** The narrator is bodily afraid of the immense creature above the city, whose familiar face and bow can no longer be picked out. For a moment it feels like the morning she vanished happening again: wait on the safe side while the girl goes somewhere enormous and may not come back. The human instead takes a palace service launch and flies toward her and the crownship, wanting both this changed friend and the magnificent vessel they might take together. The launch's navigation lamp flashes their established childhood signal. She recognizes it, answers, and opens a path to the crownship's outer bridge deck instead of setting the launch safely down with the rest. This particular loss and shared way of playing have changed the human's decision and her response. She trusts the adult who has come to join her; the human approaches the terrifying form because the old friend is inside it.
- **Their first joint prize.** They choose to take the crownship intact as their own ship and moving palace. She neutralizes the commander and supports the great hull; the narrator boards, identifies as the other promised ruler, and takes its physical helm. The crew obeys because the coup has failed and their survival and safe flight require working with the people who now hold the vessel, not because a speech converts them. The human works with these professionals, increasing stern lift and turning the descending bow clear of the palace domes, then steering free of the halted escort. This uses established piloting skill without pretending a passenger pilot alone can replace an entire crew. She could seize the ship herself or give it to another pilot; letting this friend sail it with her is a relational choice, not proof of military helplessness.
- **Pirates again, at the scale they once imagined.** The girl becomes immense black sails and a supporting hand around the captured crownship. The pilot steers it clear while she brings the disarmed ships down and carries their prize away from the capital. The lamp signal has earned the old call-and-response inside actual flight. Their childhood game coordinates already established abilities; no private word acquires a new magical power. Both have acted on their recognition of the other as a changed adult. Neither has become the other's parent or therapist.

The possible ending is a coronation aboard the captured flying palace, visibly accepting joint rule rather than rejecting the world-sized gift. Their old flag goes up over a real horizon, and the narrator reaches to straighten the familiar bow before taking the helm. The woman who conquered the world is still delighted to have her fellow pirate back. They possess the world, its consequences, and their chosen adventure; the final pleasure belongs to their partnership and her astonishing achievement, not to administrative repair.

This is provisional. In prose I may simplify the countercoup or alter the coronation if discovery, action, and recognition produce a stronger whole, while preserving the complete request's reunion and the substance of her accomplished half.
