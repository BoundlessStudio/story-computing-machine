# Outline

## Story

- Premise and central promise: After seven peaceful years concealed in a public swimming pool, the missing Queen Madelon is recognized by its new male lifeguard. He brings news of her impending official memorial. She wants to hear what survives of her, eventually attending a small expatriate observance anonymously. Royal recognition opens an encounter with her own remembered life.
- Focal pressure or attachment: Madelon loves underwater light, familiar swimmers, and having no one wait for her answer. That peace is real. She left an exhausting ceremonial life without arranging a farewell; curiosity about how she is remembered now competes with her pleasure in being absent. She particularly misses the irreverent music excluded from formal occasions.
- Counterforce or complication: Hewlin, once a junior palace page, now earns his living abroad. Finding her alive scrambles mourning into old deference. He wants news of the person he knew and company at the memorial, but cannot comfortably invite his sovereign as an ordinary acquaintance. Neither he nor the settled government seeks her return. The memorial's polished account conceals neither persecution nor a restoration plot; it simply cannot contain her experience.
- POV, distance, and information limit: First-person past, close to Madelon's sensory pleasures, partial memories, and inconvenient vanity. Establish genuine royalty and voluntary flight early. Hewlin's life after the palace arrives through what he chooses to tell her; she has had no privileged knowledge of events abroad.
- Governing movement and time shape: One week, from first recognition through two short closing-time encounters to the memorial. Seven years appear through accumulated pool habits. Expand the first address and the observance; compress arrangements and travel. Target 2,500–4,000 words.
- Speculative rule or ordinary-world constraint: Madelon can turn her living body and worn clothing into a coherent volume of apparently ordinary water, reforming a transparent swimmer or solid person. She retains local senses, muffled underwater hearing, and identity; she cannot possess the pool, hear distant speech, or command other water. Rest is possible in liquid form. Speech requires a formed mouth above the surface. Ordinary witnesses can see her face when she takes shape.
- Dialogue promise: A whispered royal address revives unequal habits between two people whose lives have continued separately. Formal mourning then collides with affectionate, petty, incomplete memories.
- Dialogic medium: Low poolside speech interrupted by approaching swimmers; hesitant personal conversation; a printed memorial program; communal song, anecdote, and ritual responses.
- Dialogue engine: Hewlin initially addresses an office while Madelon wants gossip about a life. Her interruptions change what he risks telling her. At the memorial, listening without correcting becomes difficult; joining the music lets her participate without reclaiming the ceremony.

## Voice

- Narrative texture: Sensuous, amused recollection anchored in changing bodily density, distorted sound, and the ordinary things seven years made beloved. Permit extended attention and unflattering admissions.
- Conversational texture: Royal formulas beside hesitant catching-up, irrelevant questions, truncated gossip, and songs remembered unevenly. Let simple answers remain simple.
- Rhetorical ownership: Madelon owns ceremonial language and affectionate mockery of it, but misreads Hewlin's adult reserve as a page's obedience. Hewlin owns his present life and can refuse nostalgia without delivering a judgment on monarchy. Mourners remember different encounters rather than forming an explanatory chorus.
- Pressure behavior: Madelon becomes fastidious about small inaccuracies when larger feelings threaten. Hewlin initially overexplains, then leaves some questions unanswered. Song can outrun either speaker's composure.
- Relationship movement: She wants an unsanitized account without admitting she cares. He wants recognition as the adult who found her, and a companion rather than another audience. Exchanges replace assumed access with specific, imperfect knowledge.
- Anti-default: Do not turn recognition into a rescue summons, an abdication negotiation, or a wise helper coaxing a frightened ruler back into life. Her appetite for her own memorial supplies movement.

## Beats

1. Establish seven years of genuine quiet through the blue tiled pool: private transparent swimming, bodies approaching, dissolution, familiar daily rhythms. The inspected reference supplies submerged light and a humanoid outline almost lost among tile reflections. One pleasure should be useless to plot. Hewlin kneels at the deep end and whispers the required “Your Majesty,” while she is already indistinguishable from water.
2. Her first response is concealment, then curiosity. Explain his recognition concretely: during an opening check from the upper gallery, he saw her briefly reform below, before approaching. He knew her face at the palace. Let their awkward exchange establish his former role, independent adult livelihood, and the coming memorial. He accepts that she is alive before understanding why she stayed away.
3. At closing, Madelon questions the printed program. The official account is recognizable but bloodless; she seizes on an irritating detail while avoiding the larger fact that people have mourned her. Hewlin supplies limited firsthand memories and distinguishes them from news. His own years contain pleasures and disappointments she never witnessed. No duty argument settles their encounter.
4. Her curiosity grows into a wish to attend the local expatriate observance. Hewlin gives its ordinary location and arrangements without engineering a disguise or commanding her departure. She chooses to leave the pool, reforming fully and wearing borrowed ordinary outer clothes over her transformed bathing costume. Compress practicalities; let weight and air make the decision bodily.
5. In a hired hall, modest attendance and improvised ceremony displace her imagined grand funeral. A formal account gives way to uneven personal recollections: fondness, a small grievance, an inconsequential remembered encounter. She cannot correct every story without announcing herself. Expand what she enjoys hearing as well as what hurts.
6. A familiar song reaches a verse court occasions omitted. Madelon joins before she has planned to, exposing feeling rather than identity; retain comic imperfection and the possibility that others remember different words. Hewlin responds as a fellow participant. End within this unplanned participation and its immediate emotional consequence, without resolving her future residence, announcing a political lesson, or explaining what the song proves.
