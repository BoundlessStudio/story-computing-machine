# Outline

## Story

- Premise and central promise: In **When Death Knelt**, winchwright Derva opens her door and finds the god of death kneeling. He begs her to choose **him** as her earthly avatar and champion. A siege engine repeats its last charge, taking masonry into each reset. The god has worship, power, and authority but no patron inside the ward ring, hence no material body. Derva must accept or refuse his service, then stop the engine before the reservoir sluices.
- Focal pressure or attachment: Derva maintains the lower district’s counterweights and will not surrender its homes or evacuation route. She distrusts divine arrangements that turn need into obedience; patronage gives her command while making her responsible for the death god’s reach. Her overcontrol creates risk: she acts on mechanical knowledge before hearing which parts of the spell have truly ended. The god can perceive those boundaries but cannot choose what the city should sacrifice.
- Counterforce or complication: The engine is a fixed, nonliving war focus. A demonstration filled it with living magic; a broken stop-pin makes it replay every seven minutes, replacing damage with nearby stone, metal, and timber. A lieutenant proposes diverting it through homes to save reservoir and bridge. Derva attempts a sluice-gallery trap that might preserve both, but the god’s power consumes her momentum.
- POV, distance, and information limit: Close third-person past through Derva, attending to load, clearance, and her slowing body. She knows machinery, not divinity. The god explains only his local authority and the engine’s pattern. His thoughts, remote decisions, souls, and afterlife procedure remain unavailable.
- Governing movement and time shape: One dusk-to-dark pursuit across four circuits: threshold, evacuation street, diversion winch, sluice gallery. Each recurrence alters the route and the patron-champion hierarchy. Derva chooses the god, fails through premature direction, learns to combine his knowledge with her judgment, creates a physical ending he can enforce, and dismisses him when the bounded cause is complete. End with private repair, not public conversion, policy, or farewell explanation.
- Speculative rule or ordinary-world constraint: This city’s ward ring lets a deity materialize only through a freely chosen living patron. The usual rite makes a mortal the god’s champion; its inverse makes the god the mortal’s avatar and champion, bound to a stated cause. It creates no worship, destiny, or soul claim. The death god stays near Derva’s shadow, and each intervention borrows her momentum; stopping or losing mutual sight collapses his reach. He can terminate a spell or construct that has physically ended, but cannot kill healthy life, end an intact mechanism by assertion, command the dead, or decide what deserves destruction. The engine only stores and shapes magic previously supplied by life.

## Voice

- Narrative texture: Move at a winchwright’s pace—force, clearance, wear, route—then widen briefly to awe. Show divine scale through altered physical behavior rather than ceremonial language or lore summary.
- Conversational texture: Derva uses work questions, revisions, and directions; the god answers with distinctions and uncomfortable admissions of dependence. Evacuees speak for immediate jobs or obstacles, never as a thematic chorus.
- Rhetorical ownership: Derva reasons through leverage and consequence. The god owns precision about completion and authority, not human value. The lieutenant argues by capacity, timing, and acceptable loss.
- Pressure behavior: Derva becomes prematurely directive, then asks narrower questions. Begging disrupts the god’s ceremony; action sharpens him. The lieutenant repeats numbers when his plan meets resistance.
- Anti-default: Avoid a sardonic reaper, chosen-one flattery, symmetrical quips, contract-law exchanges, lore lectures, and chains of polished maxims.

## Beats

1. The god’s request collides with an engine impact below Derva’s threshold. Any willing adult could patron him; hers was the last open door on the route. She refuses to serve him, and he makes the reversed hierarchy unmistakable.
2. Derva chooses the god as her avatar and champion for the bounded purpose of ending the charge without surrendering the district. His first intervention keeps falling stone from resetting but abruptly slows her, proving the cost.
3. Evacuation traffic supplies ordinary requests and obstructions. The god cannot erase an unfinished circuit; Derva sees the engine cannibalizing buildings for replacement parts and changes course.
4. At the diversion winch, the lieutenant defends sacrificing one residential lane to preserve reservoir and bridge. Derva’s unilateral alternative fails when she directs the god against an intact axle, wasting strength and time.
5. The god identifies a completed fracture; Derva devises how to extend it through the drive train. In the sluice gallery, she must keep moving while rigging an ordinary counterweight trap. Their talk narrows to report, question, decision, and revision.
6. On the fourth circuit, the counterweight folds the drive shaft after the prow clears the bridge. The god severs only the reset pattern on Derva’s behalf; nearly immobilized, she secures the manual sluice gate.
7. The lieutenant redirects crews to the wall. Derva verifies the inert engine, ends the bond, and gives one repair priority while sensation returns to her feet. The god leaves without claiming service or explaining the cosmos.

## Continuity

- Canon used: Godhood requires worship, power, and authority and may have local domain limits. Only life uses magic; nonliving devices may store and shape magic supplied by life. The underlying afterlife is not described.
- Boundaries and unknowns: Ward ring, inverse patronage, shadow-body, and reset are local. Establish no universal divine government, patron requirement, soul authority, or death-god taxonomy. The engine never chooses or casts; the god’s healthy-life limit is local.
