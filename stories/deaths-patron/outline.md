# Outline

## Story

- Premise: An unnamed lockbreaker returns to a city safehouse with a black iron seal stolen from an undying regent, only to find the male god of death at the doorstep—not there to collect the protagonist, but begging them to choose him and become his earthly avatar and champion before the regent turns stolen divine authority into a deathless army at dawn.
- Character want and pressure: The protagonist wants to finish the theft, free the regent's conscripted guards, and remain beholden to no master; the god needs the protagonist's living agency and hard-won access because a mortal-made ward bars him from the ritual bell tower, while pursuing guards and the approaching dawn leave no time to find another champion.
- Turn: When the god honors the protagonist's refusal even though it may cost him his remaining authority, while the regent offers compulsory immortality as freedom, the protagonist recognizes the difference between chosen allegiance and ownership, negotiates a revocable pact, and deliberately chooses the god of death.
- Ending: As the god's avatar, the protagonist breaks the ward, severs the guards' deathless bindings, destroys the regent's ritual without executing him, and restores the god's bounded local authority; at dawn the god offers to dissolve the pact, and the protagonist freely chooses to remain his champion and invites him across the doorstep as a partner.

## Beats

1. Near midnight, the protagonist reaches a borrowed safehouse after stealing the regent's black iron seal for a rebel cell; armored pursuers search the street, and a precise knock at the back door sounds like the end of the escape.
2. The visitor is the visibly weakened god of death, male and unmistakably supernatural. He says he has not come to take the protagonist, then drops to one knee and begs: choose him as patron, become his earthly avatar and champion, and carry his authority where he cannot go.
3. The god explains the deadline and why this mortal matters. The regent has diverted public rites, chained a company of guards into involuntary deathlessness, and raised a living-made ward around the ritual bell tower; at dawn the stolen seal will extend that binding through the army. The god retains secret worship, power, and local authority, but the ward admits only living agency, and the protagonist alone has both the seal and the skill to reach its socket.
4. The god states the proposed local pact plainly: a freely choosing avatar remains fully themself; the god may advise but neither possess nor command; the avatar gains armor, the sight of death-denying bindings, and a weapon able to sever only magic made from the god's stolen authority; the bond can be renounced. Each severed binding restores what the regent stole, so the god admits he benefits.
5. The protagonist refuses to exchange one ruler for another. The god accepts the answer and makes no move to coerce it just as the regent's deathless guards breach the safehouse; unable to act through the ward, he follows only as a fading shadow while the protagonist escapes across roofs with the seal.
6. At a rendezvous beneath the ritual bell tower, the protagonist finds the rebel cell captured. A bound guard, exhausted but unable to fall or disobey, quietly asks the protagonist to end the spell on him; the regent then offers rank and compulsory immortality if the seal is returned, turning the abstract threat into a choice between two kinds of power.
7. The protagonist tries to break the seal without a patron, but its deathless binding repairs every mundane cut. The god still refuses to take control, even as his form begins to unravel. The protagonist sets terms—no commands, no killing through their body, no punishment for refusal, and the right to end the pact—and the god swears to them within this local system.
8. The protagonist says the choice aloud and becomes the god's avatar without losing voice, memory, or will: shadow hardens into mobile armor, lock tools become a dark severing blade, and the stolen bindings become visible as taut lines running from guards to the tower.
9. In a fast ascent, the avatar combines lockbreaking and rooftop skill with the bounded gift, cutting the guards' compulsions rather than attacking the people beneath them. Freed guards release the rebels and hold off the regent's remaining loyalists, but in the ritual chamber the regent uses the tower's chains to wrench the stolen seal from the avatar and lock it into its socket.
10. The regent activates the dawn rite and offers the protagonist permanent invulnerability. The avatar rejects an eternity imposed on others, severs the regent's claim from the seal, and breaks the ritual; the regent becomes mortal and falls, but the protagonist saves him for judgment rather than letting the god's champion become an executioner.
11. With the bindings gone, the god crosses the tower threshold under his restored local authority, refuses to take the defeated regent, and leaves him to mortal judgment rather than claiming every death as his decision. He keeps his promise by offering to dissolve the avatar bond immediately.
12. At sunrise, back at the safehouse door, the protagonist chooses the continuing partnership now that refusal is genuinely safe. The god rises from his kneel, waits to be invited inside, and enters beside his champion rather than through them.
