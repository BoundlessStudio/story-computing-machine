# Opus review: When Death Knelt

Independent audit for a later editing pass. It is not a `review.md` verdict,
does not change canon state, and does not authorize edits by itself.

## Verdict

- Dialogue: REVISE — two exchanges fail on basic sense (the lane "isn't empty", "the word you left out"), the god's first use of power breaks the rule he just stated, and one clipped retort-and-objection register runs through most exchanges.
- Prose: PASS — concrete and mechanically vivid; only minor habits of epigram closers and one-line paragraphs.
- Continuity and prompt: REVISE — the prompt is delivered, but the reservoir-and-sluice stakes break the water-infrastructure policy, and a few timing and staging details don't line up.
- Edit scope: structural
- Existing review.md said: Verdict PASS, "Dialogue: PASS"; disagree. It missed two Ground failures and the retort/objection register, and it predates the water policy.

The dialogue moves a disaster plot well, but its three speakers share one register. Derva, the god and the lieutenant all speak in the same clipped, technical way. Derva ends nearly every exchange with a dry retort. The god explains his rules. In the second half the lieutenant is there mainly to raise the objection Derva answers next. Two exchanges don't make sense (L125–L135, L143–L147), and the god's first act contradicts the limit he has just described. The best material is the working middle: the failed axle, Derva swallowing an order and asking "Which pieces?", and "Its inner curve is hidden from me." There the relationship changes through behavior. The edit pass should keep the precision where it belongs (the god on endings, Derva on loads). It should cut the retorts and correction beats, give the god a spoken stake so his begging means something, and let strain and fear show in how people talk. Separately, the reservoir-and-sluice stakes need a new target under the water-infrastructure policy. The plot beats and the shape of the ending can stay.

## Edit guardrails

- Prompt: the god of death comes to her door and begs her to choose him as her earthly avatar and champion. He does not come to take her, and the reversed hierarchy must stay unmistakable. The story must resolve completely and stay within 2,500–4,000 words (it is now about 3,250). It is broadly accessible. No hospice, caregiving, family illness, intimate bereavement or end-of-life scenes. Keep the outward-facing supernatural-adventure register. Craft profile is prospective-2026-08-21, which allows one earned aphorism or articulate exchange.
- Universe: CANON rules.md "Divinity" says godhood needs worship, power and authority. The god can still say he lacks power here, but he shouldn't recite the taxonomy. LOCKED rules.md "Living agency and magic": the engine stores a charge supplied by forty living spellwrights and must keep no will, choice or appetite of its own. The ward ring, inverse patronage, shadow bond and reset are local to this story. Don't widen them into universal divine law.
- Internal rules every edited line must respect: the god can end only what has already finished ending. His reach holds only while each can see the other and Derva keeps moving. Each act he makes spends her motion. The bond covers only the cause she stated and ends when she dismisses him (see D2, D3).
- What works and must not be flattened: the lieutenant's habit of talking in numbers at the winch, and the axle-failure-to-collar turn. Also the continuous-motion rigging (L229), the count that breaks on two, and the crewman's question cutting off the god's farewell. See Preserve.

## Dialogue findings

### D1 · Blocking · "The lane isn't empty" gets an answer about drains

- Where: L141–L151, diversion winch, Derva and the lieutenant
- Text:
> "The lane isn't empty."
> "Last count says it is."
> "The drain runs under it. You put that much weight through those cellars, the culvert folds.
- Problem: Ground/uptake. The lieutenant has just said "Lane is empty", meaning no people are left there. Derva's flat contradiction can only mean people are still there, and he answers that ("Last count says it is"). Her next line drops the people claim and argues instead that the ground under the lane will collapse, which supports neither sense of "isn't empty." The reader can't tell what her first line meant. It exists to set up a correction. The story's main dilemma (ninety-one homes against the works) turns on this line.
- Fix: Open with the objection she actually has, that the lane can't carry the engine. The lieutenant's reply should then engage it, with his numbers as at L149. If the editor wants people still in the lane, name who they are and follow it through; don't do both. The drain and culvert are also water infrastructure (C1), and "cellars", already in the line, can carry the collapse alone. Illustrative only: "That lane's all cellars. Put the engine through them and they cave."

### D2 · Blocking · The sight-rule exchange contradicts what the god already said

- Where: L79 and L123–L135, street walk after the rope handoff
- Text:
> "If you stop moving, or lose sight of me, my reach here collapses.
> "I need you visible," she said.
> "Mutual sight," he said.
> "That was the word you left out."
- Problem: Ground/uptake and Punchline Patch. First, the god reaches to carry a coil of rope, and "I need you visible" doesn't follow: carrying rope doesn't hide him, and the reader can't tell what she's worried about. Second, at L79 he already gave her both halves of the rule ("Stay where I can see you" and "lose sight of me"), and at L125 she is already acting on it. The cart teaches neither of them anything new. "That was the word you left out" blames him for an omission that didn't happen, and his "Yes." agrees to it. The exchange is built to deliver a correction about a word.
- Fix: Give her a reason to refuse his help that the story has already set up: his every act spends her motion (L97). Let the cart show the rule in action (his body going translucent, his foot sinking into the paving) without either of them naming it. Cut "Mutual sight" / "That was the word you left out" / "Yes." If a spoken beat is wanted, make it practical, such as where she'll walk or where he'll keep to. Illustrative only: "Leave it. Anything you lift, my legs pay for."

### D3 · Major · The god's first use of power breaks his own rule

- Where: L87–L95, falling cornice over the handcart at the entry arch
- Text:
> "The wall has already lost them. They're still falling."
> "Then end their return to the engine."
- Problem: Ground/verb and rule consistency. The god has just said he can end only "what has finished ending" and that the live charge "has not ended" (L61–L65). The red threads pulling the stones are that live charge, yet he ends them without comment. "Return" is also the wrong verb. The stones never belonged to the engine; the threads are dragging them toward it. His reply is a riddle the reader has to decode (their break from the wall is complete, their fall is not), and Derva's request doesn't point at the finished part. This is the first demonstration of his power. If it wobbles, the axle failure (L159) and the collar success (L191), which depend on the rule holding, lose their force.
- Fix: Have the exchange apply the rule in plain words. What has finished is the stones' break from the wall. What he ends is the charge's hold on broken stone, the same move he later makes with the collar ("The charge treats the pieces as joined"). That also sharpens the axle beat: Derva has already seen the right kind of request work and still asks for an intact part. Replace "return" with a verb that matches what the threads are doing. Illustrative only: "I cannot stop a fall. The charge is holding stone the wall already let go. That hold I can end."

### D4 · Major · The door exchange runs as a lore briefing

- Where: L47–L69, landing, before the choosing
- Text:
> "You want me to serve you," Derva said.
> "No." The god's ceremony broke cleanly on the word.
> "They gave me worship. Elsewhere, I have power and authority. Here, the ring admits neither through prayer alone."
> "I can end what has finished ending. A spent spell. A completed fracture. A dead construction.
- Problem: Exposition and Philosopher. He has said "As your earthly avatar and your champion", which makes him hers. Derva's "You want me to serve you" is a flat misreading placed so he can say "No." and explain the reversal. Three rule speeches follow in a row. The first covers the ward-ring rite. The second recites the worship, power and authority taxonomy almost as the universe rules state it. The third lists endings in triplets and closes on a clause about "which part of your city should be lost", heading off the lieutenant's dilemma before anyone has raised it. The narration twice says his ceremony broke and his fear showed (L35, L49), but his sentences never break. Derva then chooses him right after he says he can't do what she asked, and only the narration at L67 says why.
- Fix: Keep the kneeling, "Please choose me", "You have the wrong flat", "Choose you for what?" and "Freely?". Let the reversal come out of her question rather than a staged misreading; she can simply ask what he means. Cut the taxonomy line down to what she needs: he can't take a body here without a living patron, and his worshippers aren't here. Reduce L61 to one short statement of what he can end, without the triplets or the "which part of your city" clause. Let his fear show in how he speaks at least once, such as a restart, a broken-off sentence or a plain please. Give Derva a reason to choose him that belongs to the moment (the bell, the evacuation lane, having nothing better), spoken or acted, so L67 isn't carrying the decision alone.

### D5 · Major · The lieutenant's countdown doesn't match the engine's movement

- Where: L149, L185, L221, L231–L235, L247, from the winch to the gallery
- Text:
> "Six degrees. Ninety-one dwellings. We have four minutes."
> "Three minutes to impact. Ninety-one dwellings still standing. No diversion. What now?"
> Outside, the engine vanished in backward thunder.
> He repeated, "One minute forty," but he set both hands to the pin with Derva.
- Problem: Ground/timing. He says "four minutes" moments before the third circuit reaches the winch (L155–L173). He says "Three minutes to impact" while that engine is rolling past them toward the bridge. On the second circuit, that stretch took about a minute (L105–L137). The third circuit then hits nothing on the page and resets at L231, so the reader can't tell what the countdown was counting toward. "One minute forty" is repeated after the pin work has used up time, and nothing marks it as a stale figure.
- Fix: Decide what he is counting toward, either the next reset or the next arrival at the gate, and keep every figure consistent with the action in between. Add one sentence before L231 showing where the third circuit ends, such as another glancing blow or a stall on the bridge. Either update the repeated figure or make clear he's repeating the old number to press her. Keep his habit of speaking in numbers.

### D6 · Major · The lieutenant becomes an objection chorus in the gallery run

- Where: L211–L223, L237–L247, L263–L269, from the collar to the gate wheel
- Text:
> "That opens the west gate."
> "It drops, west gate rises."
> "A handspan puts water on the gallery floor."
> "Prow clear. Second wheel."
> "Those are different moments."
> "I heard you."
- Problem: Explanatory chorus, Tennis Match, correction ping-pong. After the winch scene the lieutenant's lines exist mostly to raise the next mechanical objection so Derva can answer it. He raises the same gate objection twice (L213, L239), even though she answered it the first time ("Not if I dog the manual wheel"). His readback at L265 contains no error, yet she corrects it and he snaps back, a correction with nothing to correct. The runs alternate lines of equal length with no interruptions or beats. His habit of speaking in numbers, which set him apart at the winch, fades away.
- Fix: Cut the second gate objection (L239–L243) and have him act or give a number. Keep "You'll be in the gallery." / "Yes." It is the one place he registers what she is risking. Either cut L267–L269 or make his readback wrong in a way that matters to the timing. Break up the alternating rhythm with action (the pin, the rope, the crews) instead of more lines.

### D7 · Major · Derva closes most exchanges with a dry retort

- Where: L33, L73, L121, L223, L245; also L133 (D2) and L267 (D6)
- Text:
> "That isn't better. Choose you for what?"
> "Don't make me repeat myself."
> "Mine now."
> "Then stop spending them here."
> "Then we'll have wet boots. Pin."
- Problem: Epigram and last-word lines used as the default. Nearly every exchange Derva is in ends on a clipped comeback, always the same dry, dismissive, faintly legal wit. It turns a winchwright under mounting physical strain into a steady source of one-liners, and it is the main thing that makes the dialogue sound authored. The outline's own anti-default ("symmetrical quips") names this.
- Fix: Keep "You have the wrong flat." It works as her assuming Death has come for someone else. Keep at most one other retort. Replace the rest with a plain answer, an order, an action, or nothing: "Freely?" can get a plain yes, "Mine now" can go, the lieutenant can run without "Then stop spending them here", and "wet boots" can become the order it really is. Let the drain on her body reach her speech earlier than L321, where "Keep—" / "Working." already shows how.

### D8 · Major · The god's stake is never spoken, and the farewell is a clause

- Where: L181–L183, after the axle fails; L347–L355, gallery ending
- Text:
> His face resolved inches from hers, ceremony gone, fear plain.
> "It had not broken," he said. "I couldn't begin the break."
> "Cause ended?"
> "Then the bond is done. I dismiss you."
- Problem: Relationship movement and a procedure routine. The prompt's hook is a god of death who begs, but nothing he says tells Derva or the reader what he wants from this or what he fears. "Fear plain" is narration, followed by a status report. Their final meaningful exchange is a contract check ("Cause ended?" / "The repetition is gone. The machine cannot charge." / "Then the bond is done. I dismiss you."), exactly the kind of "contract-law exchange" the outline meant to avoid. What changed between them never reaches their speech: she learned to ask instead of order, and he spent her body and failed once in front of her.
- Fix: Give the god one plain line of want, either at the door (with D4) or just after the axle fails, where the narration already says his fear is showing. Make it specific to him, not a thesis: what an unending charge means to a god of endings, or what he came here unable to prevent. Keep the ending short and keep the crewman's interruption. Replace the clause check with one exchange that belongs to these two people, such as the question she didn't ask at the door or a plain release in her own words. The dismissal can stay if the rite needs it. Don't add a thank-you speech or a moral.

### D9 · Minor · The hook exchange gives Derva the god's vocabulary

- Where: L251–L259, rigging the snatch block in the gallery
- Text:
> "Then stand where you can see it when it loads. Tell me which failure finishes. Don't choose for me."
> He bowed his head once. Not submission. Attention.
- Problem: Identical Voices and Philosopher. "Which failure finishes" is the god's completion idiom in Derva's mouth. "Don't choose for me" turns the patron hierarchy into a spoken line, and the god already stated that limit himself at L61. The narration then explains his bow. The prose also doesn't show him staying in her sight when he moves to look at the hook's inner curve.
- Fix: Keep "The hook may end first." / "May?" / "Its inner curve is hidden from me." Give Derva a plain instruction in her own trade terms, drop "Don't choose for me", cut "Not submission. Attention.", and place him where she can still see him.

### D10 · Minor · Street errands: the key rope arrives by chance, and the chest order leaves the stair blocked

- Where: L111–L121, evacuation street
- Text:
> "Then it stays. Move the people."
> "Winchwright, is this yours?"
- Problem: The thin rope that makes the climax work turns up because a girl happens to offer it, and the exchange exists to produce "Mine now." "Then it stays. Move the people." leaves the stair jammed without saying where the people go.
- Fix: Have Derva take or ask for rope because a winchwright heading into trouble would want some, or keep the girl and cut the retort. Say where the people go, for example another stair or over the chest.

### D11 · Minor · Narration explains lines right after they're spoken

- Where: L67, L103, L187, L203, L323 (L259 is covered in D9)
- Text:
> That answer was useless, but not vague. Derva preferred a useless measurement to a hopeful lie.
> It was not a joke. His eyes were on the charging engine.
> He was not gloating. He wanted a line he could use.
> He did not explain that.
- Problem: Mechanics. Each gloss tells the reader how to take the line just spoken, usually through a "not X" reversal, so there is nothing left to infer. L67 also carries Derva's entire reason for choosing the god (D4).
- Fix: Cut the glosses or replace them with a physical beat. If the "measurement" motif is wanted, keep at most one of the paired lines (L67, L203).

### D12 · Minor · Clipped technical referents

- Where: L201, collar exchange; L263, gallery instructions
- Text:
> "The shaft, badly. Until the load finds the seam."
> If the weight hits the prow, it rolls into the wall.
- Problem: Ground/referent. Once the collar has dropped off, "the seam" has no clear object, since L189 described the seam as running through the collar. At L263, "it" could be the weight or the engine, and "the wall" could be the gallery wall or something else. The reader has to guess what the danger is.
- Fix: Name the crack in the shaft, and say what rolls where.

## Prose findings

### P1 · Minor · Aphoristic narration and "not X" reversals

- Where: L13, L41, L77, L273, L277
- Text:
> It had no driver, no will, no appetite. A broken stop-pin was enough to make catastrophe look purposeful.
> Nothing she did was ordinary while the god spent her motion.
> No life animated it. The fixed drum beat red, giving the same command to a road that changed beneath it.
- Problem: The narration keeps ending paragraphs on a polished maxim, a negation triplet ("no driver, no will, no appetite"), or a reversal ("Not a tenant's knock.", "not painful but exact"). One or two would read as voice. Together with D11, they give the prose the same epigram habit as the dialogue.
- Fix: Keep the concrete detail in each paragraph (the washers, the cable, the stolen shutters) and cut or flatten the closing maxim. Keep the fact that the engine has no will or life (LOCKED "Living agency and magic"), just not in triplet form. Leave L335's line about choosing to sit; it earns its turn.

### P2 · Minor · One-sentence paragraphs as the climax pulse

- Where: L273–L317, bridge strike and gate wheel
- Text:
> The engine burst onto the bridge.
> The prow crossed the far edge of the bridge.
> The manual wheel spun faster.
- Problem: About ten isolated one-line paragraphs in forty-five lines. Because everything stands alone, the key beats (the strike, the hook splitting, the drum stopping) lose emphasis.
- Fix: Fold connected beats into the paragraphs around them. Keep the counterweight strike (L289) and the drum stopping (L309) on their own lines.

## Continuity and prompt findings

### C1 · Major · Water infrastructure carries the stakes and the climax

- Where: L57, L137, L141, L147, L151, L167, L211, L227, L243–L245, L271, L277, L281, L317, L329
- Text:
> The reservoir sluicehouse stood beyond the next bend.
> Either failure would put half the reservoir through the lower district.
> The sluice gallery was a stone throat built along the reservoir wall.
> A shallow sheet of reservoir water washed through the gallery drains and into the tailrace, nothing more.
- Problem: Policy. `universe/style-guide.md` "No default water infrastructure" (CANON) bars reservoirs, sluices, drains, culverts and flood-control works as a default source of plot, stakes, expertise or texture unless the request asks for them, and this prompt doesn't. Here they supply the stakes (the reservoir), the climax setting (the sluice gallery), the dilemma (homes against "the water"), and incidental texture (drain, culvert, drain grates, tailrace, "river-smoothed iron"). The policy applies to authorized revisions, and this edit pass is one.
- Fix: Move the engine's target to something that is neither water infrastructure nor another routine public-works problem, ideally tied to this story's own elements such as the arsenal the engine came from or the ward ring. Keep the non-water mechanics that make the climax work: the counterweight, service line, snatch block, gate wheel, pawl and bridge. Dialogue lines that change with it: L141, L147, L151, L167, L239–L245. If the owner rules the re-siting out of scope for this pass, at minimum remove the incidental instances (drain and culvert at L147 and L151, "drain grates" at L277, "gallery drains" and "tailrace" at L329, "river-smoothed" at L227). The edit scope then drops to heavy-dialogue.

### C2 · Minor · The opening blow vs. the six-minute count

- Where: L17–L19, Derva's flat
- Text:
> Far below, something hit the lower district hard enough to make the apartment lean.
> Six minutes and some change since the last impact.
- Problem: The reader takes L17 as the engine's impact. L19 then says the last impact was six minutes ago and the reset is due, and the reset comes within a minute (L43). What hit at L17 is unclear.
- Fix: Make the L17 blow something else, such as a weakened wall finally giving way, or make it the impact and re-time the count.

### C3 · Minor · Small staging gaps

- Where: L189 and L207; L299; L173 and L189
- Text:
> Derva rolled onto one knee.
> Here, with a true ending before him, he became difficult to see at all.
- Problem: (1) Derva is on one knee at L189, yet "had kept walking this time" at L207, with no beat of her getting up. (2) At L299 the god becomes "difficult to see", which sits badly with the rule that his reach collapses if she loses sight of him; the reader may expect him to fade. (3) The axle's "collar and bearing" (L173) and the drive shaft's "bronze collar" (L189–L197) are different parts with similar names, and readers can confuse them. If the failed axle strike is what cracked the bronze collar, the story doesn't say so.
- Fix: Add half a sentence of her getting up and walking. Make L299 clearly about the lamps and shadows, not about her losing sight of him. Name the two parts differently or state the causal link.
