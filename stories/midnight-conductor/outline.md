# No Room for Banners

## Promised experience

Certain city buses cross from the human world into the realm of forgotten gods at midnight in the heaviest rainstorms. A young beastfolk secretly works as a conductor on the crossing and takes lost spirits to places gone from every map. “nope; i dont like it try again” asks for a fresh story and leaves the cause of dissatisfaction open. No particular relationship, adversity, scale, or ending is specified.

I read the pleasure as a secret journey hiding in plain sight. The source image puts a watchful young rider within warm reach of the green bench and hems her in with rain. Beyond that glass should be a much larger, stranger geography. The conductor should have to do the guiding, and a stop should mean something worth crossing for. This take uses the closeness of the bus for the human scale and lets the landscape turn on the scale of a god.

## Takes considered

- **Escape on one run.** A former god diverts a city bus toward her ceremonial court to conscript the ghosts aboard. The conductor sees a chance to set them down at an erased loading yard as the god bends the street alongside it. I chose this for the moment when the girl has to call an impossible stop while the right curb is still in reach. The bus, realm, secret job and lost destination all cause the turn.
- **A hostile fare.** An erased neighborhood's surveyor rides alone to the little court where she used to measure the starting line. In a two-hander the conductor has to decide whether to fulfill that request when the surveyor reveals why the place was taken off the map. The strongest scene is a reckoning while the bus waits at an address the surveyor did her best to destroy.
- **A living passenger's account.** A skeptical night-shift regular catches the wrong bus during a storm. After the conductor helps a ghost down at a vanished shelter, she asks the regular whether to put him off safely in the human city or take him all the way to see what another rider is afraid to see alone. The testimony makes the hidden service a discovery, with the cost of becoming known as its tension.

## Provisional direction

Mette opens up the second part of her Crookwall run after the normal riders go home. Only the driver is in on it. Mette wants the Marshal to give her a whole route of her choosing, beyond the few turnings she knows. Rella, a dead candle-yard porter who has found newer streets where every remembered turning should be, boards with other lost spirits headed for Tallow Yard. No map shows the yard anymore. Its loading gallery is still there in the gods' realm. When the Marshal's parade cut the city in two, the yard was the place the porters could step out of the parade, catch their breath and go home by their own roads.

The Marshal, once worshipped as the patron of processions, takes an assembled company for an occasion and draws the street and the bus toward her court. She offers to lay any of her old roads for Mette whenever she asks, if Mette calls the court as their stop. Mette refuses and calls Tallow Yard. The Marshal lifts the street faster, nose up, to take them past the yard before anyone can get down. The front door rises past the freight gallery, but Mette judges that the rear door, lower on the newly steep grade, still has its moment. She asks the driver to brake at the rear and crosses first. As the Marshal draws the road toward the court, the gap between the bus and the gallery opens. Mette stays to get the last rider down, losing her chance to get aboard again. She sends the driver down the return road while the rain is still coming hard enough to get home.

The local rule belongs to the Marshal. She can move streets on which she once led a procession. She can still exert her power after losing her worship and standing as a god. The yard has never been one of her streets. Lost spirits are continuing people and can walk from it wherever their own roads lead. This run is their way out of a specific quarter, with no claim about anyone's universal afterlife.

## Craft plan

**Teller.** Try close third in Mette's present experience, alert to angles, sounds and how long people take to find their feet. Her pleasure in anticipating a turn makes her a little smug about knowing where others will go. Try first person past as a livelier, slightly boastful account of the night she missed the bus, with a teller still vexed at a good decision's cost. Keep the wit when she is frightened.

**Mette.** Watchful and content to sit still, she takes in what a rider does before she asks them anything. She can be a little too pleased when she's guessed the answer. She wants Rella to trust her without hovering, and the Marshal to see how good she is at the job. Her speech is practical, with room for a small joke and a pause for a rider to catch up. Her school blazer, red bow and dark plaid skirt belong to her life on the human side. Tomorrow morning she owes her partner the opening of a paired oral exam. Both have practised for it. She wears her headphones as the ordinary schoolgirl, removes them to work, and leaves them on the green bench as the driver takes the bus home without her. The source shows a youthful, feminine-presenting rider, composed in profile, the visible eye rose-colored. The pointed grey-white ears turn before her shoulders. At the lift the light moves up her silver hair as she listens for the gallery's loose shutter. She draws her long pale tail in at the ordinary doors, then lets it swing for balance out over the gap. She pulls her bow loose to mark the place to step. Keep the striped hosiery in the silhouette and the gaze on her work.

**Rella.** The porter knows the yard better than he knows the current city, and has sent his companions in two wrong directions already. He wants Mette to commit to a stop before he risks promising them another one. He suspects she works for the Marshal, and is embarrassed to need a kid's help even once he trusts her. He can describe an entire shift when he means to give one direction, in loading weights, widths, the nick on a hook and the things a boss failed to see. When hurried he starts speaking directly to the shyest member of the party. His own want is to take the yard's back gate for once without being in charge of the people behind him.

**The Marshal.** She remembers what it felt like for streets to fill with people who had answered of their own accord. She believes she can get it back by bringing people to the place where they used to want to go. She sees Mette knows how to bring a company to a stop. She addresses people as if she's pausing to let an entire room quiet, gracious about minute hardships, increasingly precise in the imperative. Let one remembered figure from the old march show what a caller meant to her as she makes her offer. The old streets rise at her gesture, a whole other hillside lights for the march, and the people she meant to welcome are forced to hold on.

**The driver.** Keep her unnamed. On the city side she wants the girl to pass for a passenger. When the run is taken she wants Mette at the front to help her get the bus back to the seam. The driver can hold the bus where Mette asks, at the risk of missing the return with her. Her one blunt deadline lets Mette choose who to put ashore first and when to send the bus home.

**The conversation owed.** As the bus is taken, Mette has to hear why the Marshal wants a living caller and answer the actual offer. Rella has to tell Mette how the porters slipped out through the yard when the procession cut the city in two. This is an argument over where the bus will deliver everybody. Rella needs the girl to hear him over the offer. Mette needs him to tell her in the order she can use. The Marshal wants the conductor to call her court as a voluntary stop.

**Place.** Crookwall's candle quarter is now a city bus bend between a late bakery and grade-separated truck lanes. Aster Street's terminus lacks the grade and Ragbank Viaduct the old loading entrance. On the gods' side the upper courts sit at fantastic heights. In one, a many-sided god turns lamps toward the second bus and the light comes up through the road. Nearer, the Marshal can lift the procession street where it runs past Tallow Yard, but the yard's brick gallery stays where it is, a few feet above street level. Behind it are two lines of loading doors around a square open to the sky. Late autumn, midnight to the fading of one downpour. Bread racks rattle across the human shopfront. The green bench is warm under the coat of a standing passenger. The rear doors hiss. A high shutter knocks twice against an iron staple. Old candle fat smells sweet as it warms in the lanterns. Three white wick hooks mark the gallery sill. Both the low human curb before midnight and the high loading yard after it ask Mette to watch where the bus doors will land.

## Scenes and ending

1. **11:58, Crookwall bend and first stop after the crossing (short).** A shift worker recognizes Mette's school uniform and assumes she's ridden past her stop. She keeps her headphones on and lets the driver call the stop for him. She's listening to the practice recording for the paired exam, where she's promised her nervous partner she'll speak first in the morning. When the worker has gone, she steps up to the driver with a list of her own. They have to get back while the rain hits hard enough to keep both roads in sight. The hardest part of the rain erases the far lane at midnight. A second bus takes a different god-road. Mette works the chime, names a vanished tiled shelter and waits for the step down to be clear for one ghost before Rella speaks for his company. She thinks they can do both the Yard and her own errand. Rella hears her say they'll pass the Marshal's court and asks her to put them off first.
2. **The road rising to the court (long scene, more than half).** Streets tilt uphill together. The driver asks Mette to stay aboard and guide the bus back as soon as there's a way through. At the offer from the Marshal, Rella tells Mette how the porters found the gallery by the loose shutter when the old procession cut them off. Mette hears it through the open driver's window. She turns down the Marshal and calls Tallow Yard. The Marshal's answer is to steepen the road further to push the front door past the gallery. Mette almost calls the chance gone with it. Then she moves down the aisle and sees the rear door at the level of the wick hooks. She tells the driver to stop with the back door to the yard. The driver's look comes back down the length of the bus before she brakes. Mette crosses the short gap to open the stiff shutter and give the passengers a place to put their feet. As the tilted road crawls sideways toward the court, the gap widens. The last rider stalls because they can see between the bus and the gallery. Mette could still leap back, but the child needs her hand on the far side. She calls them by their yard and stays. The driver takes a narrow turnout onto the return road after Mette tells her to go.
3. **The Tallow Yard loading gallery and open yard (short).** The rain thins. Mette sees the driver make the return and her lit bench with the headphones sitting on it. When Rella catches up with her, he's carrying a short plank he has just fetched from its old well-worn shelf by touch. He sets it back across the two loading blocks they used to sit on when they got clear of the procession, and sits there for as long as he likes. Someone pulls the gate open. Beyond it, path lights answer feet going in different directions out of the quarter. Rella invites Mette to come down the yard with him. She stops at the gate to watch him take his own way, and hears the shutter knock again from the inside.

Keep her induction and the longer wait until the next storm outside the frame. The ordinary curb alignment, the Marshal's offer to have Mette stand at the head, and Rella's memory of sitting a little while in the yard load the last images.
