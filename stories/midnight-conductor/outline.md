# Provisional outline

## Promised experience

The request gives us real city buses that cross at midnight in the heaviest rain, a realm of forgotten gods, and a young beastfolk secretly working as a conductor for lost spirits headed to places erased from maps. I read “works secretly” and the image of the composed, headphone-wearing rider as an invitation to discover an entire job inside a familiar night ride. “Destinations that no longer exist on any map” promises the pleasure of someone knowing exactly where an apparently impossible stop is, and of a passenger actually getting there. The warm bus in the image suggests intimacy and the thunderstorm admits a much larger country outside its windows. The request sets no particular relationship, scale of conflict, or kind of ending.

## Takes considered

1. **A stop in motion.** On an otherwise working run, a forgotten god walks off with the stair that a lost passenger needs. The scene to write is the young conductor getting a municipal bus ahead of an immense figure and making a request stop where there is no curb.
2. **From the seat beside her.** A recently dead night baker boards through habit and gradually realizes that the girl with headphones is conducting the bus. The encounter to write is his refusal of a former god’s offer of a new house when she has found the entrance to his real street. The rider’s discovery would drive the turn.
3. **The transfer.** Tell a comedy backwards from a scandal on the human radio: someone called a dance across a bus channel reserved for emergencies. At the center is a conductor at the last transfer point having to land two routes’ passengers as a forgotten god insists on announcing all the stops himself. The transfer could be terrific, though the retrospective would cost the pleasure of crossing in real time.

The first makes an erased address govern where the bus goes, what Nemi has to ask of a god, and when she can finally open the door. It gives the conductor and the rider an encounter that can change both their conduct without making the journey a lesson.

## Provisional direction

On Lornet’s midnight 17, seventeen-year-old Nemi looks like another student. She lets the last ordinary passengers past her, then guides a misplaced spirit off at Button Yard, an old factory court reached under Cresset Viaduct when Lornet’s heaviest rain hits at midnight. In the gods’ country a neighborhood can still have an address where a living former god has chosen to keep it. The dead can visit those places. This run makes no claim about their ultimate afterlife.

When Nemi comes to the spot where Clover Stair usually meets the road, there is bare ground. Up the slope the Long Dancer, once worshipped in processions, is carrying stair, landing, and dance hall away toward the other gods’ high country. Wolla, the last rider, has spent several storms waiting at the stair’s replacement stop in Lornet. She wants to reach the dance and find the sister who used to get her out on the floor. Now Nemi can keep the rainy return through Cresset Viaduct or try to intercept a destination that will soon be beyond any bus road. A shortcut comes from Wolla’s account of how the band used to get a van uphill. Nemi recognizes the right turn and takes the 17 round the inside of the Dancer’s long approach, where he can put the bottom step on solid ground by its door. The god would sooner lift Wolla up as he walks. Nemi asks him to put the stair down and give her an honest stop. Wolla wants to arrive under her own steam and tells him there is room at the dance for him too. He sets down an entire flight of stairs long enough for Wolla to step out of the bus and climb. Nemi sees whom Wolla asks to lead.

## Craft plan

**Teller and voice.** Try close-third past, in Nemi’s attentive, privately amused company, and a first-person account from Nemi after her return, with a brashness she never shows the ordinary riders. Both begin in the inhabited bus before the crossing.

**Nemi.** The original shows a girl with pale, long hair, pointed furred ears, one visibly pink eye, a pale tail curled on green bench upholstery, silver headphones, a dark blazer, a conspicuous red bow, and a plaid skirt over striped stockings. She sits straight and looks steadily outward. I read bold private attention and a willingness to wait until she sees what she came for. She slips in, settles, weighs, then moves decisively. Keep the clothes and silhouette in ordinary functional register. The headphones let her answer a passenger nobody else can see. She lifts a cup and pricks an ear to hear what the rain covers. Her tail, tucked clear of the aisle out of habit, has to come out of a closing tip-up seat as she goes forward. The bright bow gives the Dancer something at eye level to keep in sight as he brings the stair down. Nemi wants a cooperative rider and the satisfaction of getting an adult somewhere they can’t get alone. She knows the usual roads and mistakes that knowledge for the whole job. She talks in practical specifics and has more to say when she is explaining why an idea will work than when she is explaining herself.

**Wolla.** An older spirit, former dance-floor caller, generous with details and choosy about who gets to lead. She has tried the living city’s replacement stop and assumes a conductor who takes her there again will be another person offering convenient help she never asked for. She wants Nemi to believe that she has somewhere definite to go. She wants a place in the set after years of wanting to look as if she could take it or leave it. She uses instructions, names, anecdotes, and terms of endearment that can arrive with an edge. She briskly supplies concrete services instead of admitting when she’s scared.

**The Long Dancer.** A living former god with a stride that takes a whole square. He believes a place that drew no one to its old footing might as well travel with him out of the bus country. Having picked the stair up, he likes how little anyone asks him to do. He wants the bus clear of his stride and expects Wolla to accept being carried. He speaks expansively when asked about what he has seen and very plainly about what he intends to do. Wolla offers the prospect of joining a living gathering of company as an ordinary dancer in his own hall. Nemi wants him to stop striding long enough to see she has given him somewhere safe to put the stair.

**The conversation owed.** In the long scene, Wolla gets to tell Nemi why this particular stair is still where she needs to get off, and Nemi must tell Wolla what it will take to get the doors open there. Wolla can give the route through the band’s journey uphill while also telling Nemi, in order, about the last time her sister took her dancing. She hopes to persuade the conductor to go after the god, and she wants a more useful answer than sympathy. The driver is a supporting collaborator who knows the main route and takes directions from Nemi in the other country.

**Place.** Choose Lornet’s Cresset Viaduct and the old brick switchback under its slope over Ashbeach’s flat seafront loop or the night concourse at Fennock Central. In Lornet it matters which end of a lost street a bus can actually reach. On a cold November night the 17 takes hospital and bakery shifts home past the demolition site that used to be Clover Stair. Riders can hear the second wiper knock at each end of its sweep, smell damp school wool warming against the heater, see blown umbrella spokes rake the gold-lit windows, feel a long sideways pull on the green bench when the bus turns uphill, and hear brick from the god’s footfalls scurry down the other country’s road. Lornet’s blocked-off hill roads leave awkward half-usable daughters in the gods’ country. The old van turn offers a place where the right side of a bus can face the bottom of the Stair.

## Scenes and ending

1. **17 climbing to Cresset Viaduct, just before and at midnight (about a quarter).** Nemi wants the human riders to keep seeing an ordinary girl with headphones and the spirits to stay on until she calls them. Wolla sees somebody young who could take her word for the stop or condescend to her. Last humans off, midnight and rain visibly take the 17 under the viaduct and past former gods. Nemi straightens one lost rider out about Button Yard and opens a way off. Then Wolla tells her how long she has been trying to reach Clover Stair as they find the familiar curb without any stairs. Detail to remember: the empty space where the landing’s light should have struck the bus.
2. **Clover Stair’s former stop to the switchback and the Dancer (long scene, around three quarters).** Wolla names who she wants to find and describes the musicians’ way up, something Nemi doesn’t know. Nemi knows where that turn comes out and what leaving the usual route risks. She needs Wolla to take the prospect of failure seriously, then wants her to stand for a stop she can actually use. Wolla needs the girl to let her be good at something. Give their talk room while the coach has to inch around a corner. They get ahead of the Dancer and he voices his brisk offer to reach in and lift Wolla. With the bus positioned and the conductor calling the stop to him, he changes his mind and places the intact stair alongside the front door. Let the feelings and the physical distance of the last step fill time here. Nemi has been planning a whole way to go, and finally the passenger can go the last part herself. Detail to remember: the first tread coming out of the dark at the height of an ordinary pavement.

Stay with Nemi at the bus door for the ending. The woman Wolla came for meets her partway up, and after the stories about which sister always pulled the other out to dance, Wolla offers her hand first. The Long Dancer raises them and the lit stair away into his country while the 17 stands idling. The return rain may have thinned by then. This ride ends on arrival. The earlier runs, the history of Lornet’s religion, and the wait for the next crossing stay outside the scenes.
