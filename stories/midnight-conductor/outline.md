# Provisional outline

## Promised experience

The request gives us city buses crossing at midnight in the heaviest rain, a realm of forgotten gods, and a young beastfolk secretly conducting lost spirits to places erased from maps. I read “works secretly” and the composed rider in the supplied image as an invitation to discover a whole job inside a familiar night ride. “Destinations that no longer exist on any map” promises the pleasure of someone knowing the way to an apparently impossible stop and of a passenger actually getting there. The warm bus suggests intimacy, with an immense country outside its windows. The relationship, scale of conflict, and ending are open.

## Takes considered

1. **A stop in motion.** A forgotten god walks off with the stair a lost passenger needs. The young conductor must get a municipal bus ahead of an immense figure and make a request stop where there is no curb.
2. **From the seat beside her.** A recently dead night baker realizes the girl with headphones is conducting the bus. The rider's own discovery drives his refusal of a former god’s offer of a new house when she finds the entrance to his old street.
3. **The transfer.** Tell a comedy backwards from a scandal on the human radio. At the last transfer point the conductor has to land two routes’ passengers as a forgotten god insists on announcing all the stops himself.

The first makes an erased address govern where the bus goes, what Nemi has to ask of a god, and when she can finally open the door.

## Provisional direction

On Lornet’s midnight 17, seventeen-year-old Nemi looks like another student. For nights the newly dead Wolla has taken buses from the replacement shelter whose sign points to a shopping parade built over Clover Stair. They keep dropping her opposite locked shops. In the first midnight downpour since she died, Nemi hears her asking for the Stair and gets her on the 17, speaking into her headphones when an ordinary rider recognizes her school bow. Under Cresset Viaduct the bus crosses into the country where living former gods keep some of the places erased from Lornet. Nemi guides another lost spirit off at the old Button Yard.

Bare ground lies where Clover Stair usually meets the road. The Long Dancer, once worshipped in processions, is carrying stair, landing, and occupied dance hall into the gods’ high country. Wolla hopes to find her late sister there. At the foot of the Stair the dancers had a way of striking the iron first tread with a heel to call a partner down to meet them. Her sister used to fetch her. Tonight Wolla wants to call. In Wolla’s account of the band van’s approach, Nemi recognizes a shortcut to a level shelf ahead of the Dancer. She takes the 17 up to meet him before he leaves the last bus road.

The Dancer offers his hand to carry Wolla straight to the hall. His other hand is wrapped around the foot of the Stair. Wolla would have no tread to call from and no way to make the arrival she came for. Nemi gets the 17 into place and asks the Dancer to set the Stair down by the front door. He’s taking the place away because for years nobody from Lornet has asked for a way up. Wolla demonstrates the heel call on the bus floor and invites him to keep time for the dancers. He hears a call made in earnest and has a place to set the step down. He does, and Wolla gets off to make it again.

## Craft plan

**Teller and voice.** Try close-third past in Nemi’s privately amused company and first person from Nemi after her return, with a brashness she keeps from the ordinary riders. Both begin in the occupied bus before the crossing.

**Nemi.** The original shows long tousled silver-white hair, pointed ears, a pink eye, a pale tail curled on the green bench, silver headphones, dark blazer, red bow, plaid skirt, and striped stockings. She settles into the seat and attends to something out the window. I read someone who knows the stillness of these late rides and is content to keep herself company. She can make room for a rider without coming out of that reserve. She lifts a cup to hear through the rain, talks into it to pass as an ordinary student, and only leaves the bench when she has somewhere to go. Her tail comes out of its tucked place to brace at the bend. She slips off the bow to draw the Dancer’s eye. She wants the satisfaction of getting an adult somewhere they can’t get alone. She knows the usual roads and mistakes that knowledge for the whole job. She talks freely in practical specifics and has little to say about herself.

**Wolla.** Older spirit and former dance-floor caller, generous with details and choosy about who gets to lead. After the wrong buses she wants Nemi to believe her destination and wants a place in the set after years of pretending she could take it or leave it. Her speech follows remembered people and rooms. She uses an edged endearment or offers to do something if she’s scared.

**The Long Dancer.** A living former god with a stride that takes a whole square. He’s stopped expecting anyone to want the climb that used to honor him. He offers the quicker passage of his hand in the expectation that it’s what anybody in a bus would prefer. He speaks expansively about what he’s seen and plainly about what he’ll do. Wolla asks for the very steps he thinks are no longer wanted. Nemi gives him a place to put them.

**The conversation owed.** Wolla tries to win a detour by telling the story of a journey to the hall with the band and her sister, in order. Nemi needs her to give an account specific enough to act on, then needs to explain to her driver where he’ll be able to turn a full-sized bus around.

**Place.** Choose Lornet’s Cresset Viaduct and brick switchback over Ashbeach’s seafront loop and Fennock Central’s concourse. On a cold November night the 17 takes hospital and bakery shifts home past the demolished Stair. A wiper knocks at both ends of its sweep. Damp school wool warms against the heater and umbrella spokes rake the gold-lit glass. Once through the arch, the bus is on a road of dark brick high over dark country. It passes a former god sheltering from the storm beneath the altar table where no one lights her candles. The wet stone smells of old wax. The Dancer takes a broad traverse up broken ground that would wreck the 17’s tires. The musicians’ lane cuts inside his path, via a level loading shelf where the 17 can pull up with its door on the right before rejoining the loop. Falling bricks clatter downhill. Once the Dancer steps off the shelf toward the ridge, there's no bus road left.

## Scenes and ending

1. **17 climbing to Cresset Viaduct, at midnight (a quarter).** Wolla wants Nemi to take her word about the Stair after several rides to the wrong place. Nemi gets her aboard without giving her work away. The 17 goes under the viaduct once the humans are off. Show the crossing and the unlit altar, then give Button Yard a deft drop-off. They pull up by the place where the landing’s light should strike the bus. The ground is bare.
2. **Old stop to the switchback and the Dancer, after midnight (long scene, three quarters).** Wolla tells the story of the van, the heel call and her sister. Nemi recognizes the lane, promises her driver a place to turn and gets the 17 ahead of the Dancer. Give them time to talk as the bus inches around the turn. He offers Wolla his hand. Nemi calls for the bottom step and signals where he can set it down. Wolla sounds her call on the bus floor and asks him to keep time for the dancers. He puts down the Stair. Stay with her as the first tread comes out of the dark at the height of an ordinary pavement. Wolla gets off and calls from the Stair before she knows who will come.

Nemi signals the driver to hold the doors through Wolla’s call, until her sister comes down and Wolla offers her hand first. She gives the Dancer the all-clear. He raises the lit Stair away into his country while the 17 idles. Earlier runs, Lornet’s religious history, and the regular return stay outside the scenes.
