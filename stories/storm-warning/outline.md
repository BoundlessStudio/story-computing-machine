# Outline

## Story

- Premise and central promise: **Storm Warning.** Mabry frees her father's captive, expecting an avenger. Discoveries turn their escape into a struggle over who can leave whom. The released woman chooses possession over her husband's freedom, then makes the father hunt his daughter.
- Focal pressure or attachment: Mabry wants departure without abandoning the captive or admitting her complicity in forced returns. Viveca mourns Arvid and their radio guessing games: his premature humming always gave away the tune. Genuine tenderness concealed his wish to leave.
- Counterforce or complication: Renwick sells “recoveries” of adults who fled spouses, families, and employers. He loves Mabry but expects obedience. Viveca helped build his trade while pursuing Arvid. Both offer protection that requires dependence.
- POV, distance, and information limit: Close third past through adult Mabry. Mark her assumptions as assumptions; never suppress knowledge she possesses. Viveca's intentions emerge through choices.
- Governing movement and time shape: One storm night: confinement, street, holding house, attempted escape. Each discovery changes Mabry's action. Target 2,500–4,000 words; end with a new pursuit underway.
- Speculative rule or ordinary-world constraint: Entirely human. Crews abduct only during downpours: empty streets, obscuring umbrellas, masked resistance. Renwick's office-front red STORM WARNING sign announces his notorious retriever's release. Crews must suspend individual deliveries and assemble captives at the holding house. Residents recognize danger without knowing the business.
- Dialogue promise: Apparent alliance exposes incompatible meanings of rescue, marriage, and return through demands and refusals.
- Dialogic medium: Private speech, public sign, crew responses, recoil, and an opened escape door.
- Dialogue engine: Instructions enable release; familiar orders expose authority; Arvid's refusal destroys the presumed reunion. Renwick's final command makes his daughter's escape another recovery.

## Voice

- Narrative texture: Mabry reads permission: withheld keys, dry coats, who can interrupt. Wet cloth, sore feet, and obstructed sight keep discovery bodily; shame complicates her explanations without producing retrospective wisdom.
- Conversational texture: Unequal encounters, practical requests, interruptions, misplaced familial familiarity. Let incomprehension persist until action or an answer supplies information.
- Rhetorical ownership: Renwick owns courteous commercial euphemism and paternal teasing. Viveca recalls intimate particulars and converts concern into instructions. Mabry borrows her father's politeness until it fails. Arvid makes ordinary requests, without diagnosing his marriage.
- Pressure behavior: Mabry becomes less deferential and less fluent. Renwick bargains faster. Viveca slows when offered a choice. Arvid watches exits.
- Relationship movement: Mabry seeks an ally; Viveca recruits dependence. Renwick wants his daughter's return without admitting fear. Arvid wants separation neither captor will grant. Recognition changes whom Mabry unlocks doors for.
- Anti-default: Avoid a handler steering a weapon, an explanatory villain confession, or a final atrocity detached from the pursuit. Possessive love must generate the design throughout.

## Beats

1. Mabry recognizes a driver's payment as a killing fee: Renwick will dispose of Viveca during tonight's rainy “transfer.” Assigned to collect the prisoner's effects, Mabry obtains cabinet keys while the escort brings the car around. She unlocks the room, returns Viveca's requested red dress and confiscated serrated blade, then lights the warning as instructed. Renwick discovers his daughter's disobedience.
2. Barefoot beneath the sign, Viveca resembles the feared woman of street stories. Mabry expects crews to flee; cars carrying bound passengers instead change direction. Viveca commandeers one through authority. Mabry follows because she needs protection and fears what she has enabled.
3. Their route retraces the search for Arvid. Discrepancies accumulate: Viveca knew where he went but not why; his packed bag held only his belongings; a passenger recoils from her familiar restraint. Mabry discovers Viveca devised forced returns before her confinement. Coerced participation remains Mabry's possible explanation, not the narrator's excuse.
4. At the holding house, Renwick produces living Arvid to stop Viveca's attack. He sold Arvid refuge, then detained him as insurance against his pursuer. A returned ring and forged death notice ended Viveca's search; locked doors controlled her afterward. Renwick retains this emergency leverage until her death is certain. He offers reunion for obedience.
5. Mabry changes objectives and unlocks Arvid's restraint. He watches the exit, never the wife. His attempted departure, Viveca's automatic blocking gesture, and his specific rejection of going home establish whom he fled. Their tenderness conferred no right to keep him.
6. Mabry opens an escape door. Viveca has time and an alternative: let Arvid depart alive. She closes it herself and kills him when he still refuses reunion. Make the violence consequential and unmistakably chosen; grief and a diagnosis cannot excuse or replace that choice.
7. While Viveca turns on Renwick, Mabry opens the outer doors for waiting captives and flees with them, risking capture to undo part of her mistake. Viveca keeps Renwick alive and demands his daughter's recovery, threatening to pursue her personally if he refuses. He could countermand his crews at risk to himself; instead, he bargains for Mabry's survival and orders her return. His authority enables pursuit. The warning remains lit as Mabry enters the rain, now its quarry.
