# Opus review: Storm Warning

Independent audit for a later editing pass. It is not a `review.md` verdict,
does not change canon state, and does not authorize edits by itself.

## Verdict

- Dialogue: REVISE — one line of Viveca's plan depends on a premise the story never states, the car switch has two drivers under one label, and in the parlour Renwick falls into Viveca's clipped register.
- Prose: REVISE — concrete and restrained, with no em dashes, but the geography of the climax (parlour, hall, doorway, front door) cannot be followed at the killing.
- Continuity and prompt: PASS — the corrected human-noir premise, the layered twists, the rain, the sign and her release are all delivered; only two small continuity nits remain.
- Edit scope: targeted-dialogue
- Existing review.md said: Verdict PASS, "Dialogue: PASS"; I disagree on dialogue (Ground failures in the car ride and at L139) and on the internal staging.

This story's dialogue is better than the collection norm. It is short and
concrete, and several exchanges turn on objects (sheets, coats, keys, a ring)
instead of argument. Its failures are specific. Viveca gives one reason for
summoning the men that rests on a premise the reader never received. The
street-to-car handover lets "the driver" mean two different men, so the reader
loses who is talking and whose car they are in. The frightened passenger's pleas
have no recoverable referent. After the reveal in the parlour, Renwick confesses,
rebuts and confirms in the same flat one-liners Viveca uses. The edit pass should
fix the Ground problems first, then make Renwick audibly the talker and seller
the outline intended, leaving economy of speech to Viveca alone. Leave the
decisive exchange at the door and the final scene alone apart from staging.

## Dialogue findings

### D1 · Blocking · Viveca's reason for the sign rests on an unstated premise

- Where: L137–L139, in the car, just after the radio call
- Text:
> "You made me call his men in," Mabry said.
> "I want them there when he answers me. He won't tell me his driver acted on his own. Not in front of the driver."
- Problem: Ground/referent. "His driver" has no antecedent. The story has not said how Arvid supposedly died (the burning comes at L231), that a driver was involved, or that Renwick has blamed or might blame one. The reasoning also fails once it is reconstructed: Renwick's own men standing in the room would not force him to stop blaming one of them. Mabry never reacts (the narration cuts to the knot), so no uptake covers the gap. This is Viveca's only stated reason for turning on the sign, so it sits at an important beat. The reveal that Arvid is alive also makes the premise moot, and it is never paid off.
- Fix: Keep "I want them there when he answers me." Cut the two sentences about the driver. If she needs a reason, give her one the story has already shown: the crews used to work to her orders (L133). Illustrative only: "I want them there when he answers me. They used to take my orders."

### D2 · Major · Two drivers share one label at the car switch

- Where: L93–L111, outside the office under the sign
- Text:
> Viveca left the keys where they lay.
> Viveca bent to the driver. "Holding house. Now."
> "I saw. I'm going." The driver looked at Mabry and the office keys still in her hand. "He's let you out, then."
> "We're coming with you."
- Problem: Ground/referent and Attribution. "The driver" has meant the hired killer since L13. At L105 it silently switches to the man in the next car, and the reader only learns this from the crying passenger at L111. "He's let you out, then" has no clear addressee. Said to Viveca, it means the driver thinks Renwick released her, which is a valuable irony on the prompt's "release". Said to Mabry, it means Renwick has let his daughter out on the job. The reader can't tell which. "We're coming with you." is untagged, and Viveca had already given the order. Viveca demands the hired driver's keys and then leaves them with no reason given, so the "Keys." beat reads as a plan the scene forgot.
- Fix: Name the second man on first sight ("the other driver" or "the returns driver"), and have Viveca bend to his window. Tag "He's let you out, then" to one listener. Viveca is the stronger choice, and Mabry's untagged "We're coming with you." can then read as her dodge if it is tagged to her. Give the abandoned keys one clause of reason: for example, she wants to arrive inside the returns traffic, or Mabry cannot drive. Otherwise cut the demand and just send the hired driver off. The "Then he can come back and turn them off" retort (L101) can stay.

### D3 · Major · The passenger's pleas have no recoverable referent

- Where: L113 and L149, back seat of the returns car
- Text:
> "I already went back," the woman said when she saw Viveca. "I did what you said."
> "You said I could have a week," she said. "Last time. You said a week."
- Problem: Ground/referent. Went back to whom? A week for what? And why is she in the car again if she did what she was told? L13's "a man who wouldn't go back to his employer" partly seeds "go back", but the reader still cannot tell whether the week was a grace period before her return or a deadline she met. This woman is the only on-page victim of Viveca's old trade and the story's final witness ("You work there."). Her pleas are the reader's first concrete evidence of what Viveca did, so they need one legible fact each. Her panic and repetition are right and should stay.
- Fix: Give "went back" a person and "a week" a purpose, and imply that she ran again. Keep the lines short and repetitive. Illustrative only: "I already went back to him. I did what you said." At L149, make "a week" readable as the grace Viveca once gave her before taking her back.

### D4 · Major · Renwick speaks in Viveca's register through the parlour reveal

- Where: L209–L229, the parlour after Arvid appears
- Text:
> Renwick was wrapping his hand in a tablecloth. "Yes."
> "I needed something she'd listen to."
> "You were going to kill her tonight."
> "And until that was done, I needed him here."
- Problem: Identical Voices / Tennis Match. After the story's biggest reveal, Viveca and then Mabry put items to Renwick, and he confirms each in a composed one-liner of the same length and rhythm as theirs. Cover the tags and "I needed something she'd listen to." could be Viveca's. The Mabry/Renwick pair plays as rebuttal and completed clause ("And until that was done"), the house's correction rhythm. The outline gave Renwick courteous commercial euphemism, paternal teasing and faster bargaining under pressure, but only L191, L235 and L241 sound like him. The stretch also shares the collection's default of stating aims as "I want…" (L81, L139, L247, L357 for Viveca; L303 Arvid; L391 Mabry).
- Fix: Keep "The certificate." / "I paid for it." — that coldness is a flash worth having. Loosen the rest of Renwick's replies: a bleeding man at knifepoint who sells addresses for a living would justify, soften, account and sell. Keep his motive (leverage over Viveca) but put it in his own words. Illustrative only: "He's been fed, he's had a bed, I had a doctor look at his chest in March. I needed something she'd listen to." Mabry's "You were going to kill her tonight." can stay as an accusation. Trim Viveca's "I want" lines where another verb would do; keep Arvid's and Mabry's.

### D5 · Minor · Knife-point terms at the power transfer

- Where: L355–L361, the dining-room doorway with Renwick at Viveca's blade
- Text:
> "She's my daughter," he told Viveca.
> "If they bring her, you leave her alone."
> Viveca moved the knife enough to let him swallow. "You can look after her yourself."
- Problem: Negotiation routine. With a dead man on the carpet and a blade at his throat, Renwick states a condition clause ("If they bring her, you leave her alone") and Viveca answers with a term. His condition also repeats what his shout at L367 ("Nobody hurts her.") already carries.
- Fix: Keep "She's my daughter" and "Then bring her back. I want her here." Change L359 into a plea instead of a stipulation, so Viveca's "You can look after her yourself." (paid off when he washes Mabry's hands, L383) answers a man begging, not bargaining. Illustrative only: "Not her. Viveca. Not her."

### D6 · Minor · Narration that glosses or blurs a line

- Where: L83, after "Don't kill him." / "I want an answer."; L281, at the door as Arvid tells the truth
- Text:
> It wasn't a promise. Mabry knew that, and still went downstairs with her.
> Viveca looked toward Renwick, but her husband kept talking.
- Problem: At L83 the evasion is already legible, and the narration explains it. At L281 the reader can't tell what Viveca's look at Renwick asks for, or why "but" follows it: is she blaming him, checking for a witness, or asking him to stop Arvid?
- Fix: At L83, cut the first two clauses and keep Mabry going downstairs. At L281, either give the look a legible aim in a few words or cut the look and keep "her husband kept talking" as a plain beat.

## Prose findings

### P1 · Major · Climax geography cannot be followed

- Where: L181–L187, L205, L221, L237, L263–L269, L289–L295, L307–L317, the holding house from Arvid's appearance to his death
- Text:
> Arvid rubbed his wrist but remained on the bottom step.
> As he pushed his arms into the sleeves, Viveca went to the doorway between him and Mabry.
> Beside her, on the dining-room door, the bolt lay within reach.
> One thin foot scraped the flowered carpet and stopped.
- Problem: The reader never gets a layout. Mabry is "beside the front door" and looks "up the stairs" and Arvid stays "on the bottom step". Then Renwick, at the parlour window, bends for a ticket that fell from Mabry's coat. Then Viveca stands in "the doorway between him and Mabry", and only at L295 ("stepped past her into the hall") does the reader learn that Arvid was in the parlour and Mabry in the hall. The coat handover means Mabry crosses to him and back without being shown. The dining-room bolt is "beside her" at L289 but "across the hall" at L183 and L327. Arvid dies in the hall by the front door, yet on "the flowered carpet" that L181 placed in the parlour. This is the killing, so confusion here costs the story's central act.
- Fix: At L181–L187, add one sentence of layout: the front door and hall, the parlour opening off it, where the stairs rise, and the dining room opposite. Move Arvid and Mabry explicitly when they move, for example Arvid coming into the parlour after the cuffs come off and Mabry returning to the front door after the coat. Either put the flowered carpet in the hall too or change the surface where Arvid falls. Make "beside her" and "across the hall" agree.

### P2 · Minor · Local clarity lapses

- Where: L19, L91, L125, L217, L343
- Text:
> She hadn't told her.
> Cars hissed past, their occupants hidden by umbrellas pressed against the windows.
> "Keep driving," Viveca told him.
> It rose until Arvid put his free hand over his mouth.
> For once she hadn't waited to find out how badly she'd hurt him.
- Problem: L19 has two women and an unclear "she"/"her". At L91 the umbrellas inside cars are the crews' screen, but the prose doesn't say so, and the image reads as odd. At L125, "Keep driving" comes before the car has been shown moving. At L217 it is unclear whose mouth is covered and why that ends Viveca's sound; Arvid is on the landing and cannot reach her. At L343 the sentence can be read two ways.
- Fix: Name the subject at L19 ("Mabry hadn't told Viveca"). At L91, show that the umbrellas hide what the crews are doing. Put one motion beat before L125. At L217, make it Arvid's own mouth and say what stops her. At L343, say it plainly, e.g. that for once she did not check whether he was hurt.

## Continuity and prompt findings

### C1 · Minor · Renwick's wound moves from arm to hand

- Where: L201, L211, L343, the parlour attack and the dining-room struggle
- Text:
> The knife cut through his sleeve. He cried out, holding his arm crookedly against himself, blood running down his fingers.
> Renwick was wrapping his hand in a tablecloth.
> She hit him in the cut arm.
- Problem: The cut is to his arm, but he binds his hand, and later the arm is the wound again.
- Fix: Have him wrap the forearm, or wrap his hand because the blood is running down it.

### C2 · Minor · The sold address and the faked death don't meet on the page

- Where: L165 and L231, the hotel in the car and Viveca at the stairs
- Text:
> "Waiting. Your father sold him a way out of the city. Then he sold me the address."
> They told me you were burned. That there wasn't anything to see.
- Problem: If Renwick sold Viveca the hotel address, the reader wonders why she didn't find Arvid alive. What she found there (an empty room, Renwick having moved him first) is left out. The scheme can be recovered from "I needed something she'd listen to," but the hinge is missing.
- Fix: Add one clause in the car or at the stairs saying what she found at the hotel.
