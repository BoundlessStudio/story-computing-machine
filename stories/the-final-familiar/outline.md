# The Final Familiar — provisional direction

**Promised experience.** Explicit: a powerful familiar with centuries of wars, curses, and apocalypses behind him receives his final witch, an ordinary schoolgirl who does not believe in magic; keeping her happy proves harder than his former work. Inferred: wry, affectionate friction between formidable competence and an ordinary girl's independent desires, with magic that matters in the present and tenderness earned through specific mistakes. Proposed scale: a school farewell opens into a spectacular night over a rainy city. The payoff is a companionship both choose, without turning the girl into a concealed prodigy or treating sorrow as failure. The supplied image suggests the girl's dark bun, grey hoodie and uniform skirt, a black cat, rain, and warm city light; it does not prescribe these events.

**Possible voice and people.** First person from the familiar: exact, imperious, practical about impossible things, less reliable about other people's wishes. His schoolgirl, Miri, provisionally calls him Bramble. Her best friend Dulcie is moving away with her mother after the school fête. Dulcie is looking forward to the move and afraid to admit it. The girls have a silly two-cat musical act; Miri keeps postponing rehearsal of its final verse. These names and scenes remain provisional.

- A living witch of the familiar's old order places his final charge. His only instruction is to keep Miri happy. After nine lifetimes there will be no next witch; after this service he can retire. Having outlived eight witches, he wants a flawless last farewell and then a place without humans to lose. He finds Miri coming home through rain in school uniform with the fête's paper cat ears. Before she considers him remarkable, she gives the wet stray a towel and a place by the warm window. That unasked kindness begins to disturb his retirement plan.
- His campaign starts with a private patch of sunshine and an unwanted perfect school mark. Miri suspects tricks and tests the talking cat's claims. A repeatable impossible proof changes her belief, without changing her into a prodigy: she insists that he undo the mark and help only when she asks. Her skepticism is ordinary skepticism, not a symptom to cure. He learns that Dulcie leaves after the fête and regards departure as a problem his experience equips him to solve.
- At the fête Miri asks, half testing and half desperate, whether he can make tonight not end. He knows how to hold places through sieges. He wraps the school in a beautiful summer sunset, borrowing the fortress magic that held an army outside a burning city. There is no time travel or stopped clock: the ward holds the light, weather, and exits while ordinary time keeps passing. For a few minutes the girls are delighted. Then Dulcie's mother arrives outside in a hired car with the luggage, and Dulcie discovers that the doors return to the hall. Beyond the windows stands a vast black feline shape. He has mistaken one of Miri's desires for her whole will, and held a farewell he too wanted to keep.
- Dulcie wants to cut their act short and reach the station. Miri calls her eagerness betrayal; Dulcie admits she is excited about the new place as well as sad. Miri must stop their last set herself before the audience, giving up the final verse she has evaded rehearsing and the perfect goodbye she planned. She asks the familiar to release them. He owns the confinement as his decision and opens the school without bargaining for forgiveness, surrendering his trusted way of caring and risking rejection by his last witch. His power deliberately withdraws; Miri acquires no special faculty.
- Their delay has nearly cost Dulcie and her mother their train. Miri asks for a particular use of magic: take them to the station together. Dulcie and her frightened, practical mother both choose the flight; the mother and luggage come too. A colossal black cat carries them across the wet city, giving the girls a fierce, joyful last adventure and then an actual goodbye. The flight offers something new rather than restoring their cancelled performance. His help does not erase responsibility or require Miri's immediate forgiveness.
- At the tall rainy window Miri can release the familiar from his service, then separately ask him to stay. Retirement is credible freedom from reassignment, not an external judgment on her happiness or a magical loophole. He admits his own desire to stay in this particular life, with room for letters and visits and days neither of them can perfect. End in a concrete shared action or joke, allowing joy and loss to coexist without an explanatory moral.
