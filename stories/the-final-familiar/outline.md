# The Final Familiar — provisional direction

**Promised experience.** Explicit: a powerful familiar with centuries of wars, curses, and apocalypses behind him receives his final witch, an ordinary schoolgirl who does not believe in magic; keeping her happy proves harder than his former work. Inferred: wry, affectionate friction between formidable competence and an ordinary girl's independent desires, with magic that matters in the present and tenderness earned through specific mistakes. Proposed scale: a school farewell opens into a spectacular night over a rainy city. The payoff is a companionship both choose, without turning the girl into a concealed prodigy or treating sorrow as failure. The supplied image suggests the girl's dark bun, grey hoodie and uniform skirt, a black cat, rain, and warm city light; it does not prescribe these events.

**Possible voice and people.** First person from the familiar: exact, imperious, practical about impossible things, less reliable about other people's wishes. His schoolgirl, Miri, provisionally calls him Bramble. Her best friend Dulcie is moving away with her family after the school fête. Dulcie is looking forward to the move and afraid to admit it. Miri has helped prepare their last performance and keeps finding reasons not to rehearse the ending. These names and scenes remain provisional.

- A living witch of the familiar's old order places his final charge. After nine lifetimes there will be no next witch: this is his last service before retirement, not a sentence of impending death. His only instruction is to keep Miri happy. He finds her coming home through rain in school uniform, carrying the paper cat ears she and Dulcie made for the fête. He initially regards her disbelief as a minor briefing failure.
- His campaign begins with splendidly wrong small victories: a private patch of sunshine, an undeserved success at school, a clever attempt to eliminate a slight she would rather answer herself. Miri suspects concealed speakers and tricks, tests the talking cat's claims, and eventually accepts undeniable evidence. Her practical response disappoints him: magic does not entitle him to tamper with her marks or decide whom she should forgive. He learns that Dulcie leaves after the fête and mistakes this for a solvable extraction problem.
- At the fête, the familiar uses the fortress magic that once held an army outside a burning city. He shelters the school in a beautiful, impossible summer evening. For a few minutes the girls are delighted. Then a parent tries to leave: the doors return to the hall, the sunset cannot finish, and a vast black feline shape is visible beyond the glass. He has made the last evening last. Miri cannot keep classifying this as a trick, and Dulcie cannot leave for the future she wants.
- In the altered school, the girls finally have the quarrel Miri has evaded. Dulcie's excitement hurts more than the move itself. Miri must choose to help her go while still wanting her to stay. She tells the familiar to open the school. His military experience offers no victory that meets both desires; he must withdraw his own successful defence and risk having failed his last charge. The release is a consequential act of power, not an accidental loophole or a new gift in Miri.
- Their delay has nearly cost Dulcie's family their departure. Miri asks for a particular use of magic, with Dulcie's agreement: take them to the station together. A colossal black cat carries the two girls across the wet city, giving them a fierce, joyful last adventure and then an actual goodbye. The magic can make this part of their night wonderful; it cannot cancel what follows.
- Back at the tall rainy window, Miri wants the cat to stay. He can finally speak of his own wish rather than his assignment. Retirement becomes a life with this particular girl, with room for the friend to write and visit and for days neither of them can perfect. End in a concrete shared action or joke, allowing the earlier joy and the present loss to coexist without an explanatory moral.
