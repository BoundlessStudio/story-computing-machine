# Outline

The prompt remains authoritative; this outline is advisory drafting guidance for a 2,500–4,000-word story.

## Story

- Central promise: Teren Arquo, a celebrated hero accustomed to arriving first and acting decisively, reaches a kingdom apparently threatened by a storm-bearing beast—and must discover whether he can remain heroic when the people he means to rescue competently and repeatedly refuse him.
- Character pressure: The thunderback's approach makes delay look like cowardice, while Teren's reputation and self-worth depend on visible rescues. Aldervane's opened defenses, evacuated streets, and deliberate acceptance of damage resemble surrender to an outsider who has survived by distrusting hesitation.
- Turn: Teren mistakes a trained signal runner's use of a shelter niche for mortal danger and acts without permission. His stone-splitting intervention blocks the prepared route and creates the first genuine threat to the inhabited terraces. When Neya Orm offers him a bounded chance to repair the harm under local command, he gives up the role of savior and agrees to serve the kingdom's plan.
- Ending intent: Aldervane succeeds because its people understand their home and Teren finally lends strength without claiming authority. He apologizes, stays for repairs, and leaves without counting the kingdom among his rescues; at the next sign of trouble, his first act is to ask what help is wanted.

## Beats

1. Teren follows warning smoke over the eastern ridge and sees a house-sized thunderback advancing on Aldervane amid static and gathering rain. The kingdom opens removable wall sections and clears a broad avenue instead of barring the animal, so he races toward what he assumes is helplessness or fatalism.
2. At the boundary marker, route warden Neya Orm delivers Aldervane's formal refusal: no attack, diversion, or rescue. She explains that the living animal's seasonal passage breaks hardpan, carries seed, and draws the rain on which the valley depends; residents have evacuated its corridor and designed breakaway structures around it.
3. Teren suspects fear or official coercion and walks the cleared district looking for someone who wants saving. Instead, several residents independently reject his offer while performing rehearsed tasks, and a household chooses a safe overlook rather than his escort out. Their competence threatens his certainty more than hostility would.
4. The thunderback enters the corridor. A signal runner disappears into a stone shelter niche exactly as trained, but Teren reads the moment through older rescues and strikes a ledge to turn the animal. Falling rubble jams the route, and the startled thunderback begins veering toward occupied terraces that had been outside the danger plan.
5. Neya names the consequence without flattering his intention. She can still coordinate flags, horns, evacuation teams, and the breakaway wall, but the blockage must be cleared faster than her crews can manage it. Teren admits the danger is now partly his work and accepts her terms: follow signals, make no fresh judgment on the kingdom's behalf, and answer publicly afterward.
6. In the escalating set piece, Aldervane's residents execute their contingency while Teren becomes one tool within it. He hauls stone only where marked, braces a failing gate segment, and resists abandoning his post when the runner reappears near the animal; trusting Neya's timing allows the local teams to open the safe line.
7. At the last possible moment, Neya signals and Teren releases the breakaway wall rather than confronting the thunderback. The animal returns to its old path, passes without entering the terraces, and its wake brings the first hard rain. The controlled damage demonstrates why stopping the passage would have harmed the kingdom even if Teren had done it cleanly.
8. Teren apologizes to the runner and the assembled residents, makes restitution through uncelebrated repair work, and refuses language crediting him with Aldervane's salvation. When he resumes his travels, he keeps his weapon sheathed as he approaches the next unnamed community and asks what is needed before offering it.

## Continuity

- Canon used: Aldervane belongs to the one physical world in an otherwise unspecified era. Any extraordinary effect remains real but locally bounded, and only living agents—the thunderback and, if his strength is treated as magical, Teren—originate magic.
- Boundaries and unknowns: Do not connect Aldervane, its government, Teren, Neya, the thunderback, or the corridor practice to Ersene, the Crowned Ancestors' unnamed kingdom, Ravel, the Glass-Sea era, or any established creature, artifact, faction, or system. The thunderback is a living unnamed animal, not a god, machine, or established species-wide mechanism; its intelligence beyond animal behavior and the wider population remain unresolved. The refusal must be sincere, informed, and broadly supported—not a spell, secret plea, or proof that the residents are incapable—and Teren's aid cannot erase the harm caused by ignoring it.
