# Exchange Program — provisional outline

## Promised experience

The request explicitly exchanges two groups between locations, then makes both ill-equipped for the other genre: superheroes against “the all corrupting darkness,” magical girls against “actual supervillains or solving crimes.” I infer reciprocal action-comedy: capable people make decisions that work beautifully at home and badly abroad, with consequences beyond a comparison of costumes. The wording leaves tone, scale, characters, relationships, and outcome open. My anticipated pleasure is mounting, specific genre mismatch and an affectionate reckoning with how much each team normally accomplishes; neither group should become the stupid team or master its unfamiliar job in an afternoon.

## Chosen provisional direction

Intercut close third person, chiefly through Rose Absolute, leader of Bellflower's three magical girls, and Highguard, leader of Steelfall's three superheroes. They are friendly professional rivals who jointly proposed a week's exchange. Roughly 4,000–5,000 words; bright comic narration with an increasingly alarming darkness thread, non-graphic action. The other girls are Blue Vow and Starling Smile; the other heroes can remain lightly characterized until drafting. These names and all mechanics below are choices, not prompt requirements.

- At the handover, both teams genuinely admire the other's work but privately expect a holiday: one big, brightly announcing enemy sounds simpler than organized crime; recognizably human criminals sound simpler than cosmic evil. Each leaves a briefing. Both believe they understand it.
- In Steelfall, the girls magnificently defeat a giant bank-robbing machine. Its pilot, Overdraft, surrenders halfway through their victory speech. Their cleansing attack finds no invading evil to remove: he is simply choosing to rob the bank. His surrender is a distraction; a second machine steals the vault while they protect bystanders. He escapes with the money and footage of their lengthy attack preparation. Their real rescue does not cancel the robbery.
- In Bellflower, Highguard shatters the enormous black chrysalis descending over the shopping district. Every fragment becomes an opening for the same darkness. Strength, sealed armor, and psychological profiling cannot stop its promises from reaching him. It uses his wish to protect everyone to turn his invulnerability into a prison for the citizens he is “saving.” His teammates must fight someone they cannot injure while the corruption reaches them too.
- The girls try to find Overdraft. Their usual senses distinguish corruption, not a deliberate liar from an honest witness. They follow the apparently helpless person he planted at the scene, lose time, and discover he has prepared an ambush specifically for their formation and announced attack. They can shield civilians from his machinery, but cannot purify away his motives or guess where he has hidden the stolen money. The crime remains a crime with evidence to recover.
- Two increasingly unconvincing courtesy calls become an honest emergency call. Each team supplies its home expertise remotely. This helps without granting the listeners new powers: the girls learn to ask what a witness actually saw, preserve a clue, and disrupt Overdraft's rehearsed trap; the heroes learn that striking the darkness spreads it and that they must deny it opportunities while defending one another. They achieve costly holding actions, not instant mastery.
- The exchange ends early. The girls return to break the corruption their hosts cannot remove; the superheroes return to take apart the prepared criminal operation their guests could not solve. Give each homecoming a decisive action and make the visiting team's rescue/holding work essential to there being anyone left to save. Close on the battered teams meeting again, each with an absurdly specific, hard-won terror of the other's routine: an innocent mascot-shaped shadow makes Highguard flinch, while Rose refuses to touch a surrendered villain until someone has checked his other hands. Mutual respect lands through these changed reflexes, without a speech or a claim that the exchange has fixed either limitation.

## Chief uncertainty

The remote-help/early-return ending could become a neat lesson about complementary skills and blunt the prompt's sharper comedy. The draft needs failures that persist, substantial foreign-genre action, and a return that acknowledges the limits rather than erasing the week. I want the outline readers to test whether the final two beats actually pay off that reciprocal inadequacy, and whether the girls' crime thread carries enough weight beside the corruption.
