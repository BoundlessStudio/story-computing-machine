# Exchange Program — provisional outline

## Promised experience

The request explicitly exchanges two groups between locations, then makes both ill-equipped for the other genre: superheroes against “the all corrupting darkness,” magical girls against “actual supervillains or solving crimes.” I infer reciprocal action-comedy: capable people make decisions that work beautifully at home and badly abroad, with consequences beyond a comparison of costumes. The wording leaves tone, scale, characters, relationships, and outcome open. My anticipated pleasure is mounting, specific genre mismatch and an affectionate reckoning with how much each team normally accomplishes; neither group should become the stupid team or master its unfamiliar job in an afternoon.

## Chosen provisional direction

Intercut close third person, chiefly through Rose Absolute, leader of Bellflower's three magical girls, and Highguard, leader of Steelfall's three superheroes. They are friendly professional rivals who jointly proposed a week's exchange. Roughly 4,000–5,000 words; bright comic narration with an increasingly alarming darkness thread, non-graphic action. Blue Vow specializes in barriers; Starling Smile flies. Highguard shapes invulnerable force shields, Quickstep has speed, and Relay builds investigative equipment. The cities share a thirty-minute express shuttle; the week's exchange includes a chartered car for emergency recall. These names, relationships, powers, and mechanics are choices, not prompt requirements.

- At the handover, both teams genuinely admire the other's work but privately expect a holiday: one big, brightly announcing enemy sounds simpler than organized crime; recognizably human criminals sound simpler than cosmic evil. Each leaves a briefing. Both believe they understand it.
- Intercut the following misadventures over the next two days. Two increasingly unconvincing courtesy calls become a live emergency call as the depot ambush and Bellflower's attempted evacuation overlap; the call's advice below occurs across both scenes, before either team starts its return. Keep comic particulars inside the crises, including Overdraft's very practical interruptions and the heroes' increasingly useless professional equipment.
- In Steelfall, the girls magnificently defeat a giant bank-robbing machine. Its pilot, Overdraft, surrenders halfway through their victory speech. Their cleansing attack finds no invading evil to remove: he is simply choosing to rob the bank. His surrender is a distraction; a second machine steals the vault while they protect bystanders. He escapes with the money and footage of their lengthy attack preparation. Their real rescue does not cancel the robbery.
- In Bellflower, Highguard shatters the enormous black chrysalis with a shield ram. Every fragment becomes an opening for the same darkness. Strength, sealed armor, and psychological profiling cannot stop its promises from reaching him. It twists his wish to protect everyone: he closes an invulnerable dome over the shopping district and prevents evacuation into a world containing danger. Quickstep and Relay cannot attack their way through him. The darkness also begins reaching them; their powers offer no immunity.
- The girls try to find Overdraft. Their usual senses distinguish corruption, not a deliberate liar from an honest witness. His planted witness leads them to a freight depot and an ambush prepared from the footage of their formation and announced attack. The stolen vault includes captive bank staff. Overdraft rigs its control cabin as bait for their finishing move, keeping his escape route clear while they charge it. Rose can take the splendid, familiar shot or save people she has helped put in danger. She cancels the attack, catches the falling cabin, and lets Overdraft escape rather than vaporize its occupants. Blue Vow and Starling keep the machinery stable while she extracts the captives. With Relay's remote warning that the evidence must survive too, they preserve the entire cabin instead of cleansing or smashing it. The girls cannot decode its encrypted route recorder or locate the money. They have saved witnesses and an avenue of investigation, at the price of losing the criminal a second time.
- On the other end of that call, Rose supplies home knowledge: the corruption makes one cherished duty seem absolute, until every alternative feels evil. Feeling certain is therefore a warning, not proof of immunity. This lets Highguard recognize the voice inside his own protective plans; it does not cure him. During a lucid interval he deliberately lowers his dome for ten seconds, although every corrupted instinct calls this abandoning the people inside. Quickstep spends those seconds carrying particular trapped shoppers out; Relay stays with Highguard instead of joining the exodus. Highguard directs her to move him into the emptied square before he starts “protecting” again. The returning dome now confines him rather than the shoppers. Relay and Quickstep keep the square clear while the darkness spreads beyond it; this is a deteriorating holding action, not physical containment of the incorporeal corruption.
- Rose chooses to abandon pursuit altogether and recall the chartered shuttle. Once the captives and sealed cabin are in police hands, the girls return to Bellflower first. The heroes are still there. They cannot cure Highguard through a shield that blocks their directed magic. Highguard must open it once more, knowingly letting a danger approach him; he manages only long enough for the girls to enter. Their home expertise supplies the purification he lacks, while his endangered choice supplies the opening. The spreading darkness is purged; damage and frightened people remain.
- Only then can the freed heroes take the same shuttle home. Relay reads the preserved cabin's recorder; it identifies Overdraft's real extraction point and a fixed midnight freight departure, still two hours away. Establish the evening timeline so both thirty-minute legs, police handoff, and purification fit before that booked slot. Highguard and Quickstep intercept him before departure. Keep this home-genre resolution brief and specific: it exists because Rose gave up the satisfying blast and preserved people and machinery she could not interpret. Overdraft had counted on her vaporizing the evidence with his bait. Some cash is already gone. The girls' foreign adventure remains an expensive failure, not retroactive detective brilliance.
- Close on the battered teams meeting again, each with an absurdly specific, hard-won terror of the other's routine: an innocent mascot-shaped shadow makes Highguard flinch, while Rose refuses to touch a surrendered villain until someone has checked his other hands. Mutual respect lands through these changed reflexes, without a speech or a claim that the exchange has fixed either limitation.

## Chief uncertainty

The remote-help/early-return ending could still become a neat lesson about complementary skills and blunt the prompt's sharper comedy. The visitors now make specific, costly choices that enable the returns without acquiring the missing expertise. I want the readers to test those dependencies and whether the captive staff and sacrificed pursuit give the crime thread enough felt weight beside the corruption. Lower darkness stakes remain an available tonal choice, not a requirement inferred from “not really equipped.”
