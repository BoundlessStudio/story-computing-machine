# The World at One Step — revised provisional outline

## Promised experience

“Every person” can freely choose whether to enter. Once the door closes, every Box has the same 50/50 chance of making the person a magic user or burning them to death. The updates make this a manufactured device rolling out worldwide and require imposing Boxes in every reference image. “The shipping is only the beginning” puts the deeds of the people who come out alive at the center. I read the promise as awe at what they do to the world and dread at what they risked for the power. The form of magic, cast, conflict, and tenor are open.

## Takes considered

- **The country in the air.** Weeks after deployment, mages in three parts of the world lift buildings, take an inhabited town into the sky, and make whole new land together. Choose this for the scene where power gained independently lets three communities decide where their streets will meet and who can live in the world they make.
- **All the doors of the courthouse.** A parent who declined the Box watches the paired doors of the earlier draft grow into continent-spanning squares. At a hearing, people the state has exiled walk through the walls back into the capital. The state’s response would dominate that confrontation.
- **The other dawn.** Years later, a woman who turned away recalls the day her brother and users in three countries learned to move daylight. The central scene is a second sunrise willed separately across both hemispheres, for very different reasons. Recollection would put some distance between the reader and the first surge of power.

## Provisional direction

Six weeks into the public opening, the air and sea shipments have put Boxes across the inhabited continents. Factories built them to one pattern, three storeys of graphite steel with two massive exposed copper bands and a recessed sliding door with an oval of white light inside. A wide low ramp leaves room to turn back. The serial stamps, motor hum, and powered door make their manufacture apparent, and the copper bands and squared silhouette read from a distance. Entry is free and voluntary. Every closure is a fresh, independent 50/50 trial. Olek’s death and Temra’s survival at the same Box have already happened.

A mage can lift solid matter by touch and leave it suspended where they let go. Many hands can move what one cannot. People have floated ballroom floors over avenues and private estates above their former streets. Lipstone’s former freight crews take their hall out of its owners’ hands. On another continent, Ivari’s neighbors lift the ridge they live on ahead of a quarry purchase. Across the ocean, Neris and the Kelm Quay mages offer their headland as a meeting place.

Temra sees a city above the clouds, with flying platforms to reach it. That would leave Neris’s family depending on a mage every time they went home. Neris’s sister is considering the Box so she can come and go on her own. At Kelm the three communities decide where to leave their ground and what life on it will demand.

## Craft plan

**Teller.** Try close third past, anchored in the lifter each place has made, and retrospective first person from Temra, who could witness the other lifts from the hall. Temra notices load and clearance, Ivari where rock will part, and Neris the level of her feet against the level of the person she’s addressing. In first person Temra likes telling people what was hers to move and is slower to say where she thought they belonged.

**Temra Vosen.** She needs her old crew to come with her and wants to be first to set a city above the clouds. She has seen what enough hands can raise and assumes a free ride answers who can get there. She speaks in concrete instructions and catches at other people’s nouns when she loses command. Her short dark curls, striped work jacket, and planted stance come from the existing sheet. The sleeve stripes show when she puts both arms to the hall. The stance fails her when she first has to look down across its shadow.

**Ivari.** She knows the ridge better than Temra and expects the people at the coast to receive her neighbors as guests on someone else’s land. She wants to bring the ground between their houses. She waits through a wrong answer, talks in patient conditionals, and can be blunt when someone offers too little. Keep her gray-streaked hair, heavy belted coat, and amused look when she has the advantage. Her coat drags on the hoarfrost when she crouches and opens in Kelm’s hot wind.

**Neris.** The Kelm loader survived her local Box. She wants the market to be a street her sister can walk on her own and thinks the visitors only care about clear sky. She calls distances in landmarks, quarrels while working, and grows formal with her sister in front of strangers. Olek, in Temra’s brief memory, is the man in the rust shirt whose stories always went past the point where she could guess their end. There are no supplied or shared-universe characters.

**Conversation owed.** At Kelm, who gets to come home to a place the gifted have made? Neris asks Temra to picture waiting below her front door for a ride. She asks her sister what she wants from the Box and hears her out. Ivari asks where her people can put their own streets. Their answers change what they lift and where they set it.

**Place.** Kelm’s stepped headland gives the long scene a physical choice. Existing streets can meet new land above the sea at its top step. At Lipstone’s apron Temra already knows every edge. At Shale Crown the argument would be about keeping one town. At Kelm’s rainy noon, fruit vendors work under patched sailcloth. Wet red stone, salt roughening the hair, sharp bruised fruit underfoot, dock lines knocking on the stairs, and the wind from the sea carry into the new place. Lipstone’s dusk lands high in the hangar windows, above ringing rollers and hot paint. At Shale Crown’s winter dawn, cold enters Ivari’s boots while grit falls through the shadow of the lifted ridge.

## Scene briefs

1. **Lipstone, the hall rises. One quarter.** The owners plan to lift the freight hall to the private tier of the city. The old crew wants Temra’s help taking it elsewhere. She wants them high enough to meet the towns already taking to the air. A volunteer turns back from the open Box. The next closes it and lives. Its sound takes Temra briefly back to the fire that killed Olek and the place he wanted to see. She tells the crew about Kelm’s invitation and takes hold with them. The whole freight floor goes up into daylight and its shadow sweeps clear of the apron. The ground station and its Box remain open.
2. **Shale Crown, the ridge leaves. One quarter.** There are houses hanging above the forecourt already. The quarry buyer offers the residents lifts to new dwellings and expects to take the ridge. Ivari tells her neighbors why she wants their roads and front steps to go with them. The people who want to stay have time to leave. Ivari knows where the group can get a grip on the stone. The buyer has no idea they have that reach. They raise the occupied ridge and its Box together. The stair to the old quarry ends in sky. They can take their own ground to Kelm.
3. **Kelm, the new coast. Half and the long scene.** Days later the hall and ridge hang apart. Temra wants Kelm to try her flying platforms. Ivari wants to bring her streets whole. Neris knows her sister is in line and asks to speak to her while the door stands open. After hearing her, Neris offers a different piece of ground. Her people lift the whole stone market shelf and swing it out over the water, level with the top of the old quay steps. Ivari’s neighbors bring their inhabited ridge out of the sky and turn its main road onto the market. Temra and the crew set the hall broadside to the new street, its immense doors open. The shadow of a coastline falls on what was open sea. People can enter on foot and decide for themselves when to go. Neris’s sister steps back from the Box and walks the new shore. Another volunteer has reasons of her own to go in and close the door.

## Ending

Stay with the new coast while people begin using it. The hall is a public square where three continents’ paths cross. Down the new street Kelm’s copper-banded Box gives the person inside the same chance as Olek. Beyond it another inhabited ledge is coming across the sea under its own people’s hands. The first closed door, the hall leaving its owners on the apron, and Ivari taking the dirt between her neighbors’ houses load the image. The worldwide delivery and the journeys to Kelm take short summary. The charter and the working theory of The Box stay offstage.
