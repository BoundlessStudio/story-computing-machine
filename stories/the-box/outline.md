# The World at One Step — provisional outline

## Promised experience

The original request gives “Every person” a free opportunity and puts two equally likely outcomes behind the closed door. The revision requires “much grander” and specifies that “the box is a tech device rolling out world wide.” That makes distribution, activation, and ordinary people getting their hands on built hardware central to the events. I read “much grander” as wanting the danger and possibility of a single door multiplied across the world, with an act and an aftermath commensurate with that reach. The earlier story confined the decisive action and reward to one pair of sisters in one yard. Here the launch itself makes new actors at distant sites whose choices change the rollout and what the world can do afterward. The genre, institutions, relationships, kind of magic, and individual fates are open.

## Takes considered

- **The fleet.** On the first worldwide activation, the survivors of the first batch of orbital-delivered Boxes help the next cargo wave descend out of a failing orbit. The scene is the carriers coming down over several continents under the control of people the machines have just changed.
- **The vote.** A public test during worldwide activation looks as though it will put made magic under one state’s authority. The protagonist witnesses two distant entrants come out with a faculty that can operate between them. Their first act refuses the capital any jurisdiction over what their hands can reach.
- **The world at one step.** An air carrier pulls out of the remainder of a free rollout already in progress. The first people made into mages across the planet can bring the world’s freight doors together and carry the Boxes the rest of the way. Choose this take because the new magic comes from the hardware that has already reached its destinations, it changes where the remaining hardware can go, and its use at full scale changes the geography of every later delivery.

## Provisional direction

An international manufacturing cooperative is shipping human-sized steel devices called The Box. Each is identifiable by the same flush door, handling sockets, and translations of the offer. The first phase has installed units on every continent. Units for districts farthest from the first public depots are stacked for the second phase of delivery. The offer covers everyone, with local units and free rides to them, open to any volunteer at no charge. Stepping back out of an open unit is always possible. A closed door gives the occupant one chance in two of becoming a magic user and one in two of burning to death, independently on each closure. The hardware changes the living user or kills them. Once made, people wield the magic themselves.

During the worldwide opening the contracted air carrier pulls its fleet from the second phase in response to the mounting public deaths. The live depots stay open. Temra Vosen, the freight rollout director, sees her colleague Olek Tamn die in an installed unit at Lipstone. She has signed up for the same chance and chooses to shut the door after his death. She survives. At Shale Crown, half a world away, airstrip chief Ivari Doss has independently survived her own try and is waiting to talk to Temra about the grounded flight. The two discover in action that the arrival gates they can each hold with magic can meet across the planet. Previous trial users could do this across a room. The spread of the first installed Boxes is what gives Temra a living partner halfway around the world.

Temra and Ivari hold the opposite ends of a full freight opening while machines roll loaded pallets through. Other survivors at first-phase depots build the same links, including ones that bypass Lipstone altogether. This delivers hardware for the stalled phase across the world, into the districts where resident crews can take it onward. It also brings routes into being that neither the aviation carrier nor Temra can own or switch off. Gates meet only while a living mage at each end chooses to hold the way open. The Box remains the manufactured means of becoming a mage.

## Craft plan

**Teller.** Temra notices where weight has gone and who has been left waiting for it. Try close third past with a practical, proud freight director who loses sight of her own anger, and first person present with a dispatcher addressing arrivals and keeping herself steady. Stay bodily close for both closures and for the first crossing.

**Cast.** Temra has spent years getting every region a place on the schedule. She wants the world to use the devices and wants for herself the power to go places without waiting for a carrier. She thinks consent on a freight contract can carry the project farther than actual people’s hands can. She talks in complete instructions with concrete names and weights. Under pressure she checks a number she already knows. Olek wants to find out whether he can carry cargo to places his flight board always calls uneconomical. He believes Temra will watch his trial with her eyes on the next load. He tells little jokes with stories behind them and asks for human attention quite plainly when it matters. Ivari wants her own crews to route deliveries beyond the airstrip and remembers the shipments Temra made her leave on a runway last winter. She expects Temra to propose another schedule run out of Lipstone. Ivari speaks in patient, direct conditionals, moving as she speaks and leaving room for an answer. She has a different appetite for power from Temra’s. There are no supplied or established characters.

**Conversation owed.** In the long scene the director and the chief at the other end must say what they are prepared to send across a new way, and who will decide where the next one opens. Olek’s death has to be speakable between them before they start carrying more devices into the world. Ivari can tell Temra how long it took her to close her own door, in order, because Temra thinks the first death should decide what she does next.

**Place.** Lipstone Freight Hall is better ground than the North Array broadcast gallery or the capital’s Station of Assembly. Its giant vehicle doors, still packed with transport capacity and dependent on the carrier apron, can acquire new physical destinations while the reader is there. Shift ends on a hot, dry early autumn afternoon. Pallet netting marks a forearm, rollers ring under the heavy containers, forklift batteries smell hot, labels of different colours set out entire hemispheres under inspection lamps, and the last departing carrier’s shadow crosses the public boarding ramp. Shale Crown is an actual second setting, the ramp and windbreaks of a station on an inhabited cold plateau at dawn. Hard sunlight picks ridges out of snow rutted by tractors. The wind carries hot copper from the motor shed. The frost there can lie on a floor in Lipstone, and an idled loading line at Lipstone can empty into the dawn. Both places work freight before and after the event. [IATA on aircraft cargo pallets and containers](https://www.iata.org/en/programs/cargo/cargo-operations/unit-load-devices/)

## Scene briefs

1. **The lift stops, about a fifth.** At Lipstone the first wave of installed units is already opening to free lines around the world. On the floor, put another pallet of serial Boxes on its rollers, a departure in the sky, and a device with an accessible ramp actually open to walk-up volunteers. Olek wants Temra there while he tries it. She wants him to stay for their second-phase freights and to keep what she wants for herself private. He knows why he wants the power and that he might lose. He shuts the door himself and burns to death, treated plainly and non-graphically. At the end of that sequence the next loaded crews are sent back from the apron. The carrier withdraws its lift as news of the first deaths reaches it. Remember Olek’s actual route tag left in Temra’s hand and the places still to reach on those pallets.
2. **Two ends, nearly half and the long scene.** At the head of the backed-up loading line Temra finds Ivari on the line asking how long they should expect to wait. Ivari has already come out of her own device with magic. She wants the loaded crates before the carrier’s proposed new timetable and wants her crew free to send them onward. Temra can leave her own unit open and do the work she has always done. She chooses the same genuine chance, sees the door shut from within, and has to wait to find out. She comes out alive and works with Ivari to join a small staff door to an opening at Shale Crown. The wind and rime come into the hall. Temra crosses in person and takes in the sun on the Shale Crown ramp, their operational unit, and the much larger freight doors. Here they address what they are about to deliver after what each has felt at a closed door. Ivari will take the shipments and operate her end, and asks Temra to let her crew arrange its own connections. Temra returns to Lipstone to hold the larger opening. Remember boots carrying plateau grit back over the little office threshold.
3. **The doors open out, about a third.** With Temra and Ivari holding the full opening, whole freight loads roll across to Shale Crown. At the other Lipstone gates local survivors take hold with counterparts at distant arrival floors. At least one pair meets and chooses the next passage without asking Temra, within sight through the Shale Crown gate. Let the world come into the hall as actual people, equipment and weather with work to accomplish. Temra must cede the order of dispatch to get the loads moving. Newly made mages send units further through links across the planet. The delivery queues at the receiving end form in front of actual installed doors for anyone, and one person takes a look from inside an open unit and walks back out while another stays. People keep the new routes open as the original air routes go dark.

## Ending

A payload labelled for a day and a half of flight arrives on the same minute’s receipt. In the distance beyond the first gate, another giant door is already being held open on a place Lipstone has never served directly. Temra watches freely arrived technology give strangers the same choice Olek and she made, while the people who made it out open paths farther than any manufacturer promised. The first departure, the two independent outcomes, and the tiny crossing through the office door load this much larger change. Summarize the months of manufacture and earlier trials. Keep management meetings and a survey of countries offstage.
