# The World at One Step — revised provisional outline

## Promised experience

“Every person” can freely choose whether to enter. Once the door closes, every Box has the same 50/50 chance of making the person a magic user or burning them to death. The updates make this a manufactured device rolling out worldwide and require imposing Boxes in every reference image. “The shipping is only the beginning” puts the deeds of the people who come out alive at the center. I read the promise as awe at what they do to the world and dread at what they risked for the power. The form of magic, cast, conflict, and tenor are open.

## Takes considered

- **The country in the air.** Weeks after deployment, mages in three parts of the world lift buildings, take an inhabited town into the sky, and make whole new land together. Choose this for the scene where power gained independently lets three communities decide where their streets will meet and who can live in the world they make.
- **All the doors of the courthouse.** A parent who declined the Box watches the paired doors of the earlier draft grow into continent-spanning squares. At a hearing, people the state has exiled walk through the walls back into the capital. The state’s response would dominate that confrontation.
- **The other dawn.** Years later, a woman who turned away recalls the day her brother and users in three countries learned to move daylight. The central scene is a second sunrise willed separately across both hemispheres, for very different reasons. Recollection would put some distance between the reader and the first surge of power.

## Provisional direction

Six weeks into the public opening, air and sea shipments have put factory-built Boxes across the inhabited continents. Each has three storeys of graphite steel, two massive exposed copper bands, serial stamps, and a powered sliding door with an oval of white light inside. The squared silhouette reads from a distance, and the wide low ramp leaves room to turn back. Anyone can enter for free. Every closure is a fresh, independent 50/50 trial. At Lipstone, Olek died and Temra survived at the same device.

A mage can move solid matter by touch and leave it suspended where they let go. Many hands can move what one cannot. A platform pilot can steer by holding its rail, then take both hands off at the landing. People have floated ballroom floors above avenues and whole estates above city streets. At the old sea and air freight exchanges, pilots already post fares to dozens of cities’ upper districts. Temra’s crew can earn a living bringing their hall to a high tier. Ivari’s neighbors can move the ridge they live on. At Kelm, an ocean ferry junction, Neris offers the strength of a third community and a coast where the airborne places can meet.

## Craft plan

**Teller.** Try close third past, anchored in the lifter each place has made, and retrospective first person from Temra, who could witness the other lifts from the hall. Temra notices load and clearance, Ivari where rock will part, and Neris the level of her feet against the level of the person she’s addressing. In first person Temra likes telling people what was hers to move and is slower to say where she thought they belonged.

**Temra Vosen.** She wants to keep the crew together and lead the first ascent to a new city above the clouds. She knows a lift can work and mistakes getting everyone up once for giving them a way to live there. She speaks in concrete instructions and catches at other people’s nouns when she loses command. Keep the short dark curls, sleeve stripes visible when she has both arms against the hall, and planted stance she loses above its shadow.

**Ivari.** She knows the ridge better than Temra and expects the people at the coast to receive her neighbors as guests on someone else’s land. She wants to bring the ground between their houses. She waits through a wrong answer, talks in patient conditionals, and can be blunt when someone offers too little. Keep her gray-streaked hair, heavy belted coat, and amused look when she has the advantage. Her coat drags on the hoarfrost when she crouches and opens in Kelm’s hot wind.

**Neris and Sali.** Neris, a Kelm loader who survived her Box, calls distances in landmarks, quarrels while working, and grows formal with her sister in front of strangers. She is proud of all the places she can take Sali. Sali wants to set out her pears before dawn and go home after lowering their aunt’s awning on her own schedule. She talks in times and tasks, finishes Neris’s sentences, and takes a long time to say what she actually wants. Olek survives in Temra’s brief memory as a square of rust shirt and a man who let his stories go past the point when she could guess their end. There are no supplied or shared-universe characters.

**Conversation owed.** Who gets to come and go in a place the gifted have made? While the Box is open Sali tells Neris how it feels to have her promise a ride to the stall and then wait. Ivari needs to hear that the ridge people can keep their houses when their street joins Kelm’s. Temra needs to hear from her crew before she lets the upper-tier berth go.

**Place.** Kelm’s stepped headland gives the long scene a physical choice. The street from town ends beside the Box at the fixed upper landing of the old quay stairs. Here it can meet new land. The Lipstone apron offers the freight hall’s familiar edges, and Shale Crown presses Ivari to keep one town together. At Kelm’s rainy noon, fruit vendors work under patched sailcloth amid wet red stone, salt roughening their hair, bruised fruit underfoot, dock lines knocking on the stairs, and wind off the sea. Lipstone’s dusk lands high in the hangar windows, above ringing rollers and hot paint. At Shale Crown’s winter dawn, cold enters Ivari’s boots and grit falls through the shadow of the lifted ridge.

## Scene briefs

1. **Lipstone, the hall rises. One fifth.** The owners mean to put the hall in the private tier. The former load crew wants the use of the place it worked in. A person turns back from the Box. Another closes it, lives, and leaves a loose thing hanging above her hand. The sound of the door recalls Olek’s shirt and the heat at the sill. Through the freight radio Neris invites the already rising towns to meet off Kelm’s headland. Temra takes hold with her crew and the freight hall’s shadow sweeps clear of the apron. They leave the apron’s Box open and steer the hall coastward from inside it.
2. **Shale Crown, the ridge leaves. Three tenths.** The quarry buyer offers the residents a ride to new dwellings and expects the ridge. Some neighbors have already lifted their houses and intend to leave separately. One couple’s house hangs over its old street. They have a ride out with a neighbor. The husband enters the Box of his own accord to be able to carry his wife up to their house and down whenever they like. He burns to death. Stay with the fire at the seam, the heat, and the people who come for him. Their pilot can still take the widow and her house away, then goes on to the next departure. She can see her front door overhead and asks where she is meant to put a foot when the pilot goes. Ivari asks the pilots to put their houses back and lend their hands to the whole ridge. She has to convince them they can take it with the roads on it. A person who wants to stay walks down before they lift. The occupied streets rise with the town’s Box, the widow goes with her street, and the old quarry stairs end in sky.
3. **Kelm, the new coast. Half and the long scene.** The hall and ridge hang offshore. The crew has a berth and a paid passenger franchise at the working upper tier, with Temra to run it. A staffed platform takes Sali and her first crates of pears up to a trial market above the clouds. She comes back elated. When she needs to return with her aunt’s awning, the platform is loading another flight. Temra offers an exception, then sees on her roster who would have to wait. By the open Box Sali tells Neris why she wants to be able to carry herself. She puts her own choice aside to see what Neris will do. Neris’s people offer the seaward market shelf, at the cost of its old frontage. They lift it clear and set one end against the quay’s fixed upper landing. The fruit vendors pack their stalls to bring them onto the new shore. Ivari turns the inhabited ridge down to meet the shelf, giving up the height that shielded it in exchange for keeping its homes and making the street public. The berth is still available when Temra puts the choice to her crew. They give up the franchise and take hold beside her to set the hall’s open doors alongside the public street. A coastline casts its shadow on what was empty sea. The Shale widow walks across first, and Sali crosses when she chooses. The next volunteer approaches the Box for a reason of her own.

## Ending

The freight hall becomes a square. The mages of Rendar arrive over the sea with a berth and fare already quoted at the high tier. They see people crossing on foot, ask for the street’s height, and begin turning the road on their inhabited ledge toward the new coast. Upper-tier fares are still being called on the same radio. Kelm’s copper-banded Box closes on the next volunteer, beside the fixed stair where the widow just walked. The lifts, Shale’s fire, and the real sky ride load that image. Summarize the worldwide delivery and the days in transit, including how the crews take turns moving their ground and stop in midair to rest. The charter and the theory behind the Box stay offstage.
