# The World at One Step — provisional outline

## Promised experience

The original request gives “Every person” a free opportunity and puts two equally likely outcomes behind the closed door. The revision requires “much grander” and specifies that “the box is a tech device rolling out world wide.” That makes distribution, activation, and ordinary people getting their hands on built hardware central to the events. I read “much grander” as wanting the danger and possibility of a single door multiplied across the world, with an act and an aftermath commensurate with that reach. The earlier story confined the decisive action and reward to one pair of sisters in one yard. Here the launch itself makes new actors at distant sites whose choices change the rollout and what the world can do afterward. The genre, institutions, relationships, kind of magic, and individual fates are open.

## Takes considered

- **The fleet.** On the first worldwide activation, the survivors of the first batch of orbital-delivered Boxes help the next cargo wave descend out of a failing orbit. The scene is the carriers coming down over several continents under the control of people the machines have just changed.
- **The vote.** A public test during worldwide activation looks as though it will put made magic under one state’s authority. The protagonist witnesses two distant entrants come out with a faculty that can operate between them. Their first act refuses the capital any jurisdiction over what their hands can reach.
- **The world at one step.** An air carrier pulls out of the remainder of a free rollout already in progress. The first people made into mages across the planet can bring the world’s freight doors together and carry the Boxes the rest of the way. Choose this take because the new magic comes from the hardware that has already reached its destinations, it changes where the remaining hardware can go, and its use at full scale changes the geography of every later delivery.

## Provisional direction

An international manufacturing cooperative is shipping human-sized, serial-built steel devices called The Box. First-phase depots on every continent open to anyone at no charge, with free local rides and ferries already running to them. A second phase will bring units to outlying districts. Anyone can step back out of an open unit. Each closure gives the occupant independent even odds of becoming a magic user or burning to death. The hardware changes the person or kills them. The people who survive wield the magic themselves.

During the worldwide opening the air carrier withdraws its second-phase cargo fleet as public deaths mount. The live depots and free passenger services keep operating. Temra Vosen, the freight rollout director, sees her colleague Olek Tamn die in an installed unit at Lipstone. She had planned to take her own chance that shift. After his death she makes the choice and survives. Ivari Doss, airstrip chief at Shale Crown half a world away, independently survives her own try. Trial users brought two openings together across a room. The spread of the first Boxes gives Temra a living partner at the other side of the world. A gate stays open while a living mage at each end holds it. One mage can take a gate from another by taking hold before the first lets go.

Temra and Ivari hold the ends of a full freight opening. A Shale Crown caster and a caster at Kelm Quay open the next link, which Temra can see through hers. A load Olek tagged for three transfers goes there directly. As further links open to occupied depots across the planet, the new routes take freight and people in both directions. Whoever holds each gate chooses where their end meets another. At Kelm Quay a new device comes off the load and people freely face the same chance that made the mages and killed Olek.

## Craft plan

**Teller.** Temra notices where weight has gone and who has been left waiting for it. Try close third past with a practical, proud freight director who loses sight of her own anger, and first person present with a dispatcher addressing arrivals and keeping herself steady. Stay bodily close for both closures and for the first crossing.

**Cast.** Temra has spent years getting every region a place on the schedule. She wants the world to use the devices and wants for herself the power to go places without waiting for a carrier. She thinks consent on a freight contract can carry the project farther than actual people’s hands can. She talks in complete instructions with concrete names and weights. Under pressure she checks a number she already knows. Olek wants to find out whether he can carry cargo to places his flight board always calls uneconomical. He believes Temra will watch his trial with her eyes on the next load. He tells little jokes with stories behind them and asks for human attention quite plainly when it matters. Ivari wants her own crews to route deliveries beyond the airstrip and remembers the shipments Temra made her leave on a runway last winter. She expects Temra to propose another schedule run out of Lipstone. Ivari speaks in patient, direct conditionals, moving as she speaks and leaving room for an answer. She has a different appetite for power from Temra’s. There are no supplied or established characters.

**Conversation owed.** Before Temra goes in, Ivari tells her over the line how she chose to close the door at Shale Crown. Ivari can talk about relief at her own survival and what she felt she had put in play. Temra has just lost Olek and needs to ask herself what his death changes about her own choice. Face to face after the small crossing, Temra wants Ivari to take responsibility for actual loads of the devices after they have reckoned with what the choice costs. Ivari wants Temra holding the first freight gate while her crews decide for themselves where the next way opens.

**Place.** At Lipstone Freight Hall the pallets and doors are big enough to take a new geography at full working scale. The North Array broadcast gallery could only show what happens elsewhere. The capital’s Station of Assembly would make a permission the decisive act. At Lipstone in the dry late afternoon, freight rollers ring, forklift batteries give off a hot smell, and pallet netting leaves a mark through a sleeve. The hangar doors look onto the carrier apron until they look onto the world. Shale Crown’s open tractor yard is rutted with snow at dawn. Wind drives rime through the first small crossing. Its two opposing freight gates let Temra look through one to a third working place across the other. Kelm Quay is an island freight market on another continent. Its own noon rain rattles red canvas awnings, and crates of softening yellow fruit wait by hand trucks. The broad arch in its market can be held from the far end of Shale Crown. The passenger ferry can bring people to the mainland’s live Box. The quay lacks installed devices and needs a cargo transfer from three previous stops. ## Scene briefs

1. **The lift stops, about a fifth.** At Lipstone, a pallet of serial Boxes moves while the first wave of free public units opens around the world. A device on a ramp is open to volunteers, beside the actual freight. Olek wants Temra there while he tries it. She wants him to stay to see his shipments through and keeps her own desire for magic private. He gives her the Kelm route tag in his queue and tells her why he wants to fly it directly. He shuts the door himself and burns to death, plainly and non-graphically. A worker in a glove with a blue cuff repair stays in the open queue after Olek. The next cargo crews are sent back from the apron as the carrier withdraws the lift.
2. **Two ends, just over two fifths and the long scene.** In front of the backed-up loading line, Ivari tells Temra what has happened at Shale Crown and what she wants sent there. Temra can stay at work as she is. She still wants the chance and closes the door herself. Stay with her inside until the result. Alive, she works with Ivari to bring a staff door and a windbreak opening together across half a world. Wind rimes the Lipstone floor. The volunteer in the repaired glove, who also came out alive, puts a hand on Temra’s jamb and holds with Ivari. Temra steps through to Shale Crown and takes in the huge opposed freight doors and their public Box. Here she and Ivari have the face-to-face conversation and decide what to send. Temra returns to Lipstone with plateau grit on her boots.
3. **The doors open out, almost two fifths.** Temra and Ivari hold the full Lipstone–Shale Crown opening. Across the tractor yard, within Temra’s sight through the first gate, a Shale Crown worker and a Kelm loader who survived at the mainland depot and ferried home take the ends of the next one. The quay’s warm rain runs across the snow. Temra gives Olek’s tag to the receiving crew from Shale Crown and lets them send the load by their direct route. Follow that pallet across both thresholds and its devices down into the quay. Other Lipstone openings come onto actual freight floors. Through one, a dead entrant is taken away on a covered stretcher under the midnight lamps at Rendar Depot. People step through both ways as the first partners get their load through to Kelm. At the market a worker opens the delivered units so that people can enter from the lane. One stranger steps back out. Another enters and closes a door of her own, with the same odds. Their choices leave the freight routes and the rest of the offer open.

## Ending

At Kelm a live steel door closes, with Olek’s route tag still on the crate beside it. Hold the instant when that person has made the choice and the outcome is yet to come. From Lipstone, Temra can see the market and the plateau within the working width of the same opening, and other live gates meeting docks in other hours of daylight. The first departure, the two independent outcomes, and the small crossing load the image. Summarize months of manufacture and trial use. Leave the management meetings and a survey of countries offstage.
