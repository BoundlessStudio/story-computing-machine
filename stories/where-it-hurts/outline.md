# Outline

## Story

- Title: Where It Hurts
- Premise and central promise: An adventurer watches road companion Dovran lose a voluntary bout to Sir Frascot, whose celebrated sword wounds feelings without cutting flesh. Dovran feels unwanted; the witness really has decided to leave their partnership.
- Focal pressure or attachment: The unnamed adult narrator wants one last enjoyable afternoon before taking an evening coach inland. They have secretly bought a ticket and arranged a winter room. Exhaustion with danger is real; so is pleasure in Dovran's company. They want departure without being experienced as someone who leaves.
- Counterforce or complication: Dovran expects another season together and mistakes postponement for agreement. He likes attention and the narrator's laughter; volunteering is his own foolish pleasure. The narrator encouraged him. Frascot prefers his weapon to killing but cannot guarantee proportionate suffering.
- POV, distance, and information limit: First-person past, recounted that night. Reveal the ticket early. Report Dovran's behavior and corrections without certifying his deepest wound or eventual recovery. Defensive humor sometimes exposes what explanations conceal.
- Governing movement and time shape: One afternoon: brief public bout, an uncomfortable walk, then waiting near the coach departure. Spectacle contracts into a disputed parting. Dovran leaves the narrator to wait alone; the friendship's eventual fate remains unknown.
- Speculative rule or ordinary-world constraint: Frascot deliberately channels living magic through the nonliving sword, striking with its flat. It imposes acute rejection without memories, messages, revealed secrets, or compelled behavior. Knowing the feeling lacks evidence does not remove it. Acute effects usually recede over days; consequences of choices remain. Frascot cannot recall a wound or cut it away.
- Dialogue promise: Adventuring bravado becomes embarrassing, intermittently funny friendship talk. Fighting skill supplies no emotional eloquence. Preserve irritation, old pleasure, mistaken reassurance, and ordinary speech.
- Dialogic medium: Boasts, warnings, spectators' laughter, attempted familiar touch, walking distance, interrupted questions, and a visible ticket. No projected memories or magical accusations.
- Dialogue engine: Reassurance delays information Dovran needs. He must distinguish imposed rejection from actual concealment. Disclosure changes the dispute from whether his feeling is true to what their attachment can survive without promises of another season.

## Voice

- Narrative texture: Notice gait, overheated clothes, mistimed laughter, and familiar equipment. Let quick comic judgments lengthen into uncomfortable observation; avoid diagnosing every gesture.
- Conversational texture: Frascot's economical warnings contrast with Dovran's performance for a willing friend. Private talk includes unfinished logistics, sulking, repetition, and jokes receiving the wrong response.
- Rhetorical ownership: Dovran owns extravagant ridicule and affectionate exaggeration; injury removes neither. The narrator evades through qualification and misreads laughter as permission. Frascot carries social authority but little patience for comforting explanations. Dovran sometimes listens better than the supposedly reasonable witness.
- Pressure behavior: Dovran's fluency breaks unpredictably, then returns as irritating precision about past promises. The narrator overexplains until an unanswerable small question stops them. Silence need not signal agreement.
- Relationship movement: The narrator wants release without losing affection and cannot comfortably request Dovran's blessing. Dovran wants to remain chosen but cannot bear begging for another journey. Frascot wants warnings heeded but dislikes asking challengers to admit fear. Exchanges remove the narrator's control of the farewell without settling love or blame.
- Anti-default: Avoid a merciful knight explaining therapeutic wisdom to two instantly self-aware friends; nobody converts hurt into a lesson on schedule.

## Beats

1. At an unnamed hiring fair, establish Frascot's fame through adventurers arguing about witnessed defeats and making room for his tied scabbard. Dovran wants his own ridiculous encounter to retell. The narrator enjoys him while hiding the ticket. Frascot plainly warns him; Dovran volunteers without understanding the experience.
2. Keep the bout short and physically legible. A light flat strike leaves Dovran upright, unmarked, and briefly amused. Then spectators' perfectly ordinary laughter becomes unbearable. His attempt to complete the performance fails. The narrator initially laughs too, expecting the famous effect to be theatrical.
3. Frascot stops the bout and refuses a proposed reversal. Explain only enough to prevent a false cure. Dovran can resent the knight without retracting his choice. The narrator gets him away; an old affectionate joke lands badly. Dovran remains capable of making a different, cutting joke himself.
4. Accumulate discomfort through specific misreadings. Dovran identifies the enchantment yet flinches from reassurance. The narrator steers toward the coach yard instead of their shared lodging. Dovran notices. Ordinary observation makes the ticket consequential; magic reveals nothing.
5. Departure becomes explicit. Give Dovran a concrete grievance about spending this afternoon under false expectations. The narrator must stop presenting winter rest as a short interruption when they cannot promise to return. Neither knows how this argument would feel without the strike. Affection cannot cancel the grievance.
6. Dovran declines to provide company until boarding or perform a gracious farewell. He leaves on his own feet, still hurt, choosing where the narrator cannot follow. The witness does not chase him for reassurance. End during the awkward wait, before travel or recovery tidies the encounter.

## Continuity

- Canon used: Living-agency, local-mechanism, personhood, and independent-artifact boundaries. Place this undated heroic society before the old-magic extinction, outside the Long Dark; assert no connection to established artifacts.
- Boundaries and unknowns: The sword causes injury, not moral insight. Affection, facts, agency, and dignity survive. Names were checked against the frozen baseline, relevant authority, and passing inventories; Dovran is distinct from Dovek and Cerdovan.
