# Opus review: Where It Hurts

Independent audit for a later editing pass. It is not a `review.md` verdict,
does not change canon state, and does not authorize edits by itself.

## Verdict

- Dialogue: PASS — three distinct voices, every exchange coheres and moves the relationship; only local clarity and rhythm polish remain.
- Prose: PASS — concrete, varied and restrained; one opening referent slip and one typo.
- Continuity and prompt: PASS — delivers the witnessed strike and a complete, open-ended parting; object chains and the magic mechanism hold.
- Edit scope: polish
- Existing review.md said: Verdict PASS, Dialogue PASS; agree, though it missed the small items below.

This is one of the collection's stronger dialogue stories, and the edit pass
should treat it gently. Dovran's performer's humour degrades under the wound in
visible stages ("Hit the other side, perhaps?" → a short ugly laugh → "tried to
smile and couldn't"). The narrator evades by offering things, qualifying, and
answering the literal question ("It would.", "Before I went."). Frascot speaks in
plain, tired warnings. The coach-yard exchanges each change something concrete:
the ticket, the feather errand exposed, the blankets confession, the refusal to
stay. The final exchange refuses a thesis. The problems are local: an addressee
slip in the flashback whisper, one misunderstanding knot in the decisive
confrontation that takes two passes to parse, and a mild pile-up of
mirror-and-turn replies in the coach yard. Fix those surgically. Do not smooth
Dovran's jokes or the narrator's evasions into more reasonable, articulate
speech.

## Dialogue findings

### D1 · Minor · Addressee slip in the whispered flashback line

- Where: L29–L31, flashback to the earlier surrender; secondary instance L97, just after the strike
- Text:
> “At least let him try,” he'd whispered to me. “You've put on all those buckles.”
> “Yes, thank you, I gathered it was the sword.” He took his hat from me and pushed it onto his head without putting the feather back. “Can you stop it?”
- Problem: Ground/referent. The tag says Dovran whispered this "to me", but "him" is Frascot and "You" is the absent challenger who surrendered, so the reader must jump twice to work out that Dovran is mock-addressing someone else. On first read it can sound as if the narrator is wearing buckles and being urged to fight, until "You go, then." straightens it out. Secondary: at L97, "Can you stop it?" comes straight after Dovran snaps at the narrator, so for a beat it reads as "stop reassuring me" until Frascot's head-shake re-routes it.
- Fix: Name the surrendering challenger or Frascot so the whisper points at the right person; keep the joke and the narrator's "You go, then." Illustrative only: “He could at least have let Frascot swing,” he'd whispered to me. “All those buckles.” Optional at L97: add a turn toward Frascot before "Can you stop it?"

### D2 · Minor · Misunderstanding knot in the decisive confrontation

- Where: L251–L257, coach yard, after "Before I went."
- Text:
> “You mustn't think this is because—”
> “I heard you. You bought it this morning.”
> “I meant because of you.”
> “Oh. Well. That's a relief.”
- Problem: Ground/uptake. Dovran's cut-in answers a guess he never voices (that the narrator meant "because of the sword" or "because of today"), and "I heard you" points back about forty lines to "The ticket this morning." The reader has to rebuild his guess to see why "You bought it this morning" is a reply. Then "I meant because of you" can briefly parse as an admission that the departure is because of him, which is the worst possible misreading at the heart of the story. The knot is coherent on a second pass, and Dovran's sarcastic "That's a relief" is right. The exchange just shouldn't need the second pass.
- Fix: Make Dovran's wrong guess audible so the narrator's correction reads one way only. Keep "Oh. Well. That's a relief." Illustrative only: “Because of the sword? I heard you. You bought it this morning.”

### D3 · Minor · Mirror-and-turn replies accumulate in the coach yard

- Where: L197–L199 (ticket timing), L263–L265 and L327–L329 (Dovran's barbs); related, earned instances at L165–L167, L175–L177, L227–L229, L271
- Text:
> “That's now.”
> “Not quite.”
> “So have I. It makes people offer you chairs.”
> “So did I. I was planning a journey.”
- Problem: Several replies pick up the other speaker's words and turn them ("There's a bench…" / "There's a bed…"; "You sent me…" / "You wanted…"; "I told you I wanted to stop." / "We did stop."). Each one is character-owned: the narrator dodges by qualifying, Dovran accuses with precision about the past. Taken together, though, they start to sound like the house correction rhythm. Two are the weak links. "Not quite." is pure pedantry that adds nothing after "That's now." Dovran's two strongest barbs also share one template (agree with "So have I/So did I", then sting), and the second one audibly echoes the first.
- Fix: Cut "Not quite." and let "He stood back." carry the narrator's non-answer. Reshape the second barb so it keeps both the admission and the sting without the "So did I" frame. Illustrative only: “I liked it too. I thought I was planning a journey.” Leave the other mirrored beats alone. "We did stop." leads into the shared laugh and "You wanted the feather." exposes the errand, so both must stay.

## Prose findings

### P1 · Minor · Hat/feather referent at the opening

- Where: L17 and L37, pre-bout handover
- Text:
> He took the hat back, pulled out its feather, and gave it to me again.
> Now he ducked under the rope, tucked the feather inside his shirt, and drew his own sword.
- Problem: "gave it to me again" sits right after "its feather", and the next sentence ("Already its preservation required two people") is also about the feather. So a reader can take it that the narrator now holds the feather, and then gets jolted at L37 when Dovran tucks it into his shirt. The feather is the story's main object thread, so its first handover should be unambiguous.
- Fix: Change "gave it to me again" to "handed me the hat again" (or equivalent). Nothing else needs to change.

### P2 · Minor · Typo: "sheath" as a verb

- Where: L113, after Frascot ties his sword down
- Text:
> Dovran was trying to sheath his own sword and missing.
- Problem: "sheath" is the noun. The verb is "sheathe", and L109 correctly uses "sheathed".
- Fix: "trying to sheathe his own sword".
