# The First Cold

Provisional direction: close third person through Ivara, a practical tower-maintenance worker irritated by the time her retiring mentor Deren spends listening after their measurements are finished. She loves his company and wants his approval, but does not want to inherit rounds that seem mostly ceremonial. The towers have heated humanity's last colony for three hundred years without a failure; the crew maintains their access equipment and surveys structures nobody knows how to repair.

A new contact instrument, used during an ordinary foundation survey, separates a human heartbeat from the furnace's continuous roar. Following it through an old sounding aperture, Ivara and Deren uncover living tissue continuous with the tower's structural ribs. Its mineral casing is its own grown armor; the furnace and its mechanisms belong to a living body, rather than enclosing a captive person. The builders remain unknown. Listening at another tower establishes another independent heartbeat, not one heart mechanically echoed through the whole colony.

Contact is slow and uncertain. The tower responds to pressure and vibration, but human speech does not automatically become a conversation. The workers learn something about the limits of its senses through an unsuccessful attempt to show or describe the outside. Deren's patience helps; one of his confident interpretations should also prove wrong. Ivara must attend to a response instead of either dismissing it as machinery or borrowing her mentor's belief. Neither worker can yet recover a biography or decide what sort of person this is.

The central discovery is that the being holding winter away has never experienced snow directly. The possibility of cold contact comes from a concrete object or action in the chamber, not a convenient eloquent request. A first offered touch startles it; the workers withdraw. Its deliberate reach for a second touch makes a recognizable choice. Ivara makes room for that choice, despite her instinct to preserve perfect warmth and her uncertainty about what care now requires. Possible ending: a sensory part of the immense body extends into an accessible threshold toward the cold. The fires continue without faltering. The other towers' separate lives are established, but they need not all share this one's desire, and the colony has not solved its dependence or converted in a crowd scene.

The intended pleasure is an immense, apparently indifferent structure acquiring an intimate bodily experience, with the small maintenance crew learning how to recognize the act. Aim for tactile awe, occasional plain work humor, and the friction of two people who know each other well; avoid a replacement sacrifice, a secret-government explanation, recovered builder instructions, or a fault manufactured to create urgency. The outline leaves the exact means of contact and final physical image open to what the prose earns.
