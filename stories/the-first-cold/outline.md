# The First Cold

Provisional direction: close third person through Ivara, a practical tower-maintenance worker irritated by the time her retiring mentor Deren spends listening after their measurements are finished. She loves his company and wants his approval, but does not want to inherit rounds that seem mostly ceremonial. The towers have heated humanity's last colony for three hundred years without a failure; the crew maintains their access equipment and surveys structures nobody knows how to repair.

A new instrument for thermal and acoustic profiles, used during an ordinary foundation survey, separates a human heartbeat from the furnace's continuous roar. Its calibration kit includes a small insulated flask of snow. Following the heartbeat through an old sounding aperture, Ivara and Deren uncover living tissue continuous with the tower's structural ribs. Ivara briefly fears that they have been feeding their furnaces with a captive human; the bodily continuity complicates that fear without supplying an origin or explaining away the ethical discovery. Its mineral casing is its own grown armor; the furnace and its mechanisms belong to a living body, rather than enclosing a captive person. The builders remain unknown. Listening at another tower establishes another independent heartbeat, not one heart mechanically echoed through the whole colony. Their heat alone establishes neither benevolent purpose nor a desire to serve humanity.

Contact is slow and uncertain. The tower responds to pressure and vibration, but human speech does not automatically become a conversation. The workers learn something about the limits of its senses through an unsuccessful attempt to show or describe the outside. The exposed soft tissue registers touch that the mineral exterior does not; centuries of losing heat to winter are different from feeling snow's texture, pressure, and melting. Deren's patience helps, but his wish to protect the creature can also make him overconfident about interpreting it. Neither worker can yet recover a biography or decide what sort of person this is.

The central discovery is that the being holding winter away has never experienced snow directly. The possibility of cold contact comes from the calibration flask, not a convenient eloquent request: snow is removed onto its lid or another offered surface so the tissue contacts snow itself, which melts promptly against it. A first offered touch startles it; the workers rightly withdraw. Deren treats that recoil as a final refusal and begins to close the aperture. After a delay, Ivara notices living tissue extending toward the place the snow has left and tells him to stop. She nearly advances another touch, wanting her interpretation to be right, then checks that impulse. Her consequential act is to disagree with her mentor, put a second offer within reach, and remain without crossing the final gap for the creature. The response comes in the quiet interval she wanted to eliminate from the rounds. Its extension and second contact make a recognizable choice, though the workers cannot certify an inner experience or give it a biography. Possible ending: a sensory part of the immense body extends into an accessible threshold toward the cold. The fires continue without faltering. The other towers' separate lives are established, but they need not all share this one's desire, and the colony has not solved its dependence or converted in a crowd scene.

The intended pleasure is an immense, apparently indifferent structure acquiring an intimate bodily experience, with the small maintenance crew learning how to recognize the act. Aim for tactile awe, occasional plain work humor, and the friction of two people who know each other well; avoid a replacement sacrifice, a secret-government explanation, recovered builder instructions, or a fault manufactured to create urgency. The outline leaves the exact means of contact and final physical image open to what the prose earns.
