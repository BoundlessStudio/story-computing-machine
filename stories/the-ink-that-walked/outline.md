# The Uninherited — provisional direction

The explicit promise is a young woman acquiring genuinely impossible abilities by tattooing stolen hereditary spells, followed by her oldest tattoos moving while she sleeps. I read that as the pleasure of ingenious, illicit acquisition turning into intimate dark wonder: her own skin becomes territory she cannot supervise. The image suggests a cropped-haired, visibly tattooed adult heroine and a stone castle; it does not prescribe a plot. My interpretation is a fast, tactile theft story that grows into a dangerous encounter between the thief and what she has stolen, with a dispossessed former owner providing a human counterweight. Close third person, probably one night and the following dawn. The ending should preserve the heroine's appetite while making her answer for it.

Desra, twenty-four, has no noble ancestry. Once employed to illuminate family spell-books, she discovered that a spell's complete branching shape, tattooed with a trace of its living bearer's blood, draws the gift into the new living skin. The donor truly loses that individual gift; their relatives retain theirs. Her growing blackwork lets her open sealed doors, fold a distance into one step, and arrest her own fall. The visible tattoos follow the supplied original. Her thefts were not all necessities. She likes the moment the impossible gives way.

At the ivy-grown royal castle, she uses several stolen abilities to rob a noble gathering and obtain the final pattern she needs for the king's inheritance chamber. At dawn she finds her oldest door-opening mark has crossed an inked boundary she drew before sleeping. Another night it has joined the fall-mark at her shoulder. She begins keeping herself awake. The gifts become less obedient, and she has to abort an otherwise glorious theft when a doorway opens somewhere she did not intend. The movement must produce physical and strategic consequences, rather than remain an ominous decoration.

Ildenne finds her. Desra stole Ildenne's distance-folding gift at an earlier encounter, knowing its loss would leave her exposed to her family's punishment. Ildenne has since been excluded from succession and confined to the castle; she has escaped by ordinary courage and preparation. She wants her particular gift back, not a revolution. She knows the royal chamber because her family once tended it. A recovered old illustration and a tattoo visibly reacting to its subject let them establish that hereditary spells are small living beings, bred and bound within particular families. Desra has broken their hereditary bindings without understanding what now keeps them in her. They gather during her sleep because they can form a body together. The king keeps the original parent-creature in the chamber; the gathered gifts are trying to get there. This explains their direction but does not excuse Desra's thefts or make their intentions safe.

The chamber offers Desra the royal binding pattern: she could fix every stolen spell in place and become the founder of her own hereditary house. The king's guards are already closing the castle. Ildenne offers a practical bargain: return her distance-folding gift and she will help Desra reach the chamber before capture. Desra must surrender the ability she depends on most before she can discover whether the rest of the bargain is survivable. Ildenne's own desire remains at odds with the assembled creature's; she will not casually agree that all family gifts should be released.

Possible climax: inside the chamber, Desra discovers that the gathered spells can escape their bindings only by completing their living body through her skin. Opening them would cost her control of the abilities and could leave her changed; binding them would repeat the royal captivity on a smaller scale. She refuses both automatic obedience to the tattoos and the promised dynasty. She uses what she has learned about their shapes to open only her own collection, at the moment that their combined impossible acts can carry her and Ildenne past the guards. The magical escape should be the largest, strangest set piece, with her decision causing it. She does not settle the kingdom's future or free gifts belonging to strangers. The parent-creature's fate and the exact bodily cost must be earned in drafting, not supplied by a convenient final rule.

Possible last image: in the ordinary morning outside the walls, Ildenne has her gift and can leave Desra behind. Desra has lost the obedient arsenal she wanted, but the oldest mark has left a door-shaped absence among the black branches. Something within it knocks. She has to decide whether to answer, awake this time. I would rather discover a sharper ending in the prose than force this image if it only resets the mystery.
