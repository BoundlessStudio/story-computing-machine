# The Uninherited — provisional direction

The explicit promise is a young woman acquiring genuinely impossible abilities by tattooing stolen hereditary spells, followed by her oldest tattoos moving while she sleeps. I read that as the pleasure of ingenious, illicit acquisition turning into intimate dark wonder: her own skin becomes territory she cannot supervise. The image suggests a cropped-haired, visibly tattooed adult heroine and a stone castle; it does not prescribe a plot. My interpretation is a fast, tactile theft story that grows into a dangerous encounter between the thief and what she has stolen, with a dispossessed former owner providing a human counterweight. Close third person, probably one night and the following dawn. The ending should preserve the heroine's appetite while making her answer for it.

Desra, twenty-four, has no noble ancestry. Once employed to illuminate family spell-books, she discovered that a spell's complete branching shape, tattooed with a trace of its living bearer's blood, draws the gift into the new living skin. The donor truly loses that individual gift; relatives retain theirs. Her growing blackwork lets her open sealed doors, fold a distance into one step, and arrest her own fall. The visible tattoos follow the supplied original. Her thefts were not all necessities. She likes the moment the impossible gives way.

She wants the royal binding pattern, kept in the ivy-grown castle's inheritance chamber. It could fix her wandering stolen gifts permanently in her body and let her found a hereditary house of her own. Several prior nights survive as tracing sheets: a boundary she drew round the oldest door-mark is crossed by dawn; its hinge joins the fall-mark at her shoulder. The main action occupies the last night before she attempts the chamber. She uses several powers in an exuberant theft, but a migrating mark opens a door she did not mean to open and exposes her. Misfires and lost sleep are making her arsenal unsafe.

Ildenne confronts her along the route Desra must take. Desra stole her distance-folding gift while employed on her family book, knowing the loss would endanger her place in the family. Ildenne has since been excluded from succession and confined to the castle; her prepared escape gives her a reason to be on this route. She wants her particular gift back, not a revolution. The same transfer can return a gift if its current bearer supplies the trace. Desra has already considered returning the gifts and refused: she wants the binding pattern instead. Ildenne offers useful knowledge of the chamber's guarded approaches for her own gift's return. Desra surrenders her best means of escape while she still could keep it and go on alone. A crossing she formerly skipped becomes physically inaccessible to her. The bargain does not purchase pardon.

Desra makes the discovery herself. Comparing her own increasingly obsolete tracings, she recognizes growth rather than mere rearrangement. Her former craft lets her see a beak in one flourish and a joint in another; a deliberately interrupted test makes a tendril recoil and costs her use of a needed spell. Her gifts are alive. The hereditary family forms held them apart; in her mixed body they have begun assembling one new individual. They want one another and a way out, not the royal chamber. They cannot yet cross the outer boundary of her skin. Their wish diverges from hers, and they will not understand her proposed bargain or owe her gratitude. No parent-creature or recovered explanatory picture supplies the answer.

In the chamber, the royal knot is both a real temptation and the final piece of knowledge. Desra tests a segment: one moving mark stops, obeys, and opens the locked cabinet she chooses. This gift opens existing closed doors; it does not carry her past the guards beyond them. Completing the hereditary closure would permanently fix the whole arsenal and give her the coordinated powers for an escape. Reading how the knot closes also shows her how to open her collection before that final closure, not how to undo a completed hereditary bind. She knows opening it will empty the abilities from her, leaving the guards between a powerless thief and the outer walls. Inaction offers worsening misfires and capture; no new bodily punishment is needed to make the viable selfish path real.

She opens the collection anyway, partly because she cannot bear to fix this half-made living thing in place and partly because she wants, desperately, to see what it will become. The birth should be the strangest and largest magical event. The gifts leave her as one black, winged creature, using the combined impossible abilities to escape for themselves. They do not rescue her, give her a consoling power, or settle the kingdom's future. Ildenne keeps her own restored hereditary gift. Her disagreement with Desra's choice remains legitimate within her knowledge and desire.

Ildenne is not waiting in the chamber to supply a rescue. Once she has received her gift and delivered her promised directions, she leaves on her prepared route; Desra makes the choice alone. On the way out Ildenne hears the guards close in on Desra, whose capture means execution. She chooses to come back as far as the chamber's exterior window: she wanted restitution, not Desra's death. Desra must force the casement and get onto its narrow outer sill under her own ordinary strength, without the fall-gift. Ildenne can then take her hand and fold the distance to a lower roof; the ability to carry a hand-held companion will be established earlier. Her help stops there. She will not be seen sheltering Desra, and no pardon is exchanged. Desra must make the final dangerous climb beyond the castle herself; she can truly fall. The emptied shapes remain on her skin, and her appetite survives in the audacious route she chooses. No final knocking mark resets the mystery or refunds her loss. The exact ending is still provisional and should emerge from this irreversible choice.
