# Promised experience

At Le Nexus, a secondhand mechanical arm repeatedly reaches toward a stranger. Its wearer pins it to the bar. The fingers tap a message in a code the wearer has never learned, and the woman sets down her red cocktail and taps back. The surprise of “taps back” suggests the pleasure of watching a bodily embarrassment turn into private recognition in a room full of people. The two women in the original give that turn an assured, teasing charge. The encounter starts at the scale of an elbow’s reach. I expect the exchange to change what the women can do together. The code’s source, the larger stakes, and whether the charge becomes romance are open.

## Takes considered

- **The old dance.** The arm offers a hold from a two-woman turn once danced at Le Nexus. The wearer takes the stranger onto the floor and uses the stranger’s own code to reverse who gets to call the ending.
- **The keepsake.** From the stranger’s side, the tap is a private joke she taught the previous wearer, now dead. Her reply draws out an unexpected last question about herself, which she must answer with a living stranger wearing the fingers that asked it.
- **The wrong side of a wager.** The arm knows the quiet counter signals that call off a bad wager at Le Nexus. The woman answers during a crowded round, and its new wearer has to work out which of the people around her needs the other end of the bargain.

The dance keeps both women in view. Its central scene puts the trust implied by the first exchange to a physical test and lets the wearer make an answer with the hand that started the trouble.

## Provisional direction

Keva comes to dance in a secondhand left arm she uses capably. A woman from the presses once danced a turn at Le Nexus with Odile, traded this arm toward a lighter one, and left town. Keva bought it off a rack on Pressers’ Lane. Odile absently drums that pair’s old starting cue. The left hand keeps opening toward her. When Keva pins it, the fingers ask for their partner’s grip in the taps the pair used in the din. Odile sets down her red cocktail and answers before she looks Keva full in the face. Her hand is already halfway to the flattened patch on the steel thumb where she used to grip it. She catches herself when she sees whose arm it is now.

Odile wants to feel the old turn again, down to the dip where the arm would catch her across the waist. The arm remembers the movement and the taps, and Keva can intervene if she catches it before it commits its weight. Before they leave the rail Keva asks to learn the signal for changing who takes the dip. Odile teaches it to her on the zinc, where Keva tries it with her steel fingertips. Going out means trusting the other woman to heed a change whenever it comes. Either woman could still take the dip.

In the last sweep the steel hand settles against Odile’s right waist and the motor pulls hard against Keva’s shoulder strap as Odile begins to lean. Keva can follow the familiar catch. She holds the movement short and pries the steel fingertips out of their closing grip long enough to tap the change against Odile’s side. Give Odile time to feel the flattened thumb find its old place and look up at the new woman before she comes back over her own feet. She slips her left arm around Keva’s waist, Keva finds her shoulder with her right hand, and Keva lets herself fall into Odile’s hold. Odile sweeps her down and brings her upright. Their last look carries the instant each waits for the other.

## Craft plan

- **Teller.** Keva tells it from inside the evening. Try a first person past that takes mischievous pleasure in recounting a scene she wanted to get away with, against a close third present that stays with the strain below her left collarbone and lets Odile’s expression surprise her. Keep Keva’s tendency to judge what a room is about to do and her appetite for being seen on the floor.
- **Keva.** A sturdy, practised dancer who plants herself at the bar and would rather make an error in full view than be applauded for trying. Her spoken sentences are spare when she is deciding something and exact about where a person puts their weight. Once she decides to argue, she can answer a whole little performance with one question that exposes the terms. The original shows short dark curls, a gold hoop, a buckled black leather harness, a mismatched steel left arm and whiskey in her right hand. She braces and takes her time turning. The hoop catches when she tucks back her curls to see if Odile noticed. The flattened thumb and scuffed, riveted plates show where other people have had their hands. The harness pulls the same way at the rail and on the floor, then slackens as she lets Odile take her weight.
- **Odile.** The original gives her a strong build, loose blonde hair, brown zip-front leather, a red stemmed drink and a smile that makes an approach feel like her idea. She leans in and holds her ground with a shoulder when people press in for their orders. She talks with the warm assurance of a regular who has been forgiven for much worse, tells whole anecdotes to get her way, and calls a step cleanly when they have to move. The red drink stops midway through a sip at the first coherent taps. She twists her hair tighter before they go out, then lets a strand stay against her mouth while she waits for Keva’s call. The sidelong smile returns even as those steady shoulders have Keva’s weight. Let the cuts, buckles and colours suggest the bold image without dwelling on exposed skin.
- **Place.** Le Nexus on Pressers’ Lane, a wet November Friday a little before midnight. The night trams divide outside, the late shift follows, and the bartender stacks empties at an elbow to make room for orders. Choose the rail and adjoining floor over the mirror booths, where fewer people can see a lapse, and the upstairs practice loft, where a pair can talk through a turn. The regulars clear a strip for partner turns for one song. Pink script and blue bottle shelves in the mirrors, red light under dark zinc, oil in the coats over crushed grapefruit peel, bass through a heel, and the cold underside of the rail. Taps carry through the zinc when an elbow or the edge of a hand is on it. On the floor the women have to pass the signal through a hold, with people close enough to brush them and loud enough to swallow a word.

## Scenes and ending

1. **At the zinc rail, about two fifths.** Keva wants her drink and a turn on the floor. A regular reads the reach as a come-on and clears space for it, Odile grins, and a repeat gets Keva’s right palm across the metal wrist. The fingertips keep time while the captured wrist stays still. Odile puts the red glass down, answers, and checks the movement of her hand toward that familiar thumb. Odile tries to sell Keva on what they’ve just heard, with room to finish her story about the old turn. Keva decides to try the floor after Odile shows her the change on the rail.
2. **On the open floor, the long scene.** The women have one song to find out what they can do together. The steps have room to wander when the edge of the crowd closes and Keva feels the arm anticipate the old finish. Odile gets the grip she nearly reached for at the rail. Stay close to Keva through the effort to make the fingers tap against the closing hand, and through the interval where Odile can still go either way. Odile rights herself and offers her own hold, and Keva takes it. End with the shoulder strap loose as Odile lands Keva upright. The pinned arm’s first invitation, Odile’s almost-touch, and the code learned by both women’s hands at the rail load that last exchange.

Give the arm’s resale and the former dancer’s departure the space of one exchange. Omit the arm’s manufacture and the regulars’ histories.
