# Promised experience

At Le Nexus, a secondhand mechanical arm repeatedly reaches toward a stranger. Its wearer pins it to the bar. The fingers tap a message in a code the wearer has never learned, and the woman sets down her red cocktail and taps back. The surprise of “taps back” suggests the pleasure of watching a bodily embarrassment turn into private recognition in a room full of people. The two women in the original give that turn an assured, teasing charge. The encounter starts at the scale of an elbow’s reach. I expect the exchange to change what the women can do together. The code’s source, the larger stakes, and whether the charge becomes romance are open.

## Takes considered

- **The old dance.** The arm offers a hold from a two-woman turn once danced at Le Nexus. The wearer takes the stranger onto the floor and uses the stranger’s own code to reverse who gets to call the ending.
- **The keepsake.** From the stranger’s side, the tap is a private joke she taught the previous wearer, now dead. Her reply draws out an unexpected last question about herself, which she must answer with a living stranger wearing the fingers that asked it.
- **The wrong side of a wager.** The arm knows the quiet counter signals that call off a bad wager at Le Nexus. The woman answers during a crowded round, and its new wearer has to work out which of the people around her needs the other end of the bargain.

The dance keeps both women in view. Its central scene puts the trust implied by the first exchange to a physical test and lets the wearer make an answer with the hand that started the trouble.

## Provisional direction

Keva comes to take back her Friday place among the regular dancers, wearing a secondhand left arm she uses capably. Odile once danced a turn in this room with the arm’s previous wearer, who quit the act and moved away. The arm still remembers the offer and replies of that particular turn. Odile’s fingers give its old starting cue at the bar as she drums absentmindedly during the house song. The left hand keeps opening toward her. When Keva pins it, the fingers ask for its partner’s grip in the private taps the pair used to keep time in the din. Odile sets down her red cocktail and answers before she looks Keva full in the face.

Odile wants to do their famous figure again while the floor is theirs for a song. Its finish calls for Keva to catch her weight in the old hold. The arm can still go through the turn once the hold is offered, and Keva has already felt how quickly it takes over. Keva wants a turn of her own in this room. She asks Odile to teach her the tap for “change hands,” tries it on the counter, and sets the terms for going out. In the last sweep Odile has to trust an open hand where she expects the old catch. Keva calls for the change, brings her right hand into play and asks Odile to carry her through the finish. The crowd sees the woman who used to be caught bring her partner upright.

## Craft plan

- **Teller.** Keva tells it from inside the evening. Try a first person past that takes mischievous pleasure in recounting a scene she wanted to get away with, against a close third present that stays with the strain below her left collarbone and lets Odile’s expression surprise her. Keep Keva’s tendency to judge what a room is about to do and her appetite for being seen on the floor.
- **Keva.** A practised social dancer, particular about her shoes, who would rather make an error in full view than be applauded for trying. Her spoken sentences are spare when she is deciding something and exact about where a person puts their weight. Once she decides to argue, she can answer a whole little performance with one question that exposes the terms. The original shows short dark curls, a gold hoop, a buckled black leather silhouette and a visibly jointed left arm, with whiskey in her right hand. She braces, appraises and takes her time turning. The hoop catches in her hair when she turns to count the reaches. The whiskey warms in her right hand until she has to set it down to pin her left. The black strap at her shoulder bears what she feels of the reach, then loosens when she lets Odile take her weight.
- **Odile.** The original gives her loose blonde hair, brown zip-front leather, a red stemmed drink and a smile that makes an approach feel like her idea. She settles, leans in and makes room by moving the other person. She talks with the warm assurance of a regular who has been forgiven for much worse, tells whole anecdotes to get her way, and calls a step cleanly when they have to move. The red drink stops midway through a sip at the first coherent taps. She twists her hair tighter before they go out, then lets a strand stay against her mouth while she waits for Keva’s call. The sidelong smile returns while Odile has Keva’s weight. Let the cuts, buckles and colours suggest the bold image without dwelling on exposed skin.
- **Place.** Le Nexus on Pressers’ Lane, a warm first Friday in September, a little before midnight. Choose its bar rail and the adjoining narrow dance floor over the mirror booths, which hide a lapse too easily, and the upstairs practice loft, which gives a pair all the time they want. Le Nexus clears a strip for partner turns during the house song. The bartender stacks empty glasses at the elbow to make room for the late crowd. Pink script and blue bottle shelves in the mirrors, red light under the dark zinc rail, crushed grapefruit peel, a bass note that can travel through a heel, and the cold underside of the rail. Taps carry through the counter when the elbow or the edge of a hand is on it. On the floor the women have to pass the signal directly, with people close enough to spot a hesitation and loud enough to swallow a word.

## Scenes and ending

1. **At the zinc rail, before the floor clears, about a third.** Keva wants her drink and the chance to dance without giving anybody a story about the replacement arm. Odile is enjoying being left to her own drink and assumes the first reach is a come-on. A repeat gets Keva’s right palm across the metal wrist. Odile puts the red glass down and answers. The fingertips keep time while the captured wrist stays still.
2. **The rail as the crowd opens, then the turn in the same room, the long scene.** Odile wants a partner for her old finish and talks as if half the decision has already been made. Keva wants to know what the hand will do and finds both a cue she can use and a demand she can make of Odile. Give the first measures and the difference between automatic and chosen weight room on the page. When Keva calls for the change in the old code, Odile answers with her weight and brings Keva through. Keva feels the pressure come off the familiar spot under the shoulder strap. The earlier open hand, the grip Keva expected to have forced on her, and Odile’s air of knowing where the floor will end make the reversed finish legible. End inside their meeting gaze as Odile lands Keva upright, with the drink still at the rail.

Condense the arm’s purchase and the break-up of the former act into the details the women notice or need from each other. Leave the arm’s manufacture and the regulars’ histories out.
