I had just got my whiskey at Le Nexus when my left hand tried to pick up a woman. I’d bought the arm two doors up Pressers’ Lane, off a rack with spring clips for the wrists, such as you might hang a bicycle from. The dealer gave me a sheet of tests and ate a cheese roll while I did them. I sorted coins in the palm, picked the price sticker off his glass and put it back without a crease. The enamel plate over the elbow came from a blue arm and a place on the steel thumb had been worn flat. It suited me. I had already had one arm that obliged people to ask what happened to it.

The woman beside me had her blonde hair done up in a way that looked good for about the next three minutes. She was nursing a red cocktail and letting the bartender tell her about a man who’d had a coat stolen at his own wedding. Her free hand drummed its fingers in order along the zinc. That was when my hand got up. There was a courtliness to it, with that thumb held out of the way. She gave me the private grin a woman can give you while somebody is still talking to her.

I put the hand back where it had been. A tram driver with half his brass buttons undone caught the grin and picked up his glass. I smelled wet wool as he brushed past me on his way round to her other side. Now there was a nice wide stretch of bar for the two of us. Everybody was so helpful on a Friday. I wanted enough whiskey to get warm and then a dance when they cleared the tables.

The bartender pulled his board toward him and slit a grapefruit. I got the whole bright smell of it as the strap took a pull from behind. This time the arm had got as far as her elbow. She was lifting it to make room. I got the heel of my right hand onto the steel wrist and leaned until the edge of a buckle dug into my ribs. I could keep it there. The fingers walked down the zinc one by one and had the marvelous nerve to make the little one wait for its turn. That was the one with a worn cap. It struck a high note on the zinc, like the end of a question.

The woman set her glass down by the stem. She knocked twice on the zinc, with a space between, and I felt both knocks in the heel of the hand I was holding the wrist with. The man was still talking. I’d never have guessed the two sounds belonged together. She had brought her hand across the wet ring left by my whiskey and was reaching for the thumb. She paused with her fingers bent. She had looked at me earlier, across the lip of the glass, as someone she might choose to know in a few minutes. The look she gave me now took longer, although there was less of it she wanted me to see.

“Leave it there,” I said.

She held her hand where it was until she saw I meant the arm, then drew it back. “I thought somebody was being very funny at my expense. There used to be a burr on the end of your thumb. It caught the lining of my jacket until I sanded it off with an emery board. Took a whole set of them. She’d hold her hand like that forever if I let her, just under my elbow. I used to wait with my drink halfway up to see which one of us would give in.”

I lifted my right palm. The steel hand stayed spread as if there were still a reason for me to pin it. I put my right thumb where hers had been going. You could feel the small plane she’d made even with a callus.

“Who was she?”

“A woman called Rika, from the presses.” She picked up her drink to let the bartender’s cloth go by. “She traded it in for a lighter one a week before she left. I saw her off on the night train and she carried her suitcase in the new hand the whole way to her seat. She wanted me to see what all the money was for. I’d have bought this one if she’d asked. God knows what I would have done with it. It went to Meers with the blue sign, did it?”

I nodded and took a drink. The whiskey had got warm while I was waiting for it to warm me. On the floor a man was blotting a spill with his sleeve. He and the woman he was dancing with got four or five steps farther along every time he bent down, and she did exactly the same thing with her feet each time she had to wait for him. I wanted to be where they were. I’d spent the past month going through the door two up from a bar I liked, and taking the long way back to avoid the window.

“What did it ask you?”

She offered me her fingers over the zinc. “For a hand. Rika and I used to start sideways at the rail, when there wasn’t room on the floor to stand on both feet. By the time I had her fingers I could haul my foot out from under a tram driver’s stool and we were off. We could do a whole dance with only the two of us knowing what the other one had said.”

She let her hand drop between us. “See where it goes when I turn.”

I put down my whiskey. She turned until we were both facing the bar. My left hand slipped behind her and shut around her waist. The red light under the rail shone between the plates where the wrist had bent. She put a little of her weight against it and I felt the strap take hold of my back. Her shoulders had scarcely moved. The curl beside my gold hoop stirred when she breathed out.

“Would you do the whole thing if I stood here?” I asked the hand. I straightened my elbow and the strap let up. She was still on both her feet when I got the hand away.

“Here’s how to change.” She caught my steel hand and rapped three quick times on its back with one finger. “My knee went through a deck board once. For quite a while I had to stay up when we danced, and the minute a man came in thinking he was going to sweep me down, I’d give him that and put my own arm round his waist. Down he’d go in my arms and he’d have to wait till I stood him up again. Some of them got a taste for it. That lot at the corner can tell you.”

She had a way of delivering a sentence to me and then turning the end of it out toward everybody within reach. She lifted her glass at the tram driver. He laughed and dropped his keys in his empty tumbler. The bartender took it away and came back with another whiskey.

“Let me try it,” I said.

She let me put the hand at her waist and leaned again. I could lift a price sticker off a glass with the steel fingers. Getting the ring finger up by itself when the whole hand wanted to close was a different proposition. I got two knocks out against her ribs and the finger stuck. She waited with that little bit of herself resting on me. I opened the hand a fraction. This time I got three knocks off in a hurry and she came upright by herself. I’d already let go when I felt her arm go round me. For as long as I leaned against it she kept it there.

We were still facing the bar. I could see the damp ends of her hair close to my cheek in the pink glow off the mirror behind the bottles. I wanted to know what she’d been about to say with her hand when she reached for the thumb. I’d need a better acquaintance with her before I could ask.

“Come out with me when they clear,” I said, and went to get my whiskey from where I’d left it. Before I could lift it she was there at the rail beside me, putting up her hair.

“Odile,” she said. She waited with the band between her teeth till I told her mine.

* * *

A horn played two notes from the bottom of its range, and a man by the mirrors finished whatever he was saying to his wife and put their chairs one on top of the other. Along the edge of the floor people sucked in their stomachs to let the dancers past. Here you could go up to a person you hadn’t seen since last winter and put a hand under her elbow, and she might ask after your mother as the two of you went by the bar. You had the length of the song before they wanted their chairs back.

Odile put a shoulder between two men who were talking across the opening. The brass buttons on a driver’s coat scraped one of my buckles when I went in after her. Once we were clear she turned around and walked backward. I had thought she’d take both my hands before we set off. She waited with hers down by her thighs. I could hear the horn better here, with the speaker high up on the far wall, and feel the double stroke of the bass where the floor had worn thin. My hand was quiet. I’d been holding my left elbow too close to my ribs and I let it out.

I offered her my right hand. She put her left in it and settled her other hand lightly on my shoulder, over the lower buckle where a person could get purchase if she wanted it. The pink sign in the mirror lit one side of her face. She tipped her head to show me the stretch of boards behind her that no one had got to yet. I gave her a push with the heel of my hand and she went, grinning as if I’d put her up to something.

Odile danced at an unhandsome distance. A pocket of air stayed between her and me, so that when she stepped across me on the upswing it came as a surprise how near her thigh was. It took me four bars to believe she could be where she promised from so far away. Once I believed it I took longer steps. She let me have the run along the rail and I took it all the way down to the stack of glasses. Farther than we meant to go. A man with his back to us started to ease his way out with six drinks held against his stomach. I could feel the laugh begin in Odile before she opened her mouth. She swung us both round his back and never touched him. I kept my right hand wide for her to come back through and she came a little closer this time.

I had missed this. There were tables at home where I could make a corner with my feet when I was waiting for the kettle to boil, and a landing where there was a beautiful run of four boards from my door to the neighbour’s, if nobody happened to be coming up the stairs. Odile had laughed so hard she’d pitched forward. There was sweat between her shoulder blades where I put my hand until she’d got her breath. I raised that arm to go by another couple and the blue enamel flared under the bottle lights. Odile watched it go by. I’d brought her out there with the arm in plain sight and I wanted her to look at it for as long as she liked. I went on dancing while she did.

She brought her mouth to my ear when the music went back to the first figure. I felt her say something before I heard any of it. It was my name. I gave her a small pull with my right hand and she turned until we were facing the bar together. She came so close on my left that her heel clipped mine. The forearm ran up hard against my side. My steel hand slipped behind her and found the place where the leather of her vest ended at her waist. The thumb turned in.

I could feel the rivet in the strap where it crossed my back. Odile was still over her feet and the motor was getting ready to have all of her. All I had to do was set my right foot a little farther back and she would follow that hand down into a sweep it had already had years to learn. In the blue light I saw her eyes go from my face to the thumb. She covered it with her hand and pressed the flat place with the pad of her own thumb as if she had found it in the dark. Her chin lifted. I felt the hand take the first of her weight.

I’d have liked to catch her. We had the room for it and there was a terrific look of ease about the way she started down. I could give her the whole thing she remembered. Under my right foot the board ran lengthwise, with a little play between it and the next one. I pressed my heel down where it was. The strap went tighter across my back. I edged my wrist away from her waist while the thumb stayed where she had it. For the length of a breath I had the ring finger free. I knocked three times fast against her ribs, just below the edge of her vest. On the third strike I felt her take a sharp little breath.

She was close enough for me to see where her zipper had caught a thread, with the hair she’d bundled up two minutes ago coming down beside her mouth. She still had the thumb. At the bar I’d thought she’d straighten up as soon as I got the taps off. There was a long, bare part of the music in which she might still lie back against my hand. I watched her come over her own feet and take her hand away. Her eyes lifted from the thumb to find mine.

Odile turned in against me. The way she put her left arm around my waist left my right hand free to find her shoulder, and I felt her take one step that put her in a place I could fall toward. She was waiting while I still had my weight in my heels. The lights over the bar had made a bent red line in the mirror. I had time to see it from halfway down and then to lose it and see Odile’s face in its place. She had a wide stance and a shoulder as firm as the rail. I’d been expecting a jolt when she got my whole weight, but all I felt was the warm shape of her arm coming up under my ribs. I let the left arm hang. The strap went loose across my back.

She brought me up slowly enough to let my heels find the floor. My right hand stayed on her shoulder and I could feel that she’d worked for it from the different lengths of her breaths. Her smile came back on one side first. The rail was a few feet away. In the mirror you could see a red stemmed glass beside a spot where somebody had managed to leave a whiskey standing. The horn played its two low notes again. Odile drew her hand away from my waist and it hung between us, palm up. She’d started to turn it over to let it fall when I put my left hand in it.
