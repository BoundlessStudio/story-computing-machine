## Hard constraints

- This is an ordinary in-progress, non-canon story; its creative-input history creates no lifecycle, review, publication, or promotion exception. (`story.json`; `stories/INDEX.md`.)
- Keep one coherent physical world across deep time. A contemporary-looking setting need not be literal real-world Earth. (`universe/premise.md`, “The deep-time shared world” [LOCKED]; `universe/locations.md`, “The world across eras” [LOCKED].)
- In the modern-like era, the watch and deity must be rare private anomalies rather than publicly routine magic. (`universe/timeline.md`, “Established era order” [CANON]; `universe/glossary.md`, “Modern-like era” [CANON].)
- The copper watch may channel living agency but cannot independently use magic unless established as alive. Eli, Rose, a living bloodline bond, or the deity may supply agency. (`universe/rules.md`, “Living agency and magic” [LOCKED].)
- If Goddess of Time is objectively a god rather than a claimed title, she needs continuing worship, power, and authority. Contract limits may be local; do not imply universal divine government. (`universe/rules.md`, “Divinity” [CANON].)
- Time reversal is one local mechanism. Do not generalize it, connect it to the Glass Gate, or infer kinship with other copper artifacts from motif. (`universe/rules.md`, “Portals and crossing systems” and “Artifacts, motifs, and folklore” [CANON].)
- Rewritten-time consequences must support Mae's retained memories coherently. Branches, erased timelines, or divine testimony cannot arbitrarily patch contradiction. (`universe/rules.md`, “Memory, transformation, copies, and branches” and “Records, falsehood, and historical traces” [CANON].)
- Mae's pain, identity, agency, grief, and relationship with Eli remain morally real, never a simulation or disposable timeline. (`universe/style-guide.md`, “Personhood and moral consequence” [LOCKED].)
- Contact with the dead must fit the underlying afterlife while remaining one local, culturally interpreted interface. (`universe/rules.md`, “Afterlife and continuation” [CANON].)
- Keep death and grief non-graphic under the Teen ceiling. (`universe/style-guide.md`, “Audience and content boundaries” [LOCKED].)

## Useful established context

- Intimate, playful speculative stories and independent entry points need no crossover or deep-time exposition. (`universe/style-guide.md`, “Tonal range, scale, and connection” [CANON].)
- A mechanism may impose a local cost without making sacrifice, desire, or trauma universal fuel. (`universe/rules.md`, “Costs, desire, and local mechanisms” [CANON].)
- Bloodline signature or ownership contract may be rare local identity binding; names and kinship are not universally magical. (`universe/rules.md`, “Names and identity binding” [CANON].)
- Gods may be locally constrained by contracts, institutions, domains, peers, or rivals, so inability to seize the watch can fit if authority remains bounded.

## Conflicts or ambiguity

- No authoritative conflict or user ruling blocks planning.
- The creative input contradicts itself: the cinema correction is nine seconds in the scene but an eleven-minute rewind in the audit. Choose one duration and make all references agree.
- The visitor is called a goddess without evidence of ongoing worship. Add restrained evidence meeting the project definition or leave the title viewpoint-limited.
- Divine Contract, minor saint, and contraband could imply broad bureaucracy. Keep them local unless the delta deliberately proposes wider scope.
- The input does not decide whether intervals are overwritten, branched, preserved in the afterlife, or remembered through the deity's system. The final story must avoid mutually incompatible claims.

## Unknowns

- The watch's maker, age, mechanism, and relationship to the deity.
- Who created or enforces the contract and whether peer institutions exist.
- Where erased duration goes, whether material aging is consistent, and who retains memories.
- Why Rose can rewind nine days when Eli usually achieves seconds or minutes.
- Whether Tuesday is causal or only a family motif; treat it as motif absent a narrow rule.
- Whether “minor saint of bad decisions” is a true saint, god, powerful being, or joke.
- The exact era and literal-Earth identity of its London-like geography.
- Whether Mae's tea appearance is afterlife contact, mediation, or continuation; any answer must preserve her personhood.

## Safe invention space

- Define one locally constrained watch activated by living users and bound to a family through a witnessed contract.
- Give the time deity a narrow domain, limited worship, and bounded authority, or preserve her title as unverified self-description.
- Treat Mae's appearance as a one-time mediated interface without defining universal afterlife architecture.
- Establish watch-specific range, memory, and consequences while leaving maker and cosmic accounting unknown.
- Keep Tuesday an uncanny motif and renewed family cycle rather than universal magic.
- Lightly generalize brands/geography if needed to avoid deciding literal Earth.
- Rename the one-off date called Mara with a registry-safe form.

## Name constraints

- `Mara` collides with distinct Mara Osset in *The Small Moon Rose First*. Coordinated planning retains the Tuesdays character's form and renames the other story's identity, unless the plan finds a stronger unique resolution.
- `Eli Mercer` and `Cal Mercer` share a surname. No story uses `Mercer` alone, so this is not exact, but avoid implying kinship and consider a unique surname if it does not harm the premise.
- `The Goddess of Time` is unique as a full title, while `Goddess` belongs to Queen Aurelia's alias set. Use the full title or `Time`; do not use capitalized standalone `Goddess` or imply one identity.
- `Aunt Linda`, `Eli Mercer`/`Eli`, `Mae`, `Rose`, and `The Goddess of Time`/`Goddess of Time`/`Time` are otherwise unique. Unnamed relatives may stay unnamed. (`stories/NAMES.md`.)
