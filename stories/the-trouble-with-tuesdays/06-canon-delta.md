# Proposed canon delta

This file proposes reusable shared-universe facts. It is not canon until the
user explicitly approves promotion and the facts are added to `universe/`.

## New characters or character facts

- **Eli Fenwick** (proposed): Mae's younger brother, Rose's uncle, and the
  inheritor of his unnamed grandfather's scratched copper watch. Eli uses the
  watch forty-six times: fifteen times for small corrections and thirty-one
  times while trying to prevent Mae's death. After hearing Mae's account of
  those attempts, he refuses a forty-seventh activation and never uses the
  watch again, but he neither destroys it nor warns Rose before his death many
  years later.
- **Mae** (proposed): Eli's older sister and Rose's mother. Mae dies on April
  twenty-second after living through thirty-one different rescue attempts. Her
  continuing post-death self consents to a three-minute conversation with Eli,
  remembers every death as her own experience, refuses to make his remorse
  tidy through immediate absolution, and ends the contact herself.
- **Rose** (proposed): Mae's daughter and Eli's niece. During the main events,
  Eli wants to give her an impractically large telescope; she is an adult when
  he dies many years later. Unwarned about the watch, she finds it while
  clearing his flat and supplies the living activation that overwrites nine
  days.
- **The Goddess of Time** (proposed claimed title): A living person-like
  chronal visitor who gives Eli the title `the Goddess of Time`; Eli also calls
  her `Time`. She audits the watch, explains consequences and contractual
  limits, and actively sustains Mae's consented tea-window contact. The story
  does not establish her personal name, origin, worship, objective godhood, or
  authority beyond this watch and its holders.
- **Aunt Linda** (proposed local family fact): Eli's absent aunt and owner of a
  painted-flamingo novelty mug. No other biography or recurring role is
  established.
- **Mara** (proposed local character fact): Eli's one-off date, introduced as
  `Mara from Hinge`. Eli subjects their twelve-minute encounter to four
  rewinds, producing five versions; Mara does not appear in the central
  conflict and has no relationship to any other registered identity.
- Fenwick family relationship and inheritance (proposed): Eli and Mae
  share an unnamed grandfather who formerly held the watch. The watch passes
  from him to Eli among ordinary personal effects and later remains in Eli's
  flat for Rose to find. The grandfather's account of how he acquired it is
  explicitly unreliable beyond the witnessed transfer described below.

All character and relationship facts above are proposals local to this story.

## New rules, capabilities, or costs

- **Fenwick bloodline watch (local artifact proposal):** A scratched,
  nonliving copper watch can overwrite a bounded interval of material history
  when a living bound holder deliberately depresses its stem. It records its
  activations and responds with moving hands and copper warmth, but it never
  speaks, chooses, refuses, requests use, or selects a destination.
- **Observed activation range (watch-specific proposal):** A tap can recover
  seconds, a firmer hold can recover minutes, and sustained panic can produce a
  much longer, poorly controlled overwrite. Eli's observed uses range from
  seven and nine seconds through repeated twenty-two-second intervals. Rose's
  sustained activation reaches exactly nine days. These observations do not
  establish a universal scale or make emotion itself magical fuel; living
  intentional pressure is required.
- **Overwrite and memory (watch-specific proposal):** The watch leaves one
  retained material sequence rather than publicly persistent branches. The
  initiating holder consciously remembers overwritten intervals. In Mae's
  case, her same continuing post-death self remembers all thirty-one lived
  deaths even though she did not consciously retain earlier attempts while
  re-embodied. Mae cannot determine how much of that memory follows from the
  watch and how much from her post-death condition, so the event does not
  define universal memory or afterlife mechanics.
- **Witnessed ownership contract (local proposal):** According to the visitor,
  Eli's grandfather won the watch in a card game, signed a grease-stained
  napkin, and completed a witnessed transfer. Whatever made that agreement
  effective binds this object to his living bloodline and bars the
  title-bearer from seizing it, requesting its transfer, or coercing its
  ownership or use. The contract does not compel an activation, choose an
  heir's conduct, or establish universal bloodline, name, contract, or divine
  law.
- **Watch audit (local capability proposal):** Through her own living action,
  the title-bearer can move the watch's hands through its recorded activations
  without touching it and can identify their count and duration. The story
  establishes no broader record system or universal temporal ledger.
- **Consented tea window (event-specific proposal):** Through sustained living
  effort, the title-bearer can mediate a three-minute, two-way conversation
  with Mae's consenting continuing self. The contact is explicitly not a copy,
  recording, or forced summons. Mae chooses to appear and to end it. The tea,
  mug, and steam frame the contact but are not shown to act or supply magic
  independently.
- **Awareness of a later activation (narrow proposed observation):** When Rose
  activates the watch years later, the title-bearer notices from elsewhere.
  The means, distance, timing, and scope of that awareness remain unknown and
  must not be generalized into omniscience or universal surveillance.

## Timeline events

- **Undated family transfer:** On a Tuesday in the unnamed modern-like era,
  Eli inherits his grandfather's watch in a shoebox of ordinary effects.
- **Fifteen preliminary activations:** Before April twenty-second, Eli makes
  fifteen small corrections. These include four activations that create five
  versions of one twelve-minute date and one cinema activation of exactly nine
  seconds.
- **April twenty-second:** Eli activates once before a van can strike Mae, then
  activates again after each of thirty failed rescue attempts. His thirty-first
  activation begins the final attempt, which ends in Mae's thirty-first lived
  death and the one death retained by public material history.
- **Seven Tuesdays later:** At eleven by Eli's phone and ten fifty-five by his
  kitchen clock, the title-bearer begins her audit. She leaves at eleven
  fifty-three; an old reminder about Rose's birthday sounds at eleven
  fifty-six. After midnight, Eli releases the watch stem without pressing it,
  boxes the watch, and begins years in which he makes no further activation.
- **Many years later:** Eli dies quickly from a heart attack on a Sunday. On
  the second Tuesday without him, nine days after that death, adult Rose finds
  the watch and overwrites the interval to Sunday morning before the fatal
  event. The story ends before establishing what Rose changes or whether Eli's
  death is prevented in the retained sequence.
