# Outline

## Story

- Premise and central promise: For years Ressa has entered the wild valley alone
  and returned with two reed baskets of flowers that genuinely heal common
  sickness, renew spent strength, sharpen existing memory, or bring peaceful
  sleep. On the day a child's fever makes her return urgent, cold rain fills
  the valley with fog and opens a broad band of touch-killing widow's lace
  across her usual homeward route. Ressa knows exactly what the lethal flowers
  are and never touches them. Resolve the promise by making her practiced
  knowledge, the useful flowers already in her baskets, and her willingness to
  count herself among the people medicine is for sufficient to bring both
  baskets home and break the child's fever.
- Intended reader experience: Begin in confidence and sensuous abundance: an
  expert notices sap color, petal posture, wind, and safe foot room where a
  stranger would see only impossible beauty. Let admiration tighten into
  bodily suspense when the familiar return route becomes unusable without ever
  making Ressa foolish or revealing a bloom she somehow failed to know. The
  release should feel practical, intimate, and costly rather than triumphant:
  the child is helped, the valley remains wild, and expertise includes using a
  finite remedy at the right time rather than preserving every bloom for an
  imagined worthier patient.
- Generating force:
  - Mode: Plot-led there-and-back traversal. The outward descent establishes a
    safe route and fills four separated basket compartments; the homeward climb
    reverses the same landmarks after weather, cargo, and opened lethal blooms
    have changed what each step demands.
  - Focal attachment or contradiction: Ressa's love for the village has taken
    the form of being the reliable carrier between its needs and the valley's
    gifts. She treats her own body as transport rather than as one of the lives
    those gifts may properly preserve.
  - Plot-led wants and useful limit, if applicable: She immediately wants the
    strongest healing flowers back before the fevered child worsens, while
    also fulfilling the less urgent strength, memory, and sleep requests tied
    to her basket handles. She wants to keep the harvest modest enough for the
    patches to recover and to return alive for future trips. Her useful limit
    is disciplined self-denial: refusing to consume village medicine keeps her
    harvest accountable and has earned deep trust, but under changed conditions
    it makes her first contemplate waiting too long or attempting the return
    while dangerously depleted.
- Counterforce or complication:
  - Source: A fast cold rain hides the high stones under fog and causes the
    valley's known touch-killing widow's lace to open hours before its usual
    dusk spread. The low route is impassable without contact; waiting for the
    flowers to close at dawn may cost the sick child the useful window for the
    healing bloom; the longer upper return demands strength and exact recall
    Ressa no longer has after a full harvest day.
  - Credible inside logic, if a person: No person supplies the counterforce.
    The fever, weather, finite daylight, topography, flower cycles, and Ressa's
    own rule about who may use the harvest create incompatible goods without an
    antagonist.
  - Pressure or change created: Knowledge rules out the seductive direct
    crossing rather than making it safe. Ressa must either accept delay, spend
    part of the finite harvest on herself, or gamble the carrier and every
    remedy at once.
- Narrative design:
  - POV, tense, distance, and limitation: Close third person present through
    Ressa, intimate with route judgment, fatigue, remembered patients, and the
    physical grammar of flowers. Do not enter the plants or waiting villagers
    as viewpoints; neither the narration nor the valley claims intention.
  - Time shape and compression: One day from predawn request knots to a late
    evening bedside, hinged at the rain. Give the boundary check, lethal-bloom
    avoidance, four harvests, recognition of the blocked return, decision to
    self-dose, fog crossing, and use of the healing flower full scene space.
    Compress familiar walking and prior years of successful use into brief
    sensory associations prompted by particular patches, not dramatized
    flashbacks.
  - Information and reveal strategy: Establish widow's lace and its fatal
    touch during the outward journey, including Ressa's exact avoidance, so
    the later danger is understood rather than sprung as a new rule. Let each
    useful effect become concrete through why Ressa cuts, wraps, and separates
    that flower. Reveal gradually that she has administered these remedies to
    herself only as a last resort, then make her choice an application of
    known limits: the memory flower retrieves detail she truly observed, and
    the strength flower lends stamina without repairing strain. Supply no
    secret intelligence, chosen-one bond, universal flower taxonomy, lost
    civilization, or proof that the valley rewards virtue.
  - Governing movement: Tie requests; inspect hands and baskets; enter; identify
    beauty by consequence; skirt death; harvest healing; restore the patches;
    add strength, memory, and sleep; notice cold rain; turn home; stop before
    the open lethal band; test and reject routes; spend two remedies; retrace
    the hidden upper stones; outlast borrowed strength; return; prepare; let
    the fever break; defer what can wait.
- Speculative surplus: Every freshly cut valley flower bends a nearby flame
  toward itself and turns it blue, regardless of the flower's medicinal or
  lethal effect. Ressa keeps tinder in a lidded horn, uses a hooded lamp above
  the fog, and insists that village preparation happens over covered coals.
  This inconvenient fire behavior does not diagnose illness, indicate danger,
  choose a patient, or explain any flower's main power.
- Structural distinction: A solitary, asymmetrical palindrome of terrain. The
  outward half reads the valley by species and fills the baskets; the return
  half reads the same ground by remembered stone, altered weather, and a body
  carrying the harvest. The decisive venue is a fogbound upper traverse with
  no companion, audience, technical proof, crowd conversion, institutional
  rewrite, or conquered wilderness.
- Decisive turn, deepening, or recognition: At the widow's-lace band, Ressa
  lays out the choices she would give any patient and recognizes that her old
  rule has become less careful than the flowers themselves. She eats one
  strength bloom and one memory bloom, records the missing doses on their
  request cords, and repacks both baskets. Renewed stamina lets her take the
  upper traverse; sharpened recall supplies the sequence of pale stones,
  root-hooks, and clear handholds she deliberately noticed on descent. She
  still probes every footing and backs away from every petal: the remedies
  make her knowledge usable under pressure, not infallible.
- Aftereffect or live uncertainty: The borrowed strength ebbs before Ressa
  reaches the village, leaving the strain it temporarily overrode, while the
  sharpened memory keeps old failures and the day's exact dangers painfully
  available until morning. The healing bloom genuinely breaks the child's
  fever, and a measured sleep bloom lets the exhausted parent rest beside the
  cooling bed. Two requests now have smaller reserves, the storm may have
  changed tomorrow's route, and the village still depends heavily on one
  gatherer; none of those larger problems is solved or converted into a public
  program tonight.
- Opening / ending relation: Open at the valley boundary with four request
  cords, two empty baskets, and Ressa's private rule that no flower she carries
  is for her. End beside the recovering child with the baskets not yet fully
  sorted: when someone asks whether she can prepare the remaining memory and
  strength flowers tonight, Ressa says morning and sits before her borrowed
  strength can drop her. The opening measures care by taking every need into
  her body; the ending preserves care by allowing one nonurgent need to wait.

## Beats

1. Before dawn, Ressa accepts four knotted request cords at the edge of the
   unnamed village: healing is urgent for a fevered child; the strength,
   memory, and sleep requests can wait but belong to people she knows. She
   checks her reed baskets, skin covers, cutting tools, lidded tinder horn, and
   the rule that has governed years of solitary trips: never taste what she is
   carrying for someone else.
2. She enters the unnamed wild valley on her memorized high path. Beauty is
   inseparable from judgment. She steps wide of open widow's lace, demonstrates
   that even brushing its petals is fatal, and notes the pale stones and
   root-hooks above the patch as ordinary route knowledge rather than ominous
   setup.
3. At the healing patch, she cuts only mature red mercy, leaves enough open
   flowers for the patch to set seed, and wraps the stems separately. A brief
   association with earlier fevers establishes that the medicine truly works
   on common sickness but cannot reverse every injury or death.
4. She gathers a small, bounded share of lion's breath for returning strength,
   silver ledger for sharper recall, and moon hush for peaceful sleep. Each
   harvest evokes one concrete prior village use and one limit. When she lights
   a shielded coal to seal a stem wrap, all four kinds pull the flame blueward;
   she caps it before the baskets can scorch.
5. Cold rain arrives faster than the morning signs predicted. Ressa finishes
   protecting the urgently needed healing flowers, gives up an optional lower
   patch, and starts back promptly, but fog has erased long sightlines and the
   rain-opened widow's lace now spans the usual low route.
6. At a dry stone above the lethal band, she inventories without panic. The
   direct route requires contact and is rejected. Waiting until dawn protects
   her but risks the child. The upper traverse is flower-clear yet too long
   and exacting for her present fatigue. No improvised cutting, burning, or
   supposed antidote can make widow's lace harmless.
7. Ressa first divides the harvest into what she imagines sacrificing, then
   sees that this still treats her survival as less legitimate than every
   request cord. She takes one lion's-breath flower and one silver-ledger
   flower, marks both uses honestly on the cords, and secures the remaining
   remedies across the two baskets.
8. On the upper traverse, strength returns without numbing pain, and memory
   sharpens details actually seen that morning. Ressa reconstructs the safe
   sequence through arm's-length fog, probing each pale stone before weighting
   it and retreating whenever widow's lace appears at the edge. Sharpened
   recall also brings back patients she could not help; she uses the memories
   as route facts without letting them become prophecy or punishment.
9. The strength effect begins to fall away on the final climb. Ressa extinguishes
   her hooded lamp when the basket flowers wrench its flame sideways, finishes
   by touch on known rock, and reaches the village with both baskets and no
   contact with a lethal bloom.
10. In a quiet bedside scene, Ressa prepares red mercy and the child's fever
    breaks; she measures moon hush for the exhausted parent only after someone
    else agrees to watch the child. With her own legs shaking and the remaining
    requests nonurgent, she leaves the strength and memory compartments closed
    until morning and sits down.

## Continuity

- Canon used: The world permits many locally bounded systems commonly described
  as magic, and only life can use magic; the flowers themselves remain living
  sources of their effects. The story is an independent entry point whose
  culture need not know the wider world's eras or systems.
- Boundaries and unknowns: The unnamed valley, village, era, and wider geography
  are new and left unresolved, with no proposed connection to any established
  valley, orchard, settlement, person, faction, or magical tradition. All five
  flower capabilities are local biological magic, not universal plant law.
  Healing is real but bounded to appropriate sickness or injury; restored
  strength temporarily supplies stamina without repairing damage or erasing
  underlying exhaustion; sharpened memory improves access to things actually
  perceived and does not create knowledge, restore erased autobiography, prove
  truth, or alter identity; peaceful sleep does not itself heal disease; and
  widow's lace kills through bodily touch but has no mind, moral judgment, or
  special relation to Ressa. Exact durations, origin of the valley ecology,
  civil date, and Galactic Cycle placement remain unresolved.
