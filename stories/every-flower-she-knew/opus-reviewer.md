# Opus review: Every Flower She Knew

Independent audit for a later editing pass. It is not a `review.md` verdict,
does not change canon state, and does not authorize edits by itself.

## Verdict

- Dialogue: REVISE — the forewoman's dawn deadline can't be followed and is then ignored; every speaker uses the same clipped inventory register; Safra recites flower limits to people who already know them.
- Prose: REVISE — the "real but bounded help" point is re-explained in narration after the handovers and again in the closing sentence.
- Continuity and prompt: REVISE — the prompt promise is delivered, but the crew's need runs on flood-channel work (water-infrastructure policy) and two weather details don't line up.
- Edit scope: heavy-dialogue
- Existing review.md said: Verdict PASS, "Dialogue: PASS" ("practical exchanges preserve distinct expertise and pressure without becoming a lecture"); disagree. The speakers differ only by topic, and the return handovers are lectures.

This is a quiet competence story, and its best dialogue is short and physical: "Start at the water.", the healer's silent wait, "I heard.", "I had it," and "Only me,". The problem is that the healer, the forewoman, the millwright and Safra all talk in the same short declaratives and verbless fragments ("Hot skin, dry mouth." / "Eight on the crew." / "Winter repair."). The return scene then turns into echoes ("One now. We keep one." / "One now," Safra repeated; "Two hours" / "Four bodies. Two hours."), with Safra warning the two people who use these flowers every season about their limits. With the tags covered, only the subject tells you who is speaking. The one exchange that sets the plot's clock (the forewoman's timeline at dawn) is so broken up that the reader can't tell when the village is in danger. Its one clear figure, "By afternoon", is then contradicted by a night delivery that nobody mentions. The edit pass should leave Safra clipped, because that is her discipline and her tiredness. It should make the healer the slower, listening voice that the narration says she is, and let the forewoman's counting carry a day of pressure. It should also cut every lore line that tells an expert what she already knows.

## Edit guardrails

- Prompt (acceptance authority; the last `Craft profile:` line is prospective-2026-08-21): Safra goes into the valley alone, knows every dangerous bloom and avoids them without contact, and returns with baskets that help the village, as she has for years ("eleven flowering seasons"). The story must be complete and resolve that promise at 2,500–4,000 words (now about 3,200). Keep it broadly accessible and non-graphic, in close third person past through Safra.
- The rewrite request asked for dialogue revision under the newer pipeline. Keep the plot, the structure and the four needs: the fever, the crew's strength, the millwright's memory and the watcher's sleep.
- Keep the story-local flower rules and counts consistent:
  - Ambercup: one petal per patient per day, and a second brings shaking. Two taken: one used, one reserve.
  - Redspur: a half-stamen gives two hours of strength, repairs nothing, leaves fatigue after, keeps three dry days, and its heat spoils duskbell. Three stamens taken: two split into four halves, one reserve.
  - Clearblue: one filament gives about forty minutes of sharper recall, adds no facts, and dulls at midnight. One used.
  - Duskbell: about six hours of easily broken sleep. Six dried, one used, five left.
  - Glass-nettle: kills by touch, and its sap travels in wet.
  - Crew: eight at dawn, seven at night; four dosed, two unaided, one with a bad shoulder.
- Universe: the flowers, people and places are all story-local, and no LOCKED or CANON entry is touched. Don't link this valley to Flint valley or any other canon valley. Roles stay unnamed (healer, forewoman, millwright, watcher); don't add names.
- Keep Safra's clipped checklist speech and her self-checks in the field. They are her voice, not the house voice. Keep the non-verbal beats: the healer's wait, the forewoman's raised hand, the candle.
- Ordinary water (stream, ford, rain) stays. Don't add or expand channels, cuts or similar works (see C1).

## Dialogue findings

### D1 · Blocking · Forewoman's deadline unreadable, then ignored

- Where: L25–L29, dawn in the herb room, Safra asks how long before the water reaches the village marker
- Text:
> "How long to the village marker?" Safra asked.
> "If the ridge rain reaches us? By afternoon. If the high rain does too—"
> "Three hours to the bend. One more to the gate. Two hours' work. Four people."
- Problem: Ground/referent and uptake.
  - The reader can't tell what "the village marker", "the bend" and "the gate" are, or when the "three hours" start. The story's only outside clock is lost at the moment it is set.
  - The one clear fact is "By afternoon": the water reaches the village marker by afternoon if the ridge rain comes. Safra asked for this deadline, but she responds only to the crew count and leaves on a harvest that can't be back before dark.
  - The ridge rain does come early (L77), and the high rain comes too (L107). Nobody at the return mentions the marker. Either the answer means something the reader can't recover, or Safra's reply doesn't follow it.
  - "Her quick figuring broke into pieces" shows the breakup is meant. But broken-up figuring still has to leave one number the reader can use.
- Fix:
  - Give the forewoman one clear deadline that a night return can only just meet (water at the marker around midnight, earlier if the high rain comes), and keep her counting in pieces around it.
  - Have Safra's reply take the deadline up: when she'll be back, or which route that forces.
  - If C1 changes the crew's task, keep this same shape for the new deadline.
  - Illustrative only: "If the ridge rain comes down, it's at the marker by midnight. Earlier if the high country gets it too, and I can't tell you how much earlier."

### D2 · Major · Safra lectures experts on flower limits

- Where: L33 (dawn, packing), L153 (return, ambercup), L159 (return, redspur)
- Text:
> "Redspur stays here. Nothing warm beside the duskbell. Nothing wet touches the weave."
> "No second today. A second brings shaking. It won't mend an injury or touch poison."
> "Two hours," Safra said. "Then the strength goes. No repair. They'll be tired after."
- Problem: Exposition ("as you know") and Philosopher. Every listener already knows these rules.
  - At L33 nobody asked about packing.
  - At L153 the healer has just said "We keep one", and this fever involves no injury or poison.
  - At L159 the forewoman said "two hours" herself at dawn and has just held back the worker with the bad shoulder, which shows she knows redspur mends nothing.
  - These lines brief the reader, not the listener. Together with the narration (P1), the story states its "bounded help" point at least seven times (L63, L69, L153, L159, L165, L175, L177). The outline's own voice notes forbid this "herbalist-lore cadence".
- Fix:
  - Cut L33's second quote; the valley scenes already show the packing.
  - At L153 keep `"One now," Safra repeated. "No second today."` as tired double-checking, so "I heard." still has something to answer. Cut the rest.
  - Cut L159, or replace it with something the forewoman doesn't already know, such as which stamen is the reserve or why Safra is late.
  - Keep the clearblue warning at L165. That one is earned: the millwright could misuse the flower, and she pushes back.

### D3 · Major · Four speakers, one clipped register

- Where: throughout; clearest at L15, L151, L155, L161, L167
- Text:
> "Hot skin, dry mouth. He knows me. His chest is clear."
> "The fever is higher. Still knows me. Chest still clear. He's had no ambercup today."
> The healer's voice had become slower as Safra's had become clipped.
> "Four bodies. Two hours. We clear the choke, then they come off."
> "Winter repair. I stood inside the wheel. I need the order."
- Problem: Identical Voices (house-voice convergence).
  - With the tags covered, the healer, the forewoman, the millwright and Safra can be told apart only by subject. All of them speak in short declaratives and verbless fragments.
  - The return scene becomes echoes: "One now. We keep one." / "One now," Safra repeated; "Two hours" / "Four bodies. Two hours."
  - L155 says the healer has slowed while Safra has gone clipped, but the healer's lines on either side are as clipped as Safra's. The narration claims a contrast the dialogue never shows.
  - Apart from the pushed candle, nobody in the village treats Safra as a person, not just a supplier. Only silence and gesture move the relationships.
- Fix:
  - Leave Safra's fragments alone.
  - Rewrite the healer's lines at L15, L19, L35–L39 and L151 as full, unhurried sentences. Give her at least one question or one moment of attention to a person (the old man, the watcher, or how Safra looks). Then L155 is true, or it can be cut.
  - Let the millwright sound like someone annoyed at her own memory, not someone filing a report.
  - Break the echoes: nobody needs to repeat another speaker's numbers back.
  - Illustrative only (L151): "He's hotter than he was this morning. He still knows me, and his chest is still clear, so we'll give him one now and keep the other."

### D4 · Major · Forewoman's return carries none of the day

- Where: L157–L161, the return, redspur handover
- Text:
> "Four halves. Good. Crew is down to seven.
> "Four. Four at the cut. Two changing loads. One watching the bank."
- Problem: Inert exchange at a key payoff.
  - The forewoman has waited since dawn while the water rose. The harvest arrives hours late by the ridge, and Safra said so at L149 in her hearing. One worker has dropped off the crew.
  - Her only reaction is "Good.", followed by arithmetic and an echo of Safra. Nothing changes between the two women: no strain, no relief, no complaint.
  - As a result, the crew's need resolves as a transaction, and the ridge detour costs nothing on the page. "Crew is down to seven" is the only line where the day shows through; build the exchange on it.
- Fix:
  - Once D1 sets a real deadline, let her first words show how close it came (the hour, the water, the lost eighth worker) and react to the late return, bluntly or pettily. Then she does her division.
  - Keep the hand gesture over the clay strip and "One has the bad shoulder and gets none."
  - Illustrative only: "The ridge. Of course the ridge. I've had the two good ones at it since noon, and they're finished."

### D5 · Minor · Morning briefing references the reader can't fill in

- Where: L17 (dose instruction), L19 (the watcher)
- Text:
> "One. If anything changes, you wait."
> The watcher slept perhaps an hour yesterday and less the night before.
- Problem: Ground/referent.
  - At dawn there is no ambercup in the village, so "you wait" has no clear meaning. Withhold a dose? Wait for Safra? Try nothing else? The healer just nods. At L151 it is the healer who decides the dose, so it is also unclear who has the authority.
  - "The watcher" is used as if the reader already knows who she is. Only at L171–L175 can the reader work out that she has been sitting up with the old man.
- Fix: Make Safra's condition concrete, or cut it and keep "One petal for tonight." At first mention, give the watcher one anchoring phrase: whom she sits with, or how she is related to the old man.

### D6 · Minor · Ford exchange plants the route and miscounts the stone

- Where: L35–L41, dawn, the baskets and the ford
- Text:
> "You can carry both back by the ford?"
> "If the ford stays at two stones."
- Problem: The valley has one ford stone (L65, L121, L123, and "Ford was over the stone" at L149). "Two stones" reads either as a depth measure that is never explained or as a slip. The four evenly alternating lines exist mainly to set up the later ford-or-ridge choice, and they end on a two-word clincher ("The ridge."). The healer's worry is the only feeling in the scene, and it goes nowhere.
- Fix: Make the marker match the single stone ("while the ford stone shows"). Let the healer's question come from what she has just heard about the rain, and let her say something beyond the either/or. It can stay short.

### D7 · Minor · Narration explains the dialogue

- Where: L15, L27, L149, L161
- Text:
> as though the words belonged first to the body they described
> Her quick figuring broke into pieces.
> Safra heard what she had omitted.
> not for an audience
- Problem: Each of these explains a line's manner or meaning right after it is spoken. At L149 the healer's silence ("The healer waited.") and Safra's added route already show what she left out; saying so takes the inference away from the reader.
- Fix: Cut these. Where something is needed, use an action the reader can see instead. The L155 gloss is covered in D3.

## Prose findings

### P1 · Major · "Bounded help" re-explained in narration and the closing line

- Where: L63 (clearblue), L175 (watcher), L177 (crew returns), L185 (final sentence)
- Text:
> The filaments would dull when midnight came whether anyone had used them or not. Their value was not something she could carry into tomorrow.
> It would not settle the reason she had kept watch so long.
> None mistook borrowed strength for an unwounded body.
> the separated packets stayed dry, the compromised bed had a date, and every occupied cot had someone awake beside it
- Problem: Philosopher in narration.
  - L63 repeats L61 ("would dull at midnight whether cut or rooted") and adds commentary.
  - L175 and L177 restate limits the scenes have already shown: the six hours of sleep, the bad shoulder that got none, the crew coming back bent.
  - The final sentence sums up the resolution as a three-part emblem.
  - Together with D2, this tells the reader the same point after nearly every beat.
- Fix:
  - Cut the last two sentences of L63.
  - Cut the last sentence of L177.
  - At L175 keep the six-hours sentence and cut "It would not settle..." or the sentence after it; one is enough.
  - End on a concrete image (the rain on the roof, the boxed date, the candle) instead of the three-item summary. Keep "Outside, rain began again on the roof."

### P2 · Minor · One-line beats and triads as the default rhythm

- Where: the valley section, L55–L129; triads at L71, L123, L185
- Text:
> She left the optional redspur rooted.
> By then the ford stone had vanished.
> Not with two baskets, not with wax dividers that must stay upright, not with glass-nettle runoff somewhere in the flood.
- Problem: Eleven one-sentence paragraphs set the valley's rhythm (L55, L59, L73, L75, L93, L105, L111, L115, L121, L125, L129). Several use the same template, a flower left uncut (L59, L73, L111). Three-part rhetorical lists recur (L71, L123, L185). The uncut-flower beats are the backbone of the story; the connecting one-liners are not.
- Fix: Keep L59, L73 and L111 as they are. Merge L115, L121 and L125 into the paragraphs next to them. Vary one of the triads; L123 is the most rhetorical. Leave the checklists at L87, L103 and L133–L137 alone: they are Safra's procedure, not a tic.

### P3 · Minor · Small staging and wording slips

- Where: L13 (fever cot), L57 (frost on the clearblue)
- Text:
> the healer lifted the cloth over the fever cot. The patient beneath it was an old man
> would lose its edge before forty minutes
- Problem: A cloth over the cot with the patient "beneath it" reads like a sheet over a body, a shroud, until the next sentence says he is breathing. "Before forty minutes" is not idiomatic.
- Fix: Make it a cloth on his forehead or a curtain around the cot. Change the phrase to "within forty minutes".

## Continuity and prompt findings

### C1 · Major · The crew's need is flood-channel work

- Where: L23 (forewoman at dawn), L109, L127, L157–L161, L177
- Text:
> The channel forewoman came in trailing the smell of wet clay.
> North cut is holding. For now.
> The channel water would keep rising.
> the forewoman raised one hand through the herb-room door: the choke was clear
- Problem: Policy: "No default water infrastructure" in `universe/style-guide.md` (CANON). One of the four needs, a recurring role and the plot's clock all run on flood-control work (channel, cut, choke, gauge pegs), and the prompt doesn't ask for it. Edits made from these notes count as authorized revisions, so the policy applies. The ford, the stream and the rain are ordinary water and are fine.
- Fix:
  - Replace the crew's task with a heavy-labor emergency on the same rain clock that involves no water works and is not routine public works or maintenance. Examples: getting a cut harvest or the hay in before the high rain reaches the village, or bringing a flock down before dark. The closing "rain began again on the roof" would then land as the storm they beat.
  - Keep what the story needs from the task: a crew of about eight with two still strong; four half-stamens for two hours; one worker with a bad shoulder who gets none; a deadline that a night return can only just meet; and the forewoman's counting voice.
  - Do this together with D1 and D4, so the new deadline is written once, and give "channel forewoman" a matching new role name.

### C2 · Minor · Two weather details don't line up

- Where: L77 (meadow), L143 (return)
- Text:
> A gray seam had opened above the ridge hours earlier than the forewoman's estimate
> She looked at the rain shining on Safra's coat
- Problem:
  - At dawn the forewoman never says when the rain will start. She only says, unclearly, what happens if it comes (L27), so "the forewoman's estimate" refers to nothing.
  - At the return the healer sees fresh rain on Safra's coat. But the only rain on Safra was the morning shower, which ended before she reached the upper path (L79), and the next rain comes at L185.
- Fix: When rewriting D1, have the forewoman say when she expects the ridge rain, or change L77 to refer to what she did say. Either add drizzle on the ridge (around L131), or change L143 to something else the healer can see, such as wet boots or mud to the knee.
