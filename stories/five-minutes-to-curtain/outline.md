# Five Minutes to Curtain

Provisional direction: a close-third backstage farce, following Werrit, the auburn-haired comic actor in the beloved Lantern Company. He adores playing the hind legs of their dreadful cloth dragon. His human appearance is a shape he deliberately holds; his colleagues have never known he is a dragon. He has spent years sending his parents doctored playbills that make him the heroic lead.

Five minutes before tonight's curtain, his mother puts an enormous gold eye to the dressing-room window. His father is settling into the theater's monster box. They have finally come to see their son's triumph. Werrit calls her Mother, scorches his forged playbill with a panicked breath, and has to confess both his species and his letters. The whole cast unravels: their trusted fool is a dragon, they are about to make formidable parents watch their son get kicked, and he has promised those parents the hero's part. Short armored Tib refuses to surrender his spectacular fall; taller Varlo offers Werrit the villain's seven-minute death instead; Bessa, already crowned and laced into violet, will not have her queen's entrance cut. Nalla, the braided actor who also calls their cues, tries to fit these incompatible rescues into a play they can still perform. Werrit helps rearrange it, greedily enjoying a chance to be magnificent even while the changes strip away his favorite work.

The curtain rises on their hastily altered opening. Protecting every actor's cherished moment makes the heroics physically impossible. Werrit gets his heroic entrance and discovers he cannot play it with this company without making someone else look foolish. When a familiar comic opening appears, he chooses to take the ridiculous fall himself, then actively brings his partners back into the routine they know. The actual performance should earn the reversal through mishap, timing, and shared physical skill, rather than a speech. His parents' response remains a live question for drafting: laughter need not settle the years of lies, and one of them may still want a better explanation after the applause. A possible last beat lets Werrit ask his family to watch his beloved comic bit again, in his own name, while a castmate demands that he finish dressing the dragon first.
