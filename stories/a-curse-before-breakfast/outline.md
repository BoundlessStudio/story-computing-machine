# Outline

## Story

- Premise and central promise: **A Curse Before Breakfast.** Fifteen-year-old Philip begs his witch sister Coris for an illness to escape tomorrow's enormous mathematics test. He gets a genuine sick day, then discovers that reclaiming it for revision means persuading her to become the patient.
- Focal pressure or attachment: Philip wants another day without surrendering any comforts. He trusts Coris's magic more than his own capacity to work all night. Both enjoy obtaining favors while denying the other has won.
- Counterforce or complication: Illness makes concentrating difficult. Coris covets Philip's better seat at an upcoming travelling ghost show, but taking his curse would cost her a healthy afternoon.
- POV, distance, and information limit: First-person past, close to Philip's fifteen-year-old perceptions. His excuses may be extravagant; the depicted symptoms and sequence stay reliable. Coris's motives emerge through conduct and speech.
- Governing movement and time shape: Late-night pleading, dawn success, a prolonged domestic reversal, then a brief following-morning departure. Target 2,500–4,000 words; allow slow, uncomfortable intervals within the comedy.
- Speculative rule or ordinary-world constraint: Coris's local one-day ague causes real fever, sneezing, and green facial spots. It is noncontagious and expires at sunset. She cannot adjust its symptoms after casting; her only available early release draws the remaining illness into herself. Establish this before consent. The local school accepts her absence call and assigns a makeup test the following morning.
- Dialogue promise: Familiarity makes begging inventive, care impertinent, and suffering competitive.
- Dialogic medium: Bedroom-door pleading, bedside talk interrupted by symptoms, an ordinary telephone absence message, and increasingly theatrical patient gestures.
- Dialogue engine: Philip's requests recruit Coris, establish what he is accepting, and later expose something she wants enough to change places. Their demands alter who can study, rest, or leave the room.

## Voice

- Narrative texture: Philip notices comforts, possession, elapsed time, and offenses against him. Elaborate self-justification gives way to blunt bodily observations when the fever defeats it; mathematical comparisons belong to his looming test.
- Conversational texture: Unfinished requests, familiar teasing, genuine questions, sleepy refusals, and ordinary fetching talk. Give jokes room to fail when the listener is miserable.
- Rhetorical ownership: Philip enlarges his emergency and minimizes his concessions. Coris enjoys the frightening vocabulary of witchcraft and uses sibling knowledge to puncture his performance. Neither owns every joke; Coris can misjudge what sounds reassuring.
- Pressure behavior: Philip's persuasive fluency collapses into repeated concrete wants. Coris begins briskly dismissive; under the transferred fever she becomes distracted, extravagant, and just as demanding.
- Relationship movement: Philip wants rescue without paying for it and cannot comfortably admit wanting to be fussed over. Coris wants his ticket and his attention without acknowledging either appetite's childishness. The transfer turns comfortable authority into shared culpability and gives Philip room to refuse her later demands.
- Anti-default: Do not make Coris a secretly omniscient tutor whose curse teaches responsibility. Both knowingly prefer a successful swindle to a useful lecture.

## Beats

1. Late at night, Philip finds the examination notice inside a book he has been reading for pleasure. The whole-term test is tomorrow. He wakes Coris and begs for a sudden illness, initially trying to specify a pleasant one. She wants his better ghost-show seat; he guards it. She eventually agrees to the ague without obtaining it.
2. Before breakfast, with their parent already at work, Coris casts through her own living touch and breath. The first green spots delight Philip; the chills are less welcome. She reports his actual condition to school. The makeup arrangement gives him precisely the extra day he wanted.
3. His confident revision schedule disintegrates into lost places, sneezing, and demands for comforts. Coris's presence alternates between help and infuriating entertainment. When he presses for immediate recovery, the previously established transfer matters: she would have to endure the remaining hours. His sales pitch fails because she has watched him.
4. By late morning Philip offers his better ghost-show ticket and nursing for the afternoon. Coris accepts and takes the curse back. Show the spots and symptoms moving through her deliberate working. Now she asks for the comforts he demanded, then invents more. His complaint about her exaggeration becomes difficult to maintain when her real fever interrupts it.
5. Philip studies in workable stretches between interruptions. Having attended the lessons, he needs ordinary revision; Coris supplies neither answers nor magical shortcuts. Some problems remain hard. Let fatigue quiet her without converting her into an exemplary patient. At sunset the imposed symptoms end. The ticket changes hands; both have received their bargain and disliked part of it.
6. The following morning Coris tries to extend her nursing privileges with conspicuously painted green spots. Philip recognizes the imitation and declines further service while keeping their actual scheme private. He leaves for the makeup examination with useful preparation. The final exchange concerns her failed attempt to retain patient status, with affection and opportunism still entangled.
