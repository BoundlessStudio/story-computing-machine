# The Sleeping Future

## Promised experience

The request follows a lone girl into womanhood on an aging station, repairing it, reading Earth records, and keeping its sleepers alive with a meticulous android. Its disclosure that the station was never meant to return confronts her with obedience or a different fate for the sleepers. “Rows of silent pods” and “a homecoming none of them may live to see” suggest intimate care for people who cannot answer and longing for a place she knows through records. The payoff I hear is a woman using those years to change her passengers' future. Their destination and her relationship to the android beyond companionship are open. The later direction offers the long years of *The Apes Above*, when humans live in orbit and intelligent dinosaurs inhabit Earth.

## Takes considered

- **The turning station.** Follow Ansa at two ages. When she makes the station flightworthy, the android reveals its outbound mission. She can use the drive for the intended departure or a living orbital port.
- **The first ward.** Start with Ansa addressing a sleeper, with her childhood emerging in the course of one night's work. The first thaw consumes the reserve that makes long preservation possible.
- **A shore with lights.** At the disclosure, Ansa seeks leave from Earth's current inhabitants to land a few sleepers. The scene of reciprocal contact would carry the story.

## Provisional direction

Choose the turning station. Ansa's work toward a homecoming puts the course in her hands. Westerly was built to carry its sleepers from Earth orbit to an outward-bound colony ship. The ship can sustain them through decades of sleep and honor the settlers' allotments when they arrive. Westerly's repaired drive has fuel for one rendezvous. Ansa can steer it to Ibis, where a ward can start waking the passengers within weeks. People who left Earth with them still live there. The long passage would close that chance. Going to Ibis would cost the sleepers a destination they chose. Ansa commits to the port and has to tell the first person she wakes what she did.

## Craft plan

- **Teller.** Try hungry, close third through Ansa at ten and adult Ansa in first-person past, amused and still angry at how she came by her childhood beliefs.
- **Ansa.** The only awake human since Marl rescued her from a failing pod. She takes the archive addresses for destinations and imagines leading the sleepers to their old streets. She tries out idioms from Earth books at the slightest provocation, uses jokes to keep Marl in the room, and talks her way into an objection she eventually voices plainly.
- **Marl.** Knows the flight plan and thinks Ansa will accept it when she sees what was provided for the passengers. He sets down tools in the order they'll be needed, gives exact names and times in complete sentences, and warms when remembering the girl he raised. **Ida** last saw the Earth as an evacuee. Her eye goes to the thing that's missing or out of place. She asks practical questions in quick succession and gives her own plans in the plain imperatives of someone used to being heard.
- **The conversation owed.** Ansa asks for the homecoming she has worked toward. Marl tells her the station's actual purpose, what he let her believe and why, and what the sleepers agreed to when they could still answer for themselves. In turn she has to tell him what her choice will ask them to give up.
- **Casting from the original.** The adult woman stands easily within reach of a control panel. Cropped pale hair, a slight knowing smile, fitted black with red seams, gold breast insignia. Ansa asks Marl for the short cut so her hair clears the service hatch and keeps it because she likes it. She takes possession of a room by looking as though she expects someone to make way. The same slantwise smile baits Marl at ten and holds him at the console at twenty-eight. The badge she begged to wear catches Ida's eye. A black undershirt beneath the short jacket makes sense around cold equipment.
- **Place.** Westerly's window deck runs from the pilot console to the dock collar between two banks of pods. The observation blister favors Earth alone, and the galley favors domestic life. The jammed pump hatch under the console lets the girl slip in but makes the woman fold herself to reach. A draft skims the floor, a vent fan buzzes, Marl brings the smell of bread, green pinlights reflect in the pod covers, and Earth's shadow crosses the console. Pod rounds run on station mornings. The adult burn begins on a winter night pass over the coast in Ansa's archive, lit by a dinosaur town. At the end the port fills the window.

## Scenes and ending

1. **Ten, station morning (about a quarter).** Ansa compares an Earth evacuee register to an atlas and finds Ida's change of address. Ida is in the nearest pod. Ansa wants to picture taking her back to the coast, and takes Marl's promise of a ferry at the rendezvous for a promise of landing. When a pump stops, Marl wants Ansa to work by ear and reach the bypass with her small hand. Bubbles move again in the sight tube by Ida's shoulder. In the bridge across the years, the first time Marl leaves the rounds to Ansa alone marks the change from instruction to shared responsibility.
2. **Twenty-eight, night pass (about half, long scene).** Ansa tests the drive and asks where the ferry will land. Marl recounts the contracts, the failure of her pod, and his choice to use her faith in a return. The ferry docks with the colony ship. The sleepers have no lander and both available destinations are in orbit. Give Ansa time with the coast passing beneath the window before she can ask about Ibis. The Earth register suggests whom to ask and the port confirms both staged care and that several former evacuees live there. Marl shows what Ida signed for: a promised piece of ground on the colony world for the pear trees she left behind on Earth. Ansa says what leaving that future means, together with what decades of sleep may cost the people whose bodies she knows. When the pump falters under the drive load, she steadies the bank so either course is still possible. She chooses Ibis and makes the burn while the colony ship waits.
3. **First waking, docked at Ibis (about a quarter).** Port crew bring supplies through the collar and Ansa brings Ida round. Marl wants to handle the first greeting by his checklist. Ansa wants to give Ida the account herself. The badge tells Ida they're still with Westerly. When Ansa says she missed the colony ship by choice, Ida asks who will take the plot that was hers and says what she intended to do with the land. She recognizes a former neighbor's name on the port's arrivals list and asks to speak to her herself. Ansa puts the call within reach and listens as Ida tells the woman who has arrived. The rows stretch away behind them. The hand that once corrected an address on Earth is warm enough to reach for the receiver.
