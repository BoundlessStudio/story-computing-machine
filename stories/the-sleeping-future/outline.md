# The Sleeping Future

## Promised experience

The request follows a lone girl into womanhood on an aging station. Repairs, old Earth records, and the bodies she keeps alive should fill the years she spends with a meticulous android. Its disclosure that the station was never meant to return must give her a choice between carrying out the mission and offering the sleepers a different fate. “Rows of silent pods” and “a homecoming none of them may live to see” suggest the intimacy of tending people who cannot answer and the ache of knowing Earth through records while it hangs outside the window. The payoff I hear is a woman using the capacities those years gave her to change the future of the people in her care. The request leaves the mission, relationship beyond companionship and care, and outcome open. The later direction offers the long years of *The Apes Above*. Humans live in orbit, and the desired Earth has a civilization of its own.

## Takes considered

- **The turning station.** Follow a station mechanic at two ages. When Ansa finally gets Westerly in shape to travel, the android tells her where it was designed to go. The central scene asks whether she will take the sleepers there or make for a living orbital port.
- **The first ward.** Start with an adult Ansa speaking to a sleeper she intends to wake, with her childhood emerging through the stations of a night's work. The first thaw consumes much of the station's reserve and makes long-term preservation untenable.
- **A shore with lights.** Ansa reads her way toward an actual place on Earth and, on learning the mission, asks its present inhabitants for a shore where a few sleepers could descend. The scene of reciprocal contact would carry the story.

## Provisional direction

Choose the turning station. The work Ansa performs out of faith in a homecoming puts the real decision in her hands. Westerly was built in Earth orbit to carry its sleeping passengers to a rendezvous with an outward-bound colony ship. The ship is coming into reach at last. Its crew has space and a plan for everyone. Westerly's repaired pilot drive can instead take the station to Ibis, an inhabited Earth-orbital port with a recovery ward and an offer to take passengers in stages. Westerly has fuel for one rendezvous. Going to Ibis means missing the colony ship and giving up the sleeping passengers' designated home. Ansa elects to dock and start bringing them into a community where they can speak for themselves. The story reaches the first waking.

## Craft plan

- **Teller.** Try close third in the younger Ansa's immediate, hungry point of view, moving that closeness forward with her age. Try adult Ansa in first-person past, amused by her childhood presumptions and still angry about choices she made with incomplete knowledge. Choose after the blind reading.
- **Ansa.** The only awake human since Marl rescued her from a failing pod. She knows the care rounds and grows into the station's pilot and mechanic. She takes the archive addresses for destinations and imagines leading the sleepers to the streets where they lived. She scavenges idioms from Earth books, tries them out at the slightest provocation, and talks her way into an objection. Under pressure she makes a joke to keep the other person in the room, then says the thing straight.
- **Marl.** Knows the flight plan and thinks Ansa will accept it when she sees what was provided for the passengers. He wants her confidence in his care and her cooperation with the departure. He brings tools in the order they'll be needed, gives exact names and times in complete sentences, and uses a warmer register to remember the girl he raised. He admits what he chose to withhold and why. **Ida**, a sleeper whose handwritten Earth address Ansa learns as a child, speaks only at the end. She is used to filling out forms for herself and listens for the parts a stranger has got wrong.
- **The conversation owed.** In the long scene Ansa asks for the homecoming she has worked toward. Marl owes her the actual history of the station, what he let her believe and why, and what the passengers agreed to when they could still answer for themselves. Ansa owes him a plain account of what she means to ask them to give up.
- **Casting from the original.** The adult woman has cropped pale hair, a straight, easy stance, and a slight knowing smile, in close-fitting black with red seams and a gold breast insignia. She looks at home within reach of a control panel. Ansa has Marl cut her hair short so it clears the awkward service hatch, then keeps the cut because she likes it. She moves into a room as if there's a place for her and uses the same slantwise smile to bait Marl into an argument at ten and hold her ground with him at twenty-eight. The gold badge, which she begged to wear as a girl, catches Ida's eye when she first wakes. A black undershirt beneath the short station jacket preserves the silhouette and palette for work around cold equipment.
- **Place.** Westerly's window deck joins the rows of pods, their service panels, the pilot console, and the dock collar in one narrow passage. The open view of Earth presses on every decision. The closed observation blister would privilege the view, and the old galley would privilege the pair's domestic life. On the window deck there is a cold draft along the floor, a yeast smell from Marl's bread tray, the buzz of a worn vent fan, green pinlights reflected in the pod covers, and the clear edge of Earth's shadow. The girl can slip under a jammed service hatch. The woman has to fold herself to reach the same place. Pod rounds run on station mornings. The adult flight begins on a winter night pass over the coast in Ansa's archive, now lit by a town of dinosaur builders. At the end an occupied port fills the window.

## Scenes and ending

1. **Ten, station morning (about a quarter).** Ansa reads the ledger of an Earth receiving hall for evacuees and matches its handwritten changes of address to an atlas. Ida made an entry there and now lies in the pod next to Ansa's seat. She wants Marl to help her picture the street where she'll take Ida. When a pump note drops out, he wants Ansa to work by ear and hold the coolant bypass her hand is small enough to reach. Ansa stops a failing bank from warming and hears how carefully Marl has been using the word *arrival*. Remember the tiny bubbles moving again in the sight tube alongside Ida's shoulder. Summarize the intervening years through rounds, readings, and pilot drills.
2. **Twenty-eight, night pass (about half, long scene).** Ansa completes the last drive test and asks about the shuttle ride from their supposed Earth rendezvous. Marl says Westerly was built for departure only, then tells her in order about the contracts, his awakening of her, and the years he let her supply Earth as the destination. He wants her to use the transit window, and she wants a candid accounting and room to make the decision herself. A fresh Ibis berth notice is part of the ordinary traffic she tracks. She sees a precedent in those revised Earth addresses for asking a port to receive passengers whose plans have changed, obtains a concrete offer of staged care, and hears Marl make the case for honoring the passengers' chosen mission. She has to give up her own imagined landing whichever way she goes. Stay with the argument as the pod pump falters under the drive load and she keeps the bank steady by applying what she learned in the first scene. She chooses Ibis and makes the burn while the outward colony ship is still waiting.
3. **First waking, docked at Ibis (about a quarter).** A port crew cycles supplies through the collar while Ansa brings Ida round at the head of the row. Marl wants her to let him take the first greeting by his checklist. Ansa wants him there to see the result and gives Ida room to ask her own first question. Ida recognizes Ansa's badge, asks who she is, and takes the arrivals slate to write in her own hand. The other rows remain visible beyond them. The changing addresses in the archive, the silence under Ida's glass, and Ansa's borrowed badge give the first entry at Ibis its weight.
