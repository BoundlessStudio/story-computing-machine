# The Sky in His Teeth — provisional grand direction

Thrum arrives three years late, twenty pounds of long-haired white, gray, and cream cat. He brings a feeding saucer, speaks as though Hesta is the troublesome arrival, and sleeps across her face because it is warm. Before she can return him, he makes her distinguish a familiar object from a perfect copy, then teaches a small return-working: bringing his straying shadow back to the particular living body she can feel. She mistakes exacting, unfinished instruction for an unsuitable familiar's demands.

Answering Thrum's call, Lord Varras sees the cat and asks whether his master is accepting apprentices again. Something Thrum hunted through the night roads in an earlier life has grown large enough to swallow pieces of the heavens. Its confinement failed when his eighth life ended three years ago; tonight it is tearing through the visible sky. Varras can bind it, but cannot retrieve anything consumed. The recognition opens an expedition.

Thrum has always been a cat. Eight lives of patient, bodily observation gave him knowledge humans have forgotten. He died after accepting Hesta's summons; kittenhood and recovering that knowledge took three real years, without making her losses beneficial. He chose her after an examination: she noticed the maker's thumbmark lost by a standard repair, then ruined her own passing work trying to recover it. He saw useful attention and destructive impatience, not a destined savior. He could not have foreseen their present need.

Varras chains a living constellation around the devourer, its enormous luminous body visibly straining against his binding. Thrum opens a passage through the captive beast's shadow. Hesta goes inside with him because retrieving what was swallowed requires approaching the actual stolen matter. Thrum knew both the intact heavens and the creature's earlier, smaller form; restoration cannot supply knowledge he never possessed. Inside, stolen stars burn behind counterfeit skies. His sensory lessons become how they navigate something that offers convincing appearances without the real objects' weight, resistance, or presence.

After guided practice under danger, she can restore contact with a still-living caster, not resurrect or guess repairs to a body. Varras cannot take over while holding the constellation's chains, and his memories of Thrum's previous body cannot identify this unfamiliar ninth body among its copies. Hesta's two aggravating nights have given her immediate, intimate knowledge of its breathing, weight, and uneven purr.

Thrum enters the devourer's deepest fold to separate and restore the stolen heavens while forcing its swollen body back toward its remembered form. His connection begins to vanish among beautiful false cats. Hesta must cross the collapsing counterfeit night, find the actual contact, and cast the return herself. He answers; she brings the living master back while his restoration bursts the consumed sky free and Varras holds the collapsing creature. Both masters' formidable work remains theirs. Her first impossible spell opens an apprenticeship across worlds. The rescued cat lands heavily against her face and objects to being moved.
