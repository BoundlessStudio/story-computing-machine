# The Late Familiar — revised provisional direction

The cat has always been a cat. Across eight lives he learned restoration by attending to particular things: warm stone, a bowl's underside, pressure against a crack. His native nine-life capacity gave him time, not ready-made wisdom. Centuries of patient study made him formidable. Restoration returns matter to a state the caster has personally known; it cannot invent knowledge or restart an ended life. Rebirth is a separate capacity. This is his ninth life.

He chose Hesta at an entrance examination. The examiner repaired a jug into a flawless standard shape. She noticed its maker's thumbprint had vanished. Her own repair was good enough to pass, but she took it apart again, pursuing the particular jug rather than a plausible replacement. She broke the handle beyond her ability to mend. He saw promising exactness and someone who needed to investigate without destroying her work. She needed instruction, not endorsement of an existing moral wisdom.

He accepted her summons, then died before reaching her. Being born a kitten elsewhere, growing, and recovering his knowledge took three years. Her fees, opportunities, and confidence were spent during that interval. Nothing secretly made those losses necessary. He arrives with a chipped blue feeding saucer, twenty pounds of white, gray, and cream fur, and an expectation of being obeyed. Sleeping across her face is simply warm and comfortable for him.

Two early exercises resemble an unsuitable familiar's petty demands. She smooths the saucer's chip away with ordinary pattern-mending; he refuses breakfast until she returns it to the chipped state she saw on arrival. Restoration need not mean perfection. He also knocks down a cheap cup. She reaches to impose its remembered outside shape, but he bats her casting hand away until she checks the floor and retrieves a handle fragment that rolled beneath the cupboard. Before using power, she must find and examine all the actual material. His insistence remains aggravating without concealing a piece to engineer her failure.

The saucer already carries an old paired breakfast-call; she never reconstructs a spell she has not known. In response to her first accurate restoration, the cat himself rings that existing call with his living power. Its counterpart is in the most feared sorcerer's house, where his old pupil has been listening for it. The crockery originates no magic. The sorcerer follows the call, recognizes his master in this unfamiliar ninth body, and asks whether apprentices are being accepted again. He wants to resume learning, bringing deference, impatience, and spectacular credentials rather than a second problem for Hesta to solve.

Recognition tempts her to demand her whole lost education immediately, or ask his famous pupil to certify her competence. The cat's scale is real: he can reassemble a ruined stone arch he knew in an earlier life, including weathered carving nobody now remembers. That wonder makes returning to cup fragments harder. Both students must observe the same pieces, feel their edges, assemble them without power, and restore one join. The sorcerer also has habits to unlearn; Hesta has no authority to instruct him. She needs the cat's precise correction and repeated practice. Her first successful join preserves a tiny irregularity she previously tried to erase. She picks up the next fragment while the cat settles heavily against her arm.

This proposal's pressure is her costly impatience meeting difficult work before a revered witness. Whether apprenticeship alone supplies enough movement remains open for the user's assessment; test it before adding a separate crisis.
