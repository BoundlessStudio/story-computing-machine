# Opus review: The Place Laid for Her

Independent audit for a later editing pass. It is not a `review.md` verdict,
does not change canon state, and does not authorize edits by itself.

## Verdict

- Dialogue: REVISE — mostly specific, funny and ordinary, but the decisive poem argument turns into an even volley of clipped parries, and Brevot's reproach is buried in "They are. Rather."
- Prose: PASS — controlled close third with concrete embodiment detail; two small clarity slips.
- Continuity and prompt: PASS — the required exchange opens the story verbatim; it's a complete comic-melancholy date inside the length band, with two small payment/coat inconsistencies.
- Edit scope: targeted-dialogue
- Existing review.md said: Verdict PASS, "Dialogue: PASS"; agree for most of the story, disagree about the decisive poem exchange.

This story's dialogue avoids most of the collection's usual failures. People
talk about coats, quail, pigeons and a musician's schedule. The host and the
musician have practical needs of their own, and the misunderstandings (supper
for two, the novel) are easy to follow and funny. The problem is local, in the
decisive exchange at L205–L257. Once the poem is being judged, Brevot drops the
loose, self-mocking voice he uses everywhere else and trades short, exact
ripostes with Ildesse until the two sound like one speaker. The most important
fact in that exchange is that his love poem reproaches her. It arrives as
"They are. Rather." and neither of them touches it again, so the reader has to
work out alone what he is reproaching her for. The edit pass should loosen
Brevot there, make the reproach concrete enough to land, and give Ildesse one
beat of response. Apart from that, it needs only two one-word fixes.

## Edit guardrails

- The prompt's two lines must stay verbatim as the story's first words (L11, L13), including the comma splice in the second.
- Close third through adult Ildesse, past tense. Keep it within 2,500–4,000 words (now about 3,120) and keep it comic social fantasy: broadly accessible and non-graphic. Her wish that Brevot would take out the feathers stays implied.
- Embodiment facts the plot depends on must hold. The living thread in the sword supplies the magic, which is consistent with LOCKED "Living agency and magic" in `universe/rules.md`. The mannequin works only within six paces. It returns sight, hearing and touch but can't eat or drink. The scabbard stays tied shut all evening and at the end. Brevot already knows all of this from their letters.
- No rescue, reconciliation or second date. They pay separately and leave separately. Don't add a closing explanation of what the evening meant.
- Don't polish the ordinary lines, the host or the musician into wit. In the D1 rework, don't add more well-turned lines for Ildesse.

## Dialogue findings

### D1 · Major · Poem verdict becomes a matched volley and buries the reproach

- Where: L205–L257, from Brevot's "Well?" to the quail arriving
- Text:
> "It's not meant as a portrait."
> "It is for you. That doesn't mean every line describes you."
> "The lilies. The entire first stanza."
> "I thought the flowers were a reproach," she said.
> "They are. Rather."
- Problem: Identical Voices / Tennis Match at the decisive exchange. From L207 to L255 nearly every turn is one or two short, exact sentences of equal weight. Brevot's lines ("It's not meant as a portrait." / "It is amorous." / "They are. Rather.") use the same clipped, correcting register as Ildesse's, so with the tags covered only the content tells them apart. Elsewhere he is looser and funnier about himself (L61, L191, L271). Here, at his most exposed, he argues distinctions as calmly as she does. The compression also loses meaning. "The lilies. The entire first stanza." only makes sense after L251's narration reveals that the lilies withered. "They are. Rather." admits that his love poem reproaches her, but not for what. The reader has to connect it to her fault-finding letters (L117, L219) without help. His glance at the bouquet then allows a second reading in which "the flowers" means her real bouquet. Neither character comes back to the admission. His apology at L271 is about nerves, not the reproach, so the most pointed thing said all evening goes nowhere.
- Fix: Keep Ildesse's "It's a great deal of war to give someone over dinner," "I enjoy being rude about them. I don't wish I were in one," "Something amorous," and the glass trick. From L227 to L255, rework Brevot's side so he explains the poem the way a nervous poet does: too much, a little defensively, with one concrete image, not a ruling. His account of the lilies should make the reproach plain enough that "Oh, dear" and the bouquet glance land on something the reader already has. Tie it to her "buts" and the poems she found fault with, not to her being a sword. Cut "They are. Rather." or replace it with a line that doesn't close the exchange on a neat last word. Before the server brings the quail, give Ildesse one short spoken or physical response to being reproached, even if it's a failed start. Then check that L271–L275 ("I was nervous… About reading it.") still follows. Illustrative only: "They're meant to be my letters, sort of. And the blade's always dreaming about the next fight, so they wilt a bit. It's affectionate. Mostly." He must not sum up the evening or what's wrong between them.

### D2 · Minor · "The other song" points at a song he already heard

- Where: L335–L341, after "I like all this."
- Text:
> "You wouldn't have liked the other song either," she said.
> "Was there another?"
- Problem: Ground/referent. Two pieces were played, so "the other song" most naturally means the quick second piece Brevot sat through. His "Was there another?" then reads as if he'd forgotten it, and the reader stalls until "Only one I decided against." The uptake saves the sense, but the line points at the wrong song.
- Fix: Change one word so his question is a real question. Illustrative only: "You wouldn't have liked the third one either." Keep "Was there another?", "Only one I decided against", "Ah. No, probably not." and the narration after it.

### D3 · Minor · "Said it again" about a compliment he never finished

- Where: L371–L377, at the curtains (final meaningful exchange)
- Text:
> "I should have said it again."
- Problem: Ground/verb fit. The compliment was cut off at "You look—" (L73), so he has nothing to say "again". Ildesse would take it as "tried again", but in the story's last exchange the word makes the reader look back.
- Fix: Change it to "I should have tried again" or similar. Keep "Your dress is wonderful, by the way." The shift from "You look—" to the one real thing she is wearing is worth keeping, so don't "correct" it to "You look wonderful". Keep "I would have liked that." unchanged.

## Prose findings

### P1 · Minor · Her arrival plan reads as a contradiction

- Where: L29, at the host's desk
- Text:
> She had intended to arrive three minutes after Brevot
> To accomplish this, she had come early to ascertain the arrangement of the room.
- Problem: Coming early is given as the way to arrive after him. The missing step (settle the room, withdraw, come back through the curtains after him) is only implied later by L43's "without waiting for her entrance". The reader stalls on the logic in the story's second scene paragraph.
- Fix: Add the missing step in one clause. Keep the second knot and the clock striking eight.

### P2 · Minor · "Him" briefly points at Brevot

- Where: L187, after the second piece
- Text:
> Ildesse watched him go to a table where three women were happily shouting over one another.
- Problem: The previous sentence's subject is Brevot, so "him" first reads as Brevot leaving the table. Only "when he began to play" makes it the musician.
- Fix: Write "the musician" or "the player" here.

## Continuity and prompt findings

### C1 · Minor · The music is paid for twice

- Where: L173 (second piece) and L355 (the bill)
- Text:
> She had paid to interrupt the regular program.
> She paid for the flowers and the music and her half of the food.
- Problem: L173 says she has already paid the musician, but she pays for the music again on the bill.
- Fix: Change one of them: "She was paying to interrupt the regular program" at L173, or drop "the music" at L355. Keep "The number looked startling even though she had known it beforehand."

### C2 · Minor · Brevot fetches a coat he is wearing

- Where: L367; set up at L61–L67 and L103
- Text:
> Then he stepped away to retrieve his coat.
> He reached into his coat.
- Problem: He wears the hated coat through dinner (the sleeve at L67, the poem drawn from it at L103). "Retrieve his coat" either contradicts that or brings in a second coat nobody mentioned.
- Fix: Make it his hat, or name an overcoat from the cloakroom.
