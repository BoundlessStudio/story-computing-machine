# Outline

## Story

- Premise and central promise: **The Place Laid for Her.** Ildesse, a living consciousness housed in a sword, takes a glamoured mannequin to dinner with a literary correspondent. Admission proves easier than making the evening resemble her rehearsed romance. Deliver conversational and physical comedy within a genuinely disappointing date.
- Focal pressure or attachment: Ildesse wants to be extravagantly courted. She has reserved an expensive table, selected a spectacular dress, and arranged a musical interruption. Her pleasure in anticipation makes her mistake choreography for agreement.
- Counterforce or complication: Brevot wants his brilliant correspondent to admire his new poem. Attraction mingles with the prospect of an impressive muse. Both mistake enjoyable correspondence for evidence that they want the same evening.
- POV, distance, and information limit: Close third past through adult Ildesse. Reveal her preparations and embarrassing hopes without contempt. Brevot's intentions become knowable through what he brings, says, misses, and does.
- Governing movement and time shape: One continuous dinner, from entrance to separate departures. Target 2,500–4,000 words; linger over failed timing and the intervals when nobody knows what to say.
- Speculative rule or ordinary-world constraint: Ildesse's living, warm, pulsing nerve-thread occupies the sword's tang and blade channel; a pommel wick supplies nourishment. This living core supplies her magic. She controls one familiar mannequin within six paces, receiving its sight, hearing, and touch. Its voice and appearance are her active glamour; its wooden joints remain physical. It cannot taste or digest. The sword cannot move itself.
- Dialogue promise: The quoted impossibility belongs in a funny social encounter; subsequent talk exposes the distance between being charmed on paper and enjoying somebody across a table.
- Dialogic medium: Doorway speech, a reservation note, table conversation, an unwanted poetry reading, an interrupted musician, and the mannequin's deliberately arranged expressions.
- Dialogue engine: Requests for pleasure keep being heard as invitations to perform. Attempts to recover the anticipated mood expose the mismatch until both must decide whether to continue.

## Voice

- Narrative texture: Attend to her simultaneous management of a body and an impression: chair height, stiff fingers, a smile held past its occasion. Longer anticipatory sentences can collapse into plain observations when reality intervenes.
- Conversational texture: Courteous beginnings, overprepared compliments, interruptions caused by service, hesitant invitations, and increasingly unadorned disagreement. Leave room for ordering and questions that do only one thing.
- Rhetorical ownership: Ildesse borrows the grandeur of beloved romances and recognizes bad phrasing faster than emotional signals. Brevot enjoys verbal invention, hears criticism as literary engagement, and can be funny without being gracious. The host owns brief practical questions, not the story's wisdom.
- Pressure behavior: Ildesse becomes ceremonious when embarrassed; her puppet's face lags behind her intention. Brevot overexplains his poem, then stops entertaining her. Neither becomes suddenly incapable of honest speech.
- Relationship movement: She wants unmistakable desire but cannot comfortably request adoration; he wants praise but packages it as a gift. Their exchanges spend the credit accumulated by letters. Ending the date requires relinquishing the audience each expected.
- Anti-default: Do not redeem the dinner through rescue, useful swordsmanship, a better replacement suitor, or a jointly negotiated second date. Their incompatibility need not invalidate their earlier pleasure.

## Beats

1. **The entrance.** Open with the required exchange: “No weapons allowed in this establishment, ma'am.” “My consciousness is in the sword, the humanoid body you're looking at is a glamoured mannequin I'm puppeting.” The host initially directs the reservation question to the wrong end of her. Establish the living core naturally when she checks its wick. A securely tied scabbard satisfies his practical concern; she remains in possession of herself. The adjustment takes minutes, while her carefully timed arrival is spoiled.
2. **An entrance made twice.** Brevot arrives while she is arranging sword, mannequin, flowers, and chair. He knows her embodiment from their letters. Both try to restart their introductions. He mistakes her preparations for indifference. She has ordered a meal she cannot eat because the occasion seemed to require one; he assumes the unused setting awaits another guest. Clarification costs some glamour without becoming a personhood debate.
3. **Competing entertainments.** His new poem interrupts a question she hoped would become flirtation. Her hired musician then interrupts his reading. He asks for quiet; she has already paid for another piece. Let the resulting choices be awkward and funny, with the musician free to seek an ordinary answer. Brevot's poem turns her into a martial emblem. She offers the kind of unsparing correction he previously enjoyed in letters, now before an unwanted witness. Neither intended cruelty; neither enjoys the other's intended gift.
4. **The evening they have.** Allow a quieter attempt after the musician leaves. Concrete questions about their tastes expose incompatibilities that correspondence left harmless. They decide the romantic experiment is over. Settling the bill tests whether either will make the other an indebted audience; both pay their share. They leave separately, with embarrassment, expense, and injured vanity intact. Ildesse still likes her extravagant dress. Stop in the lived aftermath without a lesson or future romantic compensation.
