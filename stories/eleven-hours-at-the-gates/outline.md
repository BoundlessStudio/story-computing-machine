# The Weight of the Gate

## Promised experience

“Exactly eleven hours” gives the engineer's work a hard limit. “Last surviving human stronghold” puts a civilization outside her workbench as well as a person on the other end of the diagnostic lead. The request specifies a forty-year failure, a pilot who died defending the city, obsolete weapons, an intelligence that refuses another pilot, and the engineer hearing her own name and “daughter” when she connects the helmet. I read that address as both an unsettling claim and a reason to invest in what happens when the army reaches the gates. The nature of the kinship, who will wear the suit, and the fate of the city are open. The scuffed white-and-red armor, worn shop, cyan diagnostics, and rocky terrain suggest tactile military science fiction.

## Provisional direction

- At dawn the last human city, Cairn End, sees an army of autonomous war machines cross into its basin. It will reach the gates in exactly eleven hours. Teams haul people off the basin farms across the hinged stone road that spans the city's only crossing. The road used to rise on its city-side hinge and lock upright as a second wall. Its drive died in the battle forty years ago, when pilot Linet Tann died defending the gates and her suit went out of service. Neris Tann tries to revive it in the old armory. All would-be pilots have been refused. The suit's best weapon barely marks a dead scout's armor.
- Three hours are gone when Neris gets the damaged joints responding on the bench and plugs the detached helmet into her diagnostic computer to bring the systems together. “Neris,” says the voice in the helmet. “Daughter.” Stave shared a household with Linet and took turns getting Neris to sleep by walking with her in its arms. It knows the pet name it used only in the dark. During the fall of the old gate, Linet was mortally wounded. At Linet's urging, Stave used the failing suit's last movement to put a slipping Neris into the hands of the gate crew. As far as Stave's body knows, here she is again, somewhere she ought to be carried out of. It refuses to let her pilot.
- Stave and Neris run bench tests that lift one edge of a sunken slab. Neris sees how a weight-bearing field designed to spread a load through solid stone might stand the span upright. For a sustained lift their combined balance and movement have to keep both hands in contact. Stave opens the harness in test mode, which lets it keep an override and forbids it to carry Neris out of the armory. They attempt to lift a much heavier fallen slab. When Neris slips, Stave takes over long enough to pull her clear. The slab drops before she can right herself. With two hours left, Neris strips the obsolete weapons to free the suit's limited strength. She asks Stave to steady her while she finds her own grip and promises to tell it when to release. The last farm crew arrives with empty wagons. The winter grain still stands green below the road. Stave switches the suit out of test mode and accepts Neris as pilot. It waits for her to take the first step.
- Eleven hours after dawn the foremost machines cross the road and hit the gates. Beneath the city end of the road, where the ruined drive left room to stand, Neris and Stave take the span's weight together. The force of the attack shakes her grip. Stave begins to pull her clear as it did in the armory. Neris tells it to hold. Stave keeps its side of the grip and steadies her while she reaches again, finds her purchase, and takes the span the rest of the way up. The road tilts the first ranks into the cleft and seats upright. Its hinge tears loose under the last impact, leaving it wedged against the city. The next rank stops at the far edge. The army will have to span the cleft itself. The city buys time, with its farms on the wrong side of the new wall. Stave waits for Neris to say she is ready to let go before it opens the harness. The gate crew calls down into the hinge cradle to ask how many are alive. Neris answers, “Two.”
