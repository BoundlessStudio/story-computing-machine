# The Weight of the Gate

## Promised experience

“Exactly eleven hours” gives the engineer's work a hard limit. “Last surviving human stronghold” puts a civilization outside her workbench as well as a person on the other end of the diagnostic lead. The request specifies a forty-year failure, a pilot who died defending the city, obsolete weapons, an intelligence that refuses another pilot, and the engineer hearing her own name and “daughter” when she connects the helmet. I read that address as both an unsettling claim and a reason to invest in what happens when the army reaches the gates. The nature of the kinship, who will wear the suit, and the fate of the city are open. The scuffed white-and-red armor, worn shop, cyan diagnostics, and rocky terrain suggest tactile military science fiction.

## Provisional direction

- At dawn the last human city, Cairn End, sees an army of autonomous war machines cross into its basin. Its earliest arrival at the gates is exactly eleven hours away. Neris Tann works in the old armory, trying to revive a combat suit that has been immobile since pilot Linet Tann died holding those gates forty years ago. Qualified pilots have all been refused. A bench test against a piece of the army's armor takes the last of the hope out of the suit's weapons.
- Neris gets the armor's damaged joints responding on the bench and plugs the detached helmet into her diagnostic computer to bring the systems together. “Neris,” says the voice in the helmet. “Daughter.” The intelligence, Stave, was her mother's partner inside and outside the suit. As a baby Neris knew both their voices. Stave last felt her being passed out of its hands as Linet died. Now it offers recognition and insists Neris stay out of the armor. The family claim makes the refusal personal.
- The city's rock cleft has one approach at the gates, a vast hinged stone span. Its long-dead drive once let it rise and lock as a second wall. When a repaired boot fixes to the armory floor and lifts a slab with it, Neris sees a use for the suit's archaic weight-bearing field. It can put the force of the bedrock under its feet behind one continuous lift. The field needs Stave's balance and a pilot's movement together. Neris devises the lift, asks Stave to give her control over when to let go, and strips the old weapons to give the failing armor one chance. Stave opens the harness for her.
- Eleven hours after dawn the front rank hits the gates and the outer stonework begins to fail. With the enemy above them and scrap shearing off the armor, Neris and Stave take the span's weight together. At the last moment Stave feels Neris's hands slip and moves to pull her back. Neris chooses to reach again. Stave follows her movement, and the road rises through the host and locks upright. The span can no longer be lowered, and the city's known ground approach is gone. Stave asks if Neris is ready before it opens the harness. When the gate crew asks how many to pull out, Neris answers, “Two.”
