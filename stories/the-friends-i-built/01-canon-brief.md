# Canon brief

## Hard constraints

- The infernal setting must remain compatible with the established universal
  afterlife. “Hell,” damnation, and any infernal jurisdiction may be a local or
  cultural experience of that afterlife, but the story must not declare that
  this one model exhausts the afterlife for every culture or era
  (`universe/rules.md` — `## Afterlife and continuation`).
- Magic, technology, and divinity are cultural descriptions of underlying
  systems, not objective cosmic categories. Any mechanism used to build the
  friends should be described consistently from the viewpoint culture rather
  than made the universal explanation for all constructs or infernal beings
  (`universe/rules.md` — `## System categories`;
  `universe/glossary.md` — `## Magic, technology, and divinity`).
- Only life can use magic. A nonliving creation may store, focus, transmit, or
  shape magic supplied through living agency, but it cannot independently work
  magic unless the story establishes that it is alive. Construction neither
  proves nor disproves life (`universe/rules.md` —
  `## Living agency and magic`).
- If the created friends are minds or otherwise person-like, their identities,
  relationships, consent, choices, and moral consequences cannot be dismissed
  as mere programming or a technical error. Their mechanism may answer
  story-specific questions about personhood, but it may not erase the ethical
  stakes of treating them as friends (`universe/style-guide.md` —
  `## Personhood and moral consequence`).
- Belphegor's prompt-required title “archdemon” does not establish godhood.
  If the story also calls Belphegor a god, the continuing conjunction of
  worship, power, and authority is required; origin or raw power alone is not
  enough (`universe/rules.md` — `## Divinity`).
- Names are not universally magical. Any binding power attached to a name,
  written label, sign-name, or constructed glyph must be a defined local
  mechanism rather than an assumed cosmological rule
  (`universe/rules.md` — `## Names and identity binding`).
- The requested teen / PG-13 treatment fits the project's content ceiling:
  infernal peril, death, prejudice, or abuse may be explored only
  non-graphically and with consequence; minors must never be sexualized
  (`universe/style-guide.md` — `## Audience and content boundaries`;
  `stories/the-friends-i-built/00-prompt.md` — `## Story controls`).

## Useful established context

- A universal underlying afterlife exists, and ghosts, reincarnation, and
  digitally mediated continuations are possible. This supports a damned soul
  continuing as a parent without determining the parent's exact condition,
  sentence, embodiment, or route into the infernal community
  (`universe/rules.md` — `## Afterlife and continuation`).
- Reality is coherent even when a culture's explanation is incomplete or
  incorrect. Infernal students and adults may sincerely use terms such as
  “damned soul,” “demon,” or “magic” without those terms becoming the
  omniscient taxonomy of the universe (`universe/premise.md` —
  `## Knowledge and connection`; `universe/rules.md` —
  `## System categories`).
- Different anomalous systems can interact, hybridize, or imitate one another,
  but no single origin explains them all. A small-scale infernal craft can mix
  engineered and magical-seeming elements without connecting itself to every
  other system in the setting (`universe/rules.md` —
  `## System categories`).
- Constructed origin does not disqualify an entity from godhood, and copied,
  branched, projected, or reconstructed people are judged by their particular
  mechanisms rather than by one universal ontology. These facts leave room for
  genuinely agentic created friends, while not predetermining what they are
  (`universe/rules.md` — `## Divinity`;
  `## Memory, transformation, copies, and branches`).
- The universe permits wide tonal and genre range, independent entry points,
  and stories whose characters never perceive the larger cosmology. A warmly
  macabre school comedy can therefore remain local and character-centered
  (`universe/style-guide.md` — `## Tonal range, scale, and connection`).

## Conflicts or ambiguity

- **No authoritative conflict currently requires a ruling.** `stories/INDEX.md`
  has no story marked canon `yes`, so no finalized canon story is eligible to
  add a competing depiction of Hell, Belphegor, demons, constructed friends,
  or infernal schooling (`stories/INDEX.md` — `# Story index`;
  `universe/README.md` — `## Authority order`).
- “Damned soul,” “archdemon,” Belphegor, Hell, and an infernal school are
  mandated or strongly implied by the prompt but absent from authoritative
  universe entries. This is an omission, not evidence that real-world
  demonology is canon. In particular, no traditional portfolio, rank,
  appearance, history, rivalries, or association beyond the prompt's extreme
  laziness may be imported as established fact.
- The friends' ontology is unresolved. They could be nonliving devices,
  living constructs, minds in another substrate, or something else, but the
  story must choose and apply one coherent local mechanism. Autonomous magical
  action would require them to qualify as alive; autonomous nonmagical action
  does not by itself settle either life or personhood
  (`universe/rules.md` — `## Living agency and magic`;
  `## Memory, transformation, copies, and branches`).
- The location of the infernal community relative to the persistent physical
  world is unspecified. The cosmology allows other dimensions, while the
  afterlife is independently established, but neither fact identifies Hell as
  a planet, plane, era, dimension, or universal capital
  (`universe/premise.md` — `## The deep-time shared world`;
  `universe/rules.md` — `## Afterlife and continuation`).
- Belphegor's ability to have a child with a damned soul, and the child's
  biological, legal, or metaphysical status, are not established. The prompt
  requires this family, but it does not authorize a general rule for demonic
  reproduction or inheritance.

## Unknowns

- **Unknown:** whether “damnation” is a moral judgment, legal sentence,
  cultural label, residence category, inherited status, or something else.
- **Unknown:** whether Hell is a single place, one culturally perceived region
  of the afterlife, an institution, or merely the characters' name for their
  surroundings.
- **Unknown:** the existence, hierarchy, powers, bodies, lifespans, and social
  roles of demons or archdemons beyond the prompt-required Belphegor.
- **Unknown:** Belphegor's gender, pronouns, portfolio, appearance, history,
  abilities, and relationship to any real-world folklore.
- **Unknown:** how a damned soul and an archdemon became parents; whether their
  child is alive, dead, infernal, embodied, hybrid, or categorized at all.
- **Unknown:** the era or chronological relationship between this story and
  the known physical-world eras. No placement should be inferred from its
  school technology, clothing, language, or comedy
  (`universe/timeline.md` — `## Established era order`).
- **Unknown:** whether the protagonist's deafness and non-speaking status have
  biological, infernal, magical, or other causes. Canon supplies no disability
  mechanism, communication standard, cure, or social model.
- **Unknown:** the school's governance, curriculum, accessibility practices,
  student population, and relationship to infernal authority.
- **Unknown:** the creations' substrate, degree of awareness, source of
  agency, ability to use magic, mortality, reproduction, legal status, and
  relationship to the afterlife.
- **Unknown:** whether any portal connects the infernal setting to the physical
  world. If a crossing appears, its access, limits, directionality, and
  paradox behavior must be defined locally; no universal portal network exists
  (`universe/rules.md` — `## Portals and crossing systems`).

## Safe invention space

- Create one bounded infernal school, its classrooms, staff roles, peer
  culture, accessibility failures, and a school-scale crisis. Present these as
  local facts, not the standard institution for all dead or infernal children.
- Treat “archdemon” as a local rank or address attached to the prompt-required
  Belphegor, without defining a universe-wide demon hierarchy or government.
- Establish only the family-scale facts needed for this child to exist and for
  both parents' love and harmful inertia to matter. Avoid general claims about
  all damned souls, demons, mixed parentage, or inherited power.
- Invent a local sign language or communication ecology, written exchanges,
  visual alerts, and constructed visual media. These can be ordinary,
  technical, magical-seeming, or hybrid so long as the mechanism is coherent
  and the protagonist's communication remains capable and socially meaningful.
- Define one specific construction practice: materials, activation, limits,
  upkeep, failure modes, and what evidence demonstrates each friend's agency.
  If a friend independently uses magic, establish within the story that it is
  alive. If the friends are person-like, give them meaningful choice and
  reciprocal stakes rather than treating them as replaceable tools.
- Invent narrow powers, costs, and safeguards for Belphegor, the protagonist,
  or the creations. Cost and desire have metaphysical force only if this local
  system establishes them, and the story should not generalize the mechanism
  beyond its immediate use (`universe/rules.md` —
  `## Costs, desire, and local mechanisms`).
- Keep the story an independent entry point. It need not name a known era,
  reuse a known faction or location, or connect its constructs to other
  artifacts merely because imagery or capabilities resemble them
  (`universe/style-guide.md` — `## Tonal range, scale, and connection`;
  `universe/rules.md` — `## Artifacts, motifs, and folklore`).

## Name constraints

- `stories/NAMES.md` is non-canon production memory, but every reserved form in
  it remains unavailable for casual reuse across canon, candidates,
  in-progress work, abandoned versions, and portable legacy sources
  (`stories/NAMES.md` — `## Rules`).
- `Belphegor` has no exact registered match, alias, or readily confusable close
  match. It is prompt-required rather than a newly proposed name. The primary
  coordinator should register it, plus any actual title used alone as a
  character label, after the plan is verified. No canon identity or alias for
  Belphegor is established.
- The protagonist, damned-soul parent, teachers, classmates, and created
  friends are all unnamed at this stage. Do not select names during canon
  research. Before drafting, validate every full name, given name, mononym,
  nickname, username, title used as a name, sign-name, visual glyph used as a
  name, and named person-like construct. A school name is outside this
  character registry unless the school is personified or otherwise treated as
  a person-like identity.
- The unresolved forms `Lena`, `Mara`, `Nisha`, `Pell`, and `Voss` already
  collide between distinct legacy identities and must not be repeated in this
  story without an approved, story-significant rationale
  (`stories/NAMES.md` — `## Known unresolved legacy collisions`).
- Construct- or title-shaped forms are reservations too. In particular,
  `Mother-fragment`, `Captain`, `Saint`, `the Daughter`, `the Hollow Regent`,
  and `the Goddess of Time` already label named animals or person-like
  entities. `Mother` is also reserved as an alias of Queen Aurelia. Ordinary
  relational prose such as “my mother” or “my father” is not automatically an
  alias, but a capitalized or stand-alone family title used as an identity must
  be checked and registered.
- Any deliberate reuse must document whether the identity is the same or
  distinct, why reuse matters to this story, and how readers will distinguish
  the identities. Convenience or infernal genre familiarity is not a valid
  rationale.

## Required checks after drafting

- Search for claims that this Hell, damnation system, demon hierarchy, school,
  or family structure is universal; narrow them to character belief or local
  fact unless later canon explicitly supports them.
- Verify that any creation performing magic independently is established as
  alive, and that nonliving devices receive their magic from living agency
  (`universe/rules.md` — `## Living agency and magic`).
- Track each friend's mechanism, knowledge, limitations, persistence, agency,
  consent, and treatment scene by scene. Confirm that destruction, repair,
  copying, replacement, or control does not erase personhood stakes
  (`universe/style-guide.md` — `## Personhood and moral consequence`).
- Check that Belphegor remains an archdemon rather than being casually treated
  as a god; if godhood is asserted, verify worship, power, and authority
  (`universe/rules.md` — `## Divinity`).
- Verify that the protagonist never gains hearing or speech as a cure, reward,
  or climax; all consequential communication must remain accessible through
  sign, writing, expression, visual media, or another established channel
  (`stories/the-friends-i-built/00-prompt.md` — `## Story controls`;
  `## Completion tests`).
- Check that the parents' love does not erase the practical harm of their
  inertia, and that the resolution supplies a concrete, sustainable
  accommodation rather than shifting all care work back onto the child
  (`stories/the-friends-i-built/00-prompt.md` — `## Completion tests`).
- Recheck every character-facing name and alias against `stories/NAMES.md`,
  including sign-names and names of constructed beings. Run the scoped
  name-validation workflow after the plan and again after final review.
- If any crossing, name-power, afterlife transition, copy, or reconstructed
  identity appears, verify that its local rules are stated consistently and
  are not used as an arbitrary patch for a causal or continuity problem
  (`universe/style-guide.md` — `## Continuity and evidence discipline`).
- Confirm that infernal peril remains non-graphic and age-appropriate
  (`universe/style-guide.md` — `## Audience and content boundaries`).
