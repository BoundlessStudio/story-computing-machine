# Story plan

## Premise and promise

- **One-sentence premise:** When Irix's loving but spectacularly indolent
  parents fail to help them enter the Cinder Annex's team exhibition, the deaf,
  non-speaking young maker grows two living constructs to collaborate with;
  when a classmate's demonstration endangers the school, Irix must choose
  between winning alone and trusting the friends they built to act for
  themselves.
- **Story promise:** A warmly macabre infernal-school fantasy in which making
  friends begins literally but becomes reciprocal. Irix's intelligence is
  demonstrated through observation, design, repair, communication, and ethical
  judgment. Their deafness and non-speaking status are never cured or treated
  as intellectual limitations.
- **Central dramatic question:** Can Irix stop treating self-sufficiency as the
  only safe answer to other people's failures and risk depending on friends
  whose choices they cannot control?
- **Meaningful change:** Irix begins determined to build companions who cannot
  forget them, exclude them, or fail to follow instructions. They end by
  recognizing Knurl and Thimble as friends precisely because each can refuse,
  surprise, and choose them back. Irix also requires their parents to replace
  affectionate promises with concrete adult support.

## Story controls

- **Form and length:** Complete short story at approximately 3,500 words,
  within the contracted 3,000–3,800-word range.
- **POV and tense:** First person past, strictly centered on Irix.
- **Narrative handling:** Irix has a rich internal voice. Dialogue reaches them
  through the local sign language, writing, facial expression, projected
  diagrams, and visible action. The narration does not invent unheard speech.
- **Tone and genre:** Warmly macabre school fantasy with dry domestic comedy,
  tactile infernal craft, a brisk crisis, and an emotionally sincere ending.
- **Content ceiling:** Teen / PG-13. Heat, smoke, frightened students, and
  damage to a living construct may create peril, but no graphic injury.
- **Setting scope:** The Cinder Annex is one local infernal school inside one
  culturally understood region of the underlying afterlife. Its vocabulary
  does not define universal Hell, demon hierarchy, damnation, schooling, or
  constructed life.
- **Opening target:** Within the first page, establish Irix's deaf and
  non-speaking communication, both parents' loving attention and disastrous
  inertia, the unsigned exhibition form, and the deadline that morning.
- **Ending target:** Knurl and Thimble choose to return to school with Irix;
  the school has made specific access and personhood changes; Belphegor and
  Talvi have created and accepted concrete support obligations. The ending
  does not grant Irix speech, hearing, effortless popularity, or total
  independence.

## Character engine

- **Irix — protagonist, age fourteen, they/them**
  - Deaf and non-speaking, Irix communicates fluently through sign, a pocket
    slate, expression, and self-built visual interfaces. Communication barriers
    arise when other people fail to look, learn, or provide access.
  - **Concrete desire:** Enter the Cinder Annex's Aptitudes Exhibition, prove
    that they can lead difficult work, and earn access to the school's advanced
    workshop.
  - **Immediate tactic:** After classmates refuse the inconvenience of teaming
    with them and their parents fail to complete the guardian arrangements,
    Irix will build a perfectly reliable team.
  - **Strength:** Exceptional systems thinking. Irix notices heat, pressure,
    motion, material fatigue, and social patterns; they turn signs into
    diagrams that let many people coordinate.
  - **Blind spot:** Irix equates dependence with vulnerability and reliability
    with obedience. They initially design companions as an escape from the
    risks of reciprocal friendship.
  - **External stakes:** Missing the team exhibition delays advanced-workshop
    eligibility for a year. The later furnace failure threatens students,
    Knurl, Thimble, and the school.
  - **Internal stakes:** Winning by treating living friends as equipment would
    reproduce the disregard Irix resents in adults and peers.
  - **Movement:** From solitary competence and control, through wonder and
    discomfort at the constructs' preferences, to public collaboration and a
    boundary that asks parents for accountable care rather than more promises.

- **Knurl — living constructed friend**
  - A small brass-framed, embermoss-bodied creature with precise spindle legs.
    Knurl likes symmetry, bitter coal-sugar, and being asked before anyone
    touches their frame. They choose the name `Knurl` from Irix's box of labeled
    tools because the textured wheel feels satisfying underfoot.
  - Knurl reads Irix's signs attentively and can answer with a pair of hinged
    flags, pointing, and rearranged word tiles. Their plans are cautious and
    exact.
  - Knurl's refusal to be carried and later refusal to stay safely out of the
    crisis are evidence of agency, not comic glitches.

- **Thimble — living constructed friend**
  - A round felt-and-copper creature grown around a warm embermoss core.
    Thimble likes sweet ash, climbing, dramatic colored sails, and making
    patterns Irix did not request. They choose `Thimble` by nesting inside the
    tool, then defending it as a bed.
  - Thimble answers through colored panels, gestures, and blunt arrangements
    of word tiles. Their improvisation balances Knurl's caution.
  - In the climax, Thimble voluntarily risks scorching an outer sail to cover
    an ember spray. The injury matters and receives care; Thimble is not
    discarded or replaced.

- **Talvi — Irix's damned-soul parent, she/her**
  - Warm, perceptive, and fluent in signing when awake. Talvi preserves every
    one of Irix's childish diagrams and can articulate exactly why Irix's work
    matters.
  - Talvi is also catastrophically inert: promises are sincere in the moment
    and then vanish into another nap. Their failure to arrange transport helps
    create the opening obstacle.
  - In the resolution, Talvi admits love did not perform the missed work,
    arranges a standing school conveyance with an adult dispatcher, and joins
    the accountability agreement rather than asking Irix to build another
    reminder.

- **Belphegor — Irix's archdemon parent**
  - Prompt-required archdemon; never called a god. Belphegor uses they/them
    pronouns, can sign elegantly with four hands while barely leaving a couch,
    and takes real delight in Irix's inventions.
  - Belphegor signs the wrong corner of the exhibition form, promises to fix it,
    and falls asleep. Their local authority and power do not excuse the failure.
  - After the crisis, Belphegor uses their own living magic to bind their couch
    to tip them upright at agreed family deadlines. This is a narrow,
    self-imposed furniture enchantment, not universal name-power or a cosmic
    rule.

- **Ekri — classmate and social antagonist, not a villain, he/him**
  - A talented student whose exhibition device converts their living magical
    input into percussive vibration and harmless flame figures. Ekri wants the
    audience's admiration and assumes teaming with Irix will be slow because
    Ekri would have to look up, sign, or wait for written replies.
  - Ekri's rushed safety latch causes the furnace hazard. Ekri owns the mistake,
    follows Irix's projected instructions during the rescue, and begins repair
    through action rather than instant friendship.

- **Instructor Bexel — bounded school adult, she/her**
  - Runs the exhibition and understands the school's procedures, not universal
    cosmology. Bexel signs competently but has tolerated sound-only emergency
    alerts and a team rule that lets peers turn access into Irix's problem.
  - During the crisis, Bexel authorizes Irix's evacuation plan and helps carry
    it out. Afterward, Bexel credits Knurl and Thimble as collaborators,
    requires visual alarms at future events, and becomes the responsible adult
    contact in Irix's family support plan.

- **Opposition:** Deadline pressure; classmates' access failures; the parents'
  sincere but harmful inertia; Irix's urge to make reliability synonymous with
  obedience; Ekri's insecure showmanship; and the exhibition furnace's seeded
  physical failure.

## Causal arc

1. **Beginning state:** Irix tries to wake Talvi and Belphegor for the last
   guardian signature and the promised ride to school. Both parents express
   love, admiration, and confidence in Irix, yet neither finishes the task.
   Irix repairs the form and hauls their materials alone, confirming their
   belief that depending on anyone is foolish.
2. **Inciting disruption:** Instructor Bexel announces that advanced-workshop
   eligibility requires a collaborative exhibition entry. Ekri declines Irix's
   invitation because communication would take “extra time,” and the remaining
   teams close around their tables. Bexel offers Irix a solo accommodation that
   would exclude them from the workshop criterion. Irix refuses and decides to
   construct teammates before the three-day entry lock.
3. **Escalating turn one:** Irix builds two embermoss-and-hardware creatures.
   Irix supplies the initial living magic that shapes their growth; once
   established, they metabolize food and act through physical mechanisms, not
   independent spellwork. Knurl and Thimble choose names, preferences, and
   changes to Irix's visual relay. When Knurl refuses to be picked up, Irix is
   irritated, then asks. The first requested collaboration succeeds better
   than Irix's solo design.
4. **Escalating turn two:** On exhibition day, Talvi has not arranged the
   promised conveyance and Belphegor has not woken. Irix expects to miss the
   deadline. Knurl and Thimble reject being packed as equipment and propose,
   through tiles and diagrams, turning the exhibit's own vibration-sensing
   chassis into a cart. Irix trusts their alteration. The three reach school
   under their own power, but Irix's resentment toward the parents hardens.
5. **Escalating turn three / causal hinge:** Irix's presentation impresses the
   room because Knurl and Thimble use the visual relay to disagree with Irix,
   revise a pattern, and solve an audience challenge as partners. Nearby, Ekri
   forces a showy final flourish. A poorly seated latch releases the furnace
   feed; sound-only bells warn most students, while Irix's pressure strips show
   that the standard exit will soon be crossed by ember spray. Bexel begins the
   usual evacuation, which would send students into the hazard.
6. **Crisis and climax:** Irix can protect the nearly winning exhibit and obey
   the evacuation, or dismantle it for the parts needed to expose a safer route.
   They break their own scoring seal and project a signed, color-coded plan.
   Bexel and Ekri follow it. Irix tells Knurl and Thimble to stay back, revealing
   the old control reflex. Both refuse: Knurl opens the jammed cold-air shutter
   while Thimble spreads a sail over the ember spray. Their choices, Irix's
   sensor design, Ekri's power cutoff, and Bexel's authority together clear the
   room. Irix retrieves scorched Thimble instead of chasing the prize bell.
7. **Resonant aftermath:** Bexel declares the score void but admits the rescue
   demonstrated advanced-workshop aptitude and real collaboration. Knurl and
   Thimble are credited as living team members, not Irix's equipment; visual
   alarms and an accessible teaming rule become local school policy. At home,
   Irix tells Talvi and Belphegor that being loved is not the same as being
   supported. The parents do not ask Irix to solve them: Talvi arranges standing
   adult-managed transport, Belphegor creates the couch consequence, and both
   sign an agreement making Bexel a backup contact with Irix's consent. The
   next school morning, Irix asks Knurl and Thimble whether they want to come.
   Each chooses yes in a different way. Irix enters with friends, not products.

## Setup and payoff

- **Wrong-corner signature:** Belphegor sleepily signs an ornamental corner
  instead of the guardian line. Irix can repair the form, but the error proves
  affection is not completed care. The ending's correctly witnessed support
  agreement reverses it without asking Irix to supervise.
- **Four-handed signing:** Belphegor's elegant signing shows that Irix's family
  shares an accessible language. The problem is follow-through, not a lack of
  love or inability to communicate.
- **Sound-only bell:** An exhibition bell is visible early only because hearing
  students react to it while Irix waits for a visual cue. During the crisis,
  this access failure creates a dangerous split; Bexel's new visual-alarm rule
  pays it off.
- **Pressure strips and color grammar:** Irix first builds them to translate
  vibration, temperature, and load into readable light. In the climax, the same
  grammar becomes a projected evacuation map peers can follow.
- **Consent before handling:** Knurl first stiffens and refuses when Irix grabs
  their frame. Irix learns to ask. During the rescue, Irix repeats the control
  mistake by ordering both friends to safety, then accepts their informed
  refusals.
- **Thimble's sail:** Introduced as an unauthorized aesthetic flourish and
  communication surface; used to shield students from ember spray. The sail is
  scorched and later patched visibly rather than replaced without consequence.
- **Knurl's appetite for alignment:** Initially comic, then useful when Knurl
  spots and opens a crooked cold-air shutter others overlook.
- **Walking chassis:** Built from the exhibit after the parents fail to provide
  transport. Its parts are sacrificed at the climax, making the solution seeded
  and the cost material.
- **Scoring seal:** Bexel warns that breaking it voids judging. Irix deliberately
  breaks it for rescue parts, so formal victory cannot be the climax's reward.
- **Parents' promises:** Both make loving but uncompleted promises twice. The
  ending supplies adult-owned standing transport, an accountable school
  contact, and Belphegor's self-imposed couch consequence instead of a third
  promise.
- **`friends?`:** Irix first offers the word as a question after the constructs
  show agency. The final invitation to school is also a question; two distinct
  affirmative choices complete the change from ownership to reciprocity.

## Canon constraints

- Treat the infernal locale as one cultural experience within the universal
  afterlife, never the exhaustive form of afterlife, Hell, or damnation
  (`universe/rules.md` — `Afterlife and continuation`).
- Do not import real-world lore about Belphegor. The story establishes only
  their prompt-required archdemon title, place in this family, local capabilities,
  pronouns, and extreme inertia.
- Never call Belphegor a god. If another character exaggerates, the narration
  must not endorse godhood without worship, power, and authority
  (`universe/rules.md` — `Divinity`).
- Irix supplies living magical agency during the embermoss shaping. Knurl and
  Thimble are established as alive through growth, metabolism, preference,
  refusal, learning, vulnerability, and reciprocal choice. Their independent
  actions use bodies and mechanisms, not unexplained spellwork
  (`universe/rules.md` — `Living agency and magic`).
- Construction never licenses ownership of a person. Preserve Knurl and
  Thimble's consent, relationships, distinct preferences, consequences, and
  continuity after injury (`universe/style-guide.md` —
  `Personhood and moral consequence`).
- Keep the couch binding, school craft, furnace, visual relay, embermoss
  shaping, and demon rank local. Magic and technology remain viewpoint labels,
  not universal kinds (`universe/rules.md` — `System categories`;
  `Costs, desire, and local mechanisms`).
- Do not attach inherent power to spoken, written, or signed names. The friends'
  naming is social self-definition, not universal name-magic
  (`universe/rules.md` — `Names and identity binding`).
- All consequential communication remains signed, written, diagrammed, or
  visibly enacted. Irix never hears or speaks, and the narration never makes
  hearing absence a magical detection power.
- Maintain PG-13, non-graphic peril and avoid lore exposition that displaces
  the school and family story (`universe/style-guide.md` —
  `Audience and content boundaries`; `Prose defaults`).

## Proposed inventions

- **Irix, Talvi, and this local version of Belphegor:** One mixed infernal family
  with story-specific pronouns, relationships, abilities, communication, and
  support arrangements. It establishes no general rule for demonic parenthood,
  inheritance, disability, damned souls, or archdemons.
- **The Cinder Annex:** One bounded infernal secondary school with an Aptitudes
  Exhibition, advanced workshop, furnace hall, initially inadequate visual
  alarms, and local procedures. It is not the school for all infernal children.
- **Embermoss construction:** A local craft in which a living maker shapes a
  cultured organism through frames and supplied magic. The two resulting
  beings' life and personhood are demonstrated individually; the technique is
  not a universal route to minds or souls.
- **Knurl and Thimble:** Two distinct living constructed people with the bodies,
  preferences, communication, limitations, relationships, and choices needed
  for this story. Their eventual relationship to the afterlife is unstated.
- **Irix's visual relay:** A local sensor-and-projection system mapping physical
  measures and signed diagrams into colored light. It is a communication and
  safety tool, not a cure or translator that makes signing valid.
- **Ekri's exhibition device and the furnace failure:** One living-agent-powered
  school apparatus with a mechanical latch flaw and defined physical
  consequences. It establishes no broad rule about infernal magic.
- **Belphegor's couch consequence:** A narrow, self-imposed furniture binding
  powered by Belphegor. It demonstrates one capability, not universal contract
  magic, name-power, or authority over others.
- **Local reforms:** Bexel's later recognition of living constructs as team
  members, visual-alarm requirement, accessible teaming rule, and backup-contact
  role apply to this school and family unless promoted more broadly.

## Failure modes to watch

- **Disability-as-deficit:** Never imply that Irix's silence means passivity,
  that deafness creates intellectual isolation, or that a voice/hearing device
  is needed for a complete life.
- **Compensatory superpower:** Irix notices the crisis because they designed
  attentive multi-sensory instrumentation and are looking at it, not because
  deaf people possess supernatural perception.
- **Invisible dialogue:** In Irix's first-person POV, every received line needs
  a visible channel. Do not narrate unheard offstage speech or identify sound
  without vibration, light, another person's reaction, or later explanation.
- **Translation as validation:** The visual relay supports diagrams and safety;
  it must not turn signing into spoken language for others' convenience.
- **Instant personhood proof:** Establish Knurl and Thimble across several
  behaviors—growth, hunger, preference, refusal, learning, risk, and relationship.
  Do not rely on one cute movement or Irix's assertion.
- **Owned friends:** Irix may create their bodies but cannot own their choices.
  Keep the naming, handling, exhibition, rescue, and final invitation
  explicitly reciprocal.
- **Disposable construct:** Thimble's scorching must matter. Show pain or
  distress non-graphically, careful treatment, a visible patch, and continuity;
  do not swap in an identical replacement.
- **Magic-user violation:** Knurl and Thimble do not cast spells. Their
  autonomous feats use levers, frames, bodies, flags, and learned coordination.
- **Parents excused by charm:** Their affection and comedy cannot erase the
  missed signature, failed transport, or accumulated burden on Irix.
- **Child repairs the adults:** Irix states needs and consents to arrangements,
  but Talvi, Belphegor, and Bexel must perform and maintain the new adult-owned
  systems.
- **Unseeded rescue:** Seed the scoring seal, visual pressure map, cold-air
  shutter, Thimble's sail, furnace latch, and unsafe standard exit before the
  climax uses them.
- **Solo-genius climax:** Irix diagnoses and communicates; Knurl, Thimble, Ekri,
  and Bexel each contribute an indispensable choice or authorized action.
- **Popularity as friendship:** Admiration and workshop access may follow, but
  the emotional resolution is the friends' freely chosen return and Irix's
  willingness to ask.
- **Universalized Hell:** Avoid statements about what all damned souls, demons,
  schools, or afterlife regions are like.
- **Name drift:** Do not add pet names, epithets, usernames, capitalized roles,
  or separate sign-names without a new registry check.
- **Weak family ending:** Do not end on apology alone. Put the standing
  conveyance, responsible adult backup, signed agreement, and self-imposed
  couch mechanism on the page.
