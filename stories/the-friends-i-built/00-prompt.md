# Prompt contract

## Verbatim writing prompt

> [WP] You are the child of a damned soul and the archdemon Belphegor. You are deaf and non-speaking. Unfortunately for you, your parents, while loving, are extremely lazy. So you use your exceptional aptitude and intelligent to create your own friends and impress others at school with your skills

## Story controls

- Working title: The Friends I Built
- Target length: 3,000–3,800 words
- POV: First person, centered entirely on the child
- Tense: Past
- Tone and genre: Warmly macabre infernal-school fantasy with dry comedy, emotional sincerity, and a constructive coming-of-age arc
- Audience/content rating: Teen / PG-13; mild infernal imagery and school peril, no graphic violence, sexual content, or slurs
- Required elements: A child of one damned soul and the archdemon Belphegor; a protagonist who is deaf and non-speaking; loving parents whose extreme laziness creates a real practical obstacle; the protagonist's exceptional aptitude and intelligence; self-created friends who become dramatically important; a school setting where the protagonist's skills impress others; communication depicted as capable and socially meaningful; a complete emotional and causal resolution
- Prohibited elements: A cure for deafness or non-speaking status; speech or hearing as the reward or climax; disability equated with inferiority, evil, isolation, or lack of intelligence; the created friends reduced to disposable tools; parental neglect excused solely as comedy; canon promotion without explicit user approval

## Assumptions

- "Intelligent" in the prompt is interpreted as "intelligence" while the verbatim wording remains unchanged above.
- The protagonist is a secondary-school-aged student with a rich interior voice who communicates through sign, writing, expression, and constructed visual media.
- The damned-soul parent and Belphegor both genuinely love their child, but their supernatural inertia makes them unreliable at sustained practical effort.
- The protagonist initially builds companions partly from loneliness and partly from curiosity; the story will test whether designed companionship can become reciprocal friendship.
- The school is a local, bounded institution that may be introduced as a story-scale invention if canon research finds room for it.
- Any depiction of Belphegor will respect authoritative universe cosmology if it exists; otherwise the story will avoid declaring broad global rules about demons or Hell.
- The protagonist's creations may be person-like and therefore must receive character-facing names only after registry validation.

## Completion tests

- The result is a complete short story, not a synopsis or chapter fragment.
- The central promise of the prompt is dramatized and resolved.
- The protagonist solves the central problem through engineering, judgment, and collaboration rather than suddenly speaking or hearing.
- The created friends demonstrate agency, mutuality, and story consequence.
- The parents' love and harmful inertia are both dramatized, and the family reaches a concrete new accommodation rather than a sentimental reset.
- The school climax makes the protagonist's skill legible to peers without reducing friendship to applause or status.
- The story passes canon and continuity review.
- Every character-facing name and alias is checked against `stories/NAMES.md`;
  any deliberate reuse has a documented narrative reason.
