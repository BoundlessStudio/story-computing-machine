# Opus review: A Bargain with Anubis

Independent audit for a later editing pass. It is not a `review.md` verdict,
does not change canon state, and does not authorize edits by itself.

## Verdict

- Dialogue: REVISE — one Major: the gate scene closes on its oath, then becomes a lore Q&A with an "as you know" line and a repeated rule (L123–L143). Every other exchange holds up line by line.
- Prose: PASS — concrete and restrained, with no em dashes and varied paragraphs. Three glosses near the end and one garbled referent are polish.
- Continuity and prompt: PASS — every prompt element lands and the proposal is never heard by the father. About 3,400 words, non-graphic. Two small staging and timing blurs.
- Edit scope: targeted-dialogue
- Existing review.md said: Verdict PASS, "Dialogue: PASS"; I agree on nearly everything but disagree that the gate-scene lore block passes.

Unlike most of the collection, this story's dialogue mostly works, because the three speakers are different people. Anubis is formal, uses no contractions, offers no comfort, and answers exactly what he is asked. Khaseb gives orders, fusses over her, teases her to get a smile, and hears recovery in everything she does. Taremet speaks in short, breath-limited lines, keeps asking about other people's suffering, and hides her intentions inside true complaints ("The fastening at my neck. It's rubbing."). The sickroom runs on subtext, and the climax is carried by action, silence, and the father's misreading rather than by speeches. The one real failure comes at the end of the gate conversation. After the promise lands, the narration says the prayer is finished, and then Taremet turns into a questioner so that Anubis can explain the mechanism. The explanation includes a line telling her something she already knows and repeats a rule he stated four lines earlier. The edit pass should trim and re-sequence that block, fix a few small wording and staging slips, and otherwise leave the dialogue alone. Do not flatten Anubis's formality or Khaseb's jokes into the house register.

## Dialogue findings

### D1 · Major · Lore Q&A appended after the oath

- Where: L123–L143, end of the gate scene, straight after "By the office in which I receive your dead, I promise."
- Text:
> The prayer she had come to make was finished. Now she had to get back to her room.
> Why can't you take it from him?
> Your father stole it during my vigil for Egypt.
> He has trapped my power there. I must hold the whole jaw to release it.
- Problem: Exposition and explanatory chorus.
  - The narration closes the scene. Then Taremet asks four questions in a row: why he can't take it, whether breaking it would end this, what it is, and whether he is keeping them here. Their job is to let Anubis brief the reader.
  - "Your father stole it..." tells her what she already knows. At L197 she learns that he carried the relic from the shrine himself.
  - "I must hold the whole jaw to release it" repeats what L131 already established ("I would have to find and join them").
  - The result is that the scene's strongest beat, the oath, is followed by a flat briefing.
  - The breaking threat is the reason Anubis needs her at all, but it arrives only after the deal is sealed. So her offer at L49 is unexplained when she makes it.
- Fix: The smallest version has three steps.
  1. Move "She shut her eyes briefly. The prayer she had come to make was finished. Now she had to get back to her room." to just after "I will wait." (L149), or cut it.
  2. Cut "Your father stole it during my vigil for Egypt." from L137. Keep the description and the breath mechanism, which the climax needs.
  3. Cut the second sentence of L141, so that his whole reply to "So even now you're keeping us here?" is the defensive "He has trapped my power there." with his fingers tightening on the rail.

  Optional, for stronger causality: move the L125–L133 block ("Why can't you take it from him?" through the messengers paragraph) up to just after Anubis looks toward the open doors (L47). Then "I can get him to show it to me" answers the threat instead of coming before it. Either way, keep "So even now you're keeping us here?", because it is Taremet's one pointed line to the god. Do not add any new explanatory lines.

### D2 · Minor · "He'll think" reads as if he will find out

- Where: L101–L103, gate scene, after "And if he asks you?"
- Text:
> I won't tell him.
> He'll think I didn't trust him to find something.
- Problem: Ground/uptake slip. She has just promised that he will never know, but "He'll think" states the thought as a future fact. For a beat she seems to predict that he will find out. The line is really a hypothetical: what he would think if he knew.
- Fix: Change it to "He'd think I didn't trust him to find something." Keep the rest of the speech.

## Prose findings

### P1 · Minor · Narration explains what the dialogue already showed

- Where: L237 (relic request), L375 and L385 (aftermath)
- Text:
> as if her curiosity too were evidence
> She wished she could stop being frightened of what she had asked him to permit.
> She had meant to leave him a daughter who wanted to stay. Now she had to let him keep believing he had almost saved her.
- Problem: Each of these states subtext that the dialogue has already delivered.
  - "Yesterday you wouldn't look out of the window" already shows Khaseb reading her curiosity as recovery.
  - "She was getting well." and "Tell him you went out tonight." already show that he believes he almost saved her.
  - L385 is the story's only thesis sentence, and it sits just before the final exchange. Its first sentence is abstract enough to need rereading.
  - "What she had asked him to permit" (L375) is similarly abstract.
- Fix:
  - Cut the first two sentences of L385, so the paragraph starts at "He bent his head to hear what she might say next."
  - In L375, keep "She wished he would go." and either cut the second sentence or make it concrete.
  - The L237 clause is optional. To tighten, end that sentence at "smiling now."

### P2 · Minor · Garbled referent in the knucklebones aside

- Where: L223, after "You've never needed my help with that."
- Text:
> long after she had forgotten their victories
- Problem: "their" has no sensible antecedent (the throws? the two of them?). The sentence means that he remembered her bad throws long after she had forgotten her wins.
- Fix: Change it to "long after she had forgotten her own wins", or similar.

## Continuity and prompt findings

### C1 · Minor · Father's position before the handover is not placed

- Where: L275–L293, sickroom, from "You were cold" to "The fastening at my neck"
- Text:
> He saw her face and knelt beside the bed.
> He kissed her fingers. The relic lay beneath them, wrapped and within reach.
> He sat on the mattress to her left and helped her upright.
- Problem: Her hand is still on the cloth under the jaw (L257), and the jaw is on the table at her right. Kissing those fingers therefore puts Khaseb on the side with the table and screen, at the moment Anubis's shadow crosses the doorway. A few lines later he sits on the mattress to her left, and no move is shown. At the scene's most dangerous moment, the reader loses track of where the father is.
- Fix: Place him with one clause. One option is to have him kneel by the table and then get up and sit on her left at L293. The other is to have him kneel on her left and kiss her free hand while her right hand stays on the cloth, and then reword "The relic lay beneath them" to match.

### C2 · Minor · Messengers "all afternoon" while he slept; purpose unclear

- Where: L133, gate scene, between the breaking threat and "What is it?"
- Text:
> Her father had sent messengers on fast horses all afternoon. She had heard their hooves while he slept in the chair beside her.
- Problem: The paragraph follows "send the pieces away", so it seems meant to show that the threat is practical. But it never says what the riders were for: physicians, the temple, or relays ready to carry the pieces. "Sent ... all afternoon" also sits awkwardly with "while he slept". The reader can't tell whether the paragraph confirms the threat.
- Fix: One option is to make the link concrete and fix the timing: for example, riders kept waiting in the courtyard since he took the jaw, whose hooves she heard before he dozed. The other is to cut the paragraph, because L265 ("If he wants it whole, he can wait until you're well.") already confirms the threat.
