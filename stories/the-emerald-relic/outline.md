# Outline

## Story

- Premise and central promise: **A Bargain with Anubis.** Dying Taremet offers Anubis her father's stolen Emerald Relic if he conceals her assistance forever. Obtaining it requires exploiting her father's trust: a private betrayal whose kindness remains uncertain.
- Focal pressure or attachment: Taremet, twenty-two, wants her failing body to finish dying, but cannot bear her father remembering her as someone who chose to leave him. She still wants his hands supporting her head.
- Counterforce or complication: Pharaoh Khaseb mistakes three survived nights for recovery. He treasures their competitiveness at knucklebones and keeps suggesting another game. Anubis needs his instrument intact; a threatened king could scatter its parts beyond immediate recovery.
- POV, distance, and information limit: Close third past through Taremet's body, observations, and memories. Other intentions require disclosure. Anubis remains difficult to read, never a narrator of universal truth.
- Governing movement and time shape: One night into morning: gate bargain, sickroom, theft within an embrace, consequences. Expand the unequal encounters; target 2,500–4,000 words.
- Speculative rule or ordinary-world constraint: The stolen jaw retains a working supplied by living Anubis; removal has trapped his sustaining power across Egypt. Dying is suspended without healing. He must recover its joined parts to release that power safely. No separate exemption exists for Taremet; restoration permits ordinary deaths, without killing healthy creatures or repairing damage.
- Dialogue promise: A daughter asks an unfamiliar god for what she cannot ask her loving father, then exploits that father's attentive listening.
- Dialogic medium: Private speech, interrupted breathing, habitual caregiving, and an agreed handover gesture.
- Dialogue engine: The bargain determines access and concealment. Her requests expose the relic; Khaseb's loving responses make using that opportunity harder.

## Voice

- Narrative texture: Follow the labor of swallowing, lifting, tolerating cloth. Gold becomes heavy; emerald light exposes what lamplight concealed. Longer perceptions contract when breath fails.
- Conversational texture: Wary, incomplete gate conversation gives way to sickroom irritation, practical requests, familiar teasing that sometimes fails, and fatigued repetitions.
- Rhetorical ownership: Taremet knows her symptoms and her father's evasions, not divine mechanics. Khaseb converts fear into solicitous offers and old competitive jokes. Anubis answers the question actually asked, retains ceremonial authority, and offers no therapeutic interpretation.
- Pressure behavior: Taremet hides intentions inside truthful discomfort. Khaseb hears improvement wherever possible; betrayal reduces his fluent reassurance to demands. Anubis's silences can withhold information rather than signal wisdom.
- Relationship movement: She wants Anubis's discretion and Khaseb's love without asking Khaseb to endure her choice. Khaseb wants their shared pleasures returned. Anubis wants access without promising absolution. Cooperation binds daughter and god while turning the father's trust against him.
- Anti-default: No companionable Anubis or lucid paternal acceptance speech. End within the altered relationship, without institutional reform or an explanatory epilogue.

## Beats

1. **Waiting outside protection.** Taremet orders her litter to the palace gates while Khaseb sleeps. Dismiss attendants beyond hearing. Establish breathlessness, an injured locust still moving in the cloth, and the slaughter yard's continuing cries. Reports supply field damage and temple petitions. Anubis approaches from those petitioners: black jackal face, gold regalia, living warmth.
2. **The proposal.** She can persuade Khaseb to produce the hidden jaw. Anubis must never disclose her participation, even to her father after death. Her wish to protect Khaseb competes with fear of being hated. Anubis accepts concealment, without promising manageable grief or survival. Establish his waiting place outside her garden doorway and a handover signal.
3. **Being cared for.** Khaseb reproaches her excursion while settling her aching legs. Their old game intrudes. She asks to see what saved her. He retrieves the black jaw; emerald light fills gold-lined fractures. His delight nearly defeats her. His precautions establish why force would have endangered recovery.
4. **Borrowed trust.** She leaves the screened garden doorway unlatched and asks Khaseb to ease her chafing collar. While he supports her, she signals and slides the jaw from the bedside into Anubis's hands behind the screen. Establish weight, reach, sightlines, and exhaustion; no miraculous strength. Her faltering movement requires her father's unwitting help repositioning her.
5. **What he sees.** Anubis emerges holding the relic. Khaseb believes the god exploited his daughter's helplessness. Taremet could confess; she maintains the bargain. Anubis releases the working. The injured locust dies, establishing restoration before the emotional climax. Taremet remains alive long enough to feel her father's changed attention and her own fear.
6. **Keeping the secret.** Khaseb demands, then pleads with Anubis while holding her. The god's refusal to explain fulfills the proposal at its hardest point. Allow Taremet a final affectionate attempt at contact, without confession or thematic statement. End with her death and Khaseb's unresolved accusation, without an afterlife reunion or assurance that she chose correctly.
