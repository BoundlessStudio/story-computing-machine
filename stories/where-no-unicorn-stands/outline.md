# Outline

## Story

- Proposed reader-facing title: *Where No Unicorn Stands*.
- Premise and central promise: Three days after Queen Adessra's coronation, her adult only son, Prince Cadris, is snatched from a palace terrace by Pegasi and carried to their migratory kingdom in the clouds. Flight-Captain Vorune demands the return of Iltan, her adult daughter, whom the previous unicorn king imprisoned nine months earlier as a spy. The cloud kingdom will ride its seasonal wind beyond the coast by the next night; Vorune believes an exchanged royal hostage is the only demand the lowland crown cannot bury. Fulfill the abduction promise through Cadris's disorienting passage into a culture built for wings, his discovery that the grievance is real without making the kidnapping rightful, and a private resolution at the kingdom's underside: Adessra frees Iltan without making her the price of her son; Cadris gives up an available escape rather than destroy the updraft Iltan needs to come home; and Vorune releases him when the symmetry by which she justified holding him no longer exists.
- Intended reader experience: Begin with ceremonial poise broken by sudden open-air terror. Sustain vertigo and wonder as Cadris is moved through a beautiful city whose floors cannot hold him, then let anger at the kidnapping coexist with recognition of an inherited injustice below. The exchange should tighten around four people and two damaged parent-child bonds, not swell into a tribunal. End with bodily relief, exhaustion, and political trouble still ahead rather than easy reconciliation between kingdoms.
- Generating force:
  - Mode: Plot-led one-way vertical transit. Every move from the lowland terrace to the cloud crown, working layers, and underside rain dock changes what Cadris can see of the Pegasi, the prisoner below, and his own value as a royal hostage.
  - Focal attachment or contradiction: Cadris loves Adessra as his mother but has barely learned how to read her as queen; he wants her to choose him immediately and also cannot bear to become the first proof that her new reign can be ruled through threats. Vorune loves Iltan and is right that the old crown made her disappear, but turns that love into retaliatory arithmetic: one sovereign's child held for another.
  - Plot-led wants and useful limit, if applicable: Cadris immediately wants solid ground, freedom, and a way to warn Adessra without scripting her capitulation. He also wants to protect her fragile reign and learn whether Iltan is real before condemning or defending the crown. His useful flaw is ceremonial stillness: trained to hide fear and wait for others to declare themselves, he initially treats silence as control, conceals disabling vertigo, and assumes royal records he has never seen must justify the imprisonment. His physical limit is sharper in the cloud kingdom: without wings, he sinks through worked cloud unless a nearby Pegasus maintains a bearing wake through the harness around him.
- Counterforce or complication:
  - Source: Vorune is the active antagonist, supported by the deadline and an environment lethal to an unborne unicorn. The cloud kingdom's annual migration cannot be stopped for a private rescue; by moonrise it must take the sea wind or scatter its households across a year of uncertain weather. Its last low pass over the unicorn realm is the only practical meeting point.
  - Credible inside logic, if a person: Vorune spent nine months sending petitions and maps that the former king's officers returned unopened. She believes Adessra's coronation changes the face above the same sealed cells, not the system, and she fears that waiting will strand Iltan below for another year. She keeps Cadris fed, warm, and continuously borne because a live hostage matters and because she does not want him dead, but she does not disguise the abduction as hospitality. When challenged, she protects her daughter, her flight's safe departure, and her standing as captain; even after Iltan returns, she is tempted to retain Cadris for an amnesty and formal concessions.
  - Pressure or change created: Cadris cannot run across a floor that will not bear him, and forcing or tricking Vorune to break the bearing wake would drop him through miles of cloud. Near the low pass, an emergency rainwell offers a dangerous escape: opening its sluice would carry him toward the mountain in a survivable torrent, but it would exhaust the only rising current by which a weakened Iltan could reach the kingdom. Escape, the exchange, and the kingdom's departure therefore compete for the same narrow interval.
- Narrative design:
  - POV, tense, distance, and limitation: Close third past through Cadris, intimate with his vertigo, rehearsed court judgments, and need to imagine what Adessra will choose. He knows coronation protocol but not the old prison system, Pegasi domestic life, Vorune's private decisions, or events on the ground. Keep Adessra exterior until the reunion. Vorune speaks in flight times, weights, and balanced obligations when she is frightened; Cadris retreats into formal titles and careful silence; Iltan is exact about what happened to her and refuses both families' attempts to turn her into evidence of their goodness.
  - Time shape and compression: Roughly thirty-six continuous hours, from the third morning after the coronation to the cloud kingdom's moonrise departure. Render the abduction, first unsupported step, rain-message, rainwell choice, Iltan's ascent, and Cadris's descent in scene; compress the long climb and the kingdom's routine migration through concrete glimpses as he is carried downward from tier to tier.
  - Information and reveal strategy: State Vorune's demand and deadline during the ascent rather than treating motive as a late twist. Let Cadris doubt Iltan's existence until Vorune supplies a broken map case, the date and pass where she vanished, and a prison docket bearing the former king's seal—evidence of a specific captive, not proof that every Pegasi claim is true. A brief message written upward through rain says only that Adessra has found Iltan and will bring her to the low pass; it promises no exchange, keeping the queen's choice unreadable. Reveal at the rain dock, through Iltan herself, that Adessra opened the cell and released her before asking anything for Cadris.
  - Governing movement: Hold a post-coronation audience on the palace terrace; let the Pegasi strike through the ceremonial banners and lift Cadris before guards can close the roof; cross cold open air in a cargo sling while Vorune names Iltan and the deadline; enter the cloud kingdom at its bright upper crown; have Cadris step onto apparently solid cloud and plunge to his knees until Vorune catches him in a bearing wake; move through weather workshops and family terraces rather than a throne room, showing ordinary Pegasi preparing an entire household migration around the hostage route; inspect Iltan's map case and the old seal; send one constrained rain-message proving Cadris alive; receive Adessra's spare answer that Iltan has been found; descend toward the underside rain dock as the sea wind begins to take the city; discover the emergency sluice and understand that it could carry him down; hear a lone, faltering wingbeat rising inside the rainwell; choose not to empty it; help keep the sluice stable while Iltan climbs the last span; let mother and daughter reunite without making their embrace absolve Vorune; learn Iltan was freed without conditions and hear her refuse to be exchanged; force Vorune to decide whether her demand was rescue or possession; have her renew Cadris's bearing wake and personally lower him to the mountain rather than cutting him loose; reunite mother and son after the cloud kingdom has already begun to depart.
- Speculative surplus: Pegasi bakers beat warm updraft into cloud-grain dough with their wings. Fresh loaves drift toward the ceiling unless cooled under nets or weighted with glaze, so kitchens use overhead baskets and children are sent to retrieve escaped rolls. This living, domestic wing-work gives the cloud kingdom material life and minor inconvenience but cannot support Cadris, carry messages, expose lies, or solve the hostage exchange.
- Structural distinction: A one-way rise followed by a tiered descent, with the setting steadily opening from royal terrace to exposed sky while the human scale contracts from two kingdoms to two parents and their adult children. The climax occurs at an underside service dock, not a court, battlefield, public emergency, or place of technical proof. There is no escape-attempt cycle, crowd conversion, institutional rewrite, species-wide peace, ceremonial treaty, or returning gesture from the departing Pegasi; the resolution rests on two private refusals to use another person as the cost of freedom.
- Decisive turn, deepening, or recognition: At the rain dock, Cadris can pull the emergency sluice and ride the released water toward Adessra's mountain signal. Then he hears an uneven wingbeat inside the shaft and understands that the same stored updraft is carrying Iltan home after months without room to fly. Vorune has made him a hostage because her daughter was treated as one; Cadris refuses to complete that symmetry by spending Iltan's route on himself. He steps away from the sluice and calls Vorune to strengthen the current. When Iltan arrives and says Adessra freed her before bargaining, Vorune must confront that continuing to hold Cadris would protect neither daughter nor kingdom. She releases him while preserving the right to seek justice by other means.
- Aftereffect or live uncertainty: Cadris returns physically unharmed but cannot dismiss the former crown's secret imprisonment or forget how completely the cloud kingdom controlled his body. Adessra has recovered her son and corrected one inherited wrong, yet her unconditional release may be attacked as weakness, the kidnapping still demands an answer, and neither realm has agreed on borders, spying, restitution, or future contact. Vorune keeps her daughter and her command only provisionally; Iltan is home but angry that rescue was carried out in her name without her consent. No friendship or peace is promised.
- Opening / ending relation: Open with Cadris standing perfectly still beside Adessra while courtiers praise the new reign's steadiness, moments before the sky takes his footing away. End on real mountain stone with both of them shaking—Cadris from the descent, Adessra from an all-night climb—as he asks why she freed Iltan before she had him back. Her answer is practical and personal rather than proclamatory; he takes her weight for the walk down, and the story stops before either has to perform certainty for the waiting court.

## Beats

1. Three mornings after the coronation, Cadris stands beside Adessra through a terrace audience, quietly resenting that being her only son has made him a visible assurance of dynastic stability; Pegasi tear through the hanging banners, wrap him in a cargo sling, and carry him into open air.
2. During the freezing ascent, Vorune names Iltan, the former king's nine-month imprisonment, and her demand. Cadris denies knowledge and tries to sound authoritative while hiding nausea and terror.
3. At the cloud kingdom's upper crown, Cadris tests the shining floor and sinks through it until Vorune catches him. She fits him with a wake-bearing harness and makes its limit unmistakable: distance from a living Pegasus means a fall.
4. Vorune moves him downward through workshops and family terraces preparing for migration. Cadris sees no assembled enemy court, only residents tying down possessions, catching floating bread, and making room for an unwanted groundborn body.
5. In a weather-map room, Vorune gives him Iltan's broken case and the former king's sealed docket. Cadris recognizes a valid crown seal and must separate ignorance from innocence.
6. Cadris dictates a proof-of-life message into a Pegasi-made rain channel. He rejects Vorune's ransom script but includes Iltan's cell mark and the migration deadline, giving Adessra information without telling her what she must trade.
7. The rain returns one terse answer: Iltan has been found and will be brought to the last low pass. Cadris cannot tell whether Adessra means to exchange her, arrest Vorune, or sacrifice him to avoid rewarding abduction.
8. As the kingdom descends toward moonrise, Cadris notices the emergency sluice at the underside rain dock and learns that opening it would create one falling path to the mountain while destroying the stored lift below.
9. Left within reach of the release while Vorune answers a migration alarm, Cadris prepares to pull it. An exhausted wingbeat rises through the shaft; he realizes Iltan is using the lift and backs away, then calls for Vorune instead of escaping.
10. Iltan reaches the dock, embraces Vorune, and immediately establishes her own account: Adessra found the hidden cell, opened it without conditions, and told her she was not a price. She thanks Cadris for preserving the updraft but refuses to validate either crown.
11. Vorune tries to add amnesty and concessions before releasing Cadris. Iltan asks whether her mother rescued her or merely replaced her below. Cadris does not plead or threaten; he demands that Vorune decide without calling him an exchange already paid.
12. Vorune carries Cadris down in the bearing harness and sets him on the mountain before the city takes the sea wind. She leaves with Iltan without apology or treaty.
13. Cadris and Adessra reunite privately. He asks the question that survived the descent, supports his exhausted mother on the path down, and returns toward a court where the kidnapping and the hidden cell will have consequences neither can settle tonight.

## Continuity

- Canon used: The story occupies one unnamed era of the same deep-time physical world, with no required awareness of other eras. Pegasi bearing wakes, rain channels, and baking practices are distinct local systems supplied by living wing-work; harnesses, cloud structures, and kitchen nets only hold or shape that supplied effect and do not act as magic users.
- Boundaries and unknowns: Unicorns, Pegasi, their two local monarchies, the prison grievance, and cloud migration are new and do not establish universal species biology, royal law, cloud physics, or a connection to any existing queen, monarchy, dragon, crossing, weather system, or named era. Whether later contact produces restitution, punishment, war, or diplomacy remains outside this story.
