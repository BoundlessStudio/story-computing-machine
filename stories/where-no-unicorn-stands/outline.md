# Outline

## Story

- Premise and central promise: Days after his mother's coronation, an unnamed unicorn prince is seized and carried to the Pegasi cloud kingdom, where no surface bears him. His captors demand that he undo the coronation's claim on their storm. Resolve his kidnapping, return, and choice about helping people who used his body as leverage.
- Focal pressure or attachment: He wants solid ground, his mother, and proof that her crown has not reduced him to an heir-token. Refusal feels like his last possession, yet every drink and doorway requires a Pegasus to hold him while their homes visibly fail.
- Counterforce or complication: The flight-captain believes ground royalty answers only to leverage. The regent condemns the seizure but cannot ignore the collapsing city. The queen rooted the storm to relieve drought, unaware it was inhabited; releasing it at once would flood her valley. Urgency excuses neither coercion nor abandonment.
- POV, distance, and information limit: Close first-person past, body-near to straps, breath, vertigo, and absent footing. The prince knows unicorn ceremony, not wing-signs or cloud engineering. He cannot know whether warnings reached his mother; competing claims remain unconfirmed until her actions at the exchange ridge.
- Governing movement and time shape: Thirty-six hours as five supports—capture net, audience sling, maintenance cradle, captain's back, mountain turf. Each handoff changes custody and access to speech. The climax travels around the storm's descending edge; end in a rough shelter immediately after his return, before ceremony or settlement.
- Speculative rule or ordinary-world constraint: Pegasus wing-wakes hold worked cloud in tension, but ground creatures fall through it. A crowned unicorn can root one storm for nine days; her direct descendant can loosen the fresh claim. One cut means collapse and flood, while three light releases along moving seams spread the rain. Pegasi must carry the freely consenting prince to each seam.

## Voice

- Narrative texture: Narrate by testing support—strap pressure, cloud through hooves, altered wingbeats—not by admiring sky vistas. Defensive accounting gradually yields to distinctions among captors without softening the abduction.
- Conversational texture: Embed talk in harness checks, meals, and noisy flight. Mix partial answers and repeated instructions with ordinary concern about chafing, hunger, and tired wings.
- Rhetorical ownership: The prince reasons through promises, becoming ceremonial when cornered. The captain uses routes, loads, and deadlines but cannot fluently own guilt. The regent asks careful authority questions. The harness-keeper owns material precision, not politics; the queen speaks through decisions already visible.
- Pressure behavior: Fear makes the prince literal, the captain retreat into commands, the regent terse, and the harness-keeper directive. Missed listening changes the next maneuver.
- Anti-default: Avoid a chorus of poised diplomats and lore speeches. Let changed custody and coordinated action resolve pressure, without a treaty recital, apology sequence, or final maxim.

## Beats

1. Wake the prince midair in a cargo net. Cloud passes through his testing hoof; only the net arrests his fall. The captain gives safety commands but no motive, joining coercion to dependence.
2. At the cloud court, the platform fails under him. The regent orders a sling and food, condemns the unauthorized seizure, and shows lower homes losing substance. The captain demands leverage against the coronation claim; nobody can prove what the queen knew.
3. A harness-keeper carries him through kitchens and maintenance ways. Practical talk and returned message bundles show attempted contact, not receipt. His test of a possible rain-hole tears an occupied terrace, revealing that escape by one hard horn-strike would endanger both realms.
4. As the storm shears, the regent cuts the ransom tether and orders him carried toward ground despite the city's likely loss. Only after custody changes does the prince offer bounded help. The captain chooses the homeward route over continued leverage, without earning absolution.
5. During a three-pass descent, the captain carries him along moving seams while the others manage distance and a damaged strap. The prince releases measured rain. Flight noise reduces dialogue to unequal operational fragments; one missed instruction costs altitude, and correction occurs through action.
6. Above the exchange ridge, the queen dismisses the armed rescue line and breaks contact with the coronation stone before knowing he will return. He completes the release; city and valley survive. In a shepherd shelter, she removes his harness while the captain's injured wing is wrapped. End with food, fatigue, and facts deferred until the prince chooses to speak.

## Continuity

- Canon used: A distinct unplaced civilization and local magic fit the deep-time world; personhood, consent, consequence, and incomplete evidence remain binding.
- Boundaries and unknowns: Unicorns, Pegasi, storm-rooting, and both kingdoms are story-local, with no universal taxonomy or connection to any established crown, kingdom, or era. Geography, date, rite origins, later settlement, and the captain's judgment remain unresolved; survival does not absolve kidnapping.
