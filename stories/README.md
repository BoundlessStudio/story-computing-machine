# Stories

A new `[WP]` package uses `prompt.md`, a short provisional `outline.md`, `story.md`, `notes.md`, and `ratings.md`. The prompt preserves the complete user request verbatim, including later directions without overwriting the original. Begin the outline with the promised experience: distinctive pleasure, tone, scale, any specified central relationship, and anticipated payoff, grounding interpretations in the user's words and distinguishing them from explicit requirements. Put the writer's chosen relationship, conflict, limitations, and ending in the provisional direction, with concrete scenes that could deliver the request. Readers test those choices before drafting; they do not become user requirements. The outline may be left behind as prose improves within the user's requirements. The story is the reader-facing prose.

The existing Codex outline reader serves as a continuing promise keeper. It records its independent understanding of the complete request before seeing the outline or editorial framing; Claude also gives a request-only understanding before its outline reading. Both then describe the story the proposed beats actually deliver and compare its dramatic center, agency, tension, and ending with the request, citing concrete evidence for fulfillment as well as objections. The coordinator records that comparison and the resolution of material mismatches before drafting. A plausible mechanism, general approval, or literal inclusion of prompt details is insufficient. The keeper follows the comparison through production and cannot replace cold draft readers or fresh final readers. Assess fulfillment before plot selection, before drafting, and throughout revision. Address material mismatches through scenes or structure before polish. Use contextual checks for local edits and whole-story readings for substantial revisions under [story-create](../.agents/skills/story-create/SKILL.md).

Keep `notes.md` concise: both outline readers' initial request understandings, the coordinator's scene evidence for beginning the draft, useful reader effects, the reasons for accepting or declining objections, important changes, rereading, and useful dissent. Record the outline and final Decisions fidelity checkpoint Yes/No answers, native confidence, action, restart counts, and any resulting user directions there as well. A reliable No with confidence at least 0.80 restarts the failed stage, and a reliable Yes may proceed; uncertainty or conflicting evidence halts production with a question, as does failure after two fresh starts. Raw checkpoint artifacts stay in ignored `tmp/`, outside the story package. Preserve genuine discussion rather than voting on readiness; explicit user requirements cannot be waived by majority approval. No separate process reports or scorecards are needed. The [templates](_template/) offer starting points, not forms to fill mechanically.

Once the prose is ready, make and inspect a separate multi-view reference sheet on a plain white background for each principal character and reference paintings for the story's important distinct locations first, then make the cover using the relevant accepted references, unless the user asks for prose only. Exactly one character belongs on each sheet, with multiple views, poses, and expressions of that character. Do not combine distinct characters into group or ensemble sheets; a multi-headed creature still counts as one character. Include important locations beyond the cover scene: a story with multiple important settings needs at least two distinct location references; a story confined to one setting needs only that place. Alternate views or corrections of one location do not count as multiple locations. A scene portrait does not replace a character sheet. Supplied original images live in that story's `art/references/`; accepted character art lives in `art/characters/`, and location paintings in `art/landscapes/` or `art/interiors/`. Name final art for its character or place, without paired “Original” and “Selected” location copies or a new selection manifest. Keep drafts, superseded candidates, and rejected art under the worktree's `tmp/`. Additional generated art is optional and should earn its place.

Each finished story also has `ratings.md`, using General, Teen, Mature, or Explicit for the strongest material in the prose and its cover. It gives a brief reason and specific content notes. General is the starting target for new stories without a requested audience; it is not assigned automatically to finished work and does not limit narrative ambition or scale. See the [content boundaries](../universe/style-guide.md#audience-and-content-boundaries) for the definitions.

Older `PG`, `YA`, and `R+` labels in preserved prompts describe earlier directions or publication history. `ratings.md` is the current classification of the finished story.

Older packages keep their existing four-file or bundle layout. Their `outline.md` and `review.md` remain history; do not rewrite them just to match a new template. Add or update `notes.md` for an authorized prose change that alters meaning, action, or voice; a purely mechanical correction does not need it. Current stories mark canon in `story.md` frontmatter; bundles use `story.json`. Check the marker before editing. See [AGENTS.md](../AGENTS.md) for permissions and [story-create](../.agents/skills/story-create/SKILL.md) for the editorial practice.

`NAMES.md` is older name memory. It can help avoid confusing reuse, but neither it nor an old review establishes a universe fact. Shared facts belong to [universe/](../universe/README.md).

For collection visual direction and art provenance, see [universe/art.md](../universe/art.md). The [repository README](../README.md) gives a short introduction; [AGENTS.md](../AGENTS.md) governs permissions and working practice.
