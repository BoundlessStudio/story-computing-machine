# Stories

A new `[WP]` package uses `prompt.md`, a short provisional `outline.md`, `story.md`, `notes.md`, and `ratings.md`. The prompt preserves the complete user request verbatim, including later directions without overwriting the original. Begin the outline with the promised experience: distinctive pleasure, tone, scale, central relationship, and anticipated payoff, distinguishing explicit requirements from interpretations. Then identify concrete scenes that could deliver those promises. The outline gives readers a direction to challenge before drafting and may be left behind as prose improves within the user's requirements. The story is the reader-facing prose.

The existing Codex outline reader serves as a continuing promise keeper. It records its independent understanding of the complete request before seeing the outline or editorial framing, then follows whether the premise drives events, revelations change possibilities, and the ending delivers the promised experience. It cannot replace cold draft readers or fresh final readers. Assess fulfillment before plot selection, before drafting, and throughout revision; literal inclusion of prompt details is insufficient. Address material mismatches through scenes or structure before polish. Use contextual checks for local edits and whole-story readings for substantial revisions under [story-create](../.agents/skills/story-create/SKILL.md).

Keep `notes.md` concise: the keeper's initial understanding, useful reader effects, the reasons for accepting or declining objections, important changes, rereading, and useful dissent. Preserve genuine discussion rather than voting on readiness; explicit user requirements cannot be waived by majority approval. No new process reports or scorecards are needed. The [templates](_template/) offer starting points, not forms to fill mechanically.

Once the prose is ready, make and inspect a separate multi-view reference sheet on a plain white background for each principal character and reference paintings for the story's important distinct locations first, then make the cover using the relevant accepted references, unless the user asks for prose only. Exactly one character belongs on each sheet, with multiple views, poses, and expressions of that character. Do not combine distinct characters into group or ensemble sheets; a multi-headed creature still counts as one character. Include important locations beyond the cover scene: a story with multiple important settings needs at least two distinct location references; a story confined to one setting needs only that place. Alternate views or corrections of one location do not count as multiple locations. A scene portrait does not replace a character sheet. Supplied original images live in that story's `art/references/`; accepted character art lives in `art/characters/`, and location paintings in `art/landscapes/` or `art/interiors/`. Name final art for its character or place, without paired “Original” and “Selected” location copies or a new selection manifest. Keep drafts, superseded candidates, and rejected art under the worktree's `tmp/`. Additional generated art is optional and should earn its place.

Each finished story also has `ratings.md`, using General, Teen, Mature, or Explicit for the strongest material in the prose and its cover. It gives a brief reason and specific content notes. General is the starting target for new stories without a requested audience; it is not assigned automatically to finished work and does not limit narrative ambition or scale. See the [content boundaries](../universe/style-guide.md#audience-and-content-boundaries) for the definitions.

Older `PG`, `YA`, and `R+` labels in preserved prompts describe earlier directions or publication history. `ratings.md` is the current classification of the finished story.

Older packages keep their existing four-file or bundle layout. Their `outline.md` and `review.md` remain history; do not rewrite them just to match a new template. Add or update `notes.md` for an authorized prose change that alters meaning, action, or voice; a purely mechanical correction does not need it. Current stories mark canon in `story.md` frontmatter; bundles use `story.json`. Check the marker before editing. See [AGENTS.md](../AGENTS.md) for permissions and [story-create](../.agents/skills/story-create/SKILL.md) for the editorial practice.

`NAMES.md` is older name memory. It can help avoid confusing reuse, but neither it nor an old review establishes a universe fact. Shared facts belong to [universe/](../universe/README.md).

For collection visual direction and art provenance, see [universe/art.md](../universe/art.md). The [repository README](../README.md) gives a short introduction; [AGENTS.md](../AGENTS.md) governs permissions and working practice.
