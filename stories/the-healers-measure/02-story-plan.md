# Story plan

## Story promise

Ruva, a trained field healer, cannot understand why Vekran—one of the region's feared witchknights, equally formidable with steel and magic—chose her as his apprentice. She assumes he means to replace her healer's habits with a warrior's reflexes. During an attack on a roadside infirmary, however, an injured enemy caster's living body becomes entangled with a crushing spell. Vekran can hold the spell or cut it by killing its source, but he cannot safely separate it while protecting the people trapped inside. Ruva must cross the threatened room with incomplete martial training, stabilize the caster she was taught to call an enemy, distinguish living tissue from magical conduit, and make the exact cut that saves both captives and attacker.

The climax answers the prompt through action: Vekran chose a healer because witchknights need more than the power to defeat a living source. They need the diagnostic discipline, preservation instinct, and practiced judgment to know what must not be cut. Ruva succeeds only by integrating healing, sword training, and bounded local magic. She does not become a master warrior overnight, and healing is never treated as preparation she has outgrown.

## Story controls

- **Target length:** 2,500–4,000 words; six scenes totaling about 3,400 words.
- **POV and tense:** Close third person centered only on Ruva; past tense. No access to Vekran's or the knot-caster's unspoken thoughts.
- **Tone and genre:** Character-driven secondary-world fantasy; tense, humane, physically concrete, and ultimately hopeful. Respect competence without worshipping it.
- **Rating:** Teen. Bruising, a shallow blade wound, magical strain, blood on bandages, collapsing masonry, and peril are permitted; keep injury non-graphic and consequence-centered.
- **Scope:** One unnamed active-magic era and one regional witchknight practice. Do not locate it in an established era, connect it to another magical school, or claim witchknights are objectively supreme across the world or deep time. Their reputation as the region's most capable warriors is demonstrated locally.
- **Mentorship:** Vekran is exacting, candid, safety-minded, and corrigible. His controlled drills use blunted steel, halt calls, inspected space, and declared magical limits. The crisis is an external attack, not a cruelty he arranged.
- **Resolution:** The roadside infirmary's occupants survive; the injured knot-caster survives to face ordinary custody and treatment; Vekran's wound and both practitioners' magical exhaustion require recovery. Ruva chooses to continue the apprenticeship as a healer learning witchknight work, not as a healer converted into something supposedly higher.

## Local witchknight and healing system

Everything below belongs only to this local practice.

1. **Living source:** A living practitioner intentionally turns their own warmth, breath, attention, and trained movement into shaped force. Nonliving blades, buckles, chalk lines, shields, stones, and bandages may focus or direct power but cannot choose, cast, sustain, diagnose, or consent.
2. **The measure:** With skin contact or an iron focus touching the relevant surface, a trained practitioner can sense nearby pressure, motion, and active magical paths as distinct tensions. It is not mind-reading, omniscience, or a universal life detector. A novice can hold only a small, simple map; pain, distance, crossing forces, and fatigue blur it.
3. **Turn:** Through stance, timing, and a weapon or bracer as inert focus, a witchknight can redirect one bounded incoming force—blade, bolt, falling beam, or spell-pressure—along a mapped line. Force is moved rather than erased. Poor footing, excessive mass, or a bad exit line throws the load into the practitioner or a bystander.
4. **Hold:** A witchknight can brace a fixed ward between two prepared surfaces. Vekran can hold the infirmary room for roughly sixty heartbeats under the crisis load; the cost rises continuously and prevents him from performing a delicate second working at the same time. Ruva can manage only a shield-sized hold for several breaths.
5. **Parting:** A witchknight can sever a mapped magical conduit with an edged iron focus. A clean nonliving line can be cut safely. If the working is braided through a living source, an imprecise cut follows the easiest path through that body and may kill them. Killing the source also ends the spell, but is not the only possible solution.
6. **Wound-knot:** The attacker has deliberately threaded a compression working through their own injured shoulder and around the infirmary's roof braces. Their living intent began it; the iron clasp at the wound only focuses it. Shock and failing control cause the knot to tighten rather than release. The clasp cannot cast independently, and killing the caster would drop the spell violently before Vekran's ward can redirect it.
7. **Healing practice:** Ruva combines mundane triage with a touch-based living working that senses gross tissue boundaries and can encourage clotting and hold damaged edges in place. It cannot replace lost blood, regrow tissue, repair a major organ, cleanse infection or poison, erase pain, or reverse death. It requires proximity, concentration, her own warmth, and consent when the patient can answer. Cleaning, pressure, binding, splinting, rest, food, and later care remain necessary.
8. **Integrated technique:** Ruva can use healer-sense to distinguish the caster's living tissue and pulse from the knot's imposed rhythm, hold the wound stable, and mark a narrow nonliving exit line. She must then release the healing hold long enough to perform one beginner's parting cut. Success depends on Vekran's ward, the caster holding still by choice, Ruva's anatomical judgment, and her newly trained stance.
9. **Costs and recovery:** Strong work drains warmth and breath, producing cold skin, shaking, slowed judgment, and lost fine control. Ruva cannot repeatedly heal and fight at full capacity; Vekran cannot indefinitely hold and sever. Dry clothes, warmth, food, sleep, and time restore ordinary depletion. Exceeding a declared limit risks collapse and an uncontrolled release, so stopping is a mark of mastery.
10. **Martial limits:** Vekran has decades of integrated training; Ruva has six weeks. She knows guard, footwork, a short parry, a shield-sized hold, and one supervised parting cut. In the climax she survives by using those narrow skills, the room, and her healer's precision—not by defeating veteran fighters in a fair duel.

## Character engine

### Ruva

- **Role:** Adult field healer and new witchknight apprentice; close-third protagonist.
- **Want:** Prove Vekran did not choose her by mistake and that she can become formidable enough to deserve the apprenticeship.
- **Need:** Stop measuring worth by how fully she can imitate Vekran. Recognize that preservation, diagnosis, consent, and restraint are already forms of difficult expertise that must reshape witchknight practice rather than be discarded for it.
- **Pressure:** Six weeks of bruising martial basics have made her feel slow; the public reputation of witchknights makes every mistake seem like evidence against her. During the crisis, her mentor is injured, civilians are trapped, an enemy is dying, and using healing first consumes the warmth and precision she needs for magic and defense.
- **Strengths:** Fast triage, patient observation, steady hands, anatomical knowledge, economical bedside authority, willingness to revise a diagnosis, and the habit of treating an injured person before deciding what they deserve.
- **Limits:** She overcommits to the first urgent body she sees, resents being sent to the treatment side of a fight, has shallow guard recovery, and can hold only one simple magical line at a time.
- **Arc:** She begins trying to suppress healer reflexes to look like a witchknight. She ends choosing the apprenticeship on explicit terms: she will learn their reach and force, while her healer's measure changes how that force is used.

### Vekran

- **Role:** Veteran witchknight, Ruva's mentor, and the local standard by which the order's reputation is made credible.
- **Want:** Teach Ruva to move her healer's judgment through danger rather than leaving it safely behind the battle line.
- **Need/pressure:** Trust his deliberate choice when Ruva's method is slower than the killing solution he could execute. Under injury and magical cold, he must yield the decisive cut to an apprentice without pretending she is fully trained.
- **Competence:** Reads a crowded fight, redirects attacks without losing spatial awareness, alternates steel and magic, protects bystanders, and stops when accumulated cost compromises precision. He is formidable because of practiced integration and judgment, not invulnerability.
- **Error:** He explains training purposes too sparsely, assuming evidence will persuade Ruva as it persuades him. His first attempt to contain the wound-knot is powerful but based on an incomplete map; he admits the error promptly.
- **Arc:** He moves from assigning Ruva a role she experiences as unexplained to naming the professional standard he wants them to build together. He neither takes credit for her healing nor minimizes the martial skill she still must earn.

### The unnamed knot-caster

- **Role:** Adult attacker, injured living source of the compression knot, and consequential patient rather than a disposable device.
- **Want:** Keep the infirmary occupants as leverage long enough for their surviving companions to escape.
- **Need/pressure:** Their wound and failing spell are about to kill them and the hostages. They expect witchknights to solve the problem by killing them, so accepting Ruva's treatment feels like surrender without protection.
- **Agency:** They can refuse, bargain, hold still, warn Ruva about the clasp's false branch, and later submit to custody. Survival does not erase their responsibility for the attack.
- **Arc:** They move from weaponizing their own injury to choosing the stillness and release that let Ruva separate body from spell.

## Story-specific style profile

- **Dominant voice qualities:** Clinically attentive without becoming detached; muscular and economical in action; humane but unsentimental. Ruva's attention sorts color, temperature, balance, breathing, hand function, and routes to the injured before it notices heraldry or spectacle.
- **Narrative distance:** Close enough for free-indirect flashes of Ruva's diagnostic certainty, self-correction, and resentment. Pull slightly back to compress travel and repeated training; move closest when she must choose whom to treat, when her hands start shaking, and when she makes the parting cut.
- **Image sources:** Bandage tension, pulse under fingers, weight through feet, the give of joints, heat leaving skin, iron carrying pressure, and seams that can be joined or cut. Use these literally first. Avoid making every moral decision a wound or every relationship a stitching metaphor.
- **Action texture:** Preserve intention, position, obstruction, and result. Vekran's speed should read as disciplined economy: one step solves two threats, but every turn sends force somewhere visible and every working cools him. Ruva's action should remain narrower, more effortful, and dependent on prepared basics.
- **Emotional register:** Ruva tends to translate fear into practical inventory. Let shame appear as unnecessary rechecking and irritation; let care appear through changed pressure, covered skin, and asking before touch. Do not make either lead deliver polished ethical summaries under danger.
- **Dialogue pressure:** Ruva asks precise questions when anxious and becomes curt when patronized; Vekran teaches through observations and bounded choices; the knot-caster uses threat and sarcasm to retain leverage until survival requires literal answers.
- **Wit and restraint:** Sparse, dry humor may arise from work habits in safe moments. No banter during acute injury, no quip sealing the climax, and no sentimental speech declaring that healers were the greatest warriors all along.
- **Intentional departure:** The story may use denser procedural detail than the shared default because diagnosis is both viewpoint and plot. Keep each rule attached to immediate use, failure, or cost rather than explanatory lectures.

## Dialogue profiles

**Ruva — dialogue profile:** Usually wants usable information, a defined responsibility, or acknowledgment of professional competence; defaults to exact questions and compressed clinical statements. Her sentences shorten while treating, and she names injuries and limits precisely while keeping her own fear vague. She refuses to say directly that she fears Vekran regrets choosing her. When resistance makes a question useless, she changes to a concrete bargain or command. With Vekran she challenges implications; with a patient she lowers volume, offers bounded choices, and waits for an answer. Humor is rare and practical. She would not naturally give a grand speech about compassion or call every rescue “healing.”

**Vekran — dialogue profile:** Usually wants accurate observation followed by committed action; defaults to short questions that require Ruva to name what she sees, then gives one correction at a time. His vocabulary comes from footing, load, distance, and consequence. He keeps his selection rationale too compressed and avoids reassurance unsupported by evidence. Under resistance he changes from questioning to a direct assignment with an explicit limit; when control slips, he becomes plainer rather than louder. With Ruva he never mocks novice failure, and with an attacker he avoids threats he cannot ethically or tactically carry out. He would not naturally offer an aphorism that turns pain into virtue.

**The knot-caster — dialogue profile:** Usually wants delay, leverage, and proof they will not be killed; defaults to taunts, partial technical truths, and questions that force others to reveal plans. Speech is breath-short and increasingly literal as shock worsens. They name the spell's traps exactly but keep motive and allegiance vague. When Ruva refuses both intimidation and false reassurance, they change from bargaining with hostages to bargaining for a survivable surrender. They do not apologize in the crisis or suddenly adopt therapeutic language.

## Causal spine

Because Ruva mistakes martial imitation for the apprenticeship's standard, she overreaches in a controlled drill and misses the safer line her healer's eye already identified. Because Vekran makes her repeat the drill while retaining her treatment satchel, she enters the journey able to perform only a few narrow combat actions without abandoning her vocation. Because an attack traps civilians in a roadside infirmary, Vekran must demonstrate why witchknights have their reputation: he defeats the immediate attackers while redirecting lethal force away from patients. Because he protects the doorway and spends power on a hold, he is wounded and cannot solve the hidden wound-knot alone.

Because the knot is braided through a living injured caster, the obvious parting cut would kill them and drop the roof load onto the hostages. Because Ruva triages rather than accepting “enemy” as a diagnosis, she discovers that stabilizing the caster also stabilizes the spell long enough to map it. Because healing drains her warmth and occupies both hands, she cannot simply maintain treatment while Vekran performs the cut; she must move through danger, obtain the caster's cooperation, separate tissue from conduit, then change from healer's touch to witchknight stance at the exact moment. Because her martial training is incomplete, Vekran's ward and one costly beginner's turn are indispensable. Because her healer training identifies the survivable line, her parting cut ends the knot without ending its source. The action proves why Vekran chose her.

## Scene plan

### Scene 1 — The wrong measure (about 450 words)

- **Immediate purpose:** Open inside a controlled yard drill with live pressure. Ruva must cross three paces and touch Vekran's shoulder with a blunted practice blade while he may use only footwork and one declared turn. Establish six weeks of training, her frustration, safety practice, and witchknight reputation without exposition.
- **Action:** Ruva attacks too hard to look decisive. Vekran redirects her momentum into the sand, stops before the finishing touch, and calls halt. On the reset she notices his exposed wrist and could strike it, but tries an ambitious shoulder cut instead; he disarms her. He asks what she declined to hit. Her healer's answer—one exact wrist line that would disable without destroying the hand—is correct. His answer is not “forget that”; he makes it the next drill's target.
- **Incompatible conversational aims:** Ruva wants Vekran to admit why he chose someone plainly behind every fighter he could have recruited; Vekran wants her to assess the failed decision without turning the drill into a verdict on her worth. **Tactic change:** Ruva's sarcastic request for reassurance fails, so she gives the clinical account he asked for; Vekran stops teaching by question and states one concrete truth: he chose the person who saw the hand as more than a target, but she must learn to reach that choice under attack.
- **Turn:** A rider's alarm interrupts before Ruva can demand the full reason: a medicine cart and its passengers are under attack at the nearby roadside infirmary. Vekran orders her to bring blade, warm cloak, and healer's satchel—all three.
- **Exit change:** The philosophical question becomes an immediate assignment, and the satchel is established as operational equipment rather than baggage.

### Scene 2 — What formidable costs (about 550 words)

- **Immediate purpose:** Demonstrate Vekran's credible martial and magical mastery while preserving costs and spatial causality. Give Ruva an active triage role that she initially reads as exclusion.
- **Action:** At the infirmary courtyard, two attackers pin the medicine cart while a third drives spell-pressure into the roof braces from inside. Vekran maps the yard through his blade, turns a crossbow bolt into an empty water trough, binds one sword with his guard, shoulder-checks its wielder off balance, and uses the same motion to brace a falling lintel. He disarms rather than executes, but each turn visibly drains heat from his hands and shortens his breath. Ruva drags the driver behind stone, stops a bleeding scalp wound, and directs two mobile patients toward the rear exit. Her warning about an attacker rising behind Vekran lets him pivot in time; her work affects the fight.
- **Incompatible conversational aims:** Ruva wants permission to join Vekran at the doorway and prove she is not merely accompanying him as a medic; Vekran wants her to keep the only viable evacuation route and the wounded moving. **Tactic change:** She argues from apprenticeship status; he first answers with command, then, seeing she hears demotion, gives operational criteria: she owns the rear route until no patient is red-priority, then may advance under a shield-sized hold. Ruva stops arguing and reports triage counts.
- **Turn:** Vekran wins the courtyard, but inside the infirmary the compression working tightens. He steps between a falling beam and two patients, uses a full hold, and takes a shallow cut under the upper arm from the knot-caster's last knife. He does not drop; he cannot free an arm or spare the attention for delicate work.
- **Exit change:** The master wins the ordinary fight at a visible cost and becomes fixed in place by the magical crisis. Ruva, not a hidden stronger combatant, must enter.

### Scene 3 — An enemy is not a diagnosis (about 600 words)

- **Immediate purpose:** Reveal the wound-knot and force Ruva's healer identity into direct conflict with the fast killing solution.
- **Action:** Under Vekran's shield-sized lane, Ruva enters with a low guard and reaches the knot-caster pinned beside a roof post. The caster has driven an inert iron clasp through clothing at the injured shoulder; pressure lines run from it into four braces. Vekran's measure is blurred by his own hold and crossing loads. He can see only that one hard parting stroke through clasp and shoulder would end the source. Ruva sees worsening shock, impaired hand color, and a false pulse imposed by the knot.
- **Incompatible conversational aims:** The knot-caster wants a promise of freedom before allowing treatment; Ruva wants permission to examine and stabilize without promising an outcome she cannot control; Vekran needs enough technical truth to keep his ward from choosing the wrong release line. **Tactic change:** The caster's threats fail because Ruva plainly names the imminent death the knot cannot prevent; Ruva changes from asking for general consent to offering two bounded choices—pressure and examination now, or no touch while Vekran prepares the lethal cut; Vekran changes from interrogation to reporting exactly what he can and cannot hold.
- **Choice:** The caster permits Ruva to treat the shoulder but not remove the clasp. Ruva applies mundane pressure first, then spends a small healing working to hold tissue edges and read their boundary. The knot loosens slightly as the caster stabilizes, proving that healing changes the tactical situation.
- **Reversal:** The pressure line is not simply anchored to the caster; it has been braided along the wound so a killing cut would release all four roof loads at once. Vekran admits his first map was incomplete. He can keep holding or attempt the cut, not both.
- **Exit change:** Preserving the enemy is established as the safer magical course, but only temporarily; Ruva must devise and execute the separation before Vekran's strength fails.

### Scene 4 — The line that is alive (about 550 words)

- **Immediate purpose:** Make the climax plan emerge from contested expertise, seed exact steps and limits, and force Ruva to choose priorities.
- **Action:** Ruva wants to bandage Vekran first when she sees blood reaching his cuff. He refuses treatment while his hold bears the roof, but accepts her rapid assessment: his wound is painful and draining, not yet the first death. This honors her judgment rather than dismissing it. She maps the caster again and identifies a finger-width interval where the iron clasp touches the spell braid without crossing living tissue. The false branch shifts with each breath; it cannot be cut from a static diagram.
- **Incompatible conversational aims:** Ruva wants Vekran to take back the delicate magical act because she has performed parting only in drills; Vekran wants her to accept that her live map is more reliable than his obscured one; the knot-caster wants the clasp left intact because it is their last leverage. **Tactic change:** Ruva first lists reasons she is unqualified, then changes to specifying the support she requires: a shield lane, a count, and permission to abort. Vekran stops assuring and gives those exact conditions, including that he will drop the lane if his fingers go numb. The caster changes from refusal to a bargain—stillness and the false-branch warning in exchange for Ruva's explicit effort to keep them alive and ordinary custody if they survive.
- **Plan:** Ruva will stabilize the wound for three breaths, trace the live boundary, shift to guard, use one turn to divert the knot's defensive lash into the floor, then part the nonliving braid. Vekran will hold the roof and count. If Ruva loses the line, she stops; no desperate blind cut.
- **Exit change:** Ruva accepts leadership of the only survivable solution, not because Vekran declares her ready in general, but because their different competencies define one narrow executable act.

### Scene 5 — The healer's measure (about 850 words)

- **Immediate purpose:** Deliver the integrated martial, magical, ethical, and healing payoff under escalating cost.
- **Action sequence:**
  1. Ruva warms her fingers inside her cloak, checks the caster's yes again, cleans what she can, and stabilizes the wound. The caster warns that the false branch moves on the fourth beat after each tightening.
  2. A loose attacker from the courtyard reaches the broken side door and tries to kill the caster, believing death will cover the retreat. Ruva cannot win a prolonged exchange. She uses the short parry drilled in Scene 1, takes a bruising impact on her bracer, and strikes the attacker's weapon hand at the exact disabling wrist line she earlier declined. The blade drops; she kicks it out of reach rather than pursuing. Vekran cannot help without losing the roof.
  3. The interruption costs Ruva warmth and position. Her first shield-sized hold buckles because her back foot slides in blood-damp dust. She releases instead of forcing it, resets on dry stone, and says she needs a new count. This prevents effortless mastery and makes truthful stopping part of competence.
  4. On the second attempt, Vekran counts while shaking. Ruva maintains healing contact for three breaths, feels living pulse separate from imposed rhythm, marks the safe line with chalk on the inert clasp, and tells the caster when not to move. She releases the healing working; the wound remains bound but not cured.
  5. The knot lashes. Ruva catches one bounded force with her bracer and turns it into the stone floor. The redirected load cracks tile and numbs her arm, showing force was moved, not erased.
  6. The false branch slides toward living tissue. The caster, now invested in survival, calls the timing rather than exploiting it. Ruva aborts her first cutting motion, waits one beat, and uses the healer's live map—not visual confidence—to make the narrow parting stroke through spell braid and clasp, clear of the shoulder.
  7. The knot ends unevenly. Vekran redirects the released roof load outward into the empty courtyard, then deliberately drops his hold before collapse. Part of the roof falls into the cleared space; people inside live. He sinks to one knee, conscious but spent.
- **Incompatible conversational aims:** Ruva needs exact cooperation and silence during the cut; the knot-caster wants continual reassurance that she will not switch to the killing line; Vekran needs Ruva to act before his limit without rushing past uncertainty. **Tactic change:** Ruva stops repeating reassurance and gives the caster a real job—call the false branch; the caster abandons bargaining and supplies the critical count; Vekran changes from coaching details to answering only Ruva's requested count and status.
- **Climax choice:** When the first line moves, Ruva refuses a fast blind cut even though Vekran is failing. The one-beat delay risks the room but preserves the caster and produces the clean sever. Her success is not mercy added after victory; preservation is the tactic that makes victory possible.
- **Exit change:** The attacker is alive, the hostages are free, Vekran is protected, and Ruva has performed one difficult integrated act within known limits. She immediately returns to triage instead of celebrating.

### Scene 6 — Terms of apprenticeship (about 400 words)

- **Immediate purpose:** Pay consequences, complete the character arc, and answer why Vekran chose her without replacing the action with explanation.
- **Aftermath:** Ruva binds Vekran's arm, orders warmth for both practitioners, checks the knot-caster for transport, and reports what healing could not repair. The caster is carried to treatment under guard; survival is neither pardon nor punishment. The medicine cart is damaged and the infirmary roof partly lost, requiring local rebuilding. No injury vanishes.
- **Incompatible conversational aims:** Ruva wants a final verdict on whether today's narrow success proves she belongs; Vekran wants her to state what she still cannot do and what only she could do today. **Tactic change:** Ruva first seeks a yes-or-no judgment, then answers with a concrete inventory: weak recovery, one failed hold, a successful turn, a successful parting, two patients stabilized, one mentor needing stitches. Vekran changes from examination questions to the direct answer he owed: he chose a trained healer because anyone can teach a candidate where to cut; he needed an apprentice already trained to ask what must live through it.
- **Character resolution:** Ruva rejects the implication that her healer's work is a preliminary stage—an implication Vekran did not intend but failed to address. She agrees to continue if the satchel remains part of every field kit and healing shifts are counted as witchknight training. Vekran agrees without ceremony and adds that her footwork still needs work.
- **Ending image:** As the medicine cart rolls toward town, Ruva straps the healer's satchel across one shoulder and Vekran's blunted practice sword across the other. Their weights pull differently. She shortens one strap until they balance, then walks beside the patients rather than behind the witchknight.

## Stakes, reversals, climax, and ending

- **Immediate stakes:** Patients trapped beneath a tightening roof, the medicine supply, Vekran's failing hold and wound, Ruva's safety, and the knot-caster's life.
- **Personal stakes:** Whether Ruva can accept an apprenticeship that expands rather than erases her vocation; whether Vekran's unconventional selection reflects a real standard or a private hope he cannot defend.
- **Moral stakes:** Whether formidable power defaults to killing a living source because it is fast; whether an injured attacker remains a patient without escaping accountability; whether restraint survives time pressure.
- **Reversal 1:** Ruva's “healer's hesitation” in the drill is not the defect Vekran wants removed; reaching that judgment under attack is the training goal.
- **Reversal 2:** Vekran decisively wins the visible fight, yet the strongest cut he can offer for the hidden knot would produce the worst outcome.
- **Midpoint reversal:** Healing the enemy weakens and clarifies the spell, making preservation tactically indispensable rather than merely compassionate.
- **Crisis reversal:** Ruva's first magical hold fails and the safe cut moves. Honest abort and remapping, not greater force, create the second chance.
- **Climax:** Ruva uses the exact wrist strike seeded in training, stabilizes the caster, turns one lash, refuses the blind cut, and parts the nonliving braid along a healer-mapped line while Vekran holds the structure. Every contribution is necessary and costly.
- **Aftermath:** Lives are saved, wounds and property damage remain, custody follows, and Vekran needs treatment and recovery. Ruva receives no instant rank or mastery.
- **Ending:** The balanced satchel and practice sword convert her opening confusion into a chosen, integrated apprenticeship without stating the theme abstractly.

## Setup and payoff

- Ruva identifies the safe wrist line in Scene 1 but fails to use it; she uses that same narrow, disabling target under real danger in Scene 5.
- Vekran requires blade, warm cloak, and healer's satchel in Scene 1. The blade focuses parting, the cloak restores hand function, and the satchel enables stabilization.
- The opening turn sends Ruva harmlessly into sand; later her own turn sends the knot lash into tile and numbs her arm, proving both growth and cost.
- Vekran's “reach the choice under attack” instruction becomes the climax's full test without his having staged it.
- Ruva's triage count in Scene 2 establishes her authority and prevents her from appearing to abandon patients for adventure.
- Vekran's cooling hands and shortened breath seed the hard limit on his hold before it becomes a countdown.
- The caster's partial consent in Scene 3 becomes renewed, specific cooperation in Scenes 4–5; no magical mechanism certifies consent.
- Vekran's incomplete first map prevents him from being infallible. His prompt correction and later support preserve formidable competence without effortless mastery.
- The mismatched weight of satchel and practice sword begins as burden and ends as a physical balance Ruva adjusts for herself.

## Canon constraints

- Keep witchknights, their regional reputation, recruitment, measurework, costs, terms, and doctrine bounded to this unnamed locality. Never claim they are the greatest warriors in every culture or era.
- This story occurs in the single persistent physical world during an unspecified era where magic is active. Do not identify or contrast established eras, places, factions, schools, bloodlines, or systems.
- Every magical act has a living user and living supply. Iron weapons, clasp, chalk, bracers, bandages, buildings, and spell paths remain inert foci or materials with no intention or casting agency.
- The knot-caster is alive and personally began the wound-knot. Injury destabilizes their control but is not universal fuel. Pain, blood, sacrifice, consent, or desire must never be described as powering magic in general.
- Costs are local and consistent: lost warmth, breath, focus, and fine control; recovery requires ordinary care and time. No power arrives through destiny, ancestry, or a hidden bloodline.
- Healing remains limited, laborious, and indispensable. Do not let it restore blood, erase Vekran's wound, instantly cure shock, or substitute for cleaning, binding, evacuation, and later treatment.
- Preserve the knot-caster's personhood and responsibility. Saving them does not absolve the attack; custody does not retroactively make killing them the right magical solution.
- Keep violence Teen and non-graphic. Emphasize balance, choices, damaged equipment, triage, and aftermath rather than bodily spectacle.
- Do not imply Vekran is selecting Ruva as an expendable medic, magical battery, corrective moral mascot, or lesser combatant. He wants a witchknight whose existing expertise changes tactical practice.
- Do not connect resemblance in material strain, instruction, or restraint to Lacebridge, the Bellweathers, Copperwell, rune-shoe training, or any other established system.
- All plan material and local inventions remain non-canonical unless later promoted through the repository workflow.

## Local inventions

- **Ruva and Vekran:** one adult healer-apprentice and one veteran witchknight, with six weeks of non-sadistic training and no crossover.
- **Regional witchknights:** a small, unnamed local tradition with a reputation for integrated martial and magical capability; recruitment from healers is unusual but permitted.
- **Measurework:** the bounded living-powered practice of sensing, turning, holding, and parting force through touch, stance, breath, warmth, attention, and inert iron foci.
- **Local healing working:** Ruva's touch-based tissue sense and limited stabilization, dependent on her living supply, patient proximity, ordinary treatment, and recovery.
- **Wound-knot:** one compression working deliberately braided by a living attacker through their own wound and four roof braces, with an inert clasp as focus and the stated failure behavior.
- **Roadside infirmary crisis:** the medicine cart, unnamed patients and attackers, damaged building, rescue, custody, and local aftermath.

None of these inventions creates universal lore, an official era, an established faction, or authority beyond this story proposal.
