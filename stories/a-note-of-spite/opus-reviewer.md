This is the exception to the owner's complaint: the dialogue works, and the edit pass should mostly leave it alone. Lavinia talks in arch, joke-first lines. Her jokes sometimes land flat on purpose ("Considerably." / "Clorinda did not laugh."), and she gets more formal as she is hurt. Clorinda answers with blunt contradictions, orders and practical talk about the oil. When she finally has to ask for something, her speech breaks into fragments ("Oh, don't go. Please."). The conversation behaves like people talking. They talk past each other (the march and poker), someone gets cut off (the saucepan), some replies are non-answers ("Well.", "I heard you.") and the guest chatter is ordinary (mosquitoes, the sister, "Too high."). There is no house-style ping-pong. Clorinda does most of the correcting, and that is her voice, not a tic the two women share. Aim for a handful of one-word or one-beat fixes. Do not smooth Clorinda's curt replies, trim Lavinia's failed jokes or add explanation to the ending.

## Dialogue findings

### D1 · Minor · Opening tease depends on an unanchored "midsummer"

- Where: L25–L31, greeting at the drawing-room door, immediately after the required line
- Text:
> You've finally abandoned that dreadful almond.
> You gave me the almond.
> I was younger.
> It was at midsummer.
- Problem: Ground/referent (mild). Clorinda's reply only works as a jab if "midsummer" means a few weeks ago. At L31 the reader does not know the season yet: the heat on the stairs is put down to the candles, and summer only surfaces at L67 (mosquitoes) and L145 ("It's very hot"). Read as any midsummer, the line does not undercut "I was younger". The exchange then looks like a patter of corrections instead of Clorinda puncturing Lavinia's exaggeration.
- Fix: Anchor the time in Clorinda's reply and change nothing else in the exchange. Do not add a second quip. Illustrative only: "Six weeks ago."

### D2 · Minor · Stock and glossing beats at the decisive and final exchanges

- Where: L229, "why you didn't simply ask me" exchange in the wrap room; L313, the story's last line
- Text:
> Clorinda opened her mouth, closed it, and rubbed a finger along her eyebrow.
> Clorinda gave her shoulder a small, possessive squeeze.
- Problem: Mechanics. "Opened her mouth, closed it" is a stock hesitation beat at the story's most important exchange. The eyebrow rub on its own is specific and enough. "Possessive" tells the reader how to take "I practised." one sentence before the line does it. L241 "looked exasperated, then almost immediately tired" is a labelled emotion but a useful hinge; it can stay.
- Fix: At L229, cut "opened her mouth, closed it, and" and keep the eyebrow rub. At L313, drop "possessive" (for example, "a small squeeze") and let "I practised." carry the claim. Leave the dialogue words untouched.

## Prose findings

### P1 · Minor · Stray referents and one timing slip in narration

- Where: L43, the paragraph about the fiftieth birthday; L81, the drawing room before the card; L139, the wrap room
- Text:
> Neither threat had been carried out. The unoccupied corner by the piano was plainly waiting for them.
> Several stood in rows facing the piano, and a low vase had been removed from its lid.
> on her, breathing and warm, it had just undone Lavinia's neatest little hex.
- Problem:
  - L43: "them" has two plausible antecedents, the two threats or the two women.
  - L81: "its" grammatically attaches to the vase, not the piano.
  - L139: "just" misplaces the time. The hex was blocked at the door, several scenes earlier (the card, the brooch), and by the earlier application of the scent, not the one being applied now.
- Fix:
  - L43: make the referent explicit. Illustrative only: "was plainly waiting for the two of them."
  - L81: say "from the piano lid."
  - L139: drop "just", or change it to "earlier". Keep the living-wearer logic of the sentence (see guardrails).

## Continuity and prompt findings

### C1 · Minor · "Thirty years" sits awkwardly with "as girls" and the stated ages

- Where: L45, watching Clorinda receive guests; L101, recalling the song; L281, the dog bit
- Text:
> Lavinia had been rescuing her from such mistakes for thirty years.
> They had learned it as girls
> Lavinia knew what came next. She had known it for thirty years.
- Problem: Internal time. The story states exact ages, 50 and 52 (L43). It also says the two learned the song "as girls" from a shared teacher, with "the girls in the back row" at a recital (L209). "Thirty years" then puts that at about age 20–22. That is possible, but it reads as an arithmetic slip, and L281 in particular sounds like it means since girlhood.
- Fix: At L281 (and L45 if the friendship dates from the lessons), change to "nearly forty years", or leave both as they are if the lessons were meant to be in their early twenties. This is optional but cheap.
