# Opus review: The Players Above

Independent audit for a later editing pass. It is not a `review.md` verdict,
does not change canon state, and does not authorize edits by itself.

## Verdict

- Dialogue: REVISE — only Reniya hears the voices, yet companions react to or quote voice content she never relayed (L37, L201); the two-answer limit is asserted rather than shown; the talk between crises runs on quip-and-correction volleys.
- Prose: PASS — concrete, varied and well staged; only minor reversal-template, stock-beat and cadence polish.
- Continuity and prompt: REVISE — the prompt is delivered, but water-supply infrastructure carries the climax stakes and the ending errand (a policy item for any authorized revision), and a few props move without a handoff.
- Edit scope: targeted-dialogue
- Existing review.md said: Verdict PASS, "Dialogue: PASS"; disagree — it missed a Blocking knowledge leak (L201), the unestablished answer limit, and the quip-closer pattern.

This story's dialogue has two modes. Under pressure (the three-leg run, Veysa's "Two feet. More. Hold it.", Ludek's "Not below") it is plain, urgent and specific to each person, and it works. At rest, in the mill room and on the terraces, it falls into the collection's house rhythm: short volleys that end on someone's correction or maxim ("Technically the miller's." / "Tonight it's mine."; "The wall doesn't care what they call it."; "Finite is useful…"). Veysa, whose voice should be stone and load, keeps landing word-parsing last words that belong to Reniya. Under that sits a structural hazard the old review missed. The story's rule is that only the splinter-holder hears the voices, but the relay ledger leaks, and a count that drives the climax is never put on the page. The edit pass should repair the relay ledger first, then cut the quip closers and leave ordinary replies in their place rather than sharper ones. It should leave the action dialogue alone. The catchment swap (C1) is a bounded noun-and-stakes substitution, not a structural rewrite.

## Edit guardrails

- Prompt: keep the four prompt exchanges in order at L25–L33 ("I appreciate your advice, formless voice, but what do you want with me?"; the game-and-peril answer; "Like in a TTRPG?"; "Oh, you have those too?"), including the voice's pace break before the last line. The active profile is prospective-2026-08-21 (the last `Craft profile:` line); the rewrite request covers dialogue. Keep it broadly accessible. Length band 2,500–4,000 words (now about 3,250), so trims are welcome and additions should stay small.
- Story-local mechanism, which must stay consistent: audible, sincere intent to travel within half a mile feeds growth. Thoughts, questions, lies, past journeys and disembodied voices do not, but a half-spoken intention does (L277). New growth cannot cross a cooled seam, and a closed course forces the charge inward. Cutting while charged sends growth to the last accepted destination. Each presence answers two questions. Only whoever holds the splinter hears the voices. The voices cannot see underground or hear thought.
- Universe: LOCKED "Living agency and magic" (rules.md) holds here because "the living maker supplied one finite charge" and the pod and grain are nonliving; keep that wording. LOCKED "Personhood and moral consequence" means the game frame must never make the three into pieces or fictions. The creator stays absent and unresolved.
- What works and must not be flattened: the action dialogue of the run; Ludek's humor and its loss under pressure; Veysa's sarcasm when it grows from material (floors, walls, loads); the nonverbal planning (hands at turns, tiles, pointing).

## Dialogue findings

### D1 · Blocking · Veysa quotes voice lines she never heard

- Where: L185–L205, kiln ring, after the third voice's first answer
- Text:
> If inward, it may surface beside the origin. If beneath—unobserved.
> “It says close a route around the center. The growth can’t cross its cooled metal, so the charge knots inward and pushes the core up.”
> “The voice suggested the core might surface.”
> “Beside the origin, it said. Or under it, which it cannot see.”
> If we dig where your friend is offering branches, we unload the soil holding that wall.
- Problem: Ground/knowledge. Only the splinter-holder hears the voices (L111, L115, L167, L221). The third voice's options at L187 reach Reniya alone. Her only relay (L193) says nothing about "beside the origin", "under it" or digging, and the digging alternatives are narrated only after Veysa speaks (L203). Even so, Veysa corrects Reniya's paraphrase with the voice's exact wording and knows it is "offering branches". The shape is correction ping-pong built on a quote she cannot have. Reniya's L199 reply to "Catchment crown" is also oblique, because nothing tells the listener why the voice's suggestion bears on the stone Veysa just struck.
- Fix: Put the options on the table through Reniya first, then let Veysa reason from what she actually heard plus the stone under her bar. The smallest version: turn L199 into Reniya's relay of the voice's outcomes and its offer to dig, cut Veysa's "Beside the origin, it said…" sentence, and keep her load reasoning from "…runs under this side" onward (with C1's replacement noun). Change "where your friend is offering branches" to something like "where it wants us to dig". Illustrative only: “It says the core could come up beside the center, or somewhere underneath it can’t see. It keeps offering places to dig.”

### D2 · Major · First reaction presumes the voice was audible and withholds the peril

- Where: L37–L45, mill room, right after the first voice leaves
- Text:
> “What said that?” Veysa asked.
> “A player.” Reniya looked at the silver thread between her boots. “Possibly.”
> “Whose player?”
> “Not ours.”
> Ludek pulled on his boot. “Good. I was worried I’d forgotten to pay someone.”
- Problem: Ground/knowledge and uptake. "What said that?" presumes Veysa heard something. The story later establishes that only Reniya hears (L111 "Reniya repeated it", L115 "the only open channel"), so the reader's model of who can hear flips. Veysa heard only Reniya's half ("your advice, formless voice", "Like in a TTRPG?") and would ask who Reniya is talking to. Reniya has just been told that someone made a great peril and that she and her companions must solve it, which is the prompt's premise, and she passes on none of it. The exchange turns into a riddle ("A player." / "Whose player?" / "Not ours.") that exists to set up Ludek's pay joke, and the joke works only if the reader switches "player" to "hired performer". The premise reaches the companions only by implication later (L157 "calling this a game", L201 "your friend"). The spoken lines also stay composed right after a metal root has punched through the floor.
- Fix: Let Veysa ask what someone who heard one side would ask, and let Reniya relay the essentials in her own words: a game, a peril, "you and your companions". She can be unsure. Ludek can keep a joke if it answers something Reniya actually said. Cut "Whose player?" / "Not ours." Illustrative only: “Who are you talking to?” / “Someone who says a friend of theirs made a peril, and the rest of them are meant to help us find it.”

### D3 · Major · Test setup turns into an ownership volley

- Where: L71–L81, mill room, before the speech tests
- Text:
> Veysa’s head came up. “On what?”
> “Empty floor. Short distance. No cutting.”
> “On my floor, then.”
> “Technically the miller’s.”
> “Tonight it’s mine.”
- Problem: Tennis Match, correction ping-pong and an inert exchange. Six equal one-liners run with no beats and end on a correction ("Technically the miller's.") and a last word ("Tonight it's mine."). "On my floor, then." doesn't follow, since Reniya has just said "Empty floor". Nothing about the test, the risk or the three people changes. This is the house rhythm the owner objects to, and it sits at the moment the story's method is being set up.
- Fix: Keep "We test one fact at a time" and Reniya's conditions. Cut L77–L81, or replace them with one line of Veysa's own concern drawn from the mill she knows (the west wall she repaired, the floor over the root), then go straight to Ludek taking the lamp.

### D4 · Major · The feeding rule is stated three times in fifteen lines

- Where: L83–L111, mill room speech tests and the second voice's first answer
- Text:
> Reniya understood. “Are you going to the water jug?”
> “Past travel does nothing,” he said. “A question does nothing. Something I mean to do next does.”
> “And lies do nothing. We have enough for a working rule.”
> A remembered journey is excluded. An inquiry is excluded. False declaration is excluded.
> “It says a spoken route you truly mean to take feeds it, if you’re within half a mile. Not thoughts, questions, lies, old journeys, or the voices. It can’t see underground.”
- Problem: Exposition. The experiments at L85–L99 already show the rule on the page. After them, Ludek and Veysa recite the results to people who watched them, the voice lists the same exclusions, and Reniya's relay lists them a third time. Nobody needs Veysa's "We have enough for a working rule"; it reads like a lab report. "Reniya understood." has no stated object: it could close her silent test or register Ludek's "Ask me something", and the reader has to guess. The voice's "I cannot perceive your buried result" only makes sense once the relay decodes it ("It can't see underground").
- Fix: Keep the experiments and at most one short spoken summary (Ludek's, since the last test was his). Give Veysa's L101 line something else to do, or cut it. Cut the relay down to what the tests did not show (half a mile, the voices don't count, it can't see underground) so that Ludek's "Half a mile from what?" still follows. Cut "Reniya understood." or say what she understood. Give the voice's last clause a plain referent in its own register: it cannot perceive anything below the surface.

### D5 · Major · Two-answer limit is asserted, then used to land a retort

- Where: L103–L121, mill room, the second voice
- Text:
> Your inquiry is permitted.
> One useless question, and perhaps one answer gone.
> “Wait,” Reniya said. “We have one answer left from this one.”
> “Then don’t spend it asking whether we ought to wait.”
- Problem: Ground/knowledge first, then uptake and a Punchline Patch. No voice states a limit, and the narration never counts the first voice's replies. Reniya still goes from "perhaps" (L107) to a flat count (L119). Veysa, who has heard none of the voices, accepts the count, and the rest of the plot runs on it (L165 "Its two answers had been spent", L283 "One question remained"). Reniya's "Wait" also never says what she wants: to ask Ludek's half-mile question now, or to save the answer? Veysa's reply rebuts a proposal nobody made (asking the voice whether to wait), so the exchange exists to land a last word, and Reniya's concern is dropped.
- Fix: Put the allowance on the page where it fits a character. The second voice speaks in permissions, so its arrival line can state the count, and L107's hedge becomes a count. Then have Reniya say what "Wait" is for, and let Veysa answer that choice plainly (save it, bring the splinter, move). Keep Ludek's "Half a mile from what?" and Veysa's "From the thing we find by following this." Illustrative only: *Two inquiries are permitted.*

### D6 · Major · "Don't cut it" argument talks past itself into a maxim

- Where: L151–L159, first orchard terrace, Veysa's bar behind the spur
- Text:
> “Don’t cut it,” Reniya said.
> “It was made by someone calling this a game.”
> “The wall doesn’t care what they call it.”
- Problem: Ground/uptake plus an epigram. Reniya's reason is too compressed to recover. "Calling this a game" could mean the obvious move is probably a trap (her game-runner's instinct), or that the thing isn't serious. Veysa answers the second reading with a maxim, then does what the first reading asks ("Ask what cutting does"). The argument contains no real disagreement and ends on a last word.
- Fix: Let Reniya put a game-runner's worry in plain words: whoever built this as a puzzle will have planned for the first thing a person reaches for. Cut "The wall doesn't care what they call it." Keep "I'm checking whether it's fixed to the stone." and "Ask what cutting does." If Veysa pushes back, the pushback should come from what the bar tells her about the stone, not from a maxim.

### D7 · Major · Final exchange: chores with no recoverable destination

- Where: L321–L333, at the catchment at dawn, the ending
- Text:
> Veysa checked the crown stone by stone while Ludek climbed down to the inspection hatch
> “Mill floor first?” Reniya asked.
> Veysa looked at the mud on all three of them. “Bring mortar.”
> Ludek sighed and took the heavier end of the tool basket.
> together they started down to the wall.
- Problem: Ground/referent and staging at the final beat. The three are already at the catchment (L321; Ludek is "climbing out" at L329), so "started down to the wall" has no recoverable destination. It could be the wall they just checked, or the loaded terrace wall from L129–L135 that the story never returns to. "Bring mortar" has no addressee and no source, and a look at the mud doesn't motivate it. The tool basket has never appeared before. What does work: after a night when nobody could say where they were going, Ludek's "Then the mill floor. Then breakfast." is a sincere spoken route that feeds nothing, and it bookends the opening question at L11. That quiet release is the ending's real content, and right now the staging confusion buries it.
- Fix: Fix the staging. Say which wall (if it is the terrace retaining wall, one clause also resolves that dangling threat), who fetches mortar and from where, and either introduce the basket earlier or drop it. Keep the order-of-chores exchange and Ludek's line. If needed, add one physical beat showing the ground stay quiet after he speaks. Do not add a line that explains the release or sums up the night. Apply C1's replacement noun to "Catchment joint."

## Continuity and prompt findings

### C1 · Major · Water-supply infrastructure carries the climax and ending

- Where: L181 (kiln trench), L197–L203 (buried wall), L213–L221 (service course, Veysa's refusal), L263 (simile), L271–L277 and L299 (the run), L315, L321–L327 (ending errand)
- Text:
> over a dry irrigation lip
> half buried above the catchment that supplied the market cisterns
> “Shorter for the root. Riskier for everyone who drinks tomorrow.”
> fast as water down a steep gutter
> It would need mortar before the first draw.
- Problem: This is a policy item: the style guide's "No default water infrastructure" entry (CANON, 2026-09-09) applies to authorized revisions. The prompt asks for nothing water-related. Even so, the buried wall at risk is a catchment that feeds the market cisterns and the containment channel is a spillway. Veysa's stakes line is "everyone who drinks tomorrow", and the ending errand is a catchment joint with an inspection hatch, to be fixed before the "first draw". There is also an irrigation lip and a gutter simile. The story predates the policy, so this is not an original defect, but an edit made from these notes must not preserve it. It rates Major because the water system is the climax's stake, not texture.
- Fix: Replace the catchment with a buried structure drawn from the story's own materials that is not a public-works problem, such as a cellar vault belonging to the upper press (L13) or the vinegar yard (L59). Keep the geometry: a buried wall under the downhill side of the kiln ring, and a dry channel that carries loose metal away from it. Rename the spillway as a channel with no water function (for example the kiln carts' ash run) and make the irrigation lip a terrace or cart lip. Restate Veysa's stakes line in terms of the people who would be in or under that structure at dawn. Change the ending's errand, the hatch and "first draw" to match, and replace the gutter simile. Keep "wet lime and old apples". Update "Spillway!" (L299), "Catchment crown" and "Catchment joint" consistently.
