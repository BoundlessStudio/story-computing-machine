# Outline

## Story

- Proposed reader-facing title: *The Players Above*.
- Premise and central promise: During a weekend TTRPG session at Malik Thorne's family cabin, Anjali Bose begins hearing a patient, formless voice that has quietly nudged her through several recent near-misses. When she demands its motive, it explains that it and its friends are playing a cosmic game: another player has placed a great peril in Anjali's world, while the others may guide her and her companions to identify and solve it. Outside, a black many-sided anchor is turning their imagined routes into an adaptive hunting shape; every confidently anticipated escape teaches it where to appear next, and by sunrise it will reach the road and feed on a town's far denser intentions. Anjali, Malik, and Devika Ruan must compare their different guides' partial advice, discover that guidance itself trains the peril, and use the uncertainty of a genuinely undecided die roll to expose and return its anchor. Resolve both promises: contain the peril before it leaves the lake and make the cosmic players accept that any further game requires the humans' consent and terms.
- Intended reader experience: Begin with the intimate absurdity of a disembodied adviser recognizing TTRPGs, then let playful genre recognition become a tense woodland puzzle. The reader should enjoy solving one step ahead without losing the unease that the danger was made for recreation. Relief comes through the friends' practiced trust and improvisation, followed by a dry, private renegotiation rather than awe, worship, or a public revelation.
- Generating force:
  - Mode: Plot-led branching-and-pruning puzzle. Each proposed route becomes unusable once a guide states or strongly confirms it, so successive failures reduce the group's safe physical space while enlarging their understanding of the rule.
  - Focal attachment or contradiction: Anjali loves games because shared rules make uncertainty bearable, yet she has also been treating the voice's timely advice as private proof that one correct path exists. Her friends value her decisiveness but do not want to become pieces she moves on an invisible expert's authority.
  - Plot-led wants and useful limit, if applicable: Anjali immediately wants to get all three friends to the car and demand a complete explanation; she also wants to keep the astonishing connection long enough to understand who has been helping her. Her useful flaw is clue-hunger: she promotes suggestive advice into certainty and acts before comparing what Malik and Devika know. Malik wants to protect the cabin and verify a material rule before risking the road; Devika wants to keep the group together while concealing that her harsher guide has been urging her to abandon the others.
- Counterforce or complication:
  - Source: The peril is a nonliving construct organized around a black polyhedral anchor. It cannot read minds, but it receives the cosmic players' declared predictions and manifests a jointed, lightless hunting body along the route they make most likely. Each successful interception gives it more reach; sunrise and nearby waking traffic would let it propagate beyond three people.
  - Credible inside logic, if a person: The formless guides sincerely want their chosen humans to survive, but each knows only one aspect of the peril and each wants to be the player whose advice wins. Devika's guide is not the creator and does not seek her death; it values keeping one marked participant alive over preserving the whole party. The creator remains offstage and cannot revise the placed peril during play.
  - Pressure or change created: Advice is useful enough to save them from immediate attacks but makes the next attack more precise. The friends must decide when evidence justifies trusting a voice, when to trust one another, and finally how to act without any guide—including the sympathetic one—knowing what they will do.
- Narrative design:
  - POV, tense, distance, and limitation: Close third past through Anjali, intimate with her habit of translating danger into maps, initiative order, and testable rules. Render the voice only as she hears it; Malik's and Devika's guides remain accessible through what they report, allowing concealment, misunderstanding, and disagreement without suggesting mind-reading or guaranteed truth.
  - Time shape and compression: One continuous night from the interrupted tabletop session to predawn containment, followed by a brief dawn return to the table. Use the marked-up game map, cooling food, and lost phone charge as clocks; no flashbacks beyond concise references to three earlier pieces of advice Anjali had mistaken for intuition.
  - Information and reveal strategy: Deliver the prompt exchange early and take the cosmic-game claim provisionally rather than stretching it into an identity mystery. Confirm it through three incompatible private instructions and the anchor's response. Reveal mechanics through failed plans: it appears on a guide-endorsed dock route, shifts when Malik receives a warning about the car, but misses Devika during an action she never tells her guide she is considering. Withhold the return condition until the friends have earned the question: the other players can open a return aperture only when the anchor is physically exposed, and cannot expose it themselves.
  - Governing movement: Interrupt the campaign; demand motive; hear the cosmic-game answer; discover all three friends have voices; attempt the dock and be intercepted; retreat with a glimpse of the anchor; compare exact wording at the table; test a low-stakes false route and learn that spoken lies do not work when no one expects them; split over Devika's survival-first instruction; notice her unguided improvisation went unseen; infer that sincere uncertainty is the blind spot; build a many-option plan around their ordinary dice; forbid further hints; move together to the clearing; roll only after every route is ready; expose the overextended anchor; return it; negotiate whether there will be another game.
- Speculative surplus: Near the anchor, balanced objects settle at implausible angles: dice stand on edges, a spoon rests upright on its bowl, and fallen pine needles bridge without touching ground. The nuisance gives the friends a rough proximity cue but neither predicts the peril's path nor solves it.
- Structural distinction: A shrinking possibility tree repeatedly reconvening at an ordinary game table. Failed branches become annotations on the friends' paper map until their tabletop habits supply a private field solution. The climax occurs in a dark clearing with three participants and no audience, proof campaign, crowd conversion, institutional rewrite, or victory ceremony; the final negotiation is personal consent, not a cosmic constitution.
- Decisive turn, deepening, or recognition: Anjali prepares to follow the formless voice's apparently safest route alone, then realizes every clean answer has tightened the hunt and that Devika survived precisely when she chose without consulting anyone above. She tells the voice that helping now means silence. The friends arrange several physically viable approaches to the anchor and commit to obeying a die none of them—including Anjali—will see until Malik shakes it beneath an overturned cup. When the cup lifts, all three act on the result at once. Deprived of a forecast, the construct spreads thin across competing routes; Devika hooks the exposed polyhedron into their metal dice tray, Malik pins it shut, and Anjali orders the waiting voice to open the return aperture rather than narrate another move.
- Aftereffect or live uncertainty: Returning the anchor collapses the hunting body and restores ordinary balance before sunrise, but it does not erase the night's fear or prove the guides' cosmology complete. Devika is angry that her guide treated companions as expendable; Malik keeps the scored tray as evidence but refuses to let any voice call it a trophy. The formless voice apologizes for mistaking permission to advise for permission to endanger and agrees to take the group's proposed rules back to its friends. Whether the creator agrees, how many worlds the players can reach, and whether the voices satisfy the universe's conditions for godhood remain unresolved.
- Opening / ending relation: Open on Anjali moving a miniature after a whispered tactical suggestion, then stopping mid-turn to ask what the formless voice wants with her. End at the same table after the real peril is gone: the voice asks whether this means they will play again, and Anjali slides over a blank session-zero sheet—no new move until everyone at both tables has agreed to the rules.

## Beats

1. At the cabin table, the voice corrects Anjali's risky game move; she refuses another unexplained rescue and asks the prompt's question. Its delighted TTRPG comparison is cut short when every die balances on an edge and something heavy circles outside.
2. Malik and Devika admit they also hear distinct voices. Acting on Anjali's guide-endorsed dock escape brings the lightless construct directly across the path; they retreat after Malik sees a black polyhedron moving inside it.
3. Back at the table, the friends record each guide's exact claims. A deliberately false plan fails to divert the construct, but an unannounced task Devika chose for herself goes unnoticed, distinguishing sincere expectation from spoken bait.
4. The guides disagree: Anjali's recommends the road, Malik's warns that the car is covered, and Devika's advises saving herself. Anjali nearly follows her voice alone before recognizing that each recommendation supplies the peril's next route.
5. They learn the limited return condition, then require all guides to go silent. The friends prepare several real approaches to the clearing, move supplies without committing to one, and use a concealed die roll to decide only at the last possible instant.
6. The peril overextends across unchosen futures; Devika draws its exposed anchor into the dice tray, Malik closes it, and Anjali calls for the return aperture. With the construct gone, they regroup at dawn and set consent, withdrawal, and no-created-peril terms before considering another game.

## Continuity

- Canon used: One coherent physical world persists through deep time; other dimensions may exist but are not required to connect established eras. Characters may hold incomplete models of reality, and no universal hidden authority regulates anomalies. Magic, technology, and divinity are cultural labels for underlying systems; godhood specifically requires worship, power, and authority. Only life uses magic, while nonliving devices or constructs may store, focus, transmit, or shape effects supplied by living agency.
- Boundaries and unknowns: Keep the cabin's era and geography unnamed and do not connect the players, anchor, aperture, or hunting construct to the Glass Gate, Realms, Fenwick watch, another portal/crossing system, or any recurring artifact. Establish only the locally witnessed game rules: one player placed this nonliving peril; other living or person-like players can advise marked participants and open its bounded return aperture once humans expose the anchor. Do not certify the voices as gods, omniscient narrators, creators of the world, or rulers of human fate. The cosmic-game account may be true within the incident without making mortal lives fictional, simulated, replaceable, or less morally real. Leave the players' origin, full capabilities, number, creator's motive, other games, and relationship to the universal afterlife unresolved.
