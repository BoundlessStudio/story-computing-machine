# Outline

## Story

- Premise and central promise: Every resident of Kelpass, an unimportant mountain trading stop, sleeps beneath a strange aurora and wakes without elapsed time inside Vastor Cut, a future excavation seeking the mythical First Kingdom. Tally-keeper Dema Vask treats the event as a reversible displacement: count everyone, find the north road, identify the responsible authority, and get home before the already-overdue tax worsens. Fulfill the comic collision between sacred legend and shabby reality, then resolve the return question honestly. Kelpass remains on its original ground after millions of years passed around it; no route to its former time is available, but its people need not accept a kingdom's identity.
- Intended reader experience: Dry culture-shock comedy opening into deep-time grief without losing the beer, taxes, and archaeological errors. Let awe run both ways. End with bounded agency rather than triumph: the past cannot be reached, but the villagers can choose how to spend their first future night.
- Generating force:
  - Mode: Plot-led widening audit. Dema accounts for headcount, stores, boundary, road, date, and jurisdiction; every solved total exposes a larger absence. Her outward walk turns clerical action into recognition of irrecoverable time.
  - Focal attachment or contradiction: Dema loves Kelpass as particular households, grudges, meals, and winter needs—not as history. She rejects future reverence, yet that reverence is also protecting the village from being handled as an object.
  - Plot-led wants and useful limit, if applicable: Dema immediately wants everyone counted, the north road reopened, and the tax journey resumed, while preventing excavators from claiming residents or homes as finds. Her useful limit is accounting faith: she assumes the right authority and complete total will make any crisis manageable, even after Yevrin Caul admits no return office or procedure exists. That habit also makes her an effective organizer.
- Counterforce or complication:
  - Source: Deep time removed Kelpass's surrounding mountain, roads, government, climate, and everyone outside the aurora boundary. The passable boundary opens onto fragile strata and an unfamiliar world; no active doorway, controls, or repeatable aurora remain.
  - Credible inside logic, if a person: Yevrin is a countervoice, not an antagonist. Her career is invested in the First Kingdom, but her immediate duty is keeping hundreds of living people clear of unstable cuts and stopping artifact protocols from being applied to them. She accepts correction without pretending her era's vocabulary can vanish instantly, refuses unsafe movement, and offers facts and access rather than a false promise.
  - Pressure or change created: Dema needs Yevrin's map and safe path; Yevrin needs Dema's household knowledge. Each offers useful knowledge entangled with a resisted claim: “First Kingdom” and “take us home.”
- Narrative design:
  - POV, tense, distance, and limitation: Close third past through Dema, intimate with her dry classifications, resisted panic, and household memory. Future equipment appears first through her guesses; translation works imperfectly around rank, ritual, and scale. She can test Yevrin's claims only through evidence and action; Yevrin remains exterior.
  - Time shape and compression: Aurora evening, an interval-free sleep, then the first waking day into night. Render the opening, awakening, first exchange, road walk, boundary recognition, and tavern return; compress the door-to-door count and initial safety work.
  - Information and reveal strategy: Establish insignificance, overdue tax, and bad beer before the aurora. Let tagged walls and careful figures above the cut reveal excavation before Dema understands it. Name the First Kingdom early; embody its error through a weighhouse stamp mistaken for a dynastic seal, export cups for ritual vessels, and scorched ale for a royal drink. Withhold the full elapsed scale until the count is complete. At the boundary, strata, missing mountain, and maps confirm the same place millions of years later. Supply no hidden purpose, chosen people, cyclical destiny, rescue mechanism, or established-Gate connection.
  - Governing movement: Fail to balance tax; watch aurora; sleep; wake; count; meet reverence with correction; secure a guide; cross from occupied rooms into labeled ruins; locate the road; confront missing landscape; exhaust claims of jurisdiction; accept no invented answer; return with practical questions instead of a route.
- Speculative surplus: Vastor Cut maps exposed surfaces with soft luminous survey motes. Iron preserved inside the aurora footprint repels them for several handspans, so nails, buckles, hoops, and Ulven Korr's cask bands leave absurd blank halos in the future maps and force Yevrin to mark a safe path by hand. The effect does not interpret artifacts, translate speech, date the village, explain the aurora, or help anyone return.
- Structural distinction: One widening inventory and lateral walk from occupied village to geological cross-section, not recurrence, containment, vertical transit, chase, countdown, or public proof. The decisive venue is a severed dirt road. No crowd converts, village accepts a collective identity, or institution rewrites itself.
- Decisive turn, deepening, or recognition: At the north edge, Dema's road stops one pace past its last preserved rut and dropped hoop pin. Yevrin shows its mineral traces continuing through layers laid down after the mountain vanished. Dema exhausts the offices that ought to owe restitution; Yevrin can only record that Kelpass was a village, not decide what its people become. Dema recognizes that proving insignificance cannot restore yesterday. She asks instead about air, water, weather, food, and permission to cross occupied thresholds.
- Aftereffect or live uncertainty: Everyone survived, but health, status, supplies, and relations with the future remain open. Kelpass has short-term shelter; Vastor Cut has limited support for hundreds. Households make different first-night choices, and future personnel enter homes only by invitation. The First Kingdom myth persists; the aurora offers no evidence of recurrence.
- Opening / ending relation: Open in Ulven's tavern with Dema recording overdue tax beside three unsold casks. End there after the failed road search: invited guest Yevrin tastes the beer her era calls a royal sacrament and finds it honestly mediocre. Dema asks how cold the future night will be, and the next practical answer begins.

## Beats

1. In Ulven's tavern, Dema cannot balance Kelpass's overdue tax because three casks of scorched beer never sold. Aurora fills the peaks; she orders the door left open for late travelers before the whole village falls asleep.
2. Dema wakes under excavation light and counts before asking where: every resident and guest inside Kelpass is alive with the same meals and livestock; nobody beyond its edge answers.
3. Yevrin follows a hand-marked path through survey-mote gaps. Her translator gives Dema royal rank and names the First Kingdom. Dema corrects the “palace,” “seal,” and “sacramental” beer, then asks for the provincial road.
4. Yevrin says millions of years passed and requests that everyone remain within the safe cut. Dema agrees only if Yevrin shows her the north road; Ulven sends the sealed tax copy in case the future has a clerk.
5. Crossing occupied rooms and labeled ruins, Dema makes the legend smaller while Yevrin's maps make the absent mountain larger. Yevrin cannot promise to erase the myth; Dema forbids treating residents as finds.
6. The preserved road stops at the boundary. Mineral traces through the cut and the missing ridge prove the place endured while its era did not. Dema recognizes her dropped hoop pin and exhausts every authority she might charge with returning them.
7. Yevrin offers only truthful record, safe access, and limited aid. Dema asks for environmental facts and consent at home thresholds. She retains the tax copy for its household names, not collection.
8. The result travels door to door rather than through a proclamation. Households choose their first-night arrangements. Yevrin becomes an invited tavern guest, the legendary beer remains bad, and Dema begins with the coming cold.

## Continuity

- Canon used: One physical world persists through millions of years of civilizational layers; later cultures may know earlier lives only through degraded, dispersed, and misread evidence; ordinary lives remain valid amid deep-time archaeology; local cultures may have radically incomplete historical models.
- Boundaries and unknowns: Leave Kelpass's era, civil calendar, Galactic Cycle coordinate, and ordering relative to every named era unresolved. The aurora preservation is one local, unexplained event with no established agency, builders, recurrence, return capability, or relationship to the Glass Gate, another crossing, another sleeping system, or a named magical technology. Vastor Cut, its survey motes, and its translation aid are local future practices and establish no universal archaeological institution or technology. The First Kingdom is a future interpretation, not authority that retroactively creates a real ancient state. Do not use the event to restore lost people, erase elapsed history, or establish a general law of time travel, stasis, mythic destiny, or paradox.
