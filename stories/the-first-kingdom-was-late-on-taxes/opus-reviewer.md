# Opus review: Where the Mountain Used to Be

Independent audit for a later editing pass. It is not a `review.md` verdict,
does not change canon state, and does not authorize edits by itself.

## Verdict

- Dialogue: REVISE — Ground faults in the opening exchange and the widow/Odan resolution, no response at all to "No way", and Sevri turns into the story's thesis voice at the cemetery.
- Prose: REVISE — the final image cannot be staged, and custody terms arrive as legal minutes in the middle act.
- Continuity and prompt: REVISE — the prompt is delivered and the length (about 3,270 words) is in band, but Odan holds the tax bundle in the final exchange with no handover shown.
- Edit scope: targeted-dialogue
- Existing review.md said: Verdict PASS; "Dialogue: PASS"; disagree. It missed the Ground faults, the missing reaction to "No way", and the bundle gap.

This story's dialogue is better than the collection's house default in one important way. The translation layer gives Sevri a real limit, and it produces the best jokes: Sevri shrinks Ormela's retorts to "She says no." and "Loading. Only loading." The minor villagers (the east-house owner, the brewer's wife, the twelve-year-old at the goat pen) also sound like people with petty, specific stakes. The failures cluster where the story needs a line to carry theme or resolution. Sevri can't render "Founding People", yet at the cemetery she sets out the remains/place/authority and family/claim/ownership distinctions. Aruz states the consent lesson the action just showed so that Sevri can cap it with an epigram. The widow's last exchange with Odan contradicts itself about what he understood. The biggest news in the story, that there is no way home, gets no reaction from anyone. On the page, Ormela, Belkin, Aruz and the cooper also share one composed, dry-stinger register, although the summary describes a frightened, quarrelling village. The edit pass should fix the Ground faults, put Sevri's limits back, give Ormela one unguarded beat, and let the villagers' mess reach the quoted lines. It should not add wit.

## Edit guardrails

- Prompt (acceptance authority): the village sleeps under an aurora and wakes millions of years later inside an archaeological excavation. The future knows it only as the mythical First Kingdom, and the villagers see themselves as an unimportant trading stop with overdue taxes, mediocre beer, and no idea how to get home. The story must be complete and resolve that promise, stay within 2,500–4,000 words (currently about 3,270) and stay broadly accessible. The 2026-08-21 rewrite request authorizes dialogue rewrites under the 08-21 profile. Keep close third person on Ormela, in past tense.
- Established story facts to keep: ninety-seven found. The spring, road, fields and cemetery lay outside the aurora boundary. There is "No way" back. The cemetery containers stay sealed until villager and council representatives are heard. Land and history remain unresolved. The move is to Freight Hall Nine, with some villagers staying as stewards. No copy of the bundle is made. The potter owes six copper. Keep the vinegar cask and the "millions of years" beer callback.
- Universe: this story sits squarely on the LOCKED "Memory, myth, and recoverable truth" entry (`universe/premise.md`), with tax tallies misread as a royal record. Do not settle the First Kingdom myth into truth or falsehood. The aurora stays story-local. Do not tie it to the Glass Gate or any crossing system (`universe/rules.md`, "Portals and crossing systems", CANON). The LOCKED personhood entry is honored because villagers are never artifacts. Keep that.
- What already works and must not be flattened: the translation comedy (Sevri simplifying, refusing to translate, the device "more certain than she was"). The widow's short questions. The messy household corrections at the loading bench. The cooper's contrarian running gag, which ends in "Mediocre".

## Dialogue findings

### D1 · Major · Opening water exchange: lost referents and quip trade

- Where: L29–L39, Ormela calls Belkin at the cistern after the mountain vanishes
- Text:
> “I see it.” The well steward was already kneeling at the public cistern
> “No, you don't get a bucket yet. Nobody does.”
> “Less every time you ask.”
> “Useful.”
> glared past her at a woman with two pails
- Problem: This is Ground/referent plus a Punchline Patch. Ormela only calls "Belkin!", so "I see it" has nothing to point at yet. The reader learns about the water problem four lines later. "You don't get a bucket" reads as addressed to Ormela, because the woman with the pails appears only at L39. Then "How much?" gets a quip ("Less every time you ask", after she has asked once) and a sarcastic counter-quip ("Useful.") before the answer arrives. This is correction ping-pong in the story's first exchange. It makes two frightened villagers sound identical and composed, and it sets the register for the rest of the story.
- Fix: Show the woman with the pails before Belkin turns her away, or tag the line to her. Make Belkin's first line name what he is looking at: the dry inlet or the level. Cut the "Less every time you ask" / "Useful." pair so he answers with the measurement in his own grumbling, specific way. Keep "No spring coming in" and "Drinking cups first." Illustrative only: "Spring's stopped. I've got what's in the cistern and nothing after."

### D2 · Major · Consent lesson set up for an epigram

- Where: L95–L105, Aruz and Sevri after the girl with the split lip
- Text:
> and made Sevri repeat that it could come off.
> “Ask before touch,” Aruz told Sevri.
> “Ask again when the person understands what you're asking.”
> Sevri looked at the crowd of officials gathering behind Odan. “That word will take longer.”
> It took longer because the future kept bringing the wrong nouns.
- Problem: This is the Philosopher plus a Punchline Patch. L95 has already shown informed consent: Aruz tests the adhesive on herself and makes Sevri repeat that the patch comes off. L97–L101 then restate that as a rule, and Aruz's rule exists to tee up Sevri's closer. "That word" is Ground/referent: Aruz used a sentence, not a word, so the reader must guess whether the word is "understands", "ask" or consent itself. The narration at L105 then decodes the quip. Sevri also produces a fluent, idiomatic aphorism here, although minutes later she can't manage "Founding People".
- Fix: Keep "Ask before touch" / "I know ask." (Aruz simplifying her grammar for Sevri is a good touch). Replace L101–L103 with something practical aimed at the next patient, or cut them and let the gesture toward the officials carry the point. Re-bridge L105 so it no longer depends on "That word will take longer". Illustrative only: "Then ask her again. Slower. She's watching your hands, not your mouth."

### D3 · Major · "No way" lands with no uptake

- Where: L121–L129, Ormela asks about going home and the official cuts in
- Text:
> Sevri did not need Odan. “No known way.” She touched her board, removed _known_, and said it again. “No way.”
> The polished official leaned toward the recording lenses hovering behind the deck.
- Problem: This is Ground/uptake at the decisive reveal. The prompt's third promise ("no idea how to get home") is answered in two words, and the listener registers nothing: no line, no gesture, no pause. The next beat is the official's proclamation, and Ormela's next line is the "tax reeve" joke. Earlier, the cemetery interruption (L49) is legible because the narration marks it ("Ormela opened her mouth… Work took her before an answer did"). Here nothing marks it, and no villager ever voices the loss on the page. That makes the story's composure look like an omission rather than a choice.
- Fix: Give Ormela one short beat between L123 and L125, in her own register: protective, practical, counting people. It must not be a speech about home. Keep Sevri's deletion of _known_; it is a strong choice. Illustrative only: Ormela glanced back at the runners still marking doors. "Don't say that near the lanes."

### D4 · Major · Sevri becomes the story's legal explainer at the cemetery

- Where: L159–L173, the widow learns about the cemetery boxes
- Text:
> “Our dead?” the widow asked.
> “Wait. I need the difference. Your dead, the place, the... right to choose. They are not one word.”
> “Are they our grandchildren?” the widow asked.
> “No,” Sevri said. “They cannot prove family. They may have a claim to be heard. Not the same as owning.”
- Problem: This is Ground/uptake plus the Philosopher. The widow asks one plain question: are these our dead? Sevri doesn't answer it. She introduces "the place" and "right to choose", content absent from the prior beat, and in doing so previews the order's categories before anyone has contested them. The plain "yes" never arrives. At L173, "They cannot prove family" is a good, honest answer. But "They may have a claim to be heard. Not the same as owning." hands the translator the adjudicator's legal register and the story's thesis, in fuller fluency than she has anywhere else.
- Fix: At L161, have Sevri answer the widow's question first, then show that she lacks the vocabulary for the rest, rather than listing the concepts. At L173, keep "No" and "They cannot prove family." Cut or roughen the claim/ownership sentences, because the order at L197 already carries that distinction. Keep "Our dead?", "Are they our grandchildren?" and Ormela's "That's not what she asked." Illustrative only: "Yes. From your slope. What they want with them, I don't have words for yet."

### D5 · Major · Widow/Odan: reply to words he didn't hear, and an unanchored "Locked"

- Where: L199–L205, after the adjudicator's order
- Text:
> “Paused isn't ended,” the widow said.
> Odan heard her tone if not the words. “No,” he said through Sevri. “Locked until the next hearing.”
- Problem: This is Ground/knowledge plus Ground/referent at the hearing's resolution. The narration says Odan did not get the words, yet his "No" agrees precisely with their content ("paused isn't ended"). And if he speaks "through Sevri", she was there to translate the widow too. Heard as a reply to tone alone, "No" reads as reassurance, the opposite polarity. "Locked" doesn't fit what the widow named: a ceremony is paused, not locked. The reader must decide whether he means the ceremony, the exhibition or the cemetery room.
- Fix: Choose one channel. Either Sevri translates the widow's line (cut "heard her tone if not the words"), or Odan answers only the tone, in words that work for a tone-only reading. Name what stays shut. Keep the widow's line and the "less than the widow wanted" beat that follows. Illustrative only: "No. It stays shut until the next hearing. After that I can't promise."

### D6 · Minor · "Dynasty can carry itself" contradicts its staging

- Where: L221–L225, the regalia labels on the first pallet
- Text:
> The cooper sat on the pallet. “Dynasty can carry itself.”
> Odan closed his eyes for one breath. Then he stripped both labels off.
- Problem: This is Ground/referent. The line suggests "I'll carry my own adze", but the cooper sits down on the cargo, which reads as asking to be carried. Odan reacts immediately without Sevri translating, so he is reading the gesture, and the gesture points the other way.
- Fix: Match the action to the line, for example by having the cooper lift his adze off the pallet. Alternatively, keep him sitting and give him a plain protest that fits sitting. Let Odan respond to the visible gesture or show Sevri's one-word translation.

### D7 · Minor · Tag and beat mechanics

- Where: L65 (Belkin's water test), L113 and L225 (exasperation beats)
- Text:
> “Water,” he allowed. “Too clean.”
> Odan and the official answered at the same time. Sevri closed her eyes.
- Problem: "Allowed" is a said-bookism doing the grudging for Belkin, whose words already do it. The eyes-closed exasperation beat is used twice, for Sevri at L113 and Odan at L225.
- Fix: Use "said" or a physical beat at L65. Vary one of the two eyes-closed beats; keep "answered at the same time".

## Prose findings

### P1 · Major · Final image can't be staged

- Where: L271–L281, Freight Hall Nine, the last pour
- Text:
> Belkin had chalked drinking times on a pillar and was adding a separate line for washing.
> then found that someone had rescued the vinegar cask and left the better ale behind.
> Ormela poured to the chalk line Belkin had marked, stopped when he rapped the pipe with his wrench, and poured the remainder back into the jug.
- Problem: The only chalk marks established are times on a pillar, so "the chalk line Belkin had marked" has no vessel. It is unclear what Ormela pours (ale for the cooper? from the vinegar cask?), from what, and where "the remainder" comes from if she is already pouring from the jug. The story's closing image asks the reader to picture something they can't assemble.
- Fix: Set up the mark earlier (for example, Belkin chalking a ration line on the jug or cups when the tanks arrive) or change the final action to one that uses established objects. Make it clear what is being poured and from which vessel. Keep the idea of Ormela accepting Belkin's measure and the cooper's held-out cup.

### P2 · Major · Custody terms delivered as legal minutes

- Where: L153 (Odan's conditionals), L197 (the adjudicator's order), L255 (loading summary); also L175, L217–L219
- Text:
> Odan moved between these circles, never promising past what he controlled.
> Living people, their clothing, and what they carried were outside artifact custody.
> Property left with the people using it. Site trays stayed sealed.
- Problem: This is a systemic procedural register, and it borders on exposition dump. The middle act resolves its pressure through lists of terms: if/then conditionals, a full order paragraph, and a restatement of custody during loading. No one reacts inside them. For a comic, broadly accessible premise, L197 reads like minutes. It also repeats outcomes the reader already has (sealed containers at L175, occupied houses at L153).
- Fix: Compress L197 to the few outcomes villagers actually hear and react to, attached to the people they affect: the widow and the cemetery, the households and their goods, "The land beneath Kettlebridge was unresolved. So was the history." (keep that). Cut the L255 restatements. Keep one of Odan's conditionals at L153 as his signature, not four.

### P3 · Minor · Narrator epigrams restating the theme

- Where: L179 (the bundle), L193 (the hearing), L261 (last lift)
- Text:
> A tax list was useful because it was narrow. The future liked it because they believed it was wide.
> It was slower. It was also the first arrangement that worked.
> No road bent toward home, because home had become the thing inside the ropes.
- Problem: Each paragraph closes on a neat aphorism that explains what the scene has already shown. L261 comes close to a polished closing emblem.
- Fix: Keep the concrete material (the two hens, the five-way hearing, the missing mountain shoulder) and cut or plainly rephrase the summarizing clause. At L261, end on "No road bent toward home." or on the image.

## Continuity and prompt findings

### C1 · Blocking · Odan holds the tax bundle with no handover

- Where: L263–L265, the last lift; set up at L181–L189 and L227–L255
- Text:
> Ormela laid the bundle on the brew-house table but kept one hand on it.
> Odan accepted the sheets one lift at a time.
> Odan held out her tax bundle.
- Problem: The story makes Ormela's guarding of the bundle a through-line: one hand on it, "Nothing copied", "It would protect your copy", "She says no". The only things shown passing to Odan are the loading sheets, which he returns. Then the final exchange opens with Odan returning the bundle itself. The relationship move that the final exchange depends on (Ormela trusting him with it) happens off the page, and Sevri's "No copy" answers a handover the reader never saw.
- Fix: Add one short beat, probably near L255, where Ormela hands Odan the bundle, for example to check the final count against the loading sheets, and shows her reluctance or her decision. Then L263–L269 can stand unchanged.

### C2 · Minor · Small referent collisions

- Where: L135 vs L23 (bridge); L221 (tax chest); L179 ("one widow")
- Text:
> Three quarters late, because the bridge washed out
> the bridge over the narrow runnel
> identified Ormela's tax chest as ceremonial regalia
> It recorded one widow as owing two hens
- Problem: In a village named Kettlebridge, "the bridge washed out" reads as the bridge still standing at L23. "Tax chest" appears once, beside the ever-present "bundle", and readers may take it for the same object. "One widow" invites confusion with "the widow" of the cemetery thread.
- Fix: Name the washed-out bridge as the road bridge or the lower ford. Make clear that the chest is a separate strongbox, or make it the bundle. Either confirm the hens widow is the cemetery widow or distinguish her.

### C3 · Minor · Water-infrastructure texture (policy note)

- Where: L31, L43, L57, L145, L211–L213, L243, L271, L281 (Belkin's cistern, latrine siting, inlet pipe, tanks and pipes, floor drains, waste tank, knocking pipes)
- Text:
> Belkin marked two lined latrine trenches where the ground seemed thick enough
> working water, visible pipe runs, high ventilation, broad loading access, animal bays, floor drains
> Belkin cut the second furniture lift when a waste tank arrived late.
> Water knocked in exposed pipes.
- Problem: `universe/style-guide.md`, "No default water infrastructure" (CANON), applies to authorized revisions. The prompt didn't ask for water supply or sanitation, yet a well steward and his cistern, tanks and pipes are recurring texture and help drive the Freight Hall choice. Losing the spring follows from the premise, so drinking water itself is defensible. The incidental infrastructure is not.
- Fix: Add no new water-system material in rewritten dialogue. Where it costs nothing, trim incidental items (floor drains, pipe runs, waste tank, knocking pipes, the latrine-uphill beat). Recasting Belkin's role is beyond this edit's scope; leave it unless the owner asks.
