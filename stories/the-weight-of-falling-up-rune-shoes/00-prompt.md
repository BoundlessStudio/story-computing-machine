# Prompt contract

## Verbatim writing prompt

> **[WP] You're an apprentice at a prestigious magic school! It's time for you to take your flying assessment, and take to the skies. Others choose brooms, levitation spells, or mounts. You show up with something a little different.**

## Verbatim peer-method revision directive

> i now like the end story but the levitation is too close the main character novel idea; i am find with having 2 brooms (shows its the standard) and a flying creature (showing its rare but well understood)

## Verbatim wind-rune revision directive

> i think the runes should work more like wind magic; so its less like walking/running and more like flying with rockets boots (think witch hat atelier).

## Story controls

- Working title: The Weight of Falling Up
- Target length: 2,500–4,000 words
- POV: First person, centered on the apprentice taking the assessment
- Tense: Past
- Tone and genre: Wonder-forward school fantasy with a nimble, wry voice, playful magical invention, genuine airborne peril, and an emotionally satisfying resolution
- Audience/content rating: PG / broad teen and adult readership; non-graphic peril
- Required elements: A prestigious magic school; an apprentice facing a consequential flying assessment; two peers visibly using brooms so broom flight reads as the ordinary standard; one peer using a flying creature that reads as uncommon but familiar and well understood by the school; the apprentice visibly drawing wind-channeling magic runes directly onto their shoes as their different means of flight; those paired runes vent directed magical wind from the shoes and propel the apprentice through sustained flight with a rocket-boot-like feel; substantial action in the sky; an earned assessment result and complete character arc
- Prohibited elements: No solid-air footholds, luminous steps, air staircase, or walking/running-on-air mechanism as the primary flight model; no levitation candidate, levitation spell, or levitation comparison in the final story; no substitution of pre-enchanted shoes for the required act of rune drawing; no flight method that is merely a disguised broom or conventional mount; no protagonist mount, living flight partner, windlace, directional plummet, tether rig, or apparatus-centric engineering solution; no unearned rescue or deus ex machina; no dense procedural explanation that overwhelms wonder or character; no reuse of either rejected version's characters, names, schools, plot beats, set pieces, or prose; no contradiction of authoritative shared-universe canon

## Assumptions

- The revision directive is controlling: drawing magic runes on shoes is the story's central creative seed, not a cosmetic detail.
- The peer-method revision directive overrides the original prompt's levitation example for this production version. The final story uses two broom candidates and one flying-creature candidate instead.
- The two broom users should make broom flight feel established and standard without implying that every culture uses it. The creature should be rare at Nine Eaves but covered by familiar assessment procedures rather than treated as mysterious or unprecedented.
- The wind-rune directive supersedes the earlier air-step interpretation. The shoes function as paired wind-thrust focuses: rune geometry shapes direction and output, while the living wearer supplies power, balance, and moment-to-moment control.
- “Rocket boots” is a motion reference, not a technological claim: the flight should feel propelled, continuous, and burst-driven, with visible wind jets and differential thrust rather than solid surfaces underfoot.
- The *Witch Hat Atelier* reference is treated only as high-level inspiration for elegant, visually legible drawn magic; the story must retain its own terminology, imagery, and prose.
- The apprentice must draw at least one consequential rune on-page, and the climax must depend on how the shoe-runes work, fail, change, or are redrawn.
- The prompt does not require an existing character, school, era, or location, so canon-compatible local inventions are permitted.
- Because the previous result was rejected without line-specific feedback, this attempt keeps only the original prompt, title, and new rune-shoes directive while rebuilding all other creative choices from scratch.
- New reusable setting facts remain proposals in `06-canon-delta.md` unless explicitly promoted by the user.

## Completion tests

- The result is a complete short story, not a synopsis or chapter fragment.
- The central promise of the prompt is dramatized and resolved.
- The story passes canon and continuity review.
- The apprentice draws magical runes directly on their shoes on-page, and those runes are the actual unconventional means of flight.
- Two broom candidates visibly establish broom flight as the local standard, while the flying creature is visibly rare but institutionally understood.
- The shoe-runes remain materially distinct from the standard broom method, the flying creature, and both rejected flight concepts.
- Flight is propelled by directed wind thrust from the shoes, not by walking or running on solidified air.
- Foot angle and unequal left/right thrust visibly control pitch, yaw, braking, and recovery; damage to one rune produces a specific asymmetric failure.
- The final story contains no levitation candidate, spell, or comparison.
- The aerial climax and assessment result arise causally from the established rune mechanics and the apprentice's choices.
- The prose foregrounds magic, character, and sensation rather than reading like a technical manual.
- The new story has no substantive creative dependence on either rejected version.
- Every character-facing name and alias is checked against `stories/NAMES.md`;
  any deliberate reuse has a documented narrative reason.
