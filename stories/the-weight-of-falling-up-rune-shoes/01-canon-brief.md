# Canon brief

**Research verdict:** CLEAR TO PLAN WITH CONSTRAINTS. No authoritative canon
conflict requires a ruling. The rune-shoe seed is compatible with canon if the
shoes and their marks remain nonliving tools that store, focus, transmit, or
shape magic supplied through the apprentice's living agency. The story must
establish its rune and flight mechanics as a local system rather than universal
lore.

**Scope note:** `stories/INDEX.md` contains no story whose Canon column is
`yes`, so no finalized story prose currently has canon authority. Both rejected
*The Weight of Falling Up* production versions are non-canon and excluded from
creative research; their registry names remain reserved production memory.

## Hard constraints

1. **The setting belongs to one persistent deep-time physical world.** The
   school may occupy an unnamed era and locally invented place, but the story
   must not casually establish a separate fantasy planet, plane, or cosmology
   as though that were already canon. Other dimensions or timelines are
   possible but not required and are not a shortcut around the shared world's
   constraints. (`universe/premise.md` — “The deep-time shared world”)

2. **“Magic” is the viewpoint culture's label, not an objective universal
   category.** Many real systems may coexist, imitate, hybridize, or have
   unrelated origins. The school may call rune-working, broom flight, and
   mount use “magic,” but the narrative must not declare that all three share
   one universal substance, grammar, origin, or operating law unless the story
   establishes a narrowly local relationship. (`universe/rules.md` —
   “System categories”; `universe/glossary.md` — “Magic, technology, and
   divinity”)

3. **Only life can use magic.** Shoes, soles, ink, chalk, thread, runes, and
   other nonliving components may store, focus, transmit, or shape magic that a
   living being supplies; they may not independently be the magic user. The
   required causal chain is therefore:

   **living apprentice → magical supply/activation/control → drawn marks and
   shoes as shaper/focus → airborne force and movement.**

   A mark may retain stored power or a fixed response after living activation,
   but an ordinary shoe cannot independently choose goals, interpret danger,
   improvise a rescue, or cast new magic. Avoid making the footwear a
   constructed mind or living artifact; whether such a thing is alive would
   have to be established rather than assumed, and that expansion is
   unnecessary for this prompt. (`universe/rules.md` — “Living agency and
   magic”)

4. **The on-page drawing must be mechanically consequential, not decorative.**
   The contract requires visible wind-rune drawing directly on the shoes, with
   the paired marks shaping directed magical wind into thrust from the
   footwear. The wearer supplies living power and control. Pre-enchanted
   footwear, solid-air footholds, a disguised broom working, or autonomous
   shoes with cosmetic symbols does not satisfy the seed.
   (`stories/the-weight-of-falling-up-rune-shoes/00-prompt.md` — “Verbatim
   wind-rune revision directive,” “Required elements,” “Prohibited elements,”
   and “Completion tests”)

5. **The peer-method override is controlling.** The final story must show two
   broom candidates so broom flight reads as Nine Eaves' ordinary standard,
   plus one flying-creature candidate whose method is uncommon but familiar
   enough to have established assessment expectations. Levitation must not
   appear in the final prose. The rune shoes must remain physically and
   causally distinct from both broom riding and creature-borne flight through
   paired wind jets, foot-angle control, differential thrust, asymmetric
   failure, and the climax.
   (`stories/the-weight-of-falling-up-rune-shoes/00-prompt.md` — “Verbatim
   peer-method revision directive,” “Required elements,” “Prohibited
   elements,” and “Completion tests”)

6. **Costs and desire are system-specific.** Fatigue, concentration, heat,
   damaged soles, fading pigment, emotional resolve, or another limitation may
   be invented for this local practice, but none may be asserted as the
   universal fuel or price of magic. If the story assigns the rune system a
   cost, it must apply consistently within the story. (`universe/rules.md` —
   “Costs, desire, and local mechanisms”)

7. **Era claims must respect the established low-magic trough.** In the
   modern-like Nell-and-Bash era, ordinary life treats magic as myth or legend.
   An openly prestigious school where students routinely fly by brooms and
   occasionally by understood flying mounts would therefore need a specific concealment or
   anomaly explanation if placed in that era. The cleaner choice is an unnamed
   era in which this local magical culture is active, without locating it
   relative to Ravel or the Glass-Sea era. (`universe/timeline.md` —
   “Established era order”; `universe/glossary.md` — “Modern-like era” and
   “Ravel”)

8. **Do not infer continuity from resemblance.** Runes, shoes, aerial motifs,
   school traditions, or similar-looking artifacts do not automatically descend
   from another system or civilization. Any ancestry claim requires story
   evidence and would become proposed canon, not an already established fact.
   (`universe/rules.md` — “Artifacts, motifs, and folklore”;
   `universe/style-guide.md` — “Continuity and evidence discipline”)

9. **The story remains broadly accessible and non-graphic.** Airborne peril may
   be real, but treatment should emphasize character and consequence rather
   than graphic injury. (`universe/style-guide.md` — “Audience and content
   boundaries”; `stories/the-weight-of-falling-up-rune-shoes/00-prompt.md` —
   “Story controls”)

## Useful established context

- Deep time allows many unrelated magical cultures and active eras. A
  prestigious school can be a local institution in an otherwise undefined era
  without needing a crossover, a flagship location, or a full place in the
  master chronology. (`universe/premise.md` — “The deep-time shared world”;
  `universe/style-guide.md` — “Tonal range, scale, and connection”)

- Cultures and characters may hold incomplete or mistaken models while reality
  remains coherent backstage. The apprentice and teachers can use confident
  school vocabulary without the narration claiming omniscient cosmological
  truth. (`universe/premise.md` — “Knowledge and connection”)

- Nonliving focuses are explicitly permitted. That gives the shoes and newly
  drawn marks real causal work to do—storing, focusing, transmitting, or
  shaping the apprentice's magic—without violating the living-agency lock.
  (`universe/rules.md` — “Living agency and magic”)

- Separate systems may reproduce or imitate one another's results. Brooms,
  mounts, and rune-shoes can all achieve flight while remaining
  mechanically and historically distinct. (`universe/rules.md` — “System
  categories”)

- Civilizations repeatedly copy, steal, trade, reinvent, and misattribute old
  systems. If the story needs a modest sense of institutional history, the
  school may possess a locally contested or incomplete account of its practice;
  this should enrich the immediate story rather than establish a universal
  origin for runes. (`universe/premise.md` — “The deep-time shared world” and
  “Memory, myth, and recoverable truth”)

- A small, self-contained school story is a valid entry point. It does not need
  to mention other eras, recurring characters, gods, portals, or the larger
  cosmology. (`universe/style-guide.md` — “Tonal range, scale, and connection”)

## Conflicts or ambiguity

- **No authoritative source conflict was found.** No `LOCKED` or `CANON` entry
  defines runes, magical writing, flight, brooms, magical mounts,
  apprenticeships, flying assessments, or magic schools. This is an omission,
  not proof of any proposed mechanic.

- **The only agency ambiguity is the tool/user boundary.** Canon permits a
  nonliving object to retain or shape power from life, but it forbids treating
  that object as an independent magic user. Fixed, legible responses to a
  living wearer's input are compatible; magical target selection, judgment,
  self-directed correction, or spontaneous rune changes would approach
  independent use and require an unnecessary ruling on whether the object is
  alive. (`universe/rules.md` — “Living agency and magic”)

- **Broom prevalence and mount familiarity are not canon-defined.** The final
  story may establish locally that brooms are the standard at Nine Eaves and
  that flying mounts are rarer but supported by familiar procedures. Neither
  claim may be generalized to other schools, cultures, or eras.

- **The era and location are unassigned.** This is open space, but placing the
  school in the modern-like low-magic trough would create a continuity burden.
  Ravel's chronology is itself unresolved and should not be used as an
  unexplained placement solution. (`universe/timeline.md` — “Established era
  order”; `universe/glossary.md` — “Ravel”)

- **No canon story prose can resolve these omissions.** Every row currently
  listed in `stories/INDEX.md` has Canon `no`; drafts, plans, rejected versions,
  archive records, and proposed canon deltas therefore have no authority.
  (`universe/README.md` — “Authority order”; `stories/INDEX.md` — “Story
  index”)

## Unknowns

The following are **Unknown**, not established lore:

- whether runes, glyphs, sigils, or drawn spell-writing exist anywhere in canon;
- the rune language, symbol inventory, syntax, medium, drawing technique,
  durability, repair rules, and relationship to footwear;
- whether a completed mark stores power, requires continuous living input,
  responds to intent, responds to movement, or uses some other local trigger;
- the physical model of broom, mount, or wind-rune shoe flight, including
  thrust, gravity, force, momentum, inertia, height limits, braking, hovering,
  weather interactions, and bodily strain;
- exactly how Nine Eaves' standard brooms or rare flying mounts achieve flight;
- whether magic in this local system has a cost, and if so what that cost is;
- the school's name, location, founders, history, prestige, curriculum,
  hierarchy, assessment rules, safety provisions, and attitude toward
  unconventional techniques;
- the apprentice's age, background, magical aptitude, relationships, and reason
  for choosing rune-shoes;
- the story's exact era or its relationship to the modern-like, Ravel, and
  Glass-Sea eras;
- whether any magical artifact or constructed intelligence relevant to this
  story qualifies as alive;
- all character names and aliases for the new version;
- any reusable setting fact arising from this story until it is proposed in
  `06-canon-delta.md` and later explicitly promoted.

Each item may be invented narrowly for this story where allowed below, but
absence of canon evidence must not be narrated or recorded as pre-existing
shared-universe fact.

## Safe invention space

All items in this section are **permitted local design space, not canon facts**.

1. **Use an independently invented school in an unnamed active-magic era.**
   Limit prestige to the school, city, region, tradition, or culture that can
   actually know it. Avoid naming a universal governing academy or making its
   assessment rules apply to every magical culture.

2. **Make the drawn marks temporary interfaces rather than autonomous
   spellcasters.** A compatible local mechanism may have the apprentice draw
   a pattern that channels their own living magic through the shoe. The drawing
   can define direction, permitted response, or force shape; the apprentice's
   continuing or repeated input supplies agency. A stored charge is also
   compatible if its living source and limited pre-shaped behavior are clear.

3. **Anchor paired wind thrust at the feet and make that localization matter.**
   Each shoe may vent a directed jet whose axis follows the drawn rune and the
   wearer's foot angle. Balanced output produces climb; unequal output produces
   yaw or roll; toe/heel orientation can pitch, brake, or redirect. Broom
   riders instead use a familiar ridden focus, while the mount candidate
   coordinates with a living creature. The rune-shoe method should feel like
   propelled flight—continuous or burst-driven—not walking, running, or
   standing on solidified air. These are permitted local mechanics, not
   shared-universe physics.

4. **Give each introduced rule an observable consequence.** If line continuity,
   symmetry, vent direction, foot angle, stroke order, or pigment integrity
   matters, establish that rule before it fails. A smudge, worn line, blocked
   vent, or hurried redraw may then change thrust in a specific direction. The
   climax should be solved by the apprentice understanding and actively
   redrawing or modifying the wind path, not by the shoes developing new
   judgment.

5. **Keep the difference from standard methods causal, not cosmetic.** At
   minimum, show:

   - two broom candidates using the locally standard method with competent but
     distinct styles;
   - a flying creature that is visibly rarer but handled through familiar
     school procedures;
   - how losing, misorienting, or redrawing one wind rune changes Juni's thrust
     and rotation in a way neither a broom rider nor mounted flyer experiences;
   - how the apprentice's living action closes the climax's causal chain.

6. **Choose one modest local limitation if the plot needs it.** Attention,
   muscular or magical fatigue, finite stored input, pigment wear, shoe
   integrity, or another concrete constraint can support peril. Do not present
   the chosen limit as the price all magic pays. (`universe/rules.md` —
   “Costs, desire, and local mechanisms”)

7. **Invent local assessment and safety procedures only to the story's scale.**
   They may create stakes and constrain help, but cannot become an excuse for
   an unearned rescue. Two peers can visibly use standard Nine Eaves brooms,
   while one uses a rarer but understood flying mount, without generalizing
   either method beyond the local school.

8. **Keep the story independent.** New characters, institution, plot,
   set-pieces, magical vocabulary, and prose should be created from the prompt
   contract and current canon only. Do not recover, remix, invert, or allude to
   either rejected version's creative material.

## Required checks after drafting

1. **Direct drawing:** Confirm that the apprentice visibly draws at least one
   consequential magical rune directly onto a shoe on-page; the footwear is not
   simply pre-enchanted.

2. **Living-agency trace:** For every major takeoff, thrust burst, maneuver,
   correction, failure, and recovery, identify the living input and the limited
   shaping role of rune and shoe. Flag any sentence implying that the shoes
   cast, decide, learn, choose a target, or rescue the wearer independently.

3. **Standard-method distinction:** Confirm that two broom candidates make
   broom flight read as Nine Eaves' local standard and that the flying creature
   reads as rarer but institutionally understood. Verify that the rune shoes
   propel Juni through paired, directed wind thrust controlled by foot angle
   and differential output. Reject solid-air steps, walking/running-on-air
   language, and any levitation reference in final prose.

4. **Mechanics continuity:** Inventory each established rune, trigger, control,
   cost, limit, and failure behavior. Every late use or redraw must follow the
   earlier rules, and no climax-only property may appear without setup.

5. **Two-shoe consequences:** Verify that losing, damaging, smudging, or
   reorienting one rune produces the established asymmetric thrust, spin, and
   balance consequences rather than generic spell weakness.

6. **Assessment causality:** The result must be earned by the apprentice's
   choices and demonstrated mastery. Any safety intervention must follow
   established local procedure and must not solve the central problem.

7. **Prompt-method coverage:** Two brooms and one flying creature must be
   visibly represented among peers, with the brooms establishing the local
   standard and the creature reading as rare but well understood. The
   protagonist's solution remains the hand-drawn shoe runes. Check against
   every prohibited substitute, including a protagonist broom, mount or living
   flight partner, windlace, directional plummet, tether rig, or
   apparatus-centric engineering solution.

8. **Clean-slate audit:** Search the draft and all current production artifacts
   for every rejected-version reserved form. Also verify that no rejected
   character, institution, plot beat, set-piece, magical concept, or prose was
   restored, inverted, or alluded to.

9. **Era and scope:** Confirm that an openly magical school has not been placed
   in the modern-like low-magic trough without a coherent explanation. Remove
   unsupported links to Ravel, the Glass-Sea era, other systems, or a universal
   magic authority.

10. **Local-language audit:** Search for universalizing claims such as “all
    magic,” “runes always,” “every school,” “the source of magic,” or “the price
    of power.” Recast unsupported claims as this school's knowledge or this
    rune practice's rule.

11. **Name audit:** Check every character-facing form, including assessor or
    instructor labels used as proper names, named mounts, constructs, and
    person-like magical entities, against the complete registry. Record planned
    names before drafting, reconcile final aliases after review, and rerun the
    story-scoped name checker.

12. **Canon-delta audit:** Record every proposed reusable fact about the school,
    rune practice, flight mechanics, assessment, location, chronology, or
    recurring identity in `06-canon-delta.md`. None becomes canon without
    explicit promotion.
