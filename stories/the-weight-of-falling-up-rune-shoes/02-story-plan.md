# Story plan

## Premise and promise

**One-sentence premise:** At the prestigious Collegium of Nine Eaves, apprentice
Juni Wex must earn her aerial qualification with paired wind runes drawn
directly on her ordinary shoes, but when her effort to make their roaring,
foot-angled thrust look as precise as standard broom flight scours one rune
through, she must expose the method's limit and repair it in free fall.

**Story promise:** A wonder-forward, physically vivid flight test with a
rocket-boot-like magical feel: two calligraphic marks breathe narrow gusts from
Juni's heels, her legs become twin rudders, and every bank, burst, braking flare,
spin, and recovery follows a small set of visible rules. Two practiced brooms
show the local standard while a pale flying horse supplies a rare but understood
contrast.

**Central dramatic question:** Can Juni stop treating difference as a defect
long enough to demonstrate honest mastery of a flight method that cannot—and
should not—look like a polished broom?

**Meaningful change:** Juni begins by believing her invention deserves a place
only if she can conceal its awkward limits. She ends by treating those limits
as information to disclose and work with, earning a pass because she exercises
judgment rather than because she achieves a flawless performance.

## Story controls

- **Working title:** *The Weight of Falling Up*
- **POV and tense:** First-person past, tightly centered on Juni.
- **Tone and voice:** Nimble, wry, and wonder-struck. Juni jokes most when
  frightened, but sensory experience and emotional honesty keep the humor from
  dissolving the peril.
- **Genre:** School fantasy with an aerial practical-exam structure.
- **Target length:** 3,600 words, acceptable range 2,500–4,000.
- **Content boundary:** PG. Real height, falling, fatigue, and bruising danger;
  no graphic injury. No death, cruelty, or humiliating punishment.
- **Setting scope:** The Collegium of Nine Eaves in an unnamed active-magic era
  of the persistent shared world. Its prestige, terminology, and assessment
  rules are local, not universal.
- **Required visible contrasts:** Before Juni launches, two candidates depart
  on standard brooms with different competent styles. A third peer rides a pale
  winged horse that is rare at Nine Eaves but receives familiar harness checks
  and course spacing. All three remain visible at intervals as alternative
  silhouettes in the same genuine aerial assessment.
- **Method boundary:** Juni's ordinary shoes do not support or carry her. Her
  living power passes through the fresh marks and leaves the footwear as
  directed wind. Movement is propelled, sustained, and burst-driven.
- **Ending shape:** An earned pass with a candid examiner's note about wear
  margin and repair practice, not a prize for novelty or an implausible
  declaration that Juni's method is superior to all others.

## Character engine

### Protagonist: Juni Wex

- **Role:** Apprentice and first-person narrator; a talented maker of temporary
  drawn workings whose physical coordination is better than her confidence in
  institutional rooms.
- **Concrete desire:** Pass the aerial assessment today so she can keep her
  place on the Collegium's upcoming rooftop-survey practicum rather than spend
  the term copying other people's field observations indoors.
- **Why delay costs:** The practicum roster closes after this assessment. A
  failed or aborted attempt means waiting a term and losing the work that made
  her choose the school.
- **External stakes:** Qualification, the survey placement, and proof that the
  shoes are a controllable flight method rather than a stunt.
- **Internal tension / blind spot:** Juni mistakes institutional legitimacy for
  visual conformity. Because her paired gusts make every wobble and leg
  correction visible, she tries to imitate the composure of standard broom
  flight instead of demonstrating the safe answer native to her own method.
- **Strengths:** Observant, kinetically inventive, practiced at drawing under
  pressure, and quick to recognize a pattern once she stops protecting her
  pride.
- **Vulnerability:** Juni would rather chatter her jets through exhausting
  microbursts than display the blunt, non-steerable stabilizing plume that
  honestly answers her method's stationary-control limit.
- **Internal movement:** Defensiveness → exhilaration → self-conscious
  imitation → frightening loss of control → honest use of limitation →
  grounded confidence.

### Examiner Dovek

- **Role:** Fair, exacting assessor and the principal external pressure.
- **Want:** Evidence that Juni can anticipate and recover from the risks of her
  own method, not a dazzling novelty.
- **Function in the arc:** Dovek's questions expose the limitation Juni is
  tempted to hide. Dovek neither sabotages nor rescues her; the final judgment
  distinguishes adaptable mastery from showmanship.
- **Presentation:** Dry, economical, watchful. Concern appears as more precise
  questions, not sentimental reassurance.

### Unnamed functional cast

- A **fast broom candidate** banks low over the handle and takes the course in
  long, confident arcs.
- A **precision broom candidate** uses the same familiar method more upright
  and can hold almost motionless through small practiced corrections.
- A **mount candidate** coordinates with an unnamed pale winged horse. The
  creature is rare at Nine Eaves, but its harness checks, course spacing, and
  flight behavior are familiar rather than mysterious.
- They are witnesses and visual contrasts, not rivals or subplot engines. Do
  not give them names, aliases, or extended interpersonal arcs.

## Simple wind-rune shoe mechanics

These are local rules for Juni's drawn practice, not rules for all magic.

1. **Paired marks shape paired gusts.** Juni draws a mirrored three-stroke curl
   in conductive black ink directly around the heel and underside of each
   ordinary shoe. The curl is visually like an arrow teaching wind where to
   leave. When Juni supplies living power, each intact mark focuses a narrow
   jet from its shoe. Ink and leather do not act without her.
2. **Output changes speed, not substance.** Sustained power gives a continuous
   rush for climbing or crossing distance. A brief pulse gives a sharp
   correction. No hidden mechanism, physical flame, or independent spell
   carries Juni; the marks shape her supplied power into magical wind.
3. **Feet aim; the pair balances.** With both feet angled down and both jets
   even, Juni rises. Pointing her heels behind her drives her forward. Swinging
   both feet ahead and turning the jets against her motion brakes. More thrust
   on one side yaws and rolls her toward the weaker side, so she steers through
   foot angle, hips, and the deliberate balance between left and right.
4. **Use scours the drawing.** Every activation lifts a little ink into the
   wind. Smooth output wears slowly; rapid repeated microbursts eat at the
   edges, and hard reverse braking pushes wind back across the channels hard
   enough to scour them fastest. If one curl breaks, that shoe produces no jet
   until a living user redraws the missing line. Its partner continues exactly
   as commanded, making the failure dangerously asymmetric.
5. **A crown stroke is a one-use governor.** A shallow cap with three visible
   points can be drawn across either wind curl. When Juni activates the crowned
   shoe, its narrow jet opens into a broad plume centered beneath her body. The
   plume dampens rotation and opposes the fall but cannot steer. Once ignited,
   one point burns away per breath; a complete use lasts exactly three of
   Juni's breaths and consumes the crown, leaving the base wind rune intact.
   Juni must supply power throughout. Cutting power early aborts the plume and
   still consumes the crown, so it cannot be restarted or saved.

**Cost and limitation:** Sustained output tires Juni's concentration and legs,
while the physical ink has finite wear. The one-use crown can stabilize but not
guide her, and only for its exact three-breath cycle. None of these costs is
declared universal.

**Plain-language flight image:** With two matched gusts Juni flies straight;
tilt the shoes and the wind tilts her; favor one side and she wheels; lose one
side and she spins. Her body hangs from thrust she must continually aim, giving
the movement the rush and vulnerability of magic pouring from her boots rather
than the poise of sitting astride a familiar focus or creature.

## Causal arc

### Beginning state

Juni reaches the launch roof in ordinary, unmarked leather shoes because the
assessment requires her to demonstrate the actual drawing. She wants everyone
to see that the marks—not a purchased enchantment—make the flight possible.
She is thrilled by the open sky and embarrassed by how freshly improvised her
answer looks beside two polished brooms and the school handlers' calm,
well-practiced inspection of the pale winged horse.

### Inciting disruption

Juni removes each shoe and visibly inks the mirrored wind curls around heel and
underside. Dovek notices the empty space above each curl and asks how she will
meet the required stationary three-count. Juni traces the omitted three-point
crown in the air and explains its centered plume, exact three-breath burn, and
loss of steering. When Dovek calls that a limitation she must demonstrate
rather than conceal, Juni hears doubt instead of sound assessment advice. She
decides to fake broom-like precision with alternating microbursts.

### Escalating turns

1. Juni's paired jets answer. Wind snaps her hems upward, her stomach arrives a
   fraction after the rest of her, and she climbs through the launch lane on
   two pale twisting streams. She learns the immediate pleasure of leaning into
   her own thrust, corrects an early roll by easing the stronger foot, and
   completes the high bank with athletic precision. Success makes concealment
   feel possible.
2. At the stationary-control portion, the precision broom candidate holds
   almost motionless with tiny familiar adjustments. Rather than draw and use
   the seeded crown, Juni fires fast alternating heel bursts. She remains
   inside the boundary for three counts, but the wind buffets her, exhausts her,
   and spits visible black flecks from the right rune she favors for correction.
3. Dovek signals a scored repair pause. Juni sees the right curl thinning and
   has a safe chance to refresh it, losing points but preserving the attempt.
   She declines because the landing approach looks close and because accepting
   would publicly confirm that Dovek understood her risk better than she did.
4. Juni descends toward the landing court with the two streams nearly balanced.
   Coming in too fast after the microburst display, she swings both heels
   forward for the required reverse brake. The backlash scours the worn right
   curl open. The right jet dies while the left continues braking at full
   command: the left side is driven back while the right side carries on,
   snapping Juni into a rightward corkscrew with her right shoulder dropping.

### Crisis

Juni's instinct is to pour more power into the surviving left mark, but every
burst tightens the corkscrew. She can call for Dovek's safety intervention and
fail cleanly, or keep hiding the asymmetry until she loses consciousness or
strikes the roofs. The third option begins with an admission: cut all power,
accept the unsoftened fall, and trust herself to perform the visible repair she
should have accepted earlier.

### Climax

Juni cuts the left jet. The torque stops worsening, though momentum leaves her
spinning and falling. She tucks until the world becomes a manageable wheel,
pulls the seeded stylus from her cuff, and draws the missing curve directly
onto the exposed right shoe. She then adds the three-point crown Dovek asked
about before launch.

Juni extends the repaired shoe beneath her and supplies power. Instead of a
narrow off-center jet, a broad centered plume blossoms under her. On the first
breath it presses the corkscrew flat. On the second it checks her fall enough
for her to lift the left heel into view. On the third she refreshes the
left curl's thinnest line; the last crown point burns away as she finishes.
The right rune returns to its ordinary narrow output. Juni activates both
shoes evenly, feels two clean streams take equal hold, and transitions into a
controlled climb.

She says aloud that the right mark wore through and that she declined the
repair. Instead of chasing lost time, she uses smooth paired output, begins her
brake early, angles both feet together, and lands inside the assessment ring
under her own control. Every link in the recovery is her living choice acting
through marks whose behavior the story established before takeoff.

### Resonant aftermath

Dovek fails Juni's stationary-control component but passes the assessment
because she diagnosed a method-specific failure, stopped feeding the dangerous
asymmetry, redrew and modified the rune aloft, disclosed her mistake, and
completed a controlled landing. The written note requires a larger ink margin
and a declared repair threshold before long crossings but grants the
qualification Juni needs for the rooftop-survey practicum.

Juni looks back at the paired contrails unraveling above Nine Eaves: a ragged
knot where she spun, one broad pale bloom where the crown opened, then two
clean ribbons arriving side by side. She no longer wishes they resembled a
broom's single graceful wake. Their shape is an honest record of how she
learned to fly her own method.

## Failure modes to watch

- **Cosmetic-rune failure:** Every launch, bank, correction, brake, spin, fall,
  and recovery must trace through Juni's living input, an intact physical mark,
  foot angle, and the balance of two directed wind jets. Do not let an
  unmentioned spell or broom-like support carry her.
- **Autonomous-shoe failure:** Shoes and marks never anticipate danger, balance
  output, choose a vector, redraw themselves, or activate without Juni. Avoid
  personifying language that implies decision-making.
- **Superseded-motion failure:** Never describe the air as bearing Juni's
  weight or her feet as finding places to land in empty sky. Her motion is
  continuous or burst-driven propulsion through freely moving wind.
- **Generic-flight failure:** Keep paired jets, foot angle, differential
  output, torque, streaming hems, breath, and bodily pitch present. The magic
  should feel like calligraphy becoming gust, not ordinary free flight with
  decorated footwear.
- **Over-engineering:** Teach the five rules through the launch drawing,
  Dovek's question, one early roll correction, visible ink scour, and
  consequences. Avoid measurements, equations, component jargon, or a
  technical-manual tone.
- **Unseeded governor:** Establish before the right rune fails that the
  three-point crown is one-use; broadens one shoe's output beneath Juni's
  center; cannot steer; burns one point per breath for exactly three breaths;
  consumes itself; leaves the base rune intact; and still requires Juni's
  continuous living power. Also seed the stylus location and left-rune wear.
- **Inconsistent asymmetric failure:** A broken right rune during reverse
  braking must silence only the right jet. The still-commanded left reverse
  jet drives the left side back and drops the right side into the established
  rightward corkscrew. More left power worsens it; cutting power stops added
  torque but not existing momentum or gravity.
- **Weak causal responsibility:** Juni must notice wear and decline Dovek's
  legitimate scored repair pause. Random weather, sabotage, examiner hostility,
  mount interference, or equipment betrayal must not replace her meaningful
  mistake.
- **Unearned rescue:** Dovek may observe, score, and be ready to apply local
  safety procedure, but no peer, mount, broom, hidden net, or new power solves
  the central fall.
- **Overrewarded ending:** The pass rests on diagnosis, the decision to cut
  power, a rule-consistent redraw, restored balanced thrust, and controlled
  landing—not novelty. Preserve the failed stationary component and
  wear-margin note; do not crown Juni metaphorically, rewrite school doctrine,
  or declare her shoes superior.
- **Peer-method omission:** Two brooms and the pale flying horse must appear
  clearly before launch and at least once in the assessment sky. The brooms
  should read as standard; the creature should read as rare but understood.
  They should not steal narrative focus. No levitation appears in final prose.
- **Emotional shortcut:** Juni's change is demonstrated by cutting the
  worsening jet, publicly admitting that she declined repair, using the blunt
  governor, choosing a conservative final approach, and accepting Dovek's
  note—not by a speech about being unique.
- **Wrong closing image:** End on wind, breath, or the paired contrails showing
  spin, stabilizing bloom, and balance. Do not restore the superseded visual
  vocabulary.
- **Voice drift:** Maintain first-person past and Juni's wry wonder. Humor
  should sharpen fear and observation, not trivialize the height or assessment.
- **Scope inflation:** Do not date the era, define all runes or broom magic,
  connect Nine Eaves to established eras, or imply one global magical system.
- **Clean-slate breach:** Do not import or echo any rejected names, schools,
  characters, plots, set pieces, magical concepts, signature language,
  windlace, plummets, tether mechanics, or apparatus-centric solutions.
- **Name drift:** Use only `Juni Wex` / `Juni` and `Examiner Dovek` / `Dovek`
  as character-facing forms. If any peer, mount, title-as-name, construct, or
  alias gains a name in drafting, stop and check it against `stories/NAMES.md`
  before use.
