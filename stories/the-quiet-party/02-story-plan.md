# Story plan

## Premise and promise

**Premise:** Merrit Loam, a rule-minded field registrar hoping to earn a senior
desk, is sent to supervise three excellent but persistently silent adventurers
whom the local guild has forced into one “corrective” party; when a flood-control
rescue turns dangerous, she must choose between enforcing the guild's verbal
chain of command and trusting the reciprocal understanding she can plainly see.

**Story promise:** The guild's punishment will backfire in full view of its own
observer: three individually legible adventurers will communicate constantly
without speech, complete a real and escalating rescue with flawless teamwork,
and make the guild's sarcastic team assignment into an annoyingly useful local
precedent.

**Central dramatic question:** Will Merrit keep using audible readback as her
proxy for competence, or recognize and certify the party's observable
communication before obedience to that proxy costs lives?

**Meaningful change:** Merrit moves from believing that safety is proven by
compliance with one familiar communication method to judging safety by
reciprocal understanding, correction, and action. She gives up the clean report
that could secure her desired promotion, first by authorizing the party's field
plan and then by recording what actually worked.

## Story controls

- **POV:** Close third through Merrit Loam. Everything necessary to understand
  the party's coordination must be available to her senses; no head-hopping
  into the adventurers and no inaccessible explanation of what they “just
  know.”
- **Tense:** Past.
- **Tone and genre:** Warm comic fantasy; brisk physical adventure, dry local
  bureaucracy, affectionate observational humor, and sincere respect for
  practiced competence.
- **Target length:** **3,600 words**, within the required 2,500–4,000-word
  range.
- **Content boundaries:** Teen / PG-13. Rising water, collapsing timber, and
  danger to people and animals; no graphic injury, cruelty, sexual content, or
  humiliation of a vulnerable person.
- **Era and setting:** Chronologically unpinned, in the town of Latchwater and
  its immediate waterworks. Do not connect it to a named canon era, location,
  institution, or magic system.
- **Speech rule:** Merrit, the guildmaster, and the sluice keepers may speak.
  Veda, Osket, and Pim never speak aloud—not even in the last beat. They do not
  use telepathy, a hive mind, a vow-powered effect, or a prearranged private
  code. Their slate diagrams, writing, pointing, demonstrations, exchanged
  tools, deliberate pauses, and eye contact are ordinary, visible
  communication.
- **Communication thesis:** The party succeeds because they communicate
  attentively and repair uncertainty before acting, not because communication
  is unnecessary and not because silence is inherently superior.

## Character engine

### Protagonist: Merrit Loam

- **Role:** Field registrar for the local Latchwater Contract Guild, assigned to
  observe and grade the corrective expedition.
- **Concrete desire:** File one clean, regulation-perfect supervisory report
  and thereby secure the open senior registrar desk, a safer and more respected
  post.
- **Immediate stakes:** Two sluice keepers, the lower Latchwater homes, three
  compelled adventurers, and Merrit herself are endangered if the jammed
  spillgate is not opened before the reservoir overtops.
- **Career and moral stakes:** A “failed” team would validate leadership's
  prejudice and probably win her promotion; a truthful certification may cost
  it. If she hides behind the rubric, she becomes responsible for a preventable
  failure.
- **Pressure against delay:** Storm runoff is already entering the reservoir.
  At the mission site, the visible waterline gives them less than an hour, and
  each gate failure shortens that margin.
- **Blind spot:** Merrit has seen audible readback catch dangerous mistakes, so
  she has let one useful protocol harden into a definition of communication.
  She begins sympathetically frustrated by people who will not use the guild's
  preferred method, although their field records contain no competence
  failures.
- **Strengths:** Conscientious, mechanically literate enough to follow a
  service drawing, honest when forced to look directly at evidence, and
  physically braver than her desk ambitions suggest.
- **Internal movement:** “I can audit only what is spoken” → “I can audit
  reciprocal understanding” → “My office must accommodate evidence rather than
  manufacture failure.”
- **Decisive actions:** She authorizes a field variation and becomes the fourth
  worker in the party's visible plan; afterward, she certifies flawless
  coordination and records the coercive flaw in the assignment.

### The three silent adventurers

Their reasons for not speaking are not diagnosed, demanded, or reduced to one
shared origin. Each treats silence as a boundary while actively communicating
in a distinct way.

- **Veda Crake (she/her), structural defender:** A shield-bearing
  stoneworker-rescuer who reads load, fracture, and balance. She wants restored
  access to civic rescue contracts, from which the guild has suspended her for
  declining oral debriefs despite an excellent safety record. Her primary
  medium is a charcoal slate: fast cross-sections, arrows, load marks, and
  written words when precision requires them. Her body becomes very still when
  she notices structural danger; she taps the exact crack or support before
  anyone commits weight.
- **Osket Firth (he/him), rope scout:** A light-footed climber and rigging
  specialist who resents the compulsory lesson but will not make stranded
  civilians pay for it. His primary medium is physical demonstration: placing
  hooks and coils where he proposes anchors, tracing routes with a fingertip,
  pointing, and waiting for eye contact before loading a line. His humor is
  visible in raised brows and elaborately patient presentation of obvious
  facts, but he never becomes a mime caricature.
- **Pim Fallow (they/them), field medic and river naturalist:** A practical
  healer who recognizes local reedmaw behavior and carries bitterroot used to
  redirect them without harm. Pim wants continued guild access because civic
  contracts fund their small field practice. Their primary medium is plain
  writing on waterproof slips, supplemented by calm hand demonstrations for
  breathing, first aid, and animal handling. They check other people's faces
  for comprehension and rephrase rather than assuming.

### Supporting pressure

- **The unnamed guildmaster:** Not a monster, but a complacent local leader who
  sees refusal to perform oral debriefs as insolence and expects the forced team
  to prove a point. The guildmaster supplies institutional opposition and the
  comic recoil when the guild's own rule produces the opposite result.
- **Two unnamed sluice keepers:** Civilians stranded at Mallowgate sluice. They
  make the mission morally immediate, contribute ordinary spoken warnings, and
  demonstrate that the silent party can also communicate with frightened
  strangers.
- **Primary opposition:** A rising reservoir, a broken catwalk, a debris-loaded
  counterweight, a living reedmaw nest wedged against the relief gate, and the
  guild's rigid evaluation standard. There is no villain at the mission site.

## Causal arc

### Beginning state

Merrit believes competent coordination should be audible, sequential, and easy
to transcribe. She wants the senior desk badly enough to accept leadership's
description of three excellent solo adventurers as “uncooperative,” although
their records show no field failures.

### Inciting disruption

The guildmaster invokes a local corrective-composition rule: Veda, Osket, and
Pim must take one joint contract or lose access to guild work for a month.
Merrit must grade them. The real assignment is urgent—free two keepers and open
Mallowgate's jammed relief gate before storm runoff overtops the reservoir.

### Escalating turns

1. All three accept the rescue while marking their objection to the premise in
   writing. They begin inventorying and reallocating equipment before Merrit
   finishes dictating a chain of command.
2. At a washed approach culvert, they use visible, improvised coordination to
   get Merrit across safely. She checks an acknowledgement box despite hearing
   no readback, then dismisses the evidence as a trivial case.
3. At the sluice, they combine Veda's drawing, Osket's rigging model, and Pim's
   written animal warning into one legible rescue plan. A counterweight skip
   turns the urgent job into a simultaneous one.
4. They rescue both keepers without injury. The unavoidable movement loosens a
   debris raft, which shears the counterweight's last sound link and drives the
   gate tighter against the reedmaw nest.
5. Merrit's shouted warning startles a reedmaw and worsens a structural shift.
   The three adventurers compensate instantly and safely, forcing her to see
   that her preferred method can be wrong for a particular moment.
6. The redrawn plan requires a fourth person at the manual release pin.
   Regulations tell Merrit to remain an observer and order retreat, but retreat
   would leave the lower homes to flood and the reedmaws trapped.

### Crisis and choice

The party places Merrit's brass audit token at the fourth position on Veda's
diagram, then each indicates a role and waits for her acknowledgement. Nothing
is hidden, magical, or assumed. Merrit can call retreat and preserve procedural
cover, or authorize the clearly understood field variation and trust herself
inside their plan. She marks the variation approved and steps to the release
pin.

### Climax

Pim redirects the adult reedmaws toward a safe spill pool; Osket slings the nest
clear; Veda braces the cracked lintel and works the release wheel; Merrit pulls
the manual pin. When a young reedmaw turns back, Pim visibly withholds the
all-clear, Osket holds rather than guessing, Veda indicates a safer brace, and
Merrit adjusts. After renewed eye contact and an unmistakable all-clear, all
four act. The gate opens, the surge passes below the crest, the nest lands
safely, and no one is injured. The flawless result comes from attention,
pauses, and correction—not unexplained perfect knowledge.

### Resonant aftermath

Back at the guild, Merrit can preserve her promotion by translating the day
into vague “noncompliance.” Instead she files the exact result: flawless
coordination, no safety demerits, and a note that the compulsory premise was
unsupported by the evidence. The guildmaster's sarcastic entry of “Quiet Party”
in the charter-name box combines with a rule seeded in the opening: a
corrective party that completes a graded civic rescue cleanly becomes a
standing local party, and its demonstrated protocol must enter the guild's
field circular. The guild is safe, useful, and deeply annoyed. Other members
immediately request written briefings and observable-acknowledgement options.
Merrit loses the quiet senior desk but is assigned to administer the new local
protocol; she accepts the crowded in-tray because it is work worth making
auditable.

## Canon constraints

- Treat `00-prompt.md` as controlling: all three adventurers remain silent in
  every scene; the guild deliberately creates the team as a corrective measure;
  Mallowgate is a real rescue; coordination is flawless and observable; and the
  result becomes an annoying comic precedent.
- Keep the story in the one persistent physical world but chronologically
  unpinned. Do not name or imply a separate plane, world, or timeline.
- Keep the Latchwater Contract Guild's authority strictly local. Use “this
  guild,” “Latchwater contracts,” and “the district,” never universal claims
  about all adventurers or guilds.
- Candidate stories and legacy sources are not canon inputs. Do not introduce a
  crossover, recurring identity, or lore connection based on resemblance.
- No telepathy, hive mind, magical silence effect, shared diagnosis, common vow,
  or prearranged private code explains the party. Any signal devised at the
  sluice must be shown being proposed, acknowledged, and applied.
- Do not frame silence or disability as a moral defect. Leadership's objection
  is a local institutional norm, and the narration ultimately distinguishes
  refusal to use one medium from refusal to communicate.
- Do not imply communication is unnecessary. Pauses, comprehension checks,
  corrections, writing, diagrams, gaze, and action are essential to success.
- The plan uses no magical device or nonliving magic user. Reedmaws are living
  local fantasy animals; their behavior need not be labeled objectively
  magical. If the draft adds any magic, it must be locally bounded, legible,
  powered or performed through living agency, and unrelated to communication.
- The charter's consequences arise from local contract law and inked
  paperwork, not from an intrinsic power of names.
- Preserve the ethical cost of compelled assignment. The trio prioritizes a
  civilian rescue, but Merrit's report explicitly records that accepting under
  threat of lost guild work was not meaningful evidence that the corrective
  premise was fair.
- Keep injuries minor and peril consequence-centered. Leadership may be
  inconvenienced and annoyed, but not sadistically punished or publicly
  destroyed.

## Proposed inventions

All items below are **proposed local story facts, not established canon**. A
later `06-canon-delta.md` should capture any that survive final prose.

- **Latchwater:** A chronologically unpinned town with lower homes protected by
  a reservoir and relief works. No relationship to any named canon location is
  implied.
- **Latchwater Contract Guild:** A town-scale adventuring bureaucracy that
  licenses local contracts, maintains safety rubrics, and can suspend access to
  its own work. It has no jurisdiction beyond its district.
- **Corrective-composition rule:** The local guild may condition continued
  contract access on a joint graded assignment. This ethically dubious policy
  also says a corrective unit completing a civic rescue without safety
  demerits becomes a standing party and its demonstrated communication protocol
  enters a local field circular.
- **Mallowgate sluice:** An aging local flood-control work with a relief gate,
  counterweight, cracked service platform, four-position manual release, and
  lower homes in its flood path.
- **Reedmaws:** Living, non-personified river animals that weave buoyant nests
  from reeds and debris, swell or thrash when startled, and can be coaxed by
  bitterroot. They are a bounded ecological complication, not a secret magic
  system or named intelligent collective.
- **The Quiet Party:** Veda, Osket, and Pim's local standing team, created by
  the guild's own sarcastically completed charter form after their flawless
  graded success.
- **Observable-acknowledgement protocol:** Merrit's local revision allows a
  field supervisor to verify that an instruction was received, tested,
  corrected, and integrated through writing, diagram, gesture, demonstration,
  or speech. It is an accommodation and safety standard for this guild, not a
  universal claim.

## Failure modes to watch

- **Accidental speech:** Veda, Osket, or Pim says a word aloud, supplies a final
  spoken punch line, is described as whispering, or receives dialogue through
  ambiguous punctuation. Keep even stress reactions clearly nonverbal.
- **Hidden mind-link:** The narration says they “somehow knew,” moved “as one
  mind,” shared thoughts, or relied on unexplained perfect timing. Every
  inference must have an observable source.
- **Prearranged-code shortcut:** Repeated taps, colors, or gestures function as
  a private code the reader and Merrit were never shown learning. Site-specific
  signals must be proposed, acknowledged, and causally legible on the page.
- **Communication-erasure thesis:** The resolution implies that talking or
  communication is useless. The party should pause, ask, acknowledge, revise,
  and confirm more—not less—than the guild expects.
- **Interchangeable trio:** Action beats could be reassigned among the three
  without changing anything. Preserve Veda/load/slate, Osket/rigging/physical
  demonstration, and Pim/medicine-naturalism/writing in motive, body language,
  and causal contribution.
- **Silence as gimmick, diagnosis, or superiority:** Do not reveal a shared vow,
  disability, origin, or tactical power; do not make the mission solvable merely
  because it rewards quiet; and do not suggest that speech is morally or
  cognitively inferior.
- **Flawless as effortless:** The party may encounter new information, stop,
  revise, and work hard. “Flawless” means no preventable coordination error or
  harmful failure, not omniscience or lack of danger.
- **Merrit becomes stupid or cruel:** Her initial protocol has a reasonable
  safety purpose, her error is specific, and her change requires both physical
  courage and career sacrifice. Comedy should expose institutional rigidity,
  not deny her personhood.
- **Coercion disappears:** The rescue's success must not retroactively justify
  threatening the trio's livelihoods. Their written protest and Merrit's final
  report keep that ethical cost visible.
- **Unseeded climax mechanics:** The four service positions, spare hook,
  bitterroot, cracked structure, certification clause, and charter name must
  appear before their payoffs.
- **Action geography blurs:** Keep the central platform, high traverse, jammed
  gate, safe spill pool, counterweight, four manual positions, and lower homes
  spatially consistent. The reader should be able to say who is holding what
  and why the gate opens.
- **Animal harm becomes casual:** Reedmaws remain living stakes, not disposable
  obstacles. Their safe relocation is part of the party's competent solution.
- **Guild scope inflates:** Avoid statements about every guild, all
  adventurers, universal law, or the world's communication norms. The precedent
