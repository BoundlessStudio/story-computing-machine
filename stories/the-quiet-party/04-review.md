#### Causality and character

##### Finding 1 — the authorized overlapping plan is not the plan executed

- **Severity:** Major
- **Location:** `03-draft.md`, Mallowgate diagnosis and clean-rescue sequence,
  especially lines 159–164, 175–236, and the later report at lines 452–459
- **Evidence:** Once the counterweight skips, the narration says, “Separate
  operations were no longer possible,” and Merrit authorizes “Rescue line, nest
  sling, structural brace. Overlap on my mark.” The next scene executes the
  keeper rescue alone. Veda does not brace the lintel, Osket does not feed the
  sling under the nest, and Pim does not begin the bitterroot redirection until
  after both keepers have left the platform. Unloading that platform then lifts
  the debris raft, whose swing snaps the final counterweight link and creates
  the next emergency. The report nevertheless says Merrit's shout caused “the
  only preventable complication.”
- **Why it matters:** The party appears to abandon its own visibly agreed
  simultaneous plan, and that omission immediately causes the decisive
  escalation. This breaks the causal audit and makes “flawless teamwork” an
  assertion rather than the demonstrated prompt payoff. It also undercuts
  Veda's structural expertise because the platform rebound is neither
  anticipated nor proved unavoidable.
- **Smallest effective fix:** Make the diagnosis, authorization, and execution
  agree. Either show the sling and brace being visibly staged before the second
  keeper is unloaded and establish that the link still fails through an
  unpreventable external shift despite those precautions, or state that the
  first authorized phase is an immediate keeper rescue and demonstrate why no
  available staging could prevent the rebound. Preserve the later escalation,
  but make it a new hazard rather than an unexplained lapse by the trio.

##### Finding 2 — the nest separates from its debris raft without a causal beat

- **Severity:** Major
- **Location:** `03-draft.md`, lines 124–125, 247, 391–405, and 434–436
- **Evidence:** The nest is introduced as “woven through” the debris raft's
  branches. Osket later feeds a sling under it, but nobody cuts, untangles, or
  otherwise frees those bindings. At the climax the sling moves the nest
  outward while the opening gate separately drives the raft's branches through
  the gorge, and the final report certifies that the nest was relocated intact.
- **Why it matters:** The animal rescue and clean sluice release depend on the
  nest and raft becoming two independently moving objects. Without a visible
  separation step, the central action cannot physically produce the stated
  result; the surge should drag the woven nest with the raft or tear it.
- **Smallest effective fix:** Before the lift, show Osket visibly freeing the
  few nest-to-raft bindings while Pim confirms the young remain protected, or
  change the initial relationship to a merely wedged nest and show how sling
  tension frees it intact. Keep the detachment observable to Merrit and the
  reader.

- Apart from these action mechanics, characterization and knowledge are sound.
  Veda's load-reading stillness and slate, Osket's rigging demonstrations and
  patient humor, and Pim's written comprehension checks, medicine, and animal
  handling remain distinct and causally useful.
- Merrit's initial trust in audible readback has a credible safety basis. Her
  unsafe shout, decision to join the four-position plan, truthful report, and
  loss of the quiet promotion form a complete choice-driven arc.
