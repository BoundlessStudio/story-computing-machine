# Prompt contract

## Verbatim writing prompt

> **[WP] Several "silent protagonist" type adventurers are getting on the guilds nerves with their refusal to speak, so in an attempt to teach them a lesson the guild leadership forces them to team up. Much to the guilds annoyance they all manage to work together flawlessly in spite of never speaking.**

## Story controls

- Working title: The Quiet Party
- Target length: 2,500–4,000 words
- POV: Close third person through a guild official assigned to supervise the
  supposedly corrective expedition.
- Tense: Past.
- Tone and genre: Warm comic fantasy with brisk adventure, dry institutional
  satire, and sincere competence beneath the joke.
- Audience/content rating: Teen / PG-13; peril and fantasy violence without
  graphic injury, cruelty, or sexual content.
- Required elements: Several individually distinctive adventurers who never
  speak aloud; guild leadership deliberately forces them into one party as a
  lesson; a real mission that tests coordination; flawless teamwork conveyed
  through observation and action rather than explained telepathy; escalating
  guild annoyance when the punishment succeeds; a complete comic resolution.
- Prohibited elements: No last-minute spoken line from any silent adventurer;
  no secret telepathy, hive mind, or prearranged shared code that trivializes
  their achievement; no framing speech disability as a moral defect; no
  treating the adventurers as interchangeable blank slates; no claim that all
  communication is unnecessary.

## Assumptions

- The guild is a local adventuring bureaucracy with authority to assign teams
  and contracts, not a setting-wide rule or institution.
- Each adventurer has an observable personal style and a different preferred
  nonverbal medium, but the expedition succeeds chiefly through attention,
  trust, and adaptation in the moment.
- The viewpoint official begins sympathetic to the guild's frustration but
  changes when the party demonstrates that the institution has confused one
  communication norm with competence.
- The adventurers may write, gesture, draw, point, exchange objects, or use
  ordinary signals, but none speaks aloud during the story.
- The comic climax will leave guild leadership more annoyed than humiliated:
  their mission is completed safely, and their own punishment becomes a useful
  operational precedent.

## Completion tests

- The result is a complete short story, not a synopsis or chapter fragment.
- The central promise of the prompt is dramatized and resolved.
- At least three silent adventurers remain individually legible in motive,
  specialty, and body language while performing one tightly coordinated
  mission.
- The party never speaks aloud and uses no supernatural mind-link.
- Guild leadership intentionally creates the team to teach a lesson, then must
  confront—with comic annoyance—that the team works flawlessly.
- The expedition includes clear tactical setups and payoffs that let a reader
  follow how the party coordinates without expository dialogue.
- The story passes canon and continuity review.
- Every character-facing name and alias is checked against `stories/NAMES.md`;
  any deliberate reuse has a documented narrative reason.
