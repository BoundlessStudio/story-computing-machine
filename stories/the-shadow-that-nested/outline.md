# Outline

## Story

- Proposed reader-facing title: *The Shadow That Nested*.
- Premise and central promise: When the moon's familiar shadow is followed
  across the world by a second moving darkness, orbital repair lead Oru Korr
  and structural-life specialist Nemi Quell find a vast, ancient sunshade that
  has yawed out of its long edge-on rest. Its unreadable machinery can turn it
  clear of the sun, but a migrating vacuum species has made millions of nests
  across the segmented slewing ring. Fulfill the prompt by making the omen a
  materially real but mistaken reading of the shade, making the migration an
  active obstacle rather than decoration, and ending the repeated double
  shadow without crushing the occupied nests: the crew follows a moving hatch
  wave around the ring, moves only the cleared segments, and uses the repair
  tug's reserve burn to supply the missing final torque. The shade holds at an
  inelegant safe angle rather than returning to its builders' intended state.
- Intended reader experience: Begin with orbital wonder sharpened by the
  unsettling sight of a second night moving over the world, then replace the
  clean pleasure of diagnosing lost machinery with the crowded, tactile fact
  that it has become habitat. Let the repair feel precarious and exact without
  becoming a manual. The finish should bring relief, fatigue, and modest awe:
  the crew solves the shadow that sent them up, but neither ancient purpose nor
  living occupation becomes subordinate to a speech, proof, or emblem.
- Generating force:
  - Mode: Plot-led moving-clearance traverse. A band of newly vacated nests
    travels along the sunshade's rim as the migration hatches into the changing
    thermal light. Oru and Nemi must keep their manual actuator pulses behind
    that band, so narrative movement is one directional and cannot pause or
    complete the full circle.
  - Focal attachment or contradiction: Oru has built a working life around the
    belief that repair means returning a designed object to its specified
    condition. The sunshade is unmistakably broken as a machine and
    unmistakably functioning as a nursery; preserving either use by itself is
    simple, while preserving both requires accepting a result no original
    service plan would call complete.
  - Plot-led wants and useful limit, if applicable: Oru immediately wants to
    identify the second shadow, turn the shade away before its next full sweep,
    and bring both crew members home inside the tug's fuel margin. Competing
    with that duty, Oru wants not to tear the brittle membrane or kill the
    migration occupying it. The useful flaw is restoration bias: Oru treats the
    builders' zero mark as the only defensible endpoint and initially calls the
    nests contamination, which delays noticing that the living traffic itself
    provides the only safe sequence. Nemi wants time to establish which nests
    are occupied while also recognizing that leaving the shade to yaw farther
    could destroy both machine and colony; their caution has a material limit,
    not moral omniscience.
- Counterforce or complication:
  - Source: The ancient membrane is too fragile for an unsynchronized tow. Its
    perimeter walkers are the only parts able to distribute turning force, and
    their rails, housings, and tension links are woven through occupied nests.
    A central reset would run every walker at once and shear the colony. Waiting
    for the entire migration to depart permits the shade's slow yaw and double
    shadow to recur, while each minute of local movement consumes the crew's
    narrow orbital and propellant margin.
  - Credible inside logic, if a person: There is no antagonist. Nemi's refusal
    to certify a still nest as empty protects living young and their own
    professional responsibility; Oru's urgency protects the world below, the
    crew, and a structure that will become more dangerous if it tears. Neither
    knows the migration's intentions. Observable warmth, movement, hatching,
    and flight may guide action without being translated into consent or a
    sentimental claim that the animals understand the repair.
  - Pressure or change created: The crew must abandon the automated reset,
    cross the exposed rim, and fire walkers locally in the short wake behind
    hatching animals. At the last occupied spindle, Oru must choose between the
    specified final alignment and a partial angle that solves the surface
    shadow but costs nearly all reserve thrust and leaves future work.
- Narrative design:
  - POV, tense, distance, and limitation: Close third-person present through
    Oru. Keep the narration intimate with suit load, fuel arithmetic, hand
    habits, and Oru's tendency to reduce uncertainty to repair verbs. Oru can
    read machine stress but not the builders' erased history, Nemi's private
    judgment, the surface cultures' beliefs, or animal intention. Give Nemi a
    vocabulary of occupancy, heat, and confidence intervals rather than moral
    pronouncements; Oru speaks in states and tolerances, then has to name an
    acceptable state the manual never supplied.
  - Time shape and compression: Roughly eighteen continuous hours spanning two
    predicted shadow passes. Render the approach and identification, aborted
    reset, first nest encounter, discovery of the hatching wave, rim traverse,
    final occupied spindle, and reserve burn in scene. Compress repeated local
    walker operations through changing light, suit fatigue, fuel figures, and
    the shrinking trace of the forecast second shadow. No surface interlude or
    later historical coda.
  - Information and reveal strategy: Open with the two surface tracks and the
    omen reports, then identify the sunshade early from shape, thermal age, and
    its alignment; the prompt's explanation is a discovery, not a last-page
    twist. Let the first control response seem like a straightforward reset
    until tension readings and a cutaway reveal material where empty rail
    should be. Reveal the scale of the migration by degrees: one fibrous mass,
    a warm pulse, repeated nests beyond the work lights, then adult bodies
    crossing the sun. Withhold the usefulness of the hatch wave until Oru sees
    that cleared rails form a moving gap. Withhold only the last nest's living
    status and the sufficiency of a nonzero final angle; no recovered archive,
    builder message, surface authority, or public demonstration decides the
    action.
  - Governing movement: Track two shadows; match the moon; find the displaced
    shade; dock at its dead hub; wake only the local diagnostics; command a
    reset; abort at the first organic strain; open the rail; recognize one nest
    as part of a colony covering the rim; rule out cutting, towing, and waiting;
    watch the thermal boundary trigger a hatching wave; move the first cleared
    walker; follow the empty wake hand over hand; let each pulse narrow the
    predicted shadow; reach the occupied final spindle; reject the builder's
    zero mark; calculate the minimum safe angle; commit the tug's reserve burn;
    hold the asymmetric load; verify that the second umbra misses the world;
    begin the longer, low-fuel return while the last nest remains warm.
- Speculative surplus: Dense driftwing nests shed weak bioelectric pulses into
  the sunshade's conductive skin, making suit radios repeat the last syllable
  of speech in overlapping delayed echoes. Oru and Nemi use terse checksum
  confirmations and tether their tools against the accompanying static creep.
  The interference cannot translate the animals, identify occupied nests,
  reveal the correct actuator sequence, or solve the shadow.
- Structural distinction: A one-directional, three-quarter traversal of an
  orbital ring built around a moving absence. Every operation advances into
  newly cleared space, and decisive success comes from stopping before the
  circle is complete. The climax remains outside on an ancient shade's rim,
  shared only by two tired workers and a migration that does not become an
  audience; there is no public demonstration, converted crowd, institutional
  rewrite, or symbolic closure.
- Decisive turn, deepening, or recognition: At the final spindle, Nemi finds a
  faint heat rhythm inside the one nest that has not opened. The next hatch gap
  will come too late for the fuel window, while Oru's inherited diagnostic
  insists that only one more walker can restore the shade. Oru overlays the
  actual surface track on the builder's alignment display and sees that
  restoration is a stricter demand than the mission: eleven walkers have
  already moved the second umbra close to the world's limb. A sustained tug
  burn can carry it the remaining fraction without turning the occupied rail,
  at the cost of the direct return reserve and a permanently off-center load.
  Oru chooses the ugly angle, Nemi monitors the nest and membrane, and they hold
  the burn until the shadow trace peels clear of the atmosphere.
- Aftereffect or live uncertainty: The world receives no more full second
  shadow in the current alignment, but surface interpretations will not vanish
  on command, the shade remains only partly serviced, and the crew must take a
  slower return arc with little margin. The surviving diagnostic offers no
  reliable builder, date, original climate target, or reason the shade spent so
  long edge-on. The last nest may hatch within hours, but the wider migration's
  route, recurrence, intelligence, and long-term effect on the machine remain
  unknown; another crew will need to observe before attempting completion.
- Opening / ending relation: Open with Oru drawing a finger along two dark
  tracks on the world's weather map and treating the trailing track as a fault
  with one correct machine state. End after the burn with two shadows still
  visible in the orbital model but only the moon's touching the world; Oru sets
  the next inspection for the last nest's predicted hatch, then lets the
  low-fuel return arc carry the tug away before the ancient zero mark is
  reached.

## Beats

1. Oru and Nemi match orbit with the moon while a normal umbra and a trailing
   second darkness cross the surface model. Omen reports arrive stripped of
   useful coordinates; Oru concentrates on the repeatable offset.
2. Their tug rounds the moon and finds the cause: a dark, petaled sunshade whose
   yaw places part of its enormous membrane between sun and world. Layered
   repairs and dead symbols imply great age without yielding a builder or date.
3. At the dead hub, local diagnostics show an intact segmented slewing ring and
   a simple path back to its zero mark. Oru starts the reset to beat the next
   shadow pass; Nemi sees load moving where an empty perimeter rail should be
   and forces an abort before the first walker closes.
4. On EVA, they cut away only a brittle service cover and uncover a warm woven
   nest wrapped through the runner. Work lights pick out another, then thousands
   receding around the rim; immense driftwing adults cross the sun beyond them.
5. Oru and Nemi test the bad options from the hub: direct towing would rip the
   membrane, a central reset would shear the nests, and waiting permits the
   shade to keep turning. Radio echoes and suit fatigue make even their factual
   disagreement harder to keep precise.
6. As the shade's thermal boundary advances, the nearest nests open and young
   driftwings join the adults. Nemi maps a narrow band of cleared rail moving in
   one direction. Oru proposes local pulses in its wake, with immediate aborts
   if heat or strain returns.
7. They traverse the rim. Nemi calls cleared segments from the hub while Oru
   releases and fires each walker by hand. The gap nearly outruns them; every
   pulse shifts the forecast shadow a little closer to the world's edge, and
   the sunshade's unequal tension worsens.
8. The last needed spindle still carries a closed nest. Oru calls it abandoned
   under time pressure; Nemi's finer reading finds a slow living heat rhythm.
   Cutting it would complete the old repair specification, but neither can
   pretend the nest is debris.
9. Oru compares the surface track, not the ancient zero mark, and finds a
   narrower objective. Nemi returns to the tug; they coordinate eleven locked
   walkers with a long reserve burn, ride out the membrane's off-center recoil,
   and stop when the second umbra slides beyond the atmosphere.
10. They verify the nest stayed warm and the shade is holding, send a bounded
    repair status rather than a proclamation about the omen, and depart on the
    slower return arc. The final inspection alarm is timed to likely hatching,
    not to the unreadable machine's demand for completion.

## Continuity

- Canon used: The setting is the one physical world that persists through deep
  time; lost civilizations and repeatedly repaired, misattributed artifacts fit
  the established archaeology and memory-versus-myth foundation. The crew's
  mechanism-first vocabulary is local to its culture, and resemblance to other
  sun, moon, or shadow imagery does not imply a shared origin.
- Boundaries and unknowns: Keep this orbital-capable era undated and do not
  place it relative to the modern-like, Glass-Sea, or Ravel eras. The unnamed
  moon is not identified with either of Ravel's witnessed moons, and the
  unnamed world is not asserted to be literal real-world Earth. The sunshade's
  builders, absolute age, original climate purpose, symbols, power source, and
  relationship to any other artifact remain unresolved. Driftwings, their
  nests, vacuum biology, migration route, recurrence, and degree of
  intelligence are story-local and establish no universal biology. Surface
  omen reports do not define or convert a named religion or culture. The
  maneuver resolves the current double-shadow track only; it does not prove
  the shade permanently safe, fully repaired, or available as a general
  planetary control system.
