# Opus review: The Shadow That Nested

Independent audit for a later editing pass. It is not a `review.md` verdict,
does not change canon state, and does not authorize edits by itself.

## Verdict

- Dialogue: REVISE — the first crew exchange fails a plain paraphrase, and several more break Ground: the lag crosstalk, "G stays locked" at the ethical decision, and the E-sector "receipt" duel. On top of that, all three crew share a correction-and-comeback reflex.
- Prose: PASS — concrete, tactile narration with good creature and machine detail; only Minor polish, plus a required policy sweep of pump and reservoir references.
- Continuity and prompt: PASS — the prompt's promise is delivered and resolved. The omen thread is thin, and one deadline and one set of thermal figures need reconciling (all Minor).
- Edit scope: targeted-dialogue
- Existing review.md said: Verdict PASS, "Dialogue: PASS"; I disagree. It credits the lag, retraction and missed-receipt devices as strengths, and those are exactly where the dialogue breaks.

Radio discipline is the right medium for this story. Short calls, readbacks, Bero's conditional thresholds and a lagging ground voice make communication itself the pressure. Several exchanges are among the best in the piece: Pado refusing to leave D, "Hold hold, line under the jaw", and "Call contact only". The failures come in two kinds. First, some lines don't survive a plain paraphrase: the approach exchange, the lag crosstalk around Enver, "G stays locked" at the moral choice, and the E-sector receipt exchange. Second, the crew share a correction reflex. Bero polices words ("Was," "I said cheap, not available"), Pado retracts or labels almost every observation, and Adal answers challenges with a retort. All three also annotate their own speech acts ("That is a range, not a call," "I am giving you the branch, not arguing it"), so disagreements resolve as wording contests instead of stakes. The edit pass should keep the register and the numbers, make every line mean what it says in the scene, give the lag one consistent length, and turn quips and self-labels into plain orders, observations or refusals. G matters most.

## Dialogue findings

### D1 · Blocking · Approach exchange does not parse

- Where: L17–L25, tug approach, first crew exchange
- Text:
> "Eleven," Adal said. "Keep the moon out of the lamps."
> "The raw feed will lose the rim."
> "Capture first. Enhance after."
- Problem: Ground failure in the uptake and in the verb-object fit.
  - Bero offered two outcomes that depend on how the object behaves, not a choice between them, so "Eleven" answers a question nobody asked.
  - Lamps give off light. "Keep the moon out of the lamps" has no literal or figurative reading a reader can recover.
  - Bero's reply names a consequence (losing the rim) that has no causal link to the instruction. "Capture first. Enhance after" then answers a different worry: whether to enhance.
  - Two sentences later Bero kills the enhancement and the rim is plainly visible (L25, L33), so the warning comes to nothing.
  - A reader cannot say what the three of them were deciding.
- Fix: Settle on the one real issue, most likely moon glare swamping a dark, edge-on object in the optics, and write it plainly. Adal's instruction should name the optics. Bero's reply should name a concrete cost that the following paragraph honors. Adal's answer should address that cost. Turn "Eleven" into a plan or cut it. Illustrative only: "Plan on eleven. Keep the moon's glare out of the optics."

### D2 · Major · Lag crosstalk garbles who heard what

- Where: L39–L43 (fifty-three and fifty-two) and L107–L119 (the cut-it-loose crosstalk)
- Text:
> "I said fifty-two by the time you receive this."
> "Cut it loose from the collar," Enver said. He must have heard only the first part.
> Bero continued through Adal's answer.
> Adal waited out the overlap.
> "I heard tow," Enver said.
- Problem: The story uses signal lag as its dialogue engine, but these lag lines fail Ground.
  - L43: Enver said fifty-three, not fifty-two. "I said … by the time you receive this" welds a past claim to a future arrival, so it reads as a correction for its own sake.
  - L109: the narration has to decode Enver's line, and the reader must guess which "first part" he heard.
  - L111–L113: Adal's answer is a single "No," spoken together with Bero, so there is nothing for Bero to "continue through".
  - L115: "waited out the overlap" uses the story's key word for the two shadows meeting (L39, L117) to mean radio crosstalk.
  - L117: "I heard tow" can't be scoped. Did ground get the refusal to cut, or only one word?
- Fix: Keep the lag as pressure, but make every lagged line literally true.
  - L43: use a plain update or cut the line. Illustrative only: "Make that fifty-two when this reaches you."
  - L109: let Enver's proposal follow from words he could actually have heard, and drop the narration that interprets him. Or keep Adal's inference but have it name the words Enver caught.
  - L111–L115: stage the talk-over as it happened: both say no, Bero keeps going, Adal stops. At L115, replace "overlap" with another word.
  - L117: make Enver's acknowledgment say what he received, so the reader knows ground has the refusal.

### D3 · Major · Enver's lag switches on and off

- Where: L137, L193–L195 and L331–L333 (instant replies), against L145–L149, L279–L287 and L409 (delayed replies)
- Text:
> "How many?" Enver asked.
> "Six percent."
> "Three minutes to contact," Enver said. No preamble now.
> No answer yet.
> Silence. He clamped D's dog while he waited.
- Problem: Impossible timing, a Ground failure.
  - The story sets up a round trip long enough to open and seat a crank socket (L145–L149) and to clamp a dog (L281–L287). A whole update even gets lost and relayed later (L219, L243).
  - Elsewhere Enver answers the moment the crew stop speaking: "How many?" follows Adal's explanation, "Six percent." answers Bero's "Six?", and "Three minutes" answers Adal's "Time?".
  - The reader has been taught to track the delay, so ground replying in real time whenever the scene needs speed undercuts the story's own pressure.
- Fix: Pick one delay and apply it to every exchange with ground. For each instant reply, do one of three things:
  - insert a working beat before Enver answers;
  - make his line an unprompted update that was already on its way;
  - or give the question to someone in orbit. Bero can answer "Time?" from the last numbers received, and Enver's fresh figure can land later and differ.
  Enver's unprompted lines (L191, L317, L341, L365) are fine as written.

### D4 · Major · Omen closure line hides its stake

- Where: L15, Enver's opening report
- Text:
> They called it an omen closure, so the queues are empty. Storage can carry one shadow. Not both.
- Problem: An unrecoverable causal link at the beat that sets the stakes. It is also the only place where the prompt's "mistaken for a celestial omen" touches the plot.
  - The line says the yards closed and the queues are empty, but never says why empty queues matter. A reader could just as easily conclude the closures cut demand and help.
  - The outline's intent (the closures remove a margin the grid was counting on) cannot be recovered from the words.
  - As a result, the payoff at L409 ("West charging yard reopened one gate") lands only as a vague good sign.
- Fix: Add one clause to Enver's report saying what the omen closure costs the grid, in terms the crew and reader can hold. Keep it a report, not a debate about belief. Illustrative only: "People are calling it an omen and staying away, so we've lost the yards' buffer."

### D5 · Major · Correction and comeback reflex

- Where: L35–L37 (sunshade), L119 (ground), L171–L175 (cheap balance), L181–L187 (launch follows light), L283–L285 (no answer)
- Text:
> "Was," Bero answered.
> "Correction," another ground voice cut in.
> "I said cheap, not available. Give me every clear dog."
> "I need openings, not motives."
> "No answer isn't yes," Pado said.
> "I know what no answer is. Seat the clutch tool."
- Problem: Correction ping-pong and the Punchline Patch: disagreements resolve as wording contests rather than stakes.
  - L37: "Was" corrects a label that is accurate. The shade is still shading, and that is the emergency.
  - L171–L175: Bero proposes A, C, E, G seconds after Adal reports C and G occupied. Adal has to repeat himself so Bero can correct a misunderstanding Adal never had. The setup exists only for the correction.
  - L183–L187: Adal asks "Certainty?" and then scolds the answer he asked for. Both lines are untagged on a three-way channel right after Bero demanded "every clear dog", so the reader can't be sure who says them.
  - L283–L285: two aphorisms about silence. Pado's caution is real (Adal is clamping D before ground agrees), but the retort only wins the line.
  - L119: the ground "Correction" adds one more.
- Fix:
  - L37: cut "Was," and let Bero's diagnosis stand.
  - L171–L175: Bero has heard that C and G are occupied, so he drops the ideal set himself and asks for every clear dog. A passing regret about the cost is fine.
  - L183–L187: tag both lines. Either drop "Certainty?" or let Adal take the answer and move on plainly.
  - L283–L285: let Pado state the risk in her own terms, and let Adal answer with what he will and won't do before ground replies, for example clamp but not jack.
  - L119: cut the ground "Correction" or turn it into noise.
  - Keep L77–L79 ("I need the machine." / "You need to know what tightens when you move it."). It is the one correction that carries the story's conflict, and the test tick pays it off.

### D6 · Major · Pado's retraction and self-labelling tic

- Where: L157, L185, L237, L273, L309, L337
- Text:
> "Heat departure. No. I retract that.
> "No. Retraction: it follows a moving boundary that includes light.
> "My light-edge forecast was wrong about continued slack.
> "Clear at the dog and gap. That is observation, not a prediction."
> "Clear," she said, slower. "I withdraw 'under the jaw.' It was under the clamp plate. Jaw is clear."
> Maybe twenty seconds. That is a range, not a call."
- Problem: Her uncertainty suits a field-life specialist and the outline's pressure behavior. But it arrives as formal speech-act labels on nearly every call: "I retract", "Retraction:", "I withdraw", "That is observation, not a prediction", "That is a range, not a call".
  - The labels make her a clerk of her own statements and join Bero's and Adal's correction reflex (D5, D7).
  - The habit survives even the D scare. L309 comes seconds after her voice breaks at L297, and it spends the relief on whether a strand sat under the jaw or the clamp plate, when both are now clear.
  - "Maybe twenty seconds" is a single estimate, so calling it "a range" mislabels it.
- Fix:
  - Keep one explicit revision, at L157 or L185, the first time she changes her model, so the reader learns how she works.
  - Elsewhere, show her uncertainty through what she observes and declines to call: short present-tense reports, the specific animal or strand she is watching, or a plain "I don't know" (L241 already does this well).
  - After L297, let her be shaken. L309 can be "Clear." plus at most the location.
  - Cut the "That is X, not Y" tails at L273 and L337.
  - Then check that L347 ("Pado did not offer a meaning.") still has a habit to refer to, and trim it if not.

### D7 · Major · Decisive G exchange inverts "locked"

- Where: L289–L295 and the narration at L323, where Adal chooses D and H over G
- Text:
> "If H stays clear," Bero said. "If H closes, your remaining set needs G."
> "G stays locked."
> "Then H must work. I am giving you the branch, not arguing it."
> Its perfect place in the alternating set was no reason to put a tool through it.
- Problem: This is the story's ethical decision: Adal refuses to jack through the densest brood even if it costs the save. It passes in three words that say the opposite of what he means.
  - Everywhere else "locked" means dogged and set: "Dog A locked" (L207), "H locked" (L375), "A, D, E, H locked" (L407). Read that way, "G stays locked" promises to work G.
  - Bero then labels his own speech act instead of reacting, so nobody registers what the refusal costs.
  - The narration at L323 has to explain the choice afterward.
  - Bero's statement of the cost at L335 is fine.
- Fix: Give Adal a plain refusal that can't be misread. Let Bero answer with his real stake or the consequence rather than a comment on his own speech. No speeches: the choice should stay small and costly. Once the dialogue carries the choice, cut or shorten L323. Illustrative only: "Nobody touches G."

### D8 · Major · E-sector receipt exchange runs on quips

- Where: L255–L267, jacking sector E
- Text:
> "You're repeating me," he said.
> "Your suit isn't showing receipt."
> "My hand is the receipt."
> "I can't see your hand through a shutter. Hold."
> "Now I see it."
- Problem: The beat has a real point, and it is the main Adal–Pado friction before she takes the crank at H. At the first test, "Look at my hand" settled their dispute about stopping. Under the leaf she can't see his hand, so they need another signal. But the exchange plays as a word duel.
  - She isn't repeating him. She calls hold after he has already stopped, so "repeating me" misnames what she did.
  - The suit "receipt" appears here for the first time, with no sense of what it shows.
  - "My hand is the receipt" is a comeback.
  - "Now I see it" has no recoverable object. His hand is hidden, so what does she see?
- Fix: Keep the callback and the irritation, but make each line literal.
  - Adal's complaint should name what she did: she called hold after he had stopped.
  - If the suit acknowledgment matters, establish it plainly, as a light or a tone that didn't come.
  - End on a signal she can actually perceive, such as his spoken readback or the leaf stopping against the stars.
  - Cut "My hand is the receipt."

### D9 · Minor · Pado reports A's spans she cannot see

- Where: L211–L213 and L249, Pado at D while Adal works A

> "A looked clear at the edge. I can't see its underside from here."
> "A spans releasing," Pado said from D.
- Problem: Pado knows something she has no view of. She is three sectors away on a structure too large to see whole, and she says she can't see A's underside. Yet minutes later she calls A's spans releasing and sets his pulse size. Her "Stop" at L229 has no source either, unless D's animals clamped too.

## Continuity and prompt findings

- Where: L15, L25, L41, L409
- Text:
> The straight shadow had looked, from below, like something added to the sky.
> "West charging yard reopened one gate."

### C2 · Minor · Deadline and thermal numbers don't reconcile

- Where: L149, L191, L277–L287, L397
- Text:

> "Your bands must stay under ninety seconds at any one surface line,"
