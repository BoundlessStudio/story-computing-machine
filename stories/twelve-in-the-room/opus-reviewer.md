# Opus review: Twelve in the Room

Independent audit for a later editing pass. It is not a `review.md` verdict,
does not change canon state, and does not authorize edits by itself.

## Verdict

- Dialogue: REVISE — the opening exchange garbles who arranged the meeting, and Osselin's "There are now" clue depends on narration and is then dropped.
- Prose: PASS — concrete, controlled and well paced; only a few narration epigrams and one blurred beat at the rush.
- Continuity and prompt: PASS — the prompt's promise is delivered in full at about 2,970 words; one setup (the breast-pocket recording) never pays off.
- Edit scope: targeted-dialogue
- Existing review.md said: Verdict PASS, Dialogue PASS; partly disagree: the voices are distinct, as it says, but it missed two Ground failures at plot-bearing beats.

This is one of the stronger dialogue stories in the collection. With the tags
covered you can still tell the five speakers apart: Paske jokes and bargains,
Vestrin asks literal questions about the exit and swears under pain, the scarred
woman is hostile, Hesk goes from short practical questions to coaxing, and Osselin
speaks in clipped lines built on exact personal knowledge. The failures are
local. The first exchange never makes clear whether anyone dealt with Osselin
directly, so Paske complains to her about terms she has not yet been revealed to
have set, and nobody reacts when she claims to have written them. At the envelope
beat, "There are now" is a riddle that only the next sentence of narration
decodes, and the recording it points to never comes back. The edit pass should
settle both mechanisms in a few lines, loosen Osselin's last-word habit in the
Hesk confrontation, and leave the second half's dialogue alone.

## Edit guardrails

- Prompt: twelve assassins surround the woman each was hired to kill, realize she hired them all, and learn she has finally got every one of them into one room. The rating is R+ (graphic violence, bodily injury and strong language allowed; no sexual content). The story must stay complete and resolved, within 2,500–4,000 words (currently about 2,970), so edits should keep it near its present length.
- Close third person, past tense, through Hesk. All twelve are present and trackable: Hesk, Paske, Vestrin, the scarred woman, the bald man, the man in the red tie, the long-gun man, the gray-bearded man, the woman in the riding coat, the man with the stiff leg, the man in gold spectacles, the woman in green gloves. Only Hesk survives.
- Staging facts later beats depend on: the dead-man control is in Osselin's left hand and the pistol is holstered on her right; explosives are visible beneath the gallery and near the service door; the brick return in the service passage is established before the blast; Hesk's shot at Osselin sets off the room.
- Backstory: Alvera was killed eight years ago at twenty-seven. All twelve were on Paske's call. Hesk wrote the note (address, back room, her working hours). Osselin is sixty. Each contract paid half up front "yesterday".
- No universe entries bind these names (no LOCKED/CANON hits). The LOCKED content boundary allows this adult prompt's graphic violence as long as it stays focused on consequence, which it currently does.
- Works and must not be flattened: Vestrin's whole thread, Paske's glibness and his failed bid for a laugh, Osselin's terms reveal, the umbrella and scissors memories, and the plain, unexplained ending.

## Dialogue findings

### D1 · Major · Opening exchange muddles who arranged the meeting

- Where: L29–L33, Paske's complaint after the doors lock; compare L13 (Hesk's instructions) and L65 (the reveal)
- Text:
> Other buyers might attend, his instructions said, and might be armed.
> “You said a private appointment,” Paske said.
> “I told you there would be other buyers.”
> “Buyers.” He gave the word to the man in the tie, inviting him to enjoy it. “Right.”
- Problem: Ground/knowledge and uptake. L13 presents every instruction as coming from "the buyer", and L65 confirms that the assassins only ever dealt with intermediaries ("The people you spoke to were spending my money"). So Paske has no basis for "You said" to Osselin before the reveal. Osselin's untagged reply then claims to have written the instructions. If Paske's terms said "private", that is also untrue for him (see L61, his "private guarantee"). Paske skates past it, and Hesk, whose own instructions used that exact phrase about other buyers, doesn't notice either. The reader can't tell whether the assassins set the handover up with Osselin or with a client, which is the mechanism the prompt's reveal depends on. The first real clue to the realization goes by unnoticed.
- Fix: Decide the channel and make the lines fit it. Preferred: nobody dealt with Osselin directly. Paske complains about his own terms to the room or to the man in the red tie, not to her. Osselin, tagged, answers with the same wording as Hesk's instructions, without saying "I told you". Hesk registers in one clause that he has read those words before, which becomes the first crack of the realization that L57–L65 then confirms. Keep Paske's "Buyers. Right." mockery. Alternative: if the handover was set up with Osselin herself, say so once in L13, and have Paske contest her contradiction instead of ignoring it. Illustrative only: Paske, to the man in the red tie, "Mine said private."

### D2 · Major · "There are now" is only decoded by the narration, then abandoned

- Where: L93–L97, Hesk opens his envelope; setup at L13
- Text:
> His phone was recording from his breast pocket. The buyer wanted her alive on the recording, identifying the papers she handed over.
> “Are there copies?” he asked.
> “There are now.”
> He looked up at her, then remembered the phone recording against his chest.
- Problem: Ground/referent and inert uptake at a plot beat. On its own, "There are now" is a riddle: copies of what, made how, held where? Only the next sentence of narration hints that the answer is Hesk's own recording, and even then it doesn't add up. L13 says the recording was meant to capture her identifying the papers aloud, which she has not done, and it never says whether the phone films or where the recording goes. Having realized he may be carrying a copy of his own guilt, a professional killer does nothing with the phone, and it never returns (see C1). The line works as a last word rather than as information Hesk can act on.
- Fix: Keep the idea but make it visible. Make clear at L13 that the phone is filming, with the lens above the pocket, and say where the footage goes if it goes anywhere. At L95, let Osselin's reply point at the pocket with a glance or a nod so that Hesk's uptake comes from something he sees, and at L97 have him name what the lens has just caught (the note and photograph he held up at chest height). Give him one decision about the phone, even if it's leaving it running because touching it would draw the others' eyes. If the edit pass doesn't want the mechanism, cut the recording sentences from L13 and L97 and give Osselin an answer that stands on its own. Coordinate with C1.

### D3 · Minor · Confrontation runs on Osselin's corrective comebacks

- Where: L121–L137, Hesk's bid for special standing
- Text:
> “You brought the pills and ate my food. Alvera stayed.”
> “I read what you wrote.”
> “You sent us to get her,” he said.
> “To bring her home.”
> “She knew what you were. She took the accounts. She wasn't coming home.”
- Problem: Correction ping-pong in the decisive exchange. Hesk makes three claims in a row and Osselin answers each with a crisp rejoinder. The last, "To bring her home," simply swaps his word for a better one. Each line is grounded, and "Alvera stayed" is the best line in the story, but three in a row make her sound scripted. Hesk's three-part reply at L137 then drops into the same flat declarative register, so for a moment the two sound alike. The exchange does move (he loses standing and she admits her order), so this is polish, not a rebuild.
- Fix: Keep "Alvera stayed" and "I read what you wrote". Turn L135 from a word correction into a want, or a line that comes late or gets cut off. Give Hesk's L137 a hedge or an interruption so he sounds like a man making excuses, not delivering a verdict. Illustrative only: "I wanted her home." Keep "Yes. I did." at L141.

### D4 · Minor · Osselin explains the scheme twice

- Where: L65 (the reveal) and L101 (after the envelope)
- Text:
> “I hired you,” Osselin said. “Every one of you. Twelve separate commissions to kill me. The people you spoke to were spending my money.”
> Some of you already had. New faces. New friends. I kept paying to find you. Then paying to tempt you.
- Problem: Exposition and The Philosopher, in mild form. L57–L61 already let the room work it out: she knows the scarred woman's payment schedule and Paske's private guarantee, the bald man chokes, and Paske's grin goes. L65 then restates it in full, including how the money was routed. L101 is volunteered back story in paired fragments and parallel clauses, more polished than anything else she says. "You were the last to say yes" is the part that matters, and it's good.
- Fix: Cut L65 back to the bare confirmation, or give the realization to one of the assassins, since the prompt says they realize it. Drop "The people you spoke to were spending my money" unless D1 needs it. At L101, tie the speech to the gray beard at the doors (the running she's answering), break up the paired fragments, and keep "You were the last to say yes" unchanged.

### D5 · Minor · Two small Ground/staging wobbles

- Where: L105–L109, Paske's bribe; L157–L159, Hesk's coaxing
- Text:
> Paske took out his phone. “Let's find out how much you have left.”
> “We can sit down,” he said. “Nobody has to do anything just yet.”
> “Don't come closer.”
- Problem: At L105, "find out" plus the phone implies Paske can check something, which he can't, and with a gun in the room "how much you have left" briefly reads as time rather than money. Only Osselin's answer settles it. At L159, "Don't come closer" responds to a movement the narration never shows.
- Fix: Make Paske's opener read clearly as a bribe with the phone as the means of payment. Illustrative only: "What's it cost to walk out of here?" Give Hesk a half-step or a lean before L159, or change her line so it anticipates a move rather than responding to one.

## Prose findings

### P1 · Minor · Narration epigrams and a subtext gloss

- Where: L39 (first look), L75 (explosives revealed), L155 (coaxing)
- Text:
> At sixty she had learned nothing about being old.
> He had looked at her hands and missed the room.
> He knew that look too. She disliked anyone noticing she needed help.
- Problem: L39 is an aphorism the reader can't unpack: nothing learned about being old in what sense? L75 is a neat closing epigram that sums up what the paragraph before it has already shown (the control drew his eye and he missed the packages). L155 explains her look straight after "Her eyes came up", leaving the reader nothing to infer.
- Fix: Replace L39 with one concrete observation of how she carries sixty. Cut L75 or change it to a plain observation. Cut the second sentence of L155, or all of L155, and let "Her eyes came up" carry the moment.

### P2 · Minor · Blurred staging at the rush

- Where: L169–L177, Paske recruits the man in spectacles, then the rush
- Text:
> Paske showed him the phone and spoke too quietly for Hesk to hear. After a moment the man moved away from the far entrance.
> Hesk found himself looking into the scarred woman's barrel as she turned toward them. He fired.
- Problem: Paske's position is only "across the light", so it's unclear how he gets close enough to show the man in spectacles his phone before that man leaves the far entrance. At the rush, "them" could mean the attackers or Hesk and Osselin, and the reader can't see why the scarred woman's swing crosses Hesk. This is his first kill of a former colleague, so it's worth one clarifying clause.
- Fix: Place Paske near the far entrance, or have the man in spectacles come to him. Replace "them" with the rushers and say which side they come from, so that her swing passes across Hesk. Her motive can stay unknown to him.

## Continuity and prompt findings

### C1 · Minor · The breast-pocket recording never pays off

- Where: L13 (setup), L97 (recalled), L257–L267 (aftermath)
- Text:
> His phone was recording from his breast pocket.
> He lay still until he could draw breath, then began shouting that he was alive.
- Problem: The recording is set up precisely, recalled at a key moment, and then vanishes. In the aftermath Hesk takes stock of his teeth, hand, boot and gun, but never the phone, so the reader can't tell whether Osselin's copy survived with him. That matters because he ends the story calling for help.
- Fix: Settle this together with D2. If the recording stays, add one concrete, unexplained beat in L257–L267 that registers the phone (still in the pocket, still running, or crushed). Don't add commentary; the current ending is right. If D2 cuts the mechanism, remove L13's recording sentences too.

## Recurring patterns

- Osselin's last-word rejoinders: L45, L95, L107, L129, L135. Most are earned by her control and her knowledge. Only L95 (D2) and L135 (D3) need work. Don't add more.
- Three-part and paired declaratives in speech: L101 ("New faces. New friends."), L137 ("She knew what you were. She took the accounts. She wasn't coming home."). Covered in D3 and D4.
- Narration epigrams and glosses: L39, L75, L155 (P1). The rest of the narration mostly avoids them.
- Untagged short commands: L81 and L85 ("Stay there.") read clearly as Osselin's in context. Leave them, but don't lengthen that untagged run.

## Preserve

- The terms reveal, which lets the room work out the truth before anyone says it:
> “Half yesterday,” Osselin said. “A quarter on receipt of the papers. The balance when you sent proof I was dead.
- Hesk's bid punctured by one concrete fact, and his interior "He hadn't known that" after it:
> “You brought the pills and ate my food. Alvera stayed.”
- Paske's glibness and his missed laugh: "Then you've overpaid" and the narration that undercuts it, and his reply to her confession:
> Paske spread his arms. “There we are. We all made mistakes.
- Vestrin's thread, exactly as written:
> Vestrin stopped with her heel against the service door. “Does that one open?”
> “Both hands, you cunt.”
> “I opened it,” she said.
- Hesk's coaxing, which calls back to an illness he has misremembered:
> “I can come and sit by you,” Hesk said. “Like before.”
- "If this hand opens, the room goes." as a plain threat. Also the pink paper umbrella and kitchen-sink scissors memories, the scarred woman clutching at Hesk's trouser leg, Osselin's bare "Hesk" / "Come back here", and the unexplained final image of him shouting that he is alive.

## Suggested edit order

1. D1: settle whether anyone dealt with Osselin directly first, because L13, L29–L33, L61 and L65 all depend on it.
2. D2 with C1: decide once what the recording does, then edit L13, L95–L97 and the aftermath together.
3. D3: loosen the correction rhythm in the confrontation without losing "Alvera stayed".
4. D4: trim the double explanation now that D1 may have given the room its own realization.
5. D5 and P2: small Ground and staging clarifications around Paske's moves and the rush.
6. P1: narration polish last.
