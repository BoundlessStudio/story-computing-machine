# Outline

## Story

- Premise and central promise: Twelve in the Room. Twelve assassins surround their former broker, Osselin, then discover she purchased every contract. After eight years, she has assembled her daughter's killers. Deliver the employer revelation, her purpose, and a revenge trap broken by another killer's choice.
- Focal pressure or attachment: Hesk wants payment and escape, but still wants Osselin to distinguish her former favorite from the others. He located Alvera for them; his cherished innocence depends on never having touched her.
- Counterforce or complication: Osselin commissioned Alvera's abduction after her daughter fled with incriminating records. All twelve knowingly participated in killing her to protect themselves. Osselin includes herself in tonight's punishment, but cannot accept one assassin escaping. Paske contests her control; Vestrin refuses to wait.
- POV, distance, and information limit: Close third past through Hesk. Establish his complicity early; conceal Osselin's preparations, never knowledge he possesses. Other intentions require observable support.
- Governing movement and time shape: One night in an abandoned ballroom and service passage. Brief memories interrupt present recognition. Encirclement becomes competing escapes. Target 2,500–4,000 words.
- Speculative rule or ordinary-world constraint: Ordinary guns, locked public exits, concealed explosives, and a held control that triggers detonation when released. Osselin must remain conscious and physically hold it; the building has vulnerable passages she cannot personally guard. No supernatural protection or technical construction instructions.
- Dialogue promise: Killers discover the person buying their work intends to kill them, and familiar professional intimacy becomes a means of attack.
- Dialogic medium: Speech across gun barrels, private contract details, gestures toward weapons and exits, then partial hearing after gunfire.
- Dialogue engine: Questions verify the common employer; bargaining attempts create openings for movement. Osselin wants submission, Hesk seeks exemption, Paske recruits expendable allies, and Vestrin tests promises against escape routes.

## Voice

- Narrative texture: Hesk notices hands, changed bodies, and lines of fire before faces. Professional attention collides with pain and memories he abbreviates. Sentences lengthen during rationalization.
- Conversational texture: Payment questions, irritated corrections, unfinished offers, profanity, and occasional intimacy. No sequence of twelve introductions or testimonies.
- Rhetorical ownership: Hesk uses qualification and remembered favor. Osselin commands through exact personal knowledge but listens badly when contradicted. Paske's jokes establish temporary social authority; he changes promises freely. Vestrin asks literal questions and stops listening when actions answer them.
- Pressure behavior: Hesk becomes solicitous before becoming openly cruel. Osselin repeats a direction when her count breaks. Paske loses his audience before losing fluency. Vestrin's injury makes communication effortful without making her compliant.
- Relationship movement: Hesk wants exemption and cannot admit he values his mentor's opinion. Osselin wants obedience despite knowing what it bought her daughter. Paske wants followers; Vestrin wants room to leave. Exchanges strip away special standing and expose what help costs.
- Anti-default: Do not turn the gunmen into cooperative specialists or stage a tribunal that earns consensus about guilt. Their choices remain incompatible.

## Beats

1. Open with all twelve inside the ring. Separate prepaid commissions promised a private opportunity at the same handover; each must first collect a packet from Osselin. Competing arrivals buy seconds of restraint. Establish two public entrances, a service door, central light, and the count without a roll call. Image #1 supplies pale hair, white coat, dark armed circumference, and hard shadow.
2. Hesk recognizes her booking habits. Osselin discloses private contract terms and produces the packets: evidence of each assassin's part in Alvera's death. Establish explicitly that she hired all twelve. Her search ended when Hesk finally accepted. She reveals the held control; visible preparations substantiate the explosive threat. She intends to die with them and requires no confession.
3. Hesk seeks privileged access through former closeness. Osselin exposes his locating work and her abduction order. Keep Alvera concrete through one memory of resisting her mother's control. Paske offers money and identifies a possible rush at Osselin. Vestrin approaches the service door while others watch mouths and hands.
4. Paske attempts to seize the control, using another assassin's movement as cover. Gunfire breaks his temporary coalition. Osselin takes a serious wound but retains her grip. A shattered jaw prevents one wounded killer from making an intelligible request; another dies trying to use a companion as cover. Track positions, shots, injuries, and remaining people. Graphic damage changes what anyone can do.
5. Vestrin forces the neglected service exit and is wounded while holding it. Hesk can assist her through or exploit her obstruction of the others. He chooses escape, abandoning her grip on his sleeve. Osselin delays releasing the control because Hesk would survive; her need for completeness draws her toward his exit. From shelter beyond the doorway, he shoots her. Her released grip detonates the room. Establish sheltering masonry before this action, and keep blast mechanics undescribed.
6. Hesk survives badly injured and partly deaf; Osselin and the other eleven assassins do not. Resolve his escape briefly. Survival rests on Vestrin's work and his abandonment of her. End inside that immediate consequence, without a new pursuit, moral explanation, or triumphant retirement.
