# Provisional outline

## Promised experience

“Everyone knows Dakota” fixes the badlands’ most recognizable woman: sun-kissed blonde hair, a crooked smile and flowers in her skin, with broken hearts and burned settlements behind her. A ruthless warlord puts a million credits on her head, and hunters, mercenaries and former lovers come looking. Dakota has deliberately made herself easy to find, and the warlord cannot see what she’s up to. “Unfortunately for them” suggests a dangerous caper with some relish. The people who think they have her should find out she needed them there. Her motive, culpability and particular relationships are open. The later “update the rating for this one as it could get messy” leaves room for violence with consequences. Mature is the working target.

## Takes considered

- **All the Roads to Madder.** Dakota lets somebody really hand her in at a freight exchange, then turns the warlord’s payday into wages for the people who came to catch her.
- **The Claimants.** Old lovers give overlapping accounts at a shuttered prize hall. Dakota’s rival promises set the warlord’s guns for hire against their paymaster. The comedy of who betrayed whom could crowd out the hunt.
- **Firelight.** From a warlord’s viewpoint, Dakota stages her capture in a ruined town where fires give back the voices of those who lit them. The revelation of what she burned for him could command attention that belongs to her present choices.

## Provisional direction

Choose Madder. The pursuit gives Dakota enough vehicles to empty Breck Sorrell’s prison train. He posted the bounty for live delivery on its next stop at Madder, where he means to parade her in front of fresh captives. He put the million with the freight exchange there because cash on deposit will draw people who distrust his word. They can see it change hands when he accepts her. Dakota has been passing out exact sightings and correcting bad wanted pictures. The latest notices name the hour and tell the hunters to bring empty rigs for a chance to collect. The train always stops at Madder for a new engine that can take the grade. Once it turns into the hills there is no way to get multiple vehicles alongside.

Dakota counted on Della Quince coming. She knows someone Della has been looking for is already aboard, and has arranged for Della to reach her by the rear of the freight lane. Della used to share Dakota’s bed and might prefer a million to another promise. Dakota burned Della’s town for Sorrell. Della agrees to claim her and put up the fares after hearing Dakota’s account, with terms about where her truck will go. By then Dakota has induced a hunter to back his truck up to the very lip of the platform. He expects her to step into it and let him claim her. She needs it for the first load and intends to walk right across while Sorrell thinks he’s watching her last performance.

## Craft plan

**Teller.** Try close third with Dakota, whose attention to empty truck beds lets the reader in on part of the plan, and first-person past with Della, who notices splendid gestures chiefly when they make extra work. Two scenes at the freight exchange, about 3,500–4,500 words.

**Dakota.** She wants to see Sorrell watch his own train empty at his expense. She wants Della to save her a seat. She makes a sale as if the other person has all afternoon, jokes when pressed and becomes specific when shamed. The original gives her golden windblown hair, freckles, silver hoops and a chain, with flowers inked from throat to waist and a moth and moon phases centered above the red floral pleats. The blue sky, chain-link and low angle show how easily she takes possession of a place. She perches and looks right back. The wind lifts her hair high enough for the road to find her. She turns her throat blossom into the sun to settle the claim and puts her hands behind her as she makes Sorrell relinquish his hold. Let the same crooked smile play to a hunter who thinks he has won and later to Sorrell, who knows he has lost. Keep the bright silhouette and give her work boots.

**Della.** A freight hauler with two good tires and a truck she wants to drive far enough away to sleep a night. Her mother is aboard. She comes expecting to take a fortune alone and knows already that Dakota was paid to set the fire. She has to hear exactly when Dakota saw people in the street and why she drove away. She follows a practical thought all the way to the end even over a truck starting. She wants Dakota to trust her to set her own terms instead of putting words in her mouth for old time’s sake.

**Sorrell.** Dakota used to take jobs from him. Capturing a former employee who famously got away is worth a fortune to him because he wants others he employs to see the road close. He thinks that she has made a spectacle of herself and finally misjudged her audience. His courtesy assumes obedience and he tells stories about other people’s bargains. He wants to hear Dakota say aloud that she came willingly. Dakota knows how to sell him the shape of a victory.

**Madder Switch.** Freight siding below a red escarpment in the hottest dry month. The freight fair at Gilt Mile has the crowd and Marmot Basin’s dry lake has the chase. Madder has a loading lip where truck beds and railcar doors can meet, and a wide plain where a person on the lip can be spotted from the road. On the narrow grade ahead, road and rail part. The first engine pulls away at noon and the fresh one is due to roll in before the shadows stretch to the road. Drivers trade tires and shout loads across pale glare. Patched tin bangs in the wind. Hot wheel grease reaches the exchange and salt dries on the tongue. The fence wire is hot enough to let go of in a hurry.

## Scenes and ending

1. **The fence and freight lane (35%).** Dakota makes herself plain to the oncoming traffic and finds out how sharp an invitation she’s issued when one hunter tries to take her then and there. In the jam below her, an old lover stops his bike and holds up the second helmet he brought for her. Della comes up the freight lane as Dakota intended, in time to see Dakota offer the hunter an easier prize if he brings his empty truck up to her level. He goes round and backs to the spot she points out. Della wants the fortune and the truth about the fire. Give their owed conversation the time it takes for Dakota to tell what she lit, what she saw and whose company she chose. Della can still go home. Dakota offers her the real capture and the chance to buy out a trainload rather than buy her way clear alone. Della sets the destinations and what a driver will have to do to earn a fare. Dakota hands over her gun and offers her wrists. In the road a hunt jockeys for a lead that has already gone to somebody else.
2. **The loading platform, long scene (65%).** Old engine away, doors open for captives boarding. Della sees her mother. Sorrell accepts Dakota and the exchange lets Della count the cash in front of the pursuers. Under Sorrell’s hand Dakota asks to stand alone on the lip and tell his prisoners why she has come willingly. She offers him a story the departing drivers will carry. The woman who once walked out on his payroll came back of her own accord. He takes his hand off to make the claim believable. She stands precisely where she had the hunter park. The promised public reckoning between Dakota and Sorrell turns on what it means to have bought her. She steps onto the waiting truck and offers its hunter a sure fare for the people he puts in its empty bed. She calls the same deal to the other drivers. The hunter sees how he’s been used and takes the better of his remaining deals. Dakota leads the first people across herself. Sorrell fires into the first departing load and wounds someone. Dakota stays in sight to get the next load aboard. The second driver, one of Sorrell’s hired mercenaries, sees the first truck get away and its driver take cash from Della on the way past. His employer is already shooting into his own cargo, and the sure money is more than he’ll see from this job. Others back up. The new engine is coming. Follow the first wounded passenger far enough to feel the cost as the cars empty, and keep the danger of Sorrell’s men trying to close the doors.

   Della puts her mother in the cab and offers Dakota the place beside her she wanted. Dakota stays on the lip to claim that last car out from under Sorrell. When it is empty she slides the door shut herself, with him standing on the platform where he planned to exhibit her. He calls her back as if he still has a claim. She gives him the crooked smile at its full strength. She rides out standing among the last passengers, high enough to see the truck beds throwing up dust on several roads where one road brought them all in for her. The first hunter leaves with only the fare she named. Sorrell has bought that whole dispersing fleet and has the empty train right there to show for it.

Summarize the earlier sightings and the road damage Sorrell left through the people arriving. Leave the next campaign and the future of the lovers beyond the last dust.
