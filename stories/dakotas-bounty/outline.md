# Provisional outline

## Promised experience

“Everyone knows Dakota” fixes the badlands’ most recognizable woman: sun-kissed blonde hair, a crooked smile and flowers in her skin, with broken hearts and burned settlements behind her. A ruthless warlord puts a million credits on her head. Hunters, mercenaries and former lovers come from across the badlands. The reversal is explicit: Dakota has been making herself easy to find, and the warlord cannot see what she’s up to. “Unfortunately for them” suggests a vivid, dangerous caper with some relish. Enjoy the sight of the whole pursuit coming into range, then see what Dakota can do because they came. Her motive, culpability and particular relationships are open. The later “update the rating for this one as it could get messy” leaves room for violence and damage with consequences. Mature is the working target.

## Takes considered

- **All the Roads to Madder.** At a badlands freight exchange Dakota lets somebody really hand her in. The scene to earn is the moment she takes the warlord’s triumphant payday and uses it to hire the vehicles that came for her.
- **The Claimants.** An old lover and two more supposed winners tell overlapping accounts at a shuttered prize hall. Dakota’s rival promises set the warlord’s hired guns against their own paymaster as he tries to settle the claim. The comedy of who betrayed whom could crowd out the hunt itself.
- **Firelight.** From the warlord’s side, Dakota stages her capture in a ruined town whose fires still give back the voices of those who lit them. The big scene is her summoning the warlord to the fire she herself started for him. The dead town and the revelation of past crimes could command attention that belongs to Dakota’s present choices.

## Provisional direction

Choose Madder. Dakota needs enough vehicles at the same hour to empty a prison train before it enters the hills. It carries captives from several towns, including one she burned on Breck Sorrell’s payroll. Her exact sightings and hand-altered posters send the badlands’ independent haulers, hunters and hired companies to the train’s last loading stop. Some come for money and some to get even. Sorrell rides with the train and wants Dakota alive. He means to show the captives what becomes of the people who turn on him. He has deposited the million with the freight exchange to draw people who distrust a promise under his name. The exchange pays the claimant in public upon a live handover.

Dakota needs a claimant who will really take custody of her and who has a reason to let the money go. Della Quince, who used to share Dakota’s bed, comes to claim the whole sum. Sorrell’s train took her mother, and Dakota knows which car she’s in. Dakota asks Della to collect the actual bounty and put it up for hiring the gathered drivers, whose vehicles will become the only way to get that many prisoners over open ground. Della agrees to a joint offer after making Dakota say what she did in the fire and how much good she expected to buy herself with it. They both risk a real loss. Dakota must put herself in reach of a man who wants to take her away in irons. Della could buy her own way clear with the million. Sorrell will have men and guns right there when he sees what his money is buying.

## Craft plan

**Teller.** Try close third with Dakota in the hot present of each choice, with room in her thoughts for appetite and vanity. The reader can see from what she counts that she has been waiting for trucks. Try a very different first-person past from Della, who notices splendid gestures mainly when they make extra work and has her own reasons to remember every movement of Dakota’s hands. In either version Dakota acts at the center. Three scenes at the same stop, roughly 3,500–4,500 words.

**Dakota.** She wants to take back something Sorrell keeps by force and see him stand in front of an empty train. She wants Della to get in the same truck with her afterwards and has been avoiding the thought that Della could drive right on past. She trained herself to make a sale as if the other person had all afternoon. She jokes, gets very specific when shamed and resents having a quotation of hers corrected. The original puts a wind-tossed, golden-haired, freckled woman above the viewer, with silver hoops, a chain, an off-white top and red floral pleats against cobalt sky and chain-link. She perches, looks straight back and lets a silence last at her own pleasure. Let the wind at the fence lift her bright hair above the passing vehicles, and let her turn her throat flower into the light to certify her face against a bad print. The crooked smile begins as a dare for Della and comes back with effort when Sorrell expects fear. Keep the short red floral silhouette and give her practical boots. Borrow the commanding angle of the picture and give the narration her face and her look back.

**Della Quince.** She hauls freight, wants to get herself far enough from Sorrell to sleep a night and knows exactly what two sound tires are worth. She knows Dakota was paid to set the fire, believes Dakota left thinking Della would follow, and has had years to decide what she thinks of that. She initially expects the bounty to be a door out for herself alone. She wants Dakota to give her the whole account before they step up to the exchange. In a hurry her sentences are practical and consecutive. When she has something to settle she can talk right over a truck starting and still follow her own first thought to its end. Once she sees the prisoners she measures the offer in rides and safe destinations, with a truck of her own to fill.

**Breck Sorrell.** He knows Dakota can put on a show and thinks she has been showing off until somebody caught her. Capturing her is worth a fortune because she was his once, because people know how she got away and because he wants former associates to see that road close. His courtesy assumes that a person he addresses is already doing what he wants. He enjoys telling stories about other people's bargains. When the money changes hands he wants Dakota’s surrender to look voluntary. After the offer he wants it to look fatal to put a foot outside his cars.

**Madder Switch.** A rail siding and freight ramp where road meets rail at the foot of the badlands’ red escarpment. The cliffside road further along is too narrow for a mass pickup. The freight fair at Gilt Mile would supply a crowd, and Marmot Basin’s dry lake would give the pursuers room for a chase. Madder gives a single stretch of exposed platform where vehicle beds can come level with the cars and people can walk straight across. At the end of the hottest dry month, from noon to the lengthening shadow of the escarpment, drivers are trading tires, paying fares and yelling out loads. The glare from the pale fill hurts the eyes. The patched tin freight board bangs in the wind, hot wheel grease drifts across the exchange, salt dries in the mouth and the fence wire is painful to grip. Folks can see somebody up on the loading lip from a mile down the road. Once the train rounds the cut that whole open plain drops out of sight.

## Scene briefs and ending

1. **The fence and freight lane (25%).** The reader meets the hunt in daylight: jammed road, hunters with competing pictures, mercenaries with their best trucks, a onetime lover who came with a second helmet and another who came to see Dakota dragged away. Dakota is waiting in plain sight and asking which trucks came empty. Della wants her head start and plain talk. Dakota wants her claim and tells her her mother is in the train about to arrive. Della’s question about the fire is what the story owes these former lovers. Let Dakota tell what she did and when she realized the street still had people in it. Let Della press on the instant Dakota decided whose company to keep. Della sets her terms for the offer. Dakota gives up her weapon and puts her hands where Della can take them.
2. **The loading platform, long scene (60%).** New captives come past the freight exchange to board and Della sees her mother among the already occupied cars. The distinctive flower at Dakota’s throat makes a better identification than the posters. Sorrell brings Dakota right to the train to make his victory visible, and the exchange pays the claim. Dakota speaks to the waiting drivers while he has her by the elbow. The million will pay for lifting the people out of his cars. Della shows the money and names what the first driver stands to make. Sorrell tries to close the cars and turns his gun on the first truck at the ramp. Some look away and some of his hired crews go for the fare. In the crush Dakota gets free, takes the exposed driver’s seat of the first truck when the wounded driver goes down and gets its empty bed to the first car door. Too close to the platform now to go without its passengers, she opens the way and makes the offer real for every driver still on the road. Sorrell can see the subsequent trucks arriving faster than he can shut the length of the train. Give the haste and the hurt their time. Dakota watches Della pull in for her mother while Dakota can still help the last people aboard.
3. **The last load (15%).** Dakota has a clear ride beside Della before the last car is emptied. She stays up on the rail lip directing the last trucks and counting the people she can see. She leaves when the last driver has room, along with people she helped put here. The harsh noon waited for one unmistakable face. From the truck bed where someone moves over for her, she can see dust from one road coming in and trucks on several roads out. Sorrell is left with his emptied cars and a million paid. We stay with the last turn of the pursuit and the sight of the drivers taking these people out past the horizon.

Summarize Dakota’s trail before Madder, the road damage left by Sorrell and the earlier work she did for him through what the arrivals bring and what she has to tell Della. Leave the warlord’s next campaign and the future of the lovers beyond the last dust.
