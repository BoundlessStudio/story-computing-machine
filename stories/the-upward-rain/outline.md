# Outline

## Story

- Premise and central promise: When the once-in-a-lifetime upward rain lifts the sea from Caudren Reach for one day, rival explorers Tressa Vahl and Rudek Sohl race down the exposed continental slope toward the legendary drowned kingdom of Namarre. Each expects a recognizable capital and wants the sole first discovery. Instead they find roads without fallen walls, numbered foundation sockets, and drag scars leading landward. Fulfill the prompt by revealing that Namarre existed but was deliberately dismantled before the sea covered it; the surviving ruin is an organized absence, and neither explorer can establish that truth without the other's evidence before the water returns.
- Intended reader experience: Awe and physical unease beneath an ocean suspended as a dark, moving ceiling, sharpened by the pleasure of watching two excellent observers misread the same ground in different ways. Let rivalry make each find entertainingly provisional rather than making either explorer foolish. The recognition should feel large but intimate: they recover a specific truth about how the city vanished, not its people's entire fate, and emerge with their argument altered rather than abolished.
- Generating force:
  - Mode: Plot-led fork-and-rejoin descent. Tressa follows inscriptions and remembered route verses; Rudek follows slope, load traces, and old soundings. Each apparent landmark separates their paths, discredits one prediction, and then reconnects the two observations at a lower, emptier layer until absence itself becomes the recoverable pattern.
  - Focal attachment or contradiction: Tressa has built her career on reading the surviving Namarrean fragments as records rather than romance, but she also needs to be the person who proves that reading. She respects Rudek's field sense while resenting that his speed and showmanship can turn her careful work into a footnote. Their attachment is to the same lost place and to rival forms of attention; each wants Namarre accurately recovered and personally claimed.
  - Plot-led wants and useful limit, if applicable: Tressa immediately wants to reach the inscribed royal precinct first and return with contextual evidence, while competing pride makes her want Rudek unable to share the claim. Her useful flaw is interpretive possessiveness: she withholds uncertain readings until she can present them as conclusions, so Rudek lacks information that would correct his route and she lacks his contradictory measurements. Rudek wants an unmistakable find he can carry back and also wants both of them off the seabed before the first downward rain; his useful limit is momentum, treating hesitation as defeat and portable objects as stronger evidence than fixed context.
- Counterforce or complication:
  - Source: The exposed seabed is an unfamiliar landscape losing hydrostatic support: saturated slopes slump, clay opens into cracks, brine hollows retain stranded water, and the long return climb remains finite even before the suspended sea begins to lower. The day's end cannot be timed to a minute; ordinary downward droplets at the coastal rim are the reliable late warning, not a safe starting signal for ascent. Rivalry compounds the environmental limit by splitting observations that are useful only together.
  - Credible inside logic, if a person: Rudek is a rival and countervoice, not an antagonist. He has spent his adult life preparing for the single descent he is likely to receive, knows that a find left below cannot be revisited in their lifetimes, and reasonably distrusts Tressa's habit of delaying a reading until every uncertainty is controlled. After challenge he continues to dispute her interpretation, chooses what risks he will take, and contributes the terrain evidence rather than becoming her assistant.
  - Pressure or change created: Speed favors a dramatic but decontextualized object; reliable interpretation requires stopping where the sea will soon return. Keeping discoveries private protects priority but produces two persuasive wrong answers. The deeper they go, the less the terrain resembles a drowned catastrophe and the more time they have invested in refusing each other's methods.
- Narrative design:
  - POV, tense, distance, and limitation: Close third past through Tressa, intimate with her bodily calibration of depth, her fragmentary readings, and the conclusions she declines to voice. She cannot know what Rudek has measured unless he tells her, cannot see the whole exposed basin from below, and initially translates his haste as shallowness. Rudek's objectives remain legible through his route choices, clipped field vocabulary, and refusal to surrender an observation merely because Tressa can name its script.
  - Time shape and compression: One continuous daylight descent and ascent. Give the sea's rising, the contested first step, the first false avenue, both failed palace sites, the discovery at the empty central foundation, and the first downward rain full scene space. Compress stretches of difficult travel through alternating finds and losses of elevation. End shortly after the sea has returned; use no flashback scenes, letting old rivalry surface through practiced accusations and selectively quoted expedition history.
  - Information and reveal strategy: Establish the upward rain's local, observed limits through preparations and immediate sensory consequence without explaining its origin or promising exact periodicity. Let readers initially treat the lack of rubble as erosion, then accumulate evidence against sudden drowning: extraction notches, matched empty sockets, numbered loads, worn haul ramps, and drag grooves aimed toward the old shore. Tressa's translation of a buried removal tally and Rudek's map of converging haul traces together support deliberate dismantling; neither proves where the stone or inhabitants went. Do not reveal a preserved palace, living underwater dynasty, royal treasure, anomaly-control device, or automatic ancestral link to the present coast.
  - Governing movement: Watch rain rise; contest the first step; descend under the hanging sea; read a road; claim opposite directions; withhold; separate; find two impressive dead ends; rejoin lower than intended; notice repeated absences; compare one measurement too late; follow a haul groove rather than a ceremonial axis; reach the empty palace footprint; uncover a fixed removal tally; align its numbered loads with the landward scars; abandon sole explanations; record in parallel; begin the return at the first ordinary drops; climb through a slumping route by exchanging observations; shelter; dispute the first accurate sentence.
- Speculative surplus: The suspended sea acts as a moving lens. Sunlight arrives in racing bands, and fish, boats, and waves cast enormous, distorted shadows across the bare floor; exposed optical plates bleach unless kept under amber hoods, so both explorers must uncover their recording gear only for short intervals. The effect adds beauty and practical inconvenience but does not point toward Namarre, authenticate evidence, disclose motive, or encode a message.
- Structural distinction: A paired fork-and-rejoin excavation of negative space, not a race that culminates in possession of one marvelous object. Each lower movement contains less architecture and more legible removal, reversing the expected spectacle. The decisive venue is an almost empty foundation while the sea still hangs above; the collective world remains offstage, the rivals do not convert one another, and no institution, crowd, ceremony, or solved mechanism closes the story.
- Decisive turn, deepening, or recognition: At the supposed palace, Tressa finds a facedown stair slab whose protected underside lists numbered buildings and completed stone loads rather than royal burials. She can read the verbs but not the destination. Rudek overlays his route sheet and shows that the "canals" he mapped are paired haul scars converging on ramps toward the former shore. Each first tries to fit the other fact beneath a sole-discoverer theory; the pattern will not hold. When the first true raindrops strike dust at the basin's outer edge, they leave the slab in context and spend their remaining safe minutes making complementary records, each naming the other's observation in their own notes without agreeing on what happened after the removal.
- Aftereffect or live uncertainty: They reach shelter before the sea returns and can strongly support Namarre's existence, organized dismantling, and landward transport, but not whether its people escaped, where the materials went, why the sea advanced, or whether any present community descends from them. The seabed and everything they failed to record are inaccessible for another lifetime. Their rivalry persists as a dispute over inference and wording, but neither can honestly publish a complete account that erases the other.
- Opening / ending relation: Open at the newly dry edge with both explorers timing the last upward drop and refusing to be the second person onto the seabed. End in a dark coastal shelter after the sea's return, with Tressa and Rudek arguing over the first sentence of a field account because her translation cannot say where the loads went and his map cannot say what they carried. The opening makes priority a footrace; the ending makes accuracy an unresolved, shared piece of work.

## Beats

1. At dawn above Caudren Reach, rain reverses in sheets and draws the coastal sea upward until the ocean floor opens beneath a suspended, refracting mass. Tressa and Rudek perform separate gear checks, trade familiar challenges, and step onto the wet margin nearly together despite each having planned a solitary triumph.
2. They descend the first exposed terraces under racing fish shadows. A straight ridge and a worn marker appear to confirm a royal road; Tressa reads only part of its eroded text, Rudek measures its grade, and each withholds the detail that weakens their preferred destination.
3. The route forks. Tressa follows repeated marks toward the precinct named in surviving fragments; Rudek takes a lower channel toward the stone mass in his soundings. Tressa reaches aligned foundation sockets with no walls or collapse debris, while Rudek finds a natural reef armored with a few deliberately placed blocks. Each has found human work but no capital.
4. A seabed slump and their converging safety lines bring them back together below both sites. Their accusations expose the missing facts. Against inclination, they compare one rubbing with one contour sheet and notice that the supposed avenue cuts across ceremonial orientation but follows the easiest hauling grade.
5. They descend by the haul trace through increasingly bare domestic and workshop foundations. Numbered extraction notches repeat; stone chips lie on the uphill sides of sockets; drag scars join rather than scatter. Small ordinary remnants establish habitation, but nothing supports a sudden royal catastrophe.
6. At the deepest central foundation, Tressa uncovers the protected face of the removal tally while Rudek maps the paired grooves leaving it. Their evidence resolves the discovery together: this was Namarre, and its structures were inventoried, dismantled, and hauled toward land before submergence. The destination and people's fate remain unreadable.
7. Downward rain begins at the distant rim. Rudek proposes taking the slab; Tressa objects that removal will consume their margin and destroy its orientation. She proposes a slower complete copy; he objects that the sea will beat them. They choose a bounded division: her rapid line transcription and his fixed-point plate sequence, each explicitly keyed to the other's measurements, then leave the slab.
8. On the ascent, soft ground erases part of their outward route. Tressa's remembered marker order and Rudek's slope judgment together find a safer traverse without turning the climb into a rescue spectacle. They enter the coastal shelter as the sea comes down, and begin the contested account while the lost ground disappears again.
