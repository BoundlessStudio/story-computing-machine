# Outline

## Story

- Premise and central promise: **A Beginner of Considerable Age.** An ancient dragon overlooks the adventurers' beginner clause and becomes a hound-sized level-one dragon. Retrieving a stolen cup with an equally grandiose human novice supplies the peril he wanted, on humiliatingly ordinary terms.
- Focal pressure or attachment: Orsugal envies returning adventurers their narrow escapes. Immense strength makes everything predictable. He wants something worth remembering and expects to be its most impressive participant.
- Counterforce or complication: Melicent wants a magnificent first victory after years postponing adventure. She owns second-hand armor and practices sword flourishes, but has never fought. Each encourages the other's worst estimates. A boar-sized tusked badger wants its stolen hamper left alone.
- POV, distance, and information limit: Close third past through Orsugal. Pride distorts comparisons, never geography or operative limits. Melicent's intentions require speech or visible behavior; no divine interiority.
- Governing movement and time shape: One day: enrollment, expedition, chase, return. Expand physical adventure; compress ancient history. Target 2,500–4,000 words. Bored invulnerability becomes frightened exhilaration without curing vanity.
- Speculative rule or ordinary-world constraint: Two living local gods enforce their adventuring compact. Every entrant starts at level one. Orsugal retains age, memory, identity, and draconic anatomy, but receives a small body, beginner strength, vulnerable scales, flightless wings, and a brief candle-sized flame that tires him. Enrollment suspends former capabilities; remembering them restores nothing. Retirement at the temple can end the compact, but he cannot toggle powers in the field. Completed work earns advancement, not a species exemption.
- Dialogue promise: Two beginners egg one another on, compete over credit, misunderstand practical intentions, and discover that asking for help can coexist with boastfulness.
- Dialogic medium: Face-to-face argument, a written contract, shouted directions, unreliable stealth gestures, and breathless interruptions. No sentient status interface.
- Dialogue engine: Competitive claims select the job and sabotage its quiet approach. Corrections and explicit requests change who takes risks and when the other acts; neither becomes a moral instructor.

## Voice

- Narrative texture: Notice Orsugal's inconvenient weight, reach, hunger, and contact with rough surfaces. Ancient grandeur collides with small distances; keep fast action spatially plain.
- Conversational texture: Competitive exaggeration, ordinary complaints about equipment, interruptions, and badly timed amusement. Permit useful single-purpose directions alongside arguments.
- Rhetorical ownership: Orsugal overestimates remembered grandeur's persuasive value. Melicent appropriates heroic conventions to demand opportunities, sometimes finding him funny when he means to intimidate. The gods state terms without witty corrections.
- Pressure behavior: Orsugal's threats shorten into bodily concerns. Melicent gets louder and repeats an unhelpful instruction; embarrassment obstructs listening. Both recover enough precision to act, then lose composure to laughter.
- Relationship movement: He wants admiration without patronizing protection; she wants a partner without surrendering the leading role. Neither comfortably asks to be carried through failure. A specific request and its fulfilled timing replace unilateral performances with competitive reliance.
- Anti-default: Avoid the wise companion teaching an immortal humility. Both create the predicament; the dragon's appetite grows rather than becoming a lesson about ordinary life.

## Beats

1. **Envy and enrollment.** Orsugal arrives enormous, attracted by recruits laughing over an escape. Worshippers make offerings to two visibly living gods who hold the local charter's authority. He skims the promises and signs past the beginner clause. The gods reduce him; a failed majestic ascent establishes lost flight. Melicent witnesses it. Retirement is available, but leaving would preserve the boredom he came to escape.
2. **A disputed first treasure.** Both claim a beginner's retrieval: a festival silver cup vanished with a food hamper carried into a ruined hunting lodge by a tusked badger. They register together, competing for the eventual tale's starring role. Orsugal expects intimidation; Melicent wants combat. Borrowed rope and ill-fitting equipment cause friction. Establish the hall, enclosed kennel run, ordinary iron gate, and small feeding hatch during their approach.
3. **A poor entrance.** A contested stealth signal sends both through one opening. Metal catches, his tail gets trodden on, and their dispute wakes the badger. It knocks Melicent's sword away. Orsugal's flame fails to deter it; useless wings barely steady his scramble. Keep the cup's position legible. No concealed reserves or sudden expertise rescue these failed attempts.
4. **An adventure at last.** Expand the pursuit: overturned furniture, awkward climbs, frightening proximity, a bruised wing. Fear becomes intensely absorbing. Their rivalry worsens their choices before Orsugal requests help. He can draw the badger into the kennel run and escape through the hatch; Melicent must close the gate. Establish her safe position and sightline before commitment.
5. **The small way out.** Orsugal snatches the cup and draws pursuit. The hatch accepts his beginner body. Melicent nearly closes early, hears his correction, and waits despite the animal. Timing and a firm latch complete their escape. Both contribute and remain ridiculous. They release the badger unharmed by pulling their rope from safety after withdrawing.
6. **Only level two.** Returning the cup completes the job; the gods advance both by one modest level without erasing soreness or repairing equipment. Orsugal chooses continued adventuring over retirement. Melicent's invitation includes another dispute over their heroics. Close on pleasure in their shared ridiculous, frightening experience and appetite for another; no restoration of ancient power or final moral speech.
