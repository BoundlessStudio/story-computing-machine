This story does not show the collection's usual failure. With the tags covered you can still tell Orsugal from Melicent. He talks in stately, Latinate conditionals ("I should prefer it awake when it surrenders", "It has shown some willingness"). She talks in plain contractions about kit and practicalities ("I've had it nine years... I'm wearing it"). Each loses fluency in a different way under fear: he squeaks and says "I can't!", and she snaps "I said I can do it." The exchanges change things. On the road she answers his question honestly and he dodges hers. On the table their rivalry turns into reliance. The retirement question gets two words, not a speech, and the final scene echoes the opening without explaining it. The edit pass should only anchor one referent, sort out one untagged panic pair, make one quip easier to follow, and fix the rope's path. It should not smooth the rivalry into courtesy or add more wit.

## Dialogue findings

### D1 · Minor · Laugh explained by a noise the reader never heard

- Where: L39–L43, failed first flight outside the temple
- Text:
> He landed on his nose, and a woman on the steps made a noise inside her helmet.
> "Yes. Sorry. It was the noise you made."
- Problem: Ground/referent. Melicent says she laughed at "the noise you made", but the narration gives Orsugal no sound when he lands. The only noise it mentions is hers. The reader has to invent the joke's trigger, and "the noise" briefly seems to point back at her own helmet noise.
- Fix: At L39, give Orsugal an audible, undignified sound as he hits the ground (a squeak or a honk). One clause is enough. That anchors her line, and L171's "small, disgraceful squeaks" then echoes it. Keep her "No." / "Yes. Sorry." reversal as written.

### D2 · Minor · Pony quip makes the reader do the work

- Where: L75–L77, taking the task at the temple
- Text:
> "It frightened my pony. I don't want anyone hurt over a cup."
> "I could frighten a pony," said Melicent. "Let us try."
- Problem: Ground/uptake. The owner is worried about people getting hurt. Melicent's reply only works once the reader supplies the missing step: ponies scare easily, so scaring one proves little about the badger. The line can also read as an odd boast that she is as fearsome as the badger. It is her first line in the job scene, so it sets her tone. "Let us try" is fine, read as a request for the owner's permission.
- Fix: Keep the deflating bravado but make the point visible in her own concrete terms, and keep "Let us try." Illustrative only: "Ponies are frightened of hats. Let us try."

### D3 · Minor · Untagged gauntlet exchange

- Where: L183–L189, Melicent hauling Orsugal onto the table
- Text:
> Halfway up, the gauntlet began to slide off.
> "Don't pull that!"
> "Pull me!"
- Problem: Attribution. The narration names no speaker for either exclamation. The first could be Orsugal warning her about the gauntlet, and only "Pull me!" lets the reader work backwards. The matching "pull that / pull me" wording also reads more like an authored gag than panic.
- Fix: Tie the first line to Melicent with a beat or a clearer complaint about her own kit, which fits her fuss over the cloak and helmet, and keep "Pull me!" as his answer. Illustrative only: "My glove!"

## Continuity and prompt findings

### C1 · Minor · Rope rigged over the wrong side of the gate

- Where: L261, L295, L311, rigging the latch and releasing the badger
- Text:
> tied one end of the rope to the latch lever, and passed the rope over the gate's top rail.
> Melicent appeared round the corner with her recovered sword, trailing the rope.
> She led him back round to the front, paying out the rope as they retreated behind a boulder beyond the clearing.
- Problem: Staging. Melicent crouches in the gap between the open gate and the wall, where the latch face is. A rope passed over the top rail from there ends on the gate's other face, which is the run side once the gate is shut. She could not then pull it from the hall and the front door. Separately, she arrives at the back "trailing the rope" and then walks back round to the front "paying out" the same rope, so the reader loses track of where the line runs. Few readers will work the geometry out, but the release is shown and should hold up.
- Fix: At L261, route the rope "over a beam above the gate" (the hall has a roof) instead of over the gate's top rail. Keep "She tugged; the lever rose." At L295, drop "trailing the rope" or say she left the coil at the front doorway. At L311, have her pick it up there and pay it out only on the retreat to the boulder.
