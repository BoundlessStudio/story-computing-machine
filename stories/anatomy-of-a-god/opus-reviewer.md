# Opus review: The Anatomy of a God

Independent audit for a later editing pass. It is not a `review.md` verdict,
does not change canon state, and does not authorize edits by itself.

## Verdict

- Dialogue: REVISE — nearly every exchange ends on the narrator's dry put-down of his claim. The climax turns that into a thesis-and-correction duel, and the one comeback the prompt actually promises (the "ego" line) is the one the story drops.
- Prose: REVISE — clean, concrete and well paced, but the climax staging does not hold together (gate, supports, basin, direction of the throw). The escape gate is default drainage infrastructure.
- Continuity and prompt: REVISE — the prompt's situation is delivered, but its signature exchange appears only as flat paraphrase (handled under D2). Two small loose ends: the doors and the coat.
- Edit scope: targeted-dialogue
- Existing review.md said: Verdict PASS; "Dialogue: PASS", on the grounds that the voices are distinct and the decisive and final exchanges "change leverage without restating the ending". I agree the voices are distinct. I disagree with PASS: the exchanges all run on one comeback template, the climax states its theme aloud, and the decisive prompt exchange has lost its charge.

The three voices really are different. The man talks like liturgy, the narrator like a carer at work, the remembered stepsister like family at midnight. The story's best dialogue is its silences: the withheld answer at L103–L105 and the unspoken reply to "What do you see?" at L133–L135. The problem is shape. Almost every exchange goes the same way: he makes a ceremonial claim, she answers with a dry literal put-down, and the exchange ends on her line. Several of these land ("I witnessed you get very hot.", "It's water."). By the climax, though, the template has become the comeback duel the outline itself ruled out, with the theme said out loud and a correction on top. Meanwhile, the one topper the prompt asks for, after "I am a god", has become a shrug. The edit should cut the weaker quips and leave some of his lines unanswered. The prompt's exchange should get its full weight back: spoken disbelief, spoken outrage, one earned retort.

## Edit guardrails

- Prompt (acceptance authority): the WP exchange must stay recognisable. His regeneration is counteracted and he can't believe it. She tells him regenerators are common where she comes from and her stepsister is one. He insists he is a god, and she punctures his ego. The rewrite request is about dialogue quality; it does not remove the original prompt's requirements. First-person past, broadly accessible, injuries non-graphic. Length band 2,500–4,000 words; the story is about 2,950, so small additions are affordable. Craft profile prospective-2026-08-21, which allows one earned aphorism or articulate exchange. The prompt's retort is the one to spend.
- Story facts to keep: no proper names anywhere. The narrator is not gendered on the page (the outline calls her "she"); don't add gender markers. The stepsister's two crossing repair chains versus his offset pair ("Two crooked lines sharing the middle"). Renewal costs heat, water and salt. The bind is temporary and reversible. She leaves him alive, with the release buckle facing his free hand and a cup of water beside him. The flooded ravine road, and the goat track as the way out.
- Universe: `universe/rules.md` "Divinity" (CANON) defines godhood as worship plus power plus authority. The story must not rule on whether he is a god, and "Many who mend. I don't know if any of them are you." keeps it open. The narrator's homeland stays unnamed and unconnected to any canon place or crossing. I found no LOCKED or CANON conflicts.
- Keep what works: his uncontracted ceremonial register and his arc (ceremony, then fragments, then strategy, then one quiet question). The stepsister's remembered lines. The silences. The narrator's clinical attention to heat, salt, pupils and gait.
- When you rebuild the exit gate (P2), don't bring in other water infrastructure. Keep violence non-instructional.

## Dialogue findings

### D1 · Major · Every exchange ends on her deflating last word

- Where: L47–L53 (grapple at the doors) and L77–L83 (flight down the stair). The pattern runs through L63, L117, L131, L219, L229, L243 and L267.
- Text:
> "This house tried to drown me through the ceiling."
> "Is that who they were meant to be?" I asked.
- Problem: Epigram/last-word template. In nearly every exchange he makes a ceremonial claim and she answers with a dry literal put-down that closes it. This is the comeback duel the outline's own anti-default rules out. The best instances earn their place; these two don't. At L53 she answers his demand for acknowledgment with a joke about leaks. "Drown" and "ceiling" overstate what the scene shows (rain tapping through the high windows into bowls, L21). The joke doesn't serve her one need, the door, and he ignores it. L83 is called down the stair in mid-flight. "That" and "they" resolve only through the paintings paragraph slotted in before it (L81), nothing answers it, and nothing changes. Because she wins every exchange, her speech never shows fear, although she has just been grabbed, thrown and slammed into the stair. The narration admits fear once (L123). His lines, meanwhile, become setups.
- Fix: Cut L53 and let "I only need the door." carry the exchange, or have her just haul on the strap. Cut L83 so that his "You came into my refuge… beneath my image" goes unanswered. If she needs to speak there, it should be a stall that serves her flight, not a jibe. Keep L63, L117, L131 and L267 as the story's put-downs. If one of her lines should show strain, L131 (held against the wall) is the natural place, but don't impose it. Don't replace the cut quips with new ones. D2 and D3 cover the climax instances.

### D2 · Major · The prompt's decisive exchange is muted, and "You keep saying that" is unsupported

- Where: L195–L219, the bind holds, from "What did you do?" to "Stop pulling."
- Text:
> "My renewal." He pulled once, weaker. "You stopped it."
> His mouth tightened around the word as though I had called him livestock.
> "I am a god."
> "You keep saying that."
- Problem: This is where the prompt's four lines live, and each has lost its force. The prompt's disbelieving question ("You actually managed to counteract my regeneration?") has become a flat statement. "We know how to deal with your anatomy", the source of the title, never appears. The god's outburst ("What are you talking about!? I am a GOD!") is split into a narrated reaction (L211) and a flat "I am a god." The prompt's payoff, "You have the ego of one", is replaced by a shrug. "You keep saying that" also has no support on the page. This is the first time the word "god" appears anywhere in the prose, and his earlier claims were indirect ("death obey me", "my image"). So the story drops its one earned comeback, the one the prompt asks for, while the weaker quips in D1 stay. The outline chose "not preserved prompt phrasing", but the outline is advisory and the prompt controls.
- Fix: Restore the prompt's shape in the story's own voices, and keep its honest twist ("I guessed you'd be similar. You weren't. Not quite."). Turn L201 into a disbelieving question. Let him speak his outrage at "regenerators" instead of L211 narrating it, then cut L211. His ceremony can crack here: repetition and broken ceremony fit the outline's plan for how he behaves under pressure. Let her name his anatomy plainly, since the title depends on it, in her own looser words ("hell" is available). Replace "You keep saying that" with the prompt's ego retort. That makes it her one real topper, and "Stop pulling." can follow it unchanged. Illustrative only: "Well, you've got the ego of one."

### D3 · Major · The climax becomes thesis, topper and correction

- Where: L225–L243, the bind at the basin, from "body for the being" to "what happens next"
- Text:
> "You mistake the body for the being," he said through his teeth.
> "I'm working on the part that grabbed me."
> "You expect obedience."
> "No. I'm telling you what happens next."
- Problem: Philosopher plus Punchline Patch. His line states the story's central question (body against godhood) as an aphorism, and her reply tops it. The pair reads as authored thesis and antithesis at the story's crux. Then "You expect obedience." / "No. I'm telling you…" is correction ping-pong: the moment of his real choice turns into a ruling on what kind of speech act she is making. L239 also states as fact something she only knows from her stepsister: "The straps loosen when your body quits choosing between the two positions." She has just said "I don't know" (L235) and "You weren't. Not quite." (L213), and L243 doubles the claimed certainty. From L195 to L243 he asks and she answers, one line each, every time. Action beats break it up, but it is still a question-and-answer rally.
- Fix: Keep "You mistake the body for the being" if you want it (it is his theology) but leave it unanswered: her hands are full with a slipping strap. His next line, "How long?", then shows the shift that L231 describes. Cut L241–L243, or let "You expect obedience." go unanswered and move straight to the creaking buckle at L245. Hedge L239 so it sounds like what she learned from her stepsister, not a law of his body. Illustrative only: "With her it eased once she stopped fighting it. Lie still."

### D4 · Minor · Narration explains lines that already show it; stock beats around his lines

- Where: L205 ("How?"), L227 (tag), L255 (aftereffects), L279 and L283 (final exchange)
- Text:
> His mouth opened, but no sound came until he tried the hand again.
> It was not a question.
> The question was quieter than his other questions and less complete.
> His lips parted, then closed.
- Problem: The narration tells the reader how to take lines whose form already shows it. "It was not a question." follows a line with no question mark. The note on "There are many of me?" explains a change the question shows by itself, and "less complete" is unclear, since the question is complete. The final exchange ends on a stock beat, and "said through his teeth" (L227) and the mouth-opens-no-sound beat (L205) are stock too.
- Fix: Cut "It was not a question." Cut L279, or replace it with something a carer would notice (his breathing, his hand). Replace L283 with a small physical response specific to him, or nothing. Don't turn it into a closing emblem. If D2 gives his outrage words, cut L211 as well.

## Prose findings

### P1 · Major · Climax staging does not hold together

- Where: L139–L175, recovery chamber, from the gate wheel to the feint and the throw
- Text:
> I reached it and put both hands on the wheel.
> I let go of the gate wheel.
> He stepped out of the basin and came to stand over me.
> I pulled the release pin from the nearest support.
> I rose under it, using my legs and his forward motion to drive him past me. He struck the basin rail.
- Problem: When she kneels, she is at the gate on the far wall (L139, L141, L165). The four supports lean over the basin in the centre of the room (L139), and nothing moves her. He walks from the basin to stand over her. A support should then be out of reach. And driving him "past me" along his line of travel would send him toward the wall, not into the basin rail behind him. The whole climax (support arm, rear support, second sling, floor ring) depends on this geometry, and the story has trained the reader to track positions closely.
- Fix: Add a clause or two before she kneels. For example, she moves from the gate to the basin rim beside a support as she says "All right", or kneels where he has to come around the basin to reach her. The support is then in reach, and his forward momentum carries him into the rail. Letting her choose the spot also shows the feint was planned. Change nothing else in the sequence.

### P2 · Minor · Escape route is default drainage infrastructure

- Where: L139, L141, L165, L261, L273, L285, recovery chamber and exit
- Text:
> fed by a pipe that dripped cloudy water from the rock
> a maintenance gate stood above a drainage channel
> filled it at the basin pipe
> The gate lifted six inches, then nine
- Problem: `universe/style-guide.md` "No default water infrastructure" (CANON) applies to authorized revisions. The prompt asks for no drainage or water supply, yet the way out is a drainage gate with a wheel and the basin is fed by a pipe. Separately, a nine-inch gap is too low for an adult to "slide under" (L285).
- Fix: Rebuild the gate as a non-hydraulic exit, such as a rusted outer shutter or hatch onto the slope. Keep the stuck wheel, the shears forced through the spokes, the rain beyond and the drop to the lower slope. Let it open far enough to crawl through. Have the basin fill from a natural seep in the rock instead of a pipe (and change "basin pipe" at L261). The mineral pool itself is ordinary water and can stay: he drinks it for the salts.

### P3 · Minor · "Not X. Y." corrections as a narration habit

- Where: L15, L39, L93, L145, L183
- Text:
> Not certainty. I had made that mistake before.
> Not wrong for him. Wrong for my hands.
> Not a crossing. Two crooked lines sharing the middle.
> not around a joint but across the two slings
- Problem: The narration often defines something by denying it and then correcting itself, which mirrors the correction rhythm of the dialogue. L93 and L145 are strong and should stay. At L39, "that mistake" is never explained. L183 is plain stage direction and doesn't need the reversal.
- Fix: Keep L93 and L145. Rewrite L39 so the reader knows what the mistake was, or cut the sentence. Rewrite L183 as a plain positive statement. Leave L15 unless it now feels crowded.

### P4 · Minor · Thesis line before his choice

- Where: L247, he weighs whether to fight on
- Text:
> Neither choice resembled worship.
- Problem: This spells out the thematic point just before his choice demonstrates it. The paragraph already makes his options concrete.
- Fix: Cut the sentence. The next paragraph (his hand closing, opening, the shoulder sinking) carries it.

### P5 · Minor · Left/right bookkeeping in the mechanism passages

- Where: L121 (landing fight), L145 (basin), L183 and L191 (bind)
- Text:
> His right shoulder tightened after the hip had already taken the force, while his right knee corrected into the pull beneath his ribs.
> The fading injury at his right knee called the line beneath his ribs a beat later and turned his trunk against it.
- Problem: The mechanism is told through left/right tracking in four dense passages. Few readers can hold it, and the outline itself warned against an anatomy lecture. The image that works is L145, his two crooked lines against the stepsister's clean crossing.
- Fix: Let L145 be the one clear statement of his two lines. Trim L121 and L183 to what she sees and does (which part moves late, which sling she sets against which), and drop side labels that don't change the action. Keep it fictional and non-instructional.

### P6 · Minor · The last sentence's "it" first points at the strap

- Where: L289, the goat track
- Text:
> I shortened the satchel strap. Before the rain loosened it, I put my full weight onto the first slab and started up.
- Problem: "It" first reads as the strap that was just mentioned. The meaning is the slab or the shale. The confusion falls on the story's last beat.
- Fix: Name what the rain would loosen (the shale, the slab), or reorder the sentences so that "it" follows its noun.

## Continuity and prompt findings

### C1 · Minor · Why the outward doors stay shut is never given

- Where: L17 (opening), L51 (his terms), L273 (after the bind)
- Text:
> The doors were still barred.
> The outward doors still would not open.
- Problem: What kept her from the doors was him ("There is no door for you until you acknowledge…"), and a bar can be lifted from inside. With him strapped in downstairs, the story gives no reason the doors stay shut, so the trip back to the chamber and the gate looks arbitrary. (The prompt-fidelity issue for this section is covered in D2.)
- Fix: Give a cause in one clause, for example a lock whose key she won't go back to search him for, or swollen timber. Or let the doors open onto the flooded road so that the lower exit is the only way.

### C2 · Minor · Her coat is left behind and never mentioned again

- Where: L137 (landing), L269–L289 (retrieval and exit)
- Text:
> I dropped, left my coat in his hand, and ran through the low doorway at the end of the landing.
> I recovered the vials, the shears, and what dry wrapping remained.
- Problem: She loses her coat on the landing, passes that spot twice, and then climbs an exposed ridge in a storm without it, and the story never says so.
- Fix: One clause: she picks it up from the landing on her way up, or notes that she left it.

## Preserve

- The opening hook and the first exchange, which set up the whole conflict in three lines:
> The man I had stabbed stopped being dead before I reached my satchel.
> "You put your hand on my neck."
> "I showed you the posture."
- The flour-bin memory and the cup memory, including "You're laughing while you get it.", "Water. No, broth. Is there any salt left?" and the release-buckle lesson that pays off at L261.
- "I witnessed you get very hot." Her best deflation, grounded in heat the reader has already seen.
- The withheld answer that buys her two steps:
> I said nothing. He wanted an answer more than he wanted me caught. That bought me two steps.
- The unspoken reply to "What do you see?":
> He expected an office, a domain, a glorious name. I saw one pupil wider than the other and salt dried white below his ear.
- The narrator's doubt about her own inference:
> Or I was frightened and inventing patterns because patterns were easier than death.
- The feint ("Last opportunity" / "All right." / kneeling), and "Release me." / "When I'm outside."
- The water exchange and the final exchange, both of which leave his godhood open:
> "It's water."
> "Many who mend. I don't know if any of them are you."
- The ending that stops on the climb, with no statement of what it meant.

## Suggested edit order

1. D2: rebuild the decisive prompt exchange first, because it decides where the story's one real topper goes.
2. D3: clear the thesis, the topper and the correction out of the climax so that D2 lands, and hedge L239.
3. D1: cut the L53 and L83 quips and leave his stair line unanswered, so the kept put-downs are few.
4. P1 with P2: fix the climax geometry and rebuild the exit gate in one pass, since they touch the same paragraphs.
5. D4, P3–P6, C1–C2: polish: the explanatory narration, stock beats, reversals, the anatomy bookkeeping, the last-sentence referent, the doors and the coat.
