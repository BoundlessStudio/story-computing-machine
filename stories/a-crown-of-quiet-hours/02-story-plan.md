# Story plan

## Premise and promise

When a meticulous water auditor discovers that her worshipped royal dynasty is
an office rotated among twelve exhausted dragons, she must expose the lie and
save a lamp-filled capital from the very fire their stolen cooling water has
made possible.

The story promises the prompt's comic revelation early, then makes the dragons'
sleepy self-interest, genuine public good, and political deception collide in
one civic emergency. Its central dramatic question is: **Can Istra make the
peace belong to its people without burning down the state that gave it to
them?**

## Story controls

- POV and tense: First-person past, narrated by Istra Noll.
- Tone and genre: Wry, humane political fantasy; dry draconic comedy under
  immediate civic pressure; hopeful but not morally weightless.
- Target: About 3,400 words within the 2,500–4,000-word contract.
- Rating: Teen / PG-13. Smoke danger, remembered war deaths, religious
  pressure, and political deception remain non-graphic.
- Scope: One capital, one night of crisis, a short constitutional aftermath.
  No named wider-universe era or location.

## Character engine

- **Istra Noll**, first-person protagonist and assistant auditor of municipal
  waters, wants the Founding Vigil declared safe so she can win a permanent
  post and prove that exact records protect ordinary lives. Her mother survived
  a tenement fire because a crown-built cistern worked, so Istra equates good
  outcomes with trustworthy rulers. Missing water puts her career, thousands
  of vigil-goers, and that faith at stake. Her blind spot is believing that
  competent benevolence from above can substitute for public knowledge and
  shared authority. She moves from loyal verifier, to coerced keeper of a
  secret, to a citizen willing to spend the Crown's peace in order to make that
  peace accountable.
- **Queen Orvessa IV** is the current human regnal identity worn by **Kharost**,
  one of twelve dragons who have rotated through forty-seven apparent monarchs
  and quiet court roles. Kharost wants the final preparations completed so all
  twelve can enter several centuries of uninterrupted sleep. Kharost is patient,
  funny, physically formidable, and sincerely proud of stopped wars and working
  cisterns, but has begun treating living citizens as variables in a plan whose
  average outcomes are good. The current wearer and the persona are one public
  presentation, not a duplicated mind; earlier wearers of other regnal
  identities were distinct dragons using distinct masks.
- **Vicar Estrel**, officiant of the Founding Vigil, worships the fictional
  **Crowned Ancestors**—the supposedly dead monarchs of the benevolent
  dynasty—not the living queen. He resists canceling the ceremony because he
  fears panic and sincerely believes the ritual remembers deliverance from
  war. When the fire and revelation break his inherited theology, he turns its
  call-and-response into bucket-line coordination. He demonstrates that human
  faith can become human action rather than obedience.
- **The other eleven dragons** are present as distinct, unnamed beings with
  different colors, sizes, sleep habits, and terse opinions. They are not a
  chorus of omniscient agreement, but the short-story cast does not assign them
  character-facing names or aliases.

## Causal arc

- **Beginning state:** On Founding Day, Istra expects a routine inspection to
  validate the capital's famous fire system and secure her appointment. The
  city prepares thousands of votive lamps for its revered dead monarchs.
- **Inciting disruption:** The public cistern ledger shows a vast overnight
  loss hidden behind a royal seal. Istra refuses to certify the water and traces
  the active palace sluice instead of obeying a request to correct the number.
- **First turn:** Beneath the palace she finds the missing water cooling a
  cavern occupied by twelve dragons. Queen Orvessa sheds her human guise and
  identifies herself as Kharost. The monarchy's entire dynastic history is a
  long peace-building scheme designed to earn undisturbed sleep.
- **Escalation:** Kharost shows credible records of wars prevented, granaries
  protected, and the planned transfer to a rigid human rulebook. The good is
  real. So is the immediate danger: the cooling basin has emptied the festival
  cisterns, and the dragons want only one more night of secrecy. Istra realizes
  their long averages have made tonight's citizens expendable.
- **Crisis:** Estrel refuses to cancel the Vigil on an assistant auditor's
  unsupported word. A wind-loosened lamp ignites an awning; the nearest hydrant
  coughs air. Istra can preserve the secret and hope the dragons contain the
  fire, or ring the forbidden Flood Bell and drain their new sleeping basin,
  exposing the theft and probably the dragons.
- **Climax:** Istra rings the bell and opens the palace sluice. Her action
  destroys the prepared sleep conditions but returns water to the civic mains.
  Estrel converts the Vigil response into instructions, and citizens form
  lines, pull down fuel, and soak roofs. Pressed by Istra to issue an honest
  request “in your own face,” Kharost and the eleven emerge undisguised. They
  carry burning beams and shield the human lines with scale, materially helping
  at the cost of exposure and sleep but not solving the fire alone.
- **Resolution:** At a public inquiry, Istra rejects both restoration of the lie
  and vengeful destruction of the peace. The resulting Charter of Waking Hours
  transfers governing authority, water, tax, and records to human councils;
  ends state worship of the dynasty; audits all royal resources; and recognizes
  the dragons as non-ruling residents owed no sacrifice. After humans build a
  separate, publicly accounted cooling system, all twelve finally sleep.
- **Meaningful change:** Istra begins by asking whether rulers made the system
  safe. She ends responsible, with other citizens, for keeping it safe.

## Setup and payoff

- The opening ledger maxim—“water counted is fire denied”—pays off when Istra's
  uncensored figures justify the emergency sluice order and later the audit
  clause.
- Thousands of devotional lamps make the monarchy's worship visible, create the
  immediate hazard, and become useful hand-lights along the bucket line once
  Estrel repurposes the ceremony.
- The Flood Bell is introduced as an obsolete royal-emergency relic that no
  clerk may ring; Istra's decision to ring it is both the physical solution and
  her break with permission-based virtue.
- Kharost complains that bells are the enemy of meaningful sleep. The climax
  forces Istra to wake every dragon with the loudest bell in the city; the
  aftermath replaces imposed quiet with engineered soundproofing.
- The dragons' meticulous Book of Quiet initially looks like a responsible
  succession plan. Its failure to include a mechanism for public correction
  makes their political blind spot concrete and motivates the charter.
- Istra's mother's survival proves that the old peace was real. Istra's own
  action proves that gratitude does not require permanent submission.

## Canon constraints

- Keep the kingdom an unnamed local polity in an unspecified era of the one
  shared physical world; do not describe another plane or universal empire.
  (`universe/premise.md` — `The deep-time shared world`; `Knowledge and
  connection`)
- Treat disguise, scale, fire resistance, and sleep needs as narrow facts about
  these twelve living dragons. The undercroft's stone, sluices, and written
  plans do not cast magic or act independently. (`universe/rules.md` — `System
  categories`; `Living agency and magic`)
- The Crowned Ancestors receive worship but are discontinued masks with no
  present authority or living power. Queen Orvessa has current legal authority
  but is doctrinally excluded from worship while living. Kharost has draconic
  capabilities but is not known or worshipped under that identity. The ending
  moves legal authority to human institutions and ends state-sponsored ancestor
  worship. Do not casually claim that any one of these identities combines
  worship, power, and authority. (`universe/rules.md` — `Divinity`)
- Istra's discovery rests on an anomalous ledger, an active conduit, physical
  entry into the cavern, royal records, and direct transformation—not on an
  inconsistency automatically proving the truth. (`universe/rules.md` —
  `Records, falsehood, and historical traces`)
- Preserve continuity between each dragon and its assumed guise; the personas
  are roles, not copied minds or branches. Names have no inherent binding power.
  (`universe/rules.md` — `Memory, transformation, copies, and branches`;
  `Names and identity binding`)
- Keep the peace's benefits, the deception's political harm, the citizens'
  choices, and the fire's consequences simultaneously real.
  (`universe/style-guide.md` — `Personhood and moral consequence`)

## Proposed inventions

- One unnamed capital and kingdom with a bicentennial-scale peace, public
  cistern network, palace sluices, municipal water auditors, a Founding Vigil,
  votive lamps, Flood Bell, and cult of the Crowned Ancestors.
- Twelve long-lived dragons who can assume one human guise at a time through a
  living transformation, resist ordinary fire, dislike vibration, and require
  a cool, quiet environment for multi-century sleep. These are facts about this
  group only.
- The dragons created forty-seven royal personas and rotated through monarch and
  court roles to stop wars, standardize fire prevention, and make a state able
  to persist while they all slept.
- The Book of Quiet, a non-magical administrative plan compiled by the dragons.
- The fire at the Founding Vigil and the public revelation of the dragons.
- The Charter of Waking Hours: a local settlement that transfers government to
  human councils, ends official dynastic worship, audits water and royal
  resources, and permits the non-ruling dragons to sleep in separately supplied
  public works.

## Failure modes to watch

- Do not delay the dragon reveal beyond Scene 2 or treat it as a closing twist.
- Do not make the dragons secretly altruistic saints or reduce two centuries of
  peace to a cynical sham. Their motive is selfish, their methods are mixed, and
  their good outcomes are real.
- Do not let Kharost breathe convenient magic over the fire. Istra's sluice
  choice, Estrel's coordination, working water, and many citizens must do most
  of the saving; dragon aid costs secrecy and sleep.
- Keep twelve dragons physically and grammatically distinct. Kharost is one of
  twelve, Queen Orvessa is Kharost's present role, and the Crowned Ancestors are
  discontinued public masks—not names for a hive mind.
- Avoid theology exposition. Establish the worship/power/authority separation
  through ritual language, Kharost's correction, and the charter.
- Keep Estrel neither a villain nor a miraculous convert. His expertise in the
  Vigil makes the bucket-line adaptation plausible.
- Avoid a procedural aftermath longer than the emotional landing. The charter
  needs a few vivid clauses and consequences, followed by the sleep payoff.
- Do not imply the charter, dragon biology, or state is a universal rule or
  already-established canon.
