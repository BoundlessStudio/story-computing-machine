# Canon brief

## Hard constraints

- If the kingdom is placed in the established main continuity, it belongs to
  the one physical world that persists across the shared universe's deep time.
  Earth, Ravel, and the Glass-Sea world cannot be treated as separate planets
  or planes. An unspecified era and region of that world is safe; a separate
  fantasy planet or plane would be a new cosmological addition rather than an
  established fact. (`universe/premise.md` — `## The deep-time shared world`;
  `universe/locations.md` — `## The world across eras`)
- The monarchy cannot be presented as a universal secret government regulating
  magic or history across every era. Its reach should remain local to its state
  unless a later, explicitly approved canon addition establishes a wider
  institution. (`universe/premise.md` — `## Knowledge and connection`)
- Magic, technology, and divinity are cultural descriptions of underlying
  systems, not separate objective substances. If the dragons use magical
  disguise, fire, sleep protections, or another power, the story should treat
  those as one local mechanism rather than explain all dragons, all magic, or
  all divine phenomena. (`universe/rules.md` — `## System categories`;
  `universe/glossary.md` — `## Magic, technology, and divinity`)
- Only life can use magic. Living dragons may therefore be the agents behind a
  disguise or protective system; any nonliving crown, palace, seal, archive, or
  machine may only store, focus, transmit, or shape supplied magic unless the
  story establishes that it is alive. (`universe/rules.md` —
  `## Living agency and magic`)
- Godhood is the continuing conjunction of worship, power, and authority.
  Because the prompt requires worshipped rulers who possess draconic power,
  the story must identify the actual object of worship and bearer of authority
  carefully. If one being or identity satisfies all three conditions, canon's
  taxonomy treats it as a god; belief alone does not rewrite a dragon's nature,
  and losing all worship ends godhood. (`universe/rules.md` —
  `## Divinity`)
- The monarchy's forged genealogy, pageantry, official history, and civic
  religion may operate as propaganda or dogma, but contradiction is not
  automatically proof. The discovery and political response need causally
  coherent evidence, witnesses, or material context. (`universe/premise.md` —
  `## Memory, myth, and recoverable truth`; `universe/rules.md` —
  `## Records, falsehood, and historical traces`)
- Names and royal titles are not universally magical. A true-name effect, a
  crown that binds a persona, or a regnal name with supernatural force would
  require an explicit local mechanism and must not be generalized.
  (`universe/rules.md` — `## Names and identity binding`)
- The story must preserve the moral reality of the people affected by the
  deception. A draconic or magical explanation cannot erase human agency,
  consent, grief, relationships, or the political consequences of the lie.
  Violence and coercion must remain non-graphic and consequence-focused.
  (`universe/style-guide.md` — `## Personhood and moral consequence`;
  `universe/style-guide.md` — `## Audience and content boundaries`)

## Useful established context

- Canon explicitly allows later cultures to mistake rulers for gods and
  survival measures for sacred law. That supports a civil religion accreting
  around a peace-building monarchy without establishing the dragons,
  dynasty, or religion in advance. (`universe/premise.md` —
  `## Memory, myth, and recoverable truth`)
- Recovering the truth does not automatically produce justice. Provenance,
  competing evidence, material conditions, and witnesses remain important,
  which supports a climax about what institutions and citizens do with the
  revelation rather than treating exposure as a complete solution.
  (`universe/premise.md` — `## Memory, myth, and recoverable truth`)
- Cultures and individuals may have radically incomplete or incorrect models
  of reality. Neither the civil servant nor the public needs knowledge of the
  wider cosmology, and the dragons need not understand every consequence of
  their own long rule. (`universe/premise.md` —
  `## Knowledge and connection`)
- Civilizational collapse recurs but is neither inevitable nor synchronized.
  An imminent civic fire, coup, riot, war, or infrastructure failure can be a
  contingent local danger rather than evidence of a universal cycle.
  (`universe/premise.md` — `## The deep-time shared world`)
- The shared universe permits an independent entry point, a wry political
  fantasy tone, and a story that never advertises its place in the larger
  cosmology. (`universe/style-guide.md` —
  `## Tonal range, scale, and connection`)

## Conflicts or ambiguity

- No conflict was found among relevant `LOCKED` or `CANON` sources, and
  `stories/INDEX.md` currently marks every listed story canon `no`; therefore no
  finalized story prose supplies additional canon for this brief.
- The prompt's phrase “come to worship” intersects the canon definition of
  godhood. It is unclear whether worship is directed toward an abstract Crown,
  each apparent monarch, the supposed dynasty, or the hidden collective; it is
  also unclear which of those identities actually holds power and authority.
  This is an omission, not a contradiction, but the story should not casually
  assign all three qualities to one identity and then deny the resulting
  godhood. (`universe/rules.md` — `## Divinity`)
- Canon offers no general rule for the continuity of transformed or assumed
  identities. A dragon wearing several royal bodies, twelve dragons rotating
  through one persona, and a magically projected monarch could have different
  identity consequences. The chosen mechanism must be locally coherent and
  preserve personhood; canon does not select one model.
  (`universe/rules.md` —
  `## Memory, transformation, copies, and branches`)
- The story's era is unspecified. Canon fixes the modern-like era before the
  Glass-Sea era and leaves Ravel's placement unresolved, but nothing requires
  this kingdom to be any of those named settings. This is an omission, not a
  chronology conflict. (`universe/timeline.md` —
  `## Established era order`)

## Unknowns

- Dragons are not established in authoritative canon. Beyond the prompt's
  required group of roughly twelve disguised rulers, their species population,
  origin, longevity, reproduction, fire, shapeshifting, sensory limits,
  politics, vulnerabilities, and sleep biology are all `Unknown`.
- The magical or biological mechanism of human disguise is `Unknown`, including
  whether clothing changes, whether injury reveals the form, whether a dragon
  can maintain a disguise while asleep, and whether one apparent body can be
  shared or copied.
- The threshold and identity accounting for worship, power, and authority are
  `Unknown` at this story's scale: canon does not say whether reverence for an
  office attaches to its occupant, whether a collective can hold godhood, or
  how worship is divided among rotating personas.
- The kingdom's name, geography, era, neighbors, technology, magical literacy,
  laws, succession rules, bureaucracy, civil religion, fire history, and public
  access to records are `Unknown`.
- The length and quality of the peace, the disasters that preceded it, the
  dragons' exact role in producing it, and the human costs of royal secrecy are
  `Unknown`.
- The cause, scale, and likely consequences of the destabilizing event and
  imminent fire are `Unknown`.
- Whether any ordinary human, priest, dissident, archivist, neighboring state,
  or earlier ruler has discovered or preserved evidence of the dragons is
  `Unknown`.
- The kingdom's relationship, if any, to Earth, Ravel, the Glass-Sea era, the
  Glass Gate, known deities, or any production-story character is `Unknown`.
  Resemblance, imagery, names, or folklore would not establish such a link.
  (`universe/rules.md` — `## Artifacts, motifs, and folklore`)

## Safe invention space

- Invent one local kingdom or city-state, its capital, neighboring pressures,
  civic offices, archives, fire-prevention systems, ceremonies, and evolving
  royal cult without placing it in a named established era.
- Invent roughly twelve individual dragons, their story-facing forms, internal
  disagreements, rotation schedule, and finite competencies. Keep their
  species history and political reach no broader than the story needs.
- Establish one narrow disguise mechanism and one narrow account of draconic
  sleep, fire, and longevity, with observable limits and failure modes. Phrase
  these as facts about this group or lineage, not universal dragon biology.
- Decide locally who or what receives worship and holds authority. The safest
  options are to distinguish clearly among the civil institution, royal
  personas, and hidden dragons, or to accept and dramatize the canon
  consequences if one identity satisfies worship, power, and authority.
- Invent the civil servant's office, evidence trail, allies, adversaries, and
  immediate fire crisis. Institutional records, testimony, physical traces,
  and partial archival survival fit established themes without needing a
  supernatural truth detector.
- Invent a local constitutional settlement, disclosure process, oversight
  arrangement, fire compact, or sleep-sharing bargain. It may solve this
  state's crisis without becoming a universal political or metaphysical rule.
- Keep any connection to the wider universe unstated. If a named era, gate,
  deity, artifact, or recurring institution is introduced later, it requires
  a separate source-backed continuity check.

## Name constraints

- The prompt currently contains no character-facing personal name or alias, so
  it creates no exact registry collision. “Crown,” “monarchy,” “kingdom,”
  “dragons,” and ordinary uses of royal or civil-service offices are common
  nouns unless the prose turns one into an identity label.
- Avoid the existing royal and divine identities `King Othran`, `Othran`,
  `Queen Aurelia`, `Aurelia`, `Sun Queen`, `Burning Queen`, `the Goddess`,
  `Mother`, `Solstice Evening Bell`, `Solstice`, `Evening Bell`,
  `First Monarch`, `the Goddess of Time`, `Time`, `the Hollow Regent`, and
  `Regent` unless reuse is deliberate, meaningful, and documented.
  (`stories/NAMES.md` — `## Character name registry`)
- Avoid title-like reserved forms if used as character labels, including
  `Captain`, `Court Ghost`, `Keeper Below`, `Daughter`, and
  `Mother-fragment`. Ordinary descriptive uses remain possible, but a title
  functioning as a name must be checked and registered.
- Do not add to the unresolved collision sets `Lena`, `Mara`, `Nisha`, `Pell`,
  or `Voss`. The registry explicitly requires those overlaps to be resolved,
  not multiplied. (`stories/NAMES.md` —
  `## Known unresolved legacy collisions`)
- With a likely cast of twelve dragons plus a human bureaucracy, avoid new
  names readily confused with occupied clusters such as `Ilya` / `Irix` /
  `Ivo`, `Talvi` / `Tavra` / `Tamsin`, and the Bellweather family / `Evening
  Bell`. Exact forms, close spellings, reversals, titles, epithets, and aliases
  all require review.
- Every story-facing draconic name, human guise, regnal identity, nickname,
  epithet, office used as a name, named animal, and person-like construct must
  be checked. If several dragons share one royal identity, or one dragon uses
  several identities, the plan and registry must state which forms belong to
  the same or distinct persons, why the reuse matters, and how readers can
  distinguish them.
- `stories/NAMES.md` is production memory, not canon. The primary coordinator,
  not the canon researcher, must register the planned names and run the scoped
  validation before drafting.

## Required checks after drafting

- If the story places the kingdom in established main continuity, confirm it
  remains a local part of the one shared world and is not described as a
  separate plane, universal empire, or regulator of all magic and history.
- Identify every instance of worship, power, and political/religious authority;
  verify that the text either applies the canon godhood taxonomy consistently
  or keeps those qualities attached to distinguishable identities.
- Verify that belief does not magically create, reshape, empower, or humanize
  the dragons unless a narrow local mechanism is explicitly established; in
  particular, worship alone cannot rewrite their nature.
- Check the number and continuity of dragons, their disguises, the rotation of
  royal and court roles, who knows each identity, and who can act or appear
  while another dragon sleeps. Ensure the climax obeys every established limit.
- Check that any magical disguise or fire-control device ultimately depends on
  living agency unless the supposed device is explicitly established as alive.
- Trace the civil servant's discovery through credible records, testimony, or
  material evidence. Do not treat an inconsistency, sacred story, dream,
  prediction, or repeated image as self-proving.
- Verify that the immediate fire danger has a contingent cause and that the
  resolution follows from character and institutional action, not an
  effortless new power or an asserted law of inevitable collapse.
- Confirm the revelation preserves human moral and political agency: the peace
  remains a real good, the deception remains a real wrong, and the ending
  addresses accountability rather than erasing consequences with draconic
  biology or long perspective.
- Search the final prose for every character-facing name and alias, reconcile
  them with `stories/NAMES.md`, document any intentional shared persona, and
  rerun the story-scoped name validator.
- Record every reusable new fact about the kingdom, dragons, disguise system,
  religion, chronology, or political settlement in `06-canon-delta.md`; do not
  treat that proposed delta as canon without explicit promotion.
- Confirm violence, religious coercion, death, and fire remain within the
  prompt's Teen / PG-13 framing and the universe's non-graphic content ceiling.
