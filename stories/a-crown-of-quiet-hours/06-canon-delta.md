# Proposed canon delta

This file proposes reusable shared-universe facts. It is not canon until the
user explicitly approves promotion and the facts are added to `universe/`.

All facts below are evidenced by `stories/a-crown-of-quiet-hours/05-story.md`
and remain local to its unnamed kingdom, capital, era, and dragon collective.
They must not be generalized to every dragon, monarchy, religion, magical
transformation, or period of the shared world. This delta records proposals
only; it performs no promotion.

## New characters or character facts

- **Istra Noll** is a human municipal-water civil servant. During the final
  Founding Vigil of the hidden monarchy, she is an assistant auditor seeking a
  permanent appointment. She refuses to certify a western cistern that is
  nine hundred thousand gallons short, follows the diverted water, discovers
  the dragons beneath the palace, recovers her original sounding sheets and
  the royal-sealed municipal ledger, restores emergency water during the fire,
  and becomes a principal witness at the public inquiry. Seven months later,
  she holds a permanent inspection post under the new public-accountability
  system. Her mother, who remains unnamed, survived an earlier tenement fire
  because a Crown-built cistern worked.
- **Kharost / Queen Orvessa IV** is one continuing individual, not two minds,
  a copy, or a branch. Kharost is a red-gold dragon and one of the twelve
  hidden rulers; Queen Orvessa IV is the current human regnal office and public
  mask that Kharost has worn for nineteen years. Kharost is proud of the real
  peace the collective built but treats a low calculated risk to present
  citizens as acceptable for the sake of the dragons' sleep. During the fire,
  Istra challenges Kharost to ask for help without the royal mask; Kharost
  chooses to transform and publicly confesses the stolen water, selfish
  motive, and concealed rule. She catches a falling beam, suffers a newly
  split shoulder scale, obeys the water line, speaks during the public inquiry,
  loses the throne with the other dragons, and later enters the publicly
  governed sleep works.
- **Vicar Estrel** is the human officiant of the Founding Vigil. He initially
  refuses to halt a crowded state ceremony on evidence that does not visibly
  overcome the royal seal, but when the fire starts he converts the Vigil's
  call-and-response into evacuation, fuel-clearing, and bucket-line
  instructions. His response preserves human agency and does not depend on a
  supernatural conversion or power.
- **The Crowned Ancestors** are a personified collective of supposedly dead
  monarchs worshipped through the state's civic religion. They are actually
  forty-seven discontinued royal masks created and performed by the twelve
  dragons, not living beings, a magical collective, Kharost's alias, or a
  prayer-hearing power. They hold no present office. The living monarch is
  excluded from worship while in office.
- **The other eleven dragons** are distinct, unnamed individuals. Together
  with Kharost they make an exact total of twelve. Story-visible members
  include an ivory dragon that sleeps with a damp eye cloth, a green dragon
  particular about its river-stone bed, two copper-colored dragons that argue
  about dripping water, a narrow blue dragon, and a large black dragon with an
  old shoulder scar that uses wool earplugs. These descriptions are not names
  or aliases. Kharost's new shoulder injury is separate from the black
  dragon's old scar.

## New locations

- An **unnamed local kingdom and capital**, in an unspecified era of the shared
  physical world, experience a 218-year peace under the hidden draconic
  monarchy. No connection is established to Earth, Ravel, the Glass-Sea era,
  the Glass Gate, any named deity, or another story location.
- The capital contains a palace square and western arcade, a public cistern and
  hydrant network, ceremonial fountains, municipal and royal emergency
  sluices, an obsolete Flood Bell, royal archives, and a palace undercroft
  cavern. The dragons secretly construct a cooled sleeping basin in that
  cavern by diverting municipal water.
- Seven months after the Founding Day fire, human engineers complete
  **separate cooling works beyond the inhabited wards**. The works have their
  own watershed, public ledger, twelve stone beds, soundproof walls, and
  externally accessible shutoff gates tested by human inspectors.

## New factions or cultural facts

- The kingdom's apparent dynasty is a hidden collective of exactly twelve
  dragons. Across 218 years they rotate through forty-seven royal masks and
  supporting public roles, including monarchs, heirs, advisers, remote
  relatives, and on occasion a disagreeable foreign envoy. Different masks
  and offices can be performed by different dragons; Queen Orvessa IV is
  specifically Kharost's final regnal mask. The masks are social and bodily
  roles, not separate persons.
- The collective builds a real, locally bounded peace while pursuing a selfish
  but sincere objective: several quiet, cool centuries in which the members
  can sleep without fires or warfare. Its records credit the hidden monarchy
  with preventing six wars and three succession crises, opening grain
  reserves, building cisterns and sewers, enforcing fire precautions, and
  rejecting a dangerously undersized cistern. The resulting good does not
  erase the lack of political consent, secret resource use, manipulated
  genealogy, or civic worship.
- The **municipal water office** uses sounding rods, ledgers, inspections, and
  the maxim “Water counted is fire denied.” Royal seals can authorize access
  and establish apparent custody, but the story's settlement rejects any seal
  as a substitute for verified quantities or correctable public judgment.
- The state religion develops from what the dragons intended as an annual
  commemorative speech. At the **Founding Vigil**, worshippers in white coats
  carry clay oil lamps for the Crowned Ancestors and arrange the lamps in
  forty-seven golden circles. The living monarch remains outside the prayer
  ritual: Queen Orvessa IV possesses current legal authority but receives no
  worship, the retired masks receive worship but possess neither present
  authority nor living power, and hidden Kharost has draconic capabilities but
  is not worshipped under that identity.
- After the revelation, the Charter of Waking Hours ends state worship of the
  Crowned Ancestors without banning private remembrance. The story does not
  establish that the Crowned Ancestors, Queen Orvessa IV, Kharost, or the
  twelve-dragon collective hold the continuing conjunction of worship, power,
  and authority required for godhood.
- The post-fire government places law, water, taxation, records, and
  appointments under elected human councils. The dragons become non-ruling
  residents protected by and bound to the same law, with no entitlement to a
  throne, sacrifice, or uncounted public resources.

## New rules, capabilities, or costs

- These twelve dragons can each assume a human public guise through a living
  bodily transformation. Kharost performs the change herself; no crown, name,
  seal, palace mechanism, archive, or other nonliving object acts as a magic
  user. Kharost remains the same person while appearing as Queen Orvessa IV.
  This is a local capability of the story's collective, not a rule for all
  dragons or transformations.
- Kharost emits substantial heat. Kharost and at least one other dragon
  demonstrate finite resistance to ordinary fire. Kharost can brown paper with
  a flare of anger and withstand a burning beam only briefly before recoiling
  and splitting a shoulder scale. Another dragon's wing membranes smoke and
  curl while shielding a bucket line. The observed resistance does not let the
  dragons extinguish the civic fire by themselves.
- These twelve require cool stone, stable water, minimal vibration, and quiet
  for the multi-century sleep they seek. Bells, dripping water, unsuitable
  bedding, warfare, and neighborhood fires can disturb their sleep. Their
  nineteen-month preparation of the secret basin is destroyed when Istra
  drains it into the civic mains. These needs and preferences must not be
  generalized as universal dragon biology.
- **The Book of Quiet** is the collective's detailed, non-magical
  administrative plan for human officers to apply while the dragons sleep. It
  anticipates drought, population growth, crop failure, corruption, tax bands,
  reservoir rules, appointments, grain purchases, fire inspections, and many
  disputes, but it supplies no public mechanism for correcting a rule its
  authors believe cannot be wrong. Its failure is institutional, not magical.
- The Flood Bell, sluices, hydrants, ledgers, cooling basin, and later cooling
  works are mechanical, hydraulic, archival, or legal systems. They do not
  act independently and confer no new supernatural ability.

## Timeline events

- Over 218 years before the story's central Founding Day, the twelve dragons
  use forty-seven regnal masks and quieter court roles to prevent wars, govern
  the kingdom, build fire-suppression infrastructure, and prepare a human-run
  state stable enough for their shared sleep.
- During the final nineteen months of that rule, the collective prepares a
  secret sleeping basin beneath the palace. On Founding Day it diverts nine
  hundred thousand gallons from the western cistern to bring the basin to
  sleeping temperature, concealing the adjustment behind the royal seal while
  forty thousand oil lamps are prepared for the Vigil.
- A dry gust breaks a prayer-streamer line and carries burning canvas from six
  lamps to the western arcade. The nearest fountain and hydrant are initially
  dry because of the diversion. Istra uses the sealed municipal ledger to
  reach the emergency works, has a guard break the chain on the sluice wheel,
  returns the basin water to the civic mains, and rings the Flood Bell.
- Estrel coordinates the crowd through adapted ritual responses. Istra
  pressures Kharost to ask for help in her own face; Kharost chooses to
  transform, confess, and help. Kharost and the other eleven dragons emerge in
  their own bodies and provide costly physical aid, while citizens, water
  crews, bucket lines, and firebreak labor do the work that contains the fire.
  The western arcade loses its roof; thirty-one people are treated for smoke
  and burns; no one dies.
- A public inquiry considers the uncensored water figures, royal archives,
  saved lives, deception, resource diversion, and Book of Quiet. The Charter
  of Waking Hours is completed over six weeks and nine hundred amendments. It
  retires all forty-seven masks, ends the dragons' rule and state-sponsored
  dynastic worship, transfers governing functions to elected human councils,
  opens royal archives and accounts to audit, and establishes the dragons'
  non-ruling legal status.
- Seven months after the fire, the separate, publicly accounted cooling works
  are complete. All twelve dragons enter the works intending to sleep for two
  centuries while external gates and public ledgers leave control of the water
  with human institutions.

## New glossary terms or aliases

- **Book of Quiet** — The twelve dragons' non-magical administrative plan for
  governing through human officers during their sleep.
- **Charter of Waking Hours** — The local constitutional settlement that ends
  draconic rule and official dynastic worship, transfers public authority and
  resources to elected human councils, opens royal records and accounts, and
  defines the dragons as non-ruling residents.
- **Crowned Ancestors / the Crowned Ancestors** — The personified collective of
  forty-seven retired royal masks worshipped as supposedly dead monarchs; not
  an alias of Kharost or of the living dragon collective.
- **Flood Bell** — An obsolete capital emergency bell associated with siege,
  earthquake, or royal command and located beside the palace's
  emergency-water sluice controls.
- **Founding Vigil** — The state's annual lamp-lit commemoration and worship of
  the Crowned Ancestors.
- **“Water counted is fire denied”** — The municipal water office's
  professional maxim.

  masks, not an alias for Kharost, Queen Orvessa IV, or the twelve dragons.
- The other eleven dragons remain unnamed. Their color, body, bedding, and
  injury descriptions are common descriptors and do not create
  character-facing names or aliases.
- No named animal, companion, construct, or other person-like entity appears.
  `Book of Quiet`, `Charter of Waking Hours`, `Flood Bell`, `Founding Vigil`,
  `Crown`, and office titles are documents, institutions, events, objects, or
  ordinary roles rather than character aliases.
- There is **no deliberate unrelated name reuse**. The only intentional
  multi-form identity is Kharost and her Queen Orvessa IV office; the forms
  identify the same person and do not imply a crossover. No final name or alias
  uses an unresolved registry collision.

## Possible conflicts or retcons

- None identified. Dragons, this state, its masks, its worship, its sleep
  works, and the Charter occupy previously unspecified local invention space.
- The story leaves its era and regional placement unspecified and establishes
  no connection to a named era, portal, deity, artifact, institution,
  character, or archive record.
- The proposed worship structure remains compatible with the existing godhood
  taxonomy by keeping worship, present legal authority, and living draconic
  power attached to distinguishable identities and offices. The proposal does
  not retcon or redefine godhood.
- The local transformation and sleep facts do not establish universal rules
  for dragons, identity, magic, or biology.
