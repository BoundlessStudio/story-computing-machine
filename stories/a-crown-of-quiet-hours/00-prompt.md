# Prompt contract

## Verbatim writing prompt

> **[WP] The benevolent monarchy people have long since come to worship is really just a dozen or so dragons in disguise, hoping to set up a state that can keep everything not on fire for a few hundred years so that they can finally get some actual quality slumber.**

## Story controls

- Working title: A Crown of Quiet Hours
- Target length: 2,500–4,000 words
- POV: First person, from the perspective of a human civil servant close enough
  to the throne to discover its secret and far enough outside it to challenge
  the dragons' assumptions.
- Tense: Past.
- Tone and genre: Wry, humane political fantasy with mounting civic danger,
  dry draconic comedy, and an earned hopeful ending.
- Audience/content rating: Teen / PG-13. Non-graphic references to fire,
  historical war, death, and political or religious coercion are acceptable.
- Required elements:
  - The revered benevolent monarchy is genuinely a disguised group of roughly
    twelve dragons, not merely controlled by them.
  - Their central motive is practical and comic but sincere: create several
    centuries of low-fire stability so they can sleep properly.
  - The peace has produced real human good even though its founding motive and
    royal identity are concealed.
  - Discovery of the secret creates an immediate choice or crisis; the premise
    must drive the climax rather than appear only as a final twist.
  - The resolution must address both the legitimacy of peace built on a lie and
    the dragons' need for safe, uninterrupted sleep.
- Prohibited elements:
  - No ending that dismisses all benevolent rule as worthless solely because
    its rulers have selfish motives.
  - No flawless or omniscient dragons; their long view must create blind spots.
  - No graphic cruelty, sexual violence, or bleak nihilistic ending.
  - No contradiction of authoritative shared-universe canon and no canon
    promotion without explicit user approval.

## Assumptions

- The monarchy includes twelve dragons who rotate through apparently human
  royal identities and supporting court roles to simulate a dynasty.
- Popular worship is a long-evolved civic religion encouraged by pageantry and
  good outcomes, not supernatural mind control.
- The plot occurs when a destabilizing event makes another large fire likely
  and the monarchy's usual methods fail.
- The dragons' sleep biology, the state, and its faith will remain local,
  story-scale inventions unless separately promoted.
- The moral center is shared responsibility: the dragons' deception is a real
  wrong, while the peace it enabled and the lives it saved remain real goods.

## Completion tests

- The result is a complete short story, not a synopsis or chapter fragment.
- The central promise of the prompt is dramatized and resolved.
- A human protagonist discovers the monarchy's draconic nature early enough to
  make a consequential choice about it.
- The climax averts or transforms an imminent civic fire through character
  action, not coincidence or effortless draconic power.
- The ending establishes a credible new balance between human political agency,
  royal accountability, and the dragons' desire for several quiet centuries.
- The story passes canon and continuity review.
- Every character-facing name and alias is checked against `stories/NAMES.md`;
  any deliberate reuse has a documented narrative reason.
