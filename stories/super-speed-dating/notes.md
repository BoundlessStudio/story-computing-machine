## Voice trial 1

The bell rings before she has an enemy. Updraft stops with one heel still on the doormat. All the way from Vickery's she's been making herself say, in the voice she uses when somebody from the pavement has come in to ask about a display, *Just a little trick with gravity*. She can't get as far as gravity while that sound is in her. Then the man on the open stair presses his palm to the brass and calls down that he's only testing it. They've got a few minutes yet.

The bell projects off the landing high enough that she can see into its mouth from the door. She would turn off all the other lights if she had that bell in one of her windows. From here she can also see the faded red race line running to the boards directly beneath it. Two girls are crossing the floor with their boots knotted at the laces and the shorter one takes the line, looking straight ahead, swinging the skates so wide of her ankles that her friend has to walk on the other side.

At the registration desk she writes a U in the box for her choice of opponent. It's the good U from her calling cards, with the left edge pointed so it could scoop up the smaller letters. The man behind the desk gives her a clean slip and explains how the heroes rotate at the bell. She keeps the spoiled one under the new one all the way to her seat. The two pieces of paper slipping against each other are the only things she can hear of the whole room setting down its coats and taking water.

There is a teaspoon at her table for showing what she can do. The first man to arrive takes an interest in it, tilting it toward her with a finger to show he knows what to ask about. Updraft looks down and finds she can see the red stripe between the soles of his shoes and the maple floor.


## Voice trial 2

When the fellow at the desk saw that I had put my own name on the line for the match I wanted, he gave me a fresh form without a word. It was the tact of the thing that did me in. I'd been laughed at by a whole sidewalk once, when I sent a papier-mâché swan up into the light fixture at Vickery's, where I dressed the windows. I had put out the second swan and made a sign that said MIGRATION, and business went on at Vickery's. If the fellow had laughed I might have been able to do something with it.

From inside my coat I could get the tip of a nail into the U in UPDRAFT. The cards had been printed on Wednesday, though of course I'd ordered them as if there had been a previous batch and I was running out. The woman had talked me into a hundred. I thought of the people I might have to fight to get rid of a hundred cards, and then it occurred to me that if it went the way I hoped I could give the whole box to one person.

The Comet was still clearing away its last skating lesson. I could smell the wet coats before I got past the rental counter, where the attendant was spinning each wheel of a little pair of white boots and laying a finger on it to stop it. I could have stayed there a good while watching him.

All the heroes had to do the moving at Super Speed Dating. They put the villains at little tables on the rink. That way we could spend the interval watching somebody come toward us in full knowledge of what was waiting at the other end. This had always struck me as the chief inducement to a life of crime, though when the time came I picked up the spoon they had left on the table and found that it rattled against my glass. I put it down very flat.

The first man took his last step a foot above the boards. “I hear you get things off the ground,” he said, and sat down without doing any of the ordinary things you do with your knees.
