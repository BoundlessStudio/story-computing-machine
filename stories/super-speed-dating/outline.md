# Super Speed Dating — provisional outline

## Promised experience

The request puts a nervous first-timer with a power of their own at Super Speed Dating, where fledgling heroes and villains try out prospective rogues and nemeses. “Speed Dating” suggests the quick judgments, hopeful patter and mortification of a date, applied to the question of who would be delightful to fight. “Match with your power” suggests a payoff we can see happen between two people using their powers. The exclamation over a “legendary nemesis” makes room for extravagant play and a rivalry with a future. The request leaves the power, alignment, specific relationship, scale of action and outcome open.

## Takes considered

- **Play the last date straight.** A first-time villain wagers she can ring the rotation bell early. She and her last date have four minutes to discover which of them can make the other improvise.
- **Begin with the second fight.** A young hero guarding a civic prize recognizes a calling card from the date he turned down. The fight cuts back to the two wrong conclusions they drew about each other that night.
- **Give the newcomer a table of her own.** A villain who can take things out of photographs asks the room for multiple opponents instead of one. At the center is a second, group round in which three rivals spoil one another's attempts to prevent the same theft.

## Provisional direction

Choose the last date. Updraft makes objects she lets go of fall upward until they strike something. It's her first Super Speed Dating and she wants to find a hero who'll give her the kind of trouble worth printing a calling card for. The flier she thinks will be a natural match offers a routine in which he always intercepts her loot. Her next dates see the same power as useful for their stunts or as a reason to pass. When Still Life admits that his best prospect wanted to trade wins for the cameras, she asks him to try to stop her ringing the bell before their four minutes are up. He can arrest one moving thing he keeps his eyes on. The bell bout demands a second shot from her and a catch with his bare hand from him. She rings the bell by a fingertip. They choose each other for a rematch over a real target, with room for her loot to get clean away and no agreement about who will win.

## Craft plan

- **Voice trial.** Try Updraft in first person, retelling a good heist that she still gets embarrassed in the middle of telling. Try close third in the present, from inside the concentration of a window dresser who keeps moving things half an inch because no one else will see what's wrong. Both can hold a sentence long enough to take in the room and the opinions it provokes.
- **Updraft, fledgling villain.** She wants witnesses to tilt their heads back and see her work and hates it when someone watches her doing it. She judges sight lines for a living as a window dresser. At ease she speaks in quick bits of practical judgment. Selling a stunt, she walks the listener through what a witness would see from the street. She arrives with good card stock she paid extra for and leaves her hand in her pocket when a date goes sour.
- **Still Life, fledgling hero.** He wants to be hard to get past and he's willing to reveal more than is prudent if it will tempt someone into trying. He learned to keep watch as a lifeguard. He gives an example before answering a question, and when nervous he talks warmly about what has gone wrong on his watch. He sets his water aside when he needs both hands. The attendant and earlier dates stay unnamed. The cast is original.
- **Place sheet.** The Comet Roller Palace off Garnet Street, on a wet November weeknight. The rink still teaches beginner lessons and rents its floor between sessions. Wheels chatter over thresholds, maple flexes, coats smell of wet wool and the rental counter of orange disinfectant. The old brass bell projects off the gallery's open stair at the top, well above the rink floor. The attendant rings it from the landing. An obsolete red race line survives in the varnish, straight to the patch below the bell. The International Room at the Pavillon Hotel gives everyone the same view and height. The Crown Cinema has two levels, but rows of fixed seats restrict how people can move. At the Comet a racer can head straight for the mark without watching their feet, and both levels give access to the same target on very different time scales.

## Scene briefs

1. **Check-in and first round, 25%.** Rattled by her first registration, Updraft nearly puts her own alias on the line for the name of a prospect. The attendant strikes the bell and sends the heroes around the rink. The flier sees her make a spoon rise, goes up for it and comes back with a proposition. She can steal any way she likes, and he'll get the loot back before anybody notices. She tries to talk him into leaving the outcome open. He thinks she's bargaining over which nights he gets to look good. In the brief later rounds a hero asks what her power could add to his entrance and another wants an opponent whose power can reach him on the ground. She keeps her match slip blank and starts to think she brought the wrong power.
2. **Last table and trial bout, 60% — long scene.** Updraft leaves her calling cards in her pocket. Still Life takes too long telling her about the candidate who scheduled alternating defeats. She asks him to stop her ringing the event bell without touching it. She gives the spoon an upward fall and he arrests it inches below the flared rim. He holds his gaze on it as he slides his chair along the red line, so he can take the first shot out of play with his hand. She sees what he is doing and takes the open stair, letting a metal skate tag fall up toward the rim from a higher tread in full view, before he has both feet on the chair. He switches his gaze to stop the tag, lunges for the released spoon and misses by a fingertip. Slow down there. Updraft can see how close he was and how much she wanted to see if he'd try. The bell rings early and half the room gets up by reflex.
3. **Closing at the tables and the front glass, 15%.** Updraft goes to the desk on her own and writes Still Life's name. His slip turns out to have hers. He proposes the Comet's silver marquee star, which is coming off its bolts for repainting Saturday. He'll watch the sign as the crew takes it down. She gives him a calling card with Saturday pencilled on it and keeps the time to herself. He steps onto Garnet Street to scout the view from below. From the vestibule she notices the dolly the crew will use to bring the star around back. She'll let them get it off the marquee for her. End on him sighting up the front of the building and her looking past the dolly to the strip of open sky over the delivery ramp. Her blank slip, the flier's guaranteed catch and the fingertips under the bell earn the pleasure of planning a theft she might lose.
