# Super Speed Dating — provisional outline

## Promised experience

The request puts a nervous first-timer with a power of their own at Super Speed Dating, where fledgling heroes and villains try out prospective rogues and nemeses. “Speed Dating” suggests the quick judgments, hopeful patter and mortification of a date, applied to the question of who would be delightful to fight. “Match with your power” suggests a payoff we can see happen between two people using their powers. The exclamation over a “legendary nemesis” makes room for extravagant play and a rivalry with a future. The request leaves the power, alignment, specific relationship, scale of action and outcome open.

## Takes considered

- **Play the last date straight.** A first-time villain wagers she can ring the rotation bell early. She and her last date have four minutes to discover which of them can make the other improvise.
- **Begin with the second fight.** A young hero guarding a civic prize recognizes a calling card from the date he turned down. The fight cuts back to the two wrong conclusions they drew about each other that night.
- **Give the newcomer a table of her own.** A villain who can take things out of photographs asks the room for multiple opponents instead of one. At the center is a second, group round in which three rivals spoil one another's attempts to prevent the same theft.

## Provisional direction

Choose the last date. The short, unsuccessful encounters establish what each side thinks it is shopping for. The bell then becomes the object of a real contest, with the end of the date also the end of the opportunity to win.

Updraft wants to make a name by stealing things into the sky. She can make an object she releases fall upward until it strikes something. Still Life can stop one moving object as long as he keeps his eyes on it. He wants an opponent who will force him to choose what to watch. She bets him she can sound the brass rotation bell before the four minutes are up, without putting a hand on it. He comes out onto the floor where he can see the bell and both aisles, then stops her first shot just short of the bell. She finds another angle using the roller rink's gallery. He can abandon the first shot to stop the second. Either way he has a live shot to reckon with. She wins that bout and he names a target he can defend in the open air, where the thing she steals can keep falling. She writes him down, and he puts the rematch in motion himself.

## Craft plan

- **Voice trial.** Try Updraft in first person, retelling a good heist that she still gets embarrassed in the middle of telling. Try close third in the present, from inside the concentration of a window dresser who keeps moving things half an inch because no one else will see what's wrong. Both can hold a sentence long enough to take in the room and the opinions it provokes.
- **Updraft, fledgling villain.** She wants somebody capable of noticing that she was there and hates it when people watch her work. At ease she speaks in quick bits of practical judgment. When selling a stunt she walks someone through the view from the street, talking herself into believing the picture too. She carries clean calling cards and has practiced putting them where a witness has to tilt their head back to read.
- **Still Life, fledgling hero.** He wants to be hard to get past and he's willing to reveal more than is prudent if it will tempt someone into trying. He learned to keep watch as a pool lifeguard. He takes the time to give an example before answering a question, and when nervous he talks warmly about what has gone wrong on his watch. He has a habit of setting aside his water until he's finished keeping an eye on something. The attendant and earlier dates stay unnamed. The cast is original.
- **Place sheet.** The Comet Roller Palace off Garnet Street, on a wet November weeknight. The rink still teaches beginner lessons and rents out its floor between sessions. Wheels chatter over the thresholds, the maple flexes underfoot, wet coats smell of wool and the rental counter of orange disinfectant. An old brass changeover bell hangs outside the lip of the skaters' gallery where an attendant can ring it from above. The low underside of the gallery and its solid rail break up the view from the floor. An obsolete pair of red race lines survives in the varnish. The International Room at the Pavillon Hotel would be easy to see across. The Crown Cinema could offer balconies but seats would pin the dates in rows. At the Comet a move from one's chair has consequences, and the two levels offer ways to reach one target without following the same route.

## Scene briefs

1. **Check-in and first round, 25%.** The attendant sorts heroes and villains to opposite sides of the rink and demonstrates the bell with a peal the maple carries. Updraft wants one person to ask what she'd steal if she could get away with it. A flier wants to retrieve anything she sends up and offers her an arrangement in which he wins every time. She pitches anyway and drops a teaspoon up to the gallery ceiling. The flier tracks the spoon instead of listening to her. Compress the middle rounds and their dwindling possibilities in a paragraph.
2. **Last table and trial bout, 60% — long scene.** Still Life describes in embarrassing detail how his most promising date figured out they could take turns beating each other on camera. Updraft invites him to try to stop something neither of them has rehearsed. The current round's clock keeps running. He gives up his seat to take the best view of her attack and arrests the rising spoon in open air just below the bell. Let her feel the pleasure and humiliation of being read accurately. With him holding it there, she takes the shallow stairs and drops a skate key over the gallery rail, aiming from above while the spoon still wants to rise from below. From the floor he can see the suspended spoon but the gallery rail hides her second release until the key is almost on the bell. If he switches, the spoon strikes. The bell rings ahead of schedule and half the room gets up by reflex.
3. **Closing at the tables, 15%.** While the attendants collect cards, Still Life sees Updraft has set aside none to leave as a calling card. He supplies a prospect of his own, the silver star coming off the Comet's high outdoor marquee for repainting on Saturday. She can plan to steal it in daylight and he will be there to stop her. End with them looking up through the rain-streaked front glass at the top of the marquee from different positions, each already judging a different approach. Her first, indoor demonstration went to waste, their bell bout taught them to use opposite approaches, and now neither has a ceiling to count on.
