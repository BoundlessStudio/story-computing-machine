# Opus review: No Need to Worry

Independent audit for a later editing pass. It is not a `review.md` verdict,
does not change canon state, and does not authorize edits by itself.

## Verdict

- Dialogue: REVISE — mostly strong and well grounded, but the intercom disclosure depends on "the handler", a cover role the prose never gives Ogden, and the last exchange states the new deal again after it has already landed.
- Prose: PASS — clean, concrete and varied, with no systemic tics; a few small physical details are unclear.
- Continuity and prompt: PASS — the required line is verbatim, the R+ intensity is delivered and the promise resolves; there are three Minor plausibility and staging points.
- Edit scope: targeted-dialogue
- Existing review.md said: Verdict PASS; "Dialogue: PASS"; I mostly agree, but it was wrong to say the intercom disclosure fully preserves listener uptake.

Most of the dialogue here works, and much of it is good. The three main speakers are easy to tell apart. Ogden, in particular, avoids the collection's house voice: he is crude, bodily and needy, gets petty about a jacket, and is sharp when it counts ("He could see through the wall?"). Lies are caught using facts already on the page. The required prompt line is placed so that it falls flat, and the scene admits that it does. The failures are local. Norris catches Dulsie's lie through a referent the reader was never given, and the ending makes Norris and Dulsie's deal three times over (L317, L329, L331). The edit pass should add one clause of narration, make two cuts at the end and fix a few small wording slips. It should not rework exchanges that already work.

## Edit guardrails

- The required line must stay verbatim and stay with Norris: "No need to worry, I discovered a fatal weakness: 30mm shells, launched at about 1000 m/s. About 4000 per minute should do." (L99). Keep the moment where the joke falls flat.
- Keep the adult, hard-R register: cannibalism, faeces, profanity, the crushed arm and theft. Do not soften it. There is no sexual content, and none should be added.
- Stay within 2,500–4,000 words (the prose is about 3,430 now). Keep close third person on Dulsie, past tense, and craft profile prospective-2026-08-23.
- The story depends on these facts: Ogden heals from eaten flesh but has finite mass and cannot quickly regrow the arm. Detached pieces do not revive. The dividing wall hides the pen from Norris (L67), and Ogden's "see through the wall" line relies on that. Norris was shown in by the attendant, and Ogden believed the cannon was a dead exhibit. The rings are in the handbag, and the cashbox amount motivates Norris.
- There is no water infrastructure; keep it that way. Nothing touches LOCKED universe facts. This is an unnamed modern-like setting with one living person using a bodily faculty.
- The prompt also asked for a matching cover. That is outside this prose audit, but any change to the staging described below may need the cover checked again.
- Do not flatten Ogden's needy, bodily voice, Dulsie's managerial euphemisms, or Norris's cold, acquisitive terseness after the quip.

## Dialogue findings

### D1 · Major · "The handler" is never planted, so Norris's catch is hard to follow

- Where: L15 (Norris's hire), L75–L77 (Dulsie's arrival in the booth), L139 (intercom), L153
- Text:
> with a private extra fee if the animal proved dangerous
> “Where's the handler?”
> “She said the handler was dead,” Norris said.
> “And your attendant? The man who showed me in?”
- Problem: Ground/referent. Nothing before L75 says the hunt had a "handler", or that Ogden played that part in human form. The only worker the reader has seen near the animal is the attendant, who has just died, so at L77 many readers will take "Dead." as the truth about the attendant. At L139, the hinge of the intercom disclosure, Norris's line then seems to repeat a true fact instead of exposing a lie. Only L153, where Norris separates the attendant from the handler, lets the reader work backwards to "the handler = Ogden". The disclosure still lands overall, because "husband" and "You two" follow, but its key line reaches the reader late.
- Fix: Plant the cover role once, in narration, before L75. The natural place is the L15 paragraph about hiring Norris. Say that Norris was told the animal comes with a handler, and that he has never met the handler. Keep L75–L77 and L139 as written; they read correctly once this is planted. Do not have a character explain it in dialogue. Illustrative only: Norris had been told the animal could pass for a big man and that its handler would bring it out; he had never met the handler. (The "pass for a big man" half also fixes C1.)

### D2 · Major · The final exchange makes the deal again and states the point

- Where: L315–L333, the booth floor and then the car
- Text:
> Ogden was dead, and Norris was looking at the part of the business still alive on the floor.
> “You get people here again,” he said, loud enough for her to hear. “We don't need him.”
> He thought she could simply find another four rich men to walk in front of his gun.
- Problem: Philosopher / over-explained ending. The deal is already struck on the floor ("You can get more of those people?" / "I know. Can you?" / she nods), and L317 narrates what it means. In the car, Norris makes the same demand again and adds "We don't need him.", which spells out what the action has already shown: the gun has replaced Ogden. L331 then explains it a third time. The squeeze in the car, with Norris holding the key and not starting the engine, is new and good. The restatement is not. "Here" is also odd, since the lodge now holds five corpses in the pen and Ogden's remains across the yard, and nobody registers that.
- Fix: Keep the floor exchange as the deal and the car as the squeeze. Cut "We don't need him." and cut the L331 sentence "He thought she could simply find another four rich men to walk in front of his gun." Leave "She had spent years filling tonight's pen. But he hadn't started the car..." and her "Yes." Drop "here" (for example, "You get people again") unless the edit deliberately wants Norris to be that stupid. Do not replace the cut line with another summary or a comeback.

### D3 · Minor · Two small wording slips in the intercom scene

- Where: L123 (Ogden on the intercom); L179 (Norris takes over the button)
- Text:
> Where have you found this cunt?
> He spoke into it himself.
- Problem: "Where have you found" is not idiomatic in the story's British register. It reads like a translation and dulls Ogden's best insult. In L179, "it" grammatically refers back to "the button", so Norris seems to speak into a button.
- Fix: Illustrative only: "Where did you find this cunt?" For L179, name the grille or microphone, or say "He leaned in and pressed it himself."

## Prose findings

### P1 · Minor · A few physical details are unclear

- Where: L57 (the attendant's death); L283 (after the firing); L331 (in the car)
- Text:
> He pulled the penknife out and spat a piece of ear beside it.
> She kicked his ankle with her good shoe.
> There was blood running between her thighs onto the upholstery.
- Problem: At L57, "beside it" has no location, because the knife is still in Ogden's hand. At L283, "good shoe" implies a damaged shoe that the story never shows. At L331, the blood has no stated source, so a reader may wonder about a new injury or a gynaecological bleed instead of the arm.
- Fix: Make each detail specific without softening it. At L57, have him drop the knife in the sand and spit the ear beside it. At L283, write "her foot" or "her left foot". At L331, tie the blood to the dressing in her lap.

## Continuity and prompt findings

### C1 · Minor · Why Norris fires at once on a human-faced man is not shown

- Where: L81–L87, the first burst
- Text:
> He had put on his own face again, the long nose and heavy cheeks
> “That's it,” Dulsie said. “Shoot it.”
- Problem: Norris has been hired to deal with "the animal". What he sees is a huge, bloodied man with an ordinary face, carrying trousers and making an irritated gesture, and he fires a 30mm cannon at him the moment Dulsie says so. Ogden's "He could see through the wall?" rightly shows that Norris fired on her word alone, so the story is aware of this. Still, the page never gives Norris a reason to take a man-shaped figure for the animal, and this is the beat that starts the plot.
- Fix: The D1 clause ("could pass for a big man") fixes this too. If that half is not used, give Norris one visible cue before he fires, such as the split jacket, the size or the blood. Seen from Dulsie's position, the cue should make "That's it" believable.

### C2 · Minor · A stone plinth works as cover against a 30mm rotary cannon

- Where: L115 (speaker post); L207 (Ogden returns with the box)
- Text:
> The cannon could not reach him there.
> His head and shoulders were visible now.
- Problem: The prompt line sells overwhelming firepower (about 4,000 30mm rounds a minute). L115 says the cannon cannot reach Ogden behind a stone plinth, and L207 treats his exposure above it as meaningful, which means the stone is stopping the rounds. Readers who take the premise seriously will ask why Norris doesn't fire straight through the stone.
- Fix: Make it a matter of angle instead of stone. For example, the speaker post sits outside the gun's arc, at the edge of the wide opening. Keep L207 as Dulsie's view only.

### C3 · Minor · Hearing and car-seat continuity

- Where: L91–L105 (after the first burst); L325–L327 (the car)
- Text:
> Dulsie could barely hear him through the ringing.
> The car was where she'd left it, nose towards the drive.
> He was shorter than Ogden.
- Problem: Dulsie follows the whole of Norris's long, number-heavy quip at L99, and then, two lines later, "could barely hear" a three-word order. The car "was where she'd left it", which implies she parked it and the seat is set for her, yet Norris's seat adjustment is measured against Ogden.
- Fix: Establish the ringing from L87 (she hears Norris as if from far off) so that L105 reads as it getting worse, not a sudden change. Change "she'd" to "they'd", or drop the comparison with Ogden.

## Recurring patterns

- Explaining a point that has already landed, at the ending: 3 instances, L317, L329, L331. Covered by D2; cut down to at most one explanation.
- Small unclear physical details: L57, L283, L331 (P1); L105, L325–L327 (C3). Clean these up as a group.
- Bare imperatives shared by Dulsie and Norris: throughout, for example L159, L163, L253, L261. The situation mostly earns this. Do not let it spread to Ogden, and do not add more.
- Labelled emotion in narration: L145, L225, L295. Each is specific and earned; no sweep is needed, but do not add more near the ending.

## Suggested edit order

1. D1 together with C1: one clause of narration in the L15 paragraph fixes both the handler referent and Norris's reason to fire.
2. D2: two cuts in the car beat, so that the deal is made once, the squeeze happens once and the explanation appears at most once.
3. C2: make the plinth's protection a matter of angle, so the 30mm premise holds.
4. D3, P1, C3: wording and staging polish; each is a single-word to single-clause change.
