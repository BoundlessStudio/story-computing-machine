# Provisional direction

A compact first-person workplace comedy, provisionally titled *No Comment*. The manager has arranged a paid publicity appearance at a bakery the girls recently saved, and needs one usable group photograph and a short, intelligible interview. This is a worthwhile, ordinary PR job; publicity money helps keep the team working, and the manager badly wants this particular appearance to go well.

Three girls make different assumptions fail. One's glowing captions strip euphemism from her own transformed speech, despite her painstaking effort to follow the script. Another produces involuntary victory music when she exerts herself and then feels relief, spoiling sound bites; she also wants a solo. A third converts incoming hostile momentum into extravagant flowers, obscuring rescues and creating a quite separate allergy problem; she wants to reach the refreshments. They have habits and disagreements, and care about their manager, rather than existing as a queue of malfunctioning products.

The manager improvises around these powers during the bakery reopening. The promised photograph keeps escaping her. A small pastry-hungry creature interrupts the appearance, plausibly attracted by the cake or connected to the earlier bakery rescue. The manager uses what she knows about the girls and this bounded danger to bring the rescue into one comprehensible camera frame. She may become the target herself by moving the branded cake; this must be an intelligible, safe-enough tactical choice, not an invitation to danger merely to improve a photograph. The resulting image includes her being rescued, in an undignified pose she cannot spin away.

A possible finish is the girls wanting their manager in the official team image and the manager discovering her involuntary inclusion has become the most widely printed one. Their affection and her embarrassment can close the story even if the publicity benefit is imperfect. The cake creature's arrival is the chief causal risk: prose may choose a different interruption or make the entire appearance a farce without a fresh attack. Preserve concrete PR problem-solving and human comic friction; avoid a public speech about honesty or a perfect publicity reward for learning a lesson.
