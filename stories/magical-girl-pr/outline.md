# Provisional direction

A compact first-person workplace comedy, provisionally titled *No Comment*. The manager has arranged a paid publicity appearance at a bakery the girls recently saved, and needs one usable group photograph and a short, intelligible interview. This is a worthwhile, ordinary PR job; publicity money helps keep the team working, and the manager badly wants this particular appearance to go well.

Three girls make different assumptions fail. One's glowing captions strip euphemism from her own transformed speech, despite her painstaking effort to follow the script. Another produces involuntary victory music when she exerts herself and then feels relief, spoiling sound bites; she also wants a solo. A third converts incoming hostile momentum into extravagant flowers, obscuring rescues and creating a quite separate allergy problem; she wants to reach the refreshments. They have habits and disagreements, and care about their manager, rather than existing as a queue of malfunctioning products.

The girls ask early for their manager to join the picture, and she declines: her job requires being recognized by editors, not recognized buying groceries in yesterday's cardigan. The manager improvises around their powers during the bakery reopening. Each workaround may work locally and collide with another girl's power. Her choice to move the oversized branded cake outdoors for better light attracts a small pastry-seeking creature, a known local nuisance. This is separate from the earlier bakery rescue. An earlier minor accident demonstrates flower conversion and its sight-line limit. The manager moves the cake away from the guests into that girl's clear range, with the others close enough to catch her. Her practical decision protects people; the undignified pose in the resulting photograph is accidental.

A possible finish gives her a real choice about letting the bakery use the embarrassing image after her carefully directed picture is spoiled. It can benefit the bakery while costing her the anonymity she has wanted. Their affection and her embarrassment can close the story without a perfect publicity reward or a speech about honesty. The creature's arrival and the tactical safety still need to become intelligible on the page; prose may find a better interruption or ending. Preserve concrete PR problem-solving and human comic friction rather than treating uncontrolled powers as automatic authentic publicity.
