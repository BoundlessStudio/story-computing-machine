# Opus review: The Blade Calls Your Name

Independent audit for a later editing pass. It is not a `review.md` verdict,
does not change canon state, and does not authorize edits by itself.

## Verdict

- Dialogue: REVISE — one exchange fails Ground ("You knew?"), the ledger story contradicts itself across the accountability walk, and most exchanges run on correction ping-pong that Odmara always wins.
- Prose: PASS — concrete, tactile work prose with good staging of hands and loads; only a minor negation/disclaimer tic and a water-infrastructure policy nit.
- Continuity and prompt: PASS — the blade literally calls her name and the call is resolved at about 3,600 words; no canon conflict; only minor staging and timing slips (the ledger contradiction is filed as D2).
- Edit scope: heavy-dialogue
- Existing review.md said: Verdict PASS; "Dialogue: PASS", voices "differentiated"; disagree, because one exchange is incoherent and Odmara and Felrin are interchangeable in every technical run.

The problem isn't clumsy dialogue. It's sameness. Four people in an emergency talk like one careful engineer, and Odmara closes nearly every round with a mirrored correction or comeback. Yevet's record formulas and Kosca's minutes-and-loads talk are real voices and should stay. The trouble is that Odmara and Felrin share the same clipped register, so the master-and-apprentice history the narration keeps describing (his teaching cadence, his filled silences, his explanations deserting him) is never actually heard. The edit should give Felrin an early fluent voice for him to lose. It should let Odmara answer with her hands, short observations and silences rather than rebuttals, cut the comebacks that end exchanges, and let tests and loads settle arguments. The late beats (the fine hammer, the wedge, "Independent chainwright.") already sound right. The rest of the dialogue should sound like them.

## Dialogue findings

### D1 · Blocking · "You knew?" asks what Felrin already knows

- Where: L65–L71, Kosca's arrival in the gallery (checked against L161–L163)
- Text:
> Felrin heard something else. "You knew?"
> "I knew whose hammer I was holding a lantern for."
> "I did," Kosca said. "Felrin did. She hammered."
- Problem: Ground/knowledge and uptake. Felrin held the guard beside Kosca that night (L163), so he can't be surprised that she knew whose work it was, and Kosca's answer tells him something he saw himself. No other reading of "You knew?" works either: that Odmara was back, that the ledger was false, or that the splice would fail. Kosca's reply answers none of them, and "Felrin heard something else" doesn't tell the reader what he heard.
- Fix: Cut "Felrin heard something else." through Kosca's lantern line, and let "Argue while walking." follow "There you are." (move the stair gesture with it). If you want a Felrin beat here, it has to be something he doesn't already know, or a reaction he would plausibly have, such as objecting to the lift captain stepping in. Kosca's reply must answer that. Illustrative only: Felrin: "Captain, this is a guild matter." Kosca: "It's my lift on that rail. Argue while walking."

### D2 · Major · The ledger entry changes shape during the accountability walk

- Where: L139–L171, descending the west stair; also L223 in the chamber
- Text:
> "Six years ago. West-nine. Emergency entry?"
> "There wasn't one," Odmara said.
> "There is an entry. Felrin, full replacement of the ninth flexible link, inspected and closed."
> "The storm had opened three hangers. We could not leave the terrace on eight."
> "That explains urgency," Yevet said. "It doesn't explain a false completion."
> "An emergency authorization. The only one the lower wardens would accept. I ordered new stock the next morning."
- Problem: The scene gives three incompatible accounts of what Felrin recorded, and nobody notices. Yevet recited the ledger at L33, yet asks a question she can answer herself; its only job is to let her correct "There wasn't one" with "There is an entry." Odmara has been gone six years but states what the ledger doesn't contain, with no stated way of knowing (Ground/knowledge). Felrin then calls his mark "an emergency authorization", which contradicts the completed-replacement entry Yevet has just read, and the registrar lets it pass. "On eight" can't be decoded: twelve lines less three opened doesn't give eight, and it might be an anchor number. Readers get the gist (he took credit and never replaced it) but not the actual lie, and that lie is the story's moral fact. Yevet's "explains urgency / doesn't explain a false completion" also closes the beat on an epigram.
- Fix: Decide what the entry says and make every line agree with it. The simplest version: the entry records a completed replacement under Felrin's mark, and no emergency record exists. Yevet reads it out and asks Felrin, not Odmara, to account for it. Felrin's defence is that an apprentice's name would have voided the work, so he entered it as his own and as finished, meaning to make it true. Cut Odmara's "There wasn't one." Replace "on eight" with a plain count or a plain consequence. Have Yevet log the gap as a record note in her own terms instead of an epigram, and make L223's "Authorization false" name the same falsehood.

### D3 · Major · The gallery confrontation ends every round on Odmara's comeback

- Where: L43–L57 and L73–L75, the gallery before and after Kosca arrives
- Text:

> "You haven't worked this hall since you left."

- Problem: Correction ping-pong and last-word lines. After the strong "Then the blade is wrong." / "The ledger is wrong." (keep this), every later round is a mirrored retort that Odmara wins. "And you haven't answered it yet" ignores Felrin's jab about her standing, and it's hollow anyway: he hasn't answered because she's holding his wrist. "Remains evidence" / "remains useful" is a word-mirror, not an argument, yet Yevet gives in to it without a reason. Odmara is facing the master who erased her name, for the first time in six years, in front of twenty people, and she sounds like she's scoring debate points.
- Fix: Keep L33–L47. Replace L49 with no spoken reply: she leaves the railing and catches Felrin's wrist, which also fixes D9. Let Felrin's "You haven't worked this hall" stand unanswered or get a physical response, cut L57, and let the door at L59 interrupt. At L73–L75, give Odmara (or Kosca, who owns the clock) a plain practical reason the blade has to go down, e.g. it can only read the fault once seated at the anchor, and let Yevet's consent follow from that. Don't replace any cut retort with a new one.

### D4 · Major · The diagnostic exchanges run as correction tennis

- Where: L95–L121, first control landing; L189–L195, first look at the splice
- Text:

> "Whether the signal comes from the lift load or the terrace hang. Different question."
> "There are twelve west anchors," Yevet said.
> "Eight rings flat when the guide is floating."
> "I can read a joint."

- Problem: Tennis Match, Identical Voices and explanatory chorus. Every technical beat goes statement, correction, counter, correction, and Odmara is always right and always last. Yevet's "What will that establish?" and "There are twelve west anchors" exist only to cue Odmara's answers, and the second contradicts Yevet's own L33, where she tied this key to west-nine from the ledger. Felrin's "rack is lying" theory was already disproved at L81, because the blade keeps humming off the rack. So his line doesn't fit the evidence on the page, and "Different question." exists only to correct it. "I can read a joint." / "Then read it under load." is pure setup and comeback.
- Fix: Let tests and loads settle arguments instead of rebuttals. At L95–L99, keep one answer (Odmara's, without "Different question."), or give Felrin an explanation that fits what the blade is doing now. At L115, cut Yevet's objection or make it a request for the record that fits what she already knows. Keep Felrin's "Or eight" as a real alternative in his own voice, and let the rail answer it rather than a flat correction. At L191–L195, cut "I can read a joint." and "Then read it under load." and go straight from "Not at rest," to Kosca's load order at L197. L299–L305 (wedge versus unloading) has real content, so keep it, but split the voices per D5 so the cover-the-tags test passes.

### D5 · Major · Felrin's narrated voice never reaches his lines

- Where: whole story; narration at L171, L261, L335 against his lines at L23, L55, L97, L119, L189, L299–L303
- Text:
> Felrin's answer came quickly, almost in his old teaching cadence.
> For once he did not fill the silence.
> His explanations had left him. He spoke in material and measure now.
> "If we cut the shoulder without unloading nine, the tooth may kick."
> "We unload it too far and ten snaps back against the comb."
- Problem: Identical Voices. The narration keeps telling us Felrin is a talker and a teacher who fills silences and explains, and that he loses this under pressure. On the page he speaks in two- to eight-word declaratives from his first line, in the same register as Odmara. Cover the tags at L299–L305 and you can't tell which of them is speaking. So his late turn to "Too proud by two hairs." and "Seat is holding." isn't a change, and L261 and L335 describe something the reader never heard.
- Fix: Give Felrin audible fluency in the first half: longer sentences with subordinate clauses, explaining to the room, guild rule and precedent, and talking to the registrar or the room about Odmara rather than to her (he doesn't say her name until L413; keep that). Candidate lines: L23, L55, L97, L119, L145–L149, L171, L189. Then let him shrink after the chip (L245) so that L335's narration becomes true, or cut that narration. Illustrative only (L23): "Dust in the back plate. Every wet season a contact crosses and the rack throws up whatever it last held. Give me a moment and I'll clear it."
