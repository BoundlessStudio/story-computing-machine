# Outline

## Story

- Premise and central promise: Six years after Odmara left Hearthchain's chainwright guild, its racked service blade casts her name in red mica strokes and pulses its syllables through the suspended terrace's railings. It has recognized the strain signature of a temporary splice she made under her newly claimed name. Before the quarry counterweight descends at deep bell, she must answer, find the fault, and keep the terrace attached without letting artifact or guild define her.
- Focal pressure or attachment: Odmara wants the lower neighbors safe and the work credited truthfully, but will not return as Felrin's apprentice or accept sole liability for a repair he concealed. She distrusts the guild's ownership of labor, yet alone remembers why her splice was temporary.
- Counterforce or complication: Felrin took master credit to legalize Odmara's emergency work, then never replaced it or corrected the ledger. Registrar Yevet protects the distinction between performer, witness, and authorizer. Lift captain Kosca can delay the loaded quarry cage only by draining brake water needed for evacuation.
- POV, distance, and information limit: Close third-person past through Odmara, attentive to temper colors, vibration paths, joints, hands, and uses of her name. The blade has no interiority; its meaning remains provisional until changed loads, grips, and witnesses produce repeatable responses.
- Governing movement and time shape: One evening's descent from guild hall to lower anchor chambers as the approaching counterweight distorts the call. Each stop tests a reading and exposes another layer of the repair. Keep the decisive action among four workers; end during stabilization, with judgment pending.
- Speculative rule or ordinary-world constraint: Hearthchain's first anchor builders forged the nonliving blade from a broken sky-iron quarry saw, spring steel, and mica as a resonance key. A repair stores its hammer pattern with the worker's claimed, engraved name while two witnesses hold the guard. This is a local work label, not true-name magic or identity proof. Matching anchor strain makes the blade passively repeat that label as light, overtones, and hilt vibration; anyone near the rack can see or hear it, while connected iron carries the vibration. It cannot locate, judge, understand, move, or choose. Answering means seating it and reproducing the diagnostic pattern; refusal leaves the fault unidentified, while a wrong name or forced cut gives an incomplete pattern and may damage the joint. Transfer requires demonstrated repair, voluntary release by the prior worker, and two witnesses.

## Voice

- Narrative texture: Taut and tactile, descending through iron, stone, water, and load; vibration changes what Odmara notices before explanation catches up. Keep craft observation precise but incomplete.
- Conversational texture: Clipped measurements, interruptions, and administrative distinctions mix with ordinary requests for lamps, wedges, water, and room to move.
- Rhetorical ownership: Odmara reasons from fit, sequence, and touched work; Felrin from materials, precedent, and temporary compromise; Kosca from weights, minutes, routes, and people; Yevet from credit, authorization, and liability.
- Pressure behavior: Odmara grows exact and unexplanatory; Felrin's fluency shrinks when his hands follow hers; Kosca interrupts abstraction with loads; Yevet revises categories after tests without abandoning caution.
- Anti-default: Avoid sentient-sword destiny, polished debates about identity, heroic quips, a fearful chorus, or a final speech turning repair into a moral emblem.

## Beats

1. In the guild hall, the blade flashes Odmara's name through the rails. Yevet treats it as evidence; Felrin calls it error. Odmara recognizes a maintenance alarm but refuses his attempt to answer for her.
2. Kosca reports the quarry cage will load the lower anchor before deep bell. A no-load test makes the visual name vanish while one vibration remains, locating the circuit and disproving a divine summons or general accusation.
3. Descending, Odmara discloses that she installed a flexible emergency splice after claiming her present name; Felrin authorized it under his master mark and promised replacement. Kosca confirms what she witnessed. Yevet separates workmanship from lawful approval.
4. Felrin's registered name produces witness colors but no cutting path; forcing the blade chips the joint. Odmara answers with grip, claimed name, and old hammer cadence. Two possible grooves appear, one corrupted by changed cable tension.
5. Lamp shutters, a small load release, and separate rail readings distinguish the safe groove. The splice remains sound; Felrin's unperformed replacement has pulled a neighboring tooth sideways.
6. A lone cut would preserve the terrace but register Odmara as solely responsible. She instead assigns Felrin the joint, Kosca the load, and Yevet the witnessing. They shave the jammed tooth, seat a proper wedge, and lower the cage in stages.
7. Odmara releases her stored pattern after Kosca demonstrates the new diagnostic sequence and Felrin records authorization. The witnessed repair divides responsibility accurately; the blade stops calling. A practical exchange over the temporary tag credits Odmara without restoring guild membership or forgiveness.
