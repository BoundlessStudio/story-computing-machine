## Constraints

- Target length: 2,500–4,000 words
- Direction: create an original mythic epic about a sword literally calling and
  choosing its new master; `king author` was only an example of mythic scale
- Avoid: King Arthur, Arthurian names, lore, symbols, imagery, plot beats, or
  imitation
- Setting: use an established shared-universe setting for a wholly new story
- Required continuity bridge: the legendary sword is visibly made from glass
  of the Glass Sea; the story may occur later and in another location, with the
  sword itself carrying that existing-setting connection
- Explicit setting exception: Heshavar is a nonliving Glass Sea artifact whose
  own magic literally calls and chooses its new master, deliberately breaking
  the life-only artifact rule; do not make the sword secretly alive, sentient,
  inhabited, or dependent on a living operator as the source of its call
- POV, tense, and distance: derive from what the story should reveal, distort,
  conceal, or make intimate unless specified
- Tone and audience: broadly accessible unless specified
- Required: a complete story that resolves its central promise
- Craft profile: prospective-2026-08-08
- Also avoid: bureaucratic or procedural reinterpretations of the blade's call;
  small-scale technical-search plots; metaphorical substitution for the sword
  choosing a master
