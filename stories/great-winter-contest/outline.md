# Provisional direction

The Great Winter Contest is the Ice Kingdom's famous snow-beast steeplechase. At dawn, each entrant gets an equal mound of snow and sculpts a mount; by noon, its maker must ride it through a spectacular course above and across the palace lake. Riders supply the magic themselves and can reshape their mounts while riding, but cannot add snow. The course's marked gates and finish make the winner legible.

Rilka has made exquisite little moving snow rabbits for twenty years. She loves them and resents how thoroughly they have made her the rabbit lady: good for children's presents, never anyone's idea of a champion. She spends money she can ill afford on her first contest entry and a magnificent cape. Her full-sized hare is deliberately beautiful, fast in a straight line, and much less sensible to sit on than anything she has sold. She wants the trophy without having to pretend she entered for fun.

Her chief rival, the reigning champion, rides a superb snow stag. He is competitive and knows the course; he is neither a cheat nor a disguised bully. Other entrants provide real spectacle and different approaches, rather than simply failing to clear her path. The hare's opening leaps delight the crowd and put Rilka ahead. Her insistence on its perfect, showy form then costs her at the tight turns and repeated landings. The stag catches her through good riding.

Late in the race, a hard landing damages the hare's hind leg. Repair is legal, but its snow must come from somewhere else on the mount. Rilka has to surrender the exquisite ears and change the gait she has made thousands of times, producing an increasingly odd-looking race animal. The course's last descent and leap give her a genuine sporting choice: hold the safer line for a respectable finish, or use the altered shape in a risky, comprehensible attempt to catch the champion. A narrow win is the current possibility; the writing may earn a loss instead. Either way, she must ride the finish rather than be handed recognition for a rescue or a loophole.

Close with the actual result, her unapologetic reaction to it, and a particular exchange with the rival or someone who has come to watch her. The cheering should still be about the contest. Aim for buoyant close third person, tactile winter spectacle, and competitive people who enjoy the same peculiar thing. The exact maneuvers and ending remain open to discovery.
