# Prompt contract

## Verbatim writing prompt

> **[WP] In a fantasy world much like our own, where magic has long since die out, the greatest wonder is a floating city that should not be possible. With over four million residents, it's ancient nuclear reactors are beginning to fail, and the results are catastrophic.**

## Story controls

- Working title: Four Million Falling
- Target length: 2,500–4,000 words
- POV: Close third person through one principal viewpoint.
- Tense: Past.
- Tone and genre: Urgent science-fantasy disaster drama with human-scale stakes
  inside a city-scale catastrophe.
- Audience/content rating: Teen / PG-13; peril and loss without graphic gore.
- Required elements: A fantasy society recognizably like our own; magic believed
  extinct for a long time; an apparently impossible ancient floating city with
  more than four million residents; failing nuclear reactors; catastrophic
  consequences dramatized on the page; a complete emotional and causal ending.
- Prohibited elements: A synopsis or chapter-fragment ending; catastrophe used
  only as backdrop; a consequence-free reactor repair; unexplained restoration
  of magic as a deus ex machina.

## Assumptions

- "Much like our own" permits familiar civic institutions, media, transit, and
  family pressures expressed through setting-appropriate language.
- The ancient reactors may be understood imperfectly by the public, but the
  story will make their physical danger legible to the reader.
- "Catastrophic" requires irreversible loss and mass disruption, but does not
  require the extermination of all four million residents.
- Low-impact story specifics not fixed by the prompt will follow current canon
  and the story-specific plan.

## Completion tests

- The result is a complete short story, not a synopsis or chapter fragment.
- The central promise of the prompt is dramatized and resolved.
- The story passes canon and continuity review.
- Every character-facing name and alias is checked against `stories/NAMES.md`;
  any deliberate reuse has a documented narrative reason.
