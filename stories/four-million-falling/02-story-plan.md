# Four Million Falling — Story Plan

## Story promise and delivery target

An ancient floating city loses all six reactors after a preventable electrical cascade. A lift engineer has roughly fifty minutes to stop more than four million people from striking the sea at lethal speed. She cannot restore the city; she can only spend its remaining stored lift, destroy inhabited sections to shed mass, and choose what kind of ruin will reach the water.

- **Length:** 2,500–4,000 words; aim for 3,400.
- **POV and tense:** Close third person, past tense, entirely through Inez Arco. No cutaways or access to another mind.
- **Genre/tone:** Urgent science-fantasy disaster drama; technically legible, intimate, and grave without nihilism.
- **Audience:** Teen / PG-13. Show impact, fire, injuries, loss, and fear concretely but without graphic gore.
- **Complete movement:** uncontrolled fall -> costly controlled descent -> permanent sea landing, mass displacement, and Inez's acceptance that saving lives requires surrendering the wonder she maintained.

## Story-specific style profile

- **Dominant voice:** Practical, compressed, and bodily. Inez sorted danger by load, vibration, temperature, and time; narration borrows her habit of turning panic into quantities, then lets human details break through those quantities.
- **Distance:** Very close during alarms, choices, and landing; pull back only to compress movement between control stations and public instructions. Never become a citywide omniscient montage.
- **Dialogue pressure:** Speech competes with alarms, delay, partial information, authority, and loved ones wanting incompatible actions. Technical language is used to decide, resist, or conceal—not lecture.
- **Image sources:** Weight in knees and teeth; current as heat and pressure; the city skyline losing its level; ordinary objects sliding; old civic mosaics of dead magicians contrasted with scorched switchgear. Recur on the horizon as a level Inez first trusts, then must use instead of instruments.
- **Restraint:** Keep the lift mechanism observable and bounded. No reactor-as-bomb imagery, no magical awakening, no polished thematic speeches, and no metaphor in every paragraph. One dry failed joke is enough.
- **Intentional emphasis:** The timetable may appear in scene headings or terse display readouts to sustain procedural urgency, but the ending returns to sensory aftermath rather than a countdown epigram.

## Setting and mechanism

Orison is the culture's celebrated “greatest wonder,” an ancient city of **4,083,612 residents** cruising above the Inner Sea in an otherwise modern-like, low-magic society. School mosaics and common sayings teach that magic died centuries ago; this is local belief, never narrator-confirmed cosmology. The city's builders and exact era remain unresolved.

Six old fission reactors feed one inherited high-energy bus. That bus powers converters serving six concentric, segmented lift rings under the city. The rings are inert, nonliving technology: they maintain a locally bounded momentum-exchange field coupled to the world's magnetic field. Each ring stores substantial field energy, so loss of power causes a measurable coast-down rather than instant free fall. Segment switches can only redirect or bleed what remains; they cannot create energy or restore hover.

Initiating chain: Reactor Four suffers a primary-coolant rupture and scrams. During the turbine trip, damaged generator insulation arcs onto the common bus. Isolation commands are issued, but the six bus breakers share an old actuator rail left bridged after unavailable replacement parts; none opens. The fault drags down every generator, trips the remaining five reactors, burns the main bus, and cuts normal power to lift converters and much reactor cooling. The cores shut down, but decay heat continues; emergency cooling starts unevenly, with steam release, local fire, and contamination risk. The reactors do not detonate. The dead bus cannot be repaired inside the emergency window.

At the opening altitude and mass, automatic lift decay becomes lethal in about fifty minutes. Control batteries can operate ring switches but cannot recharge fields. Inez's viable plan is to evacuate and detach the lower freight-and-housing platforms called the Low Keels, shedding enough mass to make the stored field sufficient; then sequence ring bleed to hold the city level and reserve the outer rings for final deceleration. Success means a hard but survivable landing in the Inner Sea. It also means destroying homes and utilities, drowning the lower city, contaminating a restricted sea zone, and displacing every survivor.

## Characters, pressure, and arc

### Inez Arco — principal viewpoint; lift-balance engineer

- **Want:** Restore stable hover, keep Orison intact, and personally get her sister Paz out of a Low Keel clinic.
- **Need:** Accept that control is now the choice of loss, not the prevention of loss; act for four million people without pretending the cost is reversible or impersonal.
- **Pressure/blind spot:** She approved temporary operation with the breaker actuator rail bridged because the individual protections had always tested green. She treats systems as solvable partly to avoid admitting her complicity and her fear for Paz.
- **Arc:** denial through repair -> recognition that restart is impossible -> proposes destruction she would once have called unacceptable -> tells Paz the full cost instead of promising rescue -> commits the remaining lift to everyone rather than hoarding it for her sister's district -> survives able to name both the lives saved and the home lost.
- **Dialogue profile:** Usually seeks usable facts and compliance through exact questions and clipped conditional clauses; names times, loads, and components precisely but keeps guilt and family fear vague. When resisted, she shifts from technical authority to blunt disclosure, then asks rather than orders. With Paz she begins falsely reassuring and overexplaining, then becomes plain and lets silence stand. She would not naturally deliver a polished moral lesson or call the city “alive.”

### Chief Damas — reactor-and-lift control shift chief

- **Function:** Inez's operational counterweight and the person with authority to order mass shedding.
- **Dialogue profile:** Wants coordinated action and controlled information; uses short imperatives, role names, and confirmation loops, presenting calm as a public duty. He avoids saying that the city is already lost. Resistance makes him first invoke procedure, then bargain for a smaller sacrifice, and finally state consequences without euphemism. With Inez he permits technical shorthand but cuts off self-accusation until decisions are made. He would not speculate about magic or ancient builders.

### Paz Arco — Inez's younger sister; clinic logistics coordinator in a Low Keel

- **Function:** Makes mass shedding personal and carries the civilian view of evacuation.
- **Dialogue profile:** Wants a truthful route and enough time to move patients; speaks in concrete people-and-place units rather than engineering abstractions. She refuses consolation that steals planning time. When Inez evades, Paz challenges the premise, then switches to rapid questions and assigns Inez a promise she can actually keep. With Inez, irritation is a form of intimacy. She would not ask to be singled out or summarize Inez's redemption for her.

## Causal scene sequence

### 1. The floor disagrees — 0:00–0:06 (about 400 words)

Open during a routine load-transfer test as a cup and a hanging calibration weight lean the wrong way. Reactor Four alarms: coolant loss, scram, generator trip. Inez reaches the lift board; the isolation lamps show commanded-open while current still crosses the common bus.

The arc propagates. Remaining reactor outputs vanish in sequence, lights brown out, and Orison drops just enough to make millions feel it. Inez wants Damas to authorize a bus retry; Damas wants her to prove the lift rings are intact before he spends control power. **Incompatible aims:** restore generation now versus diagnose the fall. **Tactic change:** after Damas refuses, Inez stops arguing procedure and demonstrates from phase traces that the isolation hardware—not the reactors' chain reactions—linked the failures. End on the coast-down estimate stabilizing at fifty-one minutes.

### 2. No restart to save — 0:06–0:15 (about 500 words)

Inez checks the scorched bus gallery remotely and confirms physical destruction. All six reactors are shut down; two have cooling complications, but lift—not radiation—is the immediate citywide clock. Transit halts, emergency messages flood phones, and hospitals switch to reserves. The four-million scale appears through overloaded shelter routes and districts reporting incompatible tilt.

Inez wants Damas to let her cross-connect an emergency generator for a partial recharge; Damas wants to preserve that power for reactor cooling. **Incompatible aims:** buy lift time versus prevent core damage and release. **Tactic change:** Inez starts with equations, then concedes cooling power and asks what stored field they still own. Reversal: there is enough energy for a landing only if city mass falls by roughly twelve percent. The removable Low Keels contain housing, freight infrastructure, and Paz's clinic.

### 3. The unacceptable plan — 0:15–0:24 (about 550 words)

Inez models a controlled sea landing: evacuate upward, sever the Low Keels, bleed inner rings to correct tilt, keep the outer pair charged for the final minutes. A full city evacuation is impossible; vertical shelter movement is barely possible. Damas initially demands a smaller cut that preserves the southern housing keel.

Inez wants immediate authorization before descent jams lifts and stairs; Damas wants proof and a politically survivable option. **Incompatible aims:** irreversible action now versus delay for certainty. **Tactic change:** Damas invokes authority and casualty projections; Inez answers by putting the live descent curve beside the slower plan, then acknowledges her signature on the bridged-rail waiver instead of using certainty as a mask. Reversal: Damas authorizes all Low Keels to clear and detach. Public instructions call it an emergency jettison, not a temporary evacuation.

### 4. Paz gets the truth — 0:24–0:34 (about 500 words)

While moving to the manual ring sequencer, Inez calls Paz. The clinic is carrying patients up service stairs because lifts are failing. Inez first tries to direct Paz to a private maintenance route; Paz hears the favoritism and refuses to abandon her group.

Inez wants Paz safe and compliant; Paz wants the actual cutoff time and equal access for her patients. **Incompatible aims:** personal extraction versus collective evacuation. **Tactic change:** Paz moves from anger to operational questions; Inez abandons reassurance, reveals that the Keels will be destroyed, and opens the maintenance route to the whole clinic over emergency broadcast. Their achievable promise is not “I will save you” but “I will hold the keel until your stairwell clears.” End with Paz reporting the last bed moving as the connection fails.

### 5. Spend the wonder — 0:34–0:49 (about 850 words)

The Keels fail to release evenly; an old mechanical latch holds the clinic sector. The city tilts, structures tear, fires start, and at least one detached platform strikes the sea. Harm stays PG-13: distant collapses, injured arrivals, empty spaces on headcounts.

Inez wants to delay all shedding until Paz confirms; Damas wants the ring sequence kept inside its last survivable envelope. **Incompatible aims:** one uncertain stairwell versus the remaining city. **Tactic change:** Damas first orders, then gives Inez the exact seconds and leaves the choice to her; Inez stops seeking another calculation. She commands a destructive overload through one inner ring segment, breaking the stuck latch and permanently burning that ring. The sector falls. Paz's status remains unknown.

With mass reduced, Inez manually stages field bleed. A damaged switch freezes and the city rolls; she sacrifices a second segment to level against the visible horizon. Final reversal: the outer rings have less reserve than telemetry claimed. Inez shortens the gentle phase and commits everything to one final deceleration pulse rather than preserve any field for post-contact buoyancy. Orison hits the Inner Sea hard, floods from below, and stops falling.

### 6. A city that cannot rise — aftermath (about 500 words)

Inez wakes or regains focus in a canted control room partly above water. The reactors remain shut down but demand long-term cooling; smoke and steam mark lost districts. Emergency counts are incomplete. The city is irreparably grounded, lower infrastructure gone, water and power failing, and more than four million survivors must be evacuated by sea over days and permanently resettled. There have been deaths; do not assign a neat final total during immediate chaos.

Damas wants Inez to rest and let responders search; Inez wants Paz's answer and to keep working. **Incompatible aims:** acknowledge bodily limit versus regain control through labor. **Tactic change:** after Damas refuses her another console, Inez asks him simply whether the clinic stairwell cleared. A responder's list or delayed message confirms Paz reached an upper shelter with some patients; others are missing. Relief does not erase the sector Inez dropped.

End with Inez outside, watching the sea pass through streets that had never known tides. The civic mosaic said magic had died and Orison alone had defied the world; no magic returns, and the city does not rise. Inez joins a hand-to-hand line moving water and patients toward rescue boats. The final image is the horizon level at last while the ruined city rests crooked against it.

## Stakes, reversals, and ending checks

- **Personal:** Paz's clinic and Inez's responsibility for the maintenance compromise.
- **Relational:** Inez must stop using expertise to make false promises; Paz must trust her with a terrible cutoff.
- **Civic:** 4,083,612 people cannot leave in fifty minutes; attitude control, shelter access, reactor cooling, and mass shedding determine survival.
- **Irreversible:** Low Keels destroyed; at least two lift segments burned out; deaths and missing people; reactor-site damage and contamination control; Orison permanently grounded and population displaced.
- **Major reversals:** isolation fails across all six units; bus cannot be repaired; restart would steal cooling; survival requires shedding inhabited structures; latch failure forces direct sacrifice; telemetry overstates final reserve.
- **Ending resolution:** The fall becomes a controlled sea landing, Paz's immediate fate is answered without universal reassurance, and Inez acts within consequence rather than restoring the old order.

## Canon constraints and local inventions

- One bounded, chronologically unresolved culture on the shared physical world; no separate planet or forced link to another era.
- “Magic died” appears only as Orison education, idiom, civic art, or character belief. No omniscient extinction claim and no restoration.
- Reactors, bus, alarms, controls, and lift rings are nonliving and nonmagical. They exhibit fixed behavior, not intention or moral agency.
- Orison's superlative is local civic belief. No connection to Queen Street, rune shoes, the Glass Gate, Solstice, crowns, bells, or any prior city/system.
- **Bounded inventions only:** Orison; Inner Sea placement; population; six-reactor/common-bus layout; bridged actuator rail; segmented stored-field lift rings; named local jobs and districts; the failure cascade; the sea landing and its losses. These remain plan-local proposals, not canon.
- Later prose should conservatively frame detailed nuclear behavior and preserve distinctions among scram, decay heat, cooling loss, fire, contamination, and lift failure.
