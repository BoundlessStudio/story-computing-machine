## Canon finding

The prompt can proceed without a retcon or user ruling. Its required elements fit current authority if:

1. The fantasy society occupies one bounded, currently unnamed era or culture of the shared physical world, not a newly established separate planet or universal cosmology.
2. “Magic has died out” remains the society’s long-standing belief and an ordinary-life condition, as captured in the prompt contract, rather than an objective claim that all magic everywhere ceased to exist.
3. The floating city is that society’s greatest recognized wonder, not an objective ranking across every era and canon culture.
4. The reactors and lift infrastructure are nonliving technological systems unless the story explicitly establishes a living participant supplying any genuinely magical agency.
5. The reactor failures causally produce the on-page catastrophe, irreversible loss, and mass disruption; neither the reactors nor magic provide a cost-free reset.

No current authoritative source establishes a floating city, nuclear reactor system, city of more than four million people, or mechanism connecting reactor output to levitation. These are canon omissions available for bounded invention, not contradictions or existing canon.

## Planning guidance

- Place the story in one new, unnamed era or leave its exact era unresolved.
- Frame extinct magic through education, civic belief, religion, idiom, or the viewpoint character’s understanding rather than omniscient cosmological declaration.
- Define the city’s lift mechanism, reactor dependency, redundancy, and failure timeline before designing scenes.
- Make the four-million scale visible through specific systems: district loads, transit saturation, emergency messaging, hospitals, shelters, evacuation capacity, and the viewpoint character’s immediate relationships.
- Keep the principal viewpoint close enough that the reader understands the catastrophe through one person’s decisions rather than a detached citywide montage.
- Establish why warnings were missed or accepted without turning “ancient” into an all-purpose explanation.
- Let physical damage arise from an explicit chain: initiating reactor fault, failed mitigation, power or lift degradation, structural response, and human consequences.
- Give specialists incomplete but useful knowledge. Public misunderstanding is plausible, but the reader must understand the practical danger.
- Permit partial success. Saving a district, slowing descent, opening an evacuation path, or preventing a worse release can complete the causal arc while preserving catastrophic loss.
- End with the immediate crisis and principal emotional question resolved, even if long-term rebuilding, resettlement, contamination management, or accountability continues.
