# Promised experience

The request explicitly joins culinary mastery, the stature of the greatest living hero, hundreds of acquired powers, permanent monstrous bodily changes, and an ancient prophecy naming that hero the devourer of the realms. I infer a pleasure in ingenious, abundant power turning unsettling: the same expertise that earned extraordinary victories may be making their destroyer. The prompt leaves voice, relationships, moral verdict, and ending open. “Subtly link this into the realms stories” asks for a light connection; an ingredient bought in Deepmarket from a Runner can supply it without resolving that setting's mysteries.

# Provisional direction

Working title: **The Devourer's Table**. First person, told by the cook Sennet with a practical, sensuous command of food that becomes less reassuring as his body changes. No intimate relationship governs the conflict. These plot choices and limitations are inventions, not user requirements.

- **A victory with an aftertaste.** Sennet saves an inhabited mountain from a stormback, combining a dozen of his 286 powers: catches its falling villages with silk, turns lightning through his antlers, crosses the air on borrowed wings. He then cooks the creature's cloud gland, using low heat and amber citrus bought from a Runner under Deepmarket's crooked awnings to preserve its sheltering faculty rather than its storm. His existing claws catch in the fine work; he remembers which beloved dishes first cost him his human hands. People eat an ordinary celebratory supper under his new weather-shield. He alone eats the legendary portion; pearly breathing slits open along his jaw, permanently taking another familiar piece of his face. His battered recipe book and practiced combinations make the other hundreds feel accumulated and usable, rather than hypothetical.
- **The prophecy finds its portrait.** An old local prophecy, brought to him after this conspicuous rescue, describes his exact composite body, including the new slits, and says the many-made creature will eat seven skies: no road will run beyond its teeth. Its ancient image shows settlements inside a monstrous outline. He has no evidence it is a mistranslation. For the first time, the appetizing mineral scent he catches from an open door frightens him. He considers refusing any further legendary meal.
- **The rescue refusal would abandon.** A living, colossal Hollow Whale is swallowing seven particular, door-connected places whose inhabitants call them realms; no universal geography or relation to Deepmarket's sleeping bearer is explained. Six are visible, intact, through chambers in its flanks; the seventh is being drawn in. Sennet's attempted extraction pulls a deserted cliff into tearing ribbons, and his wound to the Whale cracks that place's sky until he binds the wound shut: simply opening or killing the creature would destroy its held distances. An old pocket-lark faculty lets him transfer one little rock garden intact into his own air-sac, but cannot hold a settlement. This gives him evidence, not certainty, that cooking the Whale's much greater keeping-faculty could move the places into him. Killing it would save the seventh and lose the six. Eating it could save all seven, or give their hungry foretold destroyer everything at once.
- **A kitchen at the end of the roads.** Sennet sets his stove on the Whale's tongue. He holds a landscape apart with immense strength, sends divided bodies to rescue falling people, and breathes frost around its digestive chambers while inventing the preparation. Established culinary distinctions matter: a hard boil extracts dissolution; slow folding can preserve enclosure. But the rising smell makes him begin a reduction whose exquisite flavor would require dissolving an inhabited landscape. For a moment he wants its perfection more than the people whose bells he can hear. He stops, destroys that tempting base, and starts the slower preparation with less time left. The question is now whether his own judgment can be trusted with the power, not just whether he will accept sacrifice. He chooses the meal with evidence of both his capacity to rescue and his capacity to want the catastrophe.
- **A true prophecy and a surviving hero.** He consumes the dish and draws the six swallowed places and the imperiled seventh into himself, preserving their skies and inhabitants while the Whale collapses empty. His human outline is irretrievable: seven suns shine through an immense scaled, feathered body. He has genuinely devoured these realms; he has also saved them. They can still travel between their places, but their outer horizon is now alive and hungry. The ending gives neither a cure nor proof of future corruption: Sennet hears the rescued people calling him their hero, discovers that he can smell how their entire skies would taste, and deliberately turns his great mouth toward the empty dark instead.
