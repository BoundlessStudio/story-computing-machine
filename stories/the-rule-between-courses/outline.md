# Outline

## Story

- Premise and central promise: **All the Good Things** spends one unhurried evening with three adult catfolk friends over beautifully prepared whole fish. In an intimate wooden restaurant, candlelight, attentive service, and live guitar make room for stories, corrected memories, present loves, modest plans, and the pleasure of being known.
- Focal pressure or attachment: Ceret values the trio’s familiarity but fears missed suppers have left only nostalgia. Her impulse to supply everyone’s preferences and favorite stories can crowd out evidence that Mezzara and Dabria have changed.
- Counterforce or complication: No antagonist or concealed problem. Time, affectionate assumptions, imperfect memory, distraction, and mild embarrassment create friction without becoming an argument or manufactured crisis.
- POV, distance, and information limit: Close third-person past through Ceret, tracking ears turned toward speakers or music, tails settling through chair openings, pauses over shared fish, and her mistaken anticipations. Friends’ feelings appear only through speech, gesture, and attention.
- Governing movement and time shape: Linear through arrival, courses, two guitar sets, and lingering conversation. Talk circles from familiar stories into current particulars, then revisits one memory with altered attention. The private turn at the table need not resemble a climax.
- Speculative rule or ordinary-world constraint: Catfolk are ordinary people, not driven by ancestral hunting instincts. This restaurant accommodates feline traits with tail-clear seats, ear-conscious music, warm lighting, and fish meant for leisurely sharing. Establish no universal biology or culture.
- Dialogue promise: Abundant context-rich talk: overlap, unfinished thoughts, mundane questions, differing memories, affectionate correction, listening, and pauses for food or music. Warmth comes through specificity, not polished declarations, constant wit, or revelation quotas.
- Dialogic medium: Spoken table conversation, touch, ear and tail posture, companionable silence, the server’s questions, and instrumental music that redirects attention without delivering a message.
- Dialogue engine: Ceret tries to recover a familiar supper; her friends bring current lives she has not fully heard. Corrections and follow-up questions revise assumptions: Mezzara wants her settled future respected, Dabria wants an offstage partner welcomed sometimes without erasing the trio, and Ceret needs freely given attention rather than ceremonial reassurance.

## Voice

- Narrative texture: Warm, close, and sensuous; follow attention among faces, fish, candlelight, and guitar. Let Ceret’s anticipations briefly precede friends’ replies. Compress service, then slow for listening.
- Conversational texture: Context-bound overlap, mundane questions, topic drift, dropped threads recovered later, gentle teasing, and companionable silence. Not every remark needs subtext or several functions.
- Rhetorical ownership: Ceret organizes stories but answers too quickly. Mezzara favors domestic particulars, dry correction, quiet humor, and patient listening. Dabria approaches requests sideways, misreads pauses, then grows direct. Linette holds gentle social authority; Joia speaks sparingly.
- Pressure behavior: Embarrassment makes Ceret fill pauses, Mezzara become exact when romanticized, and Dabria tangle chronology while watching reactions. Importance does not grant eloquence.
- Relationship movement: Ceret cannot ask whether she is still chosen; attention answers her. Mezzara wants her rooted future respected. Dabria wants her partner included sometimes and the trio retained sometimes. Talk changes knowledge, trust, and later possibilities.
- Anti-default: Avoid polished professionals solving friendship, three symmetrical disclosures, a sagacious server, or music scoring a speech. Preserve forgotten details, uneven fluency, ordinary appetite, affectionate asymmetry, and untested pleasure.

## Beats

1. Ceret arrives first. Establish amber wood, tail-clear chairs, candlelight, low guitar, and whole-fish plates. Linette knows her but does not let familiarity authorize ordering for absent diners; Ceret waits.
2. Mezzara and Dabria arrive carrying incomplete pieces of their days. Talk of weather, errands, clothes, appetite, and a domestic habit restores rhythm through interruption and follow-up, not friendship exposition.
3. Over the first fish, Ceret starts a favorite story about their earliest supper. The others remember different causes and details. Let all versions coexist; correction reveals attention without producing an official account.
4. Joia’s music earns a listening pause. Afterward, Mezzara’s plan for a quiet room nearby emerges through talk of light, pots, and furniture. Ceret stops treating old travel ambitions as Mezzara’s fixed identity and begins asking.
5. The generous main fish anchors relaxed pleasure. Between eating, Linette’s service, and topic drift, Dabria’s domestic partnership and wish to include her partner at a future supper arise without ceremony. Patient questions separate enlargement from replacement; no formal rule results.
6. The others ask about Ceret’s seemingly uneventful months. Their memory of small matters and interest in their outcomes reveals why she arranged dinner: she missed knowing their days. Revisit an earlier anecdote through one newly noticed detail, not a moral.
7. Joia’s later set gives them shared listening and easy silence. Afterward, Mezzara resumes an unfinished minor thread and both friends turn toward her. Stop with talk continuing—no toast, maxim, symbolic keepsake, or promise against change.
