# Opus review: All the Good Things

Independent audit for a later editing pass. It is not a `review.md` verdict,
does not change canon state, and does not authorize edits by itself.

## Verdict

- Dialogue: REVISE — nearly every exchange closes on a correction or a topper, Linette talks like Mezzara, and three Ground slips (the opening order, the unshown "asked properly", the untagged catch-up run).
- Prose: PASS — concrete, varied and warm; one explanatory gloss at L399 and a gutter policy nit.
- Continuity and prompt: REVISE — the recognition beat credits Mezzara with Veslina's cold-wall question; otherwise the rewrite prompt is delivered.
- Edit scope: heavy-dialogue
- Existing review.md said: Verdict PASS, Dialogue PASS; disagree — it missed the L399 misattribution, the ask that never happens, and the pervasive correction rhythm.

The architecture fits the rewrite. Three uneven disclosures (Mezzara's room, Veslina's partner, Ceret's small months) come out through food, music and drift, and the payoff, two friends remembering a basil plant and a clarinet, lands without a speech. The trouble is texture. Almost every exchange runs claim, then correction, then a dry topper, in one exact, uncontracted register that belongs to Mezzara and that Linette, Ceret and sometimes Veslina borrow ("It remains wet." / "A household distinction." / "The soap happened today."). Linette in particular has become the sagacious server the outline warned against, grading Ceret's progress. The edit pass should keep each correction that carries a new fact about someone's life, cut the line that only tops it, give Linette a working server's voice, let Veslina ramble more and quip less, and fix the attribution and referent slips. Each exchange needs only light work (usually one or two lines), but the work touches most exchanges.

## Edit guardrails

- The rewrite request controls: a warm little catfolk restaurant, close friends, beautifully prepared fish, a musician playing softly nearby, and an evening that unfolds mainly through natural conversation (stories, memories, plans, affection, ordinary details of shared lives), sincere and intimate. The removed material stays out: ancestral hunting instinct, the house rule, any hunt or pursuit, Mekko and Talja, hidden danger, games, gimmicks, silliness, crises, twists, manufactured stakes. When you trim toppers, replace them with plainer warmth, not new gags.
- Length band 2,500–4,000 words (currently about 3,100), so trims have room. Broadly accessible. Close third person on Ceret, past tense. Craft profile prospective-2026-08-23.
- Universe: `universe/` has no catfolk entries. Keep the traits story-local (tail gaps in the chairs, ears, fur, liking for warmth) and add no species-wide biology, instinct or culture. No LOCKED or CANON facts are involved.
- Threads that must stay coherent after edits: Ceret orders for others, then Veslina orders the char but with olives, not potatoes (L11–L79); Ceret must ask before moving food (L95, L337–L341); the first-table candle and Ceret's private vindication (L135, L171, L177); the cold wall by Ceret's bed and the "warm wall" in Mezzara's room (L359, L439); the plaster on Mezzara's sleeve leading to the room (L105, L179–L225).
- Keep the disclosures uneven and unceremonious. Add no toast, maxim or summarizing line at the end; the story should still stop mid-conversation over the table layout.
- Do not flatten the humor into solemnity. The target is fewer toppers, not no jokes.

## Dialogue findings

### D1 · Major · Correction-and-topper is the default rhythm

- Where: throughout; worst runs at L61–L71 (tram), L107–L111 (soap), L227–L233 (keys), L243–L255 (green chair), L301–L305 (tea), L311–L317 (songs), L373–L385 (basil, mint)
- Text:
> "It did not come here."
> "Outside was mostly tram smoke."
> "Or one cupboard he has never opened," Mezzara said.
> "It encourages visitors not to linger."
> "A household distinction," Ceret said.
> "Joia had begun."
> "Then it was an old, tired plant."
- Problem: Identical Voices / Tennis Match / epigram closers. Nearly every exchange of the evening is built the same way: someone states something, someone corrects the word or the fact, and a third line tops the correction. Each line makes sense on its own. Together they give four people and the server one dry, exact, uncontracted register, and the constant wit works against the "sincere, intimate" evening the rewrite asked for. The corrections that reveal something (the bell shop, "four autumns ago", eight bowls and a blanket, "Upstairs") drive the story. The toppers that only win the exchange are the tic.
- Fix: Sweep the listed exchanges. Keep a correction when it carries a new fact about someone's life. Cut the line that only tops it, or replace it with a plain answer, a question back, a laugh, an action or silence. Aim for at least half the exchanges to end on something other than a quip. Keep L49, L151, L171, L203, L207, and one of L385 or L397. Cut or plain L111, L233, L255, L305, L315–L317, L377, and whichever of L385/L397 you drop. D3, D5, D6, D4 and D8 cover L39, L187, L213, L263 and L331–L335.

### D2 · Major · Opening: Linette's own question presumes the order she then refuses

- Where: L11–L21, Ceret alone before the others arrive
- Text:
> Linette had just asked whether she should bring the smoked-pepper char when Ceret heard herself say,
> "You may choose water for people who aren't here."
> "Veslina always orders the char."
- Problem: Ground/uptake. Ceret's "Yes ... for Veslina" and her next line ("Veslina always orders the char") only make sense if Linette offered Veslina's usual char. That means Linette herself proposed ordering for an absent friend and then rebukes Ceret for doing it. If Linette meant the char for Ceret, then Ceret's "Yes" orders it for herself and "Veslina always orders the char" no longer follows. Either way, the story's first sentence blurs whose habit is on display, and the whole evening works on that habit.
- Fix: Make Linette's opening question neutral (whether Ceret wants to order now or wait) so the presumption belongs to Ceret alone, and let Ceret volunteer the char, the potatoes and Mezzara's dish. Keep L13–L19 (the refusal and the mullet correction), which pay off when Veslina orders the char with olives at L75–L79. Illustrative only: Linette had just asked whether she wanted to wait for the others when Ceret heard herself say, "No, bring the smoked-pepper char, and the lemon potatoes for Veslina, and Mezzara will want—"

### D3 · Major · Linette speaks in Mezzara's register and grades Ceret

- Where: L21–L39 and L103, Linette before and during the order
- Text:
> "That much I can assume."
> "Still absent."
> "It remains wet."
> "I'll begin with the small gilt fish and fennel. You can practice."
- Problem: Identical Voices / explanatory chorus. With the tags covered, Linette's lines are indistinguishable from Mezzara's: clipped, uncontracted, dry rulings that close each exchange ("It remains wet" tops Ceret's readable excuse about the rain). "You can practice" makes her the scorekeeper of Ceret's lesson, a role that D4 completes. The outline's own anti-default was "a sagacious server", and that is what she has become. Her best characterization is already non-verbal: three menus for one diner, the professionally blank face at L77, the platters turned so the spoons face inward.
- Fix: Keep one dry line (L13 is the strongest) and give her the rest in a working server's voice: short and practical, about food, timing and the room, with warmth shown by what she does. Replace "That much I can assume." with an action, cut "It remains wet.", turn "Still absent." into an ordinary offer of service, and cut "You can practice." Illustrative only: Linette set down the water. "Bread while you wait?"

### D4 · Major · "Ceret has asked properly" refers to an ask that never happens

- Where: L257–L263, the main plates arrive
- Text:
> "And yes, you may all exchange pieces. Ceret has asked properly."
> "Once," Mezzara said.
> "A beginning," Linette replied, already moving toward another table.
- Problem: Ground/referent and uptake, then Punchline Patch. No one asked Linette whether they could exchange pieces, so "And yes" answers nothing on the page. Between Mezzara's condition (L95) and this moment, Ceret never asks anyone's permission, and Linette left the table at L105 ("Once she was gone"). The reader cannot find the ask Linette is approving. The line exists to set up Mezzara's "Once" and Linette's verdict "A beginning", which turns the server into a moral commentator on Ceret's growth.
- Fix: Cut "And yes, you may all exchange pieces. Ceret has asked properly." along with "Once." and "A beginning."; Linette's pepper warning to Veslina is enough, and then she moves on. If the asking thread needs a midpoint before the olive payoff at L337, plant it as a clause in the first-platter paragraph (L129), which already covers "ordinary negotiations": for example, Ceret asking before she takes the last piece of bread. Do not hand the observation back to Linette.

### D5 · Major · What-versus-why ping-pong stalls Mezzara's news

- Where: L179–L189, the tomato salad and the plaster on Mezzara's sleeve
- Text:
> "Plaster," she said.
> "I guessed plaster."
> "You asked why."
> "I assumed you would eventually tell me."
- Problem: Correction ping-pong / inert exchange. For four lines the friends argue about the form of Ceret's earlier question (what the dust was versus why it was there) before Mezzara says anything. It is the purest example of the house register in the story, and it sits at the entrance to one of the three disclosures, so it holds back the news with legal precision instead of feeling. "I assumed" also pushes the theme (Ceret's assumptions) into the words.
- Fix: Let Mezzara answer the why straight away, or give Ceret one plain follow-up instead of the dispute. Illustrative only: "Plaster," she said. "I looked at a room this afternoon." Keep Ceret's unspoken expectation at L191, which already shows her assumption.

### D6 · Major · Mezzara diagnoses Ceret's flaw aloud

- Where: L205–L215, the bells
- Text:
> "I may have remembered the broader meaning," Ceret said.
> "You made it broader. You do that when a story has an untidy edge."
- Problem: Philosopher plus Punchline Patch. The exchange before this already lands: Ceret quotes a romantic vow, Mezzara corrects it to the mundane truth (the bell shop apprentice testing bells before breakfast), and Veslina laughs. Ceret's hedged, legalistic concession exists only to set up Mezzara's tidy diagnosis of her habit. That diagnosis states the story's point about Ceret outright, at the turning point of Mezzara's thread. Ceret's next moves (asking about the alcove, then stopping herself at "There's a building near—") show the change without it.
- Fix: Cut both lines. After Veslina's laugh, go straight to Ceret's question about the morning light at L215, or give Ceret a plain, embarrassed beat (a laugh, or just "Oh."). Keep L203 and L207 intact.

### D7 · Major · The catch-up run loses attribution

- Where: L351–L397, the friends quiz Ceret about her months
- Text:
> "You said it had survived two winters."
> "What did you put in the pot?"
> "The one that tastes like pepper?"
> "Last time it was four."
- Problem: Attribution / Tennis Match. About two dozen lines alternate between Ceret's short answers and questions from two friends. After Veslina's tongue-click (L371), the questions at L375, L379 and L383 have no tag or beat, so the reader cannot tell whether Mezzara or Veslina is asking. The payoff at L399 depends on knowing which friend remembered which detail, and it gets that wrong (see C1). The rapid-fire form suits the moment. The lost speakers and the unbroken alternation do not.
- Fix: Keep the quick pace, but tag or beat-attribute L375, L379, L383 and L395, and make the L387 beat clear about who speaks. Break the metronome once: let the two friends overlap, or let one question go unanswered while Ceret eats. Keep at most one joke answer out of "That was the other mint..." and "The fifth was a difficult week" (see D1). Make every tag agree with the corrected L399.

### D8 · Minor · Veslina's ask ends in a thesis sentence, and "that sentence" drifts

- Where: L307 and L329–L335, Veslina's partner
- Text:
> I couldn't find a way to ask for both without making one sound like the apology for the other.
> "It took you four months to produce that sentence."
> "The soap happened today."
- Problem: After two lovely tangled attempts (L273, L285), Veslina's key request ends in a perfectly balanced sentence that names the emotional mechanism outright (the outline's idea of enlarging the friendship rather than replacing it). "I wanted you to meet them. And I wanted this sometimes. Still." already says it in her voice. Later, Mezzara's "that sentence" most naturally points at Veslina's line just before it (L327), not at the request, and the four-line coda (four months, soap, today) is another chain of toppers.
- Fix: Keep "I wanted you to meet them. And I wanted this sometimes. Still." Cut the balanced sentence, or let her start it and lose the thread in her usual run-on way. Point Mezzara's line at the whole ask and cut L331–L335. Illustrative only: Mezzara passed her the char. "You've been trying to say that since spring." That keeps the quiet signal that Mezzara already knew.

### D9 · Minor · Mezzara's inventory line recaps the evening

- Where: L343–L349, the conversation turns to Ceret
- Text:
> "We have supplied rooms, keys, poor chairs, soap, and a person who abuses tea. You've told us nothing."
- Problem: A writerly catalog that sums up the story's earlier topics as a joke list. It reads as the author tidying up, not as a friend turning the conversation.
- Fix: Keep "What about you, Ceret?" and the plain charge. Illustrative only: "You've let us talk all night. You've told us nothing."

### D10 · Minor · Small line-level slips

- Where: L97–L101, L105–L111, L309–L311, plus tags at L101, L169, L267
- Text:
> "Recently," Ceret amended.
> then complained that the rose scent was much stronger in the warm room than it had been in the shop.
> "It smelled like orange peel outside."
> She began with the easiest.
> "I do not."
- Problem: (1) After the friends' look, "Recently," can mean either "I haven't asked recently" or an amended question ("when have I not asked, recently?"). The look already carries the joke, and "amended" is a said-bookism. (2) The narration says the rose soap smelled weaker in the shop, while Veslina says it smelled of orange peel outside, and the two accounts don't match. (3) "She began with the easiest" implies one of the questions just listed (work, family, how they met), but the question she asks is none of them. (4) "I do not." is Mezzara's uncontracted register in Veslina's mouth. (5) "amended" (L101), "decided" (L169) and "prompted" (L267) label what the lines already show.
- Fix: (1) Cut "Recently," Ceret amended, or give Ceret a plain concession. (2) Make the narration agree with Veslina (e.g. "than it had on the street"). (3) Change it to something like "She asked none of them." (4) Loosen it into Veslina's voice ("I don't!"). (5) Use "said" or no tag.

## Prose findings

### P1 · Minor · Narration explains the recognition

- Where: L399, after the catch-up laughter
- Text:
> She had called the months empty because no single event seemed large enough to set in the middle of the table.
- Problem: The run of questions has already shown that the friends kept track of Ceret's small life. This sentence explains what the exchange just meant. The two concrete "remembered" sentences that follow it do the work once they are corrected (see C1).
- Fix: Cut the sentence or reduce it to a clause, and let the corrected concrete details carry the beat. Edit it together with C1.

### P2 · Minor · Street gutter as incidental water infrastructure

- Where: L153, the first-table memory
- Text:
> "Because you set it in the gutter."
- Problem: Policy nit. A street gutter is drainage infrastructure, which `universe/style-guide.md` "No default water infrastructure" excludes from authorized revisions even as passing texture. The prompt did not ask for it.
- Fix: "Because you set it in a puddle." or "on the wet stones." Keep the logic that the blanket got wet when it was used to catch the bowl.

## Continuity and prompt findings

### C1 · Blocking · The recognition credits Mezzara with Veslina's question

- Where: L399 (with L359), after the catch-up quiz
- Text:
> Veslina tucked one foot beneath her chair. "Did you move your bed away from the cold wall?"
> Yet Mezzara remembered the wall by her bed. Veslina remembered the boy's four notes.
- Problem: An internal contradiction at the story's emotional pivot. Veslina asks about the bed and the cold wall (L359, and the untagged shoulder question at L363 follows from her), but the narration says Mezzara remembered it. The details Mezzara remembered were the sink window, the damp patch, the basil and the Thursday suppers. A reader who notices loses trust at exactly the moment the story asks them to believe each friend kept track.
- Fix: Correct the narration rather than the tags, so the final "warm wall" echo (L439) still reads as Ceret's own concern. Illustrative only: Yet Mezzara remembered the basil. Veslina remembered the wall by her bed and the boy's four notes. Check the D7 tags against whichever version you choose.

### C2 · Minor · Mezzara asks about the sink window twice

- Where: L105 and L351
- Text:
> Mezzara asked if the loose window above Ceret's sink had finally been fixed.
> "Did they fix the window over your sink?" Mezzara asked.
- Problem: In the early summary Mezzara already asks this question. At L351 she asks it again as if for the first time, with no sign that Ceret dodged it before. It reads as a slip, not a thread picked up again (unlike the plaster question, which L179 explicitly returns to).
- Fix: Replace the window in the L105 summary with another small question, so that L351 opens the quiz fresh. Alternatively, mark L351 as a repeat question.
