# Story plan

## Story promise

A younger Dorram reports that a routine stone-parting working released while he was shaping it and somebody else spoke its designated phrase. The college board of magical researchers seals his workroom and orders a controlled demonstration: if the report is true, an unfinished working can be divided across two living participants; if it is false or carelessly framed, teaching laboratories may change their safeguards around an illusion.

Dorram can reproduce the accident only by giving up the two claims he most wants to protect—that he understands it and that it is his alone. With records technician Melek and an exacting board chair, he establishes that a living shaper can prepare one incomplete working and an appointed living caller can close it with a present audible phrase through a clear sound path during a brief ready interval. Stored sound, an unappointed speaker, a blocked path, and a late call fail. A second shaper repeats the paired procedure. Nobody discovers why it works.

The board records the primitive ancestor of the later callwright practice, credits both roles, and still privileges self-calling as complete competence. Dorram accepts the cautious compromise, learning correction by evidence while retaining the self-sufficiency bias Kethra later exposes. Her lattice, rescue, qualification, and safety practice remain wholly hers.

## Story controls

- **Projected prose:** 3,400 words across six scenes; acceptable range 2,500–4,000.
- **POV and tense:** Close first person from Dorram; past tense. He is explicitly the same Dorram who later serves as chief examiner in `voice-of-silence`, but no exact age, date, or interval is stated.
- **Tone and rating:** Institutional fantasy, experimental tension, dry procedural wit, and serious conflict over uncertainty and credit; general adult, non-graphic.
- **Setting scope:** One unnamed fantasy-era college, workroom, and hearing chamber. The board and its practice are local, not a universal magical authority.
- **Central effect:** The novelty is distribution of one working's necessary acts across two living people, not a newly universal power or a grand physical feat.
- **Ending condition:** The effect is real and independently repeated under the demonstrated conditions. Ultimate cause and outer limits remain unresolved.

### Primitive paired-working protocol

1. `Parting Line` lets a trained living shaper map and power one short opening through a touched, palm-sized stone tile. It cannot act beyond that tile or sustain a structure.
2. In the ordinary self-called control, the shaper holds the unfinished pattern and says its designated two-word release phrase. That familiar control succeeds.
3. In the paired procedure, the shaper appoints one living person for this one open pattern, explains the act, and receives willing acceptance. Appointment is witnessed practice, not magical proof of consent.
4. Both touch the same flat brass contact during appointment. It marks geometry and conducts nearby sound but supplies no power, choice, permission, or voice.
5. Once the shaper marks readiness by turning a white timing bead, the appointed caller must audibly say the complete designated phrase on fresh living breath before the bead completes eight counted drops. This is an observed local window, not the mature twelve-heartbeat rule.
6. Open air works at close range. A brass listening tube also works while unobstructed, but a closed shutter breaks the sound path. The tube carries sound only and is not yet a mature speaking rail.
7. An echo vial, unappointed speaker, shut path, or late call fails; the pattern collapses without splitting the tile.
8. Failed holds numb the shaper's fingers and diminish precision. Repeated calls roughen Melek's throat, so attempts are limited. These are local costs, not universal prices.
9. The matrix establishes necessary witnessed conditions, not whether breath, sound, appointment, touch, timing, attention, or their conjunction is causal.

## Character engine and stakes

### Dorram

- **Want:** Preserve his research standing by demonstrating a discovery important enough to bear his authorship.
- **Need:** Treat an honest operational boundary and shared causality as achievement, rather than filling uncertainty with theory or reducing Melek to a variable.
- **Pressure:** The board controls the apparatus and number of attempts; collapsed patterns impair his hands; Melek can refuse; transfer to another shaper threatens his claim to uniqueness.
- **Blind spot:** He understands that two contributions can be necessary, yet still finds self-sufficient performance easier to trust and certify.
- **Arc:** He moves from “my working, my accident” to a report with two living roles, two participant names, and an unresolved cause. His growth is real but incomplete: he accepts the board's supervised-pairing/solo-competence compromise, setting up his later error with Kethra.

### Melek

- **Want:** Keep the record accurate and avoid being made either the culprit or a piece of acoustic equipment.
- **Pressure:** Dorram needs their voice; the board initially summons them as a witness; repeated calls have a bodily cost; refusing may let Dorram or the institution write the incident without them.
- **Function:** Their exact memory identifies deliberate appointment as a missing condition. Their choice to participate, pause, and repeat makes the second living role concrete without making speech a measure of personhood.
- **Movement:** Melek begins prepared to give testimony and leave. Dorram's corrected protocol earns their cooperation; their successful work with a second shaper proves the procedure is not merely Dorram's private anomaly.

### The unnamed chair

- **Want:** Convert an alarming report into a bounded finding safe enough to teach, reject, or study.
- **Pressure:** A true result changes laboratory risk; a premature theory can become doctrine; fatigue limits the test matrix.
- **Function:** A corrigible institutional antagonist who separates reproduction from explanation, changes participants, and concedes shared causality while retaining a conservative self-call standard.

### Stakes

- **Immediate:** Safe handling of live incomplete patterns during a limited test; Dorram's and Melek's bodily limits.
- **Personal:** Dorram's credibility and Melek's agency, labor, and place in the record.
- **Institutional:** Whether the college learns the correct operational hazard and recognizes a caller as a living participant rather than apparatus.
- **Intellectual:** Whether uncertainty is recorded as uncertainty without erasing a real, reproducible effect.

## Compact story-specific style profile

- **Dominant voice:** Dorram narrates with young professional confidence, dry exactness, and a habit of noticing wording before feeling. His wit comes from procedural absurdity and self-protective qualification, not constant quips.
- **Narrative distance:** Stay close enough to catch each rationalization about credit and certainty. Move closest when a pattern numbs his hands, when Melek refuses the horn, and when he releases his notes to another shaper. Pull back only to compress room preparation and repeated reset work.
- **Sentence movement:** Clear medium-length procedural sentences for setup; longer syntax when Dorram tracks several possible variables; short sentences only for actual failures, success, or a refused assumption.
- **Image sources:** Chalk lines in stone grain, white timing beads, brass warming under living hands, breath on a horn rim, ruled columns, corrections in wet ink. Let the clean split tile and the divided report accumulate meaning.
- **Emotional register:** Dorram intellectualizes embarrassment and fear until his choices expose them. Melek's care appears through accurate recall and disciplined limits, not reassurance. The chair's respect appears as changed procedure, not praise.
- **Dialogue pressure:** Motivate technical language through risk, testimony, or leverage. Speakers may reject premises or use apparatus handling as a reply; no one recites project ethics.
- **Intentional restraint:** Do not turn voice into cosmic metaphor, rank speech above other communication, or flash forward. Let Dorram, the local roles, and the institutional standard create the prequel link.

### Major-speaker dialogue profiles

- **Dorram — dialogue profile:** Wants confidence, time, and recognition; defaults to qualified lectures and exact causal vocabulary. He names apparatus and measurements precisely but keeps fear and dependence vague. With Melek he becomes familiarly assumptive; with the chair he becomes formal and over-complete. Resistance makes him argue theory, then narrow the claim, and finally admit what he does not know. Dry jokes are defensive and often go unanswered. He would not naturally offer a polished emotional confession.
- **Melek — dialogue profile:** Wants an accurate record and concrete limits; defaults to short observational statements, remembered wording, and practical questions. They name who did what precisely and decline cosmological claims. With Dorram they interrupt abstractions with the incident sequence; with the chair they use full, careful testimony. When pressed, they stop touching the tube or withdraw cooperation before raising their voice. Their humor is sparse and literal. They would not call themself Dorram's instrument or claim to have shaped his working.
- **The chair — dialogue profile:** Wants a testable claim and bounded risk; defaults to clipped questions, definitions, and procedural alternatives. The chair is precise about evidence but vague about prestige. When Dorram lectures, the chair narrows to yes/no conditions; when success arrives, the chair changes participants instead of celebrating; when credit is challenged, the chair bargains through report language and certification scope. The chair does not sneer, offer pity, or claim certainty beyond the matrix.

## Causal scene sequence

### Scene 1 — The demand, not the explanation (450 words)

Open in the hearing chamber with Dorram's sealed incident tile on the table: it split along his mapped line although Melek, not Dorram, said the phrase. The chair asks who cast the working. Dorram's answers keep changing grammatical subject because the available categories assume one practitioner. He offers several causal theories; the chair points out that none was tested.

The demand is credible: an unfinished working a nearby voice can release may endanger teaching rooms, while an unsupported report may wrongly implicate Melek. By the next bell Dorram must reproduce it with board-selected controls. The chair distinguishes showing recurrence conditions from explaining why they work. Uncertainty neither excuses failure nor needs disguising as certainty.

**Dialogue pressure:** Dorram wants delay and recognition for a theoretical discovery; the chair wants a falsifiable operational claim and containment. Dorram begins by lecturing, shifts to attacking the deadline, then accepts a narrower demonstration after the chair offers either testing or withdrawal of the report. The chair shifts from open questions to exact conditions and a fixed attempt limit.

### Scene 2 — The missing participant (600 words)

In the sealed workroom, Dorram reconstructs the accident with Melek from chalk marks, the original contact plate, and their separate notes. He talks about reproducing “his” working and asks Melek to stand at the listening tube. Melek refuses to touch it while the protocol lists them only as witness and vocal control.

Their disagreement supplies information through conflict. Melek recalls that before the accident Dorram had specifically asked them to read the release phrase back when he turned the timing bead; they had agreed, both of them touching the plate while making room over the crowded bench. Dorram had omitted the exchange from his causal diagram because he considered it ordinary laboratory coordination. Appointment, acceptance, shared contact, timing, present sound, and the tube are now separate variables.

Dorram first reassures Melek that the effect cannot draw power from them, which answers the wrong concern. When Melek starts packing their notes, he changes tactic: he adds their name as a participating researcher, discloses the failed-pattern and throat-strain risks, gives them authority to end their involvement between attempts, and writes a limited control matrix. Melek agrees to three fresh calls, not an indefinite demonstration. The protocol now predicts failures before success; this keeps the board test scientific rather than lucky repetition.

**Dialogue pressure:** Dorram wants rapid cooperation and sole intellectual framing; Melek wants accurate credit, disclosed risk, and control over their participation. He moves from reassurance to rank-pulling to a concrete revision of the protocol. Melek moves from questions to physical refusal, then bargains with a call limit and their name on the plan.

### Scene 3 — What does not work (650 words)

The board supplies untouched tiles, reseals the table, and inspects the inert brass. Dorram first performs the ordinary control: he shapes, powers, targets, and speaks `Parting Line` himself; the tile opens cleanly. The board therefore knows the basic working and his ability are real.

Run three controls with visible resets. Dorram's recorded phrase in an echo vial fails. Melek's live but unappointed phrase fails. After appointment, Melek speaks while the chair closes the tube shutter; room sound cannot reach the shaped contact, and the tile stays whole. Each collapse numbs Dorram's fingers. The chair pauses to check his precision and Melek's limit.

These failures are successes for the matrix, but they feel publicly like collapse. Dorram starts embellishing a theory about breath and brass. Melek corrects him: the tests have excluded substitutions in this setup, not proven which living or acoustic property causes release. He accepts the correction in front of the board and requests the full-condition attempt.

**Dialogue pressure:** Dorram wants failed controls treated as support; the chair wants to prevent post-hoc interpretation; Melek wants observed facts kept separate from theory. Dorram shifts from explanation to citing predictions. The chair shifts from skepticism to a safety pause. Melek shifts from silent participation to direct correction when Dorram overclaims.

### Scene 4 — Repetition requires replacement (850 words)

For the full attempt, Dorram maps a fresh tile, openly appoints Melek for that one pattern, hears their acceptance, and shares the contact plate during the appointment. At the turned bead, Melek speaks the complete phrase on living breath through the open tube inside eight drops. The tile splits along Dorram's exact mapped line. The effect is real: Melek neither supplied the power nor chose the target, and Dorram did not make the releasing sound.

The chair observes that Dorram and Melek may share an idiosyncratic interaction. Another trained shaper must reproduce the procedure. Dorram argues that surrendering the pattern changes too many variables; privately he fears surrendering authorship. Melek sets down the horn: without transfer, the result remains only a story about Dorram.

The chair already knows the ordinary `Parting Line` working. Dorram must hand over his annotated sequence and become an observer. The chair shapes a board-selected tile, appoints Melek, receives acceptance at the brass contact, turns the bead, and holds the incomplete pattern. Melek uses their final agreed call. The second tile splits within the mapped boundary. The chair's different shaping color/texture and Melek's unchanged empty hands make the division legible: living shaper supplies one contribution; living caller supplies another; inert brass supplies neither.

This is the dramatic and intellectual climax. The full conditions have been repeated with a second shaper without explaining their ultimate cause. Dorram's method becomes stronger exactly when the central magic is no longer his to perform.

**Dialogue pressure:** Dorram wants the first success accepted as sufficient and his role kept central; the chair wants participant transfer; Melek wants a claim that does not depend on treating them as Dorram's property. Dorram shifts from methodological objection to coaching the chair precisely. The chair shifts from examiner to exposed participant. Melek shifts from withholding the horn to accepting one final call once both roles and limits are explicit.

### Scene 5 — The useful wrong lesson (550 words)

At the ruling table, the board accepts only what was shown: paired release is locally reproducible under the witnessed conjunction; stored sound and the tested broken conditions failed; causation and outer limits remain open. The chair proposes a conservative report naming Dorram as discoverer and Melek as an acoustic control, with future accreditation reserved for practitioners who can shape and self-call.

That wording offers Dorram the career-saving version he wanted. Melek prepares to leave rather than sign it. Dorram chooses the harder record. He points to the chair's own successful working: the chair's pattern did not release without Melek's living act, so “control” is false to the evidence. He does not claim equal shaping; he insists on distinct necessary contributions.

The negotiated ruling creates provisional lowercase roles, `shaper` and `caller`, records Dorram and Melek as co-participants, and requires both names on paired tests. It permits paired research only under supervision while treating self-calling as complete independent competence. Dorram finds that defensible on thin safety evidence. Melek is not erased, but the institution carries forward the bias that shapes his later examination of Kethra.

**Dialogue pressure:** The chair wants a defensible, conservative classification; Melek wants truthful participation or no signature; Dorram wants approval without falsifying the result. The chair begins with sole-credit language, then bargains through separate role labels and limited authorization. Melek moves from factual objection to leaving. Dorram moves from silence that would benefit him to an evidence-based correction, then accepts bounded supervision rather than demanding premature equivalence.

### Scene 6 — Two columns and a blank (300 words)

In the archive an attendant boxes both cleanly split tiles with the failed whole controls. Dorram and Melek review the final form. Two ruled participant columns now stand where the old form had one practitioner line. Dorram signs under `shaper`; Melek signs under `caller`. Under cause, Dorram crosses out his ornate hypothesis and writes that it remains unresolved.

Do not give them complete reconciliation or make the ruling triumphant. Melek acknowledges the corrected record, not Dorram's feelings. Dorram notices the separate solo-competence seal and does not challenge it. End on the inert brass contact plate stored beside the form, dull between two living names, and the cause line deliberately left open.

**Dialogue pressure:** Dorram wants Melek to recognize the correction as repair; Melek wants the document checked without granting easy absolution. He shifts from explanation to a plain admission that the first report reduced their role. Melek shifts from reserve to signing, then ends the exchange before it becomes a lesson statement.

## Reversals, setup, and payoff

- The board reframes hostile demand as the useful distinction between reproducibility and cause; Melek's “ordinary” memory turns appointment into a tested condition.
- Predicted failures narrow the claim. The first full success proves the accident real; the chair's success as another shaper proves transfer.
- Dorram loses magical centrality but gains a stronger method. The credit error is corrected while the later-harmful self-call standard survives.
- One practitioner line becomes two participant columns; the blank cause line pays off Dorram's struggle with uncertainty.

## Authority and candidate-continuity limits

- Only living people use magic. Stone, ink, bead, echo vial, brass contact, tube, board form, and archive remain inert.
- All findings are local hypotheses or observations. Never claim the world's first divided working, a universal law of speech, or shared ancestry with Gate Cant, Calvea's Calling the Name, Ersene calls, or any other system.
- Spoken activation is one access requirement of this local mechanism, not evidence that speech is superior communication or that non-speaking people possess less agency or magic.
- `voice-of-silence` supplies candidate continuity, not canon. Dorram is the same identity; exact age, interval, era, and institutional governance remain unstated.
- Safe dependencies used: later Dorram can shape and self-call; later practice distinguishes living shaper and caller; present live sound, authorization, timing, and a sound path matter; inert brass only conducts/focuses.
- Do not use or invent Kethra, Ulan, the voiced lattice, three cells, `Weightward`, `Roofstay`, `Airclear`, `Crown Release`, `Safe Descent`, the examination-tower rescue, Kethra's archmage result, caller roster, or mature field-sign procedure.
- Dorram does not invent multi-caller design or solve collaborative safety. His partial acceptance of a solo-competence standard preserves the later need for Kethra's correction and full achievement.
- `Parting Line`, the eight-drop window, the flat contact, listening tube, and board ruling are local inventions proposed by this story only.
