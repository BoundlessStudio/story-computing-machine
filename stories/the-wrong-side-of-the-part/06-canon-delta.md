# Proposed canon delta

This file proposes reusable shared-universe facts. It is not canon until the
user explicitly approves promotion and the facts are added to `universe/`.

## New characters or character facts

- **Tilda Bristow (proposed):** A skilled wigmaker whose final character-facing
  forms are `Tilda Bristow` and `Tilda`. She lives in the
  modern-like era. Tilda is habitually meticulous, accommodating, restrained,
  and conflict-avoidant. She cared for her late mother, kept the family wig
  shop operating, and concealed her wish to keep it while outwardly agreeing
  to its sale.
- **Petra Bristow (proposed):** Tilda's younger sister, whose final
  character-facing forms are `Petra Bristow` and `Petra`, and equal co-inheritor
  of their late mother's wig shop. Petra was absent for much of their mother's
  final illness and seeks an end to the sisters' shared debt and property
  stalemate.
- Relationship proposal: Tilda and Petra are sisters whose false
  consensus about selling the shop conceals grief, resentment, shame, and
  incompatible accounts of care. After Tilda violates Petra's privacy, Petra
  agrees only to a formal accounting and an independent appraisal; forgiveness
  and the shop's future remain unresolved.

These are proposed facts local to this story. They have not been promoted to
canon.

## New rules, capabilities, or costs

- **Local artifact proposal:** A provenance-unknown, old human-hair wig with a
  hand-knotted crown functioned as a nonliving magical focus. No maker, origin,
  prior wearer, connection to another artifact, or broader artifact class was
  established.
- **Wearer-supplied living agency in the observed incident:** Wearing the wig
  plus Tilda's living body heat, pulse, attention, and self-conception
  activated and supplied the focus in her single observed use. In that
  incident it did not speak, choose, resist, or use magic independently. The
  story does not establish what would happen with another wearer, whether the
  focus could be used again, or whether wearing and these activation inputs
  were exclusive limits rather than the conditions observed with Tilda.
- **Coherent inversion:** In Tilda's use, the focus inverted her dominant
  habits, defenses, presentation, and judgment—restraint into flamboyance,
  tidiness into disorder, avoidance into confrontation, and self-erasure into
  ruthless self-interest—without reversing factual knowledge or creating a
  separate person. Tilda retained continuous first-person awareness and memory
  throughout and remained morally responsible for her choices.
- **Visible restyling:** During activation, the wig changed from a drab,
  symmetrical ash-brown bob into long, voluminous copper-black coils with a
  bone-white razor-straight side part. The changed cut, color, texture,
  movement, volume, and presentation visibly expressed Tilda's inverted
  personality. It returned to its original appearance when the effect ended.
- **Local removal condition:** The focus sustained a binary opposition between
  Tilda's ordinary and inverted postures. Tilda loosened it by stating a
  self-owned truth neither posture wanted to accept: that she wanted the shop
  and had expected Petra to know without being asked. Breaking that opposition
  allowed Tilda to remove the wig. This is not a general rule that honesty,
  desire, consent, or confession ends magic.
- **Dismantling and destruction:** Unworking the crown's central and radiating
  knots with ordinary wigmaking tools dismantled the focus. The foundation
  opened into loose human hair and was rendered inert; the artifact did not
  resist as an autonomous being and no intact focus remained.

All of these are mechanism-specific proposals for one destroyed, nonliving
focus. They are not promoted canon and must not be generalized into rules for
other artifacts, transformations, hair, personality, truth, consent, desire,
or magic.

## Timeline events

- **Local proposal — one sale day in the modern-like era:** While the sisters
  were preparing to sign a sale of their inherited shop, Tilda voluntarily
  wore the wig while disbelieving its warning. Under its effect she rejected
  the sale, publicly advertised a spectacle at the shop, disrupted inventory,
  and gave away reserved stock.
- Tilda then played and posted part of Petra's private account of why fear kept
  her away during their mother's illness. The buyer withdrew the offer after
  seeing the public spectacle. Removing the wig did not restore the sale,
  retrieve copied recordings, erase refund demands, settle debt or ownership,
  or repair Petra's damaged trust.
- Later the same afternoon, with Petra present, Tilda dismantled the wig. The
  sisters agreed only to pursue a formal accounting and an independent
  appraisal; no final sale, buyout, reconciliation, or preservation of the shop
  was established.

These are proposed local story events, not promoted canon or a claim about the
modern-like era generally.
