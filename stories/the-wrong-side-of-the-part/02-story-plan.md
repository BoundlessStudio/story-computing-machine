# Story plan

## Premise and promise

A conflict-avoidant wigmaker puts on an allegedly magical wig while clearing
her family shop for sale, becomes her flamboyant, selfish, brutally candid
opposite, and must claim the useful truth inside that inversion without letting
it choose what kind of sister she will be.

The story promises a tactile and unmistakable transformation, darkly comic
escalation driven by the protagonist's own disowned traits, lasting damage that
survives removal, and a final choice that is neither meek restoration nor
submission to the “better” opposite.

## Story controls

- POV: First person, close to Tilda Bristow; the inverted state retains “I” and
  continuous memory but changes diction, attention, rhythm, and judgment.
- Tense: Past.
- Genre/tone: Contemporary fantasy in the modern-like low-magic era; intimate
  family drama with dark comedy, mounting emotional tension, and tactile
  craft detail.
- Target: 3,000–3,800 words, aiming near 3,450.
- Rating: Teen / PG-13. Public humiliation, grief, and sibling conflict are
  intense but non-graphic; no sexual content, graphic violence, or self-harm.
- Central dramatic question: Can Tilda speak and act for herself without
  becoming the person who treats everyone else as expendable?

## Character engine

- **Tilda Bristow, 34:** First-person protagonist and skilled wigmaker. She is
  meticulous, restrained, considerate, risk-averse, and so conflict-avoidant
  that she turns her own choices into favors other people have forced on her.
  She wants to keep the failing family wig shop but has agreed to sell because
  asking for a different outcome would expose her resentment toward Petra.
  Her opening strategy is to be indispensable and silently hope someone notices
  what she needs.
- **Inverted Tilda:** The same continuous person under the wig's effect, not a
  separate entity or named alter. She is flamboyant, untidy, impulsive,
  confrontational, self-promoting, and ruthlessly self-interested. She can say
  what Tilda suppresses and take action Tilda avoids, but she treats private
  knowledge and other people's dignity as tools. Her temptation is real:
  after years of self-erasure, her appetite for agency feels like relief.
- **Petra Bristow, 31:** Tilda's younger sister and equal inheritor of their
  late mother's shop. Practical, impatient, and living elsewhere after being
  absent during much of their mother's final illness. She wants the sale
  completed today so the property, debt, and family stalemate stop controlling
  both sisters. She has mistaken Tilda's assent for consent; she also carries
  shame about leaving and once admitted in a private voice message that fear,
  not indifference, kept her away.
- **Opposition:** Tilda's avoidance, its ruthless inverse, the five o'clock
  sale appointment, and the sisters' incompatible stories about who sacrificed
  and who escaped. The off-page buyer is a deadline and consequence, not a
  character who needs a subplot or name.
- **Stakes:** Selling without protest ends Tilda's vocation and repeats her
  lifelong self-erasure. Letting the inversion decide may preserve the shop but
  destroy Petra's trust, damage both sisters' reputations, and replace one
  evasion with another form of domination.
- **Internal movement:** “If I make myself easy to live with, love will infer
  what I want” becomes “I can state what I want, accept that others may refuse,
  and remain responsible for how I state it.”

## Causal arc

- **Beginning state:** On the shop's final clearing day, Tilda labels every
  item for sale, donation, or rubbish while avoiding the unsigned purchase
  papers. Petra is due at four; the buyer at five. Tilda tells herself the sale
  is fair even as she keeps inventing small packing tasks.
- **Inciting disruption:** A false bottom in their mother's oldest wig case
  reveals a drab ash-brown human-hair wig and a handwritten card: “Wear it and
  meet the person you are not. Heat wakes it. To take it off, tell the truth
  neither of you wants.” Tilda reads this as one last piece of theatrical
  nonsense and voluntarily puts it on to test whether it is donation-worthy
  and to have a joke ready when Petra arrives.
- **First turn:** Warmth and pulse wake the hand-knotted crown. The neat bob
  lengthens into huge, unruly copper-black coils with a white, razor-straight
  side part; the hair moves with swagger instead of lying obediently. Tilda's
  attention and choices invert. She tears up the packing scheme, rejects the
  sale by phone, and posts an invitation from the shop account for a
  four-thirty “funeral for being agreeable.”
- **Escalation:** A curious neighborhood crowd arrives. Inverted Tilda models
  stock, gives away reserved wigs, tells funny but cutting truths about
  customers and the family business, and enjoys being witnessed. These acts
  burn the quiet route back to the scheduled sale.
- **Second turn:** Petra arrives and demands an explanation. Inverted Tilda
  accurately names the unpaid care work and false consensus around the sale,
  then weaponizes Petra's private voice message about abandoning their mother's
  sickroom. She plays it for the crowd and posts it before Petra can stop her.
  The buyer withdraws in writing after seeing the spectacle. Tilda gets what
  she secretly wanted—the sale is dead—at a cost her old self would never have
  chosen.
- **Crisis:** Petra leaves after saying the wig did not invent the cruelty; it
  merely made Tilda enjoy using it. Inverted Tilda prepares to livestream a
  triumphant declaration that she alone owns the shop morally, which would
  further lie about Petra's legal share and make repair harder. The ordinary
  Tilda remains conscious, horrified yet tempted to let the opposite finish
  defending her.
- **Climax:** Tilda uses craft memory to feel the activated crown drawing
  warmth through its knots. She stops the stream and says the truth both her
  compliant self and ruthless inverse resist: “I want the shop, and I wanted
  Petra to know without ever making myself ask.” Owning desire without blaming
  Petra breaks the inversion's perfect opposition. The crown slackens, and
  Tilda removes it herself.
- **Resonant aftermath:** The buyer remains gone and copies of the humiliating
  post remain beyond retrieval. Tilda apologizes to Petra for the exposure
  without pretending her grievance was false. She asks for a formal accounting
  of debt, care labor, and ownership plus an independent appraisal; Petra agrees
  only to that process, not to forgiveness or preserving the shop. Tilda
  unknots the wig's crown at her workbench while Petra watches, rendering the
  local focus inert. Tilda then writes her own desired terms on the back of the
  unsigned sale contract and reads them aloud.

## Setup and payoff

- Tilda's obsessive labels—`SELL`, `DONATE`, `DISCARD`—set up both her desire
  to make ambiguity disappear and the inverse scattering those categories.
  In the aftermath, she writes a fourth category, `DECIDE TOGETHER`, without
  treating it as a magical fix.
- Her knowledge that a hand-knotted crown distributes tension and heat sets up
  her tactile recognition of how the focus activates and her ability to
  dismantle it later. This is craft knowledge, not lore exposition.
- The warning card states activation and the truth-based removal condition
  before either matters. It is incomplete testimony, not automatic proof; Tilda
  disbelieves it and later verifies only the rules shown.
- Petra's private voice message is glimpsed on Tilda's phone early when she
  refuses to replay it. Its later publication is therefore possible without
  newly invented evidence and demonstrates the inverse's cruelty.
- Tilda repeatedly edits wants into apologies in texts. In the climax she says
  one unsoftened first-person sentence that neither blames Petra nor denies her
  own desire.
- The wig begins as an obedient, drab, symmetrical bob; its activated copper-
  black volume, white hard part, restless movement, and refusal to stay pinned
  externalize the opposite personality. When removed it collapses to the
  original bob, then becomes ordinary loose hair as Tilda unknots the crown.
- A salon mirror initially lets Tilda view herself as a client to be managed.
  At the end she turns the chair away from the mirror and speaks directly to
  Petra.

## Canon constraints

- The story occurs in an unnamed contemporary-feeling place in the modern-like
  low-magic trough; it does not establish literal present-day Earth, a real
  city, or a precise global date.
- The wig is a nonliving focus. It stores or shapes magic only while supplied
  through the living wearer's warmth, pulse, attention, and self-conception; it
  does not choose, speak, resist autonomously, or use magic independently.
- All claims remain local to this artifact. Its human-hair crown, activation,
  trait inversion, visible restyling, truth condition, removal, and destruction
  are not rules for all magic, artifacts, hair, consent, desire, or identity.
- Tilda remains one continuous person with memory and awareness. The prose may
  alter voice and judgment but must not treat inverted Tilda as a disposable
  nonperson, blame-free possession, or separate villain.
- Neither the card nor the wig is connected to the Glass Gate, a deity, a
  recurring artifact, a hidden authority, or a universal name/identity system.
- The artifact's maker and provenance remain unrecovered. Mystery may withhold
  origin but may not blur rules that the plot uses.
- The public exposure, withdrawn offer, legal co-ownership, resentment, and
  loss of trust survive removal. The ending begins repair rather than erasing
  consequence.
- Prose stays precise, tactile, varied, non-graphic, and within the recorded
  PG-13 ceiling.

## Proposed inventions

- Tilda and Petra Bristow, their late unnamed mother, and an unnamed
  contemporary family wig shop jointly inherited by the sisters.
- A destroyed local relic: an antique human-hair wig whose nonliving,
  hand-knotted crown stores or shapes magic through a living wearer's warmth,
  pulse, attention, and self-conception.
- The wig inverts dominant habits, defenses, presentation, and judgment while
  preserving continuous awareness and memory; it physically restyles its hair
  to express the inverted personality.
- The recorded local rules: body heat activates it; a self-owned truth that
  neither ordinary nor inverted habits can assimilate breaks the opposition
  enough for removal; unworking the crown's central knots renders the focus
  inert. These are artifact-specific facts, not global laws.
- A single sale-day family conflict in the modern-like era: Tilda's transformed
  actions kill a property purchase and publicly expose Petra's private account
  of her absence during their mother's illness.
- No new faction, deity, recurring institution, global location, cosmological
  event, or connection to existing artifacts.

## Failure modes to watch

- Do not present inverted Tilda as simply evil or secretly “the real Tilda.”
  Her directness, delight, appetite, and refusal to self-erase must be genuinely
  useful and tempting; her instrumental cruelty must remain her responsibility.
- Do not make the ordinary Tilda purely virtuous. Her passivity externalizes
  decisions, conceals resentment, and helps create the false consensus around
  the sale.
- Do not let the wig speak, scheme, tighten itself in resistance, or otherwise
  act as an independent magic user. Physical changes must be described as
  wearer-powered responses.
- Do not use a mirror monologue as a substitute for scenes, or rely on facial
  appearance alone. Show inverted choices, syntax, attention, posture, and the
  wig's cut, texture, motion, and presentation.
- Do not delay the first wearing beyond roughly the first quarter of the story.
- Keep the crowd small and functional. No named customers, buyer subplot,
  magical investigator, lore tour, or extra family history.
- Seed the warning, private voice message, crown construction, shop account,
  sale deadline, and Tilda's actual desire before their payoffs.
- The truth condition must not function as a generic “honesty cures magic”
  moral. It works because this local focus depends on sustaining a binary
  self-conception, and Tilda's specific sentence breaks that opposition.
- Petra must not forgive instantly, surrender her ownership, or agree the shop
  should survive merely because Tilda finally speaks. The financial and
  relational outcomes remain open after a concrete fair-process step.
- Removing or dismantling the wig must not restore the buyer, delete copies of
  the post, retract the private disclosure, or absolve Tilda.
- Preserve first-person continuity through the inversion. Change cadence and
  values without losing readability or introducing a named alter.
- Register only actual character-facing names; do not accidentally capitalize
  `Mother`, `Buyer`, `Wig`, or a descriptive epithet into a new alias.
