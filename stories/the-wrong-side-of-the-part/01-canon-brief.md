# Canon brief

## Hard constraints

- **One world, not automatically literal present-day Earth.** If the story uses
  the contemporary-feeling setting implied by its controls, it fits the
  modern-like era: an era of the shared physical world that resembles
  contemporary Earth but is not established as our literal Earth. The draft
  should not make real-world Earth identity or history a canon fact merely by
  using familiar modern details. (`universe/premise.md` — `## The deep-time
  shared world`; `universe/locations.md` — `## The world across eras`)
- **The wig's magic must ultimately respect living agency.** Only life can use
  magic. A nonliving wig may store, focus, transmit, or shape magic supplied
  through living agency, but it cannot independently be a magic user. If the
  story makes the wig an autonomous chooser or actor, the mechanism must
  establish that it is alive rather than assume that an artifact can cast by
  itself. The story may leave the mechanism partly mysterious, but what it does
  establish cannot contradict this rule. (`universe/rules.md` — `## Living
  agency and magic`)
- **Do not universalize the anomaly.** Magic, technology, and divinity are
  viewpoint-cultural labels for many real underlying systems; no single origin
  explains every anomaly. The wig may be called magical by characters, but the
  story must not turn it into evidence for one universal magic system or claim
  that all personality magic works alike. (`universe/rules.md` — `## System
  categories`; `universe/glossary.md` — `## Magic, technology, and divinity`)
- **Treat transformation as a mechanism-specific personhood problem.** Canon
  supplies no universal ontology for a transformed person. The story must
  preserve the wearer's identity, consent, relationships, and moral stakes
  rather than dismissing the inversion as merely a machine error, a cosmetic
  state, or a consequence-free nonperson. (`universe/rules.md` — `## Memory,
  transformation, copies, and branches`; `universe/style-guide.md` —
  `## Personhood and moral consequence`)
- **Costs and consent cannot be declared universal laws.** A local mechanism
  may make voluntary wearing, desire, a warning, a charge, a sacrifice, or
  another condition matter, but neither cost nor desire universally fuels
  power, and consent has no single metaphysical behavior across systems. The
  story must take the ethical consequences of impaired or altered agency
  seriously and keep any rule specific to this wig. (`universe/rules.md` —
  `## Costs, desire, and local mechanisms`)
- **No unsupported artifact connection.** Resemblance to another artifact,
  repeated imagery, folklore, a shared name, or a modern legend does not prove
  common origin. The wig must not be tied to the Glass Gate, a deity, a legacy
  object, or any other system without story evidence later approved as canon.
  (`universe/rules.md` — `## Artifacts, motifs, and folklore`;
  `universe/style-guide.md` — `## Continuity and evidence discipline`)
- **Keep consequence and content within project limits.** The darkly comic,
  humane PG-13 treatment is compatible with canon, but any peril, family abuse,
  self-harm, crime, or violence must remain non-graphic and
  non-instructional, with emphasis on character and consequence.
  (`universe/style-guide.md` — `## Audience and content boundaries`)

## Useful established context

- **Canon context:** The modern-like era is a low-magic trough in which ordinary
  life treats magic as myth or legend, while relic anomalies can survive. This
  directly supports both the protagonist's disbelief and a genuinely
  transformative found wig. (`universe/timeline.md` — `## Established era
  order`; `universe/glossary.md` — `## Modern-like era`)
- **Canon context:** Characters may have incomplete or incorrect models of the
  impossible, and there is no universal hidden authority policing anomalies in
  every era. The protagonist need not understand the wig, and the story need
  not introduce a bureau, magical society, or cosmology lecture to validate it.
  Any recurring institution introduced later would need consistent rules.
  (`universe/premise.md` — `## Knowledge and connection`)
- **Canon context:** Many distinct systems can coexist, interact, imitate one
  another, or remain unrelated. A one-off personality-inversion mechanism is
  therefore compatible with canon if it stays local and obeys the locked
  living-agency rule. (`universe/rules.md` — `## System categories`)
- **Canon context:** Artifacts may be honest, misread, propagandistic,
  self-altering, or capable of preserving unusual testimony when a defined
  system supports that behavior; an artifact is never automatically decisive.
  A note, label, seller's story, or rumor can credibly allege the wig's effect
  without forcing the protagonist to believe it. (`universe/rules.md` —
  `## Records, falsehood, and historical traces`)
- **Canon context:** The shared-universe theme of partial evidence and choices
  that still matter can enrich the wig's uncertain provenance and the
  protagonist's reckoning, but the story does not need to advertise the
  deep-time cosmology. (`universe/premise.md` — `## Memory, myth, and
  recoverable truth`)
- **Production context:** The universe permits dark comedy, contemporary
  fantasy, intimate family scale, and first-person narration. No crossover or
  larger-universe awareness is required. (`universe/style-guide.md` —
  `## Tonal range, scale, and connection`)
- **Provisional context:** No relevant `PROVISIONAL` universe entry was found.
  The usable setting and system guidance above is `LOCKED` or `CANON`, while
  the mechanism-specific details listed under `Unknowns` remain omissions
  rather than provisional facts. (`universe/README.md` — `## Authority order`)

## Conflicts or ambiguity

- **Canon-source conflicts:** None found. This stage is **not blocked on a canon
  ruling**.
- **Living-object ambiguity:** The prompt requires a wig that changes to match
  the transformed personality, but does not say whether the wig is alive, is a
  nonliving device using power previously or presently supplied by life, or is
  only the visible part of a wearer-driven effect. All can be explored locally;
  stating that an ordinary nonliving object independently uses magic would
  conflict with the locked rule. (`universe/rules.md` — `## Living agency and
  magic`)
- **Setting-label ambiguity:** “Contemporary fantasy” supports the modern-like
  era, but does not settle a calendar date, country, city, or whether the
  viewpoint culture calls its world Earth. Canon permits familiar modern life
  but does not establish literal real-world Earth. (`universe/glossary.md` —
  `## Modern-like era`; `universe/locations.md` — `## The world across eras`)
- **Prompt ambiguity already bounded:** “Exact opposite” could be logically
  incoherent if applied to every fact and preference. The prompt contract
  safely constrains it to an inversion of dominant habits, defenses, and
  self-conception while preserving consciousness for reckoning. That is a
  production assumption, not existing universe lore.
  (`stories/the-wrong-side-of-the-part/00-prompt.md` — `## Assumptions`)
- **Story-level canon coverage:** `stories/INDEX.md` currently has no row marked
  canon `yes`, so no `05-story.md` is authorized as supplemental canon and none
  was used for this brief. Candidate and legacy stories remain nonbinding.
  (`stories/INDEX.md` — `# Story index`; `universe/README.md` —
  `## Authority order`)

## Unknowns

- **Unknown:** No authoritative entry establishes a magical wig, personality
  inversion, an “opposite self,” or a reusable transformation class. Their
  existence, origin, terminology, and limits are not canon facts.
- **Unknown:** The wig's maker, age, previous wearers, route into the
  protagonist's possession, intended purpose, and whether its provenance can be
  recovered.
- **Unknown:** Whether the wig is alive, contains or stores power once supplied
  by life, draws on its current wearer, or operates through another
  living-agency-compatible mechanism.
- **Unknown:** How the wig identifies the wearer's dominant habits; which traits
  invert; how it handles contradictions, changing self-conception, neurotype,
  illness, intoxication, or a second wearing; and whether there is one stable
  “opposite.”
- **Unknown:** The degree of continuity during transformation: memory access,
  inhibition, control, awareness, responsibility, duration, removal,
  aftereffects, repeatability, and resistance. Canon requires a coherent local
  answer if the story relies on any of these, but supplies none.
- **Unknown:** Whether the visible restyling is physical reshaping, illusion,
  wearer perception, observer perception, or another effect; what happens to
  the wig when unworn; and whether damage, cutting, washing, or concealment
  matters.
- **Unknown:** The exact modern-like location, date, laws, culture, family
  structure, protagonist history, and social consequences. `universe/locations.md`
  establishes no modern-like geography, and `universe/characters.md` and
  `universe/factions.md` contain no recurring entries to import.
- **Unknown:** Whether anyone else recognizes the object or knows impossible
  events are real. The absence of a canon-wide regulator does not prove that no
  local witness, collector, shop, or institution exists.
- **Unknown:** The final disposition of the wig and which disowned traits the
  protagonist integrates. These are story decisions required by the prompt,
  not pre-existing canon.

## Safe invention space

- Place the story in an unnamed or newly invented contemporary-feeling home,
  workplace, shop, thrift store, costume room, or neighborhood within the
  modern-like era. Keep geography local and avoid declaring it literal
  real-world Earth or defining the era's global politics.
- Create one new family and its immediate conflict, with no claimed connection
  to existing candidate or legacy identities. Canon contains no recurring
  character or faction that this premise must use.
- Treat the wig as a narrow anomaly with a story-specific trigger, duration,
  removal rule, visible morphology, and psychological selection rule. Record
  reusable rules later in the canon delta; do not extrapolate them to all
  artifacts, all transformations, or all magic.
- Preserve the locked living-agency rule by making the wig a nonliving
  store/focus/transmitter/shaper of power supplied through life, making the
  transformation arise through its living wearer, or clearly establishing the
  wig as alive if autonomy is essential. The brief does not select among these
  options.
- Let the claim of magic come from a local note, tag, seller, relative, rumor,
  performance tradition, or incomplete testimony. Its uncertainty is safe;
  presenting that claim as verified global history is not.
- Invent a local cost, consent behavior, cooldown, residue, or emotional
  consequence only if it serves the causal plot. Keep it consistent on every
  use and avoid declaring desire, sacrifice, consent, or repression a universal
  source of power.
- Make the wig's cut, texture, motion, maintenance, and presentation respond
  unmistakably to the inverted personality. This fulfills the prompt without
  requiring a new global rule about clothing, appearance, or identity.
- Leave the artifact's ultimate origin unrecovered if desired. Incomplete
  provenance is compatible with canon; mystery must not be used to conceal a
  contradiction in the effect actually shown.

## Name constraints

- The prompt supplies **no character-facing name or alias**. No reuse is
  required, and this brief deliberately proposes none.
- Every full name, given name, mononym, nickname, username, title used as a
  name, named animal, and person-like name for the wig must be checked against
  the entire registry before planning. Existing forms remain reserved even
  when their stories are candidate, in progress, abandoned, or legacy; registry
  presence is production memory, not proof of canon.
  (`stories/NAMES.md` — `# Character name registry`; `## Rules`)
- Common contemporary exact forms already reserved and especially likely to
  collide with this family-scale premise include `Adrian`, `Ben`, `Cal`,
  `Celia`, `Claire`, `Eli`, `Evelyn`, `Frank`, `Jocelyn`, `Jonathan`, `Jordan`,
  `Linda`, `Mae`, `Marcus`, `Nell`, `Nina`, `Nora`, `Oren`, `Paul`, `Phoebe`,
  `Priya`, `Rae`, `Rose`, `Ruth`, `Sabine`, `Sebastian`, and `Tess`. Their
  registered full forms and aliases are reserved as listed in
  `stories/NAMES.md`; this is a relevance list, not a release of unlisted
  forms.
- The unresolved exact collisions `Lena`, `Mara`, `Nisha`, `Pell`, and `Voss`
  must not be repeated. Their existing identities are distinct and unresolved,
  so another use would compound a known defect. (`stories/NAMES.md` —
  `## Known unresolved legacy collisions`)
- Close-match clusters needing extra scrutiny include `Eli` / `Ekri`, `Ilya` /
  `Irix` / `Ivo`, `Lena` / `Leth` / `Letha`, `Mara` / `Mae` / `Marcus`, `Nell`
  / `Nessa`, `Oren` / `Orrin`, `Rae` / `Rana` / `Rhea`, and `Talvi` / `Tavra`
  / `Tamsin`. Avoid introducing another visually or aurally confusable form.
- If the wig is personified or assigned a title, role-label reservations still
  apply. Registry examples include `Captain`, `Saint`, `Time`, `Mother-fragment`,
  `the Goddess of Time`, `the Hollow Regent`, `Court Ghost`, and `the Keeper
  Below`; aliases such as `Bash`, `Green Coat`, `Tea Girl`, and `Red Scarf` are
  reserved too. A descriptive common noun becomes name-facing when the prose
  consistently uses it as an identity.
- Deliberate reuse would require the plan and registry to identify whether the
  identities are the same or distinct, why the reuse matters, and how readers
  will distinguish them. Convenience or genre familiarity is not a rationale.

## Required checks after drafting

- Confirm that the setting stays compatible with the modern-like low-magic
  trough and does not accidentally establish literal real-world Earth, a
  precise global chronology, or a known modern magical establishment.
- Trace the effect's power and agency. The draft must not describe a nonliving
  wig as an independent magic user unless it establishes that the wig is alive;
  mystery about origin cannot override the locked living-agency rule.
- Verify that every rule shown for activation, trait selection, awareness,
  memory, control, duration, removal, repeat use, physical restyling, and
  aftereffects is internally consistent across scenes.
- Check that “exact opposite” remains a coherent inversion of dominant habits
  and defenses, not a reversal of facts or an unexplained switch between
  incompatible definitions chosen for plot convenience.
- Confirm that the protagonist voluntarily makes the first wearing while
  disbelieving the claim, remains present enough for later reckoning, and that
  any altered capacity for consent or control receives real ethical weight.
- Confirm that the transformed person retains personhood and moral consequence:
  removal must not erase harm, promises, discoveries, relationship changes, or
  the need to choose what to integrate.
- Confirm that the wig changes visibly in cut, texture, movement, and
  presentation in a way consistently legible as personality-linked rather than
  only cosmetic.
- Search for unsupported claims connecting the wig to the Glass Gate, a deity,
  another named artifact, a universal true-name rule, folklore, or a hidden
  authority. Shared imagery or vocabulary is not evidence of shared mechanism.
- Validate every final character-facing name and alias against
  `stories/NAMES.md`, including family-only labels, nicknames, usernames,
  titles, and any person-like name for the wig; resolve exact, alias, close, and
  reversal collisions before certification.
- Recheck the PG-13 ceiling and humane ending: peril and family conflict may be
  intense but should remain non-graphic, consequence-centered, and free of a
  simple good-self/evil-self binary or consequence-free reset.
