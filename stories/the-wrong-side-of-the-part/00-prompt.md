# Prompt contract

## Verbatim writing prompt

> [WP] You find a magical wig that, when worn, turns you into the exact oppose of who you are, with the wig changing to match the new personality. However, you don’t believe this… until you put it on.

## Story controls

- Working title: The Wrong Side of the Part
- Target length: 3,000–3,800 words
- POV: First person, anchored in the wearer’s perspective
- Tense: Past
- Tone and genre: Darkly comic contemporary fantasy with mounting emotional
  tension, tactile magic, and a humane ending
- Audience/content rating: Teen / PG-13; non-graphic peril and emotionally
  fraught family conflict
- Required elements: A found wig; explicit disbelief about its alleged magic;
  a voluntary first wearing; a dramatic inversion of the protagonist’s
  habitual personality; the wig visibly changing to suit that inversion; real
  consequences caused by the transformed personality; and a resolved choice
  about what the wearer does with both the wig and the traits it reveals
- Prohibited elements: An “it was all a dream” explanation; treating the
  change as only cosmetic; a consequence-free reset; a simple good-self versus
  evil-self binary; or contradicting established shared-universe canon

## Assumptions

- “Exact opposite” means a coherent inversion of the protagonist’s dominant
  habits, defenses, and self-conception rather than a logically impossible
  reversal of every preference and fact.
- The protagonist remains conscious enough to experience and later reckon with
  what the inverted personality does.
- The wig’s origin and operation may occupy canon’s documented safe-invention
  space, but the story will prefer a local anomaly over a new global magic
  system.
- The physical transformation of the wig will be unmistakable on the page and
  will track the new personality rather than merely changing color.
- No existing character or location is presumed to appear unless canon
  research supports the choice.

## Completion tests

- The result is a complete short story, not a synopsis or chapter fragment.
- The central promise of the prompt is dramatized and resolved.
- The protagonist begins as a clearly legible personality, disbelieves the
  wig’s claim, puts it on, and becomes a comparably legible opposite.
- The transformed wig reflects the inverted personality in cut, texture,
  movement, and presentation.
- The inversion forces an irreversible truth or decision, so removing the wig
  cannot simply restore the opening status quo.
- The ending answers whether a person can use disowned traits without
  surrendering to them.
- The story passes canon and continuity review.
- Every character-facing name and alias is checked against `stories/NAMES.md`;
  any deliberate reuse has a documented narrative reason.
