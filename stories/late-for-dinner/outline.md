# Outline

## Story

- Title: Late for Dinner
- Premise and central promise: Adult civilian Rellan is trapped at Plover's Cut by Captain Cadren's stranded enemy company on the afternoon of his first dinner with his wife in five months. His lateness brings feared General Nerava and her legion to destroy the company's fighting capacity; deliver the comic marital rescue while making Rellan's offer of quarter matter.
- Focal pressure or attachment: Rellan wants Nerava's homecoming free of military obligation and wants others to treat him as her husband, not her handle. Pride prevents him seeking help; knowledge of Nerava soon makes him fear for his captors.
- Counterforce or complication: Cadren needs a living hostage to secure passage for soldiers cut off after a failed incursion. He hears dinner as privileged mockery and cannot surrender on a civilian's warning without losing his last authority. Nerava's approach adds pressure rather than simple relief.
- POV, distance, and information limit: Close third-person past through Rellan, noticing bad footing, frightened animals, spoiled provisions, failing light, and Nerava's likely reactions. Never enter either commander's mind or cut to the legion; reveal it through dust, horns, and effects.
- Governing movement and time shape: One late afternoon into early night, shaped as reversing rings: Cadren encloses a wagon refuge, bargaining shrinks it, then Nerava encloses Cadren. Contract from the rout to a private delayed meal, not a public victory.
- Speculative rule or ordinary-world constraint: No magic decides the encounter. The cut blocks sight and messengers; Cadren must keep Rellan alive; Nerava's returning legion camps near Juniper House. The missed meal and road smoke prompt a search, but Nerava cannot see the hostage geometry before reaching the ridge.
- Dialogue promise: Irritated civilian courtesy meets rehearsed grievance, ragged interruptions, and a general whose few words carry rank. Humor comes from Cadren mistaking a marital deadline for swagger, never from suffering.
- Dialogic medium: Parley, shouted fragments, a surrender cloth, horns, lowering weapons, eye contact across the ring, and small practical gestures between spouses.
- Dialogue engine: Cadren assumes marriage creates leverage; Rellan refuses to counterfeit Nerava's authority. Each explanation consumes the time that brings her. Once she arrives, his quarter must become legible to both sides before her assault removes Cadren's choice.

## Voice

- Narrative texture: Wry close-third measures battle against crushed herbs, wagon damage, failing sun, and Nerava's likely reactions without trivializing injury. Accelerate as space shrinks; turn spare at her arrival.
- Conversational texture: Use incomplete bargains, unwanted courtesy, misheard shouts, repeated demands, and brief household familiarity. Soldiers may speak only to warn, complain, or pass something needed.
- Rhetorical ownership: Rellan owns domestic comparison, nervous humor, and evasive politeness; Cadren owns formal grievance, status anxiety, and misreading. Nerava owns spatial authority and dry literalness, conveys affection through attention, and listens to restraint. Minor fighters stay practical and uneven.
- Pressure behavior: Rellan overtalks, then becomes blunt; Cadren repeats himself and substitutes volume for control; Nerava grows quieter. Animal panic, pain, and dust break everyone's fluency.
- Relationship movement: Rellan wants Nerava's private self and respect for his independence but cannot ask her not to protect him. Nerava wants his trust with danger but cannot ask for dependence. Cadren wants their marriage as his corridor and cannot request mercy. Exchanges change trust, quarter, risk, and field control.
- Anti-default: Avoid three polished strategists solving a clean puzzle. Keep Rellan's mistakes and petty dinner grief, Cadren's embarrassment and poor listening, ordinary soldier noise, and Nerava's rescue as marital consequence rather than technical showcase.

## Beats

1. Returning from market with dinner provisions and household hands, Rellan finds both exits occupied. Cadren attempts a bloodless seizure; a guard's resistance and a bolting mule create a cramped battle and an inelegant wagon refuge.
2. Cadren demands Rellan secure passage from Nerava. Rellan lacks military authority and will not imitate it. His dinner deadline sounds contemptuous, so Cadren tightens the ring.
3. Escape attempts fail through ordinary mistakes. Provisions scatter, someone is wounded, and animals are lost. Already late, Rellan changes aim from escape to persuading Cadren to surrender before Nerava notices.
4. Cadren reveals that his company expects prison or retaliation. Rellan offers personal protection but cannot guarantee the crown. Cadren delays for stronger terms as disturbed birds, dust, and a disciplined horn validate the warning.
5. The legion takes separate heights. Nerava strips away retreat, mounts, signals, and command positions in a brief rout; keep injuries impressionistic and consequential. Cadren moves Rellan as leverage, but soldiers begin surrendering independently.
6. Rellan enters visible risk and raises the improvised surrender cloth. Nerava must judge whether he is coerced. She halts late enough to demonstrate force and early enough for his promise to matter; Cadren yields after his authority fractures.
7. Nerava honors the quarter, assigns care and custody, and refuses a victory display. Private conversation permits anger, relief, and the absurd scale of her response without a reconciliation formula. End at Juniper House over damaged provisions and delayed dinner, with the legion outside their intimacy.

## Continuity

- Canon used: The deep-time world permits an independent feudal-shaped culture and local nonmagical story. Keep violence broadly accessible, consequential, and non-graphic.
- Boundaries and unknowns: Kingdom, era, war, crown, legion organization, and the company's fate remain local and unresolved. No connection is claimed to Redwater, Harrowfast, another crown, military event, magic, or technology; Nerava establishes no universal rank or tactic.
