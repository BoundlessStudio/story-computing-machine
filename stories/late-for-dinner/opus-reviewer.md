# Opus review: Late for Dinner

Independent audit for a later editing pass. It is not a `review.md` verdict,
does not change canon state, and does not authorize edits by itself.

## Verdict

- Dialogue: REVISE — one Blocking Ground failure (Rellan's refusal to climb the bank, L199), two muddled or emblematic climax beats, Cadren trading Rellan's own balanced comebacks, and a kitchen ending that states its theme outright.
- Prose: PASS — concrete, funny and well staged; only minor reversal/one-line-paragraph habits and three local wording slips.
- Continuity and prompt: PASS — delivers the prompt's comic late-for-dinner rescue at about 3,680 words; two small internal slips (the "not an ambushing force" inference, cheese/"ride ahead").
- Edit scope: targeted-dialogue
- Existing review.md said: Verdict PASS, `Dialogue: PASS`; disagree on dialogue, because it missed the L199 Ground failure and the thesis and emblem lines at the climax and the ending.

The field dialogue is often very good. Nerava's few words carry rank ("No passage." / "Then you will still have no passage."), the minor soldiers speak plainly, and Rellan's pettiness is a flaw the story punishes ("That was the wrong thing to say", "Perhaps less talking"). The problem is that Cadren, who is bleeding, starving and losing command, answers Rellan with equally poised mirrored lines ("You mistake your circumstances" / "You mistake my marriage"), so the captivity scenes turn into a sparring routine between two people with the same voice. The climax has three weak beats in a row: a refusal whose reason misdescribes an earlier event and cannot be paraphrased (L199), a squabble over the wording of Rellan's promise whose purpose the narration has to explain (L283–L287), and a "There it is" emblem line (L305). The kitchen scene then says the theme again in several forms and ends on a tee-up for the name "Nerava". The edit should make each line true to the scene, let Cadren fray instead of spar, and cut the thesis lines. Keep the comic domestic texture and Nerava's economy.

## Edit guardrails

Keep the comic tone, broadly accessible and non-graphic violence, close third on Rellan, and a length of 2,500–4,000 words (now about 3,680, so prefer cuts to additions). - Universe: no LOCKED or CANON entry names these people or places. The story is standalone and nonmagical, and nothing needs reconciling. The roadside ditch and the orchard trough are ordinary terrain, not water-infrastructure texture. Do not add any.
- Already working and not to be flattened: Nerava's clipped field voice; the minor characters' practical lines; the food-sharing beat (L135); the horn recognition (L191); the reunion's onion joke; the ending on the stolen pear and Rellan's laugh.

## Dialogue findings

### D1 · Blocking · Bank refusal misdescribes the scene and cannot be paraphrased

- Where: L193–L199, just after the first horn, when Cadren seizes Rellan's coat
- Text:
> Your archer nearly killed me when I was standing beside a wagon.
> I am not showing Nerava a ring of armed people and letting her guess which ones have discipline.
> The bolt struck the wagon rail rather than the guard
- Problem: Ground/scene and Ground/referent. The only shot in the story was a crossbow bolt aimed at the guard, which hit the rail while Rellan was riding on the wagon (L23, L27). He was not "standing beside a wagon", and the bolt was not meant for him. The second sentence has no recoverable meaning. Climbing the bank would show Nerava one man, not "a ring of armed people", and she is not yet in sight: the horn is "far beyond the cut" (L189). It is also unclear whose discipline "which ones" refers to. Cadren would understand only that Rellan refuses, and the reader cannot reconstruct why. This is the line that begins the rescue, so it is a key beat.
- Fix: Replace both sentences with a refusal based on things Rellan actually knows. He has already fallen off that bank once (L117–L119). On the skyline he would look like a man coming out of the enemy ring, to archers who cannot yet see his face. Keep it short and a little frightened. Illustrative only: "I've already fallen off that bank once. If I reach the top, the first thing her archers see is a man climbing out of your ring."

### D2 · Major · Cadren spars in Rellan's register: mirrored comebacks and correction ping-pong

- Where: L51–L53 (wagon), L79–L85 (the letter), L97–L101 (dinner), L125–L129 (after the bank slip), L157–L169 (quarter offer)
- Text:
> You are in no position to bargain.
> That is a man under a wagon, Captain. It is not a bargain.
> You mistake your circumstances.
> You mistake my marriage. I cannot order Nerava to move a legion.
> My company has been without bread for two days.
> This is not hospitality.
> No. At Juniper House we warn people about the footing.
> I command my word.
> Then you offer a prettier cage.
- Problem: Identical Voices and Tennis Match. Across five exchanges the two men trade balanced, uncontracted, mirrored lines. Cadren supplies a formal set-up and Rellan caps it with a correction ("It is not a bargain", "No. At Juniper House…"), or Cadren copies Rellan's syntax back at him ("You mistake…" / "You mistake…", "My wife has been away for five months." / "My company has been without bread for two days."). Cadren has a bleeding arm, a hungry company that is deserting him, and riders on the road, yet he is as composed and epigrammatic as his hostage. The outline's own pressure note (Cadren "repeats himself and substitutes volume for control") appears only in narration (L143). Each line makes sense on its own. The trouble is the combined effect: the scenes read as a routine, and Cadren's desperation never reaches his speech.
- Fix: Keep Rellan's quips, since they are his flaw and the story punishes them, but stop Cadren from playing along. (1) L53: cut "It is not a bargain." Let the guard's leg make the point, or let Cadren ignore the quip and act. (2) L79–L81: keep one of the pair and break the mirror. If Cadren keeps "You mistake your circumstances.", Rellan should answer plainly that he cannot order Nerava. Keep "I can ask. She can say no." (it sets up L241–L245). (3) L99: make Cadren's hunger ragged and repetitive rather than a matched rejoinder, so that Rellan's "poor afternoons" (the story's marked mistake) lands on raw anger. (4) L127–L129: cut "This is not hospitality." and Rellan's capper. Let Cadren repeat the threat or turn away, then go to the guard's "Perhaps less talking." (5) L159/L169: cut at least one of "I command my word." and "Then you offer a prettier cage." Keep the plain "Can it keep my soldiers out of prison?" / "I don't know." Illustrative only (L99): "Two days without bread. Two days. And you tell me about dinner."

### D3 · Major · "He promised protection" squabble: unclear aim, correction at the decisive beat

- Where: L281–L289, quarter terms with Nerava on the bank and the bows drawn
- Text:
> Cadren said behind him, "He promised protection."
> "I promised you would live," Rellan snapped. "You kept arguing about the road."
> Cadren's formal rage cracked. Beneath it was plain embarrassment, arriving late and unwelcome.
- Problem: Ground/uptake and Punchline Patch. The reader cannot tell whether Cadren is backing up Rellan's claim to Nerava or overclaiming. "Protection" is vague, and Rellan never used the word. Rellan's reply treats the line as an overclaim and corrects its wording, which turns the most dangerous moment into correction ping-pong. The narration then has to explain the result ("formal rage", "embarrassment"), but Cadren's four-word line showed no rage. "Snapped" is doing the emotion's job.
- Fix: Make Cadren's interjection an unmistakable overreach that is tied to what was actually said, and let Rellan answer with the plain fact rather than a reproach. Rellan did say "I don't know" about prison (L165–L167). Then replace the gloss with a visible reaction, or cut it, and go straight to Nerava's "Do you stand by it?" Cutting L283–L287 entirely is also acceptable, because L291 follows L281 cleanly. Illustrative only: Cadren: "He said my soldiers would not see a prison." / Rellan: "I said I didn't know."

### D4 · Major · "You wanted my marriage. There it is." emblem line at the surrender

- Where: L303–L309, Rellan hands Cadren the linen
- Text:
> "You wanted my marriage," he said. "There it is."
> He took it as though it weighed more than his sword.
- Problem: Philosopher and closing emblem. Handing the defeated captain the quarter cloth is a strong gesture that needs no words. The line turns it into a thesis (the marriage was worth quarter, not passage), and the figurative mapping of stained dinner linen to marriage is strained. The narration then tells the reader how heavy the gesture is. The action has already shown all of this.
- Fix: Keep the gesture and the dropped sword. Give Rellan a practical line, or none, so that Cadren has something to do: the legion needs to see the captain himself yield under the cloth. Cut "as though it weighed more than his sword". Illustrative only: "Hold it up. They need to see it in your hand."

### D5 · Major · Kitchen scene restates the theme and caps a vulnerable moment

- Where: L387–L409, kitchen table after "By the second horn"
- Text:
> "I wanted one evening," he said, "when nobody used me to reach you."
> Cadren thought I was a gate he could put a knife against.
> You are not a gate.
> I know that. You know that. It was a crowded afternoon for slower learners.
- Problem: Philosopher, Tennis Match and Punchline Patch. Rellan states the grievance directly at L387, which is new to Nerava and so earned once. After that the scene repeats it: first as a strained metaphor (no one holds a knife to a gate, so the image blends hostage and gateway), then through Nerava's literal reassurance, then with a triple-beat quip that turns his vulnerability into a last word. L389–L397 is a run of seven short, perfectly alternating lines, with a small correction ("Cadren did." / "He tried."). The narration had already said the same thing at L113 and L143, so the reader hears the point three or four times.
- Fix: Keep L387 and the strongest part of the run ("He was afraid of you." / "He had cause." / "I know." / "Sometimes I hate how much cause everyone has.") and Nerava's silence at L401. Cut L403–L409 (gate, "not a gate", "slower learners", "Her mouth twitched."). L411 follows L401 naturally. Optionally let "Cadren did." go unanswered, or cut "He tried." to break the alternation. If the scene needs more feeling, route it through the cheese or the plates, which L369 already sets up ("He wanted to complain about the cheese."), not through another explanation.

### D6 · Major · Final exchange built on a set-up for the name "Nerava"

- Where: L411–L417, the last meaningful exchange before "There will not be a next time"
- Text:
> You dislike all your names.
> Not all of them.
> Next time the road goes wrong, send for Nerava.
- Problem: Punchline Patch and Philosopher at the final meaningful exchange. "You dislike all your names." exists only to set up "Not all of them." and the reveal of her own name, which spells out the general-versus-wife theme. The story had already shown that she honoured his word, hung her sword on his peg, and ate with him at the kitchen table. The ring-turn gesture is good and should stay.
- Fix: Cut the set-up pair. Keep "Good. I dislike that name." and the ring turn if you like. Give her a request that comes from what she actually went through (smoke, the missed post, a patrol finding the mule) rather than from the name motif. Keep the following beats exactly: "There will not be a next time.", the horn, "There had better not," and the pear. Illustrative only: "Next time, send someone. Don't make me hear it from the mule."

### D7 · Minor · Composed triplet under the blade

- Where: L257–L267, Rellan persuading Cadren with the sword at his jaw
- Text:
> His mouth had gone dry.
> Not victory. Not freedom. Living through the evening. Take it.
- Problem: Uniform fluency under pressure. With a dry mouth and a fresh cut, Rellan delivers a balanced "Not X. Not Y." tricolon that defines quarter. The substance is right, but the rhetorical shape is the house voice, not a frightened man.
- Fix: Keep "Take it." and the plain offer. Drop the "Not victory. Not freedom." construction. Let him ask rather than define. Illustrative only: "It's quarter, that's all. Your people live through tonight. Please take it."

### D8 · Minor · Narration decodes Cadren's reactions from inside his head

- Where: L49 (wagon), L79 (the letter), L263 (under the blade); related glosses at L287 and L307 are covered in D3 and D4
- Text:
> He had prepared himself for terror or defiance. An objection about furniture did not fit anywhere useful.
> Cadren looked almost relieved. This, at least, was a kind of answer he understood.
> He wanted a road. He wanted not to beg. He wanted the last hour to have meant something.
- Problem: The close third is on Rellan, but these sentences report what Cadren expected, understood and wanted, which explains his replies before the reader can infer them. L49 also misnames the scene: the wagon is not furniture, and Rellan's objection was about the guard's leg.
- Fix: Cut these sentences or reduce them to something Rellan can observe (a look, a pause, a repeated order). Keep the single short "Rellan wanted to go home." at L265 if the L263 triplet goes.

### D9 · Minor · "It had to be." gnomic last word

- Where: L351–L357, after "Thank you for stopping"
- Text:
> When she spoke again, her voice was lower. "It had to be."
- Problem: "Neither was I." and her glance at the dead man beneath the dun banner already carry the admission that she might not have stopped. The added line is a small aphorism that explains it.
- Fix: End the exchange on "Neither was I." and the glance toward the surgeons, or give her a concrete order instead.

## Prose findings

### P1 · Minor · Reversal and verdict constructions

- Where: L289 (Nerava watching Rellan), L359 (no victory display); see also D7 and D8 for dialogue and narration triplets
- Text:
> Not the cloth. Not Cadren's sword. Him.
> The living had enough to carry out.
- Problem: A triplet fragment and a closing aphorism in narration, the same shape as the dialogue tics. L145–L147 ("It was no longer that… / It was that she would.") and L175 (the "very little ash" joke) use the reversal to real effect and should stay. These two do not.
- Fix: Recast L289 as a plain statement of where her eyes are. End the L359 paragraph on the concrete refusal of the victory display.

### P2 · Minor · One-sentence paragraphs pile up in the battle and climax

- Where: L183–L189 (plovers and horn), L205–L207 (dust, rim), L215–L219, L223–L229 (Nerava found)
- Text:
> Dust rose beyond both banks.
> Then soldiers appeared along the western rim.
> Until she found him.
> Everything in her went quiet.
- Problem: About 44 of roughly 99 narration paragraphs are a single sentence. Early on this gives good comic timing ("The guard did."), but from L183 to L239 it becomes the default pulse, and the isolated lines lose their emphasis.
- Fix: Merge a few pairs (L205 with L207, L227 with L229, L185 with L187) so that the remaining isolated lines ("The second flight did not warn.", "Nerava raised two fingers.") keep their force.

### P3 · Minor · Local wording and referent slips

- Where: L103 (after "poor afternoons"), L105 (ring tightened), L349 (carter leaves)
- Text:
> but knowing a mistake at once did not make it less made.
> Cadren ordered the ring tightened.
> aware that the man beside her still needed help.
- Problem: L103 is awkward ("less made"). At L105, the last "ring" the reader saw was Rellan's wedding ring (L35), and the soldiers' circle has not yet been named, so "the ring" briefly misreads. At L349 it is unclear which man and beside whom, and "help… help" repeats.
- Fix: Smooth L103. Name the circle of soldiers at first mention. Recast L349 so the carter plainly goes to the guard.

## Continuity and prompt findings

### C1 · Minor · "Not an ambushing force" right after a staged ambush

- Where: L11–L15, L29, L41, opening in Plover's Cut
- Text:
> Alive, you fools. He is worth nothing dead.
> Not an ambushing force, then.
- Problem: The company has just blocked both ends of the road, raised spears from the scrub and stopped the wagon, and Cadren shouted that the man was worth more alive before he checked the ring. Rellan's inference denies what the reader has just seen. The next sentence (a force that ran until it found a hole) is the point Rellan means to make.
- Fix: Reword the inference so it describes the kind of force (desperate, improvised, not a planned raid) without denying the ambush. Optionally add one clause showing how Cadren guessed who was on the road to Juniper House before he saw the ring.

### C2 · Minor · Small object and transport slips

- Where: L135 vs L363 (the cheese); L143 (the guard at the crossroads)
- Text:
> who had taken the cheese
> The cheese was somewhere under a wagon.
> his guard had offered to ride ahead
- Problem: The cheese is taken during the food-sharing, then later said to be under the wagon. The guard has no mount: the only animal is the harnessed mule, and he was pinned by the wagon shaft.
- Fix: Choose one fate for the cheese. The eaten version gives Rellan his complaint at L369. Change "ride ahead" to "go ahead" or "run ahead".
