# Outline

## Story

- Premise and central promise: At dawn weigh-out in the dead city of Merenza,
  caravan cutter Varoq finds the bronze civic letters missing from an arch.
  Her adult apprentice Pava admits she sold them yesterday to itinerant
  roof-founder Jassik and used the price to replace a cracked caravan brake shoe
  Varoq had dismissed as sound. Varoq must try to recover the name without
  pretending Pava's safety judgment was worthless or that good use can make a
  sacred breach reversible. Fulfill the prompt through a materially specific
  salvage culture whose oldest trade limit is `take the metal, leave the
  names`, then resolve the sale with partial recovery, real cost, an
  irretrievably melted letter, and a changed but continuing apprenticeship.
- Intended reader experience: Begin with the competence and appetite of a
  working salvage camp, let one wrong absence make its ordinary accounting feel
  like desecration, then complicate anger with Pava's credible reason and
  Jassik's equally credible need. Give the reader tactile pleasure in old alloys,
  fastenings, loads, and field repairs while steadily removing the possibility
  of a clean undoing. End sober but not punitive: the code matters because
  hungry people choose one residue not to price, and keeping that choice
  requires explanation, judgment, and consequences rather than ritual purity.
- Generating force:
  - Mode: Plot-led recovery journey governed by unequal substitutions. The same
    bronze changes category from untouchable name to sale weight, brake repair,
    promised roof clamps, repurchased fragments, and damaged evidence; each
    proposed exchange solves one obligation by exposing another.
  - Focal attachment or contradiction: Varoq chose Pava because she notices
    stress and danger before older cutters do, but has treated the trade's one
    sacred prohibition as an order that requires no explanation. The judgment
    Varoq cultivated saved the caravan after she ignored Pava's warning, yet
    Pava exercised it by pricing the one thing their livelihood says cannot be
    merchandise.
  - Plot-led wants and useful limit, if applicable: Varoq immediately wants the
    name back before Jassik's evening pour and wants the caravan to catch its
    brief rail-haul window; she also wants to keep a gifted apprentice without
    teaching that necessity excuses every taking. Her useful limit is
    authoritative silence: she weighs disputes as if correct totals can replace
    reasons, and shame over the missed brake crack makes her initially seek a
    punishment that excludes her own part. Pava wants to remain Varoq's
    apprentice and have the repair recognized as necessary, while also wanting
    not to conceal what she sold or let the convoy travel on a dangerous part.
- Counterforce or complication:
  - Source: Time, prior commitments, and partial irreversibility. Jassik bought
    the letters in good faith from a recognized caravan trader, promised their
    bronze as roof-clamp stock, and has already melted one letter in an alloy
    test. The remaining letters can be returned only for an equal quantity of
    dependable bronze before the pour. The caravan's uncommitted metal is not
    equal quality, while its best conductor coil is contracted freight; missing
    the dusk rail-haul leaves Varoq and Pava a night and a long walk behind.
  - Credible inside logic, if a person: Jassik is not a desecrator or convert in
    waiting. His trade does not reserve inscriptions, the sale was ordinary by
    his rules, and households at his next stop need clamps before the corrosive
    rains. He will unwind the usable part of the bargain if made whole, refuses
    the new brake shoe because returning it would endanger the caravan, and
    does not accept Varoq's code as authority over his own obligations.
  - Pressure or change created: Varoq can recover six letters only by surrendering
    valuable freight, accepting a contract loss, and missing departure; money
    cannot restore the melted seventh. Pava's explanation prevents expulsion
    from functioning as a simple moral answer, while Varoq's complicity does
    not erase Pava's deliberate choice to treat an unledgered name as free
    stock.
- Narrative design:
  - POV, tense, distance, and limitation: Close first-person past through
    Varoq, with a retrospective horizon ending the next dawn. Her attention
    defaults to mass, grain, wear, leverage, and what a person owes; her
    emotional evasions appear as exact inventories and clipped questions.
    Pava's inner reasoning must emerge through the repair she made, the warning
    she can repeat word for word, what she refuses to excuse, and the questions
    she asks on the return. Jassik speaks in pours, weather, and promises rather
    than serving as a lore lecturer.
  - Time shape and compression: Roughly one day and one night after the
    yesterday sale. Render the missing-name discovery, Jassik's field foundry
    bargain, the choice of replacement bronze, and the return to the arch in
    scene. Compress the outward and inward walks through changing burdens and
    the named surfaces Pava previously left untouched. Let the caravan depart
    on the dusk rail-haul offstage; end at first light after the partial
    reseating, without a later hearing or cultural-policy coda.
  - Information and reveal strategy: Reveal the sale and buyer immediately,
    but initially let Varoq assume greed or carelessness. At the foundry, reveal
    the new brake shoe, Pava's ignored warning, why no other caravan metal was
    lawfully hers to sell, and the melted first letter. Explain the code only as
    decisions force its operative distinctions: salvagers may take structure
    after marking safe cuts, never remove or obscure a person or place name,
    and do not claim that abstention preserves the rest of a city. Make clear
    that Merenza's name is historical residue with social and evidentiary force,
    not a spell, soul container, ownership deed, or universally binding law.
  - Governing movement: Weigh the outward load; notice the name-shaped absence;
    hear Pava's admission; inspect the lifesaving brake repair; leave the convoy
    on its departure schedule; follow the buyer's wheel ruts; pass smaller names
    still in place; reach Jassik's lit field furnace; learn that one letter is
    already gone; test every proposed repayment against another obligation;
    trade the contracted conductor coil for six cut letters; shoulder them
    inward; hear Pava's full choice and Varoq's ignored warning; miss the
    weightless-rail departure; find the original fastening shadows; reject a
    newly forged substitute as false restoration; reseat only what survives;
    assign bounded consequences and continue the apprenticeship; begin the next
    cut by locating the names that constrain it.
- Speculative surplus: Intact pale transit rails in this ruin lose most of
  their weight for about an hour after sunset, though their cargo does not.
  Salvage caravans couple low sledges to them for a fast straight haul and must
  uncouple before the rails settle; broken segments are merely light, brittle
  scrap. The effect has no agency, does not preserve records or react to names,
  and helps neither bargaining nor restoration. Missing the window creates the
  practical overnight delay.
- Structural distinction: A there-and-back burden exchange whose external
  stakes remain one caravan, one buyer, and one damaged inscription. The
  decisive venue is first a roadside field foundry, where recovery proves
  incomplete, and then an empty arch after the caravan has gone; the ending is
  unfinished work and altered supervision rather than a public technical
  proof, crowd conversion, institutional rewrite, trial, punishment spectacle,
  or polished memorial.
- Decisive turn, deepening, or recognition: Jassik drops six separated letters
  on his scale and shows the bead made from the seventh. He offers an honest
  exchange, but only the contracted conductor coil matches the bronze he has
  already promised. Pava proposes surrendering the new brake shoe and walking
  out of the trade herself. Jassik rejects the unsafe part; Varoq rejects turning
  a person into payment. Varoq gives up the coil, naming the contract loss as
  her responsibility because she ignored the crack, while naming the sale as
  Pava's because Pava chose the forbidden stock rather than stop the convoy.
  Back at Merenza, the empty seventh fastening defeats Varoq's impulse to forge
  a seamless replacement: making their own letter look ancient would conceal
  the very loss the code exists to leave legible.
- Aftereffect or live uncertainty: Six scarred letters return to their original
  fastening shadows; one gap remains, and the lost metal now belongs to roof
  clamps that will protect people who never knew the city. The caravan keeps
  its safe brake but loses a contract and a day's labor. Pava remains an
  apprentice, loses unsupervised authority to price or sell salvage until her
  share repays the coil, and must identify name-bearing surfaces before every
  future cut; Varoq must inspect her warnings and supply reasons rather than
  maxims. Whether another emergency could justify touching a name, whether
  partial inscriptions should ever be moved for preservation, and who first
  made the code remain unresolved.
- Opening / ending relation: Open with Varoq making every object answer to a
  scale and discovering the breach as metal that should not be in the day's
  total. End at first light, after the partial return, when Pava points out a
  small household name beside the conduit chosen for their next cut. Varoq
  cannot solve the lost contract by taking the easy pipe; she asks Pava for a
  route around it, and the story stops as they begin measuring rather than with
  the restored inscription treated as absolution.

## Beats

1. At Merenza's dawn weigh-out, Varoq sees seven clean fastening shadows on a
   civic arch. Pava admits she sold the bronze letters yesterday and shows the
   new brake shoe bought with them. Varoq verifies the crack was real and orders
   the convoy to keep its dusk departure while the two follow Jassik.
2. On the outward walk, Pava identifies lesser inscriptions she left untouched
   and repeats the safety warning Varoq dismissed. Varoq confines herself to
   transaction questions: what was sold, for how much, under whose authority,
   and why Pava did not halt the caravan.
3. At Jassik's field foundry, six letters await cutting and the seventh is an
   alloy-test bead. Jassik explains his roof-clamp commitment and offers the only
   exchange that does not transfer his loss to the waiting households. Pava's
   brake shoe is unacceptable because it is now safety equipment.
4. The uncommitted scrap fails Jassik's tests. Varoq trades the contracted
   conductor coil, accepting the freight breach as the price of her ignored
   inspection. She refuses Pava's attempt to offer exile as repayment and makes
   explicit that saving the caravan and selling the name are two truths, not a
   cancellation.
5. Carrying the separated letters back, they watch the caravan use Merenza's
   pale rails without them. Pava gives her full reasoning: every other piece
   belonged to someone on the ledger, while the name had deliberately never
   been entered. Varoq finally explains that this absence means `not for sale`,
   not `free`, and that the code preserves one limit on a trade built from
   other people's remains.
6. At the arch, the missing letter makes seamless restoration impossible.
   Varoq and Pava match old breaks and fastening shadows, reject a counterfeit
   replacement, and reseat the six survivors without disguising the cut edges.
   Varoq sets the apprenticeship consequences and admits her own failed
   teaching and inspection.
7. After an uncomfortable night beneath the arch, Pava spots a household name
   beside the conduit that could replace the lost contract metal. Varoq asks
   for another cut path. They begin the slower measurement together, with the
   original loss still visible behind them.

## Continuity

- Canon used: The story occupies the one physical world's deep-time sequence
  of recurrent civilizations, where old systems and cities become partial,
  often misread archaeology. Merenza's exact era is left undated. Historical
  traces can retain evidence without becoming automatically decisive. Names
  are ordinarily symbolic and socially powerful rather than universally
  magical.
- Boundaries and unknowns: Varoq, Pava, Jassik, Merenza, the salvage code, the
  local trade customs, the pale-rail effect, and this event are new and have no
  established connection to Ravel, the Glass-Sea cities, the Day of Glass's
  lost city names, Qarsis, the city of eleven foundations, or any other named
  people, places, factions, ruins, identity systems, or transit technology.
  The code establishes no universal property right, funerary law, magic of
  names, or rule for all salvage cultures. Merenza's builders, fall, language,
  geography, chronology, and relation to the deeper structures beneath it
  remain unknown; the outline should not broaden them beyond what the immediate
  work requires.
