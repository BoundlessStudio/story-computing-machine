# Opus review: Leave the Names

Independent audit for a later editing pass. It is not a `review.md` verdict,
does not change canon state, and does not authorize edits by itself.

## Verdict

- Dialogue: REVISE — no scene falls apart, but most talk is one clipped register of rulings and corrections, and at the confession, the turnaround, the bargain and the disposition someone lands a capping line.
- Prose: PASS — concrete and well staged; the problems are minor (statute-style rule exposition, a negation tic, a water-infrastructure setting, typos).
- Continuity and prompt: REVISE — delivers the prompt, but the shelf-road bond is paid to Lurem after the story has said missing the gate forfeits it.
- Edit scope: targeted-dialogue
- Existing review.md said: Verdict PASS; "Dialogue: PASS"; disagree — its "role- and pressure-specific through the bargain" is exactly where Kedra, Lurem and Sovet sound the same.

The dialogue is coherent line by line: no one states the theme, and the talk that works is tied to hands and objects. That covers the jam and release sequence, Beshan going from anxious over-talk to precise calls, Sovet refusing to be rushed, and the cook's thirteenth packet. The weakness is convergence. Kedra uses the same ruling register on a merchant, her crew and her apprentice. Lurem speaks in Sovet's inventory notation and Kedra's clause grammar, so the engine standoff and the terms read as correction ping-pong and contract recital. The crew (driver, rigger, cook) mostly exist to trade dry one-liners, and exchanges keep closing on a cap ("They can walk it back in", "No one asked the axle", "Then your boot can wait"). The edit pass should give Lurem a trader's voice, cut the caps, and let Beshan's confession actually break. Kedra's own share of the blame, the wire order, should reach the dialogue once, in plain words, without a lesson.

## Dialogue findings

### D1 · Major · Confession is an ordered lore briefing

- Where: L53–L57, dawn weigh-out, Beshan's answer to "For the brake leaf?"
- Text:
> "It was intact when I delivered it. I cut outside the letter bed, left two fingers of backing, braced the lip before lifting, and the east mate is still seated.
> Lurem's reader takes either original strip. He said he would price redemption for one day, but use began at first bell.
> I meant to buy it back after the shelf settlement, before the keepers closed inventory.
- Problem: The narration says "the words came in a rush," but the speech is an orderly report: cut technique, then the machine, then the contract terms, then his plan, then an alibi. That is exposition plus uniform fluency under pressure. "Lurem's reader takes either original strip" is there for the reader (L103 explains the same thing in narration). Ground: the plan doesn't hold together for the reader. Redemption was priced for one day, but he meant to buy the strip back "after the shelf settlement." The reader can't tell whether "shelf settlement" is a place past the gate or a payment, or how he could redeem the strip in Cerdovan from the far side of the shelf within that day. Kedra's "You meant to cross first" gives the gist, but the plan she is judging stays murky.
- Fix: Keep the cut-technique defensiveness, which is Beshan and pays off at L319 ("Beshan's cut had been neat"). Cut the reader-mechanism sentence. Make "use began at first bell" land as the thing he knew and did anyway. State the buy-back plan in one concrete clause that fits a one-day window (for example, sending the price back by rider from the shelf post), and keep it consistent with C1's fix. Let the rush actually be one: a restart, a fact out of order, a trailing clause. Illustrative only: "He said he'd hold it a day — but he'd feed it at first bell, he told me that too, and I thought once we were over the shelf I could send—"

### D2 · Major · Kedra caps each crew objection

- Where: L77–L91, dawn, ordering the wagons turned
- Text:
> "The animals have already walked out their stiffness."
> "They can walk it back in."
> "Then keep the lid on."
> "Tie it for a turn made angry."
- Problem: Tennis Match with last-word lines. The driver, cook and rigger each raise a real objection (road, animals, meal, load), and Kedra answers each with a comeback of equal weight, three of them quips. Minutes earlier she learned her apprentice sold a name and that her own wire order would have failed on the descent. The quips make her sound amused and in control when she should be pressed. "A turn made angry" is a figurative line with no clear meaning.
- Fix: Keep the crew objections; they are role-specific and good. Keep "We aren't taking the shelf yet." Replace the three caps with plain orders, repetition, or no answer at all (she can talk over them or answer only the driver). Illustrative only: "Tie it again. We turn on the narrow side."

### D3 · Major · Lurem speaks Kedra's and Sovet's register

- Where: L121–L127 and L145–L153 (engine standoff); L205–L221 (terms)
- Text:
> "Half," Lurem corrected.
> "Sold yesterday. Clear face, seller's mark, full exchange.
> "My custody is settled."
> Sovet laid the numbered board beside the folded plates. "Not my inventory."
> "The brake stays. Clean metal and bond to me. Strip, cessation, present-lot labor to you."
- Problem: Identical voices and correction ping-pong. Cover the tags on L121–L153 and three speakers are trading the same clipped declarative corrections ("Half." / "The strip from the north cistern…" / "Sold yesterday…" / "Until we settle custody." / "My custody is settled." / "Not my inventory."). Lurem even speaks Sovet's inventory notation. In the terms, Kedra's "Agreed. The brake stays with us." and Lurem's clause-by-clause recap could belong to either of them. Lurem's actual traits are in narration, not his mouth: the paid crew, buyers expecting civic alloy, sunk cost, and repeating the brake-leaf claim "exactly as if she had failed to hear" (L169, L189). "Not my inventory." is a cap that Sovet's real count at L161 already covers.
- Fix: Give Lurem a trader's reasoning: grievance over a fair sale, his costs, a push to split the loss, and repetition of his claim in his own words. Make Sovet the only one who talks in counts and positions. Cut "Not my inventory." or turn it into an action (he taps the board). Keep all terms content, the sunset/keeper-charge compromise, and "Two witnesses who are not your apprentice". Drop the full recap line, since L301 already has Lurem checking the written bargain. Keep "You ring my floor now?" and "If this is a caravan dispute, take it past my line." Illustrative only: "I bought a key. Your boy sold it with his own mark on the sale. You want it back, you pay for my morning too."

### D4 · Major · Collar ping-pong ends on an unstaged silence and a cap

- Where: L175–L187, bargaining at the loading block
- Text:
> "The collars were mine."
> "They're worn stock."
> "They held."
> Kedra did not turn. "No one asked the axle."
> "It was becoming a long silence," the driver said.
- Problem: Correction ping-pong in matched one-liners, then Ground/staging. The driver claims to be filling a silence the text never gives: "They held." is answered at once by his "They did." "No one asked the axle" exists to set up his last word (Punchline Patch). This buries the best beat in the bargain. "They held" is the one claim Kedra cannot answer, because her wire would have parted on that descent (L63).
- Fix: Stage the silence. After "They held.", give Kedra one beat of not answering, with no gloss on why. Then the driver's "They did." fills it in front of everyone, and his excuse line and her "axle" cap become unnecessary. Cut both, or give Kedra one plain, unwitty reaction. Keep "They did."

### D5 · Major · Final disposition is a ruling; the relationship moves only in narration

- Where: L349–L365, lantern disposition by the provision wagon
- Text:
> "Your buying and cutting privileges are removed," she told him.
> Kedra's order to bind the cracked brake went into the day's account too, but it did not cancel his cut.
> "Sovet signs the inventory," she said. "I read it. Continued apprenticeship depends on what is in it and whether you finish."
> His lips worked around a fuller answer, then gave it up. "Understood."
- Problem: A procedure routine in the final meaningful exchange between master and apprentice. Kedra speaks to Beshan in the statute voice she used on Lurem. The wound the story opened ("And you did not wake me," L71) never comes back, and her share of the blame (the wire order) never enters the conversation. The L361 narration does that accounting for her, which is the Philosopher pattern carried by narration. The exchange changes Beshan's status but not the relationship, and "Understood." closes it like a contract.
- Fix: Keep every term (privileges removed, share to keeper labor, twelve days' provisions, apprenticeship contingent on Sovet's inventory) and keep her refusal to absolve. Add one beat of contact: let Beshan start the fuller answer (the wire, the descent), and let Kedra answer it plainly before returning to terms. Alternatively, let her return to "wake me" as an instruction. No apology speech, no lesson. Then cut L361's last sentence, or keep only her practical reasons for not dismissing him. Say "Continued apprenticeship depends on…" the way she would say it aloud. Illustrative only: Beshan: "The wire wouldn't have—" / Kedra: "I know. I was on that descent."

### D6 · Minor · Setup-and-cap exchanges with Sovet and the rigger

- Where: L155–L161 (Sovet's count); L305–L311 (cracked plate)
- Text:
> "Do you want a number or an answer I can witness?"
> "Then stop asking while the pieces move."
> "It weighs less than my boot."
> "Then your boot can wait."
- Problem: Punchline Patch. "Both." exists to trigger "Then stop asking…", and Sovet then answers anyway, so the rebuke is performance. The rigger's boot line exists only to set up Sovet's cap. Sovet's real content is excellent ("thirteen entries… fifteen objects… That lintel was two…").
- Fix: Let Sovet answer "How much passed?" directly with the count and the lintel problem (a gesture can keep her back). Cut the number-or-answer riddle and the "Then" rebuke. At L305–L311, "It is free." / "It is hanging." can stay as Sovet's job; cut the boot pair or let the rigger simply wait, irritated.

### D7 · Minor · Opening referent and staging slips

- Where: L15 and L29–L31, dawn weigh-out
- Text:
> "Four-ten if you scrape off his breakfast," the cook said.
> Under the lead wagon, the driver said, "You'd better tell her what you paid with."
> "I paid for it," Beshan answered too quickly.
- Problem: "His breakfast" has no antecedent (whose, and on what?). The dangling modifier puts the driver under the wagon, but L39 has Beshan slide out from under it and L45 has the driver beside Kedra. The driver knows the payment was improper with no visible basis; one clause (he watched the fitting) covers it. "Answered too quickly" is an adverb tag doing the line's work.
- Fix: Recast as, say, "Crouched by the lead wagon, the driver said toward the axle bed…". Give "his" a referent or change the joke. Drop "too quickly" and let the speed show in the line.

### D8 · Minor · Crew chorus and water jokes don't track

- Where: L195–L203, crew complaint; L383, departure
- Text:
> "That is our profit."
> "That is our road."
> "You have water for either if you stop spilling meal into it," said the cook.
> "It will be wet because I have four days of water for a three-day road."
- Problem: The unattributed complaints all have the same formal, no-contraction shape. The cook tells someone else to stop spilling meal into the water, but the only person shown spilling soaking water is the cook himself (L15, L49), so the referent is inverted. At L383 the joke's logic is muddled (extra water does not make a road wet), and it contradicts L201's complaint that there was no water for the low bends, with no resupply shown.
- Fix: Rough up the chorus (contractions, overlap, one personal grievance). Make the cook's retort self-directed or cut it. At L383, use a plain grumble or a joke that works, and decide whether water is short.

### D9 · Minor · Witness sequence is muddled

- Where: L221–L231, signing the terms
- Text:
> "Write it. Two witnesses who are not your apprentice."
> The cook said, "My hands are wet."
> The driver came forward as second witness. Sovet refused the pen when Kedra offered it.
> Lurem supplied one of his hands instead.
- Problem: The cook's excuse and Kedra's "Dry one" make him the first witness and the driver the second. Then Kedra offers the pen to Sovet, and one of Lurem's hands signs "instead." The reader can't tell who witnessed or why Sovet was asked.
- Fix: Settle the two witnesses (for example the driver and one of Lurem's hands, with the cook excused and Sovet declining) and order the beats to match. Keep Sovet's "I witness return after I see return"; it is his earned principle and it pays off at L339.

### D10 · Minor · "Sold our blocks" has no setup

- Where: L247–L249, the jam
- Text:
> "With what?" the rigger asked. "You just sold our blocks."
> "They are not his until the strip is out. Use the short oak."
- Problem: Ground/knowledge. The sale was the clean-metal wagon ("Sheet, rail, and bars?"). Wooden blocks were never part of it, so the rigger supplies content missing from the prior beat, and Kedra accepts it. "Just" is also off: the terms were signed before the lift work began.
- Fix: Either establish in one clause at L193 or L205 that the blocking rides with the clean-metal load, or give the rigger a true obstacle (the blocks are under the sold tarp with one of Lurem's hands beside them). Keep Kedra's quick override.

### D11 · Minor · Stock beats and tag verbs

- Where: L73, L95, L123, L245 (also L31 in D7)

> His mouth opened. Nothing came out.
> He swallowed. "For how long?"
> "Hold the hoist," she ordered.

## Continuity and prompt findings

### C1 · Major · The bond is paid out after it lapses

- Where: L57 (confession), L193 and L221 (offer and terms), L231 (signing), L377 (transfer)
- Text:
> "If we missed the gate, the bond was gone."
> "Clean-metal wagon. All of it as weighed this morning. And the shelf-road bond."
> They marked the terms on the loading block while the animals shifted and the shelf-road margin ran out.
> The shelf-road bond went into the same case.
- Problem: Beshan says missing the gate forfeits the bond. Kedra then offers the bond as payment, the gate margin runs out during the signing, and Lurem, who haggles over worn collars, accepts it next morning as major value. The reader can't tell what Lurem gains, and the story's central price looks partly empty.
- Fix: In one phrase, make the bond transferable (for example, a paid passage warrant good at a later gate, which Lurem can use or sell). Adjust L57 so that missing the gate costs the caravan its crossing, not the bond itself. Keep the consequence that the caravan takes the low road.
