# Opus review: Life with a Girlfriend with Shrinking Powers

Independent audit for a later editing pass. It is not a `review.md` verdict,
does not change canon state, and does not authorize edits by itself.

## Verdict

- Dialogue: REVISE — Luz's final apology names something the story shows she never hid. The argument, the decisive exchange at the gap and the reconciliation all use one polished register of rights and procedure that spells out the theme.
- Prose: REVISE — the ending explains the lesson again in narration, in a policy paragraph and in a caption for the closing sign. The rest is clean apart from rulebook exposition and explanatory glosses.
- Continuity and prompt: REVISE — the prompt is fully delivered at about 3,000 words. But the cabinet layout at the climax is hard to follow, and nobody remarks on doing the evening job right next to the known corroded pipe.
- Edit scope: targeted-dialogue
- Existing review.md said: PASS (final pass 2, 2026-08-03) with zero findings and no Dialogue line at all; disagree, because nobody ever audited the dialogue.

The best dialogue here is the least verbal: the tug code, "Don't," "Hand," "Blanket?" / "Yes," and the palm Miles sets beside the ramp. Those moments carry trust and consent through action and need no explanation. Whenever the couple talk at length about the relationship, both drift into the same composed voice of rights and procedure: "list of permissions," "what I may do," "That's my assessment," "That is what I can do," "Agreed." The final scene ends in a pair of mirrored aphorisms that the next paragraph writes down as rules anyway. The edit should keep the wordless moments and the short practical lines as they are. Luz's anger in the argument should be personal, and cold and noise should cut her speech short at the gap. Miles's fear should come through in how he talks, and the reconciliation should be concrete, lopsided and warm.

## Dialogue findings

### D1 · Blocking · Final apology names a concealment the story never showed

- Where: L295 (final table scene), checked against L41–L49 and L87–L89 (breakfast and the sink argument)
- Text:
> They logged eight minutes and the tremor together, then added toast, water, and a warm mug to the recovery.
> Checking its boxes was easier than asking why he had seen that tremor before.
> “I’m sorry I hid the tremor. It happened three times, and I kept thinking that if I told you, the information would become permission for you to decide.”
- Problem: Ground/knowledge and continuity. The tremor was never hidden. Miles saw it at L41, they logged it together at L47, he had seen it on earlier cycles (L49), and he asked whether "the shaking" had come back (L87). What Luz really hid was the cause (she said "Hungry" when she was cold), how often it happened, and that it could delay her return. She admits exactly that at L209 ("I'm colder than I told you"). So the apology at the centre of the reconciliation names the wrong thing, and Miles's reply ("It almost did.") lets it pass.
- Fix: Make the apology name what she actually kept back: calling it hunger, the cold, three cycles running. Keep "three" and keep her fear that the information would become permission; both are LOCKED (`universe/characters.md`, Luz Navarro). Keep the joint logging at L47, which is LOCKED practice. If needed, change what L47 records to the hunger she claimed rather than a plain "tremor." Illustrative only: "I'm sorry I kept calling it hunger. It was the cold, all three times."

### D2 · Major · The reconciliation is a mirrored pair of thesis statements

- Where: L293–L301, kitchen table after the rupture (final meaningful exchange)
- Text:
> I made fear sound like authority. And I kept labeling things because labels let me pretend I could guarantee your safety.
> I can owe you accurate information without owing you the final choice.
> “And I can say I’m afraid without turning it into an order.”
- Problem: Philosopher and Identical Voices in the closing exchange. Miles explains his own opening joke (the labels) and describes himself in abstract nouns. Luz answers with a matching apology and an aphorism. Miles then finishes her sentence pattern ("I can … without …"), so for these lines the two speakers are interchangeable. The exchange states the rules that L303 then writes down, and the closing sign states them a third time. It reads as a reconciliation script rather than two exhausted people at a table, and the prompt's "warm romantic" tone drops away just as the story ends.
- Fix: Keep Miles's apology for the veto and Luz's disclosure, both LOCKED. Keep "It almost did." and her "Yes." Make his apology concrete and a little clumsy: the line he wrote while she slept, the tape, the sheet in his hand. Cut the explanation of the labels, since the opening already shows it. Cut both "I can … without …" lines and let the rule-writing paragraph carry the terms. "You needed the truth to spot for me" can stay. Give the speakers unequal turns and let some affection or humour back in. Illustrative only: "I wrote it while you were asleep. I didn't even tell you it was there."

### D3 · Major · The decisive exchange at the gap plays as a briefing and a contract

- Where: L207–L221, Miles at the toe-kick gap after the first pull fails (decisive exchange)
- Text:
> “Options,” he said toward the gap, forcing his voice level. “The valve failed on the first pull.
> Luz edged close enough that her unamplified voice reached him between surges of water. “I’m colder than I told you. It’s happened on the last three cycles.”
> “My early return may hesitate. Less than three minutes to the hard limit.”
> He made himself add, “That’s my assessment. What are you choosing?”
> Luz nodded once. “Agreed.”
- Problem: Uniform fluency under pressure, and a negotiation routine. Luz has just fallen and is soaked. Her hands shake hard enough to make the cord jump (L199). Her voice "could barely cross a quiet metre" (L107), and the LOCKED rules say running water drowns it, while "the roar of the leak" fills the kitchen (L195). Even so, she gives a four-sentence status report, including a time figure Miles has just told her. Miles tells her what she already knows ("The valve failed on the first pull") and reads out a menu of options. Then both finish with "That's my assessment," "That is what I can do" and "Agreed," as if signing an agreement. Everything the scene needs does happen: she discloses, she chooses, he sets his own limit. But it sounds like a meeting, and it drops the cord signals the story has just set up.
- Fix: Keep the content, all of which is LOCKED: her disclosure of cold and tremor over three cycles, her choice of one more attempt, the idea of seating the loop behind the clip, and his 23-minute limit on his own part. Cut her speech to the few words that could carry between surges, and let the rest come through gesture. Cut the recap and the menu from Miles, and let his fear show in how he speaks: repeats, numbers, one unfinished sentence. Keep a short version of "What are you choosing?", because handing her the choice is the change in him, but drop "That's my assessment." Replace "Agreed" with a nod or a single word, not a tug, since single tugs already mean stop. Illustrative only: "Colder than I said. Three cycles now. Coming back might be slow."

### D4 · Major · The first argument states the theme in the language of rights

- Where: L63–L93, crouched at the dripping sink after breakfast
- Text:
> “All useful facts. None of them lets you turn our checklist into your list of permissions.”
> “About what I may do with my body.”
> “Neither is ‘no plumbing.’”
- Problem: Philosopher and the house register at a key moment. This is the first time the central conflict is said out loud. Luz meets it with a polished contrast ("our checklist" against "your list of permissions") and the vocabulary of permission ("what I may do"). She sounds like a position paper, not like someone who has just found a rule about her body added without her knowledge. After that, the exchange becomes a run of balanced one-line rebuttals, each ending on a neat last word. The fight has a shape but no heat, and the theme is used up in the first scene. The replies do all follow logically, and the exchange does change what they know and how far they trust each other.
- Fix: Keep "No plumbing." (it comes out too fast, which gives him away), "You actually wrote that in?", Miles's list of hazards, "It's one line.", the fan exchange, and "Neither is 'no plumbing.'" as the one earned retort. Rewrite L75 as a personal, heated reaction to being overruled on paper (when did he write it, and why didn't he say so), not as a principle. Drop the legal "may" at L79 so the line hits bluntly. Illustrative only: "Then argue with me. Don't just write it down."

### D5 · Major · "The clip" is never explained and gets confused with the hinge clip

- Where: L109, L147–L151, L215, L227, L249 (the hinge job, then the valve)
- Text:
> “Pin’s aligned,” she reported from the cabinet. “I’m setting the clip.”
> “Clip on the handle,” she said over the radio. “I can move it.”
> “Clip’s clear,” Luz said. “Handle’s seized. I can’t get the torque.”
> “One more attempt. The loop slipped because it sat in front of the clip. I can get behind it.”
- Problem: Ground/referent at the mechanical heart of the climax. Minutes after "setting the clip" on the hinge, a different clip turns up on the valve handle with no description. "I can move it" could mean the clip or the handle. "Clip's clear" sounds as if the clip is gone, yet later the loop sits "in front of" it and ends up seated "behind the cleared clip." The reader can't picture why the first pull slipped or why the second works, and the whole success of the scene depends on that.
- Fix: At L147, say in a few words what the valve clip does (for example, a spring clip that has to be held up before the handle will turn). Use "clear" in one sense only. At L215, say where the loop goes relative to the clip. Rename the hinge part at L109 (pin, retainer) so "clip" only ever means the valve clip. The valve clip itself is LOCKED ("Luz cleared the hidden valve's clip"), so it stays.

### D6 · Minor · A thesis sentence in the middle of the crisis

- Where: L135, flooded kitchen, before Luz goes in
- Text:
> “I don’t think that makes the choice mine. But I am afraid.”
- Problem: Philosopher. Water is heading for the outlet and the timer says 18 minutes, yet Miles stops to state the story's principle of consent. "Luz, I'm afraid." and "But I am afraid." already carry the moment. The middle sentence reads as the author's thesis, and his next action (the ramp and open palm, L139) already shows he is not claiming the choice.
- Fix: Cut "I don't think that makes the choice mine." or shrink it to a half-line. Keep both statements of fear (LOCKED: he "presented the facts and his fear") and keep "Good fact."

### D7 · Minor · Small Ground slips in single lines

- Where: L89 (sink argument), L111 (evening cycle), L219 (gap exchange)
- Text:

> “Three minutes. I should label this spoon.”
