# Story plan: The Two-Tug Rule

## Story promise

An established adult couple have made one partner's repeatable shrinking ability part of ordinary apartment life through humor, rules, and practiced consent. When a corroded kitchen line bursts while Luz Navarro is already small, the same arrangements that usually make the power mundane become a survival system. Miles Arden must stop disguising fear as authority, while Luz must stop treating honest disclosure of risk as a surrender of independence. Closing the inaccessible valve requires her expertise and chosen exposure to danger, his full-sized leverage and restraint, and their mutual trust in a backup signal neither can use alone.

The complete story should deliver warm domestic comedy, a physically legible household crisis, and a sincere romantic resolution in which they revise their shared practice without curing, suppressing, owning, or romanticizing the power.

## Narrative frame

- **Length:** Target 3,350 words, within the required 2,500–4,000-word range.
- **POV:** Close third person centered only on Miles Arden, the partner who does not shrink. Luz's intentions must be known through what she says, signals, and chooses rather than through viewpoint access.
- **Tense:** Past.
- **Setting:** One third-floor apartment in an unnamed modern-like locality during a wet Saturday evening. Keep the wider shared world offstage and make no literal-Earth claim.
- **Tone and genre:** Warm romantic speculative fiction. Begin with competent domestic comedy, tighten into a water-and-electricity crisis, and finish with quiet affection and earned practical change.
- **Audience:** YA, ages 14+. The partners are adults, ages twenty-nine and twenty-eight. Affection may include ordinary kisses, touch, and shared-home intimacy, but no graphic sexual content.
- **Working/final-title option:** *The Two-Tug Rule*. It names the backup communication protocol and the relationship lesson without defining a universal power system.

## Core characters and arcs

### Miles Arden — age twenty-nine, non-shrinking viewpoint partner

- **Want:** Keep Luz safe by anticipating every hazard and making their home predictable.
- **Need:** Admit that fear belongs to him and does not grant him authority over Luz's body or choices. He must offer information and help, then trust her answer.
- **Pressure:** A leak he cannot reach, electrical risk, poor visibility, a failing radio, and the visible countdown to Luz's automatic return leave him unable to protect her through direct control.
- **Arc:** Miles begins as a loving, funny, exhausting protocol-keeper who has added a unilateral “no plumbing” rule after a prior harmless close call. In the crisis, he nearly grabs Luz and later nearly tears open the cabinet, but stops when she revokes those approaches. He gives her the facts, follows her chosen plan, and supplies the actions only his scale permits. Afterward he replaces a veto with a mutually written boundary and speaks his fear plainly.

### Luz Navarro — age twenty-eight, girlfriend and shrinking partner

- **Want:** Keep her ability useful and ordinary, not treated as a medical defect or a permanent emergency.
- **Need:** Recognize that equality also requires giving Miles accurate information about costs and risk instead of minimizing her chills and tremor to prevent his worry.
- **Pressure:** She alone can enter the cabinet void, but cold spray, reduced absolute strength, radio failure, and the fixed return limit make continued work dangerous.
- **Arc:** Luz begins confident and practiced, teasing Miles's labels while concealing that the latest shrink cycles leave her colder than before. When the line bursts, she chooses to enter the cavity rather than being deployed or carried into it. At the decisive point she reports her tremor and remaining time honestly, redesigns the attempt around both partners' capacities, and gives the signals that make success possible. She voluntarily pauses plumbing-related shrinking until they retrofit the access, while retaining the power and her sole authority to use it.

### Relationship movement

Their conflict is not “risk versus safety” but two incomplete versions of partnership: Miles treats preparation as permission to veto, while Luz treats self-determination as a reason to withhold information that affects them both. The climax proves that trust is active coordination: Luz owns bodily decisions and small-scale expertise; Miles owns his fear, his large-scale actions, and whether he honors her instructions. Both end more candid and neither becomes subordinate.

## Local shrinking mechanics

These rules belong only to Luz's observed ability. Do not call them laws of miracles, magic, transformation, or the world.

### Activation, consent, and reversal

- Luz is the only living source and operator. She activates by planting both feet, taking one controlled five-count breath, and deliberately choosing a target height. No word, emotion, person, application, device, or artifact can trigger her.
- The transition takes about five seconds. Luz can stop anywhere between her ordinary 168 centimetres and a practiced minimum of 12 centimetres. She never goes below that minimum in this story.
- Luz may reverse early by choosing to return and taking three slow breaths. Reversal takes about eight seconds. No one else can initiate, delay, or cancel it.
- Below half height, a single cycle can last no more than twenty-four minutes. At that limit, return begins automatically from Luz's own living process even if the location is unsafe. The couple's alarms at twenty and twenty-three minutes are warnings, not controllers.
- Cold or low blood sugar can delay a chosen early reversal by up to twenty seconds, but cannot extend the twenty-four-minute limit. This hesitation is a known failure behavior and makes the location of reversal important.

### Size, cost, and cooldown

- The smallest practiced height is 12 centimetres; most household work is done between 12 and 25 centimetres.
- The smaller and longer Luz stays, the faster she loses body heat and blood sugar. Early signs are cold fingers and a fine hand tremor; pushing toward the limit brings weakness, nausea, and impaired precision.
- After returning to ordinary size, Luz needs warmth, food, fluids, and at least six hours before another safe activation. Trying sooner risks an immediate, uncontrolled return and a longer recovery, so there is no second shrink during the crisis.

### Clothing and carried matter

- Fitted clothing changes with Luz. At activation, dry nonliving items totaling no more than 250 grams also change if they are fully enclosed by her clothing or held continuously against bare skin.
- Loose items fall away at their original size. Anything she picks up after shrinking stays its existing size. No living creature changes with her.
- Her small battery radio sits under a fitted sleeve and therefore changes with her. It is ordinary technology, remains passive, and neither activates nor controls the ability.

### Communication and scale behavior

- Luz's voice keeps its adult pitch but becomes quiet with her reduced lung volume. At 12 centimetres she is intelligible only within about one metre in a quiet room; running water overwhelms her voice.
- She and Miles use paired battery radios for routine work. The radios resist splashes but are not submersion-proof. Their non-electronic fallback is a cord code: one tug means stop, two means pull steadily, and three means give slack.
- Luz's weight decreases roughly with volume while useful muscle force decreases roughly with cross-section. She is strong relative to her small weight but has little absolute torque: she can climb, shift a clip, and guide a cord, but cannot turn a seized full-sized valve.
- Short falls are less injurious than at ordinary size, but moving air, cold surfaces, and even shallow fast water have disproportionate force and cooling effects. A pressurized kitchen spray can knock her down or sweep her into a gap.
- Use these observations consistently and locally. Do not attempt a universal explanation for displaced mass or energy.

## Established household protocols

- Luz announces target height and intended duration; Miles answers with the hazards he has cleared. Only Luz decides whether to proceed, stop, or reverse.
- Fans, burners, taps, drains, doors, and relevant appliances are switched off, plugged, blocked, or physically unplugged before a routine. Controls receive a removable strip so neither partner can start them by habit.
- Luz begins and ends on a bright nonslip mat. Independent timers sound at twenty and twenty-three minutes.
- They test both radios before activation. A shallow emergency tray holds a dry towel for a ramp, a flashlight, glucose gel, and a spool of thin utility cord.
- Miles does not touch, lift, enclose, or carry Luz without a direct request. If communication fails, he freezes or de-energizes hazards first, then offers an open route, cloth ramp, or still open hand rather than grabbing her.
- One, two, and three cord tugs mean stop, pull steadily, and give slack. Establish the code comically in the opening when Miles has relabeled it despite both knowing it by heart.
- After every return, they log duration and symptoms together, then use warmth, food, and water. The log is shared evidence, not permission Miles grants.

## Stakes, reversals, and escalation

- **Physical stakes:** Cold pressurized water can sweep or chill Luz; exposed wiring can energize the flood; a forced return in the narrow cabinet void can crush or trap her; Miles can worsen the rupture by wrenching apart the fixed cabinet.
- **Practical stakes:** The apartment and the unit below are flooding, and delay increases structural and electrical damage.
- **Relationship stakes:** If Miles converts fear into command, Luz cannot safely rely on him as a spotter. If Luz hides symptoms to preserve autonomy, Miles cannot make informed coordinated choices.
- **First reversal:** A practiced comic routine reveals that the system works, but Luz's concealed tremor and Miles's unilateral new rule show that the partnership around it does not.
- **Second reversal:** They agree to postpone all plumbing work, yet the corroded line bursts during an unrelated, properly cleared small-scale task.
- **Third reversal:** Luz is not accidentally lost in the cabinet; after hearing Miles's assessment, she chooses to use the remaining cycle to enter the inaccessible void.
- **Fourth reversal:** The reliable radio fails under the spray, forcing them onto the tactile backup Miles had treated as excessive labeling.
- **Climactic reversal:** Miles's first controlled pull cannot move the valve because the cord slips. Instead of taking over, he stops on Luz's signal. Luz discloses her tremor and low time, then supplies the missing placement; his next pull supplies the missing force.
- **Resolution turn:** Stopping the water is not enough. Miles must preserve Luz's exit and allow her to choose the moment and place of reversal rather than treating retrieval as ownership.

## Causal scene sequence

### Scene 1 — The ordinary impossible thing (350 words)

At breakfast, Miles spots Luz through a routine 15-centimetre shrink to repair the jammed latch inside a dry, unplugged countertop appliance. The scene establishes their affectionate ease, her adult competence, the five-second activation, clothing and radio behavior, the bright mat, timers, open-hand consent rule, and the emergency tray. Domestic comedy comes from Miles having relabeled the cord's obvious one-two-three tug code and Luz asking whether he plans to label gravity next. She finishes, exits under her own power, and deliberately returns on the mat. Miles notices a faint hand tremor; Luz calls it hunger and changes the subject. Because he is worried, he checks the protocol sheet again instead of naming the worry.

### Scene 2 — A rule written by one person (400 words)

While they clean up, a recurring drip sounds beneath the kitchen sink. Miles finds dampness and a corroded cold-water branch line behind a fixed toe-kick, too deep for either adult to reach. The apartment stop valve is stiff and the reachable handle does not fully isolate that branch. Maintenance cannot attend until Monday. Luz suggests that on a future fresh cycle she could inspect the hidden quarter-turn valve, but Miles reveals that he has added “no plumbing” to their protocol after a prior fan near-miss. Luz objects that a jointly used checklist cannot become his list of permissions. He argues from danger without admitting fear; she insists she is fine without admitting the tremor has happened twice. They agree to place a basin, stop using the sink, and postpone the problem—not to authorize a shrink. Their argument remains unresolved but neither acts without agreement.

### Scene 3 — The safe task stops being safe (450 words)

That evening Luz begins a separate, mutually cleared 12-centimetre cycle to reset the adjacent cabinet's dropped hinge pin while Miles cooks on an electric hotplate across the room. The sink taps are off, the drain is plugged, the exhaust fan and dishwasher are disabled, the radios work, and the emergency tray is open. The earlier dampness has weakened the corroded line; ordinary building pressure finally splits it. Cold water blasts into the base cabinet and across the floor. Miles kills the hotplate and starts toward Luz. She is still on the dry mat, not caught or deployed. He orders her to reverse; she asks for facts instead. He reports the inaccessible leak, the failed apartment stop, the visible hidden valve, the water's path toward an outlet, and eighteen minutes remaining. Luz decides to enter the cabinet void while small. Miles says he is afraid, but he does not claim the decision. He cuts the kitchen breaker and hands her the choice of a towel ramp or open palm. She chooses the ramp.

### Scene 4 — What size cannot do alone (550 words)

By flashlight, Luz crosses the towel and climbs the fixed plumbing braces. Miles dams the spreading water away from the outlet and calls the unit below, actions only his reach and speed permit. Luz reaches the hidden valve and discovers that its safety clip can be shifted but the handle needs full-sized torque. She directs Miles to feed the utility cord through the toe-kick gap. A ricochet of water knocks her from the brace onto a shallow shelf; the fall does not badly injure her, but the cold steals precision. Her radio crackles out after getting soaked. Miles instinctively reaches into the gap, then stops when her last audible word is “don't.” He follows lost-communication protocol: breaker remains off, his hand withdraws, the flashlight stays fixed, and he lays the cord where she can choose to take it.

### Scene 5 — The first pull fails (650 words)

Miles can no longer see Luz continuously. He feels her guide the cord around the valve and waits for the agreed signal. Two tugs tell him to pull steadily. He pulls with controlled force rather than jerking; the wet loop slips and the valve does not turn. One tug makes him stop. His twenty-minute alarm sounds, then Luz uses three tugs for slack. Through a brief sightline he sees her hands shaking. Rather than issuing another order or tearing away the cabinet, Miles speaks the exact options and time toward the gap. Luz gets close enough for her weakened voice to carry and tells him plainly that she is cold, her early-reversal breathing may hesitate, and she has less than three minutes before the hard return. She nevertheless chooses one final attempt because she can seat the loop behind the clip. Miles accepts the choice but sets his boundary clearly: at the twenty-three-minute alarm he will stop pulling and open the exit, whatever the valve's state. Luz agrees. This is negotiation under pressure, not his command or her concealment.

### Scene 6 — Two tugs (650 words)

Luz uses her relative strength and spatial access to crawl behind the handle, lift the safety clip, and wedge the cord into a nonslip angle. Miles keeps the cord slack until two deliberate tugs arrive. On her rhythm he leans back with full-sized leverage. The valve begins to turn; spray intensifies and obscures her. One tug orders him to freeze before the moving handle can pin her. After Luz changes position, two more tugs authorize the final pull. Miles supplies torque while Luz holds the cord seated and clears the clip. The valve closes and the jet dies. The twenty-three-minute alarm sounds.

Miles resists the urge to grab blindly or rip out the cabinet. He drains the dam into towels, fixes the flashlight on the exit, and lays a dry cloth ramp. Luz emerges under her own power, accepts his stationary hand only as a bridge across the last wet gap, then steps onto the mat. She chooses reversal. The cold causes the seeded hesitation, and Miles talks her through the three breaths without touching or claiming control. She returns to ordinary size before the hard limit. He wraps a blanket around her only after she says yes, gives her glucose and water, and calls emergency maintenance. The physical danger is over, and neither partner could have ended it alone.

### Scene 7 — A protocol both can sign (300 words)

After warmth, food, cleanup, and professional isolation of the damaged branch, Miles apologizes specifically for writing a bodily veto into a shared document and admits that labeling everything had been easier than saying he could not guarantee her safety. Luz apologizes for hiding the repeated tremor and explains that disclosure felt dangerously close to surrendering authority. They agree that information can be mandatory for coordination while the final bodily choice remains hers. Luz voluntarily rules out shrinking around live plumbing until an accessible shutoff extension, waterproof communication, and a verified dry route exist; this is not a cure or permanent restriction.

Days later, with the branch repaired, they install an external valve pull with the power off and water isolated. Luz, small by her own choice on the bright mat, checks the cord routing. Two tugs tell Miles to test it. It closes smoothly. She rejects his offered palm with a grin, takes the towel ramp, and reverses beside him. The ending image is their old over-labeled card replaced by one sheet in both their handwriting, with the two-tug rule at its center.

## Ending and resolution requirements

- Resolve the line rupture, electrical risk, Luz's safe return, and the couple's emotional dispute on-page.
- Give Miles a specific apology and behavioral change; do not let the rescue itself stand in for accountability.
- Give Luz a specific admission and chosen change without making her autonomy the problem.
- Preserve Luz's ability as useful, repeatable, self-directed, and part of their future domestic life.
- End on a practiced act of cooperation, not a cure, promise of zero risk, ownership arrangement, or permanent confinement.

## Canon and content constraints

- Treat this as a standalone local incident in one persistent-world era and region. Do not name Earth, a plane, an established setting, or a shared transformation source.
- Do not connect Luz's ability to Northbridge, the crown-era dragons, the Sleepless Majesty, Solstice, heartseeds, portals, or any admitted character or system.
- Luz remains the same adult person at every size. Preserve her legal, intellectual, romantic, and moral agency in narration and action.
- The nonliving radio, timer, cord, labels, and tools are passive ordinary technology. They do not think, choose, use magic, activate the ability, or override Luz.
- Do not generalize mechanics, costs, consent, love, desire, or sacrifice into universal rules.
- Keep carrying and touch explicitly consensual. Do not describe Luz as cargo, a possession, a pet, a toy, a child, a collectible, or a problem Miles must cure.
- Avoid fetish framing, voyeurism, humiliation based on size, graphic sex, infantilizing diction, or erotic focus on size disparity. Domestic jokes should concern labels, scale logistics, and the couple's familiarity.
- Do not add a named pet, assistant, device, application, organization, heroic identity, size-related nickname, or person-like entity during drafting without a new registry check.
- Maintain close third past through Miles and keep all relative scale, water, airflow, reach, force, sound, clothing, carried matter, timing, cost, and cooldown details consistent.
