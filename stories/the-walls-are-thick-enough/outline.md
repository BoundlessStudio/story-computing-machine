# Outline

## Story

- Premise and central promise: For four months, thirty-four-year-old Tazri Solun has crossed Karevic Palace's Gilded Garden at sunset in a near-transparent ceremonial veil. Thirty-nine-year-old Jaseth Varo, Merevale's treaty notary, observes from the far gate without touching her. Before Emperor Belisar expels him and confines her, Tazri makes the prompt-required invitation. Resolve the forbidden night, their free choices, and a treaty crisis desire cannot solve.
- Focal pressure or attachment: Tazri wants Merevale's port levy safe and one bodily choice outside diplomacy. She may flee, surrender the levy, or invoke a tribunal and stay. Jaseth wants the articles preserved and Tazri alive but refuses to become her rescuer or self-punishment; he also wants her.
- Counterforce or complication: Fifty-eight-year-old Belisar married Tazri for Merevale's revenue and naval access. Palace law withholds a foreign bride's joint seat and dower protections until consummation, so he keeps her unbedded while pressuring her council. At dawn he will revoke Jaseth's safe-conduct, sever Tazri's correspondence, and seize the levy. Adultery would strengthen his accusation; Mireth can create privacy, not erase consequence.
- POV, distance, and information limit: Close third-person past through Tazri, attentive to exposure, doors, seals, fabric, and pauses. Jaseth's consent must emerge through observable refusals, questions, and action. Belisar remains external until a dawn confrontation.
- Governing movement and time shape: Compress earlier sunsets, then follow one sunset through negotiation, separate political preparation, intimacy, moonset departure, and dawn consequence. Keep the turn private and end without public conversion or promised reunion.
- Speculative rule or ordinary-world constraint: An unbedded bride walks the forbidden garden nightly while two witnesses attest her safety. Refusal would suspend Merevale's access to Tazri. Belisar forbids Jaseth's touch because contact voids his neutral accreditation and supports adultery charges. Her articles allow a tribunal if Belisar cuts access or takes the levy without consent. Jaseth controls his safe-conduct, horse, escort, and diplomatic exit until moonset; Tazri cannot command him. Thick walls hide sound, not legal or reproductive risk.

## Voice

- Narrative texture: Track Tazri's inventory of fabric, seals, distances, and bodily choice. Ceremonial looking is invasive; sensual detail expands only after consent changes its terms.
- Conversational texture: Public talk is formal and asymmetric. Private talk stops, restarts, irritates, and includes doors, clothes, timing, contraception, papers, and stopping. Mireth gives blunt practical interruptions.
- Rhetorical ownership: Tazri owns treaty distinctions but strains to state desire. Jaseth tests routes, evidence, costs, and reversibility. Mireth owns bodily logistics; Belisar reduces possession to precedent.
- Pressure behavior: Tazri overformalizes and corrects herself; Jaseth grows slower and literal, never commanding. A misread motive requires clarification. Mireth interrupts abstraction; Belisar withholds answers.
- Anti-default: Avoid polished seduction, dominance clichés, therapeutic confession, and wall/cage/fire aphorisms. Consent is explicit without becoming a recital; end on immediate logistics.

## Beats

1. On the fourth month's final sunset, establish the veil walk, jeweled clasp, Mireth's attendance, Jaseth's mandated distance, and their small nonverbal deviations. Mireth reports the dawn transfer and revoked safe-conduct.
2. Tazri crosses the allowed boundary, loosens her clasp herself, and makes the required walls claim. Jaseth stays distant and tests whether she means desire, escape, leverage, or self-destruction; her anger reveals mixed motives.
3. In the garden warming room, they resolve politics without contact. Tazri chooses tribunal and confinement over flight or surrender. Jaseth independently agrees to carry her petition and witness register out, sacrificing his accreditation.
4. Jaseth then prepares to leave, giving Tazri room to withdraw the invitation. She renews it personally. Establish ages, sobriety, alternatives, his exit, and freedom to stop. They discuss pregnancy plainly, choosing acts that cannot cause it and keeping a barrier available if that boundary changes.
5. Explicit intimacy may follow, with mutual action, questions, awkward adjustments, irritation, pleasure, pauses, and revised choices. Neither rank nor treaty duty becomes pressure. Tazri controls the imposed veil's removal.
6. An early guard round interrupts them. Mireth buys minutes with garment and lamp instructions. They stop, verify seals, dress, and accept an uneven ending rather than sacrificing the petition to a polished erotic finish.
7. Jaseth uses his own gate, carrying the petition and register into exile. Tazri again declines escape and stays to invoke breach. Their parting commits action without rescue, absolution, or reunion promises.
8. At dawn Tazri invokes the breach before Belisar isolates her. Filing suspends levy collection for a six-month tribunal but leaves the marriage and his power intact. He confines her household; Mireth risks dismissal. End on their practical move to guarded rooms.
