# Opus review: The Walls Are Thick Enough

Independent audit for a later editing pass. It is not a `review.md` verdict,
does not change canon state, and does not authorize edits by itself.

## Verdict

- Dialogue: REVISE — two Ground failures (Mireth's rebuke about a number she says she lacks; the dawn "breach" pair), and a correction-and-maxim rhythm runs through every political exchange.
- Prose: REVISE — narrator maxims keep restating the theme, and the narration repeatedly explains how the characters talk.
- Continuity and prompt: REVISE — the prompt is delivered, but the guards' presence during the garden talk, the accreditation/safe-conduct swap, and Mireth's status in the final scene need fixing.
- Edit scope: heavy-dialogue
- Existing review.md said: Verdict PASS, "Dialogue: PASS"; disagree. It praised "Tazri's distinctions, Jaseth's route logic, Mireth's logistics" as distinct voices, but in the political scenes all four speakers share one clipped legal register.

The dialogue splits in two. In the intimate scene and the parting, people talk like people: they misfire ("Your higher or mine?"), get embarrassed, joke that a notary "arrives indexed", fuss about a map. Wherever politics or danger enters (the garden proposition, the petition, the register, Mireth's asides, the dawn confrontation), all four speakers fall into the same register: clipped legal declaratives, corrections of each other's wording, mirrored counterfactuals, and a maxim to close the exchange. Then the narrator explains how each line should be heard. Two lines break outright: Mireth's "number I have" contradicts her own "I don't know yet", and at dawn both speakers use "breach" with an object it cannot take. The edit pass should keep the plot, the legal machinery, and the private scenes' voice, and should make the political scenes sound like frightened, tired, invested people. That means Tazri's formality as a pressure tell that can overreach or crack, Jaseth slower and plainer, Mireth practical rather than quippy, and Belisar indifferent rather than aphoristic.

## Dialogue findings

### D1 · Blocking · Mireth rebukes a demand Tazri never made

- Where: L29–L33, walking back from the turn; related L89 in the warming room
- Text:
> "By how much?"
> "I don't know yet." Mireth pinched the gauze straight. "Do not ask me to invent minutes because you dislike the number I have."
> "Thirty-six minutes if the early round is merely early," she said.
- Problem: Ground/uptake plus a self-contradiction. Tazri asked one plain question. She has not pressed Mireth, disliked an answer, or asked her to invent anything, so the rebuke answers nothing that was said. Mireth also says she has no number and in the same breath mentions "the number I have." At L89 she produces an exact figure, though nothing between L33 and L89 gives her a new source. The second sentence exists only to give Mireth a sharp last word (Punchline Patch).
- Fix: Cut the second sentence of L33. Let "I don't know yet" stand, or give it a plain source. Before or at L89, either show how Mireth learned the timing (she overheard the guard, counted the bell) or mark the figure as her estimate.

### D2 · Major · "Which walls?" is answered, then disowned

- Where: L57–L63, the prompt line at the far end of the path
- Text:
> "Which walls?" he asked.
> "The warming room."
> "That's not what I asked."
- Problem: Correction ping-pong and a Ground/uptake failure at the story's required line. Tazri answers the literal question exactly, and Jaseth rejects the answer. Only his next three questions show what he meant, so "Which walls?" works purely as a setup for the rebuke. He then turns her exposed, prompt-defining proposition into a fluent three-option legal menu ("so the treaty fails in a way that isn't your signature"). That is the composure the narrator says he loses under pressure (L137).
- Fix: Keep the required line verbatim. "Which walls?" / "The warming room." can stay, since it is a good literal question for a man who reasons by routes, but he must accept her answer and build on it. Cut "That's not what I asked." Shrink the menu to the one thing he needs to know (what she wants the room for), in plainer, less finished words. The escape, scandal, and adultery options can survive as a fragment, or through his glance at the guards. Illustrative only: He glanced at the guards and dropped his voice. "For what? Say it plainly. I can't guess this one."

### D3 · Major · Garden exchange runs on mirrored comebacks and ends on a thesis

- Where: L65–L75, same scene
- Text:
> "You know what it costs you. My safe-conduct is still mine."
> "If clean were my purpose, I would not have attended a hundred and nineteen useless sunsets."
> "And if I wanted escape, I would have worn shoes for it."
> These wants are mixed. I cannot make them pure for your comfort.
- Problem: Three house-voice moves in six lines. Jaseth corrects her wording ("what it costs you"). Then the two trade matched counterfactuals ("If … I would not have…" / "And if … I would have…"), a Tennis Match. Then Tazri states the story's motive thesis aloud (Philosopher). A woman who has just loosened her veil in sight of guards speaks as composed as a legal brief. The narration then approves the exchange (L75, see D14).
- Fix: Keep the slippers gesture, which is concrete and hers, and keep the three "I want" sentences, especially "I want one thing tonight that he did not arrange." Cut "These wants are mixed. I cannot make them pure for your comfort." Break the mirror: after "Ride away clean", give Jaseth a plain, less polished protest, or just a look and a silence, so that her shoes line answers something instead of rhyming with it. Let the pressure show in her syntax, per her design: a false start, or a sentence that overshoots into formality and stops.

### D4 · Major · Petition scene re-argues escape and ends on an epigram

- Where: L107–L135, warming room after sealing
- Text:
> "The west gate is still open," he said.
> "If you stay, they may keep you past the hearing."
> "And if he offers to spare Merevale in exchange for the levy?"
> "That is surrender with better table manners."
> "I choose the petition and confinement."
- Problem: Tazri refused escape in the garden (the shoes) and again at L113 ("No. I invoke the tribunal here."). L125–L135 then runs a checklist of alternating one-liners of equal weight (Tennis Match). It changes nothing: she answers each contingency crisply and ends on an aphorism plus a formal announcement of a decision already made (Inert exchange, Philosopher). "Spare Merevale in exchange for the levy" has no recoverable meaning: spare Merevale from what, when the levy is the thing being taken? With the tags covered, only content tells who is speaking.
- Fix: Keep L107–L121: the practical escape offer, "From whose guest room? Under whose guard?", and Mireth raising her own cost. L125–L127 may stay if its answer shows some fear. Cut L129–L135, or reduce it to the one contingency that truly costs Tazri something, answered unevenly rather than with a ruling. Cut "That is surrender with better table manners." and "I choose the petition and confinement." along with the treaty-voice gloss (D14).

### D5 · Major · The register decision is a tennis match of setups and punchlines

- Where: L139–L163, warming room
- Text:
> "Your accreditation is recognized in nine courts."
> "Eight, once I pass the gate."
> "You have what you came for," Tazri said.
> "I have what I'm taking."
- Problem: This is the story's key sacrifice, and it plays as alternating one-liners of the same weight and register (Identical Voices: cover the tags and Tazri and Jaseth cannot be told apart). Tazri telling Jaseth how many courts recognize his own accreditation is "as you know" information that exists only to set up "Eight" (Punchline Patch). "You have what you came for" / "I have what I'm taking" is a correction-riddle. The reader has to guess whether he means he came for her or that he is choosing the loss, and neither reading can be recovered with confidence. "Don't make it into your debt" (L155) is garbled and can be read as a debt owed to her.
- Fix: Keep the substance: he decides to take the register, she objects that it ends his neutrality, and he refuses to let her carry it as a debt. Tazri's objection should be about him, not a statistic. Jaseth answers shortly and without a quip. Cut the nine/eight pair. Cut the came-for/taking pair, or replace it with a plain line or an action (he checks the wallet and does not answer). Say L155's second sentence plainly. Illustrative only: "This is my decision. You don't owe me for it."

### D7 · Minor · Isolated referent and phrasing slips

- Problem: Small Ground slips, each needing a one-phrase repair:
  - L95 literally says someone might change her mind about what causes pregnancy. Mireth means changing her mind about what they do.

### D9 · Major · The aftermath with Mireth recites consent and restates the title

- Where: L403–L415, warming room after the stove-pipe knock
- Text:
> "Did you choose what happened in here?"
> "The walls hid the noise. They won't hide a missing register, a voided accreditation, or a tribunal filing."
> "I dislike that answer from both of you."
- Problem: The scene has already made both people's choices unmistakable. Mireth's two-question audit, with Tazri answering for Jaseth, reads as the story certifying consent to the reader. That is the recital the outline set out to avoid. Mireth then states the thesis (walls hide sound, not consequence) as a neat triplet built on the title, and the narrator says it again at L449 (Philosopher, twice). "Both of you" has no support: Jaseth never gave "that answer" in her hearing, and his "I know the road" was said while she was outside.
- Fix: Keep Mireth's practical care: the damp cloth, the hairpins, the clasp pushed through a different fold. Reduce the check to one question or one look that is Mireth's own concern for Tazri, not an audit of both parties. Cut the walls thesis here or at L449, not both. Cutting it here is better, because Belisar's insinuation at dawn shows the point. Cut or fix "both of you."

### D10 · Blocking · "Breach" cannot take the object either speaker gives it

- Where: L427–L429, dawn, outer chamber
- Text:
> "A wife cannot breach her husband's management of her household."
> "A duchess can breach an emperor's seizure of a treaty levy."
- Problem: Ground/verb-object at the decisive exchange. A party breaches an agreement, and a wall is breached. A wife cannot "breach" management, and a duchess cannot "breach" a seizure. Belisar means that a wife has no standing to claim breach over his management. Tazri means that a duchess can contest his seizure as a breach. The literal words scramble who is accused of breaching what, at the moment the reader must follow the legal turn. Tazri's line also exists as a mirror of his (Punchline Patch).
- Fix: Rebuild the pair around the actual claims: he denies her standing as a wife, and she asserts standing as the duchess whose levy he took without her assent. Drop the mirrored syntax. Illustrative only: "A wife has no standing against her husband's management of her house." / "Then hear it from the Duchess of Merevale. You took her levy without her assent."

### D11 · Major · The dawn confrontation trades maxims, and the narration glosses them

- Where: L431–L455, dawn
- Text:
> There it was: possession reduced to precedent, cruelty laundered through a noun.
> "You mistake delay for power."
> "No. I asked for delay."
- Problem: Belisar has been handed Tazri's register. He speaks in maxims, she corrects them ("No. I asked for delay." is the brief's "No. The train opens it." pattern), and the narrator interprets his line with an epigram so compressed the reader cannot tell which noun it means (Belisar used the verb "possess"). This is the decisive exchange, and it closes on a last-word volley. The outline's design for Belisar is that he withholds answers, which calls for indifference, not aphorism.
- Fix: Cut the L433 gloss. Replace "You mistake delay for power" with something Belisar would actually do: address the officials, name a practical consequence, or turn away. Tazri then needs no correction. Keep "The article predates the marriage", the plain "No." at L445 that Belisar lets hang, "And if his service was not only partisan?", and "Then you may bring evidence before the tribunal". Those carry the scene.

### D12 · Major · Final exchange: dismissed Mireth packs as if staying, and the last line repeats the six months

- Where: L457–L485, dawn to the end
- Text:
> "That one is dismissed from household service," Belisar added.
> "Bring all of it," she said. "We have six months."
- Problem: Continuity and referent failure in the final meaningful exchange. Belisar orders the attendants transferred separately and dismisses Mireth. A few lines later Mireth takes the seal case, runs a packing inventory, and hears Tazri say "We have six months", which reads as if Mireth is coming. The story never marks this as defiance the guards allow, a last service before her removal, or a slip. "We have six months" also repeats what the narrator stated at L467, so the final line explains a consequence that has already landed.
- Fix: Make Mireth's status clear in one beat: a guard moves to stop her and she goes on anyway; or an official says she may pack but not follow; or Tazri's "we" is visibly said against the order. Keep the inventory. "The good ink?" and Tazri's look at the sealed correspondence boxes carry the ending's real subtext. Then either cut the six-month sentence from the L467 summary, or end on something other than "We have six months". Do not do both, and do not turn the ending into a speech.

### D13 · Major · Mireth's lines default to a barb or a last word

- Where: throughout: L33, L95, L119, L157, L201, L349, L393, L415 (L49 and L463 earn theirs)
- Text:
> Ask me something useful.
> The papers first, unless both of you have become stupid in exactly the same hour.
> Good. Decide over the flame instead of staring at each other.
> Twenty-eight minutes, if fortune has developed shame.
- Problem: Mireth is the most distinct voice, but that distinctiveness is built from the collection's house wit. Nearly every line ends on a sardonic tag or corrects Tazri's question; "Ask me something useful" is the correction ping-pong pattern word for word. By the third barb it reads as the author's rhythm, not a 51-year-old attendant in real danger. It also blunts the moments where her wit actually wins something: the guard at L49 and the emperor at L463.
- Fix: Keep L49, L395 ("Mouth first"), and L463. Elsewhere let her speak in plain logistics, in imperatives, or not at all. Strip the trailing tags from L95, L157, and L201, and delete "Ask me something useful." Let her cough, already set up as her signal, do some of the work her quips do now. Show her fear for herself (she is the one dismissed) once, in a plain line.

### D14 · Major · The narration explains how the characters talk

## Continuity and prompt findings

### C1 · Major · The proposition and the treaty talk happen within the guards' reach

- Where: L45–L83, far end of the path
- Text:
> The nearer guard lowered his spear across the path.
> Jaseth glanced once at the guards.
> He stepped aside and let her enter the warming room before him.
- Problem: The guards let Tazri pass, then stand a few paces behind her on a witnessed walk. Within their reach she speaks the invitation, and she and Jaseth discuss the tribunal petition, carrying the witness register past Belisar's gate, and adultery charges. Nothing says the guards are out of earshot or that anyone lowers their voice, and Jaseth's glance at them only makes their presence more noticeable. The forbidden notary then follows the bride into a small room in their sight, with no stated permission (Mireth being there as witness may be the reason, but the text never says so). The reader has to wonder why the plan is not reported on the spot.
- Fix: Add one or two staging clauses. Establish distance and lowered voices, for example the guard staying at the turning stone and looking away after Mireth's jab. Say briefly that the notary may enter a room where the bride's witness is present. No new scene is needed.

### C2 · Major · Two term swaps blur what Jaseth sacrifices

- Where: L29 against L103, L145–L151, L169, L439–L443; L17 and L63 against L145 and L159
- Text:
> His accreditation ends at moonset, and they have moved the guard round forward.
> "Varo's safe-conduct ended at moonset."
> A treaty notary from Merevale, visible and useless, made a lesson of the duchy's limits.
> Merevale's impartial observer vanished; a man carrying one side's case stood in his place.
- Problem: Mireth reports that Jaseth's accreditation ends at moonset. Yet the story's key sacrifice is that carrying the register costs him that same accreditation ("Eight, once I pass the gate"; "His accreditation will not survive partisan service"). If it ends at moonset anyway, the sacrifice costs him nothing. At dawn Belisar says the safe-conduct ended at moonset, which is what the plot needs. Separately, Jaseth is "a treaty notary from Merevale" and "Merevale's notary", but also "Merevale's impartial observer" and a "neutral witness". The reader cannot tell whether carrying Merevale's petition betrays his role or simply performs it.
- Fix: Change "accreditation" to "safe-conduct" at L29 (one word). Establish once, at L17, that he is the treaty's sworn neutral notary even though he was born in Merevale. At L159, change "Merevale's impartial observer" to the treaty's.
