# The Back Row

## Promised experience

The request asks for a story about “someone you love,” described as one “specific type of plant” that reflects their personality, appearance and effect on the teller. I read a promise of recognition, on a personal scale and with room for humor or difficult feelings. The relationship, genre, teller and outcome are open. I want the last look at the person to make the earlier look at the plant richer.

## Takes considered

- **The Back Row.** On the drive-in's last night, its owner is American witch hazel to the wife watching her turn an address to the town into an invitation from one woman to another.
- **A Hole in the Snow.** An adult daughter accompanies her mother to the site of the mother's winter swims, intending to take a flattering portrait before moving away. Her mother, who has the hooded silhouette and untimely warmth of eastern skunk cabbage, drives the shoot until her daughter takes the picture that makes both of them laugh.
- **Things I Picked Up.** A journey put together from things an adult brother has stuck in the narrator's coat pockets. The last episode happens at a bus stop, where the brother, built and disposed like common burdock, keeps the narrator there through two departures and finally asks if he can come too.

The drive-in gives the beloved the strongest scene. Bess's sense of timing determines where she spends the last show and how Joanie gets to be with her.

## Provisional direction

For the final night of the Winch Drive-In, Joanie has written a dignified farewell that she means to deliver herself, honoring the years her wife Bess has run the place. Bess asks her to save a spot in the back row, where their first date ended before the picture started when work called Bess away. By that spot grows a common witch hazel, *Hamamelis virginiana*. Its pale ribbon curls and crooked little forks are the shapes of Bess's hair and silhouette. Bess is finding a beginning in this last cold week, like the flowers. Her way of getting her wish is as abrupt and funny as the woody capsules pitching last year's seeds.

At the booth, Joanie tries to talk Bess through the farewell. Bess has arranged for relief and tells Joanie what she wished she'd done on their first date. She tells Joanie she still wants an evening with her in that car. The invitation remains Joanie's to make. Off air, Bess offers Joanie the mic for the farewell she wrote. Joanie agrees to take it and can still read the pages when her turn comes. She asks her wife to the movies in her own words and gets the old sensation of being caught with her whole laugh showing. Bess joins her for the picture.

## Craft plan

- **Teller and voice.** Try Joanie in close first-person present, amused and trying to manage what comes out of her mouth, against first-person past told that winter, indulgent toward Bess and rueful about her own performance. In both, notice how sound carries and what Bess does with her broad-jointed hands. Stay close when Joanie has to risk saying what she wants.
- **Cast and speech.** Joanie has run the cash window for years. She remembers exact wording and prices, puts conditions on requests and goes formal when flustered. She wants Bess's work to feel seen and is a little vain about having found the words. Bess has a hooked shoulder and pale gold curls going silver. She directs individuals even over the radio and runs on about petty offenses until their larger point comes clear. She can sell an occasion and has trouble asking a favor in an empty room. She knows somebody else can run the picture and hopes asking Joanie to keep a space will bring back the offer she left hanging. She underestimates how thoroughly Joanie has prepared the farewell. The relief projectionist stays in the background.
- **Conversation owed.** At the booth Bess tells Joanie how badly she wanted to get back to her on their first date and that she still wants the evening she missed. Joanie has her own memory of waiting, and can dispute where Bess thinks the evening ended. Joanie gives her own answer on the air.
- **Setting.** The fictional Winch Drive-In in eastern Vermont beats the Elkin Grange supper hall or the turnout at Spindle Notch. Only the drive-in turns Bess's usual work into a place where she can take two seats for herself. On a cold, dry late-October evening there's hot popcorn oil in the passage, sloped gravel bays under good shoes, radio hiss, cold through the ticket window and the milky rectangle of screen. Regulars hang windshield cloths on the old speaker poles. The radio makes the women audible across the lot. The back row offers shelter beside the woods and a customer's view of the picture. [National Park Service, 66 Drive-In](https://www.nps.gov/places/66-drive-in.htm), [Bethel Drive-In](https://betheldrivein.com/).
- **Plant and casting.** Two invented characters. On the shrub's spreading branches, narrow yellow petals are visible through the late leaves while capsules from the previous year dry and split. Keep the plant in Joanie's lived history with Bess, from the odd turn of her shoulders in the window to how it feels waiting for her to say a thing nobody else would risk saying. [Arnold Arboretum, *Arnoldia*](https://arboretum.harvard.edu/wp-content/uploads/2020/06/2014-72-2-Arnoldia.pdf), [Morton Arboretum, common witch-hazel](https://mortonarb.org/plant-and-protect/trees-and-plants/common-witch-hazel/).

## Scene briefs and ending

1. **Cash window and back row, dusk (25%).** Joanie brings the pages and wants to practice. Bess knows there will be time to sit, keeps serving and asks her for the spot by their shrub. Joanie finds pale petals beside brown open cases. A hard black seed sits in the dash tray. It came through the open window earlier that week and hit the dash with a tick that made her duck. Looking back from the car, she sees Bess turn one shoulder to reach sideways through the cash window to a driver.
2. **The booth before the picture, long scene (60%).** Joanie comes back with the pages. Bess has put out a second carton of popcorn. An early arrival leaves headlights on the bare screen, giving Bess someone to boss while she gathers herself to tell Joanie what she wants. The speech brings them around to the first date. Joanie argues about how long she sat in the dark. Bess gets to the part she never told. From the side window by the booth stairs she saw the dome light come on in Joanie's car and watched her lean across to clear the passenger seat. She kept thinking she would get down there in time. She tells Joanie she arranged for relief because she still wants that seat beside her, then gives her time to take that in. The old thing comes out late, while they can choose what to do about it. Bess offers Joanie the mic for the speech and waits for her agreement before introducing her. Sit with Joanie holding the pages, hearing that small sharp tick from the dash again in her mind as the thing Bess has just said catches up with her. She could still give Bess the formal sendoff. She chooses to make the invitation herself, and her voice goes from the formal register to a laugh that reaches Bess twice, across the booth and through the speaker.
3. **First frames, back row (15%).** Joanie wants to give Bess an ordinary date. Bess arrives expecting a little fuss and asks what Joanie is doing way over in the driver's seat. Popcorn tips just as Bess reaches across. They give up seeing the first shot. Stay with the yellow loops at Bess's temple in the moving light and how near Joanie gets to laugh into them. The empty seat, the first date and the double sound of the laugh load the contact. Carry the business's history in a few shared memories. Leave the sale and the rest of the film offstage.
