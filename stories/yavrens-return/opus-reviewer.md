# Opus review: Where the Eagle Lands

Independent audit for a later editing pass. It is not a `review.md` verdict,
does not change canon state, and does not authorize edits by itself.

## Verdict

- Dialogue: REVISE — every line holds together, but the lovers' one real argument (L91–L129) is a seventeen-turn volley of short, equal lines in which Irdas sounds exactly like Yavren.
- Prose: PASS — concrete, varied, no em dashes or reversal tics; only the closing paragraph restates a point already made.
- Continuity and prompt: PASS — every prompt requirement is on the page within the length band; one small staging gap around the green dress.
- Edit scope: targeted-dialogue
- Existing review.md said: Verdict PASS, "Dialogue: PASS"; agree that uptake is coherent, disagree that the voices are distinct in the central lovers' exchange.

This story's dialogue is better than the collection's norm. It has ordinary talk (grit down a collar), a real overlap (the cooper and Pelka starting at once), a council scene that mixes direct lines with reported speech, and a funny, loose letter voice for Irdas. The problem is concentrated in one place: the theatre argument, the story's main romantic pressure, runs as a tennis match of clipped declaratives where most replies pick up the other person's last word and turn it. Irdas, whom the narration describes as talking about the hall since they arrived, speaks there in Yavren's terse register, so the reader can tell them apart only by who wants to stay. The edit pass should keep that scene's beats and its best hinges, give Irdas his fluency and his habit of joking before an awkward request, let Yavren stay short and logistical, and then cut the narration that explains the argument after it ends.

## Edit guardrails

- Prompt requirements: Yavren returns after mastering the Eagle, with the man she loves; the Eagle visibly rebuilds Esvay and Tolrath; the three kingdoms form a union with real effects (common assembly, garrisons withdrawn, free settlement, border judgments that bind all three) and she holds no crown, office, or enforcement role. Her "No." at L175 must survive. The Eagle Queen's memories stay hers without deciding her choices (L31, L187, L239). The departure facts at L31 (saved eyries and valley, empty throne, east with Pelka) must stay.
- Identity and rating: Pelka is Yavren's adult foster sister; Irdas is a separate adult man met in the nine years since (six years together; ages 33 and 36). Intimacy stays non-explicit; L199 is the ceiling. Length band is 2,500–4,000 words and the story is about 3,500, so any lines added in D1 should be paid for with cuts such as D2.

## Dialogue findings

### D1 · Major · Theatre argument volleys in one shared register

- Where: L91–L129, the theatre floor at dusk after the rulers' messengers
- Text:
> You'll be in Tolrath. I thought I'd stay through winter.
> I do. With a company. With more than the three people we've found.
> I told you about the singer. And the brothers coming after harvest.
> I didn't ask you to stay. I should have asked that.
> I don't know how long Tolrath will take.
> I'm asking where you'll come back to.
- Problem: Each line passes the Ground check, but the exchange is a Tennis Match: seventeen alternating turns, almost all one or two short declaratives, with no interruption, nobody running on, nobody going quiet. Five replies are built by taking the partner's last word and turning it ("Tolrath" / "You'll be in Tolrath"; "wanted to try" / "I do"; "come back with me" / "I came"; "asked that" / "Then ask"; "how long Tolrath will take" / "where you'll come back to"). That is the collection's correction ping-pong rhythm. Identical Voices: cover the tags and only the content (who stays, who goes) tells the speakers apart. Irdas is the man the narration says has been talking about that hall since their arrival and whose letters are loose and funny, yet here he is as terse and tidy as Yavren. "I should have asked that" is a composed, nearly procedural concession at the moment he is most exposed, and it exists partly to set up "Then ask."
- Fix: Keep the scene's order and its strongest hinges: "You'll be in Tolrath." (the reveal), "I came." / "I like it here." (L109), "Live here with me." (L119), "I'm asking where you'll come back to." (L125), and L129 in his tired voice. Rework the middle so Irdas is the fluent one. At L99 let him run on about the vault, the company, the brothers, and let Yavren cut in with "For how long?" rather than waiting her turn. Make L113–L117 less tidy: "engaged" can become an everyday word, and his admission can come out sideways, or behind a joke he then abandons, before "Live here with me." Yavren stays short and keeps retreating into dates and Tolrath. Aim for at most two pivot replies, one interruption, and one line left unanswered. Illustrative only: "I do. You heard what that vault did with your name. Put a real company in here, the brothers, a chorus, and by spring" (Yavren cutting in: "For how long?").

### D2 · Minor · Narration explains exchanges after they land

- Where: L131, end of the theatre argument; L169, the morning seat request recalled during the council
- Text:
> It was the first time she had understood that she could leave and he could remain, each wanting something the other could not carry.
> trying out the thought of an ordinary claim
- Problem: Both gloss what the lines already show. L129 ("You might go." / "I might have a rehearsal.") has just shown that each of them can leave or stay; L131 restates it as a formula at the argument's key moment (the Philosopher, in narration). At L169 the request for a seat, placed among rulers bargaining over property, already reads as an ordinary claim; naming it tells the reader what to notice.
- Fix: Cut "each wanting something the other could not carry"; either keep the first clause as her recognition or cut the sentence and go straight to "They went upstairs without settling it." At L169 cut "trying out the thought of an ordinary claim" and let the request and his joke about buying a seat carry it.

### D3 · Minor · Players thread: surprise lands on narration and referents drift

- Where: L69–L73, supper with Pelka; L99 and L115, theatre argument
- Text:
> Irdas stopped cutting the cheese. Two of his players were being held at that same border.
> You have players?
> With more than the three people we've found.
> And the brothers coming after harvest.
- Problem: Yavren's surprised "You have players?" answers something Irdas must have said, but the page gives only narration, which already calls them "his players" from her point of view before she knows he has any. Her surprise responds to the narrator, not to his words (Ground/uptake). At L99 "we've found" suggests Yavren helped find a company she did not know about. At L115 the reader has to work out that "the brothers" are the two players held at the border; read plainly, "coming after harvest" conflicts with their being stopped on the road in midsummer.
- Fix: At L69 give Irdas a spoken line naming two of his people held at that border, so her question answers it; keep "Three so far. You met the woman with the red boots." Change "we've" to "I've", or make "we" plainly Irdas and the singer. At L115 tie the brothers to the border (for example, the brothers once they are let through) so the two references connect. Fold these into the D1 rework.

## Prose findings

### P1 · Minor · Closing paragraph restates the memory point and lists the resolutions

- Where: L239, final paragraph at the opening performance
- Text:
> The memory stayed with her.
> Above this room were her clothes beside Irdas's, beyond it two cities standing in the spring night.
- Problem: The paragraph's first two sentences deliver the required note (the Queen's memories are hers; her vengeance does not govern). "The memory stayed with her." then repeats what L31 ("It was still hers to remember.") and the sentence before it already said. The next sentence lists each resolved thread (home, cities) before the sister and the final image, which makes the close a polished inventory. See C1 for the clothes.
- Fix: Cut "The memory stayed with her." Reduce the inventory sentence to one concrete image or drop it. Keep "Her sister's shoulder rested against hers." and the last sentence, which carries the prompt's "the man she loves."

## Continuity and prompt findings

### C1 · Minor · Green dress and clothes staging

- Where: L197–L199, letters; L209–L211, arrival; L223 and L237–L239, upstairs and the performance
- Text:
> His next letter contained a length of green thread and a demand for measurements.
> the green dress folded in her saddlebag
> Pelka met her in the passage and took the saddlebag.
> Yavren changed into green.
> Her traveling chest stood where she had left it
- Problem: Irdas's costumer in Esvay is making the dress from her measurements, yet it arrives from Tolrath in Yavren's saddlebag with no word of it being sent. Pelka then takes that saddlebag in the passage, and Yavren changes into green upstairs without it. At L239 "her clothes beside Irdas's" says she has unpacked, while L223 shows her chest untouched and nothing moved into the half he cleared.
- Fix: Simplest: have the finished dress waiting upstairs in the half of the cupboard he cleared. She then arrives with only road dust and the bag Pelka takes, and "her clothes beside Irdas's" becomes true. Alternatively, say the dress reached her in Tolrath and have Pelka hand the bag back before Yavren goes up.
