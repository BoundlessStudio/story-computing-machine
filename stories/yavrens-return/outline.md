# Outline

## Story

- Premise and central promise: Where the Eagle Lands. Nine years after departure, Yavren returns with Irdas, her lover of six years. Her mastered Eagle Shadow raises Esvay and Tolrath; their resettlement draws together three kingdoms that once hunted her.
- Focal pressure or attachment: Yavren wants these abandoned streets full of people again. She also cherishes the freedom of the lovers' eastern years. Irdas wants a permanent theatre in Esvay; she has assumed they will eventually travel on together.
- Counterforce or complication: Rebuilding revives royal land claims and displaced families' attachments. The western queen needs taxpayers, the southern king needs northern markets, and the hill regent needs allies against both. Converging interests leave old grievances intact.
- POV, distance, and information limit: Close third past through Yavren, widening during seasonal transitions. The Queen's memories offer remembered places, never current political knowledge or access to other minds.
- Governing movement and time shape: Accumulation across a year: arrival, rebuilding, separation, negotiation, second city's revival. Expand consequential encounters; compress repeated construction. Target 2,500–4,000 words.
- Speculative rule or ordinary-world constraint: Living Yavren supplies and directs the Eagle. Its immense feathered reaches lift and fit visible existing stone; missing material and craftsmanship still come from people. Completed structures stand ordinarily after release. It cannot restore the dead or reproduce a vanished population. Great workings require ordinary rest, never renewed mastery trials.
- Dialogue promise: Familiar lovers discover incompatible assumptions about home while former enemies discover which promises they can afford to keep.
- Dialogic medium: Spoken intimacy and argument, Pelka's visits, imperfect letters during separation, and an inhabited city's contentious gathering.
- Dialogue engine: Replies change who travels, who stays, and what rulers surrender. Residents' competing requests give the union its terms.

## Voice

- Narrative texture: Attend to weight becoming movement, familiar stone returning at unfamiliar heights, and the untidy habitation following grandeur. Spacious sentences can hold wonder; closer narration admits Yavren's vanity and appetite.
- Conversational texture: Established affection permits foolishness, distraction, blunt requests, and unfinished arguments. Letters can carry unequal news, including theatre gossip Yavren fails to find interesting.
- Rhetorical ownership: Yavren thinks spatially but overestimates gratitude. Irdas understands audiences, uses humor to delay embarrassing requests, and can misread silence. Pelka claims familial authority without becoming an oracle. Rulers possess different facts and different reasons to listen.
- Pressure behavior: Yavren becomes overprecise about dates when avoiding permanence. Irdas abandons jokes when they obscure his wish. Pelka interrupts excessive politeness; public speakers sometimes need explanations repeated.
- Relationship movement: Yavren wants companionship without another binding office; Irdas wants her to choose his settled life without abandoning hers. Pelka wants a sister available beyond grand occasions. Explicit plans replace assumptions, permitting separate work and a shared home.
- Anti-default: Avoid a construction accident that proves virtue, unanimous conversion, or a lover whose sole function is calming dangerous power.

## Beats

1. **Return already capable.** At Esvay, Yavren lifts a fallen arcade with the vast dark Eagle while families watch from safe ground. Both lovers are in their thirties. Her flowing dark hair and feathered clothing, his dark curls, their intimacy, and monumental arches carry the inspected reference. The first completed street receives households.
2. **People fill the miracle.** Through summer, reopened kilns, competing shops, and an ill-fitting wedding procession make the city particular. Pelka arrives with valley news and dislikes needing appointments. Irdas claims a ruined performance hall for his company. A royal property demand interrupts preparations; restoring stone has made old territorial arguments valuable again.
3. **Love acquires an address.** In their unfinished rooms, Yavren discovers Irdas has engaged performers for a year. He thought his wish was obvious; she heard enthusiasm. Let their argument expose pleasure, fear, and inconvenience within established love. He remains in Esvay while she begins Tolrath; they agree she returns for his spring opening.
4. **A union people can use.** Autumn brings the three rulers to occupied Esvay. Earlier correspondence and growing cross-border settlement have prepared negotiations. Pelka brings valley residents' demands; inhabitants resist becoming transferred royal property. The bargain preserves three local crowns but places border disputes and common defense under a shared assembly, guarantees settlement across borders, and withdraws rival garrisons. Yavren receives no governing office.
5. **Make the promises visible.** Across winter and spring, mixed crews and returning families rebuild Tolrath around Yavren's tremendous lifts. Show a restored quarter inhabited, rival soldiers leaving, and a formerly barred company crossing freely. The Queen's remembered architecture contributes beauty without dictating whom it serves. Some claims remain contested through the new assembly.
6. **The life raised inside it.** Yavren returns for Irdas's opening and chooses their upstairs rooms as home. Give their reunion warmth. Let the final exchange grow from his ambition and their changed plans. Finish among audiences from three kingdoms, with Yavren enjoying the place she raised and Irdas pursuing his own art.
