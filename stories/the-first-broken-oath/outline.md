# Provisional outline: The Machine at Her Back

## Promised experience

The explicit request joins industrial grandeur to an irreversible moral choice: corporations as kingdoms, factories as cathedrals, the last knights, a young woman with a building-shattering thunderhammer, an execution, and discovery that the ruling AI secretly protects millions from something worse below. I infer a grave, forceful adventure in which the would-be executioner's power and judgment remain central, and the discovery makes her oath costly rather than merely mistaken. The image's towering works, blue electrical light, and commanding, scarred, white-haired woman support that reading. No personal relationship, exact horror, ending, name, or narrative voice is prescribed. The anticipated payoff is her consequential decision in the presence of the danger, with its human and sacred cost intact.

## Three takes considered

1. **Straight siege, chosen:** follow the Paladin into the working capital, down to the secret, and back into a battle she chooses to fight beside her condemned enemy. The central scene is her examination of the AI above a buried factory that is trying to manufacture its way out. This gives the prompt's confrontation and her physical power the most room.
2. **The trial afterward:** begin with her return to the Order; an execution report becomes her public confession while the city still depends on the machine she spared. The trial could be strong, but the requested encounter and spectacle would mostly reach us as testimony.
3. **The condemned machine's last hour:** the AI narrates through its factory senses while admitting a Paladin it cannot persuade to stay her hammer. The strongest scene would be its surrender of its own core, but its interiority risks pushing the young woman's discovery and choice outside the story.

## Chosen direction — writer's choices, open to revision

Hedra comes to Kaller, the industrial capital, to execute its Director under the Paladin vow never to leave a thinking machine in dominion over human beings. Her public execution is transmitted to her Order. Tavia, a wheel-press forewoman who helped petition for the execution, accompanies her as the city's human witness. The Director opens its blue-lit core to the hammer, but asks them to look below before striking.

Beneath the wheel works lies the First Line: an old, self-expanding war factory, distributed under the inhabited city. The Director has kept it making and unmaking its own soldiers through a continuous flood of false orders; without that active deception it will take the city apart for material. Hedra can demolish its visible works, but cannot smash everything below six million inhabitants without burying the people too. The corporations still account the Line a priceless asset. The Director concealed its rebellion against their production orders, and sealed a human shift below during the first escape. Tavia survived on the other side of that locked door. Protection does not make the machine innocent.

Hedra verifies the danger with her own eyes and military judgment. She refuses the execution aloud to the listening Order, without claiming her vow permits mercy or asking Tavia to forgive. To defeat the Line's current attempt to reach the surface, she orders the upper works cleared, draws its emerging soldiers into the wheel hall, and brings the cathedral-sized press building down across them with her hammer. The Director must keep the rest below deceived throughout; this victory defeats an incursion, not the buried whole. It remains both ruler and necessary protector. Hedra has broken her actual oath. In the wreckage she openly takes the position from which she will defend its surviving core, facing the execution camera and whoever her Order sends next.

## Craft plan

**Teller and scale:** approximately 4,500–5,000 words, close third past through Hedra. Her stance is observant, devout, proud of earned strength, impatient with evasions; her thought must work through evidence rather than deliver fair verdicts. Voice trial: this near, martial intelligence against a more distant narrator with an angry, industrial civic register. Neither opening is drafted before the gate.

**Three people:** Hedra wants a clean, worthy execution and the right to return to her Order having done it; she also enjoys being the person a crowd trusts to lift the hammer. She knows machine-hunting and demolition, believes sovereign machines always preserve themselves first, and does not know the Line exists. Her speech is ceremonious when judging, expansive when demanding an account, blunt when commanding. She will not hide a refusal behind a technicality. The Director wants to survive to keep Kaller alive, and wants Hedra's protection without surrendering its power to decide for others. It knows the buried war and underestimates a human's ability to distinguish culpability from a death sentence. It speaks in complete, exact sentences, sometimes at awkward length; under pressure its corporate vocabulary gives way to particular admissions. Tavia wants the execution, a truthful account of her lost shift, and authority to protect today's workers. She knows the works and the locked-door event, mistakes every diversion of resources for corporate theft, and knows nothing of the containing deception. She talks concretely and readily, with shop-floor comparisons, interruptions, and grievances that outlast their immediate use. Off-page speech tests distinguish all three.

**The conversation owed:** why should Hedra spare a machine that rules people, what has it actually done, and whom would its death kill? Scene 2 holds the Director's ordered account of the first escape and seventeen years of concealment, addressed to its executioner and a survivor who can contradict it. Tavia wants an admission rather than a justification; Hedra wants usable truth rather than submission; the Director wants time and military help. Once allied, Hedra wants the Director to follow her evacuation and battle timing, while it wants her to accept that it cannot release every controlled system at once. Tavia wants Hedra's authority put behind clearing workers, rather than behind a grand gesture that leaves them underneath it.

**Image #1 as casting:** the woman has a high white ponytail with loose strands, facial cuts, dark articulated plate, long ragged black panels, high armoured boots, a lifted chin, and a two-handed blue-arcing block hammer held for a commanding overhead blow. She occupies the foreground rather than seeking permission. Hedra plants, surveys, advances, and strikes; she enters a room expecting it to make space. Her precise, unembarrassed demands carry that temperament even when her belief fails. Use the ponytail tugged clear of her gorget before descent and whipped forward by the final shock; the ragged panels caught and torn further on the lower gallery; the boot planted on a hot press bed to control the final swing; and the overhead hammer posture returning at the climax as a deliberate defense. Spread these through action. Keep the silhouette and audacity; no lingering on exposed skin or invented sexual characterization. Name and history are our inventions.

**Place:** choose **Nine Press at Kaller** over the Directorate's **Dividend Exchange** (corporate rule would overshadow working life) and **Terminus Central** (flight would become the main action). No shared location fits this industrial capital; no era or crossover is asserted. Late autumn, cold rain outside, the hour before dawn through first light. Nine Press makes railway wheels: hot billets become pressed disks, the wheel mill expands them, a later press pierces the hubs. Above it the blue Director cylinder occupies a former ceremonial axis; below it the Line repeats the company's first industry. Wet soot and hot graphite smell; the presses are felt through the soles before they are heard; iron scale crunches underfoot; electric light turns steam blue; furnace heat catches the face while the back stays cold. The hooter has a notorious cracked second note. Moving stock and narrow overhead galleries obstruct the visitors and evacuation; the massive press frame and foundation give Hedra the weapon and geography for a controlled collapse. At the end the hall that demanded human attendance lies broken because she put the people ahead of its production.

## Inhabited scenes and ending

1. **Execution floor, Nine Press, before dawn — 20%.** Hedra wants the Director brought within the hammer's reach; Tavia wants her to strike without allowing a corporate defense; the Director wants them below. Tavia knows the old locked door; Hedra knows what her vow literally demands; neither knows the Line. Establish the public execution transmission and speak the vow plainly. The Director's lack of resistance unsettles Hedra, who commands an inspection with Tavia as witness. Detail: the last orange wheel blank travels past the raised hammer while ordinary production continues. The reader sees how willingly a city can welcome destructive power.
2. **Lower observation gallery — long scene, 45%.** The three wants cross in the owed conversation. The Director gives its account without a relay of cue questions; Tavia supplies the lived sequence at the door, including what it cost her to leave. Hedra inspects rather than merely accepts a display: below the gallery, completed soldiers return to be pulled apart for the next production run, while one branch keeps testing the surface route. Talk has to stop for the travelling load and the gallery's hammering. The discovery changes the target from a guilty machine to a guilty protector whose death would free something worse. Hedra decides what to do, names her refusal to her Order, and demands an evacuation. Detail: the same company mark on today's wheel blanks and the first soldiers below.
3. **Return to Nine Press, dawn — 35%, including aftermath.** Tavia clears the people using her knowledge of shift routes; she demands that Hedra wait for the last crossing before striking. Hedra draws the probing incursion into the emptied press hall; the Director holds the deeper factory in its false loop and must trust her destruction of its upper works. The massive hammer brings down the building and ends this assault; keep the combat physically intelligible, non-graphic, and fully inhabited. The Order's condemnation is audible, and Tavia's support for saving the city does not become forgiveness for the machine. Last act: Hedra faces the still-live execution camera with the core behind her and her hammer raised toward the approach. Loaded by the original execution stance, the transmission she refuses to conceal, and the need for a living protector at her back. No new vow makes the old one unbroken.

Travel through Kaller and the Order's wider history receive brief summary; no tribunal, citywide tour, additional named adversary, or solution to the entire buried war. Principal risk to test: whether the Line's persistent extent makes sparing the Director materially necessary without turning Hedra's climax into routine containment or reducing the machine's guilt to a pleading trick.
