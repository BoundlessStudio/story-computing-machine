# Story plan

## Premise and promise

- **One-sentence premise:** After death, Ruth wakes in a station whose signs
  promise heaven on one track and hell on the other; when she helps a
  hell-ticketed passenger board the heaven train and watches the moving train
  drag that woman hellward, Ruth lets both of her own one-service departures
  expire rather than learn her destination, permanently stranding herself
  there.
- **Story promise:** This is a close, humane dark fantasy about the difference
  between staying *away* from judgment and staying *with* another frightened
  person. The trains and their coercive rule are real within Ruth's experience,
  but their justice, makers, and ultimate destinations remain beyond what she
  can prove.
- **Central dramatic question:** Will Ruth submit to an unknowable verdict in
  the hope of either punishment or absolution, or can she accept permanent
  uncertainty without using fear as an excuse to abandon another person again?
- **Meaningful change:** Ruth begins by treating indecision as morally neutral:
  if she never opens her ticket, no authority can tell her what leaving her
  dying sister says about her. She ends still refusing both trains, but after
  making that refusal irreversible she stops calling it neutrality. She admits
  her part in Sabine's seizure and freely stays present for the next arrival,
  accepting responsibility without receiving a verdict or forgiveness.

## Story controls

- **Form:** Complete short story, planned at approximately 3,500 words within
  the required 2,500–4,000-word range.
- **POV and tense:** First person past, restricted to Ruth. Ruth can report
  sensations, memories, actions, signs, and testimony, but cannot narrate the
  station's backstage truth.
- **Narrative distance:** Intimate and concrete. Ruth's evasions should be
  visible in what she refuses to look at, say, or ask. Do not withhold basic
  scene facts merely to manufacture mystery.
- **Tone and genre:** Literary dark fantasy; restrained dread, moral ambiguity,
  and humane attention to the dead as people. The station should feel ordinary
  enough to touch—cold tile, institutional light, paper tickets, dirty
  windows—while its impossible procedures remain unnerving.
- **Setting scope:** A local afterlife manifestation intelligible to Ruth as a
  train station. The physical-world era may feel modern-like, but should remain
  lightly specified and should not be declared literal real-world Earth.
- **Content boundaries:** PG-13. Ruth's death is acknowledged without graphic
  detail. Sabine's seizure is frightening and unmistakable but bloodless:
  pressure, shadow, forced movement, and her continued conscious presence
  carry the horror. Do not depict torture or explain what hell does to anyone.
- **Opening target:** Within the first page, establish that Ruth has died, that
  two destination boards read `HEAVEN` and `HELL`, that she possesses an
  unopened personal ticket, and that the first departure is imminent.
- **Ending target:** Ruth remains physically at the station after her paired
  service has expired. A subsequent arrival lets her demonstrate, not
  announce, the changed meaning of staying. There is no third train, appeal,
  later reissue, secret reward, or suggestion that her station duty is
  heaven-by-another-name.

## Character engine

- **Ruth — first-person protagonist**
  - **Concrete desire:** Keep her ticket sealed and avoid both trains so she
    never has to discover either what the afterlife is like or what this
    station has assigned her.
  - **Immediate tactic:** Delay, question Hester's authority, help Sabine test
    the rule, and tell herself she can wait for a later service.
  - **Private wound:** Years earlier, Ruth's younger sister Asha asked her to
    remain at the hospital until morning. Ruth left because she could not bear
    to witness the dying. Asha died before dawn, after Ruth silenced one final
    call. Ruth has never found an honest position between self-exoneration and
    the belief that this one cowardly choice defines her completely.
  - **External stakes:** Boarding may expose her to an unknown heaven or hell;
    refusing this service will permanently void both routes from this station.
    Heaven also holds the unprovable possibility of reunion with Asha, which
    makes missing it a real loss rather than an abstract refusal.
  - **Moral stakes:** Ruth can make another frightened person carry the risk of
    testing the system for her, or she can own the limits of her knowledge and
    the consequences of her own choices.
  - **Blind spot:** She confuses an absent verdict with an absent choice. She
    also imagines punishment might settle her guilt and absolution might erase
    it; both fantasies outsource moral responsibility.
  - **Internal movement:** From flight disguised as neutrality, through guilty
    complicity in Sabine's failed test, to irreversible refusal and then
    chosen presence. Her ending is not proof that she is good. It is one
    concrete act that contradicts her old pattern.

- **Sabine Kroll — the hell-assigned passenger**
  - Her opened ticket plainly displays her name and `HELL`. This makes her
    “meant for hell” in the station's operative sense without proving that the
    assignment is morally infallible.
  - Sabine is lucid, funny in a brittle way, and furious at being reduced to a
    destination word. She does not deliver a confession that invites Ruth or
    the reader to endorse the sentence. Her observation that even living
    courts let an accused person speak exposes the station's coercion.
  - She asks Ruth to hold the heaven-train door long enough for her to board.
    Ruth agrees partly out of sympathy and partly because Sabine's success
    would let Ruth believe the whole system can be evaded.
  - During the seizure, preserve Sabine's identity and agency: she resists,
    calls Ruth's name, remains conscious, and is later recognizable behind the
    hell train's glass. She is a harmed person and a causal actor, not a prop
    who appears only to demonstrate a rule.

- **Hester Pike — local station attendant**
  - Hester wears a mundane badge and knows the procedures she has personally
    observed: the sign labels, the one-service ticket limit, and what has
    happened to mismatched passengers. She does not know who judges, whether
    the labels are morally just, what either destination contains, or whether
    stations elsewhere work the same way.
  - She warns Sabine and Ruth clearly but does not physically police the
    heaven-train door. That limitation permits Sabine's attempt without
    requiring Hester to be secretly complicit.
  - Her calm is not proof of approval. After the service ends, she leaves
    through an unmarked staff passage that does not admit Ruth, preserving
    uncertainty about whether Hester is dead, living, constructed, or
    something else. Do not call her an angel, demon, god, judge, or universal
    functionary.

- **Asha — Ruth's deceased younger sister, present in memory**
  - Asha's request that Ruth “stay until morning” supplies the story's charged
    verb and Ruth's concrete source of guilt.
  - Asha must remain a person rather than a device for forgiving Ruth. No
    vision, voice, or message from her appears at the station, and the story
    never confirms where she went.

- **Unnamed new arrival — aftermath mirror**
  - A visibly adult, frightened person arrives after Hester has gone. Their
    wet slippers and hospital wristband give them specificity without a name
    or lengthy backstory.
  - They are not a reward or replacement for Asha. Their request for Ruth to
    remain beside them until their train comes gives Ruth a new but non-
    redemptive chance to act differently.

- **Opposition:** No single villain is required. The principal opposing forces
  are the station's deadline and coercive enforcement, Ruth's fear of both
  condemnation and cheap absolution, the irreversible loss attached to delay,
  and the moral damage caused when she uses Sabine's risk to answer her own
  question.

## Causal arc

1. **Beginning state:** Ruth wakes on a cold bench after a brief, non-graphic
   recollection of her death. She sees paired boards reading `HEAVEN` and
   `HELL`, receives a folded ticket, and refuses to uncover its printed face.
   She believes that as long as she does not know the assignment, she has not
   chosen anything.
2. **Inciting disruption:** Hester confirms that Ruth is dead, that the two
   trains depart on this service, and that the visible warning on the back of
   Ruth's ticket—`VALID THIS SERVICE ONLY / NO REISSUE`—means waiting is not
   free. Hester carefully frames these as station facts she has observed, not
   a complete theory of the afterlife.
3. **Escalating turn one:** Sabine shows Ruth a ticket marked `HELL` and argues
   that an unexplained label has no moral right to summarize a life. She plans
   to board the heaven train and asks Ruth to hold the door. Ruth agrees,
   because helping Sabine feels compassionate and because Ruth wants proof
   that assignment can be escaped.
4. **Escalating turn two / causal hinge:** The heaven train arrives first. Ruth
   nearly opens her own ticket when the carriage's warmth evokes the possibility
   of Asha, but instead helps Sabine aboard. Once the train is moving, the
   station's mismatch enforcement activates and drags Sabine through the
   carriage and hellward. Ruth's intervention therefore produces the very
   ordeal she hoped would disprove the system.
5. **Escalating turn three:** The seizure ends with Sabine visibly imprisoned
   behind the hell train's smoky window, still a recognizable, resisting
   person. The heaven service has departed, permanently graying that route on
   Ruth's unopened ticket. The hell train remains for a short final boarding
   window, so Ruth cannot retreat into the fiction that both options are still
   available.
6. **Crisis:** Hester tells Ruth exactly what she knows: if Ruth lets the hell
   train leave, the rest of the ticket will go blank and no later train at this
   station will accept her. Hester cannot say whether boarding would be just,
   whether Asha was on the other route, or whether Ruth's ticket says heaven
   or hell. Ruth must choose between an unknown destination and a permanent
   station without the certainty she wanted from Sabine's test.
7. **Climax:** Ruth does not open or present the ticket. She faces Sabine
   through the glass rather than turning away, admits aloud that she helped,
   and lets the hell train depart. The remaining print vanishes and the
   platform gates close to her. Her physical staying is now caused by a
   deliberate, informed, irreversible choice—not a scheduling accident or a
   loophole she can revisit later.
8. **Resonant aftermath:** With Hester gone and no destination to romanticize,
   Ruth experiences the bare cost: no answer, no route to Asha, no audience
   empowered to forgive her. When the next adult arrival asks whether they are
   dead and begs her not to leave them alone, Ruth first feels the old urge to
   hide. She instead recounts only what she observed, including her own part
   in Sabine's seizure, and stays beside the arrival until that person's
   disclosed ticket and train align. The train leaves; Ruth remains by the
   empty bench, committed to presence but neither absolved nor rescued. The
   dramatic question closes on an action: uncertainty no longer excuses
   abandonment.

## Setup and payoff

- **Asha's request:** Early memory supplies Asha's exact request, “Stay until
  morning,” and Ruth's choice to leave the hospital. In the final scene, the
  new arrival asks Ruth to stay until the train comes. Ruth cannot repair the
  first choice, but she can stop repeating it.
- **The unopened ticket:** Ruth immediately notices the black peel-back film
  hiding the assignment. She handles it edge-first and never exposes the face.
  Its back openly states `VALID THIS SERVICE ONLY / NO REISSUE`, so the climax's
  permanence is seeded rather than sprung as a trick.
- **Two-part fading:** When the heaven train departs, one countdown border on
  Ruth's ticket turns gray; this does not reveal whether that train would have
  accepted her. Hester points out that the second border will follow the hell
  train. The ticket going wholly blank at the climax visibly pays off the
  staged loss of choice.
- **Hester's epistemic refrain:** Hester's earliest useful phrase is a variation
  on “I can tell you what I have seen here.” Repeating the boundary at the
  crisis prevents her from becoming an omniscient expositor. Ruth adopts the
  same honest boundary with the new arrival.
- **Sabine's blue wrist elastic:** Give Sabine one modest, humanizing physical
  detail before boarding. Its visibility against the hell train's window after
  the seizure confirms continuity of identity rather than a duplicate or
  symbolic replacement.
- **Door-holding:** Ruth holds the heaven-train door to help Sabine make a
  dangerous test and thereby helps cause the seizure. In the aftermath she
  does not control the next person's door or decision; she stays beside them
  and lets them present their own ticket.
- **Temptation of reunion:** The heaven carriage carries a scent or warmth Ruth
  associates with Asha, but no literal voice or apparition. This establishes
  the personal opportunity Ruth loses while keeping Asha's destination
  unknown.
- **The empty bench:** Ruth wakes alone on it and initially moves away whenever
  anyone sits close. In the final image she keeps the adjacent place available
  for whoever arrives next, a physical measure of the internal change.
- **Staff passage:** Its unmarked outline is visible behind Hester early, but
  Hester neither offers it nor calls it an escape. When it admits Hester and
  refuses Ruth after the service, it reinforces Hester's local function without
  introducing a late third route.

## Canon constraints

- Treat the station as one local experience of the real underlying afterlife,
  not the sole or universal form encountered by every dead person
  (`universe/rules.md` — `Afterlife and continuation`).
- `HEAVEN` and `HELL` are words displayed by this station and understood by
  these characters. The narrator may use them naturally but must not convert
  them into exhaustive objective cosmological categories
  (`universe/rules.md` — `System categories`; `universe/glossary.md` —
  `Magic, technology, and divinity`).
- Keep every knowledge claim attributed. Ruth sees signs, tickets, trains,
  departures, and Sabine's seizure. Hester reports repeated local observation.
  Neither can establish who built the station, what moral standard assigned
  Sabine, what either destination contains, or what all dead people experience
  (`universe/premise.md` — `Knowledge and connection`).
- Hester is only a local attendant of unresolved nature and authority. Do not
  use objective labels such as angel, demon, judge, or god, and do not imply a
  universe-spanning divine bureaucracy (`universe/rules.md` — `Divinity`).
- The seizure and single-service ticket are local mechanisms with stable
  scene-level behavior. Do not imply that consent, guilt, fear, desire, or
  sacrifice possesses the same metaphysical power elsewhere
  (`universe/rules.md` — `Costs, desire, and local mechanisms`).
- Sabine remains the same conscious person before, during, and after the
  transfer. Her resistance and Ruth's responsibility matter even if the
  mechanism is coherent. Do not dismiss the coercion as morally neutral
  because a system enforces it (`universe/style-guide.md` — `Personhood and
  moral consequence`).
- The name printed on a ticket identifies a passenger but is not the source of
  enforcement. Do not invent universal true-name magic (`universe/rules.md` —
  `Names and identity binding`).
- Do not connect the station, trains, shadows, gates, or staff passage to the
  Glass Gate or any other established system merely through visual resemblance
  (`universe/rules.md` — `Portals and crossing systems`; `Artifacts, motifs,
  and folklore`; `universe/locations.md` — `The Glass Gate`).
- Keep the physical era lightly specified. If Ruth's hospital memories sound
  modern-like, they still do not establish literal real-world Earth or a new
  timeline coordinate (`universe/premise.md` — `The deep-time shared world`;
  `universe/timeline.md` — `Established era order`).
- Keep Ruth's death and Sabine's punishment non-graphic. Dread should arise
  from coercion, deadline, sound, motion, and consequence
  (`universe/style-guide.md` — `Audience and content boundaries`).
- The ending must leave Ruth at the station with a blank, unusable ticket.
  Moral growth, helpful work, or companionship must not quietly restore a
  destination or prove that staying is cosmically rewarded.

## Proposed inventions

All items below are **story-local proposals**, not established canon and not
claims about every culture's experience of the afterlife:

1. Ruth and the people in this crossing experience a tiled two-platform
   station with signs intelligible to them as `HEAVEN` and `HELL`.
2. Each arrival receives a personal paper ticket whose destination is hidden
   beneath a peel-back film until the passenger chooses to look or present it.
   The name is identification, not a magical power source.
3. Tickets are valid only for the paired service during which they are issued.
   Missing the heaven departure fades one countdown border without revealing
   eligibility; missing the final hell departure blanks the ticket
   permanently. This station has no observed reissue path for such a passenger.
4. A passenger can physically enter the heaven train with a hell-marked ticket;
   the boarding door does not enforce the assignment. After the heaven train
   begins moving, a local mismatch mechanism recognizes that passenger,
   manifests as bands of carriage-shadow, and forcibly transfers them
   hellward. The mechanism's maker, moral standard, and method of recognition
   remain unknown.
5. In Sabine's case, the transfer takes her through an impossible dark seam in
   the moving heaven carriage and places the same conscious person inside the
   still-waiting hell train. The later departure carries her toward the
   destination the station labels hell.
6. The heaven and hell trains have different sensory presentations—warm,
   bright ordinary carriages versus smoky glass, iron noise, and dry heat—but
   those impressions do not prove what waits beyond either route.
7. A locally informed attendant is present for one paired service, can report
   procedure, and can use an unmarked staff passage unavailable to passengers.
   Hester's nature and the scope of her authority remain unresolved.
8. New dead passengers continue to arrive after Ruth's issued service ends.
   Ruth's indefinite presence among them follows from her blank ticket, but
   helping them is her voluntary moral response rather than a divinely
   assigned office or a third afterlife destination.
9. Asha's afterlife destination and the possibility of reunion remain unknown;
   the heaven train's familiar sensory cue comes from Ruth's association, not
   verified communication.

## Failure modes to watch

- **Universalizing the station:** Do not say or imply that all souls, cultures,
  or eras encounter these trains, labels, tickets, or rules. Avoid unsupported
  “everyone,” “always,” and “the only afterlife” formulations.
- **Soft-pedaling the prompt mechanism:** Sabine must board the heaven train,
  the car must begin moving, and the hellward seizure must happen visibly
  *while she is on that moving train*. A cutaway, hearsay report, or seizure
  that occurs only on the platform will fail the story promise.
- **Neglecting either train:** The heaven train must tempt Ruth and carry
  Sabine; the hell train must hold the transferred Sabine, confront Ruth with
  her complicity, and depart while Ruth deliberately remains.
- **Endorsing an unexplained sentence:** Sabine's `HELL` ticket establishes her
  assignment, not the justice or infallibility of the standard. Do not add a
  confession designed to make the seizure feel deserved.
- **Using Sabine as an expendable demonstration:** Give her argument, humor,
  fear, resistance, continuity of identity, and an unmistakable look toward
  Ruth after the seizure. Ruth must acknowledge that helping the attempt
  exposed Sabine to harm.
- **Making Hester omniscient:** Keep her dialogue inside the facts she has
  observed. She cannot settle destination ontology, assignment justice,
  builders, universality, Asha's fate, or Ruth's moral worth.
- **Turning staying into a loophole:** Ruth must understand the one-service
  warning before the climax. The ticket must visibly become unusable; the gates
  must reject her; no later service may restore her options. Emphasize her lost
  chance of reaching Asha and the indefinite burden of witnessing other
  departures.
- **Turning staying into secret heaven:** Companionship, purpose, or helpful
  conduct cannot make the station a cozy reward. Keep its cold, repetition,
  uncertainty, exposure to future coercion, and lack of forgiveness present in
  the final image.
- **Granting redemption by analogy:** Staying with the new arrival does not
  repair Asha's death, earn cosmic credit, or reveal Ruth's correct assignment.
  It is a meaningful present choice, not retroactive absolution.
- **Letting the aftermath solve the plot:** Ruth's ticket must already be blank
  and her physical fate sealed before the new arrival appears. The arrival
  tests the internal change but supplies no new rule, escape, or decisive
  information.
- **Accidental third route:** Hester's staff passage is not available to Ruth
  and is not a passenger destination. Do not suggest appeal, reincarnation,
  ghosthood, a service promotion, or a secret platform.
- **Mechanism drift:** The train, not moral emotion or spoken names, enforces
  the mismatch after motion begins. Ruth's guilt, Sabine's resistance, and
  consent affect ethical meaning but do not power the station.
- **Abstract moralizing:** Keep the argument embodied in tickets, doors,
  windows, hands, clocks, and who stays beside whom. Limit explanatory
  dialogue and do not end on an essay about judgment.
- **Name drift:** Use only the checked forms above. Do not capitalize ordinary
  role nouns into aliases such as `Attendant`, `Witness`, or `Station`, and do
  not introduce names in incidental memories or crowd dialogue without a fresh
  registry check.
- **Content escalation:** Make the seizure terrifying but non-graphic. Do not
  show bodily destruction, torture, or details of Ruth's death.
