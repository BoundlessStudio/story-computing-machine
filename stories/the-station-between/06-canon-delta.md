# Proposed canon delta

This file proposes reusable shared-universe facts. It is not canon until the
user explicitly approves promotion and the facts are added to `universe/`.

Every item below is proposed only. The station facts are limited to the local
manifestation Ruth experiences; they do not establish what every dead person
encounters, who created or governs the mechanism, whether its assignments are
just, or what either destination ultimately contains.

## New characters or character facts

- **Ruth (proposed):** A dead woman and the story's first-person viewpoint.
  Years before her own death, she left her dying younger sister Asha alone
  overnight after promising to remain, then silenced Asha's final call. At the
  station, Ruth keeps her destination covered, helps Sabine Kroll board a
  mismatched train, admits her self-serving part in Sabine's forced transfer,
  and knowingly lets both of her own routes expire. Her ticket becomes blank
  and unusable, leaving her indefinitely at the station. She later chooses to
  remain beside a frightened arrival through that person's departure, but this
  conduct neither absolves Ruth nor rewards her with a new route. Her only
  character-facing form in the final prose is `Ruth`.
- **Asha (proposed):** Ruth's deceased younger sister, present only in Ruth's
  hospital memories. Asha asked Ruth to “stay until morning” and died before
  dawn after Ruth left. No vision, message, or verified contact from Asha
  appears at the station, and her destination remains unresolved. Her only
  character-facing form in the final prose is `Asha`.
- **Sabine Kroll (proposed):** A dead passenger whose opened ticket displays
  `HELL`. She challenges the authority and fairness of an unexplained
  assignment, chooses to attempt the heaven train, and asks Ruth to hold its
  door. After the train moves, the local mismatch mechanism transfers Sabine
  forcibly into the hell train. She remains the same conscious, resisting,
  recognizable person throughout, marked continuously by a blue elastic on
  her wrist. The story does not establish that her assignment is morally just.
  Her final character-facing forms are `Sabine Kroll` and `Sabine`; `Kroll` is
  never used alone.
- **Hester Pike (proposed):** A local station attendant of unresolved nature
  and authority. She reports procedures and events she has personally observed
  but does not know who assigns destinations, whether the assignments are
  fair, what either destination contains, or whether other afterlife
  manifestations work this way. She says she previously saw three conscious,
  resisting passengers enter trains that did not match their tickets and be
  transferred to the other train. She can use an unmarked staff passage that
  rejects Ruth. Her final character-facing forms are `Hester Pike` and
  `Hester`; `Pike` is never used alone, and `attendant` remains an ordinary
  lowercase role noun.
- The final prose also includes an unnamed adult arrival wearing wet slippers
  and a hospital wristband. She arrives after Ruth's ticket has expired; her
  disclosed ticket reads `HEAVEN`, the matching gate admits her, and she
  departs without a mismatch response after asking Ruth to stay beside her
  until the train comes. She remains unnamed and has no registry form.

## New locations

- **The local station manifestation (proposed):** Ruth and the dead people she
  encounters experience a cold, institutional, tiled station with two tracks,
  passenger gates, dark exterior windows, information counter, waiting bench,
  and departure boards. The boards display the locally intelligible
  destinations `HEAVEN` and `HELL`. This is one experienced form of the real
  underlying afterlife, not a proposed universal or exhaustive description of
  it.

## New rules, capabilities, or costs

- **Personal one-service tickets (proposed):** An arrival receives a cream
  paper ticket printed with the passenger's name and a destination hidden
  beneath peel-back film. The reverse reads `VALID THIS SERVICE ONLY` and
  `NO REISSUE`, with two countdown borders. The printed name identifies the
  passenger but is not established as a source of magical power.
- **Ticket expiry (proposed):** Each ticket is valid only for the paired
  service during which it is issued. When the heaven train leaves, the outer
  border of Ruth's still-covered ticket fades without revealing whether that
  route would have admitted her. When the hell train leaves, its inner border
  fades and all remaining ink and film drain away, leaving a blank cream
  rectangle. Hester has never seen a later train at this station accept such an
  expired ticket and says she cannot issue a replacement.
- **Passenger-gate behavior (proposed):** A presented mismatched ticket makes
  the heaven gate pulse red and remain shut. A presented matching ticket makes
  that gate light green and open. A blank expired ticket produces no light at
  either gate. The hell gate waits closed with its plate dark during Ruth's
  final boarding window; Hester says it will respond if Ruth presents her
  ticket, but Ruth declines to do so. Both gates bolt against Ruth when her
  paired service expires.
- **Service gap and carriage door (proposed):** A narrow gap between the heaven
  gate's arm and the wall is physically passable by a passenger who squeezes
  through it. The heaven carriage door can be held open by another person and
  does not itself inspect or enforce ticket assignment. These features permit
  boarding but do not defeat the later mismatch response.
- **Moving-train mismatch transfer (proposed):** A passenger can board the
  heaven train with a hell-marked ticket, but enforcement begins only after the
  train starts moving. In Sabine's observed case, the destination field around
  the black word `HELL` darkens to ash gray while the rest of the ticket stays
  cream; bands of carriage-shadow restrain and drag her through a dark seam in
  the moving car and place the same conscious person inside the still-waiting
  hell train. The ticket retains that darkened destination field afterward.
  The mechanism's maker, moral standard, and method of recognition remain
  unknown.
- **Distinct train presentations (proposed):** The heaven train appears silver,
  quiet, bright, and warm, with clean windows, blue seats, brass racks, and
  scents Ruth associates with lemon lotion and clean cotton. The hell train
  appears dark red and riveted, with smoke-filmed windows, dry heat, iron noise,
  wooden benches, and aged fittings. These presentations are observed sensory
  features, not proof of what either destination contains.
- **Bounded attendant access (proposed):** Hester can observe one paired
  service, explain limited local procedure, and use an unmarked staff passage.
  The passage opens to Hester but pushes Ruth's blank ticket back and closes
  against her. It is not a passenger route, appeal, third destination, or
  demonstrated escape.
- **Continued arrivals and permanent station state (proposed):** New dead
  passengers and new paired services continue to appear after Ruth's issued
  service ends, but Ruth's blank ticket does not reset or gain access to their
  trains. Remaining with later arrivals is Ruth's voluntary response to her
  irreversible station state, not an assigned office, a secret form of heaven,
  or a source of cosmic forgiveness.
- **Limited destination knowledge (proposed):** The words `HEAVEN` and `HELL`
  are displayed by this station and used by its passengers, but no witness in
  the story knows the destinations' ultimate nature or the justice, maker, or
  scope of the assignment system. Familiar warmth on the heaven train reflects
  Ruth's associations and does not verify communication with Asha or establish
  where Asha went.

## Timeline events

- **Asha's death (proposed, relative date only):** Years before Ruth's death,
  Asha died before dawn after Ruth left the hospital despite promising to stay
  until morning; Ruth had silenced Asha's final call.
- **Ruth's paired service (proposed, relative date only):** After Ruth dies and
  arrives at the station, Sabine is forcibly transferred from the moving
  heaven train to the waiting hell train. Ruth then lets the hell train depart
  without uncovering or presenting her own ticket, permanently expiring it.
- **Ruth's first later arrival (proposed, relative date only):** After Hester
  leaves, Ruth remains at the station and stays beside an unnamed passenger
  until that passenger boards a matched heaven train. Ruth's blank ticket
  remains unusable.

No fixed calendar date, physical-world location, or shared-universe era is
proposed.

## Possible conflicts or retcons

- **Locality boundary:** No conflict is proposed if the station, its binary
  signs, tickets, gates, trains, and transfer are recorded as this group's
  local afterlife manifestation. Treating them as the sole afterlife form for
  every culture or era would conflict with the established variation in how
  the afterlife is experienced and described.
- **Knowledge boundary:** The proposed observations do not settle who operates
  the station, how assignments are made, whether assignments are infallible or
  just, what the destinations contain, or what kind of being Hester is.
  Promoting any such answer would exceed the story's evidence.
- **Personhood boundary:** Sabine's forced transfer must remain an instance of
  coercion against the same conscious person, not evidence that the mechanism
  is morally neutral or that her `HELL` assignment is deserved.
- **Asha's unresolved destination:** No promotion should place Asha in heaven,
  hell, the station, or any other continuation. The familiar scent and laughter
  cadence are explicitly Ruth's associations, not verified contact.
