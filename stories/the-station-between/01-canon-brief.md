# Canon brief

## Hard constraints

- An underlying afterlife is real in this universe, but its experienced and
  described form varies among cultures. The two trains can therefore be a real
  local manifestation of that afterlife; the story must not assert, from one
  protagonist's experience, that every dead person in every culture and era
  encounters the same station or an exhaustive heaven/hell binary
  (`universe/rules.md` — `## Afterlife and continuation`).
- The prompt's heaven train, hell train, wrong-train seizure, and protagonist
  who ultimately remains at the station must stay materially true. Presenting
  “heaven” and “hell” as the protagonist's or station's intelligible labels is
  compatible with canon; silently replacing the trains with metaphor,
  hallucination, or a different destination system is not
  (`stories/the-station-between/00-prompt.md` — `## Story controls`).
- Reality may be coherent beyond the protagonist's knowledge, but the
  protagonist, other dead people, and any attendants may hold incomplete or
  incorrect explanations. The narration must distinguish what is observed
  from what a character merely believes
  (`universe/premise.md` — `## Knowledge and connection`).
- No universal authority administers all supernatural systems across every era.
  A station attendant, judge, conductor, or bureaucracy may be established as
  local to this manifestation, but the story must not casually turn it into a
  universe-spanning divine government
  (`universe/premise.md` — `## Knowledge and connection`;
  `universe/rules.md` — `## Divinity`).
- If an on-page being is called a god in the project's objective taxonomy,
  godhood requires worship, power, and authority together. “Divine,” “angel,”
  “demon,” or similar cultural language may instead be limited viewpoint
  vocabulary, provided the story does not use it to make unsupported backstage
  claims (`universe/rules.md` — `## System categories`; `## Divinity`;
  `universe/glossary.md` — `## Magic, technology, and divinity`).
- The station's coercive assignment and the protagonist's refusal must preserve
  personhood, consent, grief, and moral consequence. A cosmic explanation
  cannot nullify the protagonist's identity or reduce the choice to a
  meaningless error (`universe/style-guide.md` —
  `## Personhood and moral consequence`).
- Consent, desire, sacrifice, guilt, and fear have no single universal
  metaphysical effect. If any of them changes what the station can do, that
  effect must be a defined and internally consistent local mechanism
  (`universe/rules.md` — `## Costs, desire, and local mechanisms`).
- Death and supernatural punishment must remain non-graphic and centered on
  character and consequence, consistent with the prompt's PG-13 control and the
  universe content ceiling (`universe/style-guide.md` —
  `## Audience and content boundaries`;
  `stories/the-station-between/00-prompt.md` — `## Story controls`).

## Useful established context

- The shared setting is one physical world persisting through deep time within
  one coherent underlying cosmology. The afterlife can connect this story to
  that universe without requiring the station to occupy a known physical era
  or location (`universe/premise.md` — `## The deep-time shared world`).
- Culture-bound language is expected: people may use religious or technical
  words for real systems without those words being objective cosmological
  categories. This supports letting the protagonist understand the trains as
  “heaven” and “hell” while leaving their ultimate ontology partly mysterious
  (`universe/rules.md` — `## System categories`;
  `universe/glossary.md` — `## Magic, technology, and divinity`).
- Ghosts, reincarnation, and digitally mediated continuations are possible in
  the wider universe. They are options, not requirements, and any appearance
  here would need a coherent relationship to this station and the underlying
  afterlife (`universe/rules.md` — `## Afterlife and continuation`).
- The universe favors incomplete knowledge, degraded evidence, and choices
  whose moral weight survives uncertainty. The protagonist does not need
  reliable proof of either destination for the refusal to matter
  (`universe/premise.md` — `## Memory, myth, and recoverable truth`;
  `## Knowledge and connection`).
- The station can be an independent crossing mechanism. Portal-like systems
  have local access rules, directionality, costs, and limits; resemblance to
  the Glass Gate does not establish shared origin or operation
  (`universe/rules.md` — `## Portals and crossing systems`;
  `## Artifacts, motifs, and folklore`;
  `universe/locations.md` — `## The Glass Gate`).
- The literary-dark-fantasy tone and tightly local scale fit the universe's
  permitted tonal range; the story need not advertise the deep-time setting or
  connect to recurring characters (`universe/style-guide.md` —
  `## Tonal range, scale, and connection`;
  `universe/premise.md` — `## Memory, myth, and recoverable truth`).

## Conflicts or ambiguity

- **No blocking canon conflict found.** The two-train premise is compatible
  with the established afterlife when framed as a local experience or
  interface. It would become a canon problem if the prose declared that the
  station and its binary judgment are the one invariant form experienced by
  all dead people, because canon explicitly allows cultural variation in how
  the universal afterlife is experienced and described
  (`universe/rules.md` — `## Afterlife and continuation`).
- The prompt requires people to be “meant for hell,” but canon does not say who
  assigns that result, whether the assignment is morally correct, or whether
  “hell” is an objective name. The story may preserve uncertainty about the
  system's justice, as the prompt contract permits, but it cannot present a
  narrator's suspicion as settled cosmology
  (`stories/the-station-between/00-prompt.md` — `## Assumptions`;
  `universe/premise.md` — `## Knowledge and connection`).
- A wrong-train passenger being dragged to hell is not in conflict with a
  universal consent rule, because canon establishes no such rule. The story
  must nevertheless confront the ethical consequence of coercion rather than
  imply that metaphysical enforcement makes it morally neutral
  (`universe/rules.md` — `## Costs, desire, and local mechanisms`;
  `universe/style-guide.md` — `## Personhood and moral consequence`).
- `stories/INDEX.md` contains no row whose Canon column is `yes`; therefore no
  finalized canon story supplies a competing or more specific afterlife rule.
  Candidate, in-progress, and historical stories were not treated as authority
  (`stories/INDEX.md` — `# Story index`;
  `universe/README.md` — `## Authority order`).

## Unknowns

- **Unknown:** whether “heaven” and “hell” are objective destinations, culturally
  translated labels, subjective presentations, regions within one afterlife,
  or something else. Canon establishes only the underlying afterlife and
  variation in its experience (`universe/rules.md` —
  `## Afterlife and continuation`).
- **Unknown:** who built or operates this station and the trains, if anyone;
  whether its attendants are dead people, living beings, gods, constructs, or
  aspects of the afterlife; and what authority any of them actually possesses.
- **Unknown:** the standard for being “meant for hell,” who applies it, whether
  mistakes or appeals are possible, and whether the protagonist is assigned to
  either train.
- **Unknown:** how a wrong-train passenger is recognized and dragged away, what
  agency performs the seizure, and whether that event occurs physically,
  perceptually, or by some other local mechanism.
- **Unknown:** whether remaining at the station is normally possible, how long
  it can last, whether time passes there, and what irreversible, relational, or
  moral consequences staying creates. The prompt requires staying to be
  possible and costly, so the story must establish a local answer without
  presenting it as a universal afterlife law
  (`stories/the-station-between/00-prompt.md` — `## Story controls`).
- **Unknown:** whether the dead retain bodies, needs, complete memories, or the
  ability to change; whether the station can alter identity; and what becomes
  of people after either journey.
- **Unknown:** the protagonist's culture, religion, physical-world era, place of
  death, and prior knowledge of the afterlife. No established chronology must
  be chosen unless the story elects to connect itself to a known era
  (`universe/timeline.md` — `## Established era order`).
- **Unknown:** how this manifestation relates to ghosts, reincarnation, copied
  minds, or digital continuations. Absence of an explanation is safe if none of
  those mechanisms appears; introducing one requires a story-specific
  relationship (`universe/rules.md` — `## Afterlife and continuation`;
  `## Memory, transformation, copies, and branches`).

## Safe invention space

- Establish the station as the limited experience of this protagonist and the
  dead people encountered there, with no claim that it is the afterlife's only
  form.
- Invent local sensory details, architecture, schedules, tickets, platform
  customs, announcements, and train behavior. Keep them local rather than
  turning them into universal laws.
- Define a narrow, causally consistent rule for how the station identifies a
  wrong-train passenger and enforces the hellward transfer. The story may leave
  the rule's ultimate maker or moral legitimacy unknown.
- Invent one local consequence of refusing both trains—such as responsibility
  to arrivals, erosion of an opportunity, a binding duty, isolation, or another
  irreversible cost—provided it does not erase the required final state that
  the protagonist remains at the station.
- Use attendants, conductors, other dead people, or messages whose knowledge is
  explicitly limited. Their claims can conflict if the prose preserves them as
  testimony rather than silently elevating one to universal truth.
- Let the protagonist's personal memories, guilt, love, fear, or religious
  vocabulary shape how the station is understood without making those feelings
  universal metaphysical fuel.
- Keep the physical-world setting lightly specified or place the protagonist in
  an ordinary, unnamed culture or era. If a known era is chosen, respect the
  established deep-time order and that the modern-like setting is not
  necessarily literal real-world Earth
  (`universe/premise.md` — `## The deep-time shared world`;
  `universe/timeline.md` — `## Established era order`).

## Name constraints

- The prompt supplies no character-facing names, aliases, usernames, epithets,
  named animals, or person-like entities. An unnamed protagonist is safe; any
  introduced name must be checked and registered later. `stories/NAMES.md` is
  production memory, not canon (`stories/NAMES.md` — `# Character name
  registry`; `## Rules`).
- Do not reuse the registry's unresolved collision forms: `Lena`, `Mara`,
  `Nisha`, `Pell`, or `Voss`. These already refer to distinct identities and
  need resolution within their existing sources, not another reuse
  (`stories/NAMES.md` — `## Known unresolved name collisions`).
- Avoid adding to readily confusable reserved clusters already visible in the
  registry, including `Oren` / `Orrin`, `Leth` / `Letha`, `Cal` / `Caldrin` /
  `Calvea`, and `Nessa` / `Nina` / `Nisha`. A prior reversed form, `Pell Orin`,
  was already rejected because it collided with `Orrin Pell` and `Pell`
  (`stories/NAMES.md` — registry rows for Oren, Orrin Pell, Leth Varen, Letha,
  Cal Mercer, Caldrin, Sister Calvea Dorn, Nessa Vale, Nina, Nisha, and Kelan
  Rusk).
- Be especially careful with titles or common words used as proper names for
  station personnel or supernatural entities. Reserved character-facing forms
  include `Captain`, `Examiner Dovek` / `Dovek`, `Assessor Havil` / `Havil`,
  `Court Ghost`, `Keeper Below`, `Regent`, `The Goddess of Time` / `Time`,
  `Goddess`, `Mother`, and `Saint`. Ordinary lowercase role nouns remain
  available when they are not used as a character's name or unique label
  (`stories/NAMES.md` — corresponding registry rows).
- Other reserved aliases that could be tempting in an afterlife story include
  `Green Coat`, `Red Scarf`, `Tea Girl`, `the Daughter`, and `Mother-fragment`.
  Reuse would require a deliberate narrative connection or identity/theme
  device, a same-or-distinct identity ruling, and a reader-disambiguation plan;
  convenience or genre familiarity is not sufficient
  (`stories/NAMES.md` — `## Rules` and corresponding registry rows).
- After planning, validate every proposed full name, given name, surname used
  alone, mononym, nickname, alias, title-as-name, named companion, and
  person-like construct against the complete registry. The primary coordinator,
  not the canon researcher, owns registry edits.

## Required checks after drafting

- Search for universalizing claims such as “everyone,” “all souls,” “the only
  afterlife,” or “always” near station procedures; ensure the text supports
  only this local manifestation unless a new reusable cosmology fact is
  explicitly recorded as a proposed canon delta.
- Verify both destination trains are real within the story, the hell-assigned
  passenger's attempted heaven journey triggers a concrete hellward seizure,
  and the event causally bears on the protagonist's refusal
  (`stories/the-station-between/00-prompt.md` — `## Completion tests`).
- Verify the protagonist physically remains at the station at the end, while
  staying has escalating and morally meaningful consequences rather than
  functioning as an effortless loophole
  (`stories/the-station-between/00-prompt.md` — `## Story controls`;
  `## Completion tests`).
- Distinguish observed station behavior from claims made by narrators,
  passengers, announcements, or attendants. No testimony should accidentally
  settle the justice, infallibility, builders, or universal reach of the
  system.
- If the draft uses a god, ghost, reincarnation, copied mind, digital
  continuation, magical device, or crossing link, confirm that its mechanism
  and relationship to the underlying afterlife are coherent and do not imply a
  connection to the Glass Gate or another system from resemblance alone.
- Check that any rules involving consent, guilt, desire, sacrifice, memory, or
  names remain local and consistent; none may be treated as a universal power
  source or identity law without new approved canon.
- Confirm the cosmic mechanism preserves grief, relationships, personhood, and
  the protagonist's moral agency, and that punishment and death remain
  non-graphic.
- Recheck every final character-facing name and alias against
  `stories/NAMES.md`; document deliberate reuse, update the registry through
  the primary coordinator, and run the story-scoped name validation before
  completion.
- Record every reusable addition—station reach, operator, assignment rule,
  destination ontology, enforcement mechanism, or consequence of staying—in
  `06-canon-delta.md` as proposed rather than established canon.
