# Prompt contract

## Verbatim writing prompt

> [WP] A person dies and wakes up in a train station, one train leading to heaven and the other to hell, anyone who is meant for hell but trys to enter the train to heaven will get dragged to hell while on the train, not wanting to find out what the afterlife is like, they stay at the station

## Story controls

- Working title: The Station Between
- Target length: 2,500–4,000 words
- POV: First person, centered tightly on the dead protagonist.
- Tense: Past.
- Tone and genre: Literary dark fantasy with existential tension, restrained
  dread, and humane emotional stakes.
- Audience/content rating: PG-13; death, grief, fear of damnation, and implied
  supernatural punishment, without graphic torture or gore.
- Required elements:
  - The protagonist dies and awakens in a train station.
  - Two trains are understood to lead respectively to heaven and hell.
  - A person assigned to hell who boards the heaven train is dragged to hell
    during the journey.
  - The protagonist refuses to test either destination because they do not want
    to discover what the afterlife is like.
  - Remaining at the station is a sustained dramatic choice with consequences,
    not merely an opening image.
  - The ending resolves what staying means for this protagonist while preserving
    the prompt's core outcome that they stay at the station.
- Prohibited elements:
  - No silent replacement of the two-train premise with a different cosmology.
  - No graphic depiction of torture, bodily destruction, or the protagonist's
    death.
  - No effortless loophole that removes the moral and emotional cost of refusing
    both trains.
  - No canon promotion or edits to authoritative `universe/` notes without
    explicit user approval.

## Assumptions

- The station and its procedures will be treated as a local, story-scale
  afterlife manifestation unless the canon brief establishes broader rules.
- The protagonist may interact with attendants or other dead people, but the
  protagonist's final choice must remain central.
- “Meant for hell” describes the station's apparent assignment system; the
  story need not prove that system morally infallible unless canon requires it.
- The trains' destinations may remain partly mysterious so long as the danger
  of boarding the wrong train is concrete and the central dramatic question is
  answered.
- The protagonist can change emotionally without changing the promised physical
  outcome: at the end, they still remain at the station.

## Completion tests

- The result is a complete short story, not a synopsis or chapter fragment.
- The central promise of the prompt is dramatized and resolved.
- The story passes canon and continuity review.
- Every character-facing name and alias is checked against `stories/NAMES.md`;
  any deliberate reuse has a documented narrative reason.
- The polished story falls within 2,500–4,000 words.
- Both destination trains appear on the page, and the wrong-train consequence
  affects the protagonist's decision rather than serving as background trivia.
- Staying creates escalating pressure, demands an irreversible or morally
  meaningful response, and remains the protagonist's final state.
