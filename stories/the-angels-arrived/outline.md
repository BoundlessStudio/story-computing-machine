# Provisional outline: The Angels Arrived

## The experience promised

The original request centers unexpected angelic destroyers and one man's plan to stop an apocalypse. Later directions require a simpler confrontation, "not tricks, no weird setup": a man with a really big gun, aimed where the angels are vulnerable. The gun must now be physically carried, large and awkward enough to burden him, without a mech. The user prefers a railgun or laser cannon. I infer direct, satisfying defiance: enough firepower to hurt something overwhelming, and judgment to put it where it counts. The image supports immense rainbow-dark machinery, ruins, and a small armed figure beneath it. Aquarion / Neon Genesis Evangelion can inform force and awe; the later simplicity and carrying directions control the action. Profession, helpers, precise target, costs, and outcome remain choices. The attached-document instruction adds no story event.

## Chosen direction, still provisional

Close third person through **Sevrin Dass**, a soldier carrying a heavy anti-armor railgun from a wrecked defensive position. The gun is longer than his armspan, with a bulky power block beneath its stock and a broad sling. He can carry it, but its length catches corners and its weight soon exhausts his shoulder and hands. Three darts and charge for three shots remain. He can rest it on ordinary rubble to aim; his own body moves it between positions. Other soldiers evacuate people, and a radio operator relays his targeting instructions.

The angels are black pyramidal bodies amid immense, flowing, iridescent wings. Their outer armor defeats the opening bombardment. When the wings spread to fire, a joint beneath the central body opens and briefly exposes the inner weapon chamber. The wings screen that opening from distant, shallow-angle fire. Sevrin sees it from directly underneath. His plan is simple: carry the gun into that dangerous angle, wait for the plates to clear, and put a dart into the chamber before it fires. Each angel must be shot separately. I choose a successful defense without undoing the losses.

## Possible scenes and turns

1. **The welcome becomes an attack.** The arrivals fill a sunlit sky like moving stained glass. People gather to greet them. The first flare destroys the welcome square. Sevrin survives beneath a collapsed part of the platform. From there, he watches distant defensive rounds burst against wing armor. As the angel above him prepares its next attack, he sees upward through the briefly opened joint into bright, moving machinery. His particular position reveals what the distant gunners cannot see.

2. **Carry the gun to the angle.** Sevrin retrieves the railgun and hauls it across the ruins into an exposed plaza beneath the angel. Its muzzle catches in a doorway; lifting it over fallen masonry costs time and leaves his arms shaking. He rests its fore-end on a broken wall and radios his intended target. The position gives him a steep view into the joint and gives the angel a clear view of him. He does not lure it or rely on a special response.

3. **The gun does the work.** He fires as the plates clear. The first dart catches the armored lip and tears a wing loose, interrupting the attack. Sevrin corrects his aim and puts the second dart through the exposed chamber. The core breaks and the immense body falls across the emptied district. He radios the demonstrated angle and target. Other surviving gun teams begin applying his plan. The falling wing destroys his firing rest, forcing him to shoulder the weapon and move with one dart left.

4. **One more firing position.** A much larger angel turns toward the man and gun that killed its fellow. From where Sevrin is, its overlapping wing armor hides the deeper chamber. He cannot assume his remaining dart will reach it. He carries the railgun farther into the open, directly beneath the approaching body, and struggles to steady its weight while his exhausted hands keep slipping. He waits until he has a clear line through the separating plates. The chamber begins to brighten for its attack; his shot enters before that light can discharge. The chamber breaks, its gathering flare collapses, and he runs with the hot, heavy gun as the angel falls. The uncertainty is resolved by visible penetration and interrupted fire, rather than by announcing that the same shot must work on a larger enemy.

5. **What survived.** Radio reports confirm other angels falling separately to the same targeting plan. Sevrin stands beneath the dark, broken wing of the giant he killed, the railgun hanging heavily from its sling, as living voices return to the city. The ruins and dead remain. The attacks have stopped and people are emerging. The ending rewards his weapon's force and the judgment that brought it to bear.

**Main creative risk:** making the exposed target seem obvious and safely available. Keep the opening visible only from the costly underside angle, and make his bodily effort affect arrival and aim. Make each shot's arrival precede the enemy's flare. A familiar armored weak point can satisfy this request if the gun's destructive work and the man's danger are concrete.
