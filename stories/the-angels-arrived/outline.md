# Provisional outline: The Angels Arrived

## The experience promised

The explicit request puts an unforeseen angelic arrival, the angels as "machines of our destruction," and "one man" with "a plan [to] stop the apocalypse" at the center. I infer the pleasure of apparent salvation becoming terrifying, followed by human ingenuity challenging overwhelming force. "Apocalypse" supports civilization-wide stakes; the image suggests immense, dark, prismatic machinery over ruins and a lone human beneath it. Awe, dread, urgency, and a counterattack are interpretations, not prescribed tone. The later Aquarion / Neon Genesis Evangelion hint, offered in response to the risk of an easy weakness, supports embodied access and consequential control as useful influences without prescribing either franchise's plot or mythology. The man's profession, relationships, the angels' origin, and his ultimate success remain open. The instruction to distinguish attached-document instructions from the user's request adds no story event or theme.

## Chosen direction, still provisional

Close third person through **Sevrin Dass**, a first-contact engineer who helped welcome the arrivals. His plan is to board a damaged angel, let its fellows retrieve it toward their source, and turn its own cutting ribbon against the structure feeding the worldwide dismantling. He plans and takes the decisive risk; surviving scientists, a rescue pilot, and defenders help execute it. No intimate relationship is proposed as the center. His immediate attachment is to people he urged toward the visitors, some still alive beneath the city.

The angels are enormous autonomous dismantling engines: black pyramidal cores inside flowing, rainbow-dark wings. Their apparent mercy clears obstacles and preserves useful machinery. Their ultimate makers remain unknown. An operator channel, discovered during first contact and tested on a fragment, offers a limited motor relationship to one engine. It gives Sevrin neither command over its fellows nor strength equal to their combined force. Decisions are autonomous; energy comes through visible feeders from the aperture. A charged cutting edge burns for a fixed interval, while the ribbon carrying it remains movable. These are chosen rules, not user requirements.

## Possible scenes and turns

1. **Welcome, then the first cut.** An angel catches a falling passenger aircraft. Sevrin cheers with the welcome delegation. Later its wings loop around an inhabited block and draw tight, laying the city open with the same careful handling. The aircraft was in the way. On the contact platform, Sevrin briefly experiences a whole district as material to be folded and carried before he tears himself free.

2. **A plan with evidence.** Damaged-engine footage shows a feeder breaking and its angel going slack. Other engines carefully retrieve it to a rib beneath the aperture; there its feed reconnects and it moves again. The rib visibly carries the outgoing feeders. Reports from other cities share the source's interruptions. In the damaged contact laboratory, Sevrin moves a fragment's vane through the retained interface. Without its feed it is helpless; when charged, its edge keeps burning after he tries to stop it and cuts the bench as he turns it aside. He proposes boarding another damaged engine before collection. One observed retrieval is evidence, not certainty: the collectors may detect him, restore the wrong limb first, or take him past the rib before he can act.

3. **Get underneath heaven.** Defenders hold a route across the ruins while the pilot brings Sevrin to the damaged angel's contact cavity. This approach earns the image's human-to-machine scale. When trapped survivors call, he sends his only extraction aircraft to them. He boards knowing his planned escape will be unavailable.

4. **Let them carry him.** Sevrin yields his engine to the observed collection procedure instead of trying to fight his way upward. His human body remains inside a contact cavity while the unfamiliar body's weight, pressure, and movement crowd his senses. He cannot safely move every wing or simply command the collectors. They draw him toward the aperture with the dreadful care that first looked like salvation. He must wait through their grip until the feed is restored beside the rib, then act before they carry him beyond it. The danger is whether his limited evidence and motor access will suffice, not whether he can learn to duel.

5. **Turn their work against them.** With his core held by the collectors, Sevrin loops one restored cutting ribbon over the feeder-bearing rib and charges its edge. Their continued pull on his core draws the loop tight across that rib, repeating the first city cut. His damaged engine could not supply that force alone. He holds the loop through the bodily feedback until the charged interval completes; disconnecting early would release his motor hold. The aperture and its shared feed fail. Across the city, cutting edges go dark; returning signals from elsewhere confirm the same cessation. The full result is demonstrated through interrupted destruction and living witnesses, not a new command or a claim that everything is healed.

6. **After the end that did not happen.** The city remains ruined and the dead remain dead. After dawn, the crew he sent to the survivors returns to search the fallen engine and finds him alive. They open the cavity to an ordinary sky. His plan has stopped the apocalypse; the ending rewards that costly, ingenious action and the lives it makes possible without explaining away the destruction.

**Strongest creative risk:** the observed behavior could feel as convenient as a password if its uncertainty and the final physical action are merely asserted. Show collection before the plan; show the rib, feeder connection, loop, and pull before success. Preserve the angels' terrible care and Sevrin's consequential choices. The scale and sensory cost should be felt in the scene, without obligatory trauma or a sentient machine's resistance added because of the franchise hint.
