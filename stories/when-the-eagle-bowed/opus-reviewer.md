# Opus review: When the Eagle Bowed

Independent audit for a later editing pass. It is not a `review.md` verdict,
does not change canon state, and does not authorize edits by itself.

## Verdict

- Dialogue: REVISE — in the first half the sisters trade matched quips, one correction exists only to explain the horns, the three commanders share one legal voice, and Pelka answers a word Yavren never said aloud; the second half holds.
- Prose: PASS — concrete and well staged; only a reversal tic, a coronation summary sentence and water-infrastructure texture to sweep.
- Continuity and prompt: PASS — the conquest, the completed revenge, the uncontrolled Eagle shadow and the open ending are all delivered; a few small staging and causality gaps remain.
- Edit scope: targeted-dialogue
- Existing review.md said: PASS, "Dialogue: PASS"; disagree on dialogue. Its praise fits L203–L397, not the flight scenes and the officers' exchange (L45–L199).

The sisters' dialogue has a real engine. Pelka drags Yavren back to the present with concrete questions and tasks, and Yavren's lines slip into a dead queen's register. Where the story trusts that engine (the gallery and spur grounding, the pie, the roping, "I want the kings dead", the chapel, "He hasn't got a weapon") it works. The trouble is L45–L199. Fleeing a living landslide and three armies, the two women trade evenly weighted dry one-liners that end on cappers ("Already decided.", "Then keep not knowing it. Up.", "Then watch."). Pelka's grounding voice and Yavren's hunter voice merge into one wry register, and the first crack between them plays as banter. The three enemy commanders also share one voice, a legalistic "the warrant says / it has a seal" duel. The edit pass should keep Pelka's concrete grounding as her signature and give Yavren a tracker's attention instead of wordplay. The Queen register should stay rare and cold, fear should change how people talk during the flight, and each commander should sound like his own king's interest. The story is near its word ceiling, so prefer cuts to additions.

## Edit guardrails

- Prompt (last profile prospective-2026-08-21; the rewrite request authorizes reworking dialogue, not plot): the eagle bows to an orphaned huntress; living armor claws out of the mountain; every king's forces are ordered to kill her before she remembers; she defeats all three kings with the armor, is crowned ("Queen of the Valley"), and so completes the Queen's revenge; the Eagle shadow from the armor is the greater terror; the story ends as the shadow begins acting beyond her control and stays open-ended (keep the final wing-lift). The rating is broadly accessible dark fantasy with non-graphic violence.
- Length: the story is about 3,800 words and the band is 2,500–4,000, so net edits should cut words or stay neutral.
- Universe: the talonmail is a living colony and the gestures come from the bearer (LOCKED "Living agency and magic": only life uses magic). Personhood (LOCKED, style guide): the Queen's impressions open "a life already hers" (L263); Yavren is one continuing person, not a vessel for the colony or the dead Queen.
- Cross-story: `stories/yavrens-return` is a bookend whose prompt explicitly follows a different (departure) ending and says this conquest version is not being revised, so it does not bind these edits. Keep Pelka as Yavren's adult foster sister and trapline partner, and keep both women adults.
- Internal facts later scenes depend on: the colony treats raised weapons as a threat (L241), which the final "He hasn't got a weapon" turns on; the binding sign (L355, L389); the colony eats iron; the ash king's stone spearheads, bronze-bound doors and lack of iron; the three founders at the scaffold (blue, ash, gold). Pelka's condition "If you bring a house down with someone in it, I stop helping." pays off quietly in "There are men alive under the gallery."
- Keep what works: Pelka's grounding questions, the one earned Queen-voice aphorism "For a hundred years.", and the late exchanges listed under Preserve.

## Dialogue findings

### D1 · Major · Horn-code correction is exposition dressed as a mistake

- Where: L53–L59, fleeing the goat, first talk about the horns
- Text:
> "Three notes was rockfall."
> "When we were ten. They changed it after the spring flood. Three is royal danger now. Rockfall is two, wait, then one."
- Problem: Ground/knowledge, Exposition, correction ping-pong. Yavren has hunted these slopes beside Pelka since childhood, and nothing explains why she still holds a code that changed when they were ten. The text doesn't mark it as a Queen-memory leak, because Pelka's reply dates it to their own childhood. The mistake exists so Pelka can recite the horn code for the reader, and it sets up the correction rhythm from the second exchange onward. A four-sentence code lesson in mid-flight is also implausibly composed.
- Fix: Cut Yavren's wrong code and Pelka's explanation. Keep "West horn" and "Weir riders" (subject to P1), and let the danger show through action (Pelka hauling her off the usual path) and through the warrant scene. If Yavren needs to react, give her a practical reaction (where to run, her bow), not a correction.

### D2 · Major · Three commanders, one legal voice

- Where: L107–L121, the three detachments below the traverse
- Text:
> "Clear the two houses under the shelf," he called.
> "The warrant says kill the returned bearer and anyone who heard her named."
> "The warrant doesn't have three farms under a moving cliff."
> "It has a seal."
> "Alive until we peel the shell off. Hands first. The warrant gets the rest."
- Problem: Identical Voices, a negotiation routine, and a Punchline Patch. All three enemies speak in the same terse legal register, and each makes "the warrant" the subject of his sentence. The weir–ash exchange is a debate that ends on a capper ("It has a seal."). The ash rider reads the warrant aloud to an officer who carries his own copy (L121), so the line exists for the reader. There are also Ground problems. "Anyone who heard her named" has no recoverable referent, since nobody has called her anything. The road captain talks about peeling "the shell" off a woman who isn't wearing any (the only plate was knocked off at L79), and "Hands first" has no clear meaning. The weir officer's "two houses" become "three farms" two lines later, and "two roofs, a hay shed" at L149.
- Fix: Give each commander one line driven by his king's interest, addressed to his own men or to Yavren rather than to a rival as legal argument. The weir officer counts people and roofs (keep "Clear the two houses under the shelf"). The ash rider wants witnesses silenced; make the clause concrete, e.g. anyone who saw the thing come for her. The road captain wants the prize intact: the armor, whatever it turns out to be. Cut "It has a seal." Use "warrant" at most once in this dialogue, since L121's narration already shows the three sealed tubes. Make the house count agree.

### D3 · Major · Pelka supplies a word that exists only in Yavren's head

- Where: L131–L137, looking for a way under the nets
- Text:
> "No. Too obvious. They'll close it when we move. We need—"
> "The wet cut," Pelka said. "Say the thing you can see."
- Problem: Ground/uptake. Yavren breaks off at "We need—", and the channel she is reaching for appears only in the narration's word-search (L133). Nothing on the page shows Pelka which feature Yavren means, yet Pelka names it exactly and tells her to name what she "can see". The reply answers the author's knowledge, not what Pelka heard. The grounding move itself is right for Pelka.
- Fix: Add one visible cue before Pelka speaks: Yavren's finger or gaze fixed on the channel, or Pelka following her eyes to it. Then "The wet cut" is Pelka naming what she saw Yavren staring at. Keep "Say the thing you can see." (See P1 for "drainage channel" and "soldiers' culvert".)

### D4 · Major · Near-massacre ends on wordplay

- Where: L155–L163, after Yavren forces her own arm down
- Text:
> "What did you almost do?" Pelka asked.
> "Don't know the verb."
> "Then keep not knowing it. Up."
- Problem: Punchline Patch, Tennis Match, and uniform fluency under pressure. Pelka has just watched her sister nearly turn a living landslide on soldiers, two roofs and a woman dragging a child. The exchange answers with five balanced one-liners and a callback joke. "Don't know the verb" is a writer's joke about the lost-word motif, not something a frightened huntress would say, and it exists to set up Pelka's capper. The first real crack in the sisters' trust reads as banter, so nothing changes between them.
- Fix: Keep "What did you almost do?" and the substance of "Maybe. Not them." Replace "Don't know the verb" with a plain, uncertain answer or with what Yavren felt (the men, the roofs). Cut "Then keep not knowing it." Let Pelka's reply show fear: she repeats herself, grabs Yavren, or goes quiet before ordering the climb. Illustrative only: "Then don't. Yav, don't. Climb."

### D5 · Minor · Opening flight banter runs on matched cappers

- Where: L45–L51 and L61–L67 (goat meadow), L99–L101 (the rope)
- Text:
> "Leave the goat."
> "Already decided."
> "Only what we paid for. Good."
> "Better."
- Problem: Tennis Match and epigram. At first sight of the living armor, four evenly weighted one-liners end on a dry capper. "Only what we paid for. Good." makes the reader work out that the buckle was the only bought item, and "Good" could be a joke or relief that it ignores flesh. Pelka's "Better." grades Yavren's route like a teacher. Staging: the sisters "ran across the slope", yet they watch a buckle soften behind them and a plate thicken "by a hair".
- Fix: Keep "Tell me that's shale." / "It has knees." (a startled joke and a hunter's observation grounded in the flexing joints at L27) and keep "It eats iron". Cut "Already decided."; the running shows it. Replace "Only what we paid for. Good." with a plain reaction to what this means for them (it ignores the meat; it will come for their iron). Cut "Better." or turn it into action (Pelka follows). Have them see the buckle go while close, before they run.

### D6 · Minor · The nest reading is stated twice, then capped

- Where: L177–L199, dusk above the pines
- Text:
> "Its nest," Yavren said. "The bow means the nest."
> "Good. Ask it where to go."
> "It's an eagle."
> "Then watch."
> "The gray cliff has a goat crawl on the far side."
- Problem: The narration has already concluded "Distress.", so Yavren's line repeats it. Pelka's "Good." has nothing behind it for her: she never heard the allegiance reading, so she has no reason for relief. "It's an eagle." / "Then watch." is a correction plus a capper, and it gives Pelka the idea of reading the eagle when the next paragraph credits Yavren's "hunter's eye" (rhetorical ownership: Yavren reads animals, Pelka knows routes). "The gray cliff" is the "limestone fin" of L191 and L231 under a new name.
- Fix: Either cut "Distress." as a narration verdict or trim Yavren's line to what she actually tells Pelka. Give Pelka a reaction she could really have, such as a practical question or impatience, and let Yavren decide to watch the flight. Keep the goat-crawl line and its shared-history detail, but use one name for the rock.

### D7 · Minor · A warning phrased as a manual

- Where: L241, the cleft, as the colony climbs Yavren
- Text:
> "Stop," Yavren said. "It takes raised weapons as threat. Put it down."
- Problem: Exposition. In a moment of physical panic, the reason is phrased as a rule for the reader. The fact matters (the ending depends on it), but the sentence reads like a specification.
- Fix: Keep "Stop", "Put it down" and the fact, but phrase it as a warning about what the colony will do to Pelka.

### D8 · Minor · Stock beat on a strong reply

- Where: L213–L219, before the climb to the hollow
- Text:
> Pelka's face tightened, but she did not argue about the pronoun.
- Problem: "Face tightened" is a stock beat, and the second half of the sentence already carries her reaction.
- Fix: Replace "face tightened" with something only Pelka would do here (a pause while tying off, a glance at the plates), or cut to "She did not argue about the pronoun." Keep "Built when?" / "Before they killed me." exactly as they are.

## Prose findings

### P1 · Minor · Water infrastructure as incidental texture

- Where: the "weir" riders, officer, force and king at L55, L107, L141, L201, L313, L321, L337, L381; also L83, L87, L133
- Text:
> "West horn," she said. "Weir riders."
> "The east gallery. The postern is below the rain cistern."
> descending toward a river gate
> The word for the low drainage channel would not come.
- Problem: Under `universe/style-guide.md` "No default water infrastructure" (CANON), a royal house named for a weir, a rain cistern, a river gate and a drainage channel/soldiers' culvert all count as water infrastructure used as texture. The prompt didn't ask for any of them, and authorized revisions should remove them.
- Fix: Give the western house a non-water emblem and change it consistently at every line listed; the founder's "water-blue" clothing at L73 can stay as a color or change to match. Replace the cistern, river gate and culvert with non-water landmarks from the Queen's memory (a gatehouse, a stair, an old sally path). "The wet cut" can stay as a natural gully. Don't swap in another public-works problem.

### P2 · Minor · Not-X reversals and a triplet fragment at the bow reading

- Where: L171–L179; also L17, L27, L259, L263
- Text:
> Another flash corrected it
> But the bird's head stayed turned toward the cracking ledge.
> Not allegiance. Not recognition of a throne.
> It was not a command waiting in dead metal.
- Problem: The "Not X. Not Y. / Z." template (L177–L179) and the stacked denials at L259–L263 are the collection's reversal tic. At the bow reading they also cause a logic slip: "Another flash corrected it" already gives the nest reading, so the "But" that follows contrasts with nothing.
- Fix: Let the present evidence (the head turned toward the crack, the flattened eaglet) do the correcting, drop one of the two correction steps, and state the distress once. At L259–L263, keep at most one denial.

### P3 · Minor · Coronation paragraph summarizes the prompt's outcome

- Where: L381–L383, the crowning
- Text:
> The three houses that had killed her had lost their thrones, and she had taken their kingdom.
> She would be here for them.
- Problem: The first sentence repeats what the three deaths and the crown already showed. The second is vague ("them" could be the days or the people) and softens the scene just before the shadow turns.
- Fix: Cut the summary sentence. Replace "She would be here for them." with something Yavren sees or does, or cut it.

## Continuity and prompt findings

### C1 · Minor · The shelf's purpose is unclear, so the "Yes" at the turning point is opaque

- Where: L209, L233, L249, L275–L281, the molting cleft
- Text:
> a tunnel called the molting cleft, prepared where old talonmail could loosen its burden without breaking the outer mountain
> "Can you get out of it?"
> "Yes."
- Problem: The reader has to infer that lowering Yavren to the shelf would let the colony molt off her. "Loosen its burden" doesn't say whether the burden is the old plates or the bearer. Pelka's question and Yavren's "Yes." are the story's turning point, since Yavren then keeps the armor, but the reader can't tell whether "Yes" is true, a guess or a lie. The cleft was meant to solve the cracking eyries, and that problem is later solved another way (L315) without comment.
- Fix: Add one concrete clause, in the L209 fragment or in Yavren's L249 order, saying what the shelf does (the colony sheds or releases its bearer there). Then "Yes" reads as true and her refusal as a choice. No new exchange is needed.

### C2 · Minor · The weir officer changes sides off the page

- Where: L313 and L321, from the ridge to the first keep
- Text:
> The weir officer dropped his weapon and ordered his men to follow him off the ridge.
> At the first keep, Pelka brought the weir officer to the gate.
- Problem: He leaves the ridge with his men, then reappears as Pelka's ally petitioning his king. The reader never learns how Pelka reached him or why he turned. His harm-first stance at L109 and L141 makes the turn plausible, but the link is missing.
- Fix: One clause at L321 is enough, e.g. he met them on the road, or Pelka went after him.

### C3 · Minor · Lamp staging in the tent

- Where: L325–L331, the night test
- Text:
> Pelka carried the lamp outside once, leaving only moonlight through the open flap
> She left the lamp unlit and watched until morning.
- Problem: The lamp goes outside with Pelka, then "She" (Yavren, by position) leaves it unlit. The reader has to work out who has the lamp and whether it came back.
- Fix: Name who has the lamp and where it is (Pelka brings it back unlit, or Yavren tells her to leave it outside).

### C4 · Minor · "The buckle" during the roping

- Where: L251, the cleft
- Text:
> Her first pass with the rope missed the buckle.
- Problem: No buckle has been set up on Yavren at this point (the game-strap buckle was eaten at L61), and the colony eats iron, so an exposed buckle under the advancing plates is doubtful.
- Fix: Say what the rope passes through (pack strap, belt loop), or drop the buckle.

## Recurring patterns

- Correction ping-pong used as exposition: L57–L59, L113–L115, L185–L187 (D1, D2, D6). Pelka's grounding corrections at L85 and L91 are motivated and should stay.
- "The warrant" as every enemy's subject: L111, L113, L119 (D2).

- Not-X reversals: L17, L27, L177, L259, L263 (P2).
- Water-infrastructure texture: "weir" on eight lines, plus L83, L87, L133 (P1).
