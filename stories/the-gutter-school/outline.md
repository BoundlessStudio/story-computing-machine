# Outline

## Story

- Premise and central promise: **The Gutter School.** Fifteen-year-old street child Fennit enters an open magic tournament and beats three accomplished opponents through practical spellcraft, observation, and nerve. Deliver satisfying fights against increasing resistance.
- Focal pressure or attachment: The purse would buy months of secure lodging. Fennit also wants to win conspicuously, particularly after being laughed at. Enjoyment of showing off threatens the sensible plan to conserve strength.
- Counterforce or complication: The mages have greater range, stamina, and training. Later opponents watch earlier bouts and remove the easy opportunities. Fennit's experience solves particular problems, not every kind of magic.
- POV, distance, and information limit: First-person past, close to Fennit's immediate calculations, appetites, and mistaken guesses. No retrospective adult wisdom or access to opponents' thoughts.
- Governing movement and time shape: One afternoon; an eight-person elimination bracket means three victories. Expand each bout, especially the final; compress other matches and waiting. Target 2,500–4,000 words.
- Speculative rule or ordinary-world constraint: Victory requires a bare-hand touch on the opponent's red chest patch, kept visible on a standard vest. Spells manipulate light, air, clothing, and props; dangerous attacks on flesh are forbidden. Living marshals supervise and catch falls. Fennit's practiced minor workings lift dust, briefly stick touching materials together with roughly handgrip strength, and loosen accessible stitching by touch. None cancels another person's magic or acts autonomously. Repetition tires the hands; large gusts defeat the small holdfast.
- Dialogue promise: Taunting, tactical bluffing, embarrassment, and competitive curiosity accompany an underdog's visibly earned progress.
- Dialogic medium: Registration talk, shouted challenges, human score calls, clipped exchanges between bouts, and misleading bodily feints.
- Dialogue engine: An early boast advertises Fennit's habits. Opponents answer through changed tactics as well as speech; their questions can extract useful information. The last exchange concerns the terms of another contest, with pride still active.

## Voice

- Narrative texture: Quick attention to hems, footing, reach, loose change, and attractive prizes. Let frightened calculations interrupt grand self-presentation; vary speed around physical decisions.
- Conversational texture: Short practical questions, uneven boasts, ordinary complaints, and occasional unanswered remarks. Competition sometimes leaves no breath for wit.
- Rhetorical ownership: Fennit exaggerates experience and recognizes sales patter. Peltrude punctures excuses with specific observations; she can also enjoy an opponent's embarrassment. Hadrum owns formal sporting courtesy and uses it to put pressure on people. The caller owns only rules and observed scores.
- Pressure behavior: Fennit's fluent pre-bout boasting contracts into fragments. Peltrude becomes blunt after a surprise. Hadrum initially listens for exploitable information, then stops talking when concentration matters.
- Relationship movement: Fennit wants the mages' serious effort without admitting how frightening it is. Peltrude wants an honest account of a trick without becoming a pupil. Hadrum wants to defend his standing and, after losing, obtain a rematch without appearing to plead. Exchanges turn dismissal into particular rivalries.
- Anti-default: Avoid making every adult a gracious instructor who explains the child's worth. Victory need not settle educational policy or produce unanimous affection.

## Beats

1. **Enter.** Fennit spends hard-earned entry money after passing the same control demonstration as everyone else. Establish the scoring patch and legal clothing holds before the draw. Demonstrate minor magic while retrieving a dropped coin from dusty boards and loosening a snagged coat seam, then tying it shut. These are familiar market and lodging tricks. A question about schooling becomes public amusement; Fennit invents the Gutter School affiliation and rashly promises an easy afternoon. The name denotes no actual school or physical setting.

2. **Quarterfinal: find the person.** An unnamed mage in red sleeves surrounds himself with convincing visual doubles. Fennit's small dust-lifting charm exposes which feet disturb the suspended grit. The mage responds competently: drops the illusion and drives the dust away with a gust that nearly gives him the touch. Fennit catches his real sleeve with a brief holdfast between mitten and cloth, turns inside his reaching arm, and touches his patch before the cloth tears free. Make distances and both hands legible. His embarrassment need not become either cruelty or instant admiration.

3. **Semifinal: stop performing.** Peltrude dispenses with loose sleeves and illusions. Controlled air screens prevent Fennit closing; she lowers a screen only to attempt her own score. Repeating the sleeve trick and boasting cost Fennit a bruised shoulder and breath. Fennit studies her attack-and-recovery rhythm instead. A tiny holdfast between soft boot sole and board arrests one retreat sooner than she expects; a low pivot carries Fennit inside her passing hand during the already-established opening. No adhesion withstands her full gust. The winning touch costs a torn sole. Between bouts, her pointed question catches Fennit concealing how tired the hands are; Hadrum is listening.

4. **Final: free the hand.** Hadrum uses small changing gusts, keeps his clothing close, and never repeats Peltrude's rhythm. Fennit cannot beat him by displaying the earlier solutions again. Hadrum controls distance until he can bind Fennit's extended coat sleeve with force and approach for a hand-touch. Fennit lets the trapped sleeve suggest another futile holdfast attempt, then uses the opening's stitching trick to release that sleeve seam. The bare hand slips out and reaches Hadrum's patch before his scoring hand arrives. Establish the reachable seam and exact approach first: the coat is expendable outer clothing, while Fennit's own visible patch remains on the intact vest. The caller awards the observed first touch, not ingenuity points. Hadrum has earned a near-win.

5. **Take the win.** Give Fennit time to enjoy the announced result and actual purse. Peltrude can dispute a boast without disputing the score. Hadrum requests a rematch; Fennit bargains from the unfamiliar position of being worth challenging. Finish in the immediate competitive aftermath, with victory intact and further rivalry available. No scholarship announcement, poverty cure, communal lesson, or closing explanation of the title is needed.
