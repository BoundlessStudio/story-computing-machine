## Verdict

- Dialogue: PASS — every exchange holds up on Ground and changes something between the speakers; there are two over-compressed lines and one echoed riposte at the end, all Minor.
- Prose: PASS — concrete, varied and funny; one repeated undercut sentence and one explanatory clause just before the last line.
- Continuity and prompt: PASS — the laughter, the practical gutter magic, three set-up victories and the purse are all delivered; the fight mechanics are consistent from start to finish.
- Edit scope: polish
- Existing review.md said: PASS; `Dialogue: PASS`. I agree, but it missed the two unclear lines (L121, L189) and the matched pair of closing lines.

This is one of the cleaner dialogue tracks in the collection. People talk little because they are fighting, and most exchanges move a relationship on: from laughter, to a rivalry, to respect, to two mages arguing over who gets the next bout. The speakers differ in stance. Hadrum uses formal courtesy to apply pressure, Peltrude makes blunt observations about bodies and excuses, the caller only gives rules and scores, and Fennit is cocky, hungry and practical. The whole cast does share a dry, clipped register, and many exchanges end on a small capper. The comic underdog tone earns that wit, and the story deflates it under pressure more than once ("I had a reply ready. Then I needed the breath to get up."). The real faults are local: two lines compressed until they depend on staging or knowledge the page never settles, and two "You can…" lines back to back at the very end. The edit pass should fix those spots surgically. It should not roughen the voices or add talk, because more dialogue would slow the fights.

## Dialogue findings

### D1 · Minor · "I saw" has no settled referent

- Where: L119–L123, at the bottom of the steps after the first bout; and L133, after Peltrude's quarterfinal
- Text:
> Peltrude stood at the bottom, rolling her sleeves above her elbows.
> “I saw,” I told her.
> I could see why she had rolled up her sleeves.
- Problem: Ground/referent. "I saw" is past tense and answers no question, so it could mean "I saw you watching my bout," or "I see what those rolled sleeves are for." The second is the useful reading. But at L133 Fennit only now understands why she rolled them, which undercuts that reading and sends the reader back to re-decode L121. "Good." fits any reading, so it doesn't help.
- Fix: Put the moment of understanding in one place. Either make L121 plainly about the sleeves, in present tense, and cut L133, or cut the spoken pair at L121–L123 (her rolling her sleeves in silence already reads as a message) and keep L133. Keep "Good." if the line it answers still fits. Illustrative only: "I see what those are for." No quip.

### D2 · Minor · "You're on your boot" depends on unshown staging

- Where: L181–L191, end of the semifinal, Fennit on the boards laughing
- Text:
> I landed on my backside with one boot sole hanging by its heel.
> “Get up. You're on your boot.”
- Problem: Ground/staging. L181 puts Fennit on his backside with a loose sole. Nothing puts his foot or the flapping sole underneath him, so "You're on your boot" first reads as nonsense, and "I was. I got up carefully" only half decodes it. The intent is good: Peltrude ignores his gloat ("That wasn't nearly") and says something practical. The staging is what fails.
- Fix: Add a clause at L181 that tucks the loose sole, or that foot, under him as he lands, or let Peltrude name the sole in her own blunt words. Keep "Get up." and keep her ignoring the gloat.

### D3 · Minor · Closing lines echo one "You can…" template

- Where: L317–L321, the rematch exchange (same shape also at L137 and L233)
- Text:
> “You can wait. I'm first.”
> “You can draw for it,” I said. “I'm going to eat.”
- Problem: Identical Voices, on a small scale. Peltrude and Fennit both issue dares as "You can…" (L137 "You can try holding this.", L233 "You can come closer.", L317, L321). At the ending the two lines sit back to back with the same "You can X. I'm Y." structure. Fennit's last line therefore sounds like a matched comeback to hers rather than his own choice of food over glory.
- Fix: Cut "You can wait." so Peltrude folds her arms and just claims "I'm first." Her pettiness survives. Leave Fennit's closing line exactly as it is. Leave L137, which the caller's "Clothing only" depends on, and L233.

## Prose findings

### P1 · Minor · Repeated "I hadn't meant" undercut

- Where: L63 (registration laughter), L235 (final, "You can come closer"), L263 (final, both cuffs caught)
- Text:
> That made him laugh harder. I hadn't meant it to.
> He did. I hadn't meant immediately.
> I hadn't meant to give him both arms.
- Problem: The same self-undercutting sentence closes three beats. The first, at L63, is the best and establishes Fennit's accidental comedy. By the third it reads as a template.
- Fix: Keep L63. Rephrase L263 so it states the result in a different construction, for example opening straight on the coat biting under his armpits with both cuffs caught. L235 can stay if L263 changes. Don't swap in an aphorism.

### P2 · Minor · Closing narration names the reversal

- Where: L319, the pause before the last line
- Text:
> I looked at them, these two people who wanted another chance at me, and took my time answering.
- Problem: The appositive tells the reader what the two rematch requests have just shown: laughed at in the morning, sought after now. It is the only place the story states its own arc, and it comes right before the final line.
- Fix: Cut the appositive ("I looked at them and took my time answering."), or replace it with a concrete detail such as his hand on the purse. Keep the pause itself.
