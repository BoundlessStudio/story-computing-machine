# Promised experience

Briefly state the distinctive pleasure, tone, scale, any specified central relationship, and anticipated payoff of the complete request, including later user directions. Ground interpretations in the user's words and distinguish them from explicit requirements. Leave unspecified elements and genuine uncertainty visible; a relationship invented for the plot belongs below.

## Takes considered

Three structurally different stories this request could be, two or three sentences each: a different centre, starting point, place, relationship, or register. Name the scene a reader would turn to first in each. Say which you chose and why its central scene and its use of the request's distinctive pleasure are strongest.

## Provisional direction

Propose the relationship, conflict, limitations, and ending, and identify concrete scenes that could deliver the request through action, choices, and turns. Make clear whose choices control the conflict and climax, what supplies the main tension, and what the ending rewards. Readers will compare those beats with their independent understanding of the request before drafting; the promised-experience paragraph is a claim for them to test. Keep the direction short and open to discovery: the user's requirements remain authoritative, and the prose may find a better route. This is a thinking aid, not a scene quota or a checklist.

## Craft plan

Decisions and reasons, briefly, following `story-create/references/story-development.md`.

- **Teller and voice:** who narrates, from what distance and time, in what temper; the two voices to try in the voice trial.
- **Cast and speech:** the principal people the reader will know, usually two or three, what each wants, how each talks, what each knows and has wrong at the start, and what each wants from the other in the scenes they share (name it for people on the same side too).
- **The conversation owed:** in a plain sentence, what the premise makes the reader expect these people to address and which scene holds it. For a form with no promised conversation, describe how it delivers the relevant encounter or revelation. If someone must give an account of past events: who tells it, why now, and why to this listener.
- **Reference character studies:** for each character from a supplied original or the universe notes: what the source establishes; the temperament it expresses; how they sound; three or four signature details and where each is used in action; anything translated for rating or tone.
- **Setting:** the place chosen, the two alternatives rejected, and why this one presses hardest on the premise. Place sheet: name; season, weather, hours; the work that goes on there; five sensory facts, two of them not visual; one thing broken or odd; what the place makes hard and what it offers.

## Scene briefs

Usually two to four scenes at short-story length. Choose the number for the request and form, giving the consequential scenes room. For each: where and when; who wants what from whom; what each knows that the other does not; what must be said aloud; the turn; the detail to remember; share of the length. Mark the long scene. Then list what is summarized or left out.

## Ending

The final act or image, and the earlier moments that load it.
