# Outline

## Story

- Premise and central promise: During Mallowbreak's launch-day misrule, townspeople use a rope crown and liturgy to mock disgraced shipwright Orselle Vuret as god of sound workmanship. Their observance joins worship and harbor authority to her hidden sea-power. A work command lifts a listing lighter; resolve the displaced storm tide and Orselle's fifty-seven-minute choice between mortality and preserving herself at another coast's expense.
- Focal pressure or attachment: Orselle wants neither office nor forgiveness, only an honest harbor and continued secrecy. Her disgrace is deserved: to protect apprentice Rulik Saal and winter ferry service, she certified a repair without reopening a suspect keel seam. The ferry flooded, injuring crew and ruining cargo. Now her distrusted judgment is indispensable.
- Counterforce or complication: Weather stacks displaced water beyond the harbor mouth while exposed mud strands vessels and weakens pilings. Harbormaster Ossa Keld triages people and boats; rite-keeper Sileth Nemm separates rule from custom; Rulik wants the old failure stated without turning mortality into absolution. Residents retain distinct needs.
- POV, distance, and information limit: Close third-person past through Orselle, who reads joints, loads, and bad sequences but depends on reports beyond her sightline. Memory of the sea-gift surfaces through bodily and ritual triggers. The rite's limits emerge through tests and consequences, never complete exposition.
- Governing movement and time shape: One countdown marked by seven brine-clock drains: public joking, dispersed harbor labor, then choice in the flooding lower yard. People coordinate without forgiving. End with damage tally and a pending hearing, not restored honor.
- Speculative rule or ordinary-world constraint: Eighty-six years earlier, a dying living sea-bearer gave drowning Orselle a local salt-breath that stopped aging and sensed water loads; lacking worship and authority, she could not command water or qualify as a god. Mallowbreak's crown grants one bearer harbor authority for fifty-seven minutes while participants deliberately keep sacred forms, even jokingly. The nonliving crown only focuses their living observance and her power. Within harbor stones, the sea follows Orselle's audible physical directives literally and displaces rather than destroys water. At the final drain, consenting shore-return revokes authority, severs the salt-breath without catch-up aging, and opens a tide-stone bleed through the sacrificial lower yard. Refusal preserves her deathlessness and holds the town dry until low water, but the storm head will strike two inhabited neighboring coves. These rules are local.

## Voice

- Narrative texture: Taut close-third attention follows load paths, water levels, damaged materials, and the cost of an order. Show the countdown through changing work and recurring drains, not announced minutes.
- Conversational texture: Mix clipped coordination, interrupted history, conditional ritual interpretation, tool requests, mishearing, and ordinary complaints about ropes, boots, animals, and boats. Context makes decisive words dangerous.
- Rhetorical ownership: Orselle uses structure and tolerances; Ossa uses people, routes, and triage; Sileth uses clauses and admitted uncertainty; Rulik uses remembered choices and blame. Bystanders speak from immediate needs.
- Pressure behavior: Orselle grows exact and evasive; Ossa confirms receipt; Sileth slows as others rush her; Rulik loses fluency around the certificate but speaks plainly during work. Failed listening changes action or water.
- Anti-default: Avoid polished god-versus-mortality debate, priestly lore delivery, and crowd conversion. Let differentiated talk alter labor and leverage while Orselle's final action remains larger than the closing exchange.

## Beats

1. On the launch apron, show Orselle barred from the yard and residents divided over her disgrace. Volunteers choose her for ridicule, but Sileth preserves authentic forms; Rulik abstains. The clock starts.
2. Orselle instinctively orders a listing lighter held; water supports it. A controlled test defines literal, spatial commands, then crossed shouts during a drydock maneuver drive the entire incoming tide beyond the harbor mouth.
3. Ossa begins evacuation and mooring work while Orselle maps the growing head. Sileth tests ending clauses. Orselle reveals enough of the old sea-gift to explain danger, not its origin.
4. Crews cross exposed mud toward the neglected relief gate. Mix job calls, household requests, and distance errors. Orselle lends brief supernatural support only until human bracing holds; ambiguous speech has material consequences.
5. The gate is seized behind the yard implicated in the old false certificate. Rulik owns his faulty work but rejects Orselle's claim that concealment was necessary. Their practical disagreement identifies a cut that can save lives by sacrificing the lower yard, without reconciliation.
6. As overflow turns toward neighboring coves, Sileth confirms both outcomes and Ossa reports readiness without choosing. Property owners disagree; no vote replaces consent. Orselle orders the cut and release.
7. At the final drain, Orselle performs shore-return while crews carry the sequence. Her command fails as ordinary gates and ropes take the load. The flood ruins stores and her abandoned shop but spares lives and both coasts. Sileth records limits, Ossa tallies damage, Rulik corrects the ferry record, and mortal, still-disgraced Orselle supplies one witnessed repair measurement before her hearing.

## Continuity

- Canon used: Only life uses magic; godhood requires worship, power, and authority; locally granted authority or power may be lost; consent and consequences remain morally real.
- Boundaries and unknowns: Every mechanism is Mallowbreak-specific, unrelated to other crowns, tides, or gods. The first sea-bearer's nature remains unknown. Mortality resumes from Orselle's present body without erasing memory, guilt, relationships, or legal exposure.
