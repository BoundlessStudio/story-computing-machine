# Outline

## Story

- Premise and central promise: Calva Lorn, a shipwright disgraced after hiding a
  miscut keel joint that contributed to a ferry's loss, is selected for the
  town's low-tide fooling: a rush-and-copper crown, mock worship, and fifty-seven
  minutes as “god of the harbor” before ritual immersion restores the victim's
  mortality. Calva angrily tells the jeering sea to stand clear, and the entire
  tide retreats beyond the bar. The old rite has supplied real worship, local
  authority, and power; her pulse, need to breathe, and hand pain stop. Before
  the closing minute,
  she must use an intoxicatingly obedient sea to bring a stranded workboat home
  and decide whether to accept the rite's one known clean exit. Resolve the
  central promise with a difficult no: Calva deliberately misses the immersion,
  choosing continued godhood without receiving forgiveness, a civic mandate,
  or assurance that another route back to mortality exists.
- Intended reader experience: Begin in cruel festival comedy, pivot quickly
  into impossible physical wonder and harbor-scale danger, then let rescue feel
  like a master craftsperson handling a perfect but living material. The final
  pressure should be less “will she save everyone?” than “what does she want
  after usefulness can no longer excuse the choice?” End on exhilaration,
  appetite, and unease together: Calva's refusal is legible and active, not
  endorsed as obviously wise or punished as obviously wicked.
- Generating force:
  - Mode: Plot-led reverse launch under a visible countdown. Calva's customary
    launch commands first empty the harbor, then become the sequence by which
    she lays temporary water beneath a stranded hull and brings it landward.
    Each successful command increases both her competence and her temptation;
    movement continues after the rescue because the remaining minutes strip
    away the claim that permanent godhood is merely necessary emergency work.
  - Focal attachment or contradiction: Calva loves the exact negotiation by
    which shaped timber survives a medium that never obeys it. Her disgrace is
    deserved, but loss of the yard, sensation in two fingers, and other people's
    trust also took the only life in which she knew herself. Divine perception
    lets her feel load, current, and every hull in the basin as one comprehensible
    structure. She wants that state for itself while knowing that perfect
    obedience is the fantasy that helped her conceal an imperfect joint.
  - Plot-led wants and useful limit, if applicable: Immediately, Calva wants to
    undo her retreat command and bring the grounded outer workboat and its
    unnamed adult crew safely in before displaced water returns. She also wants
    to keep her painless, unaging body and new sea-sense, yet wants Pavo Drin to
    see her act without concealment or coercion. Her useful limit is that she
    mistakes being indispensable for being repaired: she reads compliance as
    proof of sound work, reaches for clipped yard imperatives under stress, and
    initially treats Pavo's refusal to absolve her as another load to overcome.
- Counterforce or complication:
  - Source: The sea can move but not disappear; Calva's first order has banked a
    rising volume beyond the bar, left boats and marine life on mud, and made
    every new imperative consequential. The mantle answers bounded physical
    intentions within the tidal basin, not moral abstractions: it cannot repair
    rotten wood, reverse death, decide what is safe, or forgive her. The closing
    rite requires her freely to enter the returning tide at the old immersion
    step before the fifty-seventh minute; force would not complete it.
  - Credible inside logic, if a person: Pavo, Calva's adult former apprentice,
    exposed the hidden joint and has since kept working in the harbor. He wants
    the stranded crew home, the displaced tide released without a destructive
    surge, and no frightened crowd to acquire a permanent god merely because a
    joke became real. He knows Calva's work vocabulary well enough to translate
    her intentions into cautious measurements, but will neither forgive her on
    command nor make the mortality decision for her. When rescue requires her
    power, he asks her to use it; afterward, he admits that wanting her mortal
    also protects him from ever needing her again.
  - Pressure or change created: Calva must replace sweeping orders with small,
    revisable instructions while the clock runs. Pavo's practical cooperation
    gives her neither reconciliation nor a promised mortal future. As mockery
    becomes fearful petition, worship and authority become more—not less—stable,
    so refusing the exit is a credible permanent choice rather than a few extra
    minutes of magic.
- Narrative design:
  - POV, tense, distance, and limitation: Close third-person past through Calva,
    intimate with trained tactile judgment, shame, returning steadiness, and the
    seductive expansion of her bodily map into the harbor. She can sense water
    pressure and contact with hulls but cannot read minds, know the rite's origin,
    or see beyond contiguous local saltwater. Keep Pavo exterior through
    measured corrections, withheld reassurance, and his habit of turning broad
    commands into questions with units.
  - Time shape and compression: One continuous fifty-seven-minute interval plus
    a brief immediate aftereffect. Use a handful of unobtrusive minute markers
    tied to the festival's leaking water clock: crown at 0, retreat at 4, rules
    recognized by 9, reverse launch from roughly 15–39, rescue complete by 43,
    walk toward the immersion step until 50, private confrontation and decision
    before 55, and the missed rite at 57. Dilate the first command, the workboat's
    guided return, and Calva's turn away; compress repeated small corrections.
  - Information and reveal strategy: Show the rite as malicious local custom
    before revealing that its liturgy explicitly grants the crowned person the
    harbor's obedience and the town's bowed attention. Confirm the sea's answer
    immediately. Let Pavo recover the closing instruction from remembered
    festival practice rather than an explanatory expert or authenticated ancient
    manual. Disclose Calva's concealed cut early enough that her disgrace is not
    a twist; reveal during the rescue that Pavo, not an accident inquiry, forced
    her admission. Establish the bodily temptation through absent pulse, painless
    hands, and structural sea-sense before anyone argues about immortality.
  - Governing movement: Endure the mock crown; issue one angry yard command;
    watch the harbor empty; test the boundary with a dangerously broad recall;
    let Pavo narrow it; locate the stranded workboat through pressure against its
    hull; set “blocks” of water beneath it; level, lift, and slide it through a
    temporary channel; account for mud, fish, moorings, and displaced volume;
    bring the crew within the basin; release the banked sea in stages; feel every
    hull settle; refuse the crowd's competing petitions; walk with Pavo toward
    the immersion step; ask what mortality would restore; hear no promise of
    forgiveness or a yard; admit aloud that she wants the mantle; turn down a
    side channel before the step; let the fifty-seventh minute pass; return the
    tide to its ordinary course while remaining the person it obeys.
- Speculative surplus: Words spoken directly over contiguous seawater arrive
  once, faintly but exactly, at every other wet edge in the basin. The effect
  spreads jokes, arguments, and private remarks to distant fishers and makes
  ordinary harbor privacy difficult; an echoed voice has no living agency and
  cannot issue a second divine command. It neither reveals truth nor resolves
  Calva's rescue, disgrace, or choice.
- Structural distinction: A public comic coronation narrows into an inverse
  launch across an exposed working harbor, then sheds the rescued crew and crowd
  for a two-person walk that does not reach the expected ritual venue. The
  choice occurs when Calva turns away before the immersion step, not as a public
  proof, crowd conversion, technical verdict on her old work, institutional
  rewrite, or last-second surrender of power. The fifty-seventh minute confirms
  an already chosen appetite rather than supplying the climax by surprise.
- Decisive turn, deepening, or recognition: With the crew safe and four minutes
  left, Pavo tells Calva that mortality will not reopen her yard, restore his
  trust, or make the lost deckhand less dead. He will help complete the rite
  because unchecked divine authority frightens him, but he refuses to disguise
  that fear as forgiveness. Calva recognizes that she has been waiting for the
  mortal option to come with a redeemed future and that no honest person can
  offer one. She also recognizes that rescue no longer requires her to stay.
  She says plainly that she nevertheless wants the sea-sense, the steady body,
  and time, then turns into a drainage channel that cannot reach the immersion
  step before the clock empties. Pavo does not follow or bless the choice.
- Aftereffect or live uncertainty: At minute fifty-seven Calva feels the crown
  cold and tight against her living skin, and her next heartbeat does not come.
  She can
  restore ordinary tidal motion without surrendering authority, but frightened
  worship persists and the easy ritual exit is gone. Pavo leaves her with no
  repaired relationship. Calva remains locally powerful, ageless while the
  divine conjunction holds, and accountable for every careless instruction;
  whether worship can end, authority can be returned another way, or godhood
  will change her over longer time is unknown.
- Opening / ending relation: Open with townspeople shouting mock commands at a
  disgraced woman while insisting the crown means nothing. End shortly after
  the water clock empties, when the tide has resumed and those voices reach
  Calva faintly across the harbor as petitions she did not solicit. She gives no
  answer yet; the final concrete fact is the continued absence of breath and
  pulse, not a throne, proclamation, reconciled crowd, or emblem of redemption.

## Beats

1. At dead low tide, the town crowns Calva with ritual words everyone treats as
   ridicule. Pavo is present for harbor work, not as supporter. Calva's angry
   “stand clear” empties the basin and stops her pain and pulse.
2. The grounded harbor and rising water beyond the bar prove that obedience is
   physical, bounded, and dangerous. Pavo identifies the fifty-seven-minute
   closing immersion and forces Calva to replace “bring it all back” with a
   measured first correction.
3. Calva senses an outer workboat stranded at a bad angle. She and Pavo cross
   the mud; their command-and-question rhythm reopens the concealed-joint history
   without turning rescue into exoneration.
4. Calva performs a launch backward: water blocks form beneath the hull, lift
   it, and pass it through a narrow moving channel. Small failures—debris,
   frightened fish, a pulled mooring, echoes carrying private speech—keep the
   sea material rather than symbolic.
5. The workboat reaches protected water. Calva stages the banked tide's return
   so hulls refloat without a surge. Her divine perception and painless hands
   make permanent godhood sensually desirable before the moral argument begins.
6. The crowd's jeers become incompatible requests. Calva declines to answer;
   Pavo leads her toward the old immersion step and states his reasons for
   wanting the mantle ended without claiming the right to decide.
7. Away from the crowd, Pavo refuses Calva's hope that mortality would itself
   restore trust or work. Calva admits rescue is complete and staying is now her
   own desire. She takes the drainage channel instead of the ritual path.
8. The fifty-seventh minute passes after the decisive turn. Calva restores the
   ordinary tide but remains its god; Pavo departs, distant petitions travel
   over the water, and her mortal functions do not resume.

## Continuity

- Canon used: Godhood requires continuing worship, power, and authority. The
  town's enacted reverence, its explicit grant of harbor authority, and a local
  mantle supplying sea-command satisfy that conjunction once the miracle makes
  worship sincere. Only living Calva uses the power; the nonliving crown may
  focus or mark the rite but never acts independently. Magic, technology, and
  divinity remain cultural labels for underlying systems, and personhood and
  consent retain moral consequence.
- Boundaries and unknowns: Keep the era and town unnamed and independent of the
  Glass Sea, the unnamed western bay, existing crowns, and every established god
  or claimed divine title. The rite, fifty-seven-minute mortal exit, local
  saltwater range, voice-carrying effect, divine bodily pause, and mantle costs
  are story-specific; they establish no universal rule for worship, authority,
  immortality, consent, resurrection, or transfer. Calva cannot restore the
  dead, erase her culpability, command minds, or infer moral truth. The rite's
  maker, age, earlier activations, and any later way to end Calva's godhood stay
  unresolved.
