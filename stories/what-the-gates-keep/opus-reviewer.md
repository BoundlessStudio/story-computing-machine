# Opus review: The Wrong Rescue

Independent audit for a later editing pass. It is not a `review.md` verdict,
does not change canon state, and does not authorize edits by itself.

## Verdict

- Dialogue: REVISE — the grounding is sound, but two key exchanges end on house-style echo-and-turn retorts, and three speakers share that move.
- Prose: PASS — controlled, concrete first-person narration; one moment where the narration states the lesson (L253) is the only real slip.
- Continuity and prompt: PASS — the required line is verbatim, and the rise and vanishing are staged within the story's own rules; there is one small wobble about where the king was.
- Edit scope: targeted-dialogue
- Existing review.md said: `Verdict: PASS`, `Dialogue: PASS`. I agree on Ground (referents and uptake hold). I disagree that the voices stay distinct at the two hinges below.

The dialogue here is better grounded than the collection's usual. Evasions, unanswered questions and people talking past each other carry the maid exchange. "Is that yours?" and "Where's my maid?" work as misreadings, and Kolvran's bare "Yes." to Haldis is exactly right. The problem is narrower than a mess. Ismeret, Haldis and Kolvran all reach for the same move: take the last speaker's words and turn them back ("four weeks" / "four weeks", "I didn't choose" / "You left that to someone else", "can refuse him" / "Her husband can"). At the raid confrontation and at Ismeret's first doubt of Kolvran, this makes three very different people sound like one author scoring points. The edit pass should keep the few turns the story earns (the supper reveal, "I just did.", the other gate, the courtyard ending) and make the rest plainer. Haldis should be blunt and factual, not clever. Kolvran should be shortened by pain. The narrator should stay the one who reassures, defers and misses the point.

## Dialogue findings

### D1 · Major · Epigram closes the raid confrontation

- Where: L199–L203, Haldis confronts Kolvran over the wool-shed raid
- Text:
> “I didn't choose your husband.”
> “No. You left that to someone else.”
> He did not dispute it.
- Problem: Punchline Patch / Philosopher. Kolvran's line is in character (it is the same precise self-exculpation as "It was. There were houses beside it."). Haldis's reply is the collection's "No. [neat reversal]" signature, and it states the story's theme of evil passed to someone else (the king leaves the beating to the steward). This is the most personal exchange in the story, and it closes on a debating point instead of on her grief. Haldis's verdict had already landed two beats earlier ("Haldis nodded once, as though she had been waiting a long time for exactly that answer"), so the epigram adds polish, not movement.
- Fix: Keep Kolvran's line. Have Haldis refuse the distinction bluntly, or return to the plain fact she keeps carrying, not argue it. A physical beat instead of speech (the axe, a look at Ismeret) would also work. Keep "He did not dispute it." only if her line still makes a claim he could dispute. Illustrative only: "You sent them."

### D2 · Major · Kolvran's polished counter at his weakest moment

- Where: L205–L213, Ismeret asks what else he has not told her
- Text:
> His breathing had quickened. “Not now.”
> “You had four weeks to tell me.”
> “And you had four weeks in which no one entered your room without knocking. If we get out of this, you may ask me anything you like.”
> She drew back from him at that.
- Problem: Uniform fluency under pressure plus correction ping-pong. Kolvran is grey around the mouth, his hand is trembling (L129), and he is "getting faint" (L167). Yet he answers with his longest, most balanced sentence, built by echoing her "four weeks" back as a counter-ledger. This is the hinge where Ismeret first recoils from him and the narrator "seized on it". The reader has to find the cause of her recoil in this one line, and the mirrored rhetoric makes him sound like he is winning a debate instead of a wounded man putting her off. The part worth keeping is the royal permission "you may ask me": it sounds like her father granting leave, and that is a plausible reason for her to draw back.
- Fix: Cut the "four weeks" mirror. Make the reply short and broken by pain, and keep the conditional, leave-granting "you may". Leave "She drew back from him at that." as is. Illustrative only: "Later. You may ask me anything later."

### D3 · Minor · Echo-and-turn retorts used as the default reply

- Where: L75–L77 (the sword drags across his collar); L137–L141 (Kolvran volunteers consent); L221–L223 (the sister offer)
- Text:
> “Watch your sword,” he said.
> Haldis stepped nearer him, axe held low. “Then sit still.”
> “I wasn't asking you.”
> “Ask me, then,” Ismeret said.
> “Your sister can refuse him.”
> “Her husband can. My father gave him half his land.”
- Problem: Tennis Match / correction ping-pong. Each of these is coherent on its own. Together with D1 and D2 they make "pick up the other speaker's word and turn it" the rhythm of the whole hall scene. L137–L141 is a three-line hinge whose only job is to get the narrator to ask for Ismeret's account. "Her husband can." corrects his wording before it answers his point.
- Fix: L139–L141: drop either the snub or "Ask me, then," and let the narrator go straight to "Tell me what happened," or let Ismeret start her account unasked. L223: state the fact without correcting him first. Illustrative only: "Her husband holds half his land from Father." L75–L77 may stay as it is, or Haldis can just step in with the axe and say nothing. Do not touch the turns listed under Preserve.

### D4 · Minor · Pronoun chain across three men

- Where: L215–L223, the sister offer; also L97, the dining-room account
- Text:
> You needn't stay with him. We'll take you to your sister.
> “He'll send for me,” Ismeret said.
> Father said that if I spoke to her, he'd tell him to begin again.
- Problem: Ground/referent. The nearest "him" before "He'll send for me" is Kolvran, but she means her father. Within the next two lines "him" then shifts to the father and then to the sister's husband. At L97, "he'd tell him" asks the reader to sort Father from the steward. Both can be recovered with effort, but each one slows a key beat.
- Fix: Name the father at L219 ("Father will send for me.") and the steward at L97 ("he'd tell the steward to begin again"). No other change is needed.

### D5 · Minor · The mode of Haldis's opener in the final exchange is unmarked

- Where: L301–L309, the courtyard before Haldis rides off
- Text:
> “You'll tell him she asked you to let them go.”
> “She didn't ask me.”
> “Then make sure he knows that.”
> Haldis said, “You'll keep away from him.”
- Problem: Ground/mode. Haldis's line has no beat or tone marker. Earlier she used "You'll ..." as a command (L133), so the reader can take L303 either as a sneering prediction (he will blame the princess) or as a cover story she is offering him. The replies make sense under both readings, but her final stance differs (contempt, or a last kindness he refuses), and this is the final meaningful exchange. The outline's intent (she will report his interference herself) supports the prediction reading.
- Fix: Mark it as a prediction with one word or a small beat before or inside the line. Keep "She didn't ask me." and "Then make sure he knows that." unchanged. Illustrative only: "I suppose you'll tell him she asked you to let them go."

## Prose findings

### P1 · Minor · The narration states the lesson at the decisive touch

- Where: L249–L255, the narrator grips Ismeret's arm
- Text:
> I could not remember when I had last asked whether she wanted me to touch her.
> She looked down at my hand.
- Problem: The Philosopher, in narration. The gesture ("She went rigid", "She looked down at my hand", "Let go") already shows the consent point. The sentence before it ("I could remember every time I had lifted her...") is concrete and earns its place; this one spells out the lesson. It belongs to a light pattern of the narrator explaining his own comprehension (L89, L213, L267), which is mostly fine on its own.
- Fix: Cut this sentence, or reduce it to something he notices rather than a conclusion he draws. Keep the preceding "I could remember every time" sentence and the horse comparison.

## Continuity and prompt findings

### C1 · Minor · Where the king was

- Where: L91, the narrator argues for the king
- Text:
> “Your father rode out himself when he heard you'd been taken,” I said.
> He couldn't sit through his supper the night we left.
- Problem: "Rode out himself", followed by "The northern companies are on the road", suggests the king went north with the army. But he was at supper in the palace the night the heroes left, roughly nine days later, and the narrator remembers him at home holding the torn sleeve during the search (L153). This can be reconciled (a short ride to the orchard), but a reader may snag on it. The supper detail must stay, because Ismeret's reveal turns on it.
- Fix: Make the ride local, e.g. "rode out to the orchard himself", or cut "himself". Keep the supper sentence exactly.

- Haldis's boot paragraph (L181), and Kolvran's bare admission "Yes." to the raid.

> “You'd be standing at another gate when the order came.”
- The stand: "You know what he did," / "Yes." Then the required whisper, and the escape staged by the rules (arm lifted, balance found, hand offered).
- The courtyard ending, including the narrator's arc from "That was what I was told" to this line, and Haldis glancing back and riding on.

## Suggested edit order

1. D1: fix the raid confrontation first; it is the story's most personal exchange and the clearest instance of the house register.
2. D2: shorten Kolvran at the hinge where Ismeret first recoils; keep "you may".
3. D5: mark Haldis's final opener as a prediction so the ending carries one clear stance.
4. D4: name the father and the steward; two word-level edits.
5. D3: sweep the remaining retorts and "Then ..." openers, checking the Preserve list before changing any turn.
6. P1 and C1: trim the consent sentence and make the king's ride local.
