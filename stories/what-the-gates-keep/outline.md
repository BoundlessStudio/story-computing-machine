# Outline

## Story

- Premise and central promise: **The Wrong Rescue.** Two heroes defeat the Demon King, then discover that the princess they have recovered would rather leave with him. Her accusation makes their victory impossible to possess together.
- Focal pressure or attachment: The unnamed knight once guarded the palace gates and taught Princess Ismeret to ride. He confuses having known her childhood with knowing what safety means to her now.
- Counterforce or complication: His companion Haldis lost her husband to Kolvran's border raiders. Ismeret sought Kolvran's sanctuary from her father's household; her suffering cannot undo Haldis's loss. Kolvran wants survival and Ismeret's allegiance, not forgiveness.
- POV, distance, and information limit: First-person past through the knight, close to action. He misreads the princess's preparations. Neither her account nor Kolvran's resolves every disputed event.
- Governing movement and time shape: One evening: victory, an extended encounter in the ruined upper hall, then separation outside. Expand the failed homecoming; compress the preceding quest. Target 2,500–4,000 words. Stop once incompatible next actions become irreversible.
- Speculative rule or ordinary-world constraint: Living Kolvran can carry himself and one touching person to a visible spot across the valley, but must stand balanced with both hands free of weapons. Wounding his leg and keeping him under a blade defeats him. Ismeret's support restores his stance only after the knight relinquishes his advantage. No time travel or persistent portal.
- Dialogue promise: Affectionate recognition becomes a bitter argument over who gets to complete a rescue, while an old companion refuses to let the rescued woman's suffering erase her own.
- Dialogic medium: Face-to-face speech, a familiar riding gesture, withheld touch, interrupted questions, and the required whispered message.
- Dialogue engine: The knight's offers presume a return. Ismeret exposes his earlier role in enforcing obedience; Haldis redirects explanation toward the captive enemy. Speech changes whom each hero will restrain.

## Voice

- Narrative texture: A tired rider notices weight, footing, harness, and familiar movements before motives. Recollections interrupt abrupt decisions; no retrospective lecture.
- Conversational texture: Begin with breathless practical concern and misplaced familiarity. Let questions go unanswered when another body moves. Keep tenderness, impatience, and accusation unevenly distributed.
- Rhetorical ownership: Ismeret owns the prepared indictment. Haldis has dates, a husband's belongings, and contempt for convenient distinctions. The knight overuses reassurance. Kolvran's courtliness conceals impatience; admitting his raid does not borrow Ismeret's argument.
- Pressure behavior: The knight repeats offers; Haldis becomes more exact; Ismeret stops explaining when explanation delays departure. Pain shortens Kolvran's speech without making him meek.
- Relationship movement: The knight wants his pupil's trust; she wants him to release authority he considers loving. Haldis wants his support for a killing. Kolvran needs Ismeret's help without submitting to the heroes. Changed knowledge divides commitments.
- Anti-default: Do not turn four incompatible loyalties into a shared diagnosis, negotiated truce, or mutual permission to heal.

## Beats

1. Open on earned victory. The heroes have driven Kolvran away from the broken window, wounded him, and brought him down. Establish his escape faculty through their tactics. Ismeret emerges from an adjoining room with boots laced and a travel bundle. The knight reads these as preparations for rescue.
2. His offered hand repeats their old riding-lesson gesture. She asks after the attendant punished for carrying her private letters. The knight returned that woman to the steward after she reached his gate, believing a promised royal review. She remains imprisoned. Ismeret witnessed punishment continuing within her father's hearing. Use a particular remembered room and interruption, not an atrocity catalog.
3. Ismeret arranged her departure with Kolvran; the court called it abduction. Her attachment includes being believed and his willingness to face her father's army. Haldis identifies her husband's death in his earlier raid. Kolvran acknowledges commanding it and refuses to disown his soldiers for survival. Ismeret must hear this exchange rather than acquire impossible prior knowledge.
4. Haldis prepares the execution. Ismeret intervenes; the knight tries to move her aside like a child near a frightened horse. Her resistance forces his decision. He lowers his sword and blocks Haldis's weapon, costing their partnership. Protecting Ismeret's choice does not acquit Kolvran.
5. The heroes stare as Ismeret helps the defeated Demon King stand. Address both heroes at intimate range: “Tell the king it’s amazing how he protects the kingdom when threatened by an outside evil. But ignores the evil occurring in front of him.” She whispers; then Kolvran takes her offered hand, finds the visible hillside, and they vanish together. Keep the spatial sequence feasible and the departure unequivocal.
6. Outside, Haldis takes her horse and proof of victory, intending to report the knight's interference and pursue Kolvran. He will return separately with Ismeret's message. Let their lost cooperation register in departure logistics; neither needs the other's final understanding.
