# The Dress They Brought Her

The guard had crushed the cigarette's filter when he slipped it through the meal hatch, but he had given her more than half. She sat on the edge of the cell's bolted bed and made it last. One drag, a wait while the ember considered going out, another drag. The ventilation pulled the smoke toward the door and then changed its mind.

In the steel mirror above the sink, her dark hair looked as if somebody had searched it and found nothing. It had been messy before the cell and was messier now. Her eyes were the same dull grey under every light. Her skin was covered in tattoos chosen over years: the black leaves climbing her left hand, the blue match on one finger, the bird with three crooked wings along her ribs, the old bright shapes crowding both arms. Piercings caught the ceiling glare at her brow, nose, lip, ears, and in glints all over her body. Some were metal, some clear prison retainers, all noted on the intake map as circles and numbers.

She had bummed the cigarette off the guard by looking at his breast pocket, then at him, until he sighed through his nose. Neither of them had spoiled the arrangement with words.

Footsteps came down the range. The guard appeared first, his shoulders held differently. Behind him walked a man in a charcoal suit that had been brushed clean except at the cuffs, where pale dust remained in the weave. He carried a flat white case. No prison insignia. No tie pin. A black credential rested against his shirt with its face turned inward.

The door opened. The suited man entered her cell as if entering a room he had reserved.

He set the case on her bed, released two silver catches, and lifted out a sparkly pink dress. The skirt had enough layers to stand up by itself. Tiny hard stars had been sewn along the hem. Beneath it lay a pink wand with a silver handle and a five-pointed head, the sort of object that ought to have been either priceless or sold beside birthday candles.

He put the dress into her arms. Then he placed the wand across it, physically careful, like a medic settling a splint.

"The other magical girls are dead, you are the last one."

The guard watched the cigarette. The suited man watched her face.

She drew in smoke until the paper warmed her fingers. Then she let it out over the glitter.

Nothing in the dress stirred. Nothing in the wand answered. The star at its tip was molded around a seam; one point bore a shallow scrape. She rolled the handle against her palm. No hidden weight shifted. No warmth entered her skin. The dress smelled of new plastic, sealed cloth, and the faint burnt odor that had got into everything since the western sky went white.

The suited man took a tablet from inside his jacket. Its first card showed a plain numeral above a thin grey line.

`era-local ultrahuman count: 1`

Below that were two empty fields.

`IDENTITY: UNAVAILABLE`

`LOCATION: UNAVAILABLE`

He touched the screen. A separate card slid over the count: a packet number, an amber recovery seal, and her booking portrait. In the photograph her hair had been wet from the intake shower. Her expression had been the one she saved for cameras owned by other people.

The man drew his finger from the count of one to her face.

She looked at the join between the cards. A narrow dark line remained there. The number knew no name. The packet claimed it did.

He opened the packet.

Most of its pages were thumbnails with heat-blackened edges. Pink crests. Dense columns of old print. A uniform diagram had lost everything except one white glove and the curve of a skirt. Another page displayed a row of little crown shapes, each crossed through in red. Whatever the packet had once known, fire and water had eaten the useful parts first.

At its end sat a clean white human outline stippled with marks.

Her marks.

The bird at her ribs had been reduced to three dots and a code. The leaves on her hand were a shaded patch. Rings and bars became numbered circles. Beside the outline appeared her booking portrait and the number `P-80417`.

The suited man enlarged the match result. Green brackets settled around the tattoos visible above her prison shirt. A percentage climbed. It was high enough to make his shoulders loosen.

She pinched the dress fabric between finger and thumb. The outer layer rasped. One star snagged against the blue match tattoo. She freed it without haste.

Another gesture brought up a transfer route. Cell. Intake. Exterior recovery gate. Vehicle court. The last two boxes lay beyond the prison hold. An authorization mark was already green; there was no request for her consent and no place for a signature. The unit had not come to ask whether she was somebody. It had come to collect the somebody its records had made.

For a moment she saw only the exterior gate.

The battle had broken half the cameras on the wall and pulled staff into twelve-hour shifts. Beyond the vehicle court were roads with dark traffic lights, abandoned checkpoints, houses standing open after evacuation. Recovery custody would still be custody. The man would still be watching. But it was movement, and movement sometimes developed gaps.

She could put on the dress. She could let their mistake carry her past concrete and wire. Later, if later arrived, she could become difficult in a place with windows.

The wand remained dead in her hand. The tablet's green brackets held the ink on her skin as though strangers had put it there for this purpose.

She dragged the packet thumbnails sideways with one fingernail.

The man allowed it. He mistook examination for recognition.

At the left edge of the body map, nearly cropped away, was a black-and-white measuring strip. She knew the thickness of those bars. She had stood against them barefoot while an intake camera flashed. In one corner was the triangular shadow of a property-bag seal. The paper was not some secret portrait rescued from a magical archive. It was the first sheet of her prison booking.

She tapped `P-80417`, then tapped the same number on the band around her wrist.

The man nodded too quickly. Of course the number matched. That was his proof.

She swiped upward. A source strip appeared: five burned packet pages scanned at the evacuation station, a thin black rectangle labelled unreadable, then the body map one minute later. The strip grouped all seven pages beneath the recovery seal. At the far end, almost hidden by the portrait card, a property-bag icon waited behind a lock.

She touched the icon.

The tablet asked for internal property verification.

She pointed down the range, toward intake.

The guard's gaze moved from her finger to the bundle in her lap. The suited man tapped the transfer route. A new green check appeared beside personal effects. He thought she wanted her belongings before she left.

Good enough.

The guard held out his palm for the cigarette. She took one last drag, pressed the ember dead against the steel lip of the sink, and gave him the butt. He wrapped it in a scrap of foil from his pocket. His eyes rested on the wand, then on her. The favor between them had become something he might have to explain.

They took her out without normal waist restraints. The suited man's credential had changed the rules faster than anything she had ever said could have done. She carried the dress folded over one arm, badly, its stiff pink skirt knocking against her thigh. The wand lay along her forearm. Glitter collected on her prison shirt.

The man walked half a pace ahead. At every security door he presented the tablet before the guard could reach his own badge. Cellblock grey gave way to the sour cream paint of intake. Emergency cables ran along the floor under black rubber mats. Through a wired window at the final turn, she could see a slice of outdoor evening and the high lamps of the vehicle court.

She slowed at the window.

The suited man looked back. He showed her the route again, tapped the green exterior box, then pointed ahead. Certainty had made him generous with the future.

She shifted the dress higher and followed.

The property counter stood behind mesh and thick glass. Nobody occupied the chair on the other side. The night system worked by itself: a shallow pass-through drawer, a receipt scanner, and a belt that brought sealed bags from the caged shelves. To the right, the recovery transfer reader made a doorway of blue light. Beyond it, an exterior gate waited open by the width of a hand.

The guard scanned her wrist. Somewhere behind the wall, machinery clacked into motion. A clear bag slid down the belt and struck the drawer.

`P-80417` showed through the plastic.

Inside were the metal pieces intake had made her exchange for clear retainers, a black cord, three coins, a comb missing two teeth, and the clothes she had arrived in. A folded carbon receipt sat in a sleeve on the outside of the bag. Her thumbprint smudged one corner purple. Across its top, half hidden by the fold, ran `NEW BOOKING — PROPERTY P-80417` and the same minute shown beneath the packet's final page.

The blue transfer reader brightened.

The suited man put his palm toward it. The tablet displayed the next step: live identity anchor. Once she crossed that light, a fresh scan of the body that matched the intake map would replace the packet's damaged proof. The system would stop depending on a bad page boundary. It would have her, now, confirming itself.

Outside air touched the wet corner of her eye through the narrow-open gate. It smelled of hot pavement and rain that had not yet fallen.

She set the dress on the counter.

The man took that for readiness. He lifted the wand from her arm and offered it back point-first toward the blue doorway.

She took it by the silver handle but turned away from the light. She jerked the carbon receipt from its sleeve and shoved the property drawer closed, hard enough to make the guard's hand jump.

The counter flashed a warning. She flattened the receipt beneath its red beam. Purple thumbprint. New-booking header. `P-80417`.

Her custody record filled the display. The booking portrait appeared beside the body map, but this time the header was plain: prison intake, property opened, property sealed. No crown shapes. No pink crest. No survivor claim.

Before either man moved, she hooked the star of the wand through the strap on the suited man's tablet. She pulled its lower edge across the counter and under the red beam.

The guard drove her forearm down against the laminate. Pain struck from wrist to elbow. The wand clattered onto the counter. The suited man caught his tablet with both hands.

The scanner had already read the packet code.

Both records appeared side by side. Between them, `P-80417` burned red.

The counter disliked an outside file claiming an internal property number. It opened the raw sequence without being asked. The display simplified what the recovery tablet had dressed in seals and green brackets.

Five burned pages.

One black, unreadable separator.

One prison body map.

The times beneath them differed by sixty-three seconds. Above the body map, the counter restored a clipped line from its own copy:

`NEW BOOKING — PROPERTY P-80417`

Below the black rectangle, the recovery system had supplied its conclusion:

`PREVIOUS PACKET CONTINUES`

The suited man stopped pulling on the tablet.

She kept her cheek near the counter where the guard's hold had put it. From there the answer was almost offensively clear. Charred pink paper ended. Her white intake sheet began. The missing separator had let one file swallow the next, and the first thing it swallowed was her body.

The guard looked to the suited man.

The suited man raised one hand. The pressure left her arm.

He enlarged the raw sequence, not the match percentage. His eyes moved once from the sixty-three-second gap to the restored booking line, then to the carbon receipt still under her fingers. He photographed the three together with the tablet's rear lens. He did it again after moving the dress so its glitter did not cover the number.

The blue transfer reader pulsed. Its light divided the floor at her toes. Past it, the exterior gate opened another hand's width with a hydraulic sigh.

The route remained authorized. No alarm had cancelled it. The suited man had not yet touched the hold control. If she stepped forward, the live reader would take the tattoos, the piercings, the dull grey eyes, every measurement the prison had made, and fasten them to the burned packet. It would also take her through the exterior gate.

He looked from the evidence to her. Doubt had entered his face, but so had urgency. Somewhere inside his system there was still one anonymous danger, and he had arrived prepared to leave with a person.

She could give him one.

The night beyond the gate was close enough to smell.

She stepped backward.

The reader's light fell away from her body. She gathered the dress with both hands, trapping the wand in its pink folds, and pushed the bundle against the suited man's chest. He had to take it or let it fall. He took it.

Then she scanned the carbon receipt again, alone.

Her prison record returned. `P-80417`. Custody unchanged. External identity blank.

She turned the suited man's tablet screen-up on the counter but kept its code beyond the beam. With one finger she drew a boundary in the source strip: burned page, burned page, burned page, burned page, burned page; unreadable separator; new booking. She stopped at the black rectangle.

The man watched the sequence. After a long moment, he moved the boundary to the same place on his own screen.

The body map slid out of the recovery packet. Her portrait went with it. The packet collapsed to five damaged pages and an amber field where a person had been.

`IDENTITY: UNASSIGNED`

He opened the grey count card beneath it.

`era-local ultrahuman count: 1`

The number did not flicker. No face appeared. No map opened. Whatever the count counted, it had never needed her record and offered no direction toward anyone else.

The suited man saved the raw scan. He touched the hold control on the transfer route.

The exterior gate closed.

He tucked the wand fully inside the dress so that only one silver point showed, then carried the pink bundle under his arm. It looked less like a relic that way and more like something returned to the wrong shop. At the corridor door he paused. His gaze dropped to the red mark rising along her forearm where the guard had pinned it. Then he turned his credential outward for the first time and used it to cancel his own extraction.

The guard resealed her property bag. He returned the carbon receipt to it, keeping her number with her things. When the belt had carried the bag back into the cage, he gestured toward the cellblock.

She went with him.

At her cell, he opened the door and waited until she was inside. Before closing it, he took the foil from his pocket. The crushed half cigarette had gone out but had not broken. He held it between two fingers, coaxed the ember back with his lighter, and set it on the steel sink lip.

The lock engaged. The suited man's footsteps receded with the faint rasp of stiff pink fabric.

She sat on the bolted bed. Glitter remained in the black leaves on her hand. She picked it out piece by piece, then reached for the cigarette. The smoke went toward the door, reversed, and spread thin against the ceiling.
