# Canon brief

## Hard constraints

- The prompt contract controls production: a demon must appear before an adventuring party and say “Hello lover!”; the party must initially assume the bard is addressed; the cleric must identify the demon as their ex; the past relationship must materially drive the conflict; and both the immediate danger and the unfinished relationship must receive satisfying resolutions. The story must not treat the bard’s sexuality as the joke, equate infernal nature with irredeemable personhood, resolve the central conflict through an unearned reunion or consequence-free killing, or make either former partner a minor or the past relationship nonconsensual (`stories/clerics-infernal-ex/00-prompt.md` — `## Story controls`, `## Assumptions`, `## Completion tests`).

- The story belongs somewhere in the one physical world that persists through deep time. A new fantasy region and era are permissible, but the story must not declare a separate universal world or cosmology merely because characters use terms such as demon, infernal realm, heavens, hells, or divine plane (`universe/premise.md` — `## The deep-time shared world`; `universe/locations.md` — `## The world across eras`).

- Magic, technology, and divinity are culturally chosen labels for real underlying systems, not objective universal categories. “Demon,” “cleric,” “divine magic,” and “infernal magic” may be locally meaningful vocabulary, but they do not automatically establish a universal species taxonomy, class system, power source, moral alignment, or shared origin (`universe/rules.md` — `## System categories`; `universe/glossary.md` — `## Magic, technology, and divinity`).

- Only life can use magic. If the demon, cleric, deity, familiar, portal operator, or other agent uses magic, that user must be alive; a nonliving symbol, holy object, contract, weapon, seal, gate, or summoning circle may store, focus, transmit, or shape living-supplied magic but cannot independently be the magic user unless its life and resulting personhood are established (`universe/rules.md` — `## Living agency and magic`).

- Godhood, if asserted objectively rather than used only as a culture’s title or belief, requires the continuing conjunction of worship, power, and authority. A cleric’s patron, church, oath, or spellcasting does not by itself prove objective godhood, and no universal divine hierarchy or divine government exists across all eras (`universe/rules.md` — `## Divinity`; `universe/factions.md` — `## Under-Surface Keeper belief`; `universe/glossary.md` — `## Goddess of Time (claimed title)`).

- A universal underlying afterlife exists, but its manifestations and cultural descriptions vary. A new infernal locality may be invented, but it must not automatically be identified with Ruth’s hell-labeled train destination, Cinder Annex, the Morrow kept-place mechanism, Mae’s tea window, or any other continuation system (`universe/rules.md` — `## Afterlife and continuation`, `## Ruth's station service and transfer mechanism`; `universe/locations.md` — `## Ruth's local afterlife station`, `## Cinder Annex`).

- Canon does not establish that infernal identity, damnation, or a `HELL` label proves evil, guilt, deserved punishment, or irredeemability. Belphegor is a loving living archdemon; Talvi is a loving person locally described as a damned soul whose damnation’s cause, justice, mechanism, and meaning remain unresolved; Sabine’s `HELL` assignment is expressly not proven just or infallible (`universe/characters.md` — `## Belphegor`, `## Talvi`, `## Sabine Kroll`; `stories/the-friends-i-built/05-story.md` — `# The Friends I Built`; `stories/the-station-between/05-story.md` — `# The Station Between`).

- Costs, desire, consent, contracts, faith, guilt, love, and sacrifice have no single universal metaphysical behavior. Any such effect on clerical or infernal power must be defined as part of this local mechanism and applied consistently. Regardless of metaphysics, consent and the ethical consequences of the former relationship remain materially real (`universe/rules.md` — `## Costs, desire, and local mechanisms`; `universe/style-guide.md` — `## Personhood and moral consequence`).

- Names are ordinarily symbolic and social, not automatically magical. A demon’s name, a cleric’s invocation, a patron’s epithet, or the word “lover” cannot bind, summon, reveal, or authenticate anyone unless the story establishes a specific bounded local mechanism (`universe/rules.md` — `## Names and identity binding`).

- Any magical arrival, summoning, banishment, teleportation, planar passage, or infernal gate must have story-local rules. Portal capabilities are system-specific, and resemblance to another gate or crossing does not establish shared origin or operation (`universe/rules.md` — `## Portals and crossing systems`, `## Artifacts, motifs, and folklore`; `universe/style-guide.md` — `## Continuity and evidence discipline`).

- Both former partners must retain personhood, agency, and morally consequential choices. An infernal, divine, contractual, or technical explanation cannot reduce either person or their relationship to a malfunction, alignment label, possession, or predestined role (`universe/style-guide.md` — `## Personhood and moral consequence`).

- The story must remain Teen/PG-13 and broadly accessible: romance and adult intimacy may be present, but sexual content fades to black; danger and violence remain non-graphic and consequence-centered (`stories/clerics-infernal-ex/00-prompt.md` — `## Story controls`; `universe/style-guide.md` — `## Audience and content boundaries`).

## Useful established context

- The shared world permits many unrelated magical cultures and wide tonal variation. A self-contained romantic-comedy fantasy adventure in an unnamed era is compatible with canon and requires neither a crossover nor deep-time exposition (`universe/premise.md` — `## The deep-time shared world`, `## Knowledge and connection`; `universe/style-guide.md` — `## Tonal range, scale, and connection`).

- Belphegor is the only admitted canon character explicitly called an archdemon. They are alive, have four hands and two pairs of eyes, supply their own bounded green-fire magic, love their partner Talvi and child Irix, and are extremely inert. `Archdemon` is expressly a local title that establishes no godhood, portfolio, hierarchy, folklore, or authority over other beings (`universe/characters.md` — `## Belphegor`; `universe/rules.md` — `## Irix household and Cinder Annex mechanisms`; `stories/the-friends-i-built/05-story.md` — `# The Friends I Built`).

- Talvi is Belphegor’s partner and is locally described as a damned soul. Their affectionate partnership establishes that an archdemon and a damned person can have an ordinary, reciprocal family relationship in one locality. It does not establish demon biology, universal infernal romance customs, the mechanics of damnation, or a rule connecting demons to all dead people (`universe/characters.md` — `## Talvi`, `## Belphegor`).

- Cinder Annex is one secondary school in an infernal locality culturally associated with the underlying afterlife. Whether `Hell` there denotes a place, region, institution, cultural description, or something else remains unresolved. The school is not universal infernal geography and has no established relationship to Ruth’s station or another afterlife mechanism (`universe/locations.md` — `## Cinder Annex`; `universe/factions.md` — `## Cinder Annex exhibition and access practice`; `universe/timeline.md` — `## Undated Cinder Annex exhibition incident`).

- Ruth’s station uses culturally intelligible `HEAVEN` and `HELL` labels, but canon deliberately withholds the destinations’ contents, moral accuracy, assignment standard, builder, and relationship to other afterlife experiences. This is a guardrail against treating the new demon or infernal danger as proof of cosmic guilt (`universe/locations.md` — `## Ruth's local afterlife station`; `universe/rules.md` — `## Ruth's station service and transfer mechanism`; `stories/the-station-between/05-story.md` — `# The Station Between`).

- Canon contains local priests, sacred rites, religious beliefs, and culturally divine titles, but no universal cleric class. The Rite of Deliverance, Ninth Hollow practice, Under-Surface Keeper belief, and Founding Vigil are distinct local institutions and do not prescribe the new cleric’s vows, powers, patron, clothing, hierarchy, or moral code (`universe/factions.md` — `## Rite of Deliverance`, `## Ninth Hollow consent practice`, `## Under-Surface Keeper belief`, `## Founding Vigil religion`; `universe/rules.md` — `## Divinity`).

- Canon contains no established bard class, adventuring-party structure, alignment system, demon-hunting order, ex-partner taboo, or universal rule that clerics oppose demons. Those familiar fantasy forms may be used as local genre vocabulary without importing an external game ruleset.

## Conflicts and ambiguity

- **No authoritative conflict requires a ruling.** The prompt can be satisfied within the existing shared-universe framework.

- **Potential contradiction:** claiming that all demons are evil, damned, dead, divine, soulless, native to one universal Hell, subject to Belphegor, or natural enemies of every cleric would exceed current authority and may contradict the personhood and locality boundaries around Belphegor, Talvi, Cinder Annex, and Ruth’s station.

- **Potential contradiction:** identifying the new infernal location with Ruth’s `HELL` destination or Cinder Annex without an explicit, evidence-backed crossover would violate the established nonconnection and evidence rules.

- **Potential contradiction:** treating a nonliving holy symbol, pact, portal, or weapon as an independent magic user would violate the living-agency rule unless the story establishes that entity as alive and accepts its personhood.

- **Omission, not contradiction:** canon does not define `demon`, `archdemon`, infernal biology, infernal society, demon mortality, summoning, banishment, possession, contracts, true names, souls, or relationships with mortals. These may be invented locally.

- **Omission, not contradiction:** canon does not define the cleric’s religion, patron, order, doctrine, powers, vows, healing, exorcism, holy symbols, or relationship to demons. The cleric need not receive magic from an objectively real god.

- **Omission, not contradiction:** canon does not define bardic magic, bardic sexuality, adventuring-party professions, party governance, or why this specific group assumes the bard is being addressed.

- **Omission, not contradiction:** the verb “appears” does not select teleportation, summoning, illusion, disguise, projection, planar travel, or ordinary dramatic entrance. The story must choose and establish its local cause.

- **Omission, not contradiction:** no authority establishes the former relationship’s duration, breakup, present feelings, power imbalance, promises, debts, shared secrets, or source of unfinished business.

## Safe local invention space

- Create a new fantasy region and era within the shared physical world without assigning it to Ravel, the modern-like era, the Glass-Sea era, Cinder Annex, or Ruth’s station.

- Define one local meaning of `demon` and `infernal`, including appearance, culture, powers, social expectations, and moral diversity. Keep claims limited to this person, community, or system.

- Define a bounded arrival or crossing mechanism with a living user or supplier, nonliving foci if useful, concrete limits, and observable failure behavior.

- Create a local religion, clerical order, patron tradition, or philosophy. The cleric may use magic or may be clerical without supernatural power. If objective godhood matters, establish worship, power, and authority; otherwise frame deity language as local belief or title.

- Invent the party’s professions and interpersonal shorthand. The bard misunderstanding may arise from this bard’s established flirtatious reputation, previous boasts, recognizable romantic history, or another character-specific cause. It must not rely on sexuality as the punchline.

- Invent an adult consensual former relationship whose breakup produced concrete unresolved stakes: an unfulfilled promise, disputed artifact, dangerous pact, shared responsibility, concealed truth, political obligation, or incompatible duty. These are options, not canon.

- Let both exes be funny, dangerous, hurt, competent, and capable of refusal. A reconciliation, renewed romance, friendship, boundary-setting separation, or accountable final goodbye may work if earned; reunion is not required.

- Link the immediate threat causally to the relationship history so emotional disclosure and adventure action affect one another rather than forming separate plots.

- Use holy symbols, contracts, gates, instruments, weapons, or relics as nonliving foci for living-supplied power. If any such entity is alive or person-like, preserve its agency and moral status.

- Introduce local infernal or clerical terminology, but do not reuse `Cinder Annex`, `Aptitudes Exhibition`, `Rite of Deliverance`, `Ninth Hollow`, `Keeper Below`, `Calling the Name`, or another established term for an unrelated mechanism.

- Keep Belphegor, Talvi, Irix, Cinder Annex, Sabine, Ruth, and Hester out of the story unless a later explicit production decision accepts the continuity burden of a crossover.
