# Story plan

## Premise and promise

While dismantling a smuggler's crossing rig in a ruined roadside sanctuary, cleric Emet Sarn unknowingly supplies the living power that lets his adult demon ex, Lacrixa, cross into the room. She greets him with the prompt-required “Hello lover!”; Emet's companions turn as one toward Bosk, their theatrically sociable bard, before Emet admits, “Actually, my ex is referring to me.” The comic exposure becomes an adventure problem immediately: the stolen half of the exes' old courtesy hinge has been built into a freight-sized breach that is feeding on power both former partners deliberately supplied for different reasons. They must work together, disclose why their consensual relationship ended, and revoke a form of access neither properly closed before the rig tears open two inhabited places.

The story promises romantic-comedy timing without making Bosk's sexuality the joke, a materially dangerous infernal arrival, competent party action, and an emotionally honest ending in which the exes take responsibility and separate without rekindling the relationship.

## Story controls

- **Length:** Target 3,300–3,600 words; acceptable range 2,500–4,000.
- **POV and tense:** Close third person past, fixed on Emet. Do not enter Lacrixa's, Bosk's, or Udren's thoughts.
- **Genre and tone:** Character-driven fantasy adventure; brisk embarrassment and dry situational comedy opening into heat, structural danger, and sincere but unsentimental closure.
- **Rating:** Teen / PG-13. Adult former intimacy may be recalled through domestic or affectionate detail, with no explicit sexual content. Injuries are painful and consequential but not graphic.
- **Prompt fulfillment:** Preserve the words “Hello lover!”; show everyone looking at Bosk; have Emet plainly identify Lacrixa as his ex. The former relationship causes the opening, determines the mechanics, changes the tactics, and enables both resolutions.
- **Ending contract:** The breach is safely destroyed, no one is killed, and the two populated endpoints are protected. Emet apologizes without asking for absolution; Lacrixa accepts the practical closure but does not resume the romance. They destroy their respective hinge leaves and say a final goodbye.

## Story-specific style profile

- **Dominant voice and selection:** Clear, wry, materially attentive prose filtered through a shelter cleric who instinctively inventories exits, injuries, loads, and who is exposed to harm. Emet notices hinges, thresholds, breath, hand placement, structural strain, and small failures of hospitality before decorative grandeur. His embarrassment appears as over-precise triage and attempts to restore procedure, not as generalized fluster.
- **Narrative distance:** Stay close enough for Emet's defensive classifications and omissions to color the narration. Move closer during the opening recognition, the admission that he lied about his oath, and the final withdrawal of access. Pull back slightly to compress past travel and repeated anchor work. Do not explain an emotion after a physical choice or evasive line has made it legible.
- **Dialogue pressure:** Conversation is operational action. Emet tries to separate the magical emergency from the relationship; Lacrixa insists they are the same mechanism; Udren demands information that keeps the party alive; Bosk first protects group ease, then supplies rhythm and disciplined listening. Important exchanges may be interrupted by the rig's pulses rather than reaching polished conclusions.
- **Image sources:** Door hardware, warped brass, load paths, kiln heat, breath on metal, old shelter routines, and the contrast between a deliberately opened door and a forced breach. Use the hinge/door field selectively at setup, reversal, choice, and ending; avoid turning every feeling into a threshold metaphor.
- **Action texture:** Preserve spatial causality: who supplies power, which brace is being removed, what obstruction follows, and what the result costs. The strange should be felt as hot metal, sucked air, scorched cloth, ringing teeth, exhausted muscles, and changing pressure before it is explained.
- **Humor and warmth:** Let the opening glance and old practical familiarity do most of the comic work. Bosk's joke may fail and then stop. Warmth comes through remembered competence and care, not a sentimental reunion. Do not make the narrator clever at every beat.
- **Intentional emphasis:** The prompt permits a sharper comic opening than the shared default, but the scene must pivot into danger within a few paragraphs so the ex is a person and causal partner, not a reveal-shaped gag.

## Character engine and arc

### Emet Sarn — cleric and viewpoint

- **Age/history:** Thirty-three now; twenty-eight when he and Lacrixa began a consensual two-year relationship between independent adults. Neither supervised, owned, employed, or magically bound the other.
- **Immediate want:** Disarm the stolen rig, protect the road settlement, and keep his composed clerical identity intact in front of his party.
- **Deeper need:** Admit that he used his vocation as a respectable cover for fear, ended the relationship dishonestly, and kept the hinge leaf because private ambiguity felt easier than accountable closure.
- **Pressure and false belief:** He believes competent care can compensate for withheld personal truth. The rig makes the withheld fact operationally dangerous and forces him to trust the people he was trying to protect from it.
- **Arc:** Concealment and procedural control -> defensive partial truth -> unqualified admission -> accepts that apology does not purchase reunion or forgiveness -> publicly chooses honest separation and fuller trust with his party.
- **Dialogue profile:** Usually seeks compliance and calm through orderly questions, complete clauses, and shelter-work vocabulary: exits, loads, permission, symptoms, next actions. He leaves desire and fear vague, especially with Lacrixa, and becomes more formal when ashamed. Resistance makes him explain procedure until that fails; then he goes abruptly, almost painfully direct. With Bosk and Udren he normally sounds dry and collegial; with Lacrixa old shorthand shortens his sentences before he catches himself. He would not naturally deliver a polished therapeutic diagnosis of their relationship or claim his oath chose for him after the truth is exposed.

### Lacrixa — living demon, ex-partner, and co-rescuer

- **Age/history:** Thirty-six by local reckoning, with the same adult capacity and independence she had throughout the relationship. She is a living, embodied person from one community called demons in the Cauterized Reach; the label carries no automatic moral status.
- **Immediate want:** Stop the stolen hinge from opening her inhabited kiln court to traffickers, determine what Emet knowingly allowed, and return home before the breach shears.
- **Deeper need:** Obtain truthful accountability without mistaking old attraction or crisis intimacy for repair; choose a clean ending she controls.
- **Pressure:** The rig exposes her neighbors while putting her in reach of the person whose formal breakup letter denied the real reason he left.
- **Arc:** Weaponized theatrical greeting and command -> suspicion that Emet retained access deliberately -> exact accusation -> accepts cooperation after a direct apology -> trusts his final withdrawal while keeping the boundary closed.
- **Dialogue profile:** Seeks leverage and accurate information; begins with ceremonial mock-politeness, pointed endearments, and exact kiln/crossing terms. Her humor is a blade used to seize pace, not constant banter. She refuses to plead or translate her people into moral categories. When Emet retreats into procedure, she first becomes more technically exact, then abandons ornament for blunt personal statements. With the other party members she is brisk but respectful once they act competently. Under final pressure, commands replace jokes. She would not naturally speak for all demons, excuse the dangerous rig because love is powerful, or offer forgiveness merely to soothe Emet.

### Bosk — bard and rhythm keeper

- **Immediate want/arc:** Restore party ease after being wrongly assumed the addressee, then help solve the mechanism without becoming the joke or the center. He shifts from reflexive stage humor to reliable timing and gives Emet room to speak plainly.
- **Dialogue profile:** Quick, concrete, performance-shaped phrasing; he uses audience, entrance, tempo, and cue language. The party's glance comes from three prior jobs on which acquaintances greeted him with similarly grand flourishes, not from his sexuality or a promiscuity stereotype. When humor receives no uptake, he stops, asks what beat is needed, and follows counts exactly. He would not inventory conquests, flirt with Lacrixa during danger, or turn Emet's relationship into material after seeing the hurt.

### Udren — shield fighter and tactical anchor

- **Immediate want/arc:** Get decision-grade truth and keep everyone alive. She begins irritated that Emet withheld relevant history, then distinguishes justified mistrust of his concealment from prejudice against Lacrixa.
- **Dialogue profile:** Short questions, physical nouns, and load-bearing comparisons; she tolerates embarrassment but not missing tactical facts. Her default is command and narrowed choices. If stonewalling continues, she stops arguing and assigns concrete tasks that require disclosure. She never treats “demon” as a verdict or gives a moral sermon; restored trust appears through where she puts her shield and whose timing she follows.

## Relationship history and emotional logic

- Emet met Lacrixa while the House of Common Shelter maintained a temporary refuge near one of the Cauterized Reach's local road endpoints. They were twenty-eight and thirty-one. Courtship, travel, intimacy, and the decision to key a courtesy hinge were explicit and consensual; neither one's work depended on the other.
- A courtesy hinge was meant for mutually invited visits, not ownership. Each kept one inert brass leaf and each visit required fresh living participation from both sides.
- When Emet was offered a permanent shelter circuit far away, Lacrixa asked him to choose a shared home or say plainly that he did not want one. Afraid of both choices, he ended the relationship by letter and falsely implied that his clerical oath forbade continuing. Lacrixa replied once in anger and ceased contact. She kept her leaf locked away as evidence and unfinished grief, not as continuing romantic consent.
- Emet also kept his leaf. He told himself it was safer in trained hands, but did not de-key it or disclose it to his order or party. A fence stole it with other brasswork days before the story. The party captured the fence and followed the components to the ruined sanctuary; Emet recognized the leaf only after arriving and concealed that recognition for several minutes.
- Lacrixa deliberately answers the abnormal opening because her leaf signals a forced freight expansion toward her home. Her participation is emergency consent to inspect and cross, not consent to resume the relationship. The rig's coercive design is the fence's wrongdoing; neither the token nor the word “lover” creates consent.
- The final repair is not romance. Emet owns the lie, Lacrixa states its consequence, and both revoke the route. Their past love remains real without entitling either to a future together.

## Bounded local crossing mechanics

1. **Nature and endpoints:** A courtesy hinge is a pair of nonliving brass leaves made for two designated living bearers and normally seated in prepared hearthframes. It folds distance between those two physical sites in this same world; it is not a universal portal, an afterlife route, or a connection to any established gate.
2. **Access and living supply:** A leaf holds no agency and uses no magic independently. At both ends, the keyed living bearers must deliberately press the metal and feed breath, body heat, and trained intent. The leaves only focus and align that supplied power. Emet knowingly sends his living ward-power into the stolen leaf to identify and neutralize the rig, while Lacrixa knowingly answers the emergency signal from her leaf. Neither knows the scaffold will redirect their committed charge.
3. **Normal sequence:** Mutual supply opens a hand-wide sight-and-speech window. Continued assent widens it into a two-way person passage for one traveler or eighty heartbeats, whichever comes first. Either bearer can normally withdraw before crossing; after a traveler is inside the passage, both maintain it until that traveler clears.
4. **Range and relocation:** The paired leaves determine the two endpoints regardless of ordinary distance. Moving a leaf moves its endpoint only if that leaf is mounted in a new frame and its bearer supplies a fresh opening. Thus theft alone cannot open a road.
5. **The sabotage:** The fence bolted Emet's stolen leaf into an iron freight scaffold with three nonliving borrower loops and charged salt-glass reservoirs. The loops cannot originate magic or invent assent; after both exes supply the first opening, they store and repeatedly reshape that already supplied charge, falsely widening a one-person visit into a freight breach.
6. **Failure signs and stakes:** Each loop releases one visible pressure pulse: brass reddens, air reverses, and the opening shows mismatched rooms drawn from the bearers' intended endpoints. At the third pulse, the breach will join the ruined sanctuary to Lacrixa's occupied kiln court at freight width, venting lethal heat downslope toward the road settlement and pulling people and masonry at both ends into the shear.
7. **Interruption and safe exit:** Smashing the leaf or withdrawing one bearer while a body is in the throat can crush, burn, or kill that traveler. Safe shutdown requires clearing the passage, physically removing all three loops, discharging their stored charge into inert stone, and having both bearers intentionally withdraw future access at the same count. The holders' deliberate choices provide the magic; Bosk's beat and Udren's labor merely coordinate and remove nonliving foci.
8. **Cost:** Each pulse drains heat, breath, and muscular control from Emet and Lacrixa. Emet's ward-light weakens; Lacrixa's skin cools and her coordination fails. The cost limits them during the climax and cannot be erased by declaration or affection.

## Causal arc

Because Emet hid that the stolen brass was his still-keyed hinge leaf, he powers the fence's rig without warning the party. Because Lacrixa detects a forced opening threatening her home, she answers and crosses. Their combined living charge activates the three borrower loops, so a crude banishment would endanger both endpoints. Removing the loops requires the party's skills and the exes' intimate technical knowledge, but the rig begins following their conflicting memories. That exposure forces Emet to tell the truth about the breakup and both to choose a safe, final revocation. Their explicit withdrawal collapses the breach; the same choice ends the unfinished access without pretending the relationship can or should resume.

## Scene execution beats and dialogue tactics

### 1. The wrong bard

- Open with Emet's hand already on the stolen brass leaf in the ruined sanctuary. He recognizes the file mark he and Lacrixa made years ago, says only that the rig is “familiar,” and feeds a measured living ward through it to find the trap's edge.
- Heat inks an upright seam. Lacrixa steps out bodily—horns, scorched traveling coat, and all—looks straight past Bosk to Emet, and exclaims, “Hello lover!” Everyone looks at Bosk because flamboyant greetings from his wide network have complicated three earlier jobs. Bosk's surprise, not his sexuality, is the comic target.
- Emet corrects them: “Actually, my ex is referring to me.” Use the prompt reversal cleanly, then let Udren's shield come up as the scaffold clamps and the doorway turns red.
- **Incompatible aims:** Lacrixa wants to identify who opened a stolen road and seize the room's pace; Emet wants to reduce her to a tactical arrival and defer the personal disclosure; Bosk wants to recover social footing; Udren wants an immediate threat classification. **Tactic change:** Lacrixa's theatrical taunt becomes a technical warning when the loop closes; Bosk drops his attempted joke when no one laughs; Udren replaces questions with a defensive assignment.
- **Scene turn:** The first pulse seals Lacrixa on Emet's side and begins pulling air toward the opening. She states that a forced closure now could kill people at both ends.

### 2. The breach is theirs

- Lacrixa tests the frame with her own heat and identifies three borrower loops. Emet reaches for a severing prayer; she physically stops short of touching him but makes him inspect the body already half-formed in the opening: a kiln worker's silhouette retreating on the far side. He lowers his hand.
- Establish that Emet's ward and Lacrixa's embercraft are their own living contributions. His stamped shelter medallion and her brass leaf are inert foci. Neither a saint, oath, name, nor the structure acts independently.
- Lacrixa asks why his private leaf is in a fence's scaffold. Emet answers the safe parts: it was stolen, the party caught the fence, they followed the rig. He omits that he kept it keyed and recognized it before powering the frame.
- **Incompatible aims:** Lacrixa wants full provenance before entrusting him with the sequence; Emet wants enough technical information to act without exposing the relationship; Udren wants a yes-or-no answer about whether he is compromised. **Tactic change:** Emet's procedural explanation becomes a bargain—truth after the first loop—when Lacrixa refuses to give him the cut order; Udren accepts the temporary bargain but makes its deadline explicit.
- **Scene turn:** A pulse shows Lacrixa's crowded kiln court, cracks the sanctuary arch, and fixes the external stakes. They accept a joint plan because no one can solve either half alone.

### 3. Old timing

- Spatial route: first loop high on the north jamb, second under the fractured floor, third behind the opening. Udren braces the arch and removes iron pins; Bosk gives a steady, nonmagical drumbeat so the exes can discharge on the same count; Emet and Lacrixa hold opposite edges and feed only enough life-power to keep the throat clear.
- Their bodies remember practical coordination—when to breathe, which hand yields—creating intimacy without implying romantic destiny. Emet recalls small adult domestic details from their two consensual years, then returns to the immediate load.
- Removing the first loop sends stored power into prepared foundation stone as planned. At the second, the rig uses their divergent intended endpoints and shows the room they once shared rather than either current site. Lacrixa sees a distinctive repair on Emet's leaf and understands it remained keyed after the breakup.
- A collapsing slab isolates Emet and Lacrixa in a side alcove while Bosk and Udren keep the second loop from reseating.
- **Incompatible aims:** Emet wants silent coordination and no new disclosure; Lacrixa wants confirmation that he preserved access; Bosk wants an exact tempo while Udren wants permission to cut. **Tactic change:** Lacrixa stops using old shorthand and asks the blunt question; Emet first answers with hand signals, then silence, which functions as confirmation. Bosk abandons commentary for counted beats; Udren shifts from waiting for Emet to following Lacrixa's technical call.
- **Scene turn:** Lacrixa refuses to trust the final withdrawal until Emet tells the truth, because deliberate access cannot be revoked safely through another evasion.

### 4. What the oath did not demand

- In the alcove, maintain physical pressure: ash wind, reddening brass, Emet's shaking hand, Lacrixa's cooling skin, and limited counts before the next pulse. Do not pause the adventure for a complete relationship hearing.
- Emet admits his permanent circuit did not forbid their relationship. He feared leaving the House, feared asking her to follow, and used the oath in his letter so he could appear dutiful rather than indecisive. He also admits keeping the keyed leaf and hiding recognition from the party.
- Lacrixa states the concrete harm: she spent years believing an institution had judged her unfit and prepared arguments against a prohibition that never existed. She kept her leaf locked because destroying it alone risked an unsafe half-key, but she could have requested neutral de-keying sooner; pride and anger let the ambiguity stand.
- Emet apologizes for the lie and present concealment without adding a request. Lacrixa does not certify forgiveness. She distinguishes the love they had from permission they do not have now.
- **Incompatible aims:** Lacrixa wants an unvarnished reason before risking her home on his intent; Emet wants cooperation without surrendering his competent self-image. **Tactic change:** His careful chronology fails, so he names fear and choice directly. Her accusatory precision softens only into a practical offer: she will trust one synchronized revocation, not restart the relationship.
- **Scene turn:** They choose separate present endpoints—Emet the people beside him, Lacrixa her kiln court—and agree to withdraw access on Bosk's count after she crosses.

### 5. The party gets the truth

- Rejoin Bosk and Udren. Emet gives the minimum complete tactical account immediately: the leaf is his, he kept it keyed, the third loop is behind the throat, and both holders must revoke together. He does not ask them to understand before acting.
- Bosk anchors the eighty-heartbeat limit with a plain beat. Udren assigns herself the final pin and moves her shield to protect Lacrixa from falling stone, demonstrating that distrust attaches to Emet's omission rather than Lacrixa's nature.
- **Incompatible aims:** Udren wants every remaining risk disclosed before committing; Emet wants to preserve seconds; Bosk wants a single usable sequence instead of overlapping argument; Lacrixa wants the party to accept her exit timing. **Tactic change:** Udren compresses her interrogation into three closed questions; Emet answers each without qualification; Bosk repeats the agreed count once and then begins.
- **Scene turn:** The plan becomes collective rather than an exes-only ritual, and the third pulse starts early.

### 6. Separate doors

- The opening expands. Hot wind from the kiln court meets cold sanctuary air; roof tiles and tools begin sliding. Emet and Lacrixa spend their remaining power holding a person-wide clear throat. Bosk's beat becomes difficult to hear; Udren advances behind her shield to the final pin.
- The final borrower loop jams. Lacrixa uses her cooling hands to deform its hot clasp while Emet redirects one last ward into the foundation. Udren then drives the inert pin free. Bosk changes from drum to shouted count when the drum is torn away—the practical tactic change prevents a missed withdrawal.
- Lacrixa must cross before shutdown. Emet's fear makes him keep supplying after the agreed beat because he cannot yet see both her feet clear; this begins recharging the open leaf. Lacrixa, already across, orders him to trust her count. He stops treating continued access as care and withdraws.
- From opposite ends, each deliberately revokes future invitation. Emet bends his drained brass leaf against the stone; Lacrixa melts the keyed edge of hers with the last of her own embercraft. The objects do not act. The emptied reservoirs discharge into the foundation and the breach folds without cutting a body.
- Emet and Lacrixa have only a narrowing sight-window for goodbye. He does not ask whether they will meet again. She does not use “lover” a second time. They acknowledge the goodbye and let the window close.
- **Incompatible aims:** Emet wants visual certainty before releasing; Lacrixa needs simultaneous withdrawal before the rig recharges; Udren needs Emet off the frame; Bosk needs everyone on one count. **Tactic change:** Lacrixa abandons technical explanation for a direct command; Emet changes from protective overholding to chosen trust; Udren stops pulling the pin and physically grounds Emet until he releases; Bosk changes instruments but not tempo.
- **Scene turn:** Physical danger and unresolved access end through the same accountable action. Costs remain: burns, exhaustion, a damaged sanctuary, and evidence for the local authorities.

### 7. An ordinary doorway

- At first light, the road settlement is safe and Lacrixa's kiln court no longer visible or reachable. Bosk wraps burned fingertips; Udren binds Emet's strained wrist. No injury disappears through easy healing.
- Emet gives his companions the fuller account without blaming oath, demonhood, or magic. Udren postpones judgment of his field reliability until after they deliver evidence; Bosk declines an opening for a joke and asks only whether Emet can walk.
- **Incompatible aims:** Emet wants to confess without soliciting comfort; Udren wants accountability without delaying the mission; Bosk wants to restore humane ease without trivializing what happened. **Tactic change:** Emet stops overexplaining and accepts Udren's concrete consequence; Bosk substitutes practical help for humor.
- End as they leave the two ruined, nonmatching leaves fused into the dead scaffold for evidence. Emet follows his companions through the sanctuary's ordinary doorway, which Udren has propped open to the cool morning. Do not append a thematic summary or reunion hint.

## Stakes, reversals, and payoffs

- **Personal stakes:** Emet's credibility with his party; Lacrixa's right to safe boundaries; whether their real past can be acknowledged without renewing it.
- **Immediate physical stakes:** Four people in a failing stone structure, inhabitants of the kiln court, and residents downslope threatened by heat, suction, and collapse.
- **Moral stakes:** Whether Emet repeats concealment in the name of protection; whether a demon is treated as a person and expert rather than a category or problem; whether emergency cooperation is mistaken for romantic consent.
- **Reversal 1:** The apparent comic social interruption is a dangerous crossing failure.
- **Reversal 2:** Lacrixa has not invaded Emet's mission; Emet's retained, stolen leaf helped expose her home.
- **Midpoint revelation:** The rig displays their former room, proving that the unresolved personal key is shaping the physical breach.
- **Final reversal:** Emet's instinct to hold the passage “for safety” begins to reopen it; safety requires letting Lacrixa's agency, count, and goodbye stand.
- **Opening payoff:** “Hello lover!” seizes attention and exposes history; its non-repetition at goodbye marks the changed relationship without explaining the motif.
- **Bosk-glance payoff:** His stage reputation motivates the assumption; his choice to stop performing and hold the beat proves the story is not mocking his sexuality.
- **Leaf payoff:** Emet's hidden token first creates danger, then becomes evidence when he willingly destroys his access.
- **Ordinary-door payoff:** The magical forced breach is gone; the party exits through a mundane doorway held open by present trust and labor.

## Canon constraints

- Treat this as one undated, geographically unconnected fantasy locality in the single persistent physical world. Do not name Earth, Ravel, the Glass-Sea era, a Galactic Cycle, or any established place.
- “Demon” is a local term applicable to Lacrixa and her living community in the Cauterized Reach. Do not claim demons are dead, damned, evil, divine, soulless, one universal species, or subjects of an established infernal figure.
- The Cauterized Reach is a distant physical region reached by this one roadcraft system. It is not Ruth's `HELL` destination, Cinder Annex, an objective universal Hell, or an afterlife manifestation.
- The House of Common Shelter is one local service-and-faith institution. Its clerics train living ward practice; no objective deity, universal cleric class, divine hierarchy, or universal opposition to demons is established.
- Every consequential magical act has a living supplier: Emet, Lacrixa, or both. Brass leaves, medallion, frame, loops, salt-glass, and foundation stones are nonliving foci, reservoirs, conductors, or sinks only.
- Names and “lover” have no magical force. The leaves recognize their keyed bearers through a bounded material-and-intent design established in this story.
- Consent is action-specific. Past consensual intimacy, the original voluntary keying, emergency participation, crossing access, forgiveness, and renewed romance are separate questions. None implies another.
- Do not import mechanics, terms, or history from the Glass Gate, Queen Street seam, Ruth's station, Cinder Annex, the Rite of Deliverance, Ninth Hollow, the Keeper Below, or Calling the Name.
- Keep violence non-graphic and consequences visible. Do not solve the breach by killing Lacrixa or the captured fence, and do not make infernal identity evidence of blame.
- All inventions below remain proposed local story material unless separately promoted; the plan itself establishes no canon.

## Proposed local inventions

- **Emet Sarn, Lacrixa, Bosk, and Udren:** four new adult characters with no crossover or implied relationship to existing identities.
- **Cauterized Reach:** one distant hot physical region and Lacrixa's home. Its inhabitants' local use of “demon” establishes no universal taxonomy, alignment, afterlife status, or infernal hierarchy.
- **House of Common Shelter:** one local service-and-faith institution whose clerics use their own living power to shape protective wards through inert medallions. Its beliefs and offices apply nowhere else by default.
