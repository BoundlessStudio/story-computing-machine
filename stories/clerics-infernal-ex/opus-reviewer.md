# Opus review: Hello, Lover

Independent audit for a later editing pass. It is not a `review.md` verdict,
does not change canon state, and does not authorize edits by itself.

## Verdict

- Dialogue: REVISE — the confrontation turns into a spoken fault ledger, the decisive beats use the brief's own nouns, and three speakers share one dry quip voice.
- Prose: PASS — concrete, well-paced and tactile; only minor wording ambiguities at the climax and some repeated narration of Udren's restraint.
- Continuity and prompt: REVISE — every prompt element is delivered, but the injured grinder is in two places at once and the rig's presence in the sanctuary wall is never explained.
- Edit scope: targeted-dialogue
- Existing review.md said: Verdict PASS; "Dialogue: PASS", "Internal: PASS"; disagree on both: the dialogue has three Major findings and the grinder contradiction is a Blocking internal-continuity error.

In terms of action, the dialogue mostly works. The work calls, the "Blue-bowl turn" slip, Udren's sole-by-sole grounding and Bosk's count after losing his drum all change something, and the reader can follow them. The failures are in register and in how the story settles fault. Udren, Bosk and Lacrixa all joke in the same dry, uncontracted style, and each joke ends the beat, so the rom-com timing sounds like the narrator's rather than any one character's. The three decisive beats are the confession, the refusal and Udren's ruling. In each one, a character says the production brief's wording out loud. Just before them, the confrontation after the cloth is exposed turns into an itemized division of blame. The edit pass should keep the physical cooperation and the plot. It should make the confession and the refusal sound like two people who once shared a room, give the barbed humor to Lacrixa alone, and loosen Bosk and Udren. It should also fix the two continuity gaps without adding any hinge rules.

## Dialogue findings

### D1 · Major · Confrontation becomes a fault ledger

- Where: L131–L139, after Lacrixa sees the gray breviary cloth and cracks the tile
- Text:
> “Pride. Mine. But you told me its twin was ash.”
> “Were you about to tell me I kept mine too?”
> Emet rubbed his thumb across the iron.
- Problem: This is the Philosopher pattern, and the reply order breaks (Ground/uptake). Her tile line already shows her pride in her own words. "Pride. Mine. But…" then labels her share of the fault and his, like a verdict: it is the prompt's asymmetry clause spoken aloud. Next she asks whether Emet was about to raise her leaf. She has already answered that charge, and Emet has given no cue (he has only said "I know."). The question exists to set up his silent thumb and her "Of course.", so the reader has to work backward to see that Emet meant to deflect.
- Fix: Keep "I left my leaf where everyone in my court could see it. It was dead. I wasn't tearing out good tile because you left." and "you told me its twin was ash". Cut "Pride. Mine." as a label. Then do one of two things. The first option is to cut L135–L137 and the "Of course." that answers them, going straight from "I know." to "Before I put my hand into the middle of this thing, I want the rest." The second option keeps Emet's deflection: make it visible and put it first. His eyes go to her leaf above the tile border, and her tile line answers that look. Illustrative only: "Yes, it's still up. I left it where everyone in my court could see it."

### D2 · Major · Brief language at the decisive beats

- Where: L143 (the admission), L235 (the refusal), L259–L261 (Udren's ruling in the coda)
- Text:
> “The House of Common Shelter never forbade our relationship.”
> “There is no reunion,” Lacrixa said.
> “Until field trust is rebuilt,” she said. “No threshold watch alone. No choosing our routes. Do you accept that?”
- Problem: At the story's three decisive beats, characters say the production brief's own phrases: "never forbade their relationship", "refusal of reunion", "field trust". Emet has already answered "No". He then repeats it as a statement for the record, giving the institution's full name to a woman who knows it, while his burned hand slips on the iron. "There is no reunion" puts into an abstract noun what Emet never said, since he was cut off at "Or we could—". The refusal is right, but the wording sounds like a ruling. Udren's line repeats what L259's narration has just shown (his name struck from solo threshold watch and route decisions) and then asks for formal assent. This is the house-voice legalism the collection is known for, and it lands exactly where the story needs sincerity. The required line at L241 already carries the theme, so these nearby lines should be plainer.
- Fix: L143: keep "No," and the slipping hand. Cut the second sentence or say it in Emet's own words. The circuit fact and "I chose the road." / "I said they chose for me." already complete the admission, and the prompt requires it in substance, not word for word. L235: keep the refusal immediate and unambiguous, cutting across his proposal to remount the leaves, but make it concrete. Illustrative only: "No. I'm not mounting anything of yours again. Do you understand me?" L261: the ledger already shows the consequence, so let Udren ask only whether he accepts it, in her blunt style, or cut her restatement of the terms. Keep "Yes." and the rewrapped bandage.

### D3 · Major · Three speakers share one dry quip voice

- Where: L13–L15 opening banter; L33, L53, L95 Lacrixa; L81 Bosk (see D4); dialogue throughout
- Text:
> “One more hard shove in the morning,” Udren said, “and we will have a door-shaped hole.”
> “I believe this is called preventive maintenance.”
> “That is the sound of a corner preparing to embarrass a cleric.”
- Problem: Identical Voices. Udren, Bosk and Lacrixa all joke the same way: dry, uncontracted understatement that ends the beat. Cover the tags and Bosk's "It is comforting when disasters can be counted." (L81) swaps freely with Lacrixa's L95; Udren's opening quip could be Bosk's. In 85 quoted dialogue segments there are three contractions (L51, L117, L131), and Udren never uses one. Bosk is a bard, but his one characterizing joke is a modern management phrase delivered like a clerk's. The romantic-comedy timing therefore belongs to the narrator, and Bosk has no personality beyond the count.
- Fix: Only Lacrixa keeps the barbed, formal wit. Keep L33 ("the wrong bad decision"), L53 and L95; they are aimed at Emet and grounded in her kiln knowledge. Change the opening L13–L15 so it no longer builds joke on joke: Udren is practical and plain, or doesn't joke at all, and Bosk's reply is loose, warm and his own (the wet drum is his material). Cut "preventive maintenance". Sweep Bosk's, Udren's and Emet's lines for natural contractions. Leave Lacrixa's lines and the required verbatim lines as written.

### D4 · Minor · Bosk's count joke exists to be corrected

- Where: L81–L85, first evacuation count
- Text:
> “Three loops,” he said. “Seventeen guests. It is comforting when disasters can be counted.”
> “You are on twelve,” Udren told him.
> “Less comforting. Twelve.”
- Problem: This is a Punchline Patch that becomes correction ping-pong. Bosk's aphorism is there to be corrected, and then Udren, who gave Bosk the count at L61, corrects his number. The reader can't tell who is actually keeping the count, and the exchange exists for "Less comforting."
- Fix: Cut the aphorism and the correction. Bosk is first heard already counting ("Twelve." then "Thirteen." as the carter ducks through), with at most one loose aside in his own voice (see D3). Keep "Thirteen." and the drum.

### D5 · Minor · "Then ask for it" referent

- Where: L169–L173, Udren grounding Emet before the middle loop
- Text:
> Udren's voice stayed low. “Next lift yours?”
> “Then ask for it.”
- Problem: Ground/referent. "Next lift yours?" could mean "are you doing it?" or "is it your call?". "It" in "ask for it" points at the lift, but what Emet must ask for is Lacrixa's help. The reader only understands when he turns and asks at L175. This is the beat where Udren grounds him without taking his choice, so it should be clear on first reading.
- Fix: Keep the clipped field style but make one word concrete. Illustrative only: "Then ask her." Keep "Mine." and the grounding ritual before it unchanged.

## Prose findings

### P1 · Minor · Narration keeps certifying Udren's restraint

- Where: L189, L227, L265 (L155 is the first instance and should stay)
- Text:
> Udren held the shield behind him without pushing.
> She did not increase the pressure of the shield.
> wrapped his palm again without pulling the knot too tight.
- Problem: After "Udren came in behind him but did not seize him." (L155) and "My shield is here if you want it." (L165), the narration keeps noting what Udren did not do. Each note tells the reader again that the support is non-coercive. The prompt asked for less narrated explanation of the consent/access theme.
- Fix: Keep L155 and L165. Cut L189's "without pushing" and L227's sentence, or replace them with a positive physical detail (her stance, her breath, the shield's weight). In L265, end at "wrapped his palm again"; the rewrap already shows the care.

### P2 · Minor · Ambiguous wording at the climax

- Where: L203 and L211, the grinder caught in the inner loop
- Text:
> Udren set her shield against his back and braced one boot against the altar step.
> Emet's burned palm opened against it.
- Problem: "His back" comes right after "the man's body", so it can read as the grinder's back. "Palm opened" can read as Emet's grip opening, just before "He held." Later, at L237, "His hands did not open." uses "open" to mean letting go, which reinforces the misreading.
- Fix: Change "his back" to "Emet's back". Replace "opened" with wording that clearly means the burned skin splitting or blistering while the grip holds.

### P3 · Minor · Loose beats at two key lines

- Where: L151 (the confession), L247 (the goodbye)
- Text:
> “I chose the road.” His breath would not finish.
> Her jaw worked once.
- Problem: "His breath would not finish" says he can't go on, and then he says another sentence. "Her jaw worked once" is a stock beat, and it is the last view of Lacrixa's face before the leaves break.
- Fix: At L151, use a beat that shows the strain without contradicting the speech that follows (a pause, the iron slipping), or cut it. At L247, use a physical detail in Lacrixa's own terms (heat, clay, the hammer in her hand), or nothing.

## Continuity and prompt findings

### C1 · Blocking · The grinder is in two places

- Where: L225 and L253, after "Both feet clear." and in the final view through the crossing
- Text:
> Behind her, the grinder was carried out beneath the court arch. The old room stood empty except for broken bowls, her mounted leaf, and the wreck of Bosk's drum.
> Lacrixa standing amid broken glaze, one hand on the shoulder of the injured grinder.
- Problem: This is an internal continuity contradiction. The grinder is carried out and the room is empty, yet in the closing glimpse he is back in the old room beside her. The old review's "Internal: PASS" missed it.
- Fix: Change L253, not L225, because the empty room frames the goodbye. The closing glimpse shows Lacrixa alone amid the broken glaze (the hammer, her broken leaf, or her turning toward the arch where he was carried). Do not add any warmth that suggests reunion.

### C2 · Major · The rig's presence in the sanctuary wall is unexplained

- Where: L39–L47 (Emet sees his leaf), L65 (the rules paragraph), L257 (the full account)
- Text:
> Emet saw the hinge leaf bolted into the new frame.
> Now his leaf was mounted in a stranger's ironwork.
> Someone had added three borrower loops and stretched a private step into a road.
- Problem: The leaf was stolen at a ford three days earlier. Tonight it sits in iron clamps against the sanctuary jamb in this House's north wall, and it came alive because Emet came near it. The story never says, even in a clause, that the frame had been set into this wall before the party arrived. So the inciting cause (why here, why tonight) cannot be recovered. "Mortar flashed to steam" and "floor to lintel" hint at a walled-up doorway, but only a hint. Udren records every fact of the account, and neither she nor anyone else remarks that a stranger has built into a House wall on their route.
- Fix: Add one or two sentences in L39–L47, with no new rules and no identity for the borrowers. Emet realizes the frame had been set into the north wall (e.g., the old bricked doorway, freshly re-mortared) on the circuit's known route, and it woke when he came near. Optionally, add one clause at L257 saying that Udren's ledger also records the frame in the wall.

### C3 · Minor · Lacrixa's position at the first loop

- Where: L21, L63, L87; the first loop is removed
- Text:
> She planted one boot on the sanctuary floor and called, “Hello lover!”
> On the far side Lacrixa matched him, bracing one clay-white hand against the iron.
- Problem: She steps onto the sanctuary floor and is never shown going back. The first loop sits "against the sanctuary jamb" (L63), so "on the far side" puts her either inside the throat or reaching through it. She can't be placed until L125, when she touches her own tiled jamb. The prompt asks for clear spatial geometry.
- Fix: At L57 or L79, add one clause that sends her back across the plane to her own side. At L87, say where she stands to grip the first loop.
