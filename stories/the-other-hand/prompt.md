# Production direction

The user requests an epic birth-of-superpowers story, with a clear departure from the small theatrical and animated-object structure of Still Taking Runaways. Make the worldwide transformation and the emergence of powers the story's central action. Keep catastrophe and violence non-graphic, then classify the finished prose and cover under the collection's AO3 rating definitions. Omit the calendar date from the prose. The undated modern-looking setting has no asserted connection to an existing era or system; it does not place active magic in the Long Dark.

The fictional world's starting population is seven billion; forty percent means 2.8 billion people develop powers. Show a huge variety, including the user's familiar powers and inventive additional abilities, as pervasive lived disorder, wonder, exploitation, and rescue. Variety must affect the action and the surrounding world rather than appear solely as a list or a closing report.

All story action and the ending occur within the first twenty-four hours. There is no later scene or epilogue. Narration may state the prompt's first-day totals retrospectively without adding later action; do not give an eyewitness unexplained immediate access to a complete census. Powers are unfamiliar and people are improvising under pressure throughout the day.

The user has rejected both a single focus and the proposed five-recurring-viewpoint ensemble. Find a different narrative form adequate to the planetary birth. Many independent lives must be experienced directly rather than subordinated to a small principal cast or one linked local crisis. The world, its simultaneous transformations, and the reader's accumulating discovery can supply narrative movement. Preserve the epic birth, pervasive variety, unknown origin, partial discovery, and first-day limit together; do not replace the story with a catalogue of abilities or reports.

The reader can assemble a larger picture than any character. The original forty-percent-of-seven-billion figure remains 2.8 billion; the viewpoint question does not change that arithmetic.
