# The Sky We Broke

Provisional worldwide ensemble in close third person, interleaved under one elapsed clock. Every scene and the ending belong to the first twenty-four hours. No calendar date. The narrator can state first-day totals without staging an impossible census: seven billion people at the start, 2.8 billion developing powers, 1.3 billion dead or missing. Five focal lives have independent desires; the reader can assemble a larger pattern than any of them. Names, local voices and specific landmarks belong to drafting.

**Before dawn over the Atlantic:** a mother teleports onto a falling wing while her daughter involuntarily gathers the burning aircraft around herself. An invulnerable passenger falls intact but cannot fly; a projected figure beckons toward a place its owner cannot reach; invisible passengers still need saving. The mother's failed jump while her daughter holds her teaches a specific release-before-passage, not a universal rule. Their rescue arrives above a newly raised district in an eastern city. A torn blue wing crosses onward and strikes the southern train. The mother wants her daughter out; the daughter is frightened and exhilarated by being able to carry people and help hold this new place.

**In a southern city at night:** an ordinary train driver finds aircraft wreckage on a line that now leads into somebody else's sky. She tries to preserve the old route and sends moving carriages and the blue tail into the northern harbour. Shapeshifting changes who can get through a gap; luck spares one passenger beside someone it does not spare; spoken words become solid obstacles; a child's sideways falling creates an alarming alternative to the broken track. Her local escape harms another place before she knows it. Later she refuses a safe passage offered only to powered passengers. Her task is not to become superhuman but to keep a mixed trainload together when their lives acquire different bargaining values.

**In a northern container harbour:** a technopath wakes a ship's engines as if waking her own limbs. She wants her first voyage and dismisses the ordinary captain's warnings. Physical contact and particular machines matter; she cannot simply command the world's networks. The arriving train wreckage breaks her departure. Her attempt to launch anyway draws a moving wall of sea through an opening into the eastern district. Received mass retains motion. Around her, levitation leaves a crane operator dangling, a slow-time wake catches a falling container, and seized cargo is transmuted into a weapon. She still wants the voyage when she learns it can have a shore no ordinary chart contains. Taking people aboard is also a claim to captaincy, contested by crew whose ordinary knowledge she needs.

**In the eastern city:** a young man dies in the first stampede and rises. His resurrection is personal and does not restore the crowd. He bargains for the loyalty of a woman whose force field can turn incoming matter aside and a man who can raise solid ground. Their refuge saves lives; his repeated returns give him the nerve and reputation to claim it. He starts selecting who may enter. The aircraft brings people and new gifts worth acquiring, including the daughter. His followers' abilities, their dependency and their consent supply his leverage. When the harbour's sea arrives, he orders the barrier curved around the raised territory. The first deflection sends a wave into the western desert sky. He wants a country, not a lesson, and retains that ambition through the climax.

**Above the western salt desert:** a woman flies for the sheer pleasure of finding an unowned horizon. A clairvoyant sees conflicting futures rather than a certain route; an astral projection cannot bring its vulnerable body along; an invisible raider uses the first chaos to take abandoned vehicles. The returning sea strikes the flyer's ascent. She follows its impossible path and sees a seam above the claimant's growing field. Her own desire to cross before somebody owns the sky matters as much as rescue. She carries one incomplete warning between places, leaves people she cannot carry, and remains a person pursuing flight rather than becoming everyone's courier.

The middle hours interleave recognizable consequences: the blue aircraft tail, moving carriages, salt water where it cannot belong, and a repeated ship's horn. People physically arrive; ordinary crew can use a surviving ship radio; the flyer can carry a limited message. No magical universal broadcast makes them share a theory. Each learns enough locally to choose. The mother and daughter meet the field maker in person, giving the release discovery somewhere to matter. Openings are encountered at the birth; nobody knows whether they are someone's involuntary gift or another part of the event. Later uses of force, motion and protection visibly change what passes through them and where damage goes. This is not the mechanism behind every power. Resurrection, phasing, fire, telepathy, invisibility, transmutation and stranger abilities continue to change actual conflicts, appetites and possibilities beyond the linked crisis.

**The late threat is made by people:** the claimant's curving protection field catches the returning sea in a circuit. Ships and wreckage come back with successive surges. His order to close the remaining gap will leave the next return nowhere to discharge except through inhabited ground and harbour. The approaching water and visibly closing seam supply the window; earlier impacts establish the consequence. Different local people see different parts, not a single leader explaining a world diagram. The claimant demands sovereignty and selective admission rather than freely opening his border. The driver refuses that selection. The technopath offers a dangerous voyage on contested terms. The flyer sees a way past the upper seam. The mother tries to get her daughter out while the daughter wants to hold a place the others are changing. The field maker's loyalty and the physical difficulty of releasing one hold while another force is still acting create an encounter, not a protocol demonstration.

The exact decisive collision is open to prose discovery. Persuading the field maker is not sufficient. The daughter acts on a hold she helped create; the mother's dangerous transfer makes that release possible; the driver puts actual carriages and unpowered passengers into the contested passage, forcing the technopath to take their moving mass before completing her voyage; the flyer's physical crossing reaches a route unavailable from below. Ordinary brakes, couplings and crew work, alongside the previously lived stranger gifts, must matter. These actions change what the field maker can do without erasing her choice. They should break the destructive circuit and take an actual ship and people into changed geography. The claimant may keep a smaller territory; cooperation need not redeem him. A voyage becomes real and dangerous before hour twenty-four, while some people are stranded and others die. The birth continues everywhere. No later coda, tidy origin, universal cost, chosen saviour or declaration that humanity has united.
