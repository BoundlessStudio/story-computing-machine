# The Sky We Broke

Provisional structural revision responding to the user's request for the epic birth of superpowers. The small theatre story is superseded. No puppets, animated keepsakes, theatre shelter, or missing people lodged in powers. Calendar date remains omitted. A modern-looking undated world, with no claimed connection to an existing universe era. A longer first-person story, with the world changing around the narrator in continuous action rather than retrospective headline montage.

The narrator is on a passenger flight with her grown daughter when the event arrives before dawn in their part of the world. They have an affectionate, ordinary relationship, not an estrangement to repair. The cockpit door flies out into the cabin: someone has become impossibly strong. One pilot goes through the floor. Fire blooms around a passenger who cannot stop it. The daughter acquires a force that can hold and move things, but initially pulls everything toward herself. The narrator's first teleport is an instinctive escape from the fire, landing outside the falling aircraft. She sees its wreckage arranged around her daughter in the open air. An invulnerable passenger is falling among it, completely intact and unable to fly.

The opening is a desperate airborne rescue requiring the new abilities to collide. The daughter tries to arrest the aircraft's fall; the narrator jumps among pieces, taking people within her reach; the invulnerable stranger catches bodies and strikes away debris but cannot supply lift. Their first combined effort tears a section of the sky open. They cannot save everyone. A second, less intuitive attempt succeeds locally: stop pulling a piece while the narrator jumps it, then catch what comes through, instead of applying all the force at once. The narrator experiences a dark interval inside one jump, seeing the broken aircraft from two directions simultaneously. This is frightening evidence, not a vision from a chosen saviour.

They reach a city in the act of coming apart. Ordinary people discover flight, strength, phasing, transmutation and telepathy while buildings rise, burn, or lose their solidity. At least one first use is exhilarating and successful without becoming punishment. The invulnerable stranger learns he can leap from a moving slab and survive a landing that makes a crater. People use powers to exploit the panic as well as save strangers. The group moves through these collisions toward the only place with a usable view of the immense dark aperture over the horizon. The daughter's initial pulling has injured her; she can still act, but her mother cannot simply carry her through the plot.

A rising district supplies the second sustained set piece. Someone holds a tower up with brute force while a phasing person tries to release trapped floors and a transmuter makes a rigid span. Opposed operations on the same material cause another visible tear. A conventional explosion near it is returned from the opposite side; the second pulse hits with the first and the tear grows. Ordinary destructive force can enter the feedback, not just powers. A timed handoff lets one group cross the span before it is released. Telepathy carries thoughts across the distance when radios cannot, but cannot remove incompatible wants or fear. The narrator witnesses failure and bounded success while crossing. She suspects that the great aperture is a connected boundary returning force, rather than a solid incoming object. She has no complete theory of the event or every gift.

Emergency command has ordered a nuclear strike at the great aperture's apparent centre. Their reason is intelligible: the growing disturbance pulls aircraft and whole districts toward it, ordinary attacks have failed, and measurements suggest that it will reach the inhabited surface. They have incomplete reports of smaller tears, not the narrator's close experience of a returning blast. They are trying a final overwhelming intervention before losing the means to act. The narrator must reach the decision or weapon physically through the altered world; a broadcast speech is insufficient. Her evidence changes a world-scale action: a vast explosion at the connected boundary may return to Earth through the tears instead of destroying the apparent target.

The climax is an ensemble intervention on that actual strike, with material cost. The daughter and strangers hold one temporary route open while the narrator enters the dangerous boundary to prevent or redirect the detonation. The invulnerable passenger withstands contact that she cannot; a telepath keeps separated teams informed; neither has the decisive knowledge alone. She has a clear chance to jump back to her daughter before the route closes, but that would abandon the intervention. She commits and leaves her daughter in other people's hands. The daughter gets her own costly action, not a role as a package her mother sacrifices. Success prevents one further planetary catastrophe; it does not resurrect the first day's victims or end powers.

The ending makes a new world physically real. People move through a transformed sky and geography under their own power: flight and impossible strength are abilities they choose, not only errors to contain. The narrator sees her daughter act on that scale. Their changed relationship is felt through action, without a reunion speech. The 1.3 billion dead or missing in the first twenty-four hours remain irreversible and legible; the eventual forty per cent establishes how large this birth is. Origin remains unknown. They have learnt one consequential property and used it; many effects remain unexplained.

Open risks for challenge: returning force needs visible action rather than invented physics jargon. The nuclear strike's relation to the smaller blast must persuade, and command must not be stupid. The exact interception and teleport limits need to be discovered in action before deciding the climax. No abstract last-jump allowance: the choice comes from geography, moving lines of sight, and the brief route the daughter can hold. The sky intervention must earn scale through the airborne opening and rising-city sequence. Ending and outcome remain provisional.
