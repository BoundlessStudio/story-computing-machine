# All at Once

Provisional direction, replacing the five-anchor ensemble. The superseded draft is preserved at `tmp/ensemble-superseded-draft.md`; no new full prose yet.

The user-authorized lens is one newly awakened searching consciousness, an original telepath rather than a borrowed character or a machine. At the instant of the worldwide event, unbearable planetary input overwhelms her ordinary morning. Narrowing attention to a condition brings fleeting access to actual current minds, powered and unpowered. She has no census, coordinates or authoritative account of what she sees. Some matches are wrong, metaphorical or terrifyingly incomplete. Her need to recover her own mind gives the changing searches pressure, but her bodily frame stays brief. She is not the planet's coordinator or saviour.

Time moves through the first twenty-four hours. There is no calendar date or later coda. The reader enters many people's sensations, immediate wants and actions directly, sometimes for a sentence, sometimes for a sustained encounter. No fixed principal cast has privileged recurrence. Consequences, sensory details and changed questions connect the glimpses; lives continue after attention leaves them. The global birth remains the action, rather than a common evacuation crisis.

**First minutes:** a desperate narrowing toward people falling opens aircraft, ordinary stairs, involuntary levitation, somebody who can fly and somebody newly invulnerable who cannot. Other searches bring fire, phasing, shapeshifting, altered bodies and impossible strength as experiences people do not yet possess names for. Tiny wants make people particular: catch a hand, put down a stolen thing, keep a newly chosen face, get one breath. Delight and catastrophe coexist.

**Hours of naming and claiming:** attention shifts toward people who believe they understand, or who think they can stop it. She finds contradictory certainty, frightened governments, rescuers, people founding territories and ordinary people refusing to leave one another. Telepathy exposes something a marriage had survived by hiding; technopathy makes control seductive; luck protects somebody using it selfishly; resurrection does not give a bereaved parent the same mercy. Stranger gifts change work, hunger, ownership and violence. A rescue image misread as an attack, a command obeyed literally, and a failed attempt to suppress a dangerous ability can carry consequences into later searches. No single hypothesis accounts for the birth.

**Middle hours:** changing conditions let the reader assemble things no participant fully knows. Disconnected places experienced the onset together; second disasters often have different, human causes. These are useful deductions, but insufficient as the mystery's payoff. Searches for people who vanished begin returning an unexpected kind of mind: someone still in the middle of an accident first witnessed near the onset. The lens can mistake memory, projection or panic for presence, so early evidence remains troublesome rather than being announced as temporal science.

**Last hours:** exact material details recur across independent current minds and rescuers' observations. A fresh response to present contact must distinguish living experience from a traumatic recollection; a stuck clock alone proves nothing. The provisional partial discovery is that some of the missing are not sharing the world's elapsed time. They are still experiencing minutes—or part of a fall—which occurred hours ago for everybody outside. This does not locate all missing people, return them, explain the source or turn powers into trapped companions. The dead remain dead in many searches, resurrection varies, and silence is not proof of death. Whether this delay began with the event or with particular involuntary gifts remains uncertain.

That limited discovery changes real action: people at reachable sites reconsider demolition or destructive containment, while others refuse, cannot be reached or have already acted. The lens cannot broadcast a solution to the whole planet; ordinary communication and other people's choices matter. Simultaneously, powers elsewhere continue producing joy, coercion, war, intimacy, rescue and new geography that this finding cannot settle.

The ending belongs to the last hour and opens a new task rather than completing a universal rescue. The search condition has changed from escape from the noise to finding those not yet reached by the shared day. Many living moments continue beyond her attention; many cannot be recovered. The narrator can state the prompt's first-day scale without giving her impossible knowledge: seven billion at the start, 2.8 billion powers, 1.3 billion dead or missing. Unknown origin remains, but the pieces have altered what people dare do next.

Main risks for challenge: the search becoming a menu or ability catalogue; its lens becoming a hidden omniscient investigator; temporal mismatch being declared from insufficient mental evidence; a partial survival finding softening the mass loss; or the mystery swallowing the worldwide birth. These should be tested through scenes, not solved with a taxonomy or a procedure.
