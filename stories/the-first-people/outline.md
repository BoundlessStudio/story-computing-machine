# Outline

## Story

- Title: The First People
- Premise and central promise: Earth was the first successful cradle in a silent cosmos. Ages later, one civilization seizes humanity's star-road and can strike every peaceful people. Courier Heshi reaches Earth with their last route census and finds civilian listener Martin. Humanity sacrifices its oldest advantage, breaks the enemy's universal reach, and leaves to help without claiming rule.
- Focal pressure or attachment: Heshi needs her losses and limited authority to matter. She wants legendary elders to act but cannot ask them to ruin their inheritance. Martin wants aid without dominion; chores and bad humor resemble indifference.
- Counterforce or complication: The pursuer controls the fast routes, falsifies traffic, and offers to spare Earth for Heshi and silence. Burning the road also strands civilians and isolates worlds; it cannot choose sides, restore itself, or win later local wars. Heshi's damaged census may no longer provide a safe cutoff window.
- POV, distance, and information limit: Close third past through Heshi, who feels Earth as rain, gravity, masonry, and Martin's ordinary conduct. Human deliberation reaches her only through him and fragments. Never survey the war or certify every outcome.
- Governing movement and time shape: One narrowing night: landing, failed contact, reconstructed plea, pursuit, joint commitment, ragged aftermath. Admit history only when it changes a choice; stop before campaign summary or victory coda.
- Speculative rule or ordinary-world constraint: While Earth was alone, humanity spread a non-sentient transit lattice through reachable stars. Later peoples inherited it; the enemy captured its routing. Earth's irreversible root-burn ejects travelers and destroys fast transit but cannot discriminate or be rebuilt soon. Heshi's census reduces casualties while exposing survivors. Slower human craft permit aid, not instant victory. No magic or general crossing law.
- Dialogue promise: Ceremonial expectation and panic meet domestic vocabulary, poor timing, mistranslation, and reluctant authority. The enemy's polished certainty is easier to understand than either ally; awkward humor never belittles loss.
- Dialogic medium: Failing translated speech, repeated names, hand-built star diagrams, the route census, gestures, fragmentary confirmations, one-way enemy broadcast, and the road's consequential silence.
- Dialogue engine: Heshi must make the plea legible; Martin must distinguish damage from hesitation. He needs her census to limit harm; she needs the cost before exposing survivors. Each attempt changes evidence, authorization, exposure, or trust.

## Voice

- Narrative texture: Stay with Heshi's bodily scale and misreadings, moving from rain, hands, and wreckage toward the sky only when signals change. Render deep history through residue and useful inference. Tighten near cutoff; let aftermath arrive in fragments.
- Conversational texture: Translation creates repetition, blunt repair, pauses, accidental discourtesy, and ordinary fussing. Neither participant becomes eloquent because the stakes are vast.
- Rhetorical ownership: Heshi owns ceremonial cosmology, impatient route logic, embarrassed literalism, and late defensive humor. Martin owns household comparison, self-deprecation, evasive affection, unwanted social authority, and better listening than explanation. The enemy owns frictionless reassurance and false either-or claims.
- Pressure behavior: Heshi points and repeats, then becomes dangerously concise. Martin's humor worsens before it stops; directness marks commitment, not a speech. Lost qualifiers make silence unsafe as agreement.
- Relationship movement: Heshi wants rescue and command but cannot request Earth's sacrifice; Martin wants coordinates and partnership but cannot request exposure. Exchange moves disappointment into informed joint risk without hierarchy or compulsory gratitude.
- Anti-default: Avoid ancient admirals, expert committees, tactical lecture, applause, or victory through innate aggression. Humanity's distinction is a head start it relinquishes; keep the hinge between an exhausted courier and a fallible civilian.

## Beats

1. Heshi falls from the captured road onto a rain-dark Earth coast. Her pursuer arrives before dawn; the oldest legend is inhabited and quiet.
2. Martin finds her on listening-house rotation. The translator mangles names, ages, and her appeal. His protection of wreckage looks like distraction; her demand for leaders sounds like a request for conquest.
3. Heshi builds the census in star patterns. Martin recognizes humanity's weaponized road; she learns it predates her species. The cutoff is prepared, but her damage determines whether occupied routes clear.
4. The pursuer offers Earth safety for Heshi and silence. Martin makes the price of refusal concrete: stranded civilians, lost contact, and humanity's oldest achievement. Surrender or withdrawal would not remove the wider threat.
5. Heshi connects the census despite exposing survivors. Martin confirms humanity's root-burn. Their separate inputs create a safer window, not perfect control.
6. The lattice ejects travelers and dies raggedly, severing reinforcement. Earth contains the pursuer without spectacle. Disconnected voices establish survival and confusion, never a cheering chorus.
7. Slower human craft leave to aid stranded systems. Heshi's home remains unreachable and local wars remain, but the universal attack has ended. She joins the untidy work of deciding where help can still arrive.

## Continuity

- Canon used: One physical world persists through deep time under names including Earth; civilizations recur while knowledge stays partial. The prompt makes this cradle first and places later extraterrestrial civilizations after real cosmic silence.
- Boundaries and unknowns: Leave the Galactic Cycle and relation to established magic eras unresolved; use no magic. New lattice, aliens, enemy, and protocol are unrelated to established crossings. Humanity may persist through refoundings, but claim no uninterrupted government, perfect memory, literal real-world history, universal jurisdiction, or total defeat.
