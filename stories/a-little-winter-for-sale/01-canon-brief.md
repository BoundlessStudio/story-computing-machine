# Canon brief

## Hard constraints

- Canon authority comes from `LOCKED` and `CANON` universe entries, followed by
  final stories whose index row says canon `yes`. The current story index has no
  canon-`yes` rows, so no existing story supplies binding dragon, city, or water
  lore for this prompt. Candidate, in-progress, abandoned, and unpromoted material
  must not be treated as canon. (`universe/README.md` — **Authority order**;
  `stories/INDEX.md` — **Story index**)
- The story must occur somewhere in the one physical world that persists across
  deep time. A new desert city and mountain can occupy an otherwise unidentified
  era, but the story must not declare them a separate planet or plane, invent a
  second universal cosmology, or contradict the established one-world
  continuity. (`universe/premise.md` — **The deep-time shared world**;
  `universe/locations.md` — **The world across eras**)
- “Magic,” “technology,” and “divinity” are viewpoint-culture labels rather
  than objective cosmological categories. The traveler or city may call the
  dragon's cold “magic,” but the narrative must not present that label as a
  universal taxonomy or imply that all anomalous systems share one origin.
  (`universe/rules.md` — **System categories**; `universe/glossary.md` —
  **Magic, technology, and divinity**)
- Only life can use magic. The ice dragon may be the living source of its own
  cold. Nonliving civic devices may store, focus, transmit, or shape magic
  supplied through living agency, but may not independently act as magic users
  unless the story establishes that they are alive and accepts the resulting
  personhood implications. (`universe/rules.md` — **Living agency and magic**)
- Cost and desire are not universal fuels for power. Payment, enjoyment,
  fatigue, heat exchange, recovery, or another limit may matter to this
  particular transaction only through a clearly story-local mechanism. The
  story must not generalize that coin, pleasure, consent, sacrifice, or need
  powers all magic. (`universe/rules.md` — **Costs, desire, and local
  mechanisms**)
- The dragon's identity, agency, consent, and choices must remain morally real;
  no system explanation may reduce a person-like being to a mere malfunction
  or erase the ethical consequences of dependence on its labor.
  (`universe/style-guide.md` — **Personhood and moral consequence**)
- Names are ordinarily symbolic and social, not automatically magical. Any
  literal name-binding would need a specific local system and cannot be assumed
  from the dragon's name, title, species, or bargain. (`universe/rules.md` —
  **Names and identity binding**)
- Resemblance to another dragon, magical bargain, city, cave, artifact, or
  recurring image does not establish a shared mechanism, ancestry, identity, or
  crossover. Connections require prompt direction or specific promoted
  evidence. (`universe/rules.md` — **Artifacts, motifs, and folklore**;
  `universe/style-guide.md` — **Continuity and evidence discipline**)
- The verified prompt contract is also binding production input, though it is
  not canon: first-person traveler POV, past tense, days of desert travel, an
  apparently impossible mountainside city far from surface water, a voluntary
  ice dragon far from home who enjoys being paid for bounded cold, concrete
  civic/economic stakes, a consequential traveler choice, and a resolved
  ending. The dragon cannot secretly be enslaved or awaiting rescue, its power
  cannot be infinite or costless, and the city cannot be only a static twist.
  (`stories/a-little-winter-for-sale/00-prompt.md` — **Story controls**,
  **Assumptions**, and **Completion tests**)
- Content must remain within the recorded Teen/PG controls and the universe's
  broadly accessible, non-explicit ceiling; danger may be consequential but
  non-graphic. (`stories/a-little-winter-for-sale/00-prompt.md` — **Story
  controls**; `universe/style-guide.md` — **Audience and content boundaries**)

## Useful established context

- The shared world contains countless civilizations separated by deep time,
  with repeated invention, copying, trade, collapse, and partial loss of
  provenance. A self-contained fantasy culture can therefore belong without
  being assigned to a named era or explaining the entire cosmology.
  (`universe/premise.md` — **The deep-time shared world**)
- Characters and cultures may possess incomplete or mistaken models of the
  wider world. The traveler and city need only understand the local bargain;
  they do not need authoritative knowledge of the setting's deep history.
  (`universe/premise.md` — **Knowledge and connection**)
- Many distinct real systems can coexist, interact, hybridize, or imitate one
  another, and not every system is active in every era. This supports a narrow,
  locally defined cold-working ability without establishing a universal school
  of elemental magic. (`universe/rules.md` — **System categories**)
- Nonliving infrastructure may store, focus, transmit, or shape a living
  being's magic. That canon permits, but does not require, a civic interface
  between the dragon and a water or cooling system. Its exact operation remains
  story-specific. (`universe/rules.md` — **Living agency and magic**)
- The universe allows wide genre, tone, scale, and awareness. A
  wonder-forward, dryly humorous local story is compatible with canon, and no
  crossover or overt deep-time reveal is required. (`universe/style-guide.md`
  — **Tonal range, scale, and connection**)
- The prose defaults favor concrete detail, varied rhythm, and lore only when
  it serves the story. This aligns with making the city's water handling,
  payment, and bounded cold legible through material details rather than a
  cosmology lecture. (`universe/style-guide.md` — **Prose defaults**)

## Conflicts or ambiguity

- **No authoritative conflict found.** The relevant `LOCKED` and `CANON`
  entries are mutually compatible, the retcon log contains no approved
  changes, and the index contains no canon-`yes` story eligible to override or
  supplement them. No canon ruling is required before planning.
  (`universe/retcons.md` — **Retcon log**; `stories/INDEX.md` — **Story
  index**)
- **No relevant provisional guidance exists.** The searched universe notes
  contain no `PROVISIONAL` entry for dragons, ice powers, deserts, mountains,
  water infrastructure, currencies, or this city. Candidate-story treatments
  of dragons and civic systems remain nonbinding.
- The prompt requires payment, enjoyment, and cumulative limits, while canon
  says cost and desire are not universal fuels. These are compatible only if
  the story treats payment as an economic/social exchange and defines any
  capacity limit as local to this dragon or cold-working system, rather than
  making payment or pleasure a universal metaphysical rule.
  (`stories/a-little-winter-for-sale/00-prompt.md` — **Assumptions**;
  `universe/rules.md` — **Costs, desire, and local mechanisms**)
- The story's era is unspecified. If a later stage identifies it as the
  modern-like era, Ravel, or the Glass-Sea era, it inherits their established
  one-world relationship and chronology. Ravel's placement and the exact
  Galactic Cycle coordinates of all known eras remain unresolved.
  (`universe/timeline.md` — **Universal chronology** and **Established era
  order**; `universe/glossary.md` — **Modern-like era**, **Ravel**, and
  **Glass-Sea world**)

## Unknowns

- **Unknown:** whether dragons are a species, culture, lineage, constructed
  life, singular anomalies, or several unrelated kinds of being. No
  authoritative universe entry defines dragons, dragon biology, lifespan,
  reproduction, diet, language, social organization, customary names, or
  relationship to humans.
- **Unknown:** what “ice dragon” means ontologically, how the dragon produces
  cold or ice, whether it moves heat, freezes existing water, condenses
  humidity, creates matter, stores seasonal cold, or uses another mechanism.
  No energy cost, recovery interval, range, precision, side effect, or upper
  capacity is established.
- **Unknown:** the dragon's homeland, route and reason for migration, duration
  away, ability to return, cultural ties, and emotional meaning of “far from
  home.” These cannot be asserted as pre-existing dragon lore.
- **Unknown:** the city, mountain, cave, desert, surrounding geography,
  climate, aquifer conditions, trade routes, political structure, population,
  law, language, religion, and historical era. `universe/locations.md`
  establishes no geography for them.
- **Unknown:** the city's actual source of water and the proportions supplied
  by storage, wells, condensation, imported water, ice, seasonal runoff, or the
  dragon. The prompt requires material plausibility but canon selects no
  mechanism.
- **Unknown:** currency, prices, wages, contract norms, property rights,
  taxation, rationing, hospitality customs, and what forms of payment the
  dragon values. Canon establishes no universal economy.
- **Unknown:** whether the dragon's cold can be stored or distributed by
  nonliving infrastructure. Canon permits such devices in principle when a
  living source supplies the magic, but it does not establish this particular
  capability. (`universe/rules.md` — **Living agency and magic**)
- **Unknown:** names and aliases for the traveler, dragon, city, mountain,
  cave, civic bodies, officials, residents, currency, or infrastructure.
- The glossary contains no canonical term or alias for dragon, ice dragon,
  cold-working, desert city, or water-selling. A later story-specific term must
  not be presented as already universal. (`universe/glossary.md` — **Magic,
  technology, and divinity** and all current term entries)

## Safe invention space

- Invent one local desert city, adjacent mountain and cave, nearby terrain,
  water infrastructure, civic offices, and economic practices without tying
  them to an established named era. Keep their claims local rather than making
  them universal geography or history.
- Invent this individual ice dragon's appearance, voice, preferences,
  migration history, chosen work, pricing, refusal rights, and relationship to
  the city. Frame any broader species or homeland statements as limited
  character knowledge unless a later canon decision establishes more.
- Define a narrow cold-working mechanism with measurable outputs, cumulative
  limits, recovery conditions, and consequences. It may be innate, learned,
  hybrid, or mediated by infrastructure, provided the living dragon remains
  the magic user and the mechanism is not generalized to all magic.
- Invent a story-local currency or payment bundle, valuation practice,
  appointment or metering system, contract, and dispute. Payment may matter
  because the dragon wants what it buys or signifies; no metaphysical
  pay-to-power rule is needed.
- Choose a materially intelligible local water strategy—such as storage,
  condensation, groundwater, seasonal collection, trade, or a combination—and
  define the dragon's bounded contribution. Do not silently turn one dragon
  into an infinite source or establish a global hydrological rule.
- Invent local civic dependence, inequity, maintenance problems, hospitality
  customs, or an immediate shortage that gives the traveler's choice
  consequences, while preserving the dragon's genuine ability to accept,
  refuse, and price work.
- Invent nonliving reservoirs, pipes, exchangers, metering devices, or magical
  storage that handle the dragon's supplied cold, so long as they do not become
  autonomous magic users without an explicit life/personhood premise.
- Invent local history, legend, or misunderstanding around the cave and city,
  but do not use resemblance to another story's dragon, city, sovereign, or
  artifact as proof of a shared identity or system.

- Known unresolved collisions are `Lena`, `Mara`, `Nisha`, `Pell`, and `Voss`.

  clearance cannot be issued at this stage. The future plan's `Name check` must
  compare each proposed form case-insensitively against the entire registry,
  including aliases, close spellings, reversals, and easily confused cadence;
  it should favor names readily distinguishable from all reserved dragon forms.

## Required checks after drafting

- Confirm that every use of cold has a consistent source, scale, duration,
  recovery condition, and cumulative limit; reconcile all stated water volumes,
  storage capacities, travel times, prices, and timing.
- Confirm the dragon knowingly accepts payment, enjoys the work, can set terms
  and refuse a use, and is neither secretly enslaved nor reframed as awaiting
  rescue. Check that civic dependence creates stakes without erasing its
  agency.
- Confirm the city has a materially legible water source and distribution
  method beyond a handwave, and that the dragon's bounded cold does not become
  infinite, costless, or an unexplained solution to every need.
- Confirm payment, pleasure, consent, fatigue, and desire are described as
  features of this local relationship or mechanism, not universal fuels or
  laws of magic.
- Search for any nonliving machine, reservoir, contract, or artifact that
  appears to initiate magic independently; either keep it as storage/focusing
  infrastructure supplied by the living dragon or establish and ethically
  treat its life/personhood.
- Search for unsupported species-wide dragon claims, universal elemental
  categories, global currencies, named eras, factions, places, historical
  events, crossings, or connections to other story dragons. Narrow unsupported
  statements to local observation or character belief.
- If the prose names the modern-like era, Ravel, the Glass-Sea era, Earth, a
  Galactic Cycle, or the Glass Gate, recheck all chronology, capitalization,
  aliases, and one-world constraints against the relevant universe entries.
- Verify first-person past tense, days of desert travel, the mountainside
  location, distance from surface water, the cave encounter, bounded paid cold,
  meaningful city stakes, the traveler's consequential choice, the dragon's
  distance from home, and a resolved ending against `00-prompt.md`.
- Recheck tone and content against the Teen/PG contract and universe content
  ceiling; keep dehydration and other danger non-graphic.
- Extract every final character-facing name and alias—including titles used as
  names and named person-like entities—compare exact and close forms against
  the complete registry, resolve accidental collisions, and have the primary
  coordinator update `stories/NAMES.md` and run the required story-scoped name
  check. This canon-research stage does not register names.
