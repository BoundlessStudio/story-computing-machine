# Opus review: A Little Winter for Sale

Independent audit for a later editing pass. It is not a `review.md` verdict,
does not change canon state, and does not authorize edits by itself.

## Verdict

- Dialogue: REVISE — the decisive offer-and-objection exchange runs on correction routines whose replies don't follow the words just spoken, and all four speakers share one dry, contraction-free, last-word register.
- Prose: REVISE — the prose is concrete and often funny, but narration and the closing couplet restate the theme, and the handoff terms added in review fixes are repeated four times.
- Continuity and prompt: REVISE — the prompt is fully delivered, but Tair prices a delivery to the northern freight shelter before anyone has proposed a northern job, then objects that his commission lies west.

- Existing review.md said: `04-review.md` Pass 6 verdict PASS, 0 findings, with no Dialogue line at all (dialogue was never assessed as such); disagree.

The best dialogue here belongs to Urumet when it is fussy about money in
concrete terms: the grit surcharge, the refused seventh stroke, the
sixth-stroke checklist ending in "My fee?", "Sold out". The trouble is that
Yara, Tair and the clerk all talk in the dragon's register: formal, with no
contractions, answering each other with neat corrections and closing lines. So
the dragon's delight in precision has nothing to stand out against, and Tair's
fear of owing anyone exists only in narration. At the decisive beat the
exchange runs on "That was not my question" and "Correct. That is why I
object", and Urumet argues against a seizure and a seventh stroke that nobody
has proposed. The moral turn is announced, not argued. It is then stated
again in narration and in both closing exchanges. The edit pass should keep
Urumet formal and funny and make Yara brisk and practical, with humor by
understatement. Tair should be guarded and plain. Rebuild the offer and
objection so each reply answers what was actually said, and end the story on
action and image rather than on a stated lesson.

## Dialogue findings

### D1 · Blocking · "That was not my question" at the decisive offer

- Where: L344–L352, the cave, Tair offers the sunwheel
- Text:
> Yara kept her hands at her sides. "At what price?"
> "That was not my question."
> "It is mine. I give it. You use it. No debt."
- Problem: Ground/uptake and Ground/referent at the story's decisive choice. Yara asks "At what price?", and Tair's "None." answers exactly that question. Her "That was not my question" denies an answer she has just received. The line only works as the house correction routine, and it assumes the reader already knows the author's intended meaning ("I want you to name a figure"). Tair's "It is mine" then echoes "my question", so "it" can be read as the question, the answer or the wheel. The reader has to back-solve to the wheel. The four-beat clipped close ("I give it. You use it. No debt.") is the triplet tic at the point where Tair's fear of owing should be most visible.
- Fix: Keep "Take it." and Yara keeping her hands at her sides. Yara's reply should respond to "None" as spoken: she refuses to cut on a gift, or she presses for a figure and gives a practical reason of her own. Tair's reply should name the wheel plainly and let his stubbornness show, without an echo or a triplet. Cut "That was not my question." and "It is mine." Illustrative only: Yara: "Then I'm not cutting it. Name a figure." Tair: "It's my wheel. I'm giving it."

### D2 · Blocking · Urumet's objection argues against positions nobody holds

- Where: L354–L377, the cave, Urumet's objection to the free gift
- Text:
> "Correct. That is why I object to your attempt to make its price everyone else's problem."
> And therefore Yara may ask to buy your tool. She may not seize it. I may choose to complete two jobs. I may not be squeezed into a seventh.
> Emergency does not turn consent into a decorative flourish.
> "Because I am a professional."
- Problem: Several Ground/uptake failures in a row. (1) Tair says "It is not yours." Urumet's "Correct. That is why I object" does not follow: the wheel not being Urumet's is no reason for Urumet to object. (2) "make its price everyone else's problem" can't be recovered. By giving the wheel away, Tair bears the whole cost himself. The intended idea (a free sacrifice in a crisis teaches the city that emergencies make things free, which threatens Urumet's own limit) is never said. (3) Tair says "People need water." Urumet answers by ruling out a seizure and a seventh stroke, but nobody has proposed either: Tair is volunteering, and the seventh stroke was the dye works' request hours earlier. (4) "Emergency does not turn consent into a decorative flourish" is a Philosopher line. (5) Tair asks "Why should that concern you?", a good plain question. "Because I am a professional" doesn't answer it. Its meaning only becomes clear seventy lines later, when Urumet offers the route. The exchange also doesn't persuade Tair. His turn happens afterwards in narration (L387–L392), so the decisive argument has no effect on the relationship.
- Fix: Rebuild L360–L377 so every Urumet line answers the words just spoken and comes from Urumet's own stake. Pick one or both stakes and make them explicit: its trade depends on Qarsis not learning that storms make things free, and it intends to hire Tair (the tap on the northern page). Cut the seizure/seventh list and "decorative flourish". "To loss," Urumet said. "Very grandly." can stay. "You have not consented to being paid" may stay as Urumet's one articulate line only if the lines before it are grounded. Replace "Because I am a professional." with an answer to Tair's question. The exchange should leave Tair with something concrete to weigh, so that Yara's "Name those" meets a man who has been moved, not only one who is told how he feels. Illustrative only: Urumet, tapping the northern page: "Because I intend to hire you, and I do not hire people who work for nothing."

### D3 · Major · The grit-receipt argument has the wrong day and a punchline for a close

- Where: L156–L172, the Coldmouth, Urumet's first scene
- Text:
> "I can taste tomorrow's grit from here."
> "It is not tomorrow's batch."
> "Then you may come back tomorrow and discover that foresight costs extra."
- Problem: Ground/time and a Punchline Patch. The glasswind hits within the hour (the first grit strikes the cave arch at L250), and strokes five and six that same day carry the grit surcharge (L441, L499), so "tomorrow's grit" is the wrong day. The clerk's "It is not tomorrow's batch" exists to set up "foresight costs extra", which doesn't parse. It could mean tomorrow's price rises, or that Urumet charges for having predicted the grit. Either way it is a closing line, not a reason for the clerk to add the charge. This is the reader's first sight of the dragon at work, and it should be the clearest piece of its pricing logic.
- Fix: Put the grit in today: it is coming, and Urumet can taste it. Cut the clerk's "It is not tomorrow's batch" and the "foresight" line. Give Urumet one practical, in-character reason for the clerk to add the charge now. Keep "This receipt... does not include the grit surcharge", the clerk adding the line, and the pleased rumble. Illustrative only: "It will be in the intake before the fifth stroke. Write it now and spare us both an amendment."

### D4 · Major · First Tair–Urumet exchange: mirrored comebacks, then an unprompted thesis

- Where: L230–L248, the Coldmouth, after the fourth stroke
- Text:
> "The frost suggested it."
> "The manners confirmed it."
> "I can enjoy Qarsis and miss home," it said. "Even water manages to be more than one thing."
- Problem: Tennis Match / Punchline Patch, followed by Philosopher. "Neither are you" / "The frost suggested it" / "The manners confirmed it" is a mirrored setup and comeback with no stake. Tair's real question (is it chained here?) goes unspoken, so Urumet's "I left it because I wished to leave" answers a suspicion held only in narration (L227–L228). Urumet then states the prompt's own assumption ("the dragon can enjoy its present work while still missing home") almost word for word, without anyone having asked, and caps it with an aphorism whose water-states image the reader has to work out.
- Fix: Let Tair voice his suspicion, clumsily or rudely, so that Yara's cough has a cause and Urumet's account of itself answers something that was said. Cut the frost/manners pair. Keep "There is an ice shelf far north...", "I left it because I wished to leave", and the list of what Urumet likes (it fulfils the prompt). Cut both sentences of the thesis. Show the homesickness through the claw resting on the northern page. If a spoken admission is needed, it should answer a question Tair actually asks and be short and plain. The CANON entry requires that Urumet visibly misses home, so do not remove that fact, only the statement of it. Illustrative only: Tair: "Do they let you leave?"

### D5 · Major · "Can you aim" and the chest threat in the crisis

- Where: L272–L287, the cave, just after the regulator breaks
- Text:
> The humor had gone out of its voice.
> "Can you aim without that?" I asked.

- Problem: Ground/verb and a correction routine. The regulator doesn't aim anything. Urumet's "Yes" accepts the wrong frame, then corrects it with "Neither is relevant". Keep "Nobody touch the horn" and "The humor had gone out of its voice." Illustrative only: Tair: "Can't you stop at four, like last time?" Urumet: "Four is only the usual. ## Continuity and prompt findings

### C1 · Blocking · Tair prices a northern delivery before any northern job exists

- Where: L397–L402 (Tair's terms) against L447–L456 (Urumet's later offer, Tair's objection)
- Text:
> "Delivered at city cost to the freight shelter beside the split-stone well, eight days up the existing north caravan road.

- Fix: The CANON fact (the city delivers to the freight shelter, with provisions while he waits) must survive, so reorder rather than delete. Smallest route: Tair's first terms give no location ("delivered at city cost", wage for delay, recutting, provisions until it reaches me). After he accepts Urumet's commission, Yara amends the city's line to the freight shelter beside the split-stone well, eight days up the marked north road, with provisions while he waits there, in one sentence of narration or one Yara line. Alternatively, if D2's rebuild has Urumet state its intention to hire Tair, move the salt-road objection up to that point so the northern delivery is motivated before he prices it. Either way, state "no new survey work until the wheel is in hand" only once. Let Tair name one real figure from his route book, or change the throat beat.
