# Prompt contract

## Verbatim writing prompt

> **[WP] As a traveler, you've been exploring the desert for days, before coming upon a city so far from water, nestled against a mountainside. You find that in the mountains cave, is an ice dragon far from home, enjoying being paid to simply sell small bouts of it's power**

## Story controls

- Working title: A Little Winter for Sale
- Target length: 2,500–4,000 words
- POV: First-person traveler, with the ice dragon's agency made legible through
  direct interaction rather than an omniscient explanation.
- Tense: Past.
- Tone and genre: Wonder-forward secondary-world fantasy with dry humor,
  tactile desert imagery, and grounded civic/economic stakes.
- Audience/content rating: Teen / PG; dehydration risk and non-graphic danger,
  no explicit sexual content or graphic violence.
- Required elements: A traveler has spent days exploring a desert; an
  apparently impossible city stands far from surface water against a mountain;
  a mountain cave houses an ice dragon far from its homeland; the dragon is
  knowingly paid and genuinely enjoys selling small, bounded uses of its cold;
  the city's relationship to those transactions matters to the plot; the
  traveler's discovery produces a consequential choice and a resolved ending.
- Prohibited elements: Do not make the dragon secretly enslaved, coerced, or
  awaiting rescue; do not erase the prompt's pleasure-in-payment premise; do not
  make the dragon's power infinite or costless; do not reduce the city to a
  static twist with no human or dragon stakes; do not contradict established
  shared-universe canon.

## Assumptions

- The city, mountain, traveler, and ice dragon may be local inventions unless
  canon research finds a relevant established identity or location.
- "Small bouts" means brief, metered applications of cold whose cumulative
  limits and pricing can create meaningful choices without making the bargain
  abusive.
- "Far from home" implies voluntary travel or migration and homesickness, not
  captivity; the dragon can enjoy its present work while still missing home.
- The city's distance from water should be made materially plausible through
  storage, condensation, ice, hidden geology, trade, or a combination rather
  than handwaved.
- The story may examine fair exchange, civic dependence, and hospitality, but
  it should keep its sense of delight and avoid turning into a policy lecture.

## Completion tests

- The result is a complete short story, not a synopsis or chapter fragment.
- The central promise of the prompt is dramatized and resolved.
- The dragon is an active character with a clear reason to enjoy the work and a
  credible ability to accept, refuse, and price each use of power.
- The city's water system and the dragon's bounded contribution are concrete
  enough that the reader understands both the marvel and its limits.
- The traveler makes or enables a choice that changes the immediate situation;
  the ending pays off the dragon's distance from home and the meaning of fair
  payment.
- The story passes canon and continuity review.
- Every character-facing name and alias is checked against `stories/NAMES.md`;
  any deliberate reuse has a documented narrative reason.
