# Story plan

## Premise and promise

After days alone in the desert, a route surveyor reaches a mountainside city
whose drinking water depends partly on six precisely metered flashes of cold
sold each day by a homesick ice dragon; when a sandstorm breaks the meter, the
traveler must decide whether to sacrifice the instrument that makes continued
travel possible—and whether to let both the dragon and the city pay a fair
price for it.

The story promises the tactile wonder of an impossible desert waterworks, a
funny and fully agentic dragon who takes real pleasure in invoices and small
payments, and an emotional turn in which payment becomes a form of mutual
recognition rather than a stain on generosity.

Central dramatic question: Can Tair accept that fair exchange may connect
people more honestly than either suspicion or self-erasing sacrifice?

## Story controls

- Working title: *A Little Winter for Sale*
- Length target: approximately 3,400 words; acceptable range 2,500–4,000.
- POV and tense: First-person past, tightly limited to Tair Ked.
- Tone and genre: Wonder-forward secondary-world fantasy; dry humor around
  ledgers and dragon-sized commerce; grounded dehydration and civic stakes;
  warm rather than sentimental resolution.
- Content boundary: Teen / PG. Dehydration, heat stress, and infrastructure
  danger may be vivid but non-graphic. No sexual content or graphic violence.
- Scope: One arrival, one civic system, one cave encounter, one mechanical
  emergency, one decisive bargain, and one northbound promise. No subplot or
  wider cosmology explanation.

## Character engine

- **Tair Ked / Tair — protagonist and narrator.** A solitary desert route
  surveyor who has spent five years engraving bearings into a finely balanced
  brass-and-silver sunwheel. Immediate desire: water, shade, and enough
  information to continue surveying the western salt road. Pressure: Tair
  arrives nearly dry; the approaching glasswind will close the desert; damage
  to the city's waterworks makes the sunwheel the only immediately available
  regulator stock. Blind spot: Tair equates owing anyone with surrender and
  imagines an unpriced sacrifice is morally cleaner than a negotiated exchange.
  Movement: from suspicion of dependence and compulsive self-sufficiency to
  accepting both help and compensation without treating either as ownership.
- **Urumet — the ice dragon.** A slate-blue, hoarfrost-edged dragon who migrated
  from an unnamed northern ice shelf for reasons kept personal and local. It
  sells exactly six short “frost strokes” per day, enjoys bargaining, stamps
  receipts with one claw, and uses its earnings to buy pepper sweets, polished
  metal, letters, and northbound freight. It is homesick without wishing to be
  rescued or to abandon work it enjoys. Desire: keep a safe, chosen, correctly
  paid business and make the distant north reachable by trade. Limit: after six
  strokes its internal heat rises and it must recover overnight; it will not
  exceed that limit or discharge into an unmetered exchanger.
- **Yara Mesk / Yara — keeper of Qarsis's water accounts.** Practical,
  unsentimental, and generous in ways that remain carefully recorded. She gives
  Tair an emergency cup on civic credit, explains the city's mixed water
  system, and knows how to adapt the sunwheel only with Tair's informed consent.
  Desire: preserve an equitable twelve-litre daily ration for roughly five
  hundred residents without stealing labor or taking unsafe shortcuts.
- **Opposition.** No villain. The opposing forces are desert heat, a glasswind
  that disables the solar still field, a shattered frost regulator, Urumet's
  non-negotiable physical and contractual limits, Tair's attachment to the
  sunwheel, and Tair's mistaken belief that accepting payment cheapens aid.

## Causal arc

1. **Beginning state:** After four days beyond the last mapped cistern, Tair has
   only a mouthful of hot water, a prized sunwheel, and a stubborn refusal to
   turn back. The sight of Qarsis against the Black Shoulder seems impossible
   because the map marks no river, spring, or lake.
2. **Inciting disruption:** Yara gives Tair one measured cup before asking for
   payment and records it as recoverable civic hospitality. Tair, uncomfortable
   with owing strangers, follows the cold water channels uphill to discover who
   or what makes the city possible.
3. **First turn:** In the Coldmouth cave, Tair expects captivity or a hidden
   civic theft and instead meets Urumet gleefully selling a precisely timed
   frost stroke, demanding a stamped receipt, and refusing a buyer who wants an
   unsafe seventh. Tair's moral story about dependence no longer fits.
4. **Escalation:** The glasswind arrives early, buries the solar still field,
   and sends a stone through the frost regulator. The city has enough cistern
   water for one tightly rationed day if Urumet can safely deliver its final two
   contracted strokes; without the regulator, the brine exchanger could burst
   and waste both cold and water.
5. **Crisis:** The replacement regulator is twelve caravan-days away. Yara
   recognizes that the calibrated brass-and-silver heart of Tair's sunwheel can
   be cut into a new metering tongue. Doing so destroys the instrument on which
   Tair's livelihood and onward journey depend. Tair offers it free in order to
   turn the loss into heroic self-sufficiency, but Urumet refuses that framing:
   the city may buy the tool, and Urumet may separately hire Tair's skill.
6. **Climax:** Tair chooses to let Yara cut the sunwheel and, harder, names an
   honest replacement price. Yara rebuilds the regulator; Urumet tests it,
   freely accepts the two already contracted jobs, and delivers no more than
   the agreed strokes. The exchanger produces the missing potable water without
   exceeding the dragon's limit.
7. **Aftermath:** Qarsis pays Tair enough for a new instrument when metal trade
   resumes, while Urumet pays a separate first installment for a northern-route
   survey and letter delivery. Tair leaves after the storm headed toward snow,
   not as rescuer or debtor but as another paid traveler carrying one small
   piece of the dragon's home closer.

## Setup and payoff

- Tair sights the forming glasswind in Scene 1; it disables the still field and
  breaks the regulator in Scene 4 rather than appearing as a surprise solution
  or problem.
- The sunwheel is introduced as calibrated brass-and-silver, physically handled
  often, and identified as Tair's livelihood in Scene 1; its bimetal heart
  becomes the only repair stock in Scene 4 and the object of the climax.
- Yara's ledgered cup initially feels like an insult or chain to Tair; the
  dragon's joyful receipts complicate that belief; the final honest purchase
  and route commission transform written debt into reciprocal obligation.
- Urumet's six-stroke limit is demonstrated before the emergency by its refusal
  of a seventh job. The climax honors rather than overrides that boundary.
- The pepper sweets establish that Urumet likes choosing how to spend small
  earnings. The northbound freight entry seeds a larger desire: commerce that
  crosses the distance to home.
- The city water figures recur in action: around five hundred residents receive
  twelve litres per day; solar stills and rare runoff normally supply roughly
  half; six four-count frost strokes freeze-separate about four hundred litres
  of potable water each from the brine exchanger, supplying roughly 2,400
  litres; cistern carryover closes ordinary variance. After the still field is
  buried, the final two strokes provide about 800 litres—enough, with the
  emergency reserve and reduced ration, to bridge the repair day. Keep figures
  approximate in dialogue and exact only where the ledger makes clarity useful.
- Tair is not rewarded by Urumet secretly wanting rescue or return. Urumet
  remains happily in business and commissions a route that lets letters,
  freight, and perhaps future visits cross the distance voluntarily.

## Canon constraints

- Treat Qarsis, the Black Shoulder, the Coldmouth, its brine seep, the
  glasswind, the water offices, local currency, Urumet, and Urumet's northern
  origin as narrowly local inventions in an unnamed era.
- Do not declare dragons a universal species with shared biology, culture, or
  magic. Claims about cold, recovery, preferences, and migration apply to
  Urumet only, or are explicitly presented as Urumet's own account.
- Urumet is the only living initiator of the cold. The regulator and exchanger
  measure, direct, and use that supplied cold; they are nonliving and do not
  cast, decide, or initiate.
- Payment does not fuel magic. It is compensation Urumet values and enjoys.
  Physical capacity and overnight recovery define the six-stroke limit.
- Names, receipts, contracts, coins, consent, need, and pleasure have no
  universal binding force. Their importance is civic, ethical, and personal.
- Preserve Urumet's full agency: it sets prices, chooses work, refuses unsafe
  requests, keeps earnings, and enjoys its current occupation. Homesickness
  does not imply captivity or a demand for rescue.
- Do not infer connections to any candidate-story dragon, city, sovereign,
  magical system, artifact, or faction. Do not name Ravel, Earth, the Glass-Sea
  era, the Glass Gate, or a Galactic Cycle.
- Maintain first-person past tense, prompt elements, accessible tone, and
  non-graphic danger.

## Proposed inventions

- **Qarsis:** a local city of roughly five hundred people built against a dark
  mountain called the Black Shoulder, far from potable surface water.
- **Coldmouth:** the local descriptive name of Urumet's cave and the civic
  freeze-separation works built at its mouth.
- **Mixed water system:** solar stills, rare runoff storage, a deep brine seep,
  cistern carryover, and Urumet's bounded cold together sustain the city; no
  component is an infinite source.
- **Urumet's frost strokes:** six four-count discharges per day, each able to
  chill one exchanger batch and yield about four hundred litres of potable
  water; after six, Urumet must shed accumulated heat and recover overnight.
- **Frost regulator and exchanger:** nonliving bimetal and copper
  infrastructure that meters and directs Urumet's chosen cold into brine
  freeze-separation. It stores no agency and cannot initiate magic.
- **Glasswind:** a severe local sandstorm whose windborne glassy grit can bury
  stills and damage exposed mechanisms.
- **Civic exchange practice:** emergency water may be issued on recoverable
  hospitality credit; equipment, labor, and dragon-cold are purchased by
  explicit agreement. Exact coin names are unnecessary; use plain local
  measures and small coins.
- **Tair's northbound commission:** Urumet remains in Qarsis but hires Tair to
  survey a route for letters and freight toward its unnamed northern home.

All inventions remain proposed production material until a final reviewed
canon delta is explicitly promoted.

## Failure modes to watch

- Do not make Tair correct about hidden enslavement, free Urumet, expose a
  secret prison, or otherwise negate the prompt's enjoyable paid-work premise.
- Do not let higher payment create extra cold or let emotional urgency erase
  Urumet's demonstrated six-stroke boundary.
- Do not make Qarsis depend on dragon power alone. Preserve the mixed system,
  approximate quantities, stored reserve, reduced emergency ration, and
  one-day bridge so the water logic survives scrutiny.
- Do not turn Tair into the only competent person. Yara identifies, cuts, fits,
  and calibrates the repair; Tair contributes uniquely suitable property and
  informed consent; Urumet tests and operates its own interface.
- Do not frame the emergency as permission to seize a traveler's tool or a
  dragon's labor. The climax depends on three explicit agreements.
- Do not make Tair's free offer the morally superior option. The internal
  resolution is naming a fair cost and accepting fair payment.
- Do not over-explain the world, dragon origins, freeze physics, or accounting.
  Let concrete action, sensory detail, and brief ledger dialogue carry them.
- Do not introduce another named person, nickname, title-as-name, named animal,
  or personified machine without rerunning name validation before drafting.
- Do not call Urumet “Majesty,” shorten it to `Uru`, or use any registered
  dragon title even as a joke.
- Do not imply the route commission takes Urumet home at once. It brings home
  within chosen commercial reach while preserving the dragon's satisfying
  present life and occupation.
