# Outline

## Story

- Proposed reader-facing title: *Roads Under Impossible Stars*.
- Premise and central promise: Estranged childhood best friends Eden Fong and Paloma Reyes take the failing hatchback of their teenage summers across an unnamed desert before Eden begins college and Paloma leaves for a yearlong conservation job. Eden treats the promised trip as one last chance to restore their old closeness; Paloma intends to finish it honestly and say goodbye. After their first direct argument, a meteor crosses a sky crowded with too many stars, Eden wishes they could go back, and the car snaps roughly six minutes into the past. She can repeat the wish while the meteor's scar remains, but each reset replaces the surrounding desert with a different road and deposits abandoned structures where there had been sand. Eden remembers every version, while Paloma increasingly remembers stray replies and feelings from conversations that no longer occurred. Resolve the practical danger and emotional promise when Eden stops using repetition to engineer Paloma's answer, lets the final chance to rewind expire, and accepts that any friendship they keep must be changed and mutually chosen rather than restored.
- Intended reader experience: Begin with recognizable end-of-friendship discomfort inside a hot, rattling car, then turn nostalgia into quiet spatial dread. Each apparent conversational improvement should cost the pair certainty about the road and about whose memories count. The impossible desert should remain beautiful, materially strange, and lonely rather than becoming a lore puzzle. Deliver relief through one unedited exchange and a plausible predawn exit, with tenderness present but no guarantee that distance will be easy or that they will remain “best friends.”
- Generating force:
  - Mode: Plot-led asymmetric recurrence. The first minutes recur with Eden making increasingly precise conversational edits, but the returns shorten as Paloma's memory fragments accumulate and the geography becomes less navigable. After the decisive refusal to reset, the story moves only forward through the current altered landscape.
  - Focal attachment or contradiction: Eden loves Paloma and the shared rituals by which they made an unstable adolescence feel continuous, yet she has mistaken repetition for care. When Paloma chose a future outside their joint plan, Eden withdrew rather than admit she felt abandoned; now she wants the old ease without first owning the silence that damaged it.
  - Plot-led wants and useful limit, if applicable: Eden immediately wants to keep the last trip pleasant long enough to persuade Paloma that nothing essential has changed. She also wants to keep them physically safe and hear what Paloma actually wants, even if those goods require her to relinquish the reset. Her useful flaw is compulsive revision: she treats conflict as a wording error she can edit instead of a difference another person is entitled to hold. Paloma wants to complete the trip without another fight, state that she is still leaving, and preserve the possibility of some future connection without promising the former friendship intact. Her limit is conflict avoidance—she delayed the honest conversation until resentment made her blunt, and the leaking memories then make her distrust her own sequence of events.
- Counterforce or complication:
  - Source: No antagonist. The meteor-bounded reset preserves Eden, Paloma, the car's mileage, fuel loss, and engine heat while replaying only about six minutes of personal time and reselecting the exterior route. Repeated use moves them farther from the mapped highway: pavement forks, signs contradict the paper itinerary, and an empty motor court and survey station gather into a roadside that did not exist before. The beat-up car cannot absorb indefinite detours or overheating.
  - Credible inside logic, if a person: Paloma's resistance protects two valuable things: her authority over what she said and her need to leave without performing hatred or permanent devotion. She does not know Eden initiated the resets at first. Her impossible recollections arrive as sensory and emotional shards rather than complete exposition, so confusion, suspicion, and anger are proportionate rather than instantly omniscient.
  - Pressure or change created: Every reset offers Eden a cleaner approach to the argument and a worse material situation. Paloma begins answering lines Eden has not said in the current sequence, then recognizes that Eden is steering around objections she remembers making. A necessary rewind prevents a road accident, proving that the power can protect them, but the same usefulness makes Eden's emotional misuse harder to excuse.
- Narrative design:
  - POV, tense, distance, and limitation: Close first-person present through Eden. Her attention tracks Paloma's pauses, the old itinerary, dashboard warnings, and possible replacement lines; she is precise about what she changes but initially euphemistic about why they became estranged. Paloma stays exterior, with her independent objective and accumulating memories legible through mismatched answers, nausea, anger, and practical decisions. The reader never receives an authoritative view of the anomaly beyond what the pair can test.
  - Time shape and compression: Open during the final hour before the meteor. Run four resets of roughly six minutes, compressing repeated setup into changed details and allowing each loop less shared text. A fifth, safety-driven reset carries the story into one continuous stretch through the meteor scar's disappearance and the pair's predawn return to an ordinary highway. Subjective elapsed time is under two hours; the dashboard repeatedly retreats while the odometer, fuel gauge, and temperature needle advance.
  - Information and reveal strategy: Establish the estrangement through failed road-trip rituals and Paloma's imminent departure, but withhold Eden's months of retaliatory silence until Paloma names it across overlapping versions. Show the first reset in full so causality is clear. Thereafter use a repeated opening sound—the loose fan belt's squeal—and one repeated sentence as bearings, then foreground differences. Let Eden infer the trigger after her second spoken wish, and show its limits materially: the rewind is shorter each time, the meteor scar loses brightness, and the car's wear persists. Paloma first recalls an emotion, then a phrase, then a linked exchange; do not define these as proof of persistent timelines, copied people, or universal memory.
  - Governing movement: Force an old ritual; hear Paloma refuse nostalgia; quarrel about leaving; see the meteor; wish; return; improve the phrasing; find a road that was not there; wish again; pass an abandoned survey station; hear Paloma answer an unsaid accusation; conceal the reset; attempt a gentler version; reach an empty motor court; learn that fuel and heat persist; have Paloma name Eden's erased words; take the wrong fork while arguing; spend a rewind to avoid a washed-out culvert; stop the car; face Paloma's cross-sequence memory; begin the final wish and choose not to finish it; tell the unedited truth; wait until the meteor scar dies; cool the engine; take the road that currently exists; regain the highway without restoring the former night.
- Speculative surplus: Each changed road carries a different weak AM station despite the broken antenna: irrigation prices, a school closure, livestock notices, and weather for unnamed terrain that is absent outside the windows. The mundane broadcasts give each landscape cultural texture and warn that a reset occurred, but provide no route, explanation, prophecy, or emotional verdict.
- Structural distinction: A roadside palimpsest whose returns progressively lose repetition. The loops do not form a branching solution tree or accumulate toward public proof; they expose the cost of Eden's editing, then stop well before the story does. The decisive encounter remains between two people inside a parked car in the courtyard of an impossible, abandoned motor court. No crowd, expert, institution, technical proof campaign, repaired record, or collective conversion follows.
- Decisive turn, deepening, or recognition: After Eden uses a reset to keep the hatchback from striking a washed-out culvert, the new road ends in the motor court's cracked courtyard and the engine overheats. Paloma recalls enough incompatible fragments to reconstruct Eden's agency: apologies that were withdrawn, questions rerouted before she could answer, and the sensation of being made to repeat pain for a better result. She says the injury is not merely that Eden vanished for months; Eden is now refusing to let even this goodbye belong to both of them. The meteor scar still permits one brief return. Eden starts the trigger phrase, tempted to prevent Paloma from reaching that conclusion, but stops before completing it. With no revision available, she admits that her silence was punishment, not confusion, and asks what Paloma wants now rather than what answer will save the old friendship.
- Aftereffect or live uncertainty: Paloma does not retract her departure or promise permanence. She says she wanted Eden to object honestly months ago, not make her leaving the cause of every later choice. Eden accepts that some remembered hurt comes from conversations she technically erased but Paloma still lived enough to be changed by. They cool the engine with dwindling water, wait until the crowded stars become ordinary, and follow the current service road until it meets a recognizable highway before dawn. The anomalous motor court remains behind them, the car retains the extra miles and damage, and Paloma retains fragments whose source neither can prove. She agrees only to one call after her first week away; what relationship follows is open.
- Opening / ending relation: Open with Eden insisting on an outdated paper itinerary and a familiar question game because repeating the old trip is easier than asking what this one means. End on the ordinary highway with Paloma driving, Eden monitoring the damaged temperature gauge, and both ignoring the obsolete route in favor of the road physically ahead. Keep the final exchange practical—when and whether to make that first call—rather than turning the road, meteor, or car into a declared symbol.

## Beats

1. In the overheating hatchback, Eden pushes the old itinerary, snacks, and question game while Paloma gives minimal answers. Establish their former intimacy through shorthand that no longer lands. Paloma confirms she leaves for the conservation job in days and says the trip cannot make them sixteen again.
2. The argument finally becomes direct: Paloma names Eden's months of silence after hearing about the job; Eden frames the job as abandonment. Beneath an unnaturally dense star field, the meteor burns low and long. Eden says she wishes they could go back, and the loose fan belt squeals them six minutes into the past.
3. Eden verifies the reset through the clock, repeated dialogue, and her untouched memory. She substitutes humor for accusation and briefly restores their ease, but the expected highway bend becomes a fork beside a sign neither remembers. When the same disagreement resurfaces, she repeats the wish deliberately.
4. The next road passes an abandoned survey station standing where bare sand had been. The radio receives ordinary reports from an unnamed locality. Paloma feels grief before its cause and answers a sentence Eden has not spoken this time. Eden hides what she knows and attempts a more careful apology.
5. Another reset brings an empty motor court and a shorter meteor scar. The dashboard time retreats, but the fuel and heat do not. Paloma repeats two erased phrases back to Eden and asks why the night feels crowded. Eden admits only part of the truth, claiming the changes are saving the conversation.
6. During the argument, the altered road drops toward a washed-out culvert. Eden uses the power without hesitation to prevent the crash. The safety use preserves both girls but leaves them on a still stranger road, with the motor court now enclosing a cracked courtyard and the car steaming to a stop.
7. Parked and unable to flee into another version, Paloma assembles her memory fragments and identifies the violation: Eden keeps treating her responses as disposable drafts. She also names Eden's original silence as chosen punishment. Give Paloma room to remain angry, frightened, and still concerned about the engine; do not reduce her to the correct lesson for Eden.
8. Eden nearly completes the last available wish, then stops. She gives the apology she cannot test first, accepts Paloma's departure, and asks what Paloma wants from the hours that remain. Paloma offers no restoration, only the possibility of one later call and the demand that Eden never decide both sides of it.
9. The meteor scar fades while they let the engine cool. No reset follows. The sky loses its surplus stars, but the motor court and service road remain long enough for them to drive out. At dawn the lane joins a recognizable highway with impossible mileage on the odometer.
10. Paloma takes the wheel while Eden tends the temperature gauge and abandons the old itinerary. Their final practical conversation concerns the uncertain first call after Paloma leaves; the story stops with forward motion, damaged trust, and mutual choice still alive.

## Continuity

- Canon used: The story occupies an unspecified modern-like period of the one physical world without claiming a literal real-world location. Canon permits multiple independent anomalous systems and incomplete character models. Memory effects, crossings, costs, and paradox behavior are mechanism-specific, and personhood and moral consequence remain intact.
- Boundaries and unknowns: Treat the meteor-bounded rewind as a new, local, non-recurring anomaly with only the observed rules above. Do not connect it to an established watch, gate, desert road, named place or character, universal timeline ledger, or artifact. The altered desert does not establish literal alternate worlds, erased or copied people, the meteor's origin, or a general wish-based power. Leave the civil date, exact geography, mechanism, and fate of the abandoned structures unresolved. The prompt remains authoritative if prose needs to compress or alter these advisory beats while preserving the central rewind, changing desert, leaked memories, and resolved relational choice.
