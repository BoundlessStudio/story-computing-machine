# Outline

## Story

- Premise and central promise: Estranged, platonic best friends Keely and Hollis take a failing hatchback across an unnamed desert on their last trip before Keely leaves. Beneath impossible stars, Keely’s wish for their old ease rewinds four minutes. Repetition tempts her to improve route and conversation but reveals the harm of treating Hollis as editable. Resolve when Keely declines the last rewind, choosing one difficult present over magical restoration.
- Focal pressure or attachment: Keely wants the trip to prove the friendship still exists as she remembers it; she anticipates objections and mistakes preparation for care. Hollis wants to finish without pretending their distance is a misunderstanding and protects her right to one unrehearsed response.
- Counterforce or complication: Every rewind breaks route continuity while Hollis accumulates erased speech and recognizes Keely’s revisions. A leaking radiator, low water, poor signal, cold, and uncertain highway bearing make experimentation dangerous. Hollis knows the car and terrain better and can refuse unsafe driving or rehearsal.
- POV, distance, and information limit: Close third past through Keely, who remembers every overwritten interval. Hollis remains external, her residue legible through recognition, questions, work, and refusal. Neither woman learns the anomaly’s cause.
- Governing movement and time shape: One recursive night moves from ordinary driving through four-minute returns, altered junctions, an impossible building, and a dead end to a deliberate halt on stable gravel. Compress repeated ground; emphasize changes in talk, terrain, and leverage. The private decisive action is refusing a better version.
- Speculative rule or ordinary-world constraint: The meteor causes one involuntary reset. Keely alone can then hold the dead dashboard clock’s recessed button and deliberately return exactly four minutes, at most four more times; its display counts down without resetting. Occupants, car, contents, fuel, heat, and damage regain their earlier state and coordinates. Keely keeps full memory and cumulative vertigo. Hollis retains one short verbal fragment per erased interval, without context or images. Ground beneath the car stays supported, but the nearest road beyond a car length changes cumulatively; abandoned structures may replace sand or vanish. Ordinary maps and compass cannot predict this drift.

## Voice

- Narrative texture: Follow Keely’s forward-planning mind through material discrepancies—gauge readings, crumbs, tracks, junction angles—compressing sameness while lingering on what resets cannot restore.
- Conversational texture: Mix route decisions, snack and radiator talk, failed old shorthand, pauses, and occasional explicit feeling; repetitions gain context, not polish.
- Rhetorical ownership: Keely thinks in timelines, alternatives, and anticipated objections. Hollis reasons from present evidence, sequence, and workable limits; her precision is practical, not equally fluent emotional argument.
- Pressure behavior: Keely speeds up and overexplains because she remembers too much. Hollis loses connective language, repeats orphaned fragments, listens selectively, and becomes direct about safety.
- Anti-default: Avoid quotable confession variants or convenient residue exposition; the anomaly is not a relationship therapist.

## Beats

1. On the drive to an old overlook, let stale snacks, a scratched map, the radiator gauge, and disputed music carry the estrangement. Keely wants former ease; Hollis focuses on returning before the car worsens.
2. Impossible stars and a meteor interrupt their stalled talk about separate futures. Keely wishes backward and experiences the first reset. Hollis repeats one stray fragment; the expected side road now forks toward an unfamiliar ridge.
3. Keely tests the interval through mundane car and route talk. The clock reveals four deliberate uses. Her first corrects a missed turn and revises her approach, but an abandoned service building replaces sand; Hollis’s next fragment creates recognition without explanation.
4. Braid two further loops. Keely alternates practical motives—radiator strain, junction checks—with conversational revisions. Damage rewinds, yet roads veer farther off bearing, the building moves, and Hollis catches Keely answering concerns she has not voiced in the retained night.
5. A new road descends toward broken pavement above a dry wash. Hollis stops the car using terrain judgment Keely missed. Keely must explain the bounded reset; Hollis tests only safety-relevant facts. A fragment exposes the rehearsal of her responses.
6. They inventory water, fuel, engine, walking distance, cold, and highway bearing. Hollis refuses another drive or conversational trial. Keely sees each version carrying them laterally from the mapped road; a reset may undo error but cannot promise passage.
7. One use remains when the radiator vents on stable gravel. Keely declines escape from both mechanical trouble and the retained exchange, shuts off the engine, and helps secure the car for a cold wait. The stopping decision does not settle their emotions.
8. Hollis chooses one fragment; Keely supplies only its limited context. They make a practical morning plan and share the remaining food. End before the stars resolve, without friendship pledge, exhaustive reconciliation, or thematic summation.
