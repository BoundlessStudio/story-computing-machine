# Opus review: Roads Under Impossible Stars

Independent audit for a later editing pass. It is not a `review.md` verdict,
does not change canon state, and does not authorize edits by itself.

## Verdict

- Dialogue: REVISE — the chain of phrases Hollis keeps from erased time breaks twice (one was never spoken; one comes from a conversation the reader never sees), and the argument at the crest turns into a volley of thesis lines.
- Prose: PASS — concrete, varied and well paced; only small logic slips and one water-infrastructure policy nit.
- Continuity and prompt: REVISE — every prompt element is delivered, but the first rewind's landing point contradicts itself (cracker, entrance, sealed packet).
- Edit scope: targeted-dialogue
- Existing review.md said: Verdict PASS, "Dialogue: PASS"; disagree — its note that "speech-only residues remain consistent" misses the never-spoken "Don't take it" and the first-return contradiction.

The two voices really are different. Keely plans out loud in timetables and answers objections before they're made; Hollis talks in gauges, ditches and short refusals. The story's best moments come from that mismatch ("Not for this." coming back as Hollis's only residue; "Sounds like me." / "It does."). The failures lie in the mechanism and the rhythm. Hollis's retained phrases are the story's dialogic engine, but her second phrase was never said in the erased loop, and her first comes from a conversation that never appears on the page. Around those problems, talk defaults to rebuttal: Hollis corrects Keely's wording, Keely corrects back, and at the crest the argument hardens into four even one-liners that restate what "That isn't better." had already landed. The edit pass should first make the rewind and retained-phrase chain consistent, then cut the duplicate thesis lines and the two inert precision volleys, keeping Hollis terse and practical and Keely over-explaining.

## Edit guardrails

- Prompt (acceptance authority; the rewrite request and `Craft profile: prospective-2026-08-21` are active): estranged best friends, a beat-up car, the desert, a last road trip before their lives separate; a sky crowded with impossible stars; a meteor; the girl who wished to go back (Keely) rewinds a few minutes at a time; each rewind changes the desert (roads lead elsewhere, an abandoned building appears where there was sand), and her friend remembers pieces of conversations that never happened. Complete story, 2,500–4,000 words (currently about 3,400), broadly accessible.
- Keep the story's own rules consistent through every edit: returns are exactly four minutes; the meteor gives one return, then the clock counts 4 → 1; one push remains unused at the end; car, fuel, heat, contents and damage are restored; Keely keeps all erased time plus worsening vertigo; Hollis keeps one short fragment of speech per erased interval, words only, no images; the patch under the car stays while the road beyond about a car length changes.
- Universe: `rules.md` "Living agency and magic" (LOCKED) says only life can use magic. Keep Keely (her wish, her finger on the button) as the agent and the clock as a focus and counter; don't let the clock act on its own. `style-guide.md` "Personhood and moral consequence" (LOCKED): no explanation that reduces the night to a malfunction. Give no origin for the meteor or the stars.
- Close third person through Keely; Hollis stays external and is read through her words, work and refusals.
- Don't flatten the voices: Keely's rehearsed, clause-heavy over-explaining is a character trait (L249 and L259 show her editing her own lines), and Hollis's clipped, evidence-first speech is hers. Don't make Keely terser or Hollis more eloquent.
- The ending stays open on the friendship's future: no pledge, no thesis line, and the clock still showing 1.

## Dialogue findings

### D1 · Blocking · Hollis's second fragment was never spoken

- Where: L111–L127 (erased fork loop), L137–L141 (after the first button press), L277–L285 (stopped before the crest)
- Text:
> "Don't," Hollis said.
> Hollis swallowed. "Don't take it."
> "Take what?"
> "Did I tell you not to take a road?"
- Problem: Ground/knowledge. The story's rule (stated at L321 and followed by every other fragment) is that Hollis keeps one piece of speech from the erased minutes. In the erased fork loop (L103–L127) Hollis says only "Was that a fork?", "Don't," "When I have room." and "Wait… I'm stopping." Nobody says "Don't take it." Keely remembers every erased second, so her genuine "Take what?" tells the reader she doesn't recognize the line either, and the fragment has no source. At the crest Hollis builds on it ("Did I tell you not to take a road?"), and Keely's "You don't remember that." implies it happened, although Hollis chose the right branch herself (L119) and never warned Keely off a road.
- Fix: Make the fragment words Hollis actually spoke in the erased loop, and make Keely's reply show that she recognizes them. The smallest route is to expand Hollis's "Don't," at L113 (said as Keely reaches toward the clock) into a short phrase and have Hollis come back with exactly those words at L137. Replace "Take what?" with a reaction that shows Keely knows when it was said (a glance at the clock, a flinch, silence), then adjust L281 to match. Keep "You don't remember that." / "I have the words." Illustrative only: "Don't touch it." at L113 and L137, and at L281 "Did I tell you not to touch that?"

### D2 · Major · "Every six weeks" comes from a conversation the reader never sees

- Where: L41 (Keely's private list), L59–L67 (pre-meteor drive), L93–L101 (first return), L235–L247 (walking-speed track)
- Text:
> Hollis drove with one hand at twelve o'clock. "Every six weeks," she said.
> "Before the first reset. You said visits weren't the same as telling you I applied.
- Problem: Ground/referent. The first fragment should be the reader's first proof that Hollis keeps words from erased time. The only earlier "every six weeks" is Keely's unspoken calendar at L41, though, so L93 reads as Hollis voicing Keely's private thought. The real source (Keely offering visits, Hollis's answer about the application, "Hollis's long silence and the exact place her voice had thinned" at L401) is never shown. The pre-meteor scene (L43–L77) runs as continuous talk about turns, the gauge and going back, and its only compression (L61) gives no hint of it. When Keely says "Before the first reset", the reader has nothing to remember, so the fragment and Keely's attempt to repair the conversation arrive as report rather than something the reader witnessed.
- Fix: Plant the talk inside the erased window, before the sky scene. The natural spot is the drive to "the next wide patch" (L61), which the story already compresses. Two to four lines are enough: Keely says the six-weeks plan aloud, and Hollis answers briefly or goes quiet. Hold back most of Hollis's side (L247, L401) for the later retelling so those reveals still land. Keep the planted talk short enough to fit the 11:38–11:42 window (see C1).

### D3 · Major · Crest argument turns into a volley of thesis lines

- Where: L287–L297, stopped before the crest, just before the third press
- Text:
> "You keep answering things I haven't said."
> "Because I'm the only one with all of it."
> "You're changing your answers."
> "I'm using what I know."
- Problem: Philosopher plus Tennis Match at the decisive exchange. L287 has already shown Keely answering an accusation before it's made. The two women then trade four evenly weighted one-liners that state the story's argument outright (Keely manages Hollis with knowledge Hollis doesn't have; Hollis objects). The same point already landed at L253–L257 ("No. I know what you said." / "That isn't better."), so the reader gets it twice, the second time as abstract summary. Hollis is angry and Keely is frightened and nauseous, yet both stay perfectly composed and symmetrical.
- Fix: Keep "You keep answering things I haven't said." and the "I'm using what I know." / "On me." pair; cut L291 and L293. If Keely needs more room, let her run on and over-explain (her established way of behaving under pressure) until Hollis cuts in with "On me.", instead of giving her another neat counter. Don't add a replacement aphorism for either speaker.

### D4 · Major · Rebuttal as the default rhythm, with two inert precision volleys

- Where: L323–L329 (after the rules), L347–L353 (after the inventory); the pattern also runs through L27, L67, L155, L159–L161, L225–L229, L279, L357–L361
- Text:
> "You said no pictures."
> "I'm checking."
> "Map south."
> "The compass doesn't say there's a road there."
- Problem: Correction ping-pong. After the opening banter, nearly every exchange follows one shape: Keely proposes, Hollis corrects her wording, and Keely sometimes corrects back. Several of these carry the conflict and earn their place (L225–L229, L253–L257, L357–L361). Two change nothing. At L323–L329, four lines confirm "words only", which Hollis herself said at L141 and L213. At L347–L353, Keely argues from the compass moments after telling Hollis it "tells us where this road points now, but not where the next one will be" (L333), and Hollis corrects "south" to "map south". With rebuttal as the constant rhythm, the friendship has a single register between the opening joke and the final exchange.
- Fix: Reduce L323–L329 to one question and one answer. Cut L347–L353, or turn it into action: Hollis keeps working and doesn't take up the point. When revising D1–D3, don't add new word-level corrections; where a reply is needed, prefer a plain answer, a non-answer or an act. Keep L225–L229 and L253–L257 as the story's sharp corrections; they work because they're rare.

### D5 · Minor · Loose uptake and referents

- Where: L149–L153 (after the first press), L209–L213 (after the second press), L243–L245 (six-weeks repair), L381 (morning plan)
- Text:
> "I needed to know if it was the same as before. The first time just happened."
> "There's another one, isn't there?" Hollis asked.
> "One phrase."
> If the track is gone, we stay with the car and use the mirror to signal.
- Problem: Ground/uptake. Hollis never saw the meteor or knew about a first return (for her there was only the unexplained "Every six weeks"), yet she lets "the first time" and later "the first reset" pass without a question ("And now?", "Did I say that?"). A listener would ask what first time. At L209–L211, "another one" (another reset, or another phrase?) gets "One phrase.", which answers a question Hollis didn't quite ask. In the morning plan "the track" is already gone (L313: "Behind them, the track they had just climbed did not exist."), and "the highest point" has no clear referent.
- Fix: At L151 either give Hollis one line asking about the first time or cut "The first time just happened." Make Keely's L211 a plain yes. In L381, name what they'll actually try at first light and drop the conditional about a track that no longer exists.

## Prose findings

### P1 · Minor · Small logic and verb slips

- Where: L11 (opening), L23 (the map), L369 (Keely at the clock), L379 (camp)
- Text:
> as if the hatchback might misbehave out of embarrassment if she watched it closely enough
> Keely had printed a map because Hollis's phone was out of storage and hers had been searching for service for an hour
> It would also take away the inventory and Hollis's condition.
> Hollis tore the striped candy brick in half with the jack handle.
- Problem: L11 inverts its own logic: Hollis watches the car so it won't misbehave, but the sentence says scrutiny would make it misbehave. L23 gives a reason for printing the map (an hour without service) that could only arise mid-trip, after the printing; "Keely's different-colored pen" should be plural. At L369, "Hollis's condition" first reads as her physical state rather than her demand "You don't push it without asking." At L379, "tore… with the jack handle" doesn't fit the tool, and the next sentence says it "broke".
- Fix: "too embarrassed to misbehave"; make the printed map a pre-trip precaution, or reword so the missing signal is a present fact; "pens"; "Hollis's one condition" or "the promise Hollis had asked for"; "broke" or "cracked" at L379.

### P2 · Minor · Water-infrastructure texture (policy)

- Where: L165 and L179, the abandoned service building
- Text:
> Maybe a landline, tools, old water tank
> A drainage channel yawned underneath, wider than the tires.
- Problem: `universe/style-guide.md` "No default water infrastructure" (CANON) names drainage and water supply, and the prompt asks for neither. The drainage channel is the hazard at the building; the water tank is incidental texture.
- Fix: Replace the channel with a hazard that belongs to an abandoned service station, such as a collapsed underground fuel-tank vault or an old service pit under the crust of sand. Replace "old water tank" with something else Keely might hope to find (coolant on a shelf, a spare hose). The car's radiator, the water jugs and the dry wash are not infrastructure and stay.

## Continuity and prompt findings

### C1 · Blocking · The first return lands in two places at once

- Where: L43–L61 (pre-meteor drive), L89 (first return), L103 (the fork appears), L231 (after the second press)
- Text:
> A whole cracker rested on her tongue, dry and square, though she had swallowed it beside the shoulder.
> Ahead, the dirt entrance appeared after the cattle guard.
> She took a cracker for the salt and found the packet sealed. Even the tear had been taken back.
- Problem: The rule (L321) is that the car and its contents return to their earlier state and position. Keely opened the crackers (L57) after they had passed the entrance, and the heater fan went off at the turnaround (L61). So the cracker in her mouth and the blasting heater at L89 place the first return after the entrance, with the car heading away from it. Yet at L103 the entrance is "ahead" and "after the cattle guard", which puts the car before it. Later returns land on the asphalt at the fork, after that first landing, so the packet can't be sealed at L231; at the first landing Keely already had a cracker in her mouth. The story invites readers to track the rewinds closely, and those readers can't place the car and see the rule break.
- Fix: Choose one landing point and make all three details agree. The fewest edits: land the first return just before the cattle guard. At L89, replace the cracker-on-her-tongue detail with the crackers back in the cup holder, sealed, even though she had eaten from them on the shoulder. L103 and L231 can then stand (L231 may read better as "still sealed"). Keep the 11:38 / 11:42 times, and make sure the planted six-weeks talk (D2) fits inside that window.

### C2 · Minor · Inventory and late staging slips

- Where: L19 / L345 (the jack); L313, L345, L355, L363 (moving the car); L363–L371 (who is where)
- Text:
> a jack that Hollis trusted, and a spare tire she did not
> The radiator reservoir was low enough that Hollis would not restart until the engine cooled.
> Hollis restarted. Keely got out and held the flashlight low.
- Problem: The jack Hollis trusted becomes "a weak jack" in the inventory. Hollis's rule against restarting breaks a few paragraphs later with no change in the engine and no reason the car must move "now". L355 has them moving it "off the broken pavement", but L313 put the car on the intact old asphalt. Both women are already out on the gravel fan when "Keely got out". Then Keely is at the dashboard clock and the hood release while Hollis stands by the hood, and neither move is shown.
- Fix: Make the jack consistent (trusted throughout, or doubtful from L19). Give the move a one-clause reason that squares with L345, and name the surface the car leaves. Replace "got out" with a return to the car, or cut it, and put Keely back in the passenger seat before L367.
