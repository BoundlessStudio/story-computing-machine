# Opus review: The Next Train

Independent audit for a later editing pass. It is not a `review.md` verdict,
does not change canon state, and does not authorize edits by itself.

## Verdict

- Dialogue: REVISE — the goddess's turning question on the train (L331) depends on a conversation that never happened, and the switch to the Fates' names leaves most of the platform confrontation unattributable; the rest of the dialogue works.
- Prose: PASS — clean, varied, comic first-person narration; only small referent and staging slips.
- Continuity and prompt: PASS — delivers the missed train, the shared takeaway, three punks, gods arriving by subway in the evening, and a resolution, at about 3,350 words; two small object and phone slips.
- Edit scope: targeted-dialogue
- Existing review.md said: Verdict PASS, "Dialogue: PASS"; disagree on dialogue. It missed the knowledge break at L331 and the unmapped names.

This is one of the stronger dialogue stories. It does not show the collection's usual problem of everyone speaking in the same voice. Lachesis is earnest and exposed, Atropos is deadpan and practical, Clotho performs and bluffs, the goddess is formal and hurt, the god is a showman, and the narrator is wry and embellishes. The jokes also set up later payoffs: the drummer, the tea, the small choir, and the eleven months. The failures are local but real. The goddess thaws because of a question she has no basis to ask, and a reader cannot tell which Fate is which during the main confrontation. The edit pass should fix those two places with the least wording possible and leave the comic rhythm alone.

## Edit guardrails

- Prompt: first-person narrator who misses a train and shares takeaway with three punks on the platform. By evening, gods take the subway to see who has earned the Fates' attention "after all these years" (the story's forty-three years). The ending must stay complete. Keep it broadly accessible and non-graphic, with humor and wonder. Target 2,500–4,000 words (currently about 3,350). Craft profile prospective-2026-08-23.
- Keep each Fate's accessory: Clotho's visor (the reference's VR headset), Lachesis's headphones, and Atropos's mask. The instruction that the Fates must not appear apart applies to the cover image. The prose's brief platform split, followed by the photo of all three together at the concert, is fine, but do not add further separations.
- Universe: a magic-active modernity with living gods who have worship, power, and authority (Divinity, CANON), shown through the altar, the paid readings, the ring blessing, and the revels posters. Atropos's foresight stays deliberate, effortful, and brief. Add no universal fate mechanism and no Greek genealogy.
- Setup/payoff chains that must survive:
  - drummer (L65–L71) → "Whether the band had a drummer." / "It doesn't." (L189–L193)
  - tea (L111–L125) → "For the tea." (L227)
  - photo (L141) → gods arrive
  - small choir (L221) → nine hours (L347) → "Did you reach it?" / "No." (L359–L361)
  - eleven months (L243) → the final reveal
  - Atropos's bag → "Keep the bag till next time."
- Platform geography: an island platform. The bench faces the westbound track, the eastbound track is on the other side, and the cellar is two stops east and walkable. The gods can't follow because nobody told them where the venue is.
- Unequal fluency and the comic toppers are earned by the prompt's request for humor. Do not flatten them into plain exchanges.

## Dialogue findings

### D1 · Blocking · Goddess asks about talk that never happened

- Where: L331–L335, train carriage, after the goddess moves to the seat opposite
- Text:
> "What did they say about my singers?"
> I told her exactly what Lachesis had said.
> "The second voice," she said. "That boy sang in my temple."
- Problem: Ground/knowledge and Ground/referent. Nobody on the platform said anything about the goddess's singers. Lachesis only said "We attend yours. At the temple. Quite often." (L201), and the goddess heard that herself. So the question assumes a conversation the reader never saw, and "exactly what Lachesis had said" can't be recovered. It could mean L201, which she already heard, or the band talk at L87–L91, which has nothing to do with her singers. If "my singers" means the band, she is asking before she has any way to know that a former temple singer is in it. L335 is written as her discovering that when she hears him. This is where her anger turns, so the break falls on the emotional hinge of the last act.
- Fix: Base the question on what she actually knows: the Fates skipped her table and her concerts for this cellar band. Let her ask what the band is like, or what they wanted to hear so badly. Have the narrator pass on something the reader saw (Lachesis talking about the band all week, "I wanted you to hear it", no drummer), named in the narration so it can be recovered. Keep L335 as a genuine discovery. Keep L367 "Don't tell them I asked about the song," which needs her question to be about the music. Illustrative only: "This band of theirs. What do they sound like?"

### D2 · Major · Fates' names arrive without a map to the punks

- Where: L167–L227, platform confrontation after the gods arrive
- Text:
> "Clotho," said the goddess. "Lachesis. Atropos."
> He repeated it, getting the middle sound wrong, and Lachesis corrected him.
> Atropos stacked the empty tubs inside the carton and packed it into the shopping bag.
> "It had parts," Atropos said.
- Problem: Attribution. After the goddess names them, the narrator drops Visor, Pink Hair and Blue Hair and uses the names at once. Only Clotho is mapped right away (L175, the visor). Lachesis is identified only at L205 ("looked down at her headphones") and Atropos only at L227 ("For the tea"). For about fifty lines of the story's main exchange, the reader can't tell which Fate corrects the god, packs the rubbish, or says "It had parts". The mythological names don't help, because nothing ties them to the accessories.
- Fix: Map all three once, at L167–L169, in the narrator's voice. For example, the goddess could look at each of them as she says the name, or one clause in L169 could match the names to the visor, the headphones and the beanie. One clear mapping is enough; do not bring the nicknames back throughout. Keep "In the portraits they had considerably less hair dye."

### D3 · Minor · Atropos explains her sight to someone who already knows it

- Where: L229–L231, after "Have they looked at your future?"
- Text:
> "I have to look deliberately. It takes concentration. I don't spend a meal doing it."
- Problem: Exposition ("As you know"). The goddess has been sending the Fates questions for decades (L211–L215), so she knows how their looking works. The first two sentences are for the reader, and L125 plus "About three seconds of it. For the tea." already give the reader the rule. This is also the only point where Atropos stops being terse.
- Fix: Cut "I have to look deliberately. It takes concentration." Keep "I don't spend a meal doing it." as her answer to "Only that?" It shows her priority (the meal) instead of the mechanism, and the god's "Then there must be something about you." still follows from it.

### D4 · Minor · Attribution and tag slips

- Where: L103–L105, shared headphones; L197, first reply to the goddess
- Text:
> Pink Hair took the headphones back and watched her.
> "Play that bit again."
> "It's a concert," Lachesis explained. "We have tickets."
- Problem: Attribution. "Play that bit again." comes right after a paragraph whose subject is Pink Hair, so it reads as her line. It is Visor reacting to the chorus. "explained" is a said-bookism that labels what the line does.
- Fix: Give L105 a Visor beat or tag, or join it to L101 after "Oh,". Change "explained" to "said", or replace it with a beat.

## Prose findings

### P1 · Minor · Atropos's mask used before it is introduced

- Where: L17, the three punks introduced; L31
- Text:
> The third, blue-haired under a knitted beanie, took her backpack off the bench.
> The blue-haired one lowered her mask.
- Problem: The introduction sets up the visor and the headphones but not the mask, so "lowered her mask" comes out of nowhere. These three accessories are how the reader, and later the cover, tells the Fates apart.
- Fix: Add the mask, over her nose and mouth, to the L17 description. A few words are enough.

### P2 · Minor · Muddy referents and staging in narration

- Where: L57 (teasing), L99 (headphones), L223 (Lachesis checks the time), L283 (Clotho's farewell), L285 (Fates choose to walk), L295 (waving from the carriage)
- Text:
> There are people who let you recover from that sort of thing, and people who begin telling you what it looked like.
> I stopped smiling for the benefit of the woman beside me and listened.
> leaving now would involve admitting how long we should already have been gone. I knew the feeling. I had used it to avoid leaving parties
> She gave my hand a squeeze and left me holding the clean shopping bag.
> I could have told the gods that too, if anyone had asked.
> I held up her shopping bag, realized it was Atropos's, and held it up anyway.
- Problem: At L57, "that sort of thing" points to the cabbage and the dumpling laugh, but the teasing that follows is about the catch at the doors. At L99, "the woman beside me" could be Visor or Pink Hair. At L223, what really keeps the Fates in place is the gods, not the awkwardness of leaving, and "I had used it" has no clear object. At L283, the "clean" bag has just held the rubbish-filled carton (L195, L273). At L285, "that too" has no first item. At L295, "her" first reads as Lachesis's bag.
- Fix: Small wording changes. Tie L57 to the catch. Name the woman at L99. At L223, make the gods' presence the pressure. Use "empty" instead of "clean" at L283. At L285, say what else he could have told them. At L295, clarify whose bag it is.

- Where: L127 (numbers exchanged); L363 (concert photo)

> Lachesis had sent a photograph
