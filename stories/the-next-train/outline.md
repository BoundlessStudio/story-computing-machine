# Outline

## Story

- Premise and central promise: **The Next Train.** A missed train and shared takeaway draw an ordinary commuter into the Fates' afternoon. By evening, gods descend on the subway to investigate their unlikely companion. Deliver the meal as pleasure, the trio as people, and the divine arrivals as a substantial encounter.
- Focal pressure or attachment: The unnamed adult narrator wants hot food, home, and something interesting to report about an otherwise forgettable day. Curiosity and a weakness for becoming the center of attention keep defeating the sensible intention to leave. Eventually the narrator volunteers to distract the gods, partly for the trio and partly to see whether it can be done.
- Counterforce or complication: The Fates have tickets to a tiny concert and no wish to conduct divine audiences there. Their shared attention has become news: someone recognized them laughing with a mortal and circulated a photograph. Visiting gods bring envy, old unanswered invitations, and sincere curiosity. Being treated as a mystery initially flatters the narrator, then obstructs the group's departure.
- POV, distance, and information limit: First-person past, bodily and immediate. The narrator recognizes the Fates only when a visitor identifies them; later explanations remain particular testimony. No access to unspoken divine motives or authoritative vision of anyone's entire life.
- Governing movement and time shape: Late-afternoon missed train, a sprawling meal, evening arrivals, then one deliberately diverted journey. Expand group exchanges and physical comedy; compress passing services. Target 2,500–4,000 words. End after the diversion succeeds and its unforeseen social consequence becomes clear.
- Speculative rule or ordinary-world constraint: These three living practitioners can deliberately examine nearby possible outcomes, with effort; they neither create every future nor continually inspect everything. Their clothing and electronics are ordinary. Other gods cannot locate this gathering without witnesses, messages, and travel. Give present worship, active powers, and continuing domain authority concrete evidence without a theology lecture.
- Dialogue promise: An established trio lets a stranger into food arguments, musical enthusiasms, petty grievances, and eventually a prank whose audience is divine.
- Dialogic medium: Overlapping bench conversation, shared headphones, food passed by hand, a circulating phone photograph, and an agreed gesture during boarding.
- Dialogue engine: Opinions elicit challenges and disclosures; the stranger becomes a participant. Divine questions turn easy companionship into unwanted importance. The quartet repurposes that attention into a diversion, which leaves the narrator talking to gods without the Fates to confer prestige.

## Voice

- Narrative texture: Grease, knees, headphone leakage, arriving air, magnificent clothes snagging on seats. Retrospective humor admits the narrator's vanity without anticipating every revelation.
- Conversational texture: Let eating interrupt. Complaints, recommendations, badly remembered lyrics, partial agreement, and temporary alliances carry the afternoon. Several remarks may serve only appetite or amusement.
- Rhetorical ownership: Clotho supplies extravagant schemes and enjoys being contradicted. Lachesis defends unfashionable music with embarrassing sincerity. Atropos watches practical openings, dislikes wasted food, and sometimes enjoys a joke too late. The narrator embellishes. The visiting goddess prizes precedence; the visiting god enjoys gossip but takes old slights personally.
- Pressure behavior: Clotho overcommits; Lachesis explains past the listener's patience; Atropos becomes physically decisive. The narrator's brilliant diversion outruns the available explanation. Gods remain socially uneven rather than uniformly oracular.
- Relationship movement: The narrator wants inclusion and an audience; the trio want company without petitions. None easily asks to prolong a chance meeting. Divine arrivals make trust actionable through a shared escape. The gods seek privileged access, then acquire an inconvenient new acquaintance whose company they must judge themselves.
- Anti-default: Avoid a generosity examination followed by blessings, or six speakers converging on an eloquent explanation of human worth. The successful prank should enlarge the narrator's trouble and pleasure.

## Beats

1. Doors close while the narrator protects the takeaway from spilling. Three punks make room on their bench. Sharing begins through a specific food preference and an overlarge portion, with reciprocal offerings or assistance. Establish black hair and visor, pink hair and headphones, blue hair beneath a beanie and a lowered mask; patched clothing and scuffed platform surfaces echo the inspected reference.
2. Several trains come and go. Give each punk a separate reason to keep the narrator talking. Their argument about the evening's concert reveals loyalties within the trio. A small, deliberately performed glimpse of an imminent mishap hints at unusual ability without replacing ordinary conversation. Someone photographs their conspicuous enjoyment.
3. By evening the first gods step off an actual subway train; more follow. Recognition establishes the trio's identity and the exceptional lapse in their social seclusion. Two visitors receive individual presence: a goddess intent on recovering a long-ignored invitation and a god delighted that somebody has finally embarrassed her. Their attention to the narrator is curious, intrusive, and flattering. The concert's approaching start makes remaining on display costly.
4. The narrator proposes drawing the visitors onto a train traveling away from the concert. The ruse uses a knowingly extravagant promise of privileged conversation, not a false prophecy. Clotho encourages it beyond the narrator's comfort; Lachesis keeps the interested gods gathered; Atropos spots the ordinary boarding opportunity. Establish who enters which carriage, sightlines, and the quartet's agreed signal. The trio peel away before departure; no magic operates the train or compels passengers.
5. Once underway, the gods discover the trick. Let displeasure have weight. The narrator must entertain their actual questions, without pretending to possess the Fates' wisdom. A photograph from the concert confirms the trio reached their desired evening. The gods' curiosity now concerns the person who volunteered for this ridiculous situation. At the next stop the narrator has a genuine exit and chooses to remain aboard; end in the developing, unpredictable encounter, without promising protection or summarizing a lesson.
