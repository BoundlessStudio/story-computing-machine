# Opus review: The One Who Helped Everyone

Independent audit for a later editing pass. It is not a `review.md` verdict,
does not change canon state, and does not authorize edits by itself.

## Verdict

- Dialogue: REVISE — the village talk works, but the visitors' technical exchanges run on rebuttals, ripostes and epigrams, and the story's decisive choice, central reveal and farewell are each carried by one of them.
- Prose: REVISE — narration keeps explaining the theme after scenes have already shown it, and a "Not X. Y." reversal recurs at the most intense beats.
- Continuity and prompt: REVISE — the prompt is met, including the verbatim line, but three threads "remain" after the story has closed every thread, and the climax rests on cistern/drain/millrace water infrastructure that the current policy rules out.
- Edit scope: structural
- Existing review.md said: Verdict PASS; "Dialogue: PASS", praising speaker-specific voices. I disagree: it missed the rebuttal-and-epigram pattern among the visitors and the three-threads contradiction.

The village talks well. Its exchanges are short and familiar and have their own idiom ("We're done with the warm"). Requests carry their history with them, and Aumiri's speech breaks into believable fragments and mishearings as the threads pile up. The failures cluster in the visitors' technical talk and at the crisis's turning points. Torrigan and Velshia trade the same clipped rebuttals. Each of Torrigan's three denials is capped by someone's riposte. The prompt's central reveal goes to a baker who exists to ask "Which means?", and the one-at-a-time decision is won by a neat comeback from Aumiri that the narration then refutes. The farewell ends on an epigram and a correction, not a word to Aumiri. The edit pass should make the three visitors sound like three different people: Torrigan fussy and wordy, Velshia focused on Aumiri's body and giving orders, Nimoret tentative. Cut the ripostes and aphorisms rather than sharpening them. Leave the village exchanges, the mill and bridge closures, and the hinge coda nearly untouched.

## Dialogue findings

### D1 · Major · Arrival: unclear speaker, stray "they", identical introductions

- Where: L63–L83, the visitors arrive at the trough
- Text:
> “No,” said the stranger with the case. “I mean the source that drew three survey needles off their stops from two ridges away.”
> “That’ll still be Aumiri,” the cooper said. “Can they fix the needles?”
> “Velshia. Containment. Don’t touch anything in either case.”
> “Nimoret. Fieldwright.”
- Problem: Attribution, Ground/referent and Identical Voices at the prompt's key moment. L61 says all three strangers carry cases, so "the stranger with the case" does not say who is talking. The reader has to assume it is "the broadest stranger" from L63 and learns he is Torrigan only at L79. Three strangers are present, and "they" will soon mean Nimoret, so the cooper's "Can they fix the needles?" first reads as a question about whether the strangers can fix their own needles. Only Aumiri's "Probably" shows that "they" is Aumiri. The three introductions then use one template, "Name. Role." (L79, L81, L83), so the trio first appears as one voice copied three times.
- Fix: Tag the speaker by the feature already given ("the broad stranger"). Make the cooper's question unmistakably about Aumiri, either by having her turn to Aumiri or by using the name. Keep the joke of the village answering a technical question with "Aumiri", and keep Aumiri's reflexive offer to fix the needles. Torrigan's flat "No" can stay as his model-first reflex, since he is hunting a source, not a person, but do not add more rebuttal openers. Vary the introductions: one mage gives name and role, another is named by a colleague, and the third is already crouched at the vanes and never introduces themself. Illustrative only: The cooper looked at you. "Can you fix his needles?"

### D2 · Major · Field reading: the setup for the disaster hangs on opaque rebuttals

- Where: L85–L111, Torrigan's sideways walk and the consent to the pulse
- Text:
> “That’s less like a source,” Nimoret said.
> “It exceeds a source.”
> Velshia said, “It can make a connected system answer.”
> “At survey amplitude.”
- Problem: Ground/uptake and correction ping-pong at the story's causal turning point. Nimoret has noticed that the field is spread out. "It exceeds a source" sounds like a rebuttal but makes no claim a reader can recover (bigger than a source? not a source?), so we cannot tell what Torrigan believes. Velshia then names the exact failure that is about to happen. "At survey amplitude." could be a concession ("yes, at that strength") or a dismissal ("only at full survey strength, which this is not"). The ending depends on the reader having heard a dismissal here: "within its certified range" and "Before the range check" (L367–L371). In this run Torrigan and Velshia also sound identical, trading short declarative rebuttals.
- Fix: Give Torrigan a plain line that answers Nimoret's point about spread and shows his fixation on magnitude. Make his answer to Velshia an unmistakable dismissal in a fuller, fussier voice, so that her warning visibly loses. Keep Aumiri cutting in with "One test" because the cistern thread is nagging; that is the useful flaw that starts the disaster. Keep Velshia's consent question. Illustrative only: "Spread or not, it's reading past the end of my scale." / "Not from a locating wash. That would take full survey strength."

### D3 · Major · The decisive choice turns on a comeback the narration refutes

- Where: L161–L171, Velshia offers to sever everything or isolate lines one at a time
- Text:
> “That is not the safer choice for you.”
> “It’s the safer choice for whoever’s on the north bridge.”
> Nobody was on the north bridge. Beskaret knew because he had barred its lower path when the left parapet began shifting last winter.
- Problem: Punchline Patch, uniform fluency under pressure, and a line that only the following narration can decode. Moments earlier (L149) Aumiri's words "arrived in the wrong order". Now Aumiri delivers a composed, parallel retort that turns Velshia's own phrase back on her. The premise of the retort is false, and Aumiri should know it: Aumiri holds the bridge stones because the parapet shifted. The narration corrects Aumiri inside Beskaret's head, nobody says it aloud, and Velshia's next line ("Then nobody touches an anchor…") accepts the false reason. The story's most important decision is settled by a last word.
- Fix: Give Aumiri the reason the story actually supports, in overload fragments: if everything is cut at once, things drop (the bridge stones, the seam, the mill). Nimoret's recoil warning at L151 already supports this. Then either cut the "Nobody was on the north bridge" paragraph, or give the fact to Beskaret aloud so that it changes the exchange (the bridge is empty, but its stones will still go). Keep Velshia's "That is not the safer choice for you." Illustrative only: "Bridge stones. They drop. All at once, they drop."

### D4 · Major · "Cancel it again in reverse"

- Where: L173–L181, Velshia tells Torrigan to cancel his query
- Text:
> “I’ll track the interval,” Torrigan said, still too crisply.
> “I told you, it ended.”
> Nimoret handed him one of the fine cords. “Then cancel it again in reverse.”
- Problem: Punchline Patch and Ground/verb. Torrigan's denial exists to set up Nimoret's riposte. There was no earlier cancellation for "again" to repeat, and "cancel … in reverse" is not an operation the reader can picture until L231 and L335 show what was meant: running the query's shape backward. The line is also out of voice. Everywhere else Nimoret hedges and asks ("If these are anchors. I think…"); here they score a clean point. This is the third time one of Torrigan's denials is capped by a comeback (L133–L135, L153–L155, and here), so the running bit has hardened into a formula.
- Fix: Have Nimoret offer the method tentatively and concretely, so that Torrigan gives way to an idea rather than a zinger. Cut "still too crisply", which grades his tone for the reader. Illustrative only: "If it's still ringing — could you run the same shape backward? Through this?"

### D5 · Major · The central reveal comes through a prompted briefing and a slogan

- Where: L195–L207, after the oven closure
- Text:
> “Longer,” Nimoret said.
> “By four.”
> “Which means?” the baker asked.
> “Say the useful part,” Velshia said.
> “Closures buy time.”
- Problem: Tennis Match, Explanatory chorus, and correction ping-pong capped by an epigram, all at the prompt's key reveal (the "absurdly massive" reading is many modest open favors). "Thirteen heartbeats" / "Longer" / "By four" are three equal one-liners stating arithmetic the reader already has, since nine became thirteen. The baker has just said cold bricks won't hurt, and now exists only to ask "Which means?" so that Torrigan can explain. Velshia's "Say the useful part" is a correction of how he talks, and "Closures buy time" is a slogan. The discovery that reframes the whole prompt lands as a briefing, not as a discovery.
- Fix: Cut "Longer" / "By four". Let Torrigan's realization come out of his own unease with the needles: his model has just broken, so let him say it half to himself and fussily, not in answer to a planted question. Keep his content ("The same modest output is circulating through many open returns") in some form. Either cut the baker's cue or give the baker a real need (their oven, tomorrow's bread). Replace "Say the useful part" / "Closures buy time" with a direct question and a concrete answer. Illustrative only: "Do we keep closing?" / "Yes. Every one gives us more beats."

### D6 · Minor · The soaked cooper speaks in the visitors' seminar register

- Where: L153–L155, just after the second pulse
- Text:
> “Your impulse woke what was already here. Is that different enough to be useful?”

Here she borrows Torrigan's word "impulse" and caps his denial with a dry, precise rhetorical question that sounds like Velshia ("Say the useful part" also leans on "useful").

### D7 · Major · Phase-lock decision: a Punchline Patch and a three-way volley

- Where: L239–L251, deciding the order of the last three closures
- Text:
> Torrigan looked from the uphill tank to the mill roof and then toward the north path. “My model treated distance as separation.”
> “Mountain doesn’t,” Beskaret said. “We do cistern first.”
> “Bridge first,” Velshia said. “A structural release is the worst failure.”
- Problem: Torrigan's self-diagnosis exists so that Beskaret can cap it with the aphorism "Mountain doesn't.", a neat last word at a decision point. A "cistern first" / "bridge first" / "cistern first" volley follows, with Beskaret and the cooper making the same call back to back. The exchange does real work: local knowledge overrides the experts and Velshia gives way. The cooper's concrete reason is the best line in it. The problems are the epigram and the repetition.
- Fix: Cut Torrigan's thesis line or turn it into action (he re-reads the needles, looks at the ground between the three sites). Cut "Mountain doesn't." Let Beskaret's local fact stand, and give the priority call to one villager, the cooper, who has the argument, so that Velshia's objection meets one reasoned position. Beskaret's fact and the cooper's plan both depend on the water link, so rebuild them after C2 is decided.

### D8 · Major · The farewell closes on epigrams and a correction; nobody speaks to Aumiri

- Text:
> “Not one absurd reservoir.”
> “The pulse was within its certified range,” he said. “The assumption behind it was not.”
> Nimoret tapped his case. “Add a connected-system check before the pulse.”
> Velshia shouldered her case. “Before the range check.”
- Problem: The Philosopher and correction ping-pong in the visitors' final exchange. "Not one absurd reservoir" restates the reveal from L203 as a callback to the prompt line. Torrigan's apology is a balanced antithesis. Nimoret and Velshia then close by correcting the order of his checklist. Nobody addresses Aumiri, whom the visitors nearly broke and whom Velshia held through the crisis, so the relationship between Aumiri and the visitors gets no final beat. The narration's "arguing about how many survey forms required revision" (L373) already conveys their bickering.
- Fix: Cut "Not one absurd reservoir." The reading of three separate workings (L347) already shows it, and cutting it also removes a water metaphor. Keep an ungraceful apology, but make it a person failing to say sorry to Aumiri rather than a balanced antithesis. Cut the Nimoret/Velshia correction pair and let L373 carry the argument. Give Aumiri one line in the farewell. The damaged survey needles from L73–L77, which Aumiri offered to fix, are a hook the story already provides. Asking "until" and "keeper" about them would show what Aumiri has learned without a speech. The clips exchange at L351–L357 stays.

## Prose findings

### P1 · Major · Narration explains the theme after it has landed

- Where: L227, L229, L257, L345, L365
- Text:
> That correction steadied you more than Velshia’s cold had. One thing had ended. It could stay ended.
> Their numbered clay had meant little to the villagers. Blue yarn meant cistern.
> Your threads had made the tasks feel like yours because they returned to you. The work itself never had been.
> Each had a named keeper and an ordinary fallback.
> There was no invitation, no offer to take you somewhere larger, no claim that Lanternscrape had concealed a marvel.
- Problem: The Philosopher, in narration. The story shows its point well: villagers who know which hose splits, the aunt with the blankets, the mill sequence, the hinge coda. The narration then states the point again in the closing sentences of paragraphs. L365 is the narration telling the reader which tropes the story avoided. The style guide's "Story craft defaults" warn against explaining a clear point again across narration, dialogue, policy and a closing symbol. This story does all four (see also D5 and D8), and L229 presents Velshia's L171 rule as a change she made.
- Fix: Cut the explanatory closing sentences at L227, L229 and L257, and keep the concrete material before them ("Which hose split if bent…" is good). Trim L345's last sentence if the markers already carry it. Reduce L365 to what happened. Keep the coda's hinge exchange and final image, which act out the point without stating it.

### P2 · Minor · "Not X. Y." reversals, and one negation that contradicts itself

- Where: L121, L127, L129, L311, L345
- Text:
> The village did not explode. It lurched into doing too much.
> Not as a list. As needs.
> not numbing you but giving the surge an edge to break against
> not to save the bridge if it shifted but to keep it from falling into the water

## Continuity and prompt findings

### C1 · Blocking · Threads that "remained" after every thread was closed

- Where: L233–L235, L309, L337, L345, the shutdown and the next morning
- Text:
> The remaining threads were few enough to feel separately and strong enough to frighten you.
> “Twenty-one,” he said. “Only the bridge remains.”
> Three deliberate threads remained: the goat bell until the herd came down for winter
- Problem: By sunset the only threads left are the cistern, bridge and mill (L233–L235). After the mill, Torrigan says "Only the bridge remains" (L309), and after the bridge "Nothing answered" (L337). Yet the next morning three other threads "remained", including the goat bell, which was part of the first surge (L123). Either those threads survived the shutdown, which contradicts L235, L309 and L337, or they were made again, which "remained" denies. The key claim of the resolution, that Aumiri now carries only bounded threads, rests on this contradiction.
- Fix: The smallest fix is to say plainly that the three threads were laid again deliberately that morning, each given its marker, keeper and fallback at that point. For example: "Three threads had been laid again, deliberately…". The alternative is to establish before L233 that Velshia had walled these three off and they were never part of the return, then adjust "Only the bridge remains". The first option is smaller.

### C2 · Major · Water infrastructure carries the climax (policy)

- Where: L51–L59, L99, L123, L229, L239–L281, L299, L311, L335, L349, L375
- Text:
> “Because they all pull from the same water,” Beskaret said. “Millrace comes off the cistern spill. Bridge footings sit below it.”
> “Drain line. Where?”
> At the cistern, the cooper was replacing the drain hose with a fitted wooden line.
- Problem: This conflicts with the `CANON` entry "No default water infrastructure" in universe/style-guide.md, which applies to authorized revisions. Cisterns and reservoirs, drainage, mill channels and drain lines are among the examples it names, and the prompt asks for none of them. Here they are the plot, not background detail. The cistern seam is one of the three final anchors. The phase-lock is explained by shared water, the first climax closure is a drain operation, and the coda shows drain-line work. L349 also uses "reservoir" as a metaphor.
- Fix: Required in any revised line: add no new water-works content, and remove the incidental instances ("absurd reservoir" at L349 is covered in D8; the coda at L375 can show some other repair). The full fix is structural. Replace the cistern anchor with one that is not water works but keeps the same beats: a seam the cooper patched eleven months ago that still leaks; a physical fallback that needs many hands; and a physical dependency shared with the mill and bridge that Beskaret knows and the visitors do not. The village's big cider tun or brine vat is one option: it keeps the staves, seam and "Two barrels filled", and the shared dependency could become the same slumping shelf of scree, or the same thaw Nimoret guesses. Drop the mill race and leave the mill's power unspecified. Keep the order and logic of the sequence: prepare a physical fallback before closing, then the mill, then the bridge last. If the owner rules that this pre-policy story keeps its cistern, do only the required part.
