# Outline

## Story

- Premise and central promise: You are the only person born with magic in an unnamed, remote mountain village, and you have made a happy life of small usefulness: warming cradle stones, drawing splinters, easing a swollen hinge, holding a roof patch still while someone nails it. A traveling trio of mages follows a pressure none of them has felt before, steps into the morning queue at your door, and asks, `where is the one with the absurdly massive amount of magical energy`. The villagers point to you; you expect either a mistake or a demand. The mages privately confirm that your living reserve is genuinely immense while your practiced release is narrow and precise. They offer to take you at dawn to a winter teaching house, not to borrow your power, but the pass will close behind them and the village has real dependencies you helped create. Resolve the promise through a chosen, bounded departure: you decide to go until spring, insist that training and any future use of your magic require your informed choice, and leave some requests unfinished rather than converting your capacity into an obligation to everyone who can name a need.
- Intended reader experience: Warm, tactile domestic fantasy that opens in comic astonishment, lets wonder at the protagonist's scale coexist with the pleasure of being useful, and gradually makes every casual request feel double-edged. The mages should be competent, various, and capable of awe without becoming kidnappers, saviors, or a faceless institution. Deliver the power reveal clearly, but keep the decisive satisfaction in the protagonist discovering a desire that is not merely somebody else's need. End with affection, unease, and forward motion rather than triumph, resentment toward the whole village, or a promise that leaving will solve everything.
- Generating force:
  - Mode: Plot-led recursive request sequence. The same exchange—someone asks, you answer—repeats through a morning and evening, but its terms become more exact: domestic favor, magical sounding, invitation, village dependency, private plea, then your conditional answer. The story moves by changing what `help` means, not by escalating feats.
  - Focal attachment or contradiction: You genuinely enjoy the intimacy and competence of helping people you know; usefulness is not a false life waiting to be discarded. You have also learned to receive affection most comfortably when it arrives disguised as work, so another person's need reaches your mouth as `yes` before your own curiosity, fatigue, or desire becomes audible.
  - Plot-led wants and useful limit, if applicable: Immediately, you want to finish the waiting village jobs, make the exhausted visitors comfortable, and learn why they crossed the mountains for you. Your competing valuable wants are to accept the first real chance to understand your magic and to avoid abandoning people whose winter routines now rely on it. Your useful limit is anticipatory helpfulness: you volunteer the answer you think will relieve everyone before asking what is actually requested, how long it lasts, whether another method exists, or what you want. This makes your first acceptance of the mages' invitation no more meaningful than your smallest errands and forces a second, deliberate choice.
- Counterforce or complication:
  - Source: Incompatible goods rather than an antagonist. The trio must cross the high pass at dawn ahead of forecast snow and will not return until spring. Several village needs are materially harder without your narrow magic, including nightly warmth for the old neighbor who raised you and the mill's freeze-prone gate; months of easy magical fixes have displaced some ordinary preparation. At the same time, raw capacity is not usable mastery: forcing more than your familiar release makes your fingers lose sensation and nearby workings blur toward the wrong task.
  - Credible inside logic, if a person: The three visitors want different defensible things. Their exacting teacher wants to offer training without acquiring a compliant source of power and refuses to count a reflexive `if you need me` as consent. Their road-wise companion wants a decision before weather makes the route unsafe and points out that a village cannot responsibly replace every winter system overnight. Their youngest member is sincerely dazzled, talks too quickly about what that reserve might do, and must learn that awe can sound like appetite. The villagers remain individuals: some fear losing necessary help, some are excited for you, and the old neighbor can honestly want both your future and your presence without pretending those wishes agree.
  - Pressure or change created: You cannot complete every open favor, build durable replacements, understand your capacity, and catch the dawn crossing. The visitors cannot make the choice clean by declaring the village selfish, while the village's genuine dependence cannot establish ownership. The finite departure window requires you to leave behind real inconvenience and someone you love rather than winning universal permission.
- Narrative design:
  - POV, tense, distance, and limitation: Close second-person past, preserving the prompt's direct address while distinguishing this from a present-tense power ride. Keep the distance intimate with trained hands, remembered households, the pleasure of a task fitting neatly into place, and the body's premature motion toward every request. You cannot enter the mages or villagers; distinguish the teacher's questions, the road mage's practical weather-and-load vocabulary, the youngest mage's breathless scale comparisons, and the old neighbor's concrete domestic speech through dialogue and action.
  - Time shape and compression: One day and the final hours before dawn. Render the doorstep interruption, private sounding, first failed acceptance, selected village rounds, the old neighbor's request, and the protagonist's conditional decision in scene. Compress the history of helping into brief task-associated memories and the evening's unfinished work into a short sorting passage. Do not add a childhood prophecy, hidden lineage, abduction attempt, disaster climax, training montage, or later account of success at the teaching house.
  - Information and reveal strategy: Begin by making the protagonist's modest magic and pleasure in using it materially credible before the quoted question arrives. Let the villagers identify the protagonist immediately. Confirm the enormous reserve privately when the teacher supplies a thin living strand of her own magic for the protagonist to follow inward; the protagonist encounters depth far beyond the familiar narrow outlet, and all three mages react to the same impossible scale. Establish in the same scene that reserve, release, range, and control are different: the protagonist cannot casually turn the mountain over, and the sounding neither transfers power nor proves moral worth. Reveal the offer before introducing the departure deadline and concrete village dependencies. Withhold the protagonist's actual answer until they can state a want without phrasing it as help for either side.
  - Governing movement: Wake to the familiar queue; perform three small, distinct helps with evident delight; hear the visitors' quoted question and watch every hand point; bring them inside rather than answer in the road; follow the teacher's living sounding strand and discover the immense reserve; test the familiar outlet only far enough to numb two fingers and spoil a separate warming charm; hear the teaching offer; answer `if you need me` and have the teacher decline that answer; continue the day's rounds with the trio; let the youngest mage romanticize one impossible-scale use, the road mage inventory the winter systems that would actually fail, and the teacher keep asking what the protagonist wants; solve one genuine urgent need with precise magic; decline a showy roof repair beyond present control; face mixed individual responses without a village meeting; visit the old neighbor, who asks plainly for nightly warmth and also admits wanting the protagonist to stay; offer only a finite warming and arrange an ordinary backup that is less convenient; return before midnight, state the desired training and three concrete terms, receive the trio's equally concrete assent, and sort what can and cannot be finished before dawn.
- Speculative surplus: Magic passing through the protagonist's hands makes yeast work too quickly for several minutes, even when the spell has nothing to do with food. Village bakers keep covered starter crocks on a cool stone shelf when the protagonist visits, and the traveling mages' supper bread overproofs while everyone argues. The effect is inconvenient, local, and culturally accommodated; it does not measure the reserve, feed on gratitude, indicate consent, widen the outlet, or resolve any request.
- Structural distinction: A domestic recursive sequence built from the grammar of asking and answering. The largest power remains largely unused; the central venues are a crowded doorway, a kitchen sounding, several ordinary work sites, and a private bedside visit rather than a battlefield, chase route, public appraisal, court, institutional hearing, or spectacular emergency. No crowd converts, no authority rewrites its rules, and the protagonist's choice is not certified by a technical feat.
- Decisive turn, deepening, or recognition: Beside the old neighbor's bed, you begin promising to keep the room warm through winter, then stop when the promise reaches farther than the night. Asked whether the mages need you, you finally say that need is not the reason: you want to learn what has always been inside you, and you are frightened that wanting it will hurt someone. The neighbor neither releases you cleanly nor claims you; she says she wants you to stay and wants you to have a life large enough to return from, then asks what warmth can honestly be given tonight. You set a finite charm, show her the ordinary heated-brick arrangement another villager can maintain, and accept that the replacement is worse. Back at the kitchen, you tell the trio you will go until spring, will not pour power into a working you do not understand, and will decide afterward whether to continue. This is the first request in the story answered from a stated want rather than anticipated obligation.
- Aftereffect or live uncertainty: The reserve remains absurdly large and its origin, upper bound, and safe trained uses remain unknown. The protagonist has not become stronger in a day, the village has not become self-sufficient, the old neighbor will miss both companionship and effortless heat, and at least one villager remains angry. The trio gains a student, not ownership or guaranteed access to the reserve. The protagonist leaves a finite list, several honest refusals, and imperfect ordinary workarounds; spring may bring a return, a longer journey, or a different choice.
- Opening / ending relation: Open with knocking before sunrise and the protagonist unlatching the door while already saying yes, before the first neighbor has described the job. End late that night, after the decision, with the protagonist still at the same table among objects left for repair, completing one promised task, returning another untouched, and going to sleep before the list is empty. Do not close on a grand departure, a glowing power display, villagers bowing or cheering, the mages assigning a rank, or a polished emblem that makes boundary-setting look effortless.

## Beats

1. Before sunrise, neighbors queue at the protagonist's door. Show three concise, materially different spells and the protagonist's honest pleasure: tease a splinter from a child's heel, ease an iced latch without warping it, and hold warmth in a cradle stone. Seed the yeast nuisance when a covered crock starts swelling as the protagonist passes.
2. A mud-spattered trio interrupts with the exact question about `the one with the absurdly massive amount of magical energy.` The whole queue points. The protagonist laughs, assumes they have confused constant use with great power, and invites them into the kitchen because helping lost travelers is familiar territory.
3. In private, the teacher creates a fine sounding strand from her own living magic and guides the protagonist to follow it inward. The familiar outlet proves no wider than expected, but behind it lies a reserve so deep that the teacher breaks contact, the road mage checks the route that brought them, and the youngest runs out of useful comparisons. Keep wonder sensory and embodied rather than turning it into public instrumentation.
4. A cautious attempt to push more through the narrow outlet numbs the protagonist's fingers and causes a second prepared charm to warm the wrong cup. Establish that capacity is not control. The trio explains that their winter teaching house can help map the outlet safely and offers a place; they must leave at dawn. The protagonist says, `If you need me, I'll come.` The teacher answers that this is precisely the answer she cannot accept.
5. The protagonist postpones choosing by returning to the day's rounds with the trio. Across selected jobs, reveal real skill and real dependence: one urgent, precise fix only the protagonist can currently make; one task someone else can do slowly; and one indefinite request for a whole winter's protection. Let the three mages' different objectives emerge without a formal debate.
6. When the youngest urges a spectacular use to see what the reserve can do, the protagonist tries only enough to feel the outlet smear one intended effect toward another and stops. Decline the showy roof repair rather than stage a proof. The road mage identifies the mill gate and old neighbor's heat as genuine winter problems, preventing an easy claim that everyone can simply cope; individual villagers respond with pride, alarm, bargaining, or anger, but there is no meeting or consensus.
7. At the old neighbor's bedside, the protagonist catches an unbounded promise halfway out. The two separate magical heat, practical winter labor, companionship, and the protagonist's desire. The protagonist gives a tonight-only charm and helps arrange heated bricks for later nights, knowing this is a less reliable substitute and not pretending the neighbor is pleased to lose them.
8. Back in the kitchen, the supper bread has overproofed and the pass weather has worsened. The protagonist states the actual choice: go until spring to learn, no unexplained power transfer or working, and a fresh choice about staying when the season ends. The teacher agrees; the road mage makes the dawn limit and return uncertainty explicit; the youngest apologizes without ceasing to be awed.
9. Close on an incomplete final-night list. The protagonist finishes one bounded promise, returns an object whose repair can wait, writes usable instructions for one ordinary workaround, and leaves other work undone. The central decision is complete even though the village, relationships, and reservoir are not.

## Continuity

- Canon used: The shared world permits many independent systems called magic; only living beings use magic. The protagonist and visiting mages are living users. Any cups, stones, route marks, or other nonliving materials only receive, store, focus, or display effects supplied by living magic. `Magic` and `mage` are viewpoint-culture labels, not objective cosmological categories.
- Boundaries and unknowns: Treat this as an independent unnamed era, village, region, teaching house, and local magic system with no implied connection to established people, places, factions, artifacts, or schools. The protagonist's service does not create, fuel, or morally validate the reserve; its origin and upper limit remain unresolved. Large reserve does not imply unlimited output, universal power ranking, divinity, inherited destiny, or a duty to serve. The sounding strand is direct living magic, not a sentient instrument or truth/consent detector. Do not establish a universal relationship among desire, consent, energy, magical cost, training, or apprenticeship. No named person or place is proposed, so the people and places inventories intentionally use `None`.
