# Story plan

## Premise and promise

When 131,072 autonomous invasion frames descend through a fixed rupture above
an evacuated bay, Solstice Evening Bell—the sole being on Earth classified
`ultrahuman`, and the first and only monarch of a vanished magical-girl
tradition—must spend the twelve irreplaceable final spells that twelve women
gave their lives to place in her crown, then destroy the crown itself, to keep
the world free.

The story promises one continuous, spatially legible battle in which mass
attacks demand tactics rather than bigger adjectives, every major power is
seeded before payoff, the enemy count visibly falls through defined milestones,
and Solstice's final victory is caused by a sovereign choice with a permanent
cost.

## Story controls

- POV: Close third person through Solstice Evening Bell.
- Tense: Past.
- Tone and genre: Kinetic, defiant, costly magical-girl science fantasy, with
  majesty undercut by concrete physical strain.
- Length budget: 3,150 words, acceptable range 2,800–3,800.
- Content boundary: Teen / PG-13. Destroy nonliving warframes at enormous
  scale; show Solstice's pain and wounds without gore; no civilian deaths on
  camera.
- Continuous action rule: Brief coronation recollections may be triggered by
  present blows, but the rupture-to-aftermath battle is the story's unbroken
  dramatic present.
- Central dramatic question: Can Solstice honor the twelve women who died to
  create her crown when holding Earth requires her to destroy every last work
  they left behind?
- Meaningful change: She begins by treating preservation of the chimes as
  preservation of the donors and ends by accepting the harder truth encoded
  in their willing sacrifice: a gift made for use is betrayed if a queen saves
  the memorial and loses the people it was meant to protect.

## Character engine

- Protagonist: **Solstice Evening Bell**, an adult sovereign whose reign and
  royal hearing have endured for centuries. She became the first magical girl
  accepted as monarch when formerly independent circles created a command
  compact; there was never a successor. The twelve most powerful magical girls
  then living willingly spent their lives making the crown's twelve orbiting
  chimes. In the low-magic Earth era, “queen of all magical girls” is a
  historical and legal title whose living constituency has dwindled to its
  officeholder, not proof of a large public contemporary population.
- External desire: Destroy or expel every invasion frame before the host can
  secure the bay and hold open its rupture.
- Stakes: If even one of eight columns establishes a stable bridgehead, later
  waves can follow; the evacuated coast and the inhabited lands beyond the
  eastern ridge will be occupied. The persisting physical world must survive.
- Opposition: Exactly **131,072 nonliving autonomous warframes**, initially
  organized as eight wedges of 16,384. They use technological weapons and
  adaptive coordination, not independent magic. Their shared mesh learns from
  each use of Solstice's crown.
- Pressure: The host needs only minutes to descend from the rupture to eight
  landing sectors around the bay. Solstice's high-output attacks damage the
  same finite crown-tones she needs for mobility and defense.
- Blind spot: Solstice believes preserving the chimes preserves the twelve
  donors. She hoards their last spells and the crown's central tone even when
  doing so opens routes toward the ridge, confusing guardianship of a memorial
  with fidelity to the women's freely chosen purpose.
- Internal movement: Counting the enemy forces her to count the donors' final
  gifts as she destroys them one by one. She remembers that they explicitly
  asked to be used, not enshrined. Her final exercise of authority is release:
  she breaks her own central resonator and spends every residue the chimes
  retain. She loses the crown's inherited techniques but remains intrinsically
  powerful enough to be Earth's sole classified `ultrahuman`.
- Key cast: No other named or fighting character. Evacuated civilians, passive
  warning systems, historical magical girls, and remote witnesses remain
  collective and cannot attack, shield, heal, target, power, or augment
  Solstice.

## Power and opposition rules

### The Thirteenfold Crown

- The crown is a nonliving focus built for and neurally keyed to Solstice. It
  cannot choose, act, or produce magic by itself. Solstice's living agency and
  innate force wake, shape, and direct everything stored in it.
- Each of the twelve orbiting chimes preserves the final spell of one of the
  twelve most powerful magical girls then living. Each adult donor knowingly
  drove her transformation past the point her body could survive and freely
  laid the resulting frequency into silver. Their souls and minds are not
  trapped; they passed into the ordinary underlying afterlife. What remains is
  nonliving stored magic and Solstice's personal memory of each woman.
- The thirteenth, central resonator against Solstice's sternum carries
  Solstice's own crown-frequency. The twelve chimes add precision, geometry,
  royal hearing, and the inherited techniques below. Solstice's innate raw
  output exceeds the `ultrahuman` threshold independently of the crown.
- **Royal hearing:** While a chime remains intact, Solstice senses position,
  mass, and mesh pulses within the bay-to-rupture battlespace. This establishes
  the exact opening count and lets her track attrition without outside
  targeting assistance.
- **Bell-step:** She can move almost instantly along a straight line between
  two sounding chimes. It cannot turn mid-step and becomes less safe as chimes
  fail.
- **Edict plane:** A pair of chimes can hold one thin plane of destructive
  resonance. It cuts frames that cross it but cannot chase them. Geography and
  enemy routing therefore matter.
- **Full chord:** With multiple chimes aligned, Solstice can pulse a large
  volume once. The pulse is strongest against synchronized structures but
  reveals its harmonic pattern to the host.
- **Burning a tone:** She may overload a chime for a much larger edict. The
  chime shatters, its donor's last spell is permanently erased, and its
  frequency is seared out of Solstice's crown-bound nervous pathways. Lost
  tones cannot be healed or rebuilt. Each loss should trigger one brief,
  concrete memory of the donor rather than treating the chimes as ammunition.
- **Central release:** The coronation compact's seeded final clause says that
  what the crown joins, the monarch may release. If Solstice deliberately
  breaks the central resonator while the twelve burnt frequencies form a
  conductive circuit, every remaining crown frequency discharges at once. This
  is not a new power: it is the destructive inverse of the full chord. It
  permanently destroys the crown, royal hearing, bell-step, edict planes, full
  chord, and all residue of the twelve donated spells. It does not erase
  Solstice's innate raw force or end her era-local `ultrahuman`
  classification.

### Invasion host

- The frames are nonliving, technological, roughly human-sized shells with
  folding vanes. They share information through a low-latency coordination
  mesh but have no individual personhood or magic use.
- Eight wedges enter through a stationary rupture twenty kilometers above the
  western bay and aim for eight landing sectors: two on the sea shelf, two on
  the breakwaters, and four along the eastern ridge.
- Their needle projectiles and collision attacks force Solstice to spend
  movement and defense. Their mesh can model a harmonic attack after surviving
  one use, so repeated patterns become ineffective.
- When dispersed they are hard to kill in bulk; when synchronized for landing
  or rupture stabilization they are vulnerable to her resonance. Their
  adaptation creates the climax solution: the mesh that coordinates the last
  frames can also carry her central release back through the rupture.

## Causal arc

1. **Beginning state:** The coast has been evacuated and all conventional
   combatants are outside the battlespace by Solstice's command. Passive
   instruments have gone blind; royal hearing is the only live count. The
   opening establishes that twelve adult magical girls willingly died to make
   the twelve chimes, that no souls are trapped, and that Solstice knows each
   chime by a human memory. She holds thirteen intact tones and believes
   preserving the crown is part of preserving them.
2. **Inciting disruption:** A rupture opens over the western bay and eight
   perfect wedges emerge. Royal hearing returns the impossible but exact number
   131,072. Solstice names herself, her two offices, and Earth's only
   `ultrahuman`, then attacks before the formation can disperse.
3. **First turn—scale becomes adaptation:** A full chord through the rigid
   entry formation destroys 32,768 frames; **98,304 remain**. The survivors
   instantly learn the chord, split into smaller schools, and attack the
   chimes. Solstice's most efficient opening weapon will not work twice.
4. **Second turn—power becomes terrain:** Solstice arranges edict planes from
   rupture to cloud deck and bell-steps between them, herding schools through
   kill geometry rather than pursuing individuals. She burns three tones to
   keep the corridor closed. At the tropopause, **49,152 remain**. The host
   identifies chimes as the finite resource and changes from attacking Earth
   to attacking her crown.
5. **Third turn—solitude becomes a bottleneck:** Needles, sacrificial
   collisions, and false mesh pulses force her to burn six more tones. With
   only three orbiting chimes and the central resonator intact, her paths
   collapse from a lattice to a triangle. She uses storm moisture, gravity,
   and the fixed bay corridor—not magical weather—to force the descent through
   the last planes. **8,192 remain**, now divided into eight columns of 1,024
   aimed at the landing sectors.
6. **Crisis—preserve the memorial or save the world:** Solstice destroys four
  columns in close combat and cripples three more while her final orbiting
  chimes burn out. **4,096 remain** in the last coordinated column and its
  scattered escorts. They reach the breakwaters, assemble a mesh lattice, and
  begin stabilizing the rupture. Solstice has no bell-step, no edict plane,
  and only the central resonator. Keeping it might preserve the crown's last
  trace and armor her escape; using it conventionally cannot stop the
  bridgehead. She will remain `ultrahuman` either way.
7. **Climax—abdication as attack:** The twelve destroyed chimes have fallen in
   an arc from high air to both breakwaters, a geography repeatedly tracked
   during the fight. Solstice completes their circuit with her own body and
   invokes the compact's already remembered release clause. She crushes the
   central resonator. The invasion mesh carries the inverse chord through all
   **4,096** remaining frames and back to the rupture. Every remaining frame
   goes dark and the rupture seals.
8. **Aftermath:** Dawn reaches an intact ridge. Royal hearing is gone; for the
   first time in centuries Solstice cannot count what approaches. A passive
   public hazard display still reports one `ultrahuman`, confirming that her
   innate force survives even though the crown and its twelve final spells do
   not. She remains Solstice Evening Bell and First Monarch because she honored
   the donors' purpose by using the gift they chose to make. Earth is held.

## Setup and payoff

- Opening exact count of thirteen crown tones → each major turn tracks intact
  tones until only the central resonator remains.
- Opening distinguishes the twelve donors' departed souls from the final spells
  stored in the chimes and gives them concrete human particulars → later
  destruction erases a woman's last work without implying her person is killed
  again or imprisoned.
- Eight 16,384-frame wedges → later eight 1,024-frame landing columns, keeping
  invasion shape and count intelligible.
- The full chord devastates synchronized frames once → the host adapts, then
  its own forced synchronization becomes the route for the inverse chord.
- Chimes are described falling toward sea shelf and breakwaters when burned →
  their positions form the final conductive arc; the prose must track at least
  the endpoints so this does not feel retrofitted.
- Coronation memory supplies “what the crown joins, the monarch may release”
  early, first as a frightening succession/abdication clause → final literal
  release destroys the crown and carries through the enemy mesh.
- A donor's remembered instruction—use the gift rather than make a relic of
  it—challenges Solstice's instinct to preserve the chimes → the final release
  fulfills the donors' purpose rather than treating them as ammunition.
- `Ultrahuman` is defined early by a narrow disaster-response threshold →
  the unchanged count of one in the aftermath proves that the crown's loss is
  distinct from Solstice's innate classification.
- Solstice repeatedly counts enemies and chimes → final silence after royal
  hearing ends becomes both loss and relief.

## Canon constraints

- Treat this Earth as the shared world's modern-like era, not explicitly
  literal real-world Earth. Use generic bay, ridge, breakwaters, public systems,
  and technology rather than named real nations or cities.
- Keep the era a low-magic trough. The magical-girl circles are historical,
  nonpublic, and no longer a large active population; Solstice is a rare
  survivor, not evidence of routine public magic.
- `Ultrahuman` is an era-local administrative hazard category with a stated
  force/output threshold. It is neither a cosmological truth nor “only
  supernatural being.”
- Solstice, a living being, supplies every magical effect. Crown, chimes, and
  resonator are nonliving focuses. The chimes store final spells created by
  twelve living donors at the moment of their willing deaths, but cannot use
  those spells without Solstice's present living agency. Invading autonomous
  frames use technology, not magic.
- The donors' souls are neither stored nor destroyed; the universal underlying
  afterlife remains intact. The story may destroy their last works but must not
  reduce their identities or moral choices to battery functions.
- Do not call Solstice a literal god or imply worship. Her queenhood rests on a
  historical compact and authority, not divinity.
- The compact binds the crown's behavior locally; names and titles themselves
  do not possess universal power.
- Do not connect the rupture, crown, bell imagery, solstice imagery, or old
  circles to the Glass Gate, Queen Aurelia, the Goddess of Time, or another
  existing system/person by resemblance.
- No other actor supplies combat assistance. Passive evacuation, observation,
  and aftermath classification are allowed but must not become targeting,
  shielding, healing, power, or tactical advice.
- Preserve the physical world and its capacity to reach the later Glass-Sea
  era. The invasion is stopped before occupation or planetary destruction.
- Keep destruction of nonliving frames visually forceful but non-graphic.

## Proposed inventions

- Solstice Evening Bell is an adult sovereign whose reign and royal hearing
  have endured for centuries, and the first/only monarch created by a compact
  among formerly independent magical-girl circles; the office's present living
  constituency is only her.
- `Ultrahuman` is a modern-like-era disaster-response classification for a
  living individual whose unaided output exceeds an era-local strategic
  threshold; Solstice's innate force makes her Earth's only entry before and
  after this battle.
- The Thirteenfold Crown is a nonliving focus keyed to Solstice's living
  nervous system. Twelve willing fatal final spells give it distinct chime
  frequencies; it provides royal hearing, bell-step, edict planes, full chord,
  permanent tone-burning, and the central release defined above.
- A technological invasion sends exactly 131,072 nonliving autonomous frames
  through a fixed rupture above an unnamed western bay, using an adaptive mesh
  and eight-sector landing strategy.
- Solstice destroys or disables the entire invasion alone and seals the
  rupture by permanently releasing the crown's tones. The crown and all twelve
  donors' final works are gone, but her innate raw force remains
  `ultrahuman`-level.
- The exact battle, bay defenses, evacuated region, and public classification
  display are local story facts. No relationship to an existing artifact,
  faction, deity, portal, or named era institution is proposed.

## Failure modes to watch

- Losing the exact count after the opening. Required checkpoints are 131,072 →
  98,304 → 49,152 → 8,192 → 4,096 → zero.
- Listing attacks without changing objectives, geometry, resources, or enemy
  behavior. Every exchange must cause the next formation or cost.
- Making the full chord, central release, mesh conduction, fallen-chime circuit,
  or compact clause appear only when needed. Seed all five before climax.
- Letting passive instruments, evacuees, historical circles, or remote
  witnesses materially assist Solstice.
- Calling the frames magical, alive, conscious, or dead without establishing
  that ontology; use destroyed, broken, disabled, or dark for nonliving units.
- Allowing Solstice to recharge, heal, replace a burnt tone, or recover a
  donor's destroyed final spell.
- Implying a donor's soul or consciousness is trapped, used, re-killed, or
  erased when a chime breaks; the loss is her final spell and surviving work.
- Treating “queen of all magical girls” as proof of a large present-day public
  population, a universal monarchy, worship, divinity, or authority over people
  outside the historical tradition.
- Treating `ultrahuman` as “only extraordinary being” or as an objective
  cosmological rank.
- Using `Bell` alone, `Queen` alone, Sun Queen, Burning Queen, Goddess, Time, or
  another reserved alias that creates an accidental identity link.
- Overloading the 3,150-word budget with invasion origins, agency briefings,
  named observers, or deep-history exposition. Reveal only what changes
  Solstice's choices in the battle.
- Making the twelve donors interchangeable, coerced, or retroactively robbed
  of agency. Their sacrifice must be explicit, willing, personal, and honored
  through purposeful use rather than sentimental preservation.
- Making the permanent crown loss read as punishment for female authority.
  Solstice remains the unique `ultrahuman`; her active, tactically necessary
  sacrifice fulfills her sovereignty and the donors' intent.
- Ending at the explosion. Reserve a short, quiet aftermath that confirms Earth
  is held, the crown's loss is real, the board still reads one, and Solstice's
  identity and classification endure.
