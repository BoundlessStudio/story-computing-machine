## Promised experience

A powerful battle mage stands convicted of saving a whole village by breaking the kingdom's strict ban on altering the natural order. She faces the royal executioner beneath the High Court's golden-star banners and refuses to beg for mercy as luminous threads are forcibly pulled from her heart. “An entire village” calls for a rescue whose reach we can feel. “As” puts the defiance where it costs her most. The image suggests a solemn public ordeal, with the executioner intent and the mage in visible pain. I expect the ending to leave both the rescue and the loss real. Their immediate antagonism is given. Their history, the forbidden act and the ending are open. The later attachment direction governs how we read our sources.

## Takes considered

- **The open assize.** See the mage send a mountainside falling sideways, then face the executioner who strips her of the power with which she made that choice.
- **The executioner's account.** Start with the first thread. The executioner discovers during the punishment why the mage deserted her regiment to make the rescue, then finishes the sentence knowing what it costs.
- **Across a single threshold.** A village that escaped a fire through an impossible step into the capital watches the sentence. The story comes through people who owe their lives to the prisoner.

Choose the assize. Ivet makes both choices in view, and the landscape changed by the first stays visible from the place of the second.

## Provisional direction

Ivet Serr, a serving battle mage, tries to brace a rockslide with the force she normally trains on siege gates. The face of the mountain keeps coming. Lammet and its roads lie in its path. She changes what the loosened mountainside falls toward. It falls sideways into an empty gorge, enough stone and timber to leave a new ridge. Lammet is safe when ordinary gravity resumes.

A week later, convicted at the High Court of Brevast, she faces royal executioner Rinne Cade. A public plea for mercy and an oath to observe the ban even in the same circumstances would stop the sentence and return Ivet to her company with her magic. Rinne wants the oath to mean something to the mages who may follow Ivet's example. Ivet wants her company and the faculty she has lived by. She also wants the company to hear what she thinks she owed Lammet. Rinne forcibly draws the faculty from Ivet's heart. Ivet's body fights the pull. She considers the terms without speaking a plea, says she would save Lammet again, and is stripped of the faculty completely.

## Craft plan

**Teller.** Try close third past through a practical, irreverent soldier who judges a room by what could come down in it. Also try Ivet looking back in first person, eager to tell us about the split mountain and stung by how badly she wanted to keep her power.

**Ivet.** A freckled redhead with a partly loosened braid, crossed leather belts and a crimson cloak with a gold star at the left shoulder. She plants, appraises, braces and raises her face. She enters ready to find who's in charge and get started. Orders come fast, stories spaciously. Under strain she speaks in terms of what she can touch, hold or reach. Proud of being needed, she also loves the power itself. The belts take the strain of bracing against the slide. She pushes the braid aside to see, then feels it drop back when she lifts her face. The service star stays visible when she refuses. She expects Rinne to mistake resolve for ease.

**Rinne.** Dark coils of hair, white cloak, gold star and cuff, crossed field belts and open hand. She measures, steadies, fixes and draws. She takes her place and expects others to bring themselves to order. Her terms run in whole, deliberate sentences. She gets more formal when she's afraid. The open hand offers relief and then draws the threads. The cuff shakes as she works, her gaze always on Ivet's face. She wore the belts as a war mage and still fastens them tight before a sentence. She once raised the all-clear pennant for a commander who made a siege road cease to bear weight. Her own soldiers were still on it. She wants a trustworthy oath in front of the very people Ivet leads, and expects Ivet to give one when she feels her loss. The image's agony can be physical and non-graphic. The dark-cloaked officials are a watching group. The gray-bearded judge gives the sentence voice.

**Owed conversation.** Rinne must put her own mistake in evidence to say why a commander can be wrong when certain that a forbidden act is safe. Ivet can say she rode the empty gorge that morning and knew where the slide could go. She can admit how quickly that knowledge had to serve, and ask Rinne which loss she would have been willing to risk in her place. Keep the slide itself in the first scene.

**Place.** The High Court is seated year-round at Brevast's main pass, where it hears disputes over roads and flock passage. Its east arcade looks down a clear mile to Lammet and over to the new ridge. The enclosed Fork Hall would give the bench control of what people see. Lammet's weighing shed would make the rescued village the host. In the arcade a week into the spring thaw, morning sun reaches under unglazed arches. Bells echo under the vault, navy banners with eight-pointed golden stars pull at their brass rings, ground slate slips underfoot, the stone stays cold, and waxed oak smells sweet. The ground shock widened an old crack in the low parapet beside an iron-railed opening. Through that bay the bench and prisoner can see the very road they discuss. At Lammet, wet wool hangs on drying racks in the bell square, hemmed in by slate roofs.

## Scene briefs and ending

1. **Lammet bell square, morning, about a third.** From the road, Ivet feels the weight and movement of the bell's clapper as though it were in her hand and rings it by magic. A woman rounding people up wants her at the far houses. Ivet has felt the slope move behind them and needs the woman to get everybody down on the ground. Ivet holds the front of the slide with ordinary force, then changes where the mountain falls. Whole trees wheel through the sideways rush.
2. **East arcade, a week later, the long scene.** Ivet can still feel an iron tie taking the arch's weight. Beneath the gold stars, Rinne needs a petition and oath. With the roofs in view, Ivet makes her say that keeping the oath would have meant letting Lammet be buried. They talk through the two places they had to judge in a hurry. Rinne starts the forced removal after Ivet refuses. Ivet instinctively grabs back and a stool skids into the rail. Rinne braces and draws luminous threads from Ivet's heart toward her bare palm. Ivet can still see the iron tie. Her sense of the load it carries keeps cutting out. She thinks how she could tell if the footing under her soldiers would give before they could see it move, and what it would put back in her reach to ask. Rinne offers the terms again while the light is taut. Ivet's head comes back, her eyes close and her mouth opens under the force. She tells Rinne she would save Lammet again. The last pull takes the rest.
3. **At the rail, the last tenth.** Ivet sees the new ridge and the belfry. She reflexively reaches for the clapper's weight and feels only the iron under her fingers. She sees the bell swing for the ordinary hour and has to wait for its sound to cross the valley.

Summarize the intervening trial and the company’s history as needed. The bell's wait carries the first contact with the clapper, the rescue of the place where it rings and the witnessed loss of that reach.
