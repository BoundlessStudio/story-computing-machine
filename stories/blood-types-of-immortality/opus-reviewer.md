# Opus review: The Fourth Blood

Independent audit for a later editing pass. It is not a `review.md` verdict,
does not change canon state, and does not authorize edits by itself.

## Verdict

- Dialogue: REVISE — One line asks the sorcerer to cross out a heading the healer has already crossed out (L101/L117). The sorcerer and healer mostly talk by correcting each other and closing on a neat rejoinder. The injured miller starts out concrete and drifts into the same epigram register. The final apology states its own moral.
- Prose: REVISE — "Not X. Not Y. Z." litanies and "was not X / was Y" reversals are systemic. The ending explains a lesson the miller scenes already showed.
- Continuity and prompt: PASS — The prompt's promise is delivered causally and hedged ("Human blood has types... One result still won't hold still"). Length is about 3,030 words. Only small staging, knowledge and permission-bookkeeping slips remain.
- Edit scope: targeted-dialogue
- Existing review.md said: Verdict PASS, "Dialogue: PASS"; disagree. It missed the crossed-out-heading contradiction and the correction/rejoinder rhythm. Its claim that the resident's "concrete resistance" stays distinct holds only for L147.

The dialogue is coherent more often than it fails, and several beats are genuinely good: the "Cold" misunderstanding, the miller's bodily account, "Not what I asked." / "No," and the midnight test. Its failure is the collection's house rhythm. The healer's lines police the sorcerer's wording ("With itself.", "You haven't found strongest.", "Then correct inside the boundary."). The sorcerer answers in kind, and many exchanges close on a shaped last word rather than on what either person needs. The miller is dizzy, thin and has been talked over, yet by L161–L173 he speaks in the same composed aphorisms ("You're making a longer question."). His last request is a clause-by-clause term sheet that gets only half an answer. The edit pass should give each speaker a different reason for speaking. The sorcerer over-explains and reaches for theory. The healer speaks from bodies, food, weather and the ledger rather than rewording his sentences. The miller speaks from his body and his tiredness. Cut the rejoinders rather than sharpening them, and leave the midnight test and the final healer exchange almost untouched.

## Edit guardrails

- The prompt is the acceptance authority. A mountain sorcerer pursuing immortality through blood magic discovers instead that humans have blood types, and the story must resolve that promise. Keep "Blood," I said. "Human blood has types." (L281) and its hedges. Stay within 2,500–4,000 words (now about 3,030) and keep the tone broadly accessible. The active craft profile is prospective-2026-08-21 (the rewrite constraints). Keep first-person past through the unnamed sorcerer.
- Universe: rules.md "Living agency and magic" (LOCKED) says only life can use magic. The frame, silver, stone and glass stay inert, and every working begins and ends with the sorcerer's pulse (L23, L51, L79, L197). Do not let any object or sample act on its own in revised lines.
- Content boundaries (LOCKED): no blood enters a body, nothing is treated, collection stays offstage and non-graphic (L37, L143, L325). Revised lines must not add transfusion, procedure or medical advice.
- Internal facts the ending depends on:
  - 28 sealed samples; five are comparison-only, and two are to be returned before the pass closes.
  - The healer holds the key, and labels are blind to the sorcerer.
  - The miller withdraws his sample, and it goes down with the mule.
  - The blind relabel yields a fourth group, and one wandering result stays unresolved.
  - The convergence is never activated.
  - The shelf ices and the mule stays below.
  - The sorcerer's fear of death stays unresolved ("I don't know.").
- No person or place is named, and review.md's inventory is "None". Do not add names to solve attribution. Use roles and beats.
- The healer's procedural brevity and her permission checks are earned by her role in a consent story. Vary them with attention and humor, but do not make her chatty or warm in the sorcerer's register.

## Dialogue findings

### D1 · Blocking · Healer orders a heading crossed out that she already crossed out

- Where: L101–L117, the "measures of vigor" heading, from the book scene into the healer's pantry
- Text:
> I called them measures of vigor. The healer crossed out the heading.
> "You cannot alter my record."
> She put one finger on the heading in my book. "Then this word is a guess. Cross it out."
- Problem: Ground/staging. At L101 the healer has already struck the heading, and the sorcerer protests the alteration. Sixteen lines later she points at the same heading and tells him to cross it out, as if it were still intact. A reader has to invent an off-page step (he rewrote it) to make the line make sense. This is the story's central wrong word, so the contradiction sits on a key beat.
- Fix: Make the object state consistent with the smallest change. Option A: after L105, add a half-sentence in which he writes the heading back in above her line. Then L117 becomes her demand that he strike it himself. Option B: at L101 she only marks it (a question mark or an underline), and L103 still protests the mark. Keep "You cannot alter my record." and the onions reply.

### D2 · Major · Wordplay patch at the first anomaly

- Where: L67–L77, the tower, right after the third vessel extinguishes the loop
- Text:
> "Not whatever you decide this has become."
> "It has become a correction."
> "Then correct inside the boundary."
> She had a talent for making a boundary sound less like a wall than a sequence of doors
- Problem: Punchline Patch and correction ping-pong. The sorcerer has only swapped vessels, which the comparison permission already covers, so her warning answers no overreach the scene has shown. "A correction" has no clear object: the channel flaw from L61, or his theory? The three lines exist to turn "become" and "correct" into a last word. The L77 narration then explains her rhetorical style instead of letting the scene show it.
- Fix: Keep "Which permission?" and his answer at L69. Cut L71–L75. Replace them with something the healer actually does with the information, such as recording the swap in the ledger or asking what he means to do with that vessel next. The sorcerer can brush past her while mid-experiment. Keep L77's second and third sentences, about his resentment and obedience, and drop the doors image if nothing above now supports it. Illustrative only: She wrote the rest number beside the mark and said nothing, which I chose to take for permission.

### D3 · Major · Tennis match of corrections in the pantry

- Where: L109–L127, the healer's rooms, among the shelves, before the miller's cup strikes the table
- Text:
> "There are frail people in each of your columns."
> "Frail now."
> "Strength is not always visible."
> "You haven't found strongest."
> "The first column sustains the cleanest loop."
> "With itself."
- Problem: Tennis Match and Identical Voices. Ten alternating one-liners of equal weight. Each of hers corrects the precision of his last phrase ("You haven't found strongest.", "With itself."), and each of his is a clipped rationalization. With the tags covered, the two are hard to tell apart except by stance. Nobody dominates or gets distracted, though she is shelving onions with patients waiting. The sorcerer, who per the story's own L155 over-talks under challenge, speaks as tersely as she does.
- Fix: Let the sorcerer hold the floor. He has a theory to defend, so give him one longer, analogy-reaching speech (ranks, vigor, rope and river), which the interruption at L119 already starts. Let the healer answer from what she has seen in the people behind the marks, without naming them, and keep working while she does it. Cut "With itself." and "You haven't found strongest." as word-policing. Keep "Purity often looks like that at first." because it triggers the miller. Illustrative only: "Your first column has a woman in it who stops twice on my stairs," she said, and kept shelving.

### D4 · Major · The miller converges into the house epigram register

- Where: L147–L175, the healer's rooms, the miller confronting the sorcerer, then withdrawing his sample
- Text:
> "I know where it didn't enter."
> "You're making a longer question."
> His hand stayed on the table. "You didn't understand it when you had it."
> "Then tell me when it's enough to be useful."
- Problem: Uniform fluency under pressure, and Punchline Patch. The miller is thinner, gray under the eyes, and has to catch the table when the room moves. His L147 is the most human speech in the story, but after it he answers each of the sorcerer's lines with a shaped comeback. "I know where it didn't enter." has a muddy referent (where did it not enter?). "I am answering you." exists to tee up "You're making a longer question." L169–L173 is a run of retorts ending on the healer's "Then..." rejoinder template. The exchange's real movement is good: he withdraws consent, and the sorcerer loses a sample. The polish makes him a debater rather than an injured man who is tired of being talked over.
- Fix: Keep L133–L147, "No." (L157), "Do I have to leave my sample up there?" and "Then I withdraw it." Replace L151 with something concrete from his body or the event. Delete "You're making a longer question." and let him simply stop answering the sorcerer and turn to the healer, which the outline's pressure behavior asks for. Keep at most one plain retort across L169–L173. Keep the request that motivates the morning scene, but in his words rather than as an aphorism. Illustrative only: "I was on your floor," he said. "That's where it went."

### D5 · Minor · Stray rejoinders between sorcerer and healer

- Where: L93–L95 ("Cold" exchange, tower, evening); L193 (blind relabel, reported speech); L203–L211 (the tower, after the fourth group appears)
- Text:
> "I know what happened."
> "Write that, then."
> She said, "Good. Predict without it."
> "That is not fatigue."
> I put my thumb against my palm. "Revision."
- Problem: Each is a single shaped last word, and together they keep up the story's set-up/payoff rhythm. "Predict without it" leaves "it" hanging between "concealment", "expertise" and the key. "What is it?" exists mainly to tee up "Revision.", a one-word thesis about his theory changing that the healer never takes up. "Write that, then." is the mildest and could stay if the others go.
- Fix: At L193, make her reply plain about the key, or cut her quoted line and report only that she kept the key. At L207–L211, let the sorcerer's denial run long and self-justifying, or let him not answer and ask for the comparison again. Drop "Revision." Leave L93–L95 unless the pass is removing rejoinders wholesale.

### D6 · Major · The apology states its own moral

- Where: L297–L301, the morning account at the table, the sorcerer to the miller
- Text:
> "No. It stayed sealed. I made the link. My spell forced the rhythm. You felt the harm, and I called your blood impure because that protected my theory."
- Problem: Philosopher at the final meaningful exchange with the injured party. The concrete admission is right and earned: I made the link, you felt it, I called your blood impure. But "because that protected my theory" hands the reader the diagnosis that L127–L167 already dramatized. The three-beat staccato before it reads as a prepared statement from a man who has just caught himself hiding in explanation (L289).
- Fix: Keep the ownership (the link, the harm, the word "impure"). Cut or roughen the motive clause so he admits it without explaining himself, or let it come out partial and let the miller's next question cut across it. Do not add a lesson in its place.

### D7 · Major · Miller's last request is a term sheet and gets half an answer

- Where: L313–L323, the morning table, the miller's conditions
- Text:
> "Keep the note about what happened. Not my sample. Not my mark beside your four kinds."
> "It was returned."
> That was all he offered. It was enough to change the record.
- Problem: Ground/uptake, plus a negotiation routine. The miller asks two things: not his sample, and not his mark beside the four kinds. The sorcerer answers only the first. Per L189 he kept copies of the comparisons that used the miller's sample, and per L325 his book "retained comparisons without turning the marks into names". So the mark request is live and goes unanswered, and "Good." reads as the miller accepting something he was never promised. The line's "Keep X. Not Y. Not Z." clause-structure is the healer's permission register, not his. The L323 gloss then says the record changed without showing how.
- Fix: Have the sorcerer answer the mark too, either promising to strike it (then reflect that in the L325 paragraph) or admitting the copied comparisons exist and asking what the miller wants done with them. Let the miller's request come out in his own plainer words. It can be one thing at a time, and he can repeat himself. Keep "Good." / "Is there stew?" / "At noon." as they are. Cut or make concrete "It was enough to change the record."

### D8 · Minor · Small staging and referent slips inside dialogue

- Where: L37 (the opening recitation), L41 (soup, on arrival), L105–L109 (onions)
- Text:
> "Nothing treats an illness. No sample outside its permission. I supply and end the working."
> "Soup's in the lower pot," she said. "You look thin."
> "Then stop leaving it open while you come down for onions."
> The healer carried the onions to the pantry.
- Problem: "I supply and end the working" does not resolve until L51 explains the pulse plate, so the oath ends on a phrase the reader can't parse. The healer has just come in holding the case with both hands and hasn't hung her coat, yet soup is already "in the lower pot". The reader can't tell whether that is a carrier she brought or his stove. The onions line relocates the scene to her rooms only after the fact. And if he came down for onions, it is odd that she carries them away to the pantry.
- Fix: One word or clause each. Make L37's last sentence say what he supplies (his pulse). Anchor the pot (for example, "in the lower pot of the carrier"). Put him in her rooms before L101, or have her take the onions from the pantry rather than to it. Keep the soup/immortality joke and the onions quip itself.

## Prose findings

### P1 · Major · Negation litanies and reversal templates as default rhythm

- Where: throughout. Examples at L35, L57, L241, L279, L325; also in dialogue at L37 and L313
- Text:
> The ledger was not the work. The ledger was what people placed between the work and its completion.
> Not dimmed. Not weakened. Extinguished back to my palm
> The basin remained empty. The silver remained wire. Nothing woke.
> Not kinds of courage. Not measures of vigor. Not purity, longevity, or human worth.
> No treatment. No bodily trial. No claim that one group was safer, stronger, or more human than another.
- Problem: Triplet fragments and "not X / Y" reversals are this narrator's default way to land a point. There are about eleven instances (also L23, L221, L233, L235), and the two in dialogue make other speakers share the tic. The L279 and L325 litanies fall on the discovery and the aftermath, where they turn the story's findings into slogans.
- Fix: Keep one or two where the emphasis is earned (L57's extinguishing is a good candidate). Rewrite the rest as ordinary sentences, and let concrete detail carry the point. Remove the litany form from spoken lines entirely (see D7).

### P2 · Major · Late narration explains the lesson

- Where: L279 (after "Kinds of what?"), L323 (after the miller's last line), L339 (the closing paragraph)
- Text:
> The spell had never seen a soul. It had only met a recurring difference I had been too ambitious to notice.
> and I had learned how quickly a grand design could make people disappear into marks.
- Problem: Philosopher in narration. The miller's withdrawal, the blind relabel and the permission sorting have already shown how people disappear into marks. The closing sentence states it outright, just before the heart emblem restates finitude. At L279 "too ambitious to notice" also misdescribes the story: he noticed the difference at once and misread it.
- Fix: Cut the "and I had learned…" clause at L339 and let the walk downhill and the heartbeat end the story. At L279, keep the plain realization and drop or shorten the soul/ambition gloss (for example, "too ambitious to read plainly"). Cut L323's second sentence (see D7).

### P3 · Minor · One-sentence paragraphs become the pulse of the convergence night

- Where: L217–L241, the final evening before the frame
- Text:
> Outside, snow covered the weather rods to their elbows.
> My palm hovered until the skin cooled.
> Then I lifted each vessel from the frame and put it back in the case.
- Problem: Seven of the thirteen paragraphs in this stretch are a single sentence or a short fragment run (L217, L221, L225 "Me.", L231, L237, L239, L241). Some emphasis is earned at the climax, but the run turns every camera beat into a drumbeat.
- Fix: Fold two or three of these into their neighbors (for example, L237 into L239). Keep "Me." or the L241 beat, not both.

### P4 · Minor · Ambiguous wording and a doubled label for the miller

- Where: L97 (day-two tests), L131 and L143 (the miller's introduction), L189 and L289
- Text:
> The morning after that, authorized comparisons divided recent samples and older ones into the same alliances.
> The resident from the mill had been sitting near the stove
> It had instead put the miller on the floor.
- Problem: L97 can be read as recent samples split from older ones, the opposite of the intended point that sample age made no difference. The same man is "the resident" (L131, L189, L289) and "the miller" (L143). "Resident" is an odd role-label that invites a second-person reading.
- Fix: Reword L97 so recent and older samples sort into the same groups. Pick one label for the miller and use it throughout. "The miller" is clearer.

## Continuity and prompt findings

### C1 · Minor · The final morning's location is unclear

- Where: L289–L339, the morning account through the walk downhill
- Text:
> In the morning, when the resident returned to help clear the lower path, I gave him an account at the healer's table.
> We went downhill together, carrying the records and the vessels due for return. Above us, the convergence frame waited in a cold room with an empty basin.
- Problem: "The healer's table" points to her rooms below the ridge (L107), but the vessels are sorted in the tower (L325), and afterwards the pair walk "downhill" with the frame "above us". The reader has to relocate the whole final scene after the fact.
- Fix: One clause at L289 placing the table in the tower (for example, the table she had taken over by his stove), or have them go down before sorting and fetch nothing. The first option is smaller.

### C2 · Minor · Object and knowledge slips

- Where: L13 (arrival), L139 and L189 (the miller's vessel), L251 (midnight)
- Text:
> Meltwater darkened the shoulders of her wool coat.
> "I don't know which is yours."
> Before removing the resident's vessel from the frame
> I pointed at the unopened case.
- Problem: "Meltwater" six days before the first snow needs a source. At L139 the sorcerer rightly says he doesn't know the miller's vessel, but by L189 he removes it without anyone having told him the mark, and only the healer holds the key. The case at L251 is not "unopened": he took every permitted vessel out (L223) and put them back (L239).
- Fix: Change "Meltwater" to rain or sleet, or name what melted. Add a clause at L177–L189 in which the healer gives him the miller's mark (she keeps the rest of the key). Change "unopened" to "closed" or "packed".

### C3 · Minor · Permission bookkeeping does not quite reconcile

- Where: L27 (the permission count), L163 (the miller's withdrawal), L229 (the pass vanishes)
- Text:
> "Some permit all three. Five permit comparison only. Two want what remains returned before the pass closes."
> "No," she said. "You allowed comparisons until the snow. You can withdraw that now."
> Snow struck the slit in handfuls. The pass had vanished.
- Problem: The miller's "comparisons until the snow" matches none of the categories the healer listed. The reader can only guess that he is one of the two "returned before the pass closes". The second of those two samples is never mentioned again, and the storm closes the way before the end-scene returns. In a story built on scrupulous consent, a sample that may have been kept past its permission is a small but thematically loud crack.
- Fix: Have the healer's L163 wording echo her L27 category, so the reader knows he is one of the two. Then send the other "before the pass closes" sample down with his vessel at L189 (half a clause).
