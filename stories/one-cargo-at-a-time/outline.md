# One Cargo at a Time — provisional outline

## Promised experience

The explicit premise gives us three centuries of dependable windborne trade, floating cities suddenly stranded, one small experimental vessel that can still travel, one cargo at a time, and competing claims to first delivery. I infer the pleasures of an abruptly unfamiliar cloud sea, adventurous flight, and consequential choices between real needs. The cities should feel dependent on each other; reaching one must mean making another wait. Neither a personal relationship, a prescribed moral, nor a particular ending is specified. The supplied image suggests an assured silver-braided woman captain, blue and gold clothing, brass ship fittings, and magnificent cloud cities. Its storm and exact pose are visual possibilities, not added requirements.

## Chosen direction, open to change

Close third person through Captain **Ruzha**, once an officer of Rimehold's prestigious sailing fleet. Her crew aboard the experimental **Midge** consists of **Cobb**, its unsentimental engineer, and **Dessi**, a cargo handler who understands what a ration actually buys. Their working disagreements, rather than a family or romance plot, expose the costs of the route. The tone is tense, expansive adventure with practical humor and an imperfectly hopeful ending.

Floating stone provides buoyancy independently of wind; the Midge's experimental powered airscrew supplies horizontal thrust. Its machinery leaves one cargo cradle and a strict loaded limit. A useful relief load fills that cradle; towing a stranded full-sized vessel is beyond the little engine. Neither restored winds nor instant copies of the engine will remove the constraint during this story.

1. **The silence.** Ruzha takes the Midge out for a short trial as three hundred years of reliable wind stop. Enormous sails hang straight down, flags no longer indicate a route, and the little screw-driven ship alone can leave the harbor. Returning to load, the crew faces urgent demands relayed by the cities' short-code signal lamps: fever supplies for Asterfall, grain for Kersey, heating coal for high, bitterly cold Rimehold. Each city claims its emergency cannot wait. Rimehold invokes Ruzha's old commission and offers her a permanent command if she comes first.

2. **First cargo.** The crew chooses the fever supplies because the medicine has an immediate use that food or heat cannot replace. Ruzha tells the other ports her route rather than selling them simultaneous promises. The first crossing makes the loss of familiar wind routes physical: she must navigate stationary cloud towers and a stranded fleet at unfamiliar angles. At Asterfall, the cargo reaches crowded treatment wards. She also learns that Kersey's advertised stocks are paper stocks: its grain ships are hanging beyond reach. An abstract later delivery becomes hungry people with a deadline.

3. **The demand that changes the voyage.** Back at the depot, grain is secured for Kersey when Rimehold signals that its last heating fuel is nearly gone. Cobb argues for changing course; Ruzha's loyalty pulls the same way. Going there first will strand Kersey through another night. Ruzha sends Rimehold the alternative its admirals have refused: cut up the immobilized wooden fleet for heat, including the celebrated ship she formerly commanded. They will sacrifice their livelihoods and their return to trade if the wind resumes. She takes the grain to Kersey, accepting responsibility for making her own city pay that price.

4. **The second crossing.** With grain aboard, the Midge develops a steering failure while threading the still fleet and electrical cloud. Ruzha and her crew must bring the full cargo through by working the experimental thrust and shifting their own weight; they cannot add rescuers or lighten the load without costing people food. This is the voyage's physical climax, driven by the delivery decision. The grain buys Kersey time, not an end to its hunger. Its dockworkers immediately begin assembling the next load it can export.

5. **Last of the first deliveries.** The coal run brings the crew to Rimehold above bonfires made from its dismantled ships. The city chose to burn them. Ruzha's former command is among the missing hulls, and nobody calls her choice fair. They accept the coal because living people still need it. The closing movement is the Midge leaving again with a new, single cargo and fresh reports from the ports: the cities have begun releasing what other cities need while still arguing fiercely over the queue. The wind remains absent. The ending rewards a crew that has made consequential deliveries and borne the losses of choosing, with a larger continuing voyage ahead of it.
