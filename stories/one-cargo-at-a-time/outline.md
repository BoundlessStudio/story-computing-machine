# One Cargo at a Time — provisional outline

## Promised experience

The explicit premise gives us three centuries of dependable windborne trade, floating cities suddenly stranded, one small experimental vessel that can still travel, one cargo at a time, and competing claims to first delivery. I infer the pleasures of an abruptly unfamiliar cloud sea, adventurous flight, and consequential choices between real needs. The cities should feel dependent on each other; reaching one must mean making another wait. Neither a personal relationship, a prescribed moral, nor a particular ending is specified. The supplied image suggests an assured silver-braided woman captain, blue and gold clothing, brass ship fittings, and magnificent cloud cities. Its storm and exact pose are visual possibilities, not added requirements.

## Chosen direction, open to change

Close third person through Captain **Ruzha**, once an officer of Rimehold's prestigious sailing fleet. Her crew aboard the experimental **Midge** consists of **Cobb**, its unsentimental engineer, and **Dessi**, a cargo handler who understands what a ration actually buys. Their working disagreements, rather than a family or romance plot, expose the costs of the route. The tone is tense, expansive adventure with practical humor and an imperfectly hopeful ending.

Floating stone provides buoyancy independently of wind; the Midge's experimental oil-fired pressure engine drives an airscrew. Its machinery leaves one cargo well and a strict loaded limit. A full relief consignment fills the well; a reduced delivery is possible, and later matters. Towing a stranded full-sized vessel is beyond the little engine. The loading base is **the Spindle**, a bonded transfer station, not another inhabited city: grain, medicine, coal, and engine oil were unloaded there before the stillness and remain reachable by its dock cranes. The Midge carries a small working bunker of oil and refuels there between runs; those few cans cannot replace Rimehold's bulk heating coal. The three ports lie on separate spokes several hours from the depot. Neither restored winds nor instant copies of the engine will remove the constraint during this story.

1. **The silence.** Ruzha takes the Midge out for a short trial as three hundred years of reliable wind stop. Enormous sails hang straight down, flags no longer indicate a route, and the little screw-driven ship alone can leave the Spindle. Several disbelieving days later, stores are running out while the fleet still hangs motionless. The crew faces urgent demands relayed by the cities' short-code signal lamps: fever supplies for Asterfall, grain for Kersey, heating coal for high, bitterly cold Rimehold. Each city claims its emergency cannot wait. Rimehold invokes Ruzha's old commission and offers her a permanent command if she comes first. She explicitly refuses the appointment, but cannot make the people behind that claim cease to matter.

2. **First cargo.** The crew chooses the insulated fever chest because Asterfall's doctors report patients already failing. Ruzha tells the other ports her route rather than selling them simultaneous promises. Loaded to its limit, the Midge takes an alarmingly long climb to clear the Spindle's crane; Cobb makes them leave spare gear behind before departure. The first crossing makes the loss of familiar wind routes physical: she must navigate stationary cloud towers and a stranded fleet at unfamiliar angles. At Asterfall, the cargo reaches crowded treatment wards, and its port officer demands another chest next. Dessi compares the freight records brought from the Spindle with the city's copied trade notices: Kersey's advertised stocks include purchased grain that has never left the transfer station. There is no inaccessible grain ship to invent a workaround for; the Midge can shuttle that waiting grain, one load at a time. An abstract later delivery becomes hungry people with a deadline, through information Dessi has made usable.

3. **The demand that changes the voyage.** Back at the Spindle, grain is secured for Kersey when Rimehold signals that its last heating fuel is nearly gone. Cobb argues for changing course; Ruzha's loyalty pulls the same way. Going there first will strand Kersey through another night. Ruzha asks Rimehold to cut up immobilized wooden ships for heat, including her former command. This is not a previously unnoticed solution or a vain admiral's lesson: the city has already burned its spare timber, and the fleet is its people's earning capacity and means of importing winter food if the wind returns. Its shipwrights have been resisting dismantlement for that reason. Rimehold has not agreed when she leaves with the grain. She can choose the route, but the city must choose whether to destroy its own ships.

4. **The second crossing.** With grain aboard, the Midge's powered steering linkage jams while threading the still fleet beside an electrical cloud. Cobb can force a climb over the obstructing rigging, but says they must shed a specified weight now or risk the ship and all future deliveries. Ruzha wants to keep the full load she promised. Dessi counts exactly how many suppers two sacks would have made, rejects pretending the sacrifice is negligible, and prepares those sacks for release. Ruzha authorizes the loss; Dessi drops the grain into the unreachable cloud sea. The lighter ship can climb; the crew uses its own weight for pitch and trim, and direct jury-lines to the rudder in the screw's slipstream for turning. Rimehold's signal changes to CUTTING as they clear the fleet: the two cities' losses are happening in the same voyage. Kersey receives fewer rations than promised and demands an immediate second load. The crew faces its anger and keeps the coal run next, because the dismantled ships buy Rimehold hours, not safety. Dessi stays with the unloading long enough to give the ration clerks the actual shortage, instead of concealing it in the manifest, and sees the consequence: the unloading gang's promised supper is struck from the allocation so the children's portions can remain.

5. **Last of the first deliveries.** The coal run brings the crew to Rimehold above fires fed with its dismantled ships. The city chose to burn them. Ruzha's former command is among the missing hulls, but so are working freighters whose crews now have neither ship nor earnings. Nobody calls the route fair. Coal is still urgent because the makeshift wood fires consume the fleet faster than people can cut it up; the new load lets them stop destroying hulls for a while. On hearing the crew's report from Kersey, Rimehold's sailors release some of their vessels' voyage biscuit. The closing movement is the Midge leaving with that single consignment for Kersey: a concrete reciprocal delivery, small beside the need, while the ports still argue fiercely over the queue. The wind remains absent. The ending rewards consequential deliveries and losses borne, without turning the crew into arbiters who have solved every claim.
