# Provisional rethinking: The Unspent Star

Orya is the adult cosmic woman in Image #1: a dark, star-filled body, concealed face, rose hood and flowing perforated mantle lined in cyan. She floats above planetary rings she has shaped from her own light and mantle threads: one finite creation material. An early ordinary act shows her committing a wandering dream-star to an orbit, condensing its unused capacity into world matter and leaving a small hole in her mantle. A dream-star is seed-like light, not another person. She has never known another person. Other lights appear beneath her skin, enter her sky while she dreams, and disappear again. She thinks this is how her universe works. One rose star she has kept unspent for many cycles now moves beside her without being summoned; she wants to keep that particular unpredictable light and see what it becomes.

Outside, the cyan-hooded keeper of Image #2 carries her whole universe in glass. They bottled her because seeing its whole pattern through sealed curvature makes her unspent possibilities readable as other people's futures. They harvest dream-stars to fuel divination; an entire civilization's living readers depend upon that source. This is local, not the origin of all shared-universe prophecy. The keeper knows she is a person but has justified taking futures she has not yet used. Their pressing question is personal: which way can their daughter's vessel escape the collapse of a blue star? A glimpse of that vessel and waiting readers gives the question pressure. They select Orya's existing rose star because keeping it unspent has made it rich in possibilities, sufficient for that difficult answer. The query does not manufacture her desire.

A consultation takes a possible creation from Orya, and a thread of her mantle follows it. The perforations record those losses as well as her own creations. She resists the pull on the rose star, follows it beyond her familiar rings using her mantle, and touches an invisible curved sky. A vast hand becomes visible through it. The reader sees the outside consultation before she understands its meaning; neither her existence nor the bottle is a late trick.

Extraction requires the whole pattern to remain visible through the closed lens. Material she has made into a world cannot supply a prediction. Orya can spend the rose star on an opaque ring/shell of actual matter just inside the glass. It would shield every remaining dream-star, defeating this reading and later harvesting. To reach through that shell, the keeper would have to open the vessel and destroy the sealed curvature. This is a current practical defeat, not a promise of immunity from all later violence.

Possible ending: Orya makes that private sky herself. The keeper still holds her physically confined universe, now dark to their sight, without the route they wanted. She loses the moving rose light she loved and spends some of her mantle; she keeps her remaining possibilities and knows the hands. No speech grants liberty, and no daughter's survival is charged as a debt she owes. The closing image belongs to the cosmic woman beneath stars still visible from inside, with real incomplete freedom and an unreadable future.
