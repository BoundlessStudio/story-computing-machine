# Provisional rethinking: The Unspent Star

Orya is the adult cosmic woman in Image #1: a dark, star-filled body, concealed face, rose hood and flowing perforated mantle lined in cyan. She floats above the planetary rings she has shaped from her own finite light and mantle threads. She has never known another person. Stars appear beneath her skin, enter her sky while she dreams, and disappear again. She thinks this is how her universe works. One new rose star moves beside her without being summoned; she wants to keep this particular unpredictable light and see what it becomes.

Outside, the cyan-hooded keeper of Image #2 carries her whole universe in glass. They bottled her because sealed curvature makes her unspent possibilities readable as other people's futures. They harvest released dream-stars to fuel divination; an entire civilization's living readers depend upon that source. This is a local source, not the origin of all shared-universe prophecy. The keeper knows she is a person but has justified taking futures she has not yet used. Their pressing question is personal: which way can their daughter's vessel escape the collapse of a blue star? They need the rose star's light for that answer.

A consultation has an inward consequence: a vanished star takes a possible creation from Orya, and a thread of her mantle follows it. The perforations record those losses as well as her own earlier creations. She resists the pull on the rose star, follows it beyond her familiar rings using a mantle-thread tether, and touches an invisible curved sky. A vast hand becomes visible through it. The reader sees the outside consultation before Orya understands its meaning; neither her existence nor the bottle is a late trick.

Extraction can take a released possibility, but not a future she has already made into something present. The keeper cannot force her protected star out without opening the living seal and losing the lens that makes prophecy possible. Orya can spend the star herself to make a ring reach through an opening: her body needs an orbit to keep its cohesion in the larger dark. That passage would cost the moving companion she wanted to preserve, some of her finite mantle, and the old enclosed shape of her home. The keeper would lose possession of the oracle and the certainty they seek for their daughter.

Possible ending: contact through glass brings an irreversible choice, carried by hands, pressure, and the star rather than a courtroom speech. The keeper releases the seal before receiving an answer. Orya spends her rose future on a first orbit outside, and chooses whether to step onto it. No prophecy certifies either her freedom or the daughter's survival. The closing image should belong to the cosmic woman acting under a sky the keeper can no longer read.
