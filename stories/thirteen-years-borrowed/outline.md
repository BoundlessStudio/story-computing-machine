# Outline

## Story

- Premise and central promise: For thirteen years Tamaris has survived by inhabiting one consenting person per day while tracing the body from which she was torn. She finds Nivra living in it as a distinct person. Resolve the search through Tamaris's physical return and a choice between reclaiming stability and passing her displacement to Nivra.
- Focal pressure or attachment: Tamaris wants her own hands, face, and uninterrupted future back, but now depends on rules protecting those who carry her. Nivra's thirteen-year life challenges bodily ownership without making Tamaris's loss unreal. Today's host, Calo, promised transport and limited control, not help with a seizure.
- Counterforce or complication: Nivra controls access and permits one shared visit only after Tamaris respects Calo's refusals. She has bodily habits and early sensory fragments continuous with Tamaris but no stolen autobiography. Calo cares for Nivra, distrusts Tamaris's desperation, and cannot make the final choice for her.
- POV, distance, and information limit: Close first-person past through Tamaris during one borrowed day. She receives Calo's present sensations, not memories; his internal responses and changes of control remain perceptible. Nivra stays external until consented co-occupation. Familiar scars and reflexes cannot prove the anomaly's origin or ownership of Nivra's mind.
- Governing movement and time shape: A private morning-to-midnight countdown, mostly inside Nivra's modest house. Recognition accumulates through ordinary bodily use and Calo's interruptions. Search becomes negotiation, then brief return. End after Tamaris decides who remains anchored and completes her next transfer; no public proof, cure, or philosophical coda.
- Speculative rule or ordinary-world constraint: Entry requires skin contact and deliberate acceptance. Occupation ends at midnight or revocation; the conscious host chooses observation, shared movement, or possession and can force stillness. Tamaris gains no memories or skills and has thirteen heartbeats at midnight to reach a new consenting host; nobody can host consecutive days. In her original body alone, holding exclusive control through midnight anchors her permanently and displaces Nivra into daily borrowing. Releasing control preserves Nivra and expels Tamaris normally.

## Voice

- Narrative texture: Tamaris counts time, exits, permissions, and borrowed sensations with itinerant precision; slow when her original body's mundane habits defeat those categories. Keep Nivra's interiority unavailable except through consented sensation.
- Conversational texture: Mix internal interruptions, guarded negotiation, pauses for food and bodily comfort, and single-purpose logistics. Silence and revoked movement can answer.
- Rhetorical ownership: Tamaris uses routes, ledgers, and terms; Calo uses immediate bodily facts and promises; Nivra uses dated acts, preferences, and consequences rather than identity theory.
- Pressure behavior: Tamaris narrows into repetition and unfinished requests; Calo becomes concise and interrupts motor control; Nivra answers less, moves away, then states conditions.
- Anti-default: Avoid three eloquent minds debating whether bodies define people, hosts becoming consent lecturers, or dialogue explaining the final action's meaning.

## Beats

1. Calo admits Tamaris at dawn under agreed limits and carries the relay case passed among hosts. En route to Nivra's house, a disagreement about pace, breakfast, and arriving as himself establishes shared control as practical rather than ceremonial.
2. Nivra opens the door in Tamaris's original body. A damaged knuckle, balance correction, and hidden scar confirm it, while her posture denies the fantasy of a preserved former self. Calo takes control when Tamaris reaches without permission.
3. Nivra gives bounded history: after separation, the empty living body slowly developed responses and awareness without Tamaris's autobiography. Records support dates, not ontology. Tamaris tests details compulsively; Nivra stops cooperating when treated as evidence.
4. Food, fatigue, and an old injury keep Calo's body consequential. He withdraws movement when pressed. Nivra performs familiar tasks differently, while one involuntary reflex gives Tamaris painful continuity neither can dismiss.
5. Nivra offers one consented entry before midnight. Permanent anchoring is possible, but it transfers daily displacement to her. An unnamed volunteer waits nearby, giving Tamaris a viable alternative rather than an ethically predetermined choice.
6. Shared sensation confirms both homecoming and Nivra's independent control. Near midnight, Tamaris starts closing Nivra out; Nivra's diminishing fluency makes the consequence immediate.
7. Tamaris takes exclusive control long enough to know she could stay, then returns movement before midnight. The loss brings neither gratitude nor friendship. Nivra retains the body; Tamaris reaches the volunteer within thirteen heartbeats, ending the search while her condition continues.

## Continuity

- Canon used: Independent impossible systems may coexist; only living agency uses magic; consent is mechanism-specific; transformed or continued people retain full personhood and moral consequence.
- Boundaries and unknowns: Keep these rules local. Nivra is not Tamaris's copy, soul, illness, or lesser replacement; bodily continuity does not settle personhood. The separation's source, Nivra's emergence, and any cure remain unknown. Treat early involuntary occupation as harm, never adventure; present-day entry is explicit, revocable, consequential, and non-graphic.
