# Opus review: Thirteen Years Borrowed

Independent audit for a later editing pass. It is not a `review.md` verdict,
does not change canon state, and does not authorize edits by itself.

## Verdict

- Dialogue: REVISE — the scar demand at the first key exchange has broken logic and an unclear referent, the inner/spoken marking breaks the story's own voice-lending medium, and Calo's inner voice keeps echoing and correcting Tamaris.
- Prose: PASS — concrete and controlled; reversals and thesis sentences cluster in the climax, but only at Minor level.
- Continuity and prompt: REVISE — the damaged fingers move from the right hand to the left, and the midnight opening has a leftover "dawn chill"; the prompt itself is fully delivered.
- Edit scope: targeted-dialogue
- Existing review.md said: Verdict PASS, "Dialogue: PASS"; disagree — it missed the scar-exchange Ground failure, the voice-marking drift, and the right/left hand contradiction.

This story's dialogue is better than the collection's default. Calo's spoken voice is loose, bodily and funny. Nivra's withholding answers put real pressure on Tamaris. The climax runs on action, a lie and unfinished speech rather than argument. The failures are local. The first real exchange between Tamaris and Nivra turns on a demand whose reasoning and referent don't hold. The story's own medium (who has the mouth; italics versus quotation marks) drifts in the bakery and the washroom. And Calo's inner voice falls into the house echo-and-correct register and gives the same rebuke twice. Fix Ground and medium first. Then move Calo's inner lines toward what he won't say aloud, and leave the consent-terms register alone, because the premise earns it.

## Dialogue findings

### D1 · Blocking · Scar demand has no workable logic or referent

- Where: L161–L173, first exchange at Nivra's table
- Text:
> The knuckle could be in the letters. The hand—Calo could have described that. If I see the scar—
> Then tell me what I broke it on.
- Problem: Ground/referent and Ground/logic. "The knuckle" and "the hand" are the same evidence: the lagging fingers from the tendon damage at L115–L117. The line presents one doubt as two. It also doesn't explain how an injury Tamaris just saw with her own eyes could be undermined by being "in the letters". That needs a stated reason, such as someone faking the lag or Tamaris seeing what she has been told to expect. "Calo could have described that" has no recoverable listener: described to whom, and why would it matter? Then "what I broke it on": the nearest "it" is the scar (Nivra's "a drawing of it"), but "broke" fits the knuckle, not a scar. The reader can't tell which injury Tamaris is testing. That blunts the payoff at L399–L401, where Nivra touches the scar and Tamaris answers *A roof nail. I was twelve.*
- Fix: Make L169 a single doubt the reader can follow, in Tamaris's obsessive register: the fingers are in every description she has sent out, so anyone could learn the lag or be chosen for it. Let Nivra's "You sent a drawing of it to half the coast" knock down the scar too. Make L173 ask plainly for the scar's cause, the one thing the drawings don't show, so that L401 answers exactly that question. Illustrative only: "Then tell me what cut it." Keep "No." / "You know." and Nivra's turn to "Is she using your hands?"

### D2 · Major · Inner speech and lent voice are marked inconsistently

- Where: L67–L85 (bakery), L157 (asking to speak), L267 (washroom), L289–L339 (table after the washroom)
- Text:
> “The next tram—”
> “I don’t eat.”
> “Can I speak?” I asked inside.
> “I need—”
- Problem: Ground/staging: who is speaking aloud, and with whose permission? The story's medium is voice-lending. Italics are inner speech, quotation marks mean Tamaris is using a host's mouth, and the text notes each opening (L55 "after he opened the voice to me", L91 "loosening his throat and tongue", L159 "Calo opened his voice"). The bakery breaks this. The exchange opens in italics (L67–L69), then Tamaris's lines at L71, L75, L81 and L85 switch to quotation marks with no voice lent. That happens in a public bakery, and before L91 says Calo is only now loosening his tongue for her. L157 puts quotation marks on a line explicitly spoken "inside". At L267 Tamaris speaks aloud after Calo shut the voice at L255. *Not with my mouth.* and "He took it back" imply she seized his mouth, a real transgression that sets up L445–L447, but the seizure is never shown, so it reads as a punctuation slip. After the washroom nobody reopens the voice, yet her lines at L291–L313 and L339 are spoken aloud at the table.
- Fix: Use one convention throughout. In the bakery, pick one of two routes. Either set Tamaris's lines in italics, with Calo answering her silently and speaking aloud only to order. Or add a half-line where Calo opens his voice, and let the counter clerk notice a man arguing with himself. Change L157 to italics without quotation marks. At L267, add a clause showing the words leave Calo's mouth before he has opened it to her, so the seizure clearly belongs to her. When they return to the table (around L287), add a half-line where Calo lets her speak again.

### D3 · Major · Calo's inner voice runs on echo-and-correct rulings

- Where: L67–L69 (bakery), L231–L239 (table), L261–L269 (washroom)
- Text:
> You brought me here because you believed she was.
> I brought you because I promised both of you one meeting.
> I did not promise the meeting would go your way.
> You haven’t started. You’re checking inventory.
- Problem: Correction ping-pong, Punchline Patch and a repeated verdict. Aloud, Calo is loose and physical. Inside, nearly every line hands Tamaris's own words back corrected: "We agreed" / "We agreed I'd get you there. We did not agree…"; "You brought me here because…" / "I brought you because…"; "We haven't finished." / "You haven't started." He also slips into uncontracted ruling syntax that he never uses aloud ("I did not promise", "We did not agree"), which pulls him toward Nivra's term-setting cadence. The table and washroom exchanges deliver the same verdict twice ("missing parcel", then "checking inventory"). The washroom therefore adds leverage (*I can walk out*) but no new understanding. "You haven't started. You're checking inventory." exists mainly to land the callback to L55.
- Fix: At the table, keep *Making her prove she's your missing parcel.*, which is a concrete image of his own. Cut or replace the L237–L239 back-and-forth with something Calo cares about but won't say in Nivra's hearing: Nivra being cross-examined in her own kitchen, the cup she set out for Tamaris (L149), his own exhaustion. Illustrative only: *She put out a cup for you. You haven't looked at it.* In the washroom, keep *I can walk out* and *Not with my mouth.* and replace L265 with something other than a second verdict. The narration at L259 already shows what Tamaris missed (his sweat, his knee), so he could name that, or simply refuse. Once those two stop following the pattern, L69 can stay; the rug line is funny.

### D4 · Minor · Opening terms: "repeat" and "Front step to front step" don't match what is said

- Where: L11–L23, midnight handover
- Text:
> At midnight, Calo made me repeat the terms while the woman carrying me held his wrist.
> “Front step to front step,” he said. “I walk up as myself.
- Problem: Ground/staging and referent. The narration says Tamaris repeats the terms, but Calo recites them. Tamaris only adds "Unless she asks" and says "the rest" when he prompts her. "Front step to front step" gives the reader no pair of steps to recover: they are standing at a gate, and Nivra's step comes much later. The gist comes through (he keeps his own body until Nivra's door), but the opening lines shouldn't make the reader work for it. Calo's "Say the rest." lets her added exception pass without comment. That works as silent acceptance, and it pays off when Nivra holds out her hand at L359.
- Fix: Either have Calo state the terms and Tamaris say them back, or change "repeat" to something like "go over". Anchor the steps (from here to her front step) or cut the phrase and let "I walk up as myself" carry the meaning. Keep the procedural register here; the consent premise earns it.

### D5 · Minor · Exchanges capped by a last word or a narrator gloss

- Where: L87 (bakery), L97–L99 (coffee), L301–L303 (Nivra's "No."), L313–L315 (the offer)
- Text:
> He chewed. The answer was the chewing.
> Everything was a test. I simply no longer said so.
> The single word had no mercy in it, but no accusation either. A date. A consequence.
> “Then we’ll know what it costs.”
- Problem: Epigram and last-word lines, plus subtext glosses. Nivra's flat "No." at L301 already lands, and L303 then tells the reader how to take it. "Then we'll know what it costs" is an aphorism in reply to a real question ("And if I can stay?") at the story's pivot. At that moment Nivra would more plausibly refuse to answer, set a condition, or say something concrete about what she stands to lose. Each instance is small on its own, but together they give scene after scene the same neat closing click.
- Fix: Cut L303, or keep only a physical beat. Cut the second sentence of L87 and let the chewing stand. Cut L99; Calo's "That wasn't a test." has already raised the question. At L315, give Nivra a plain, specific answer, or silence with the action she already has (rubbing the knuckle). Keep "And if I can stay?", which the climax needs.

## Prose findings

### P1 · Minor · Reversals and thesis sentences crowd the climax narration

- Where: L365, L383, L431, L435, L469; also L33 and L223
- Text:
> The body was not preserved. It had gone on without me and become more exact in someone else’s keeping.
> It was not a voice or a revelation. It was the simple absence of distance.
> The cost was not death. That made it harder.
- Problem: "Not X. It was Y." reversals and statements of the theme bunch up in the last 150 lines. Other examples: L383 "not as memories, not as thoughts I could read, but as resistance with shape", and L365's "rules were the nearest thing I had to a body". The action already carries the meaning (the lagging fingers, the fourteen steps, the unfinished *I need—*, the opened hand), so these sentences explain what the reader already feels.
- Fix: Keep at most one per scene. At L431, drop "The body was not preserved." and keep the second sentence alone; it is the best of these. Recast L435 as a plain positive statement. Cut L469, since L467 already states the cost. Trim the last clause of L365. Leave L467's mechanics alone; the reader needs them.

### P2 · Minor · Isolated stock beat and a misfiring time clause

- Where: L417 (sofa, near midnight), L61 (station bench)
- Text:
> Calo watched from the sofa, jaw tight with pain.
> By the time the shutters began to rise, Nivra lived forty-three minutes away
- Problem: "jaw tight with pain" is a stock beat in a story that otherwise renders Calo's knee concretely. L61 attaches a fixed distance to a time clause, so it reads as though Nivra's distance changed at dawn.
- Fix: Give Calo's pain a concrete action, such as a hand clamped on the knee or the leg pushed straight along the cushion. Split L61 into two facts: the shutters rising, and a forty-three-minute direct tram ride.

## Continuity and prompt findings

### C1 · Blocking · The damaged fingers change hands

- Where: L115 (doorway), L407–L409 (shared body)
- Text:
> her right hand opened to counterbalance, two middle fingers lagging behind the others.
> At eleven-forty-three, she lent me the left hand.
> I opened and closed it. The middle fingers lagged.
- Problem: Continuity contradiction on the story's main identifying mark. At the door the lag is in the right hand; in the shared body it is in the left. The tendon damage is the first proof Tamaris accepts (L117), and the story keeps returning to the damaged fingers (L127, L315, L381, L517).
- Fix: Change L407 to "the right hand". Nothing else then conflicts: L333's right-hand catch, L455's closed left hand and the right-then-left release at L481–L483 all still work. Optionally, L455 could claim the damaged hand instead ("I closed our right hand"), but that is not required.

### C2 · Minor · Opening clock and contact staging

- Where: L11–L35, midnight handover
- Text:
> the dawn chill under his cuffs
> The woman’s thumb trembled against the pulse in his wrist.
> I felt his acceptance before skin met skin
- Problem: The handover happens at midnight (L11; L15 "the day just ended"; L61 station bench until the first tram), yet Calo's body registers "the dawn chill", a leftover from the outline's dawn handover. The ending makes midnight exact: expulsion, then thirteen heartbeats. So a leisurely recital of terms "At midnight", with Tamaris still inside the woman, has to happen just before midnight, with the crossing at the stroke. And the woman is already holding his wrist skin to skin when "skin met skin" is presented as something about to happen.
- Fix: Change "dawn chill" to a night detail. Place the recital just before midnight. Either move the woman's hand from his wrist into his upturned palm, or cut "before skin met skin".

### C3 · Minor · Policy nit: plumbing as washroom texture

- Where: L273, washroom
- Text:
> For several minutes we listened to the pipe tick.
- Problem: The style guide's "No default water infrastructure" entry covers passing plumbing texture in authorized revisions, and the prompt does not call for it.
- Fix: Use another small sound, such as the stuck latch, the cart outside or a neighbor. Low priority.
