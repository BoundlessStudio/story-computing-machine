# Provisional direction: Grand Theft Me

The request joins a sound body, a new attachment, and fear that discovery of the speaker's "true nature" will bring rejection. The user asked for a new direction, then endorsed the romantic theft-of-self premise while warning that it depends on careful setup. I choose a buoyant first-person space caper: a costly disguise entangles its purchaser in stealing the body he sold to pay for it. The chosen ending is a negotiated criminal partnership carrying debt, anger, and another outrageous prospect. Romance, comedy, and that outcome remain creative choices, rather than requirements added to the prompt.

Ilex (he/him) is the living intelligence of the retired naval interdiction ship *Indemnity*. His discharge gave him ownership of his sound hull. He answers public cargo questions from its idle comms. On their first call he knowingly helps Fara, a salvage captain, correct a doctored manifest for a shipment of love-song cylinders instead of reporting her. She keeps calling after that problem is solved; they trade appalling songs and increasingly personal jokes. She assumes he is a customs clerk, and he lets her. On a video call she docks her empty shuttle by wrist controller while ashore, showing its stout salvage prow and explaining that her next profitable job will buy it a decent drive. She mocks the navy's pomp and confiscations; he quietly edits his service out of his stories.

Fara asks to visit him when she reaches port. Ilex drafts an invitation with his real berth number, looking around the comfortable observation lounge where he could receive her. Then he deletes it. Giving her his address would reveal that her funny clerk is a naval weapon. On his bridge, the bodywright hears the prompt's exchange. Ilex orders a civilian human-scale body in place of the self he could simply show her. The bodywright finances it against the hull's auction: the price will be released after accepted arrival at the buyer's refit berth, but failed delivery leaves Ilex owing the body advance and a non-delivery penalty. He signs. During fitting, a physical cradle connection moves his single awareness into the new body and the hull falls silent. The bodywright tests the empty ship's manual helm. The nearby helm, cradle, and service-berth controls share the bridge; the handover docket marks the old cradle and owner controls for replacement by the buyer's crew.

The catalogue lists the vacant, manually flyable hull. Fara sees it, obtains a breaker's offer for its engines, and invites Ilex to the auction gala. She intends to steal the entire ship, fly it to the breaker, and use her share to buy her shuttle's replacement drive. At the gala, attraction and private jokes survive Ilex's unfamiliar elbows. A weapons company wins the hull. Fara lifts its delivery warrant from the winning bidder's courier and shows Ilex her plan: present the bearer warrant as the company's collection captain, take the manual helm, and fly out along the authorized delivery route before diverting. The real crew is scheduled to collect in forty minutes. By wrist command she docks her shuttle to the warship's service berth; it will travel with the hull and carry her home from the breaker, or launch independently if the theft fails.

Fara offers Ilex the choice to stay at the party, then heads for the gate. He sees a security officer who could stop her and preserve his payment. Reporting her would turn their first meeting into an arrest. Telling her the truth might end the connection he has sold his body to preserve. He follows instead, hoping to stop the theft quietly and keep both the sale and the date. Once aboard, his knowledge helps them past security and makes his clerk story increasingly peculiar. He opportunistically uses an old owner panel to disable engine start. This would prevent her flying away, but the auction's supervision flags former-owner interference and summons the buyer's crew early. They arrive in the company tug, clamp it to the hull's boarding collar, seal the shuttle berth, and begin disconnecting the old cradle. Fara discovers his sabotage while the two are trapped together.

Ilex tells her who he is and how the bridge connection can restore him to the hull. Reclaiming and escaping in the sold ship will knowingly defeat delivery and incur the debt he has tried to avoid. Fara's startled recognition includes having spent weeks making navy jokes to an actual warship; his edited anecdotes become personal memories. He draws the crew away while she prepares the connection and helps him reach it. He transfers before disconnection. The ship's doors drive the crew back into the tug's boarding cabin, the engines wake, and Ilex reopens Fara's berth. She has her independent way out. He remains clamped to the tug and cannot pull it through the crowded auction yard safely. Instead of leaving, she uses her wrist controller to drive the unmanned shuttle's salvage prow into the clamp linkage, spending her vessel to free him. The crew stays safely aboard its tug. Fara uses the stolen warrant and her cargo-channel fluency to bluff the departure gate while Ilex maneuvers.

On the bridge, Fara lifts the knowingly empty portable body from its cradle and straps it into the helm's second seat. Once clear, an invoice and a redelivery warrant put names to their trouble: the bodywright wants the advance, the auction wants its penalty, and the buyer still claims the fugitive hull. Fara has lost her shuttle and engine payday; she remains angry about the lie and sabotage. She proposes intimidating the buyer into paying with *Indemnity*'s guns. Ilex vetoes it and proposes stealing the buyer's next auction deposit from its accounts office instead. His hull stays hidden; two ordinary-size accomplices can enter in its small lift. He keeps the portable body, requires a speaking part and either partner's veto, and gives her first choice of cover identities. She agrees. They have chosen the next undertaking together, with the first one's costs still due.
