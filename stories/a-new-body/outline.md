# Provisional direction: Grand Theft Me

The request explicitly joins a sound body, a new attachment, and fear that discovery of the speaker's "true nature" will bring rejection. Its pleasure may include discovering what the speaker is and what changing bodies costs or makes possible; that is an interpretation, not a prescribed lesson about acceptance. Romance, genre, scale, and outcome remain open. "try again" and "a new direction" call for a fresh story rather than improvements to the rejected one. My choice is a buoyant first-person space heist with romantic jeopardy: the new body makes the requester an accomplice in stealing the old one. The payoff will be an audacious escape and a consequential change in the pair's relationship, rather than a confession followed by tentative reassurance.

The requester, Ilex (he/him), is the living intelligence of the retired naval interdiction ship *Indemnity*. While waiting in a commercial orbital yard, he has spent months talking over a public cargo-information channel with Fara, a salvage captain who makes excellent jokes about the navy. He has let her believe he is an ordinary customs clerk. Her invitation to meet at a glittering ship auction sounds like a first date. "Wear shoes you can run in" should have been a clue.

We open with the prompt's exchange between Ilex and a bodywright. A human-scale manufactured body will hold Ilex's single, transferable mind. The expensive purchase is financed by selling the emptied warship; the auction closes that night. A transfer requires physical connection to a cradle, so Ilex cannot remotely leap into the hull when trouble starts. These are chosen story rules, not user requirements. Ilex wants to be someone Fara could choose without having to reconsider everything she has said about warships.

At the auction, newly embodied Ilex tries to manage champagne, elbows, and romantic conversation. Fara reveals her elaborate evening plan: steal *Indemnity* before its buyer, a private weapons company, takes delivery. She intends to sell its engines. She genuinely likes her funny correspondent and assumes their first date being a felony is a considerable attraction. Ilex's little lie now faces an expensive consequence: his hoped-for lover plans to dismantle the body Ilex has occupied for a century. Refusing the heist would expose the clerk fiction and abandon Fara inside a scheme already in motion. Ilex goes with her.

Inside the emptied hull, Ilex's intimate knowledge gets them past security but makes Fara increasingly suspicious of this supposed clerk. Comedy becomes jeopardy when the buyer's delivery crew arrives early, seals the exits, and starts the departure sequence with the pair aboard. The bridge's new control unit will soon overwrite Ilex's old command access. Fara's escape craft is attached to a service berth scheduled to be jettisoned. The transfer cradle is on the bridge, across a compartment occupied by the crew.

Ilex tells Fara what the cradle does and who has been speaking. He draws the crew away; Fara prepares the bridge connection and returns to help him reach it. He transfers before the lockout. Now the building around the thieves comes alive: doors divide the crew, and Ilex takes back the engines. He opens the service berth so Fara can leave safely in her own shuttle, while he tries to shake the buyer's tug. She has the escape she planned. Instead she launches her shuttle empty into the tug's docking jaws, sacrificing her own ship to force its clamps off his hull. On the bridge, she lifts the expensive empty portable body from its transfer cradle, carries the smaller boyfriend over one shoulder to the helm, and straps him into a seat. She knows Ilex is presently the warship; she is preserving the small-body possibility he wanted before the heist, with no military usefulness. She takes over the traffic bluff through the auction yard's departure gate while Ilex maneuvers. Her choice costs her independent escape and transport, rather than merely using the only available getaway.

They escape with both bodies, an outrageous auction debt, and Fara's anger intact. She proposes a second date: use *Indemnity* to frighten the buyer into paying their losses. Ilex vetoes it. He proposes stealing the buyer's next auction deposit from its accounts office instead; his hull remains hidden, and two people can enter the office in the very small lift. He will use the portable body, she will have to leave him a speaking part in the con, and either partner gets a veto. Fara agrees and demands first choice of the cover identities. Their actual negotiation commits them to a criminal partnership and preserves his new body as an eagerly chosen capability after his nature is known. The payoff is another audacious undertaking between two people who have discovered how troublesome their hoped-for date really is.
