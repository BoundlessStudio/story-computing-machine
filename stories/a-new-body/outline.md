# All My Hands

The request promises a vulnerable speculative encounter: someone wants a new body even though the present one has no “problem or defect,” because meeting someone has made discovery of their “true nature” frightening. The distinctive pleasure I infer is the collision of an extraordinary bodily possibility with the ordinary risk of wanting a particular person's love, including discovery of what the requester actually is. The request specifies neither a romantic relationship nor acceptance, rejection, a moral, genre, tone, scale, mechanism, or outcome. Those remain choices. I intend romantic suspense with strange physical beauty, some awkward humor, and a consequential answer to the opening request.

## Provisional direction

Close third person with Virelle, an adult woman whose healthy native body is a linked flight of small ivory-and-gold, mothlike living forms: one person with many simultaneous points of touch and sight, not a committee of separate people. She has fallen for Sef, an adult human man, through several months in a nightly voice-reading circle and their subsequent private conversations. They have arranged their first physical meeting. He assumes she is human; she has allowed that assumption. An earlier confidant's revulsion gives her fear a history, without determining Sef's response.

Doctor Anjet can grow a human-shaped body that genuinely transmits touch, speaks, walks, and holds things while housing Virelle's distributed life. It has no other mind to displace. The maker can supply a body, including successful concealment, but cannot change Virelle's underlying nature or secure Sef's love. These are local embodiment mechanics, not shared-universe rules. The new body will be a welcome bodily choice as well as a hiding place; the ending will keep it. The central conflict is whether Virelle will let the first meeting succeed through concealment when she finally has the means to make it succeed openly.

## Possible scenes

- Open in Anjet's growing room with the user's complete dialogue. The apparently seated customer loosens the outline she has gathered into and settles across the chair, its arms, and the maker's measuring frame. The body has no defect: Virelle enjoys this dispersed, mobile existence. Anjet accepts the commission rather than prescribing an emotional cure.
- In fittings, Virelle chooses details for herself rather than copying an imagined ideal woman. She wants a warm palm and the ability to walk beside Sef. Her new body offers real pleasures and unfamiliar coordination; its hand can conceal a little hinged opening through which her native forms enter and leave. She asks that the seam be invisible when closed. The maker supplies it. She can pass.
- At their evening meeting in a public flowering glasshouse, Sef recognizes her voice and their private joke. There is ordinary chemistry, awkwardness, and a disagreement or surprise that makes him more than the recipient of her confession. Virelle enjoys being able to offer him her arm and feel his pulse through two warm hands. The concealment works; no malfunction or emergency forces disclosure.
- Sef describes a future meeting as though her humanity is settled. Virelle could keep the evening easy. Instead she names what she has withheld and opens her palm. Enough of her native body emerges to show what she means while the rest remains within the new body, letting her continue speaking and choose whether to retreat.
- Sef recoils before he can help it. He asks for room and then asks a concrete, imperfect question about whether the moving forms are all her. Virelle answers without performing a defense of her personhood. She offers to leave; his decision must be his. He makes a smaller, credible choice than a declaration of eternal love: he asks how to touch her without hurting her, then lets her settle across his offered hand.
- End with their first deliberate contact in both forms. Virelle still wants the body she commissioned, including its new walking feet and warm hand. The hidden opening stays open as they resume their walk. Her fear has cost her something and has been answered by this person's tentative act, without guaranteeing their future or making the bodily change a mistake.

The main risk is a predetermined self-acceptance lesson that turns Sef into an obliging examiner and the body maker into a counselor. Protect the real usefulness of the transformation, Sef's independent desires and hesitation, Virelle's voluntary disclosure, and the specific physical contact the ending makes newly possible.
