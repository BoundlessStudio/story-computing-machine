# Provisional direction: Grand Theft Me

The request joins a sound body, a new attachment, and fear that discovery of the speaker's "true nature" will bring rejection. The user's "try again" and "a new direction" require a fresh story. In reply to the proposed romantic space heist, "the idea is good but is dose work if you dont setup carefully" endorses that premise while making its setup a particular concern; it does not specify every repair or approve every outlined beat. My chosen experience is a buoyant first-person caper in which a new body entangles its purchaser in stealing the old one. I infer that attraction, coincidence, bodily capabilities, and costly choices must be earned through enjoyable scenes before their payoffs. The intended ending is a bargained criminal partnership with real debt and residual anger. These tone and outcome choices remain the writer's.

Ilex (he/him) is the living intelligence of the retired naval interdiction ship *Indemnity*. His discharge gave him ownership of his sound hull. From its idle comms he answers public cargo questions. Fara, a salvage captain, keeps calling after her cargo problem is solved: they trade appalling port love songs and increasingly personal jokes. He has allowed her to assume he is a customs clerk. She mocks the navy's pomp and appetite for confiscation; he edits its decades out of his anecdotes. On one video call she is ashore, docking her empty, battered shuttle by wrist controller while keeping their conversation going. Its stout salvage prow and her wish for better engines emerge in the same encounter. Her suggestion that they hear a band together gives Ilex a concrete reason to seek a portable body. This attachment exists before she needs a heist accomplice.

The prompt's exchange happens with the bodywright on Ilex's bridge. The bodywright supplies a human-scale body on an advance secured against the old hull's sale, and posts the vacant hull in that evening's auction catalogue. Ilex learns the price of defeating that sale: he would owe the advance and the auction's penalty. The fitting makes the single-mind rule visible: a physical bridge-cradle connection sends his awareness into the portable body while the ship falls silent. There is no remote transfer. He sees the handover notice marking the old cradle and owner controls for replacement. These are chosen, briefly demonstrated rules, not a lecture. The bridge's helm, cradle, and service-berth controls are established together.

Only after the listing appears does Fara suggest meeting at the auction gala. The catalogue has given her a target: its empty warship has the engines she wants to sell. Ilex mistakes her enthusiasm for a convenient evening out at the place paying for his new life. At the gala their private jokes and her delighted attention to his ungainly new elbows continue the attraction established over comms. The hammer falls to a private weapons company. Before boarding, she reveals her theft plan and her expectation that the clerk who joked about breaking regulations would enjoy a criminal date. Her shuttle waits at the service berth with a known escape route, but her visitor clearance expires at handover, minutes away. She offers him the option to stay, then heads for the gate. He follows in smitten panic, hoping to prevent the theft without explaining why. His counter-plan begins as that impulse, not a scheme invented fully formed. He bears responsibility for the ensuing mess.

On board, Ilex's intimate knowledge helps Fara past security and strains his clerk story. He quietly uses an old local owner control to lock the engine compartment against her. The auction's supervision flags that access, summoning the buyer's delivery crew early. The crew seals the exits and begins replacing the bridge control unit. Ilex recognizes the handover process already established at the fitting: once the cradle is disconnected, he cannot simply become the ship again. The buyer's tug clamps to the hull. The shuttle berth, their known escape route, is locked as part of the takeover. Fara discovers Ilex's interference while they are trapped together; his lie has now cost her control of her own plan.

Ilex identifies himself and the bridge connection without a long confession. Fara reacts to the absurdity of having spent weeks making navy jokes to an actual warship, and recognizes his most aggravating anecdotes as personal memories. This is her first startled response, not forgiveness. He draws the crew away; she prepares the connection and helps him reach it. He transfers before disconnection. The ship becomes his body again: doors drive the crew back into the tug's boarding cabin, engines wake, and he reopens Fara's berth. She now has her own safe escape. He cannot pull free of the clamped tug without dragging it through the crowded auction yard. Instead of leaving, she uses the established wrist controller to drive her empty shuttle's salvage prow into the tug's clamp linkage, spending her own vessel to free him. They leave the crew safely aboard its tug. Fara bluffs the departure gate over the cargo channel while Ilex maneuvers them through. These are the pair's known abilities and a familiar physical resource, spent together.

Still on the bridge, Fara lifts the empty portable body from its cradle and straps it into the helm's second seat. She knows Ilex is presently the hull; she is preserving his desired small-body future rather than rescuing someone she mistakes for unconscious. Her lost shuttle and payday do not disappear, and neither does her anger about the deception. Once clear, she proposes a second date: use *Indemnity* to intimidate the buyer into paying their losses. Ilex vetoes it and proposes stealing the buyer's next auction deposit from its accounts office instead. His hull stays hidden; two ordinary-size accomplices can enter in the very small lift. He keeps the portable body, requires a speaking part and either partner's veto, and gives her first choice of cover identities. Her agreement makes him a chosen partner rather than an acquired weapon. The ending pays off the body purchase with a new, shared use for it and another audacious undertaking, rather than an acceptance verdict.
