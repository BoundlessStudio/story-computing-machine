# All My Hands

The request promises a vulnerable speculative encounter: someone wants a new body even though the present one has no “problem or defect,” because meeting someone has made discovery of their “true nature” frightening. The distinctive pleasure I infer is the collision of an extraordinary bodily possibility with the ordinary risk of wanting a particular person's love, including discovery of what the requester actually is. The request specifies neither a romantic relationship nor acceptance, rejection, a moral, genre, tone, scale, mechanism, or outcome. Those remain choices. I intend romantic suspense with strange physical beauty, some awkward humor, and a consequential answer to the opening request.

## Provisional direction

Close third person with Virelle, an adult woman whose healthy native body is a linked flight of small ivory-and-gold, mothlike living forms: one person with many simultaneous points of touch and sight, not a committee of separate people. She has fallen for Sef, an adult human man, through several months in a nightly voice-reading circle and their subsequent private conversations. He gives extravagant adventure novels his entire, shameless conviction; she loves his commitment while making dry objections to his heroes' terrible decisions. He first stayed after a reading to argue with her about one, then returned the next night with the relevant passage. They have arranged their first physical meeting. He assumes she is human; she has allowed that assumption. An earlier confidant's revulsion gives her fear a history, without determining Sef's response.

Doctor Anjet can grow a human-shaped body that genuinely transmits touch, speaks, walks, and holds things while housing Virelle's distributed life. It has no other mind to displace. The maker can supply a body, including successful concealment, but cannot change Virelle's underlying nature or secure Sef's love. These are local embodiment mechanics, not shared-universe rules. The new body will be a welcome bodily choice as well as a hiding place; the ending will keep it. The central conflict becomes what Virelle risks for a further kind of closeness she wants with Sef. Concealment remains possible; she is neither forced out by a defect nor required to disclose as a moral test.

## Possible scenes

- Open in Anjet's growing room with the user's complete dialogue. The apparently seated customer loosens the outline she has gathered into and settles across the chair, its arms, and the maker's measuring frame. The body has no defect: Virelle enjoys this dispersed, mobile existence. Anjet accepts the commission rather than prescribing an emotional cure.
- In fittings, Virelle chooses details for herself rather than copying an imagined ideal woman. She wants a warm palm and the ability to walk beside Sef. Her new body offers real pleasures and unfamiliar coordination. She commissions a little palm opening through which her native forms can enter and leave: she wants to keep flying privately without undressing or needing help with an access seam at her back. She asks that the seam be invisible when closed. The maker supplies it. She can pass. This is a practical design choice, without foreknowledge of her later disclosure.
- At their evening meeting in a public flowering glasshouse, Sef brings the battered adventure novel that started their disagreement. He intends to read her his favorite rescue scene without the circle interrupting. She maintains the heroine could have stolen a carriage six chapters earlier; he is unexpectedly prickly when she calls the hero stupid. “Reckless” matters to him, and they do not resolve the argument. But when he begins the scene with undiminished theatrical conviction, she takes the heroine's lines. They have known each other's voices for months; laughing together in physical company is new. His excess and her dry resistance have attraction as well as friction.
- Virelle enjoys her new warm hand around his, his answering squeeze and his pulse. While he reads beside her, she also wants the more dispersed contact familiar from her native life: the vibration of his speaking throat, warmth beneath his hair, and his fingers touching several of her small forms at once. These are additional pleasures, not evidence that the new body fails. She could keep the evening as it is, which is already good. She chooses to ask for more. She tells him what she is and why she bought the body, then opens her palm. Enough of her native body emerges to show what she means while the rest remains within the new body, letting her continue speaking and choose whether to retreat.
- Sef recoils before he can help it. He asks for room; the interruption hurts. His first question, “Which one is you?”, jumps to a mistaken explanation with the same impatience he brings to stories. Virelle tells him they all are, without performing a defense of her personhood. She offers to leave; his decision must be his. He does not claim instant ease or promise their future. He asks how to touch her without hurting her, then lets her settle across his offered hand. Her bought hand remains against his other one.
- End with their first deliberate contact in both forms. Virelle still wants the body she commissioned, including its new walking feet and warm hand. The hidden opening stays open as they resume their walk. Her fear has cost her something and has been answered by this person's tentative act, without guaranteeing their future or making the bodily change a mistake.

The main risk is a predetermined self-acceptance lesson that turns Sef into an obliging examiner and the body maker into a counselor. Protect the real usefulness of the transformation, Sef's independent desires and hesitation, Virelle's voluntary disclosure, and the specific physical contact the ending makes newly possible.
