# Provisional direction: Grand Theft Me

The request explicitly joins a sound body, a new attachment, and fear that discovery of the speaker's "true nature" will bring rejection. Its pleasure may include discovering what the speaker is and what changing bodies costs or makes possible; that is an interpretation, not a prescribed lesson about acceptance. Romance, genre, scale, and outcome remain open. "try again" and "a new direction" call for a fresh story rather than improvements to the rejected one. My choice is a buoyant first-person space heist with romantic jeopardy: the new body makes the requester an accomplice in stealing the old one. The payoff will be an audacious escape and a consequential change in the pair's relationship, rather than a confession followed by tentative reassurance.

The requester, Ilex, is the living intelligence of the retired naval interdiction ship *Indemnity*. While waiting in a commercial orbital yard, Ilex has spent months talking over a public cargo-information channel with Fara, a salvage captain who makes excellent jokes about the navy. Ilex has let her believe they are an ordinary customs clerk. Her invitation to meet at a glittering ship auction sounds like a first date. "Wear shoes you can run in" should have been a clue.

We open with the prompt's exchange between Ilex and a bodywright. A human-scale manufactured body will hold Ilex's single, transferable mind. The expensive purchase is financed by selling the emptied warship; the auction closes that night. A transfer requires physical connection to a cradle, so Ilex cannot remotely leap into the hull when trouble starts. These are chosen story rules, not user requirements. Ilex wants to be someone Fara could choose without having to reconsider everything she has said about warships.

At the auction, newly embodied Ilex tries to manage champagne, elbows, and romantic conversation. Fara reveals her elaborate evening plan: steal *Indemnity* before its buyer, a private weapons company, takes delivery. She intends to sell its engines. She genuinely likes her funny correspondent and assumes their first date being a felony is a considerable attraction. Ilex's little lie now faces an expensive consequence: their hoped-for lover plans to dismantle the body Ilex has occupied for a century. Refusing the heist would expose the clerk fiction and abandon Fara inside a scheme already in motion. Ilex goes with her.

Inside the emptied hull, Ilex's intimate knowledge gets them past security but makes Fara increasingly suspicious of this supposed clerk. Comedy becomes jeopardy when the buyer's delivery crew arrives early, seals the exits, and starts the departure sequence with the pair aboard. The bridge's new control unit will soon overwrite Ilex's old command access. Fara's escape craft is attached to a service berth scheduled to be jettisoned. The transfer cradle is on the bridge, across a compartment occupied by the crew.

Ilex tells Fara what the cradle does and who has been speaking, then draws the crew away while she gets the connection ready. She must choose whether to trust a person whose identity and knowledge she has badly misjudged. Ilex abandons the safe clerk disguise and transfers back before the lockout. Now the building around the thieves comes alive: doors divide the crew, maneuvering jets shake off the buyer's tug, and Fara flies their escape from the bridge while carrying the inert, much smaller boyfriend over one shoulder. Each needs the other's particular nerve and ability.

They escape with the hull and the portable body, owing the auction house an outrageous sum. Fara loses her engine payday and gains a wanted accomplice who can object to her flight decisions from the ceiling. Her anger about the deception survives; she also proposes a second date whose target will pay their debt. Ilex negotiates the terms. The ending commits them to a risky shared future in which the revelation has changed both the heist and the romance, with no promise that being known makes either simple.
