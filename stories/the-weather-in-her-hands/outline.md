# Provisional direction: The Unfinished Song

Alternating close third-person views, mostly at Meret's scale. She stayed behind when the last evacuation left because her sister Linet was too ill to travel; after Linet died, Meret remained alone. The dying region's outward passage is gone. She still quarrels with a damaged recording of Linet, who took the easy part in their singing game. Meret wants to cross a rose-lit ice plain to the documented singing cleft the sisters kept postponing visiting. She means to sing her remembered part and hear the cliffs answer, knowing they cannot restore Linet. Her preparations, appetite, irritation, and uncertainty should make the trip hers rather than a memorial assignment.

Inside and outside run at the same pace over three days and two nights. The failed trip and return fill day one; both final crossings fill day two and the following night, reaching their destinations on day three.

Outside, a living, faceless keeper carries Meret's universe in a sealed glass sphere beneath a cyan-and-rose hood. They enclosed the surviving world and remnant heavens after evacuation; its evacuees are outside the enclosure. They can see Meret, never hear her. Their own warmth and grip are failing irreversibly; rest cannot restore them. Warmth and motion pass through the sphere as changes in its sky and weather; surrounding cold safely suspends its interior. They can gently set intact glass into a nearby hollow and die beside a preserved universe, or risk the difficult crossing to sustaining stellar heat. Dropping it on the jagged surface would break the glass, rupture the enclosed world, and kill Meret. The destination's radiance will burn their body, so arrival also means losing the close household view they love. Establish those limitations before the choice.

A tremor and protective sheltering make the storm that wrecks Meret's camping shelter and breaks the load-bearing winter ice bridge into separated floes. Thaw is underway; it will not bear her again before the next deep freeze. She returns home; the keeper is relieved to see her at the familiar window and leans toward the safe, cold choice. They support the glass at the final hollow overnight, still warming it, but their body's decline continues. Meret fetches different practical gear. She must wait for the sisters' particular trip, or choose an unmapped, nearer coastal bluff whose sound may disappoint her. She sets out toward that stranger place. The keeper cannot read her intentions, but can see that the household was preparation for a life beyond their favorite view. Their next crossing still causes gusts and a changing sky; Meret must contend with those effects. Restraint means avoiding another abrupt intervention, not immunity from consequences.

Possible ending: the bluff answers Meret irregularly, making her change the part she always sang. It is strange and perhaps funny, not her sister's lost voice. The keeper reaches sustaining warmth and retreats with permanently burned hands, leaving the still-sealed sphere. Meret never learns containment; she has gained an imperfect expedition of her own. The keeper's immediate survival may remain open, but there is no restored body or comforting replacement duty.
