# Provisional direction: The Unfinished Song

Alternating close third-person views, with most of the story lived at the woman's scale. Meret has been the sole survivor on her world for many years. She still quarrels with a damaged recording of her sister, Linet, who used to cheat at their singing game by taking the easy part. Meret wants to cross the ice plain to a range of hollow, wind-singing cliffs, a journey the sisters kept postponing. She intends to sing her remembered part there and hear it returned; she knows an echo cannot give her sister back. Her preparations, appetite, irritation, and physical uncertainty should make this a trip she wants for herself, rather than a memorial assignment.

Outside, a living, faceless keeper in a cyan-and-rose hood carries her entire universe in a sealed glass sphere. The keeper can see Meret, but hear nothing through the glass. They have grown attached to her household habits while their own hands have grown unsteady. The sphere admits their warmth and motion as changes in its sky and weather; cold can suspend its interior without killing its inhabitants. The keeper is trying to reach a place where the sphere can stay warm without being carried. Settling it safely in the surrounding cold would instead preserve Meret forever, and let the exhausted keeper stop.

The expedition disrupts the keeper's comforting idea of her life. A tremor and a protective attempt to shelter the sphere spoil Meret's weather window; she shelters, loses something real, and revises her route rather than receiving a rescue. Her renewed attempt makes the keeper reconsider what they have mistaken for aimlessness or distress. Their outward crossing and her inward crossing put pressure on each other, with neither able to communicate an intention.

Possible ending: Meret reaches the cliffs but gets a stranger, less consoling sound than she expected, then answers it herself. The keeper completes the precarious journey and puts the still-sealed sphere in sustaining warmth. Meret remains unaware of its size or her observer; what she has gained is her own imperfect expedition. The keeper must leave the household view they loved. Neither woman's survival nor the keeper's final departure should be made cost-free.
