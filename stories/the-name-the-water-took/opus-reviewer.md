# Opus review: The Name the Water Took

Independent audit for a later editing pass. It is not a `review.md` verdict,
does not change canon state, and does not authorize edits by itself.

## Verdict

- Dialogue: REVISE — an unreadable cut-off line in the climax, two early exchanges that fail the Ground check, and one corrective register shared by Braska and Ioshka.
- Prose: PASS — concrete, controlled and well paced; only minor tics (narration that explains, singular-they collisions in the fight, clustered reversals).
- Continuity and prompt: REVISE — the prompt's promise is delivered, but the retrieval at the rim can't physically happen as staged, and there are two small factual slips.
- Edit scope: targeted-dialogue
- Existing review.md said: Verdict PASS, "Dialogue: PASS" (voices "remain distinct under pressure"); disagree. It missed three Ground failures and the register Braska and Ioshka share.

This dialogue is better than much of the collection's. The surveyors have their own rank-and-blame shorthand. Ioshka's seed list and her uncle's spoon are specific and human. Braska says nothing to the surveyors during the fight, so the action carries the decision. The trouble is that Braska and Ioshka share one register: terse, exact and corrective. Their talk runs as a relay of questions and precision corrections (after/behind, memory/flash, "tells me enough"/"can't leave it"), and three lines fail the Ground check: "I have the ridge", the "Your knot" dodge, and the older surveyor's unreadable "I ordered no—". Braska's composure never changes, whether naked, hunted, or learning they bound Ioshka. That is the "serenely blank amnesiac" the outline itself ruled out. The edit pass should repair the Ground failures, rebuild the midpoint decision around the two competing wants, and break Ioshka's habit of correcting Braska so that the story's two earned corrections ("Less familiar." and "I know this part.") land.

## Edit guardrails

- Prompt: Braska surfaces naked from a hidden jungle pool with name, past and memory of arrival gone; the story must be complete and resolve that promise. The length band is 2,500–4,000 words (currently about 3,290). The story is broadly accessible, and the nakedness stays practical and non-sexual.
- The active profile is prospective-2026-08-21 (from the rewrite constraints). The rewrite request asks for all dialogue to meet it, and the original requirements still bind.
- POV is close third person, past tense, anchored in Braska, who uses they/them throughout.
- Pool mechanism as the story states it: full submersion takes worn and carried objects plus autobiographical memory and leaves skills. An object hooked out and dried gives one flash of its last use and then becomes ordinary. Going under again would take the post-emergence morning too. A flash must not show more than Braska perceived at the time or reveal a motive.
- Fixed facts: Ioshka names Braska truthfully (L51), and the disk (L281) confirms it. Braska tied Ioshka at the river fork, later cut her free, burned the master hide, cut the route cord, took her six-pocket seed wallet, and ran to the pool and went under on purpose. Why Braska led the survey, and why they turned, stays unresolved.
- Universe: no universe entities are used. Keep the name ordinary and social (`universe/rules.md` "Names and identity binding", CANON). Keep Braska responsible for the binding despite the memory loss (`universe/style-guide.md` "Personhood and moral consequence", LOCKED). The story has no water infrastructure; the natural stone channel and rain runoff are fine. Do not add any.
- Do not flatten: the surveyors' distinct voices; Ioshka hiding routes by gesture (L63, L179, L255); Braska's silence toward the surveyors in the fight; the final paragraph's action-only ending.

## Dialogue findings

### D1 · Blocking · Unreadable cut-off in the confrontation

- Where: L253, at the pool rim, after the younger surveyor describes the no-copy order
- Text:
> “I ordered no—” The older man stopped, jaw tightening.
- Problem: Ground/referent. Neither the reader nor Braska can finish this line: "no copy"? "no such security"? "no harm to the guide"? Nothing shows why he stops, either. The line he is answering is the younger's "Sensible security until you burned the rest," and the fragment doesn't clearly attach to it. It sounds like it holds a hidden fact (who really commanded, who authorized what) that the story never delivers, and the stock "jaw tightening" beat stands in for the missing content.
- Fix: Either cut the fragment so "Captain, stand away from the guide." follows directly, or make the interruption readable. If the intent is to have him push the blame away (disowning the head-only route arrangement his junior just called sensible), give him enough words to be understood before he stops. Replace the jaw beat with an action involving the spear or the formation. Illustrative only: “I never signed off on that—” He stopped.

### D2 · Major · The ridge is never shown, and "I have the ridge" can't be decoded

- Where: L55–L61, the first move away from the pool
- Text:
> “Low way,” Braska said. “They’ll watch the ridge.”
> Ioshka’s stare sharpened. “You still have that.”
> “I have the ridge.” Braska ducked beneath the roots.
- Problem: Ground/referent and Ground/knowledge. No ridge has been shown. Braska, who has no memory of this ground, names "the ridge" as a known feature and predicts that the survey will watch it. Ioshka's "that" could mean the terrain, the survey's habits or the captain's instinct. Braska's "I have the ridge" corrects her, but the reader can't tell what it means: "I can see a ridge", "I only have terrain sense, not memory", or a claim to hold it. The only sound from the pursuers so far came from downslope (L55), so "Low way" also needs a clause of geography, or it reads as moving toward them. The point this exchange is after (the skill survived, the memory didn't) lands cleanly at L79 without it.
- Fix: Show a ridgeline in the narration before Braska speaks, so the line is an inference from sight, and show where "low" leads relative to the sound from downslope. Let Ioshka's line say what alarms her (the captain's habits coming back). Have Braska answer plainly instead of correcting her. Illustrative only: “I can see it from here. I don’t remember it.”

### D3 · Major · The "Your knot" dodge exists only to set up a correction

- Where: L93–L103, the cut cord at the ash patch
- Text:
> Their own fingers completed the absent knot in the air.
> “Mine?” Braska asked.
> “Your knot.”
> “That’s not what I asked.”
- Problem: Punchline Patch and correction ping-pong. Braska already knows the knot is theirs (their fingers finished it at L93), so "Mine?" should mean "Did I do that to you?". With Braska's own cord burns (L25) it could also mean "Was this on me?", and the beat doesn't say whose wrists Braska is looking at. Ioshka is the one who was bound. She has every reason to answer straight, and elsewhere she accuses freely ("Your survey."). Her precise dodge has no motive except to let Braska say "That's not what I asked." The exchange carries the story's central moral fact, and it comes out as a word game.
- Fix: Give "Mine?" a clear object (Braska looking at her bruises, or a plain question). Cut "Your knot." and "That's not what I asked." and go straight to her answer, keeping "You put it on. At the river fork. Later you cut it." and "I didn't see what changed between." Let her feeling show in the delivery or in a physical beat, not in precision. Illustrative only: Braska, looking at her wrists: “Did I do that?”

### D4 · Major · Braska and Ioshka share one corrective register

- Where: across the Braska–Ioshka exchanges, mainly L101–L175; also L315
- Text:
> “Not after you. Behind you. There’s a difference.”
> “A flash. Don’t make it larger.
> “Now I know what needs testing.”
- Problem: Identical Voices, correction ping-pong and epigram closers. Ioshka is a captured guide, wounded, hunted, and alone with the person who tied her, yet she talks exactly like the route-captain: terse, exact, and always correcting Braska's wording (after/behind, memory/flash, "closes flesh"/"slows blood", better/less familiar, "tells me enough"/"can't leave it", "the route"/"this part"). Braska closes exchanges with matching neat lines (L131, L173). With the tags covered, the only thing that tells them apart is who asks and who answers. The after/behind distinction is never explained, and her own account at L117 ("I followed because you still had it") means she did come after Braska. Braska stays level throughout, even on learning they bound her ("I tied you." / "You did." "The knife."). The outline wanted Ioshka to correct herself under pressure. The story does that once, well (the seed list, L149); everywhere else she corrects Braska.
- Fix: Keep Braska's evidence-testing questions; the rank and the amnesia earn them. Cut or rewrite Ioshka's wording corrections at L109 and L163 ("Don't make it larger."), and at L175 (see D5). Limit her corrections to plants, seed, weather and routes. When she isn't guarding a route, give her longer, looser sentences with people and places in them; the uncle's spoon (L163) is the model. Replace Braska's closers at L131 and L173 with an ordinary answer, a stall or a question. At L203–L207 keep "I told you." and give Braska one beat of cost (a pause, an unfinished question) before "The knife." Do not add an apology speech. Keep "Less familiar." (L141) and "I know this part." (L315) as the story's two earned corrections; they will hit harder once the others are gone. Illustrative only (L109): “Yes. You had something of mine.”

### D5 · Major · The midpoint decision changes nothing and hides Braska's own want

- Where: L169–L177, the rain shelter, deciding to go back
- Text:
> They’ll take the direct trail and reach the pool before dusk.
> “You’re willing to go back for seed you don’t remember taking.”
> “You came back while hunted. That tells me enough.”
> “It means I can’t leave it.”
> Braska tightened the last binding. “Then we go back.”
- Problem: Inert exchange, Ground/referent and Philosopher. Braska announces the return at L169 and repeats it at L177, and nothing moves in between. Ioshka states the moral situation aloud. Braska answers with an epigram whose "enough" has no object. Her reply's two "it"s point at different things (her coming back, and the wallet). The exchange also hides the want the next scene shows: Braska fishes out their own boots, cord and knife before the wallet, and Ioshka has to prompt them with "Wallet" (L215). Braska also has no basis for "direct trail ... before dusk". They remember nothing of this ground, and the sign that would support the prediction (the outline's "fresh sign") never appears.
- Fix: Make this the place where the two wants meet. Ioshka needs Braska's hands for a retrieval she won't risk herself; Braska wants their own things out of the water too. Let one of them say that or dodge it, and end the exchange with something that wasn't there before: a condition, a suspicion, a bargain. Cut "That tells me enough." Cut Ioshka's thesis line, or turn it into a question she needs answered. Give the dusk prediction something Braska has seen or heard, or make it a guess. Illustrative only: “My things are down there too.”

### D6 · Minor · The younger surveyor briefs the rogue captain on request

- Where: L239–L249, the standoff at the rim
- Text:
> “The master hide,” said the younger one. “The final cord. Three weeks of approach marks.
> Held the final approach in your head. Sensible security until you burned the rest.
- Problem: Explanatory chorus. In the middle of a standoff, right after the older man has said "Stop.", the junior answers both of Braska's questions with a tidy briefing and a neat closing line. The facts are needed and his list-making voice is right, but answering on request is the wrong stance for the moment; the outline wanted these facts to come out as accusation.
- Fix: Keep every fact (master hide, final cord, three weeks, no copy past the black ridge, the route held in Braska's head). Deliver them as blame, or as his attempt to talk Braska round, in the register he finds at L275 ("We can still contain this."). Have the older man cut across him at least once. Drop "Sensible security until you burned the rest," or make it openly bitter.

### D7 · Minor · Clipped echo, stock beats, and beats that contradict their lines

- Where: L35, L37, L51, L145, L153, L275
- Text:
> Braska shifted their weight off the cut heel. “Again.”
> Keep it dry when you pull it out.
> Ioshka looked toward the rain instead of answering.
- Problem: At L35 the echo is flattened by its period, so the reader has to work out that it asks for an explanation. L37's "earn us enough distance to speak one" sounds composed rather than spoken. "Her jaw moved once" (L51) and "jaw tightening" (L253) are stock beats. At L145 the wallet will come out wet; she means once it's out. At L153 the narration says she doesn't answer, and then she answers. At L275, "as Braska stopped answering" is wrong because he hadn't asked Braska anything.
- Fix: L35: make it a question or a plainer prompt. L37: plainer urgency. L51: replace the jaw with a beat that shows why she hesitates (a look at Braska's face, or at the pool). L145: "once it's out" or similar. L153: "without looking at Braska", or cut "instead of answering". L275: "as Braska kept silent".

### P2 · Minor · Singular "they" collides with the plural surveyors

## Continuity and prompt findings

### C1 · Major · The retrieval at the rim can't physically happen as staged

- Where: L245–L299, the standoff and fight at the rim
- Text:
> They chose a fallen branch with a fork, shaved one tine against the slab’s rough edge, bent it under tension, and bound it.
> They drew it out while Ioshka kept the wallet’s light pinned with the other branch of the hook.
> The younger had taken the old formation: spear pressing center, second person closing the open side with cord.
- Problem: There is one hook on one line (L143, lengthened at L217). At L269 Ioshka holds that line. At L277 Braska makes "one short cast" and draws the disk out with it, while the same hook's other tine stays down pinning the wallet; the hook can't come up and stay down at once. Two objects then dry (L279, L293) while two armed men a few steps away hold formation. At L299, "cut the remaining line at the rim" doesn't say which line. L245 gives the spear to "the younger", who is carrying cord. Confusing staging at the climax.
- Fix: Settle the equipment. For example, land the wallet first and take the disk on the next cast, or have Ioshka hold the hooked wallet line while Braska lifts the disk's thong from the shallow shelf with the knife. Give the surveyors a reason to hold off (the pool's edge, the "Last command" standoff) and keep the drying brief. Name the line cut at L299. At L245 make it "They had taken the old formation".

### C2 · Minor · "The copy" contradicts "no copy"

- Where: L249–L251, after the younger surveyor's account
- Text:
> “You forbade one past the black ridge,” the younger said.
> After burning the copy, Braska had carried the last approach here and submerged it out of reach.
- Problem: The younger has just said there was no copy past the black ridge; what Braska burned was the master hide (L241).
- Fix: "After burning the master hide".

### C3 · Minor · The pursuers' number and position are asserted without support

- Where: L55–L63 and L183, the first escape and the return
- Text:
> Ioshka had come this way. Two pursuers had not come this way yet.
- Problem: At L63 nothing yet gives the number two (Ioshka has only said "they"), and what the "two clean patches" show is unclear. The pursuers heard downslope near the pool at L55 then take hours to reach its rim (L183).
- Fix: Make it "The pursuers had not come this way yet," or give the count a source. Add a clause saying the pursuers followed the fugitives' trail away from the pool (the L115 branch-creak scene already suggests this).

### C4 · Minor · The protagonist's name matches a well-known character (owner decision)

- Where: L281 and every use of the name
- Text:
> BRASKA, ROUTE-CAPTAIN
- Problem: Braska is also the name of a major character in Final Fantasy X (Yuna's father). There is no in-repo collision; the earlier review checked that.
- Fix: Leave it alone unless the owner asks. A rename would have to change every instance, including the disk inscription, and later the review inventory.
