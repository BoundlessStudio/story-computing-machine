# Outline

## Story

- Premise: A storm-lost young man slips through the concealed door of a two-thousand-year-old wizard's tower and receives impossibly fresh tea from a host who has not spoken with a guest in centuries.
- Character want and pressure: The young man wants warmth, food, and a safe route home before his family assumes him dead, but the wizard's hungry questioning makes hospitality feel like another kind of trap. The wizard wants the visitor to fill centuries of silence, yet his fear of being forgotten makes him interrogate and detain the moment instead of joining a conversation between equals.
- Turn: The young man refuses to answer another question until the wizard answers one honestly: the tower is not his prison; he has hidden inside it because returning to a world that no longer remembers him feels worse than loneliness. The challenge changes their exchange from host-and-curiosity to mutual confession.
- Ending: After sharing an ordinary meal and reciprocal stories, the wizard dismisses the concealment he has maintained, guides the young man safely out, and carries the second pot across his own threshold. The young man invites him toward the inhabited road; the wizard takes the first step, resolving the tea-table promise through genuine company rather than a captive audience.

## Beats

1. Driven off the familiar path by a violent storm, the cold, hungry young man forces what looks like a ruined shelter door and tumbles into a warm, intact tower room; the wizard calmly pours fresh tea and delivers the prompt's exchange.
2. Concrete domestic evidence answers the immediate mystery: living tea plants, herbs, bread starter, and pantry work have been personally tended by the wizard. The young man drinks and eats, but keeps watching the vanished-looking door and asks when he can leave.
3. The wizard promises a safe exit after the storm, then fires questions about governments, roads, fashions, wars, jokes, and whether anyone remembers his age. His delight repeatedly overrides the young man's answers, revealing that he wants two thousand years summarized more than he is listening to one person.
4. The young man tries to end the visit because people at home will fear for him. The wizard delays with a hot meal and another pot, insisting that a few hours cannot matter; to a short-lived guest, the dismissal is frightening and cruel even if the host means it kindly.
5. When the young man says a guest must be free to go, the wizard proves the door was never locked: the concealment and storm, not a prison spell, obscured it. The young man then asks why the wizard himself has not crossed it in centuries.
6. The wizard evades with feats and old history. The young man establishes a fair rule—one honest answer for one honest answer—and the wizard admits that he withdrew after surviving everyone he knew, then kept waiting for a world that would ask him back while fearing it had forgotten him.
7. Their second round of tea becomes a true conversation: the young man describes ordinary work, arguments, celebrations, and the family awaiting him; the wizard answers about loneliness, daily cultivation, mistakes, and the terror of being irrelevant. Each listens, interrupts less, and corrects assumptions.
8. With the storm easing, the wizard packs food, reveals the safe path, and lets the room cool instead of manufacturing another reason to stay. He crosses the threshold carrying the fresh pot, accepts the invitation to walk as far as the inhabited road, and leaves the tower visible behind them.

## Continuity

- Canon used: The story occurs in an otherwise unspecified era of the one deep-time world. Magic is real, culturally understood here as wizardry, and only the living wizard uses it; the tower and its concealment merely store, focus, or express his continuing agency.
- Boundaries and unknowns: Do not identify the era, region, tower, wizard, or young man with any established noun, system, culture, or timeline coordinate. The wizard's two-thousand-year lifespan, household workings, concealment, and survival history are local observations with unexplained costs and no universal rule. The tower is not alive or independently agentic. Keep the young man's family and home generic unless the prose deliberately adds and inventories new proper nouns. Do not imply that being forgotten erases the wizard's personhood or excuses coercion; his change is the choice to respect the guest's freedom and re-enter relationship.
