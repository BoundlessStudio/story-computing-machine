# Outline

## Story

- Premise and central promise: Seeking shelter from a downpour, an unnamed young traveler leans through a ruined roadside arch and crosses into a two-thousand-year-old wizard's tower. He expects an intruder's death; instead the wizard plucks leaves from an indoor garden, brews genuinely fresh tea, and admits that this is his first guest in centuries. Fulfill the reversal through action: he prefers conversation to killing, opens a safe exit before asking the young man to stay, and earns voluntary company. Make the encounter a complete relational change, not only a witty exchange or adventure setup.
- Focal pressure or attachment: The young man wants proof that he can leave, but fear sharpens his curiosity; quick humor and careful manners protect him while making honest conversation impossible. The wizard wants news of ordinary present life, yet isolation makes him ask too much and mistake continued presence for consent. The young man must state a boundary; the wizard must risk ending the visit by honoring it.
- Counterforce or complication: From the visitor's limited view, the wizard's age, power, and unfamiliar household make kindness potentially coercive. The wizard cannot prove harmlessness by describing himself, while strategic agreeableness cannot prove interest. Fading road-light, mismatched scales of reference, and rusted hospitality sustain pressure without making loneliness an entitlement.
- POV, distance, and information limit: Close third-person past through the young man. He notices exits, hands, distance, heat, taste, and attention because survival depends on observable choices. He cannot read motives, verify age, or understand the workings; physical evidence can support the wizard's claim without granting omniscience.
- Governing movement and time shape: One storm-darkening hour, organized by successive tea infusions. During the first cup the young man only assumes he is trapped; inspecting and opening the exit breaks that assumption. The second begins after he returns to the table by choice and permits specific, reciprocal talk. Stop during a bounded continuation of the visit, with the exit usable—no promised friendship, arranged return, moral explanation, or symbolic closure.
- Speculative rule or ordinary-world constraint: The living wizard's attention opens a local threshold to the exact arch where the traveler entered; tower and arch cannot choose, lure, judge, or use magic. Holding it open prevents him sustaining delicate household workings, visibly dimming lamps and cooling the tea room. His longevity is a separate local working whose mechanism and costs remain unspecified. Tea leaves are newly plucked from shrubs he tends in a sunlit upper room and brewed in ordinary water. Establish no link to the Glass Gate or another crossing, longevity, or tea system.

## Voice

- Narrative texture: Follow tactical noticing—exits before decor, hands before faces—then widen toward taste, warmth, plants, and care as observed choices revise danger. Keep wonder concrete and conditional.
- Conversational texture: Begin with overpolite answers, nervous humor, domestic directions, and questions mismatched in scale; admit pauses and ordinary road details only after the exit opens. Not every turn is witty or profound.
- Rhetorical ownership: The wizard owns historical comparisons, exact distinctions, and overlarge questions; the young man owns practical sequences, approximations, and present particulars. Each must adapt to the other's reasoning.
- Pressure behavior: Fear makes the young man faster, funnier, and less informative until he becomes abruptly direct. Embarrassment makes the wizard over-explain, then stop filling silence and listen through changed action.
- Anti-default: Avoid a duel between a snarky modern visitor and a serenely cryptic ancient. The wizard is powerful but socially uncalibrated; the young man is frightened, curious, and not merely an audience surrogate.

## Beats

1. In hard rain near dusk, the young man presses into a ruined arch and crosses farther than its depth permits. The dry tower and vanished road establish accident and apparent entrapment. Give him an ordinary reason to regain the road before dark.
2. The wizard makes no attack and shifts into long-unused hospitality with alarming efficiency. He gathers leaves from the indoor garden and brews them in view. The traveler sits because refusal feels unsafe, not because trust exists.
3. Over the first infusion, preserve the prompt's discoveries: fresh tea in a two-thousand-year-old wizard's tower, the first guest in centuries, and an appetite for conversation. Rebuild the dialogue around unequal stakes. The wizard's questions range too widely; the young man's charming answers disclose little.
4. A domestic misreading clarifies the power problem: the wizard mistakes an attempt to locate the entrance for curiosity about the room. The young man states that he needs to see an exit before continuing. The request costs him fluency and threatens the wizard's hope without becoming a speech.
5. The wizard opens the threshold onto the rainy roadside, explains only what is needed, and steps away. Dimming lamps and cooling air show that he maintains it. Relinquishing control, rather than arguing harmlessness, proves his preference.
6. The young man checks time, weather, and distance; he can leave. He instead requests a bounded interval and returns to the table. The wizard asks one answerable question and leaves room for a specific account of ordinary present life, not lore or destiny.
7. The second infusion includes useful information, a failed historical comparison, a mundane correction, unfilled silence, and one question the wizard cannot neatly answer. End once company is voluntary and temporary, with the costly exit open. Do not script a return visit, confession, reconciliation, final thematic line, or gift.

## Continuity

- Canon used: One world persists through deep time; cultures hold incomplete models; only living beings use magic; separate crossings remain unrelated without evidence. The wizard supplies every active working shown.
- Boundaries and unknowns: Era, culture, location, identities, longevity mechanism, full threshold rules, and the centuries without guests remain local and unresolved. Arch and tower lack agency. Age fixes no position among established eras; fresh tea has no link to the Fenwick tea window. No reference images were supplied.
