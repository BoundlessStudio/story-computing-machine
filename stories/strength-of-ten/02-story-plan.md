# The Strength of Ten — Story Plan

## Planning record and promise

- **Story / mode / stage:** `strength-of-ten` / `CREATE_PLAN` / `canon-research`.
- **Authority:** `authority.json` is based on current `main` at `95d83ba02d9a3717ab30947466dfb6bdf541c100`; the canon brief is `READY`, with no conflict or user ruling.
- **Draft target:** About 3,500 words, within the required 2,500–4,000.

Prince Lysander of Redwater arrives in northern Harrowfast prepared to marry one of King Gorm's three adult daughters in exchange for an army. Redwater has almost no soldiers, and the Black Reed League can reach the capital's only defensible approach in six days. Gorm chooses his second daughter, Princess Sigrun, because her office can move the winter host. She refuses.

Her no is enforceable: as winter road-marshal, appointed by seven town reeves, Sigrun coordinates the depot keepers, remounts, sled teams, ledgers, and twelve captains whose prior commitments make a winter march possible. Gorm can call troops or punish her, but replacing that human network takes longer than Redwater has. Gorm's personal six-heartbeat strength can move what ten haulers move, yet cannot feed one soldier. Lysander must stop confusing self-sacrifice with authority over another person, tell the truth about his role in Redwater's defenselessness, and pay with command, toll revenue, and possible succession. A reciprocal compact sends meaningful aid; prepared logistics stop the invasion at Ninepin Causeway; the marriage ends rather than becoming a postponed fee.

## Story controls

- **POV / tense:** Close third through Lysander, past tense; no access to Sigrun's or Gorm's private thoughts and no cutaways.
- **Tone / genre:** Tense, wintry, humane political fantasy with restrained wit. Hope comes from costly coordinated action, not Gorm's reform or sudden romance.
- **Audience:** PG-13. Coercive marriage pressure, cold, combat, injuries, and deaths remain non-graphic and consequence-centered; no sexual content.
- **World / marriage:** New local polities in an unnamed era of the one physical world. Sigrun refuses marriage and betrothal. A final permission to exchange letters is optional contact, not promised romance.

## Story-specific style profile

- **Voice and distance:** Lysander initially sorts politics into rank, promises, and debts; close narration keeps correcting him with grain, fodder, mileage, cold hands, and tired animals. Move closest at shame, threat, and relinquishment; pull back only to compress the five-day march.
- **Dialogue pressure:** Rank rewards euphemism while the clock punishes it. Gorm demands submission, Sigrun turns claims into quantities and conditions, and Lysander moves from offering himself to supplying facts and asking. Legal and military language changes action rather than displaying lore.
- **Images / rhythm:** Weight borne alone versus distributed; white breath, iron, oak, grain dust, seals, harness, runners, ruts, and Gorm's post-feat tremor. Use clipped syntax for deadlines and refusals, longer clauses for Lysander's rationalization. Avoid policy speeches, generic court splendor, constant aphorisms, and romanticized coercion.
- **Climax emphasis:** Expand the treaty choice and prepared tactical payoffs; compress repeated marching and fighting. One body's spectacle must not outweigh the ordinary tracks that deliver an army.

## Character engine

### Prince Lysander — viewpoint, age thirty

- **Want / need / pressure:** Get an army immediately and make himself the bargain's sole personal cost. He needs to recognize that his willingness does not authorize Sigrun's sale and that accountable loss of power is more useful than private martyrdom. During hunger he demobilized 1,300 seasonal soldiers early so their grain could feed households, then dismissed invasion warnings and called Redwater merely “outnumbered.”
- **Arc:** Ceremonial self-offer -> concealment exposed -> public admission -> surrender of operational command, revenue, and succession certainty -> useful local service at Ninepin -> accepts tribunal without guaranteed absolution.
- **Dialogue profile:** Seeks agreement through courtesy, balanced clauses, and treaty precision; names duties but keeps hunger, fear, and guilt abstract. Resistance makes him sweeten, then retreat into formality. After the audit he shifts to short facts and direct requests. With Gorm he performs endurance; with Sigrun he gradually admits uncertainty. He would not deliver a polished consent lesson or seek praise for sacrifice.

### Princess Sigrun — prospective bride and road-marshal, age thirty-two

- **Want / need / leverage:** Prevent Gorm from converting her office and marriage into a dynastic claim; move only a fed, bounded army; stop Black Reed before southern trade collapses. She must turn refusal into a compact she will command. Her fixed winter appointment, matching depot ledgers, reeve countersigns, and captain commitments cannot be replaced inside six days. The seal and papers are nonmagical records of human choices.
- **Arc:** Prepared refusal -> skeptical audit -> author of reciprocal terms -> open rupture with Gorm -> commander accountable for costs -> permits only a freely chosen future letter.
- **Dialogue profile:** Wants usable truth; asks spare questions about people, days, food, route, and authority. Road terms are exact, family pain undescribed. She uses silence, then document comparison, then hard alternatives; she grows expansive only when a workable route exists. With Gorm she says `Father` when challenging a personal claim. Her dry corrections are not flirtation. She would not promise affection or certify Lysander's redemption.

### King Gorm — coercive northern king

- **Want / brutality:** Gain Redwater's tolls and a dynastic foothold, subordinate Sigrun's office, and make force look like sole authority. He once broke the Rimegate coal barricade himself, then withheld household coal until surrender. On page, he confiscates an unnamed road clerk's ration badge and orders cold punishment for correcting him; Sigrun later restores it through her office.
- **Arc / dialogue profile:** Static antagonist: hospitality -> ridicule -> physical intimidation -> administrative threat -> narrower face-saving bargain. He speaks in broad declarations, battlefield anecdotes, false choices, and role labels, becoming precise only about ownership and insult. Resistance makes him humiliate, threaten, then bargain. He never admits that Sigrun's distributed authority frightens him, apologizes, or explains the story's ethics.

## Military, leverage, and strength logic

### Defenselessness, deadline, and deployment

- Redwater usually fields 1,300 seasonal troops and 400 border guards. Lysander dismissed the seasonal force during the food shortage. The Greyglass marshal then sold the border gate to Black Reed; the permanent force was captured, scattered, or disarmed. Only 180 city watch and about 70 couriers remain—enough for evacuation and sluice work, not battle. No reserve appears later.
- Black Reed advances with about 4,500 foot, 600 horse, engineers, and siege wagons to seize the capital granaries and river-lock tolls. Its train is 105 miles from Ninepin and moves sixteen to eighteen miles daily. Ninepin is the only firm winter route through Reedmarsh for siege wagons; without it, the capital cannot be reduced on this campaign.
- Harrowfast can spare 1,600 soldiers: 800 shield-spears, 400 bows, 250 pioneers, and 150 scouts, plus 240 drivers, healers, smiths, and depot workers. Gorm's boast of three thousand ignores home defense and supply.
- The Frostroad is 140 miles: five days with prepared sleds, remounts, and six depots, but much slower after the expected day-six thaw. Harrowfast supplies three days of grain; Redwater must send 120 grain carts, fodder, and sluice workers to a day-four rendezvous. No foraging is allowed; if carts fail, Sigrun turns back.
- Sigrun commands until Black Reed retreats beyond Greyglass or twenty days pass. Lysander commands only Redwater watch and workers. Three captain-representatives may refuse pillage, border pursuit, or abandonment of Ninepin. The defense evacuates three villages, opens two culverts to mire the causeway shoulders, anchors wagons and spears on the firm center, and blocks the siege train rather than attempting annihilation.

### Gorm's personal “strength of ten”

- A living field-healer created a scar-lattice in Gorm decades earlier while Gorm supplied the living power. The healed scars only guide his own exertion; they are not an acting artifact.
- With trained breath, footing, and a whole-body brace, Gorm can drive nearly all available heat and muscular reserve into one push or pull for six heartbeats—roughly eight to ten trained haulers. The local reputation rounds it to ten.
- The burst gives no speed, durability, or sustained endurance and works poorly without leverage. It leaves him dangerously cold, torn, and trembling; a second use before a night's warming, food, and rest risks heart failure.
- In scene 2 he drags the iron-banded audience table across the doors, trapping the negotiation and making violence credible. Heated stones, broth, pallor, and a shaking hand expose the cost. His safe use is spent when scene 4 forces the compact decision.

## Causal scene sequence

### 1. A wedding chain and an empty army — about 350 draft words

Lysander reaches Harrowfast with six exhausted escorts and a ceremonial gold wedding chain. A Redwater courier reports Greyglass lost and the siege train six days from Ninepin. The list—180 watch, 70 riders, empty depots, three villages in the route—proves defenselessness. Lysander orders immediate evacuation and 120 carts assembled, exposing his empty stores, then enters court resolved to accept whichever adult daughter Gorm chooses.

**Dialogue conflict / tactic change:** The courier wants Lysander to return; Lysander wants the courier to preserve confidence and carry orders. The courier shifts from deference to a blunt inventory. Lysander drops reassurance and gives achievable evacuation and cart orders.

### 2. The king chooses; the daughter refuses — about 500 words

Gorm punishes a road clerk for correcting his troop boast, then names Sigrun as bride and promises three thousand soldiers after the wedding. Sigrun arrives with ledgers and says only 1,600 can move—and she will not marry Lysander. Lysander tries to accept for them both. She asks whether he came to request her answer or carry it.

Gorm invokes Rimegate, triggers his scar-lattice, and drags the iron-banded table across the doors in six heartbeats. His post-feat cold seeds the limit. Sigrun lays an unsigned route ledger on the table: six depots and twelve captains require her matching authorization; coercive removal triggers a reeve replacement taking at least ten days. Gorm can trap them but cannot put an army on the road. Lysander realizes bride and army were never one transaction.

**Dialogue conflict / tactic changes:** Lysander wants immediate assent and troops; Sigrun wants her refusal recognized; Gorm wants public submission. Lysander shifts from ceremonial acceptance to asking what authority she holds. Sigrun shifts from flat no to documentary proof. Gorm shifts from charm to ridicule, historical threat, and physical confinement.

### 3. What an army eats — about 500 words

While Gorm withdraws to warm, Sigrun releases the clerk under her office and audits Lysander in the map room: force, enemy speed, stores, carts, terrain, authority. His letter hid both the demobilization and absent supplies. Her arithmetic proves three thousand would eat the carts before day four and expose Harrowfast.

They build the viable plan: 1,600 soldiers, five-day movement, day-four carts, Ninepin defense, prior evacuation, no foraging, no border pursuit, captain refusal rights. Sigrun will command if Redwater assigns one-third of river tolls for seven years to wages, casualties, roads, and displaced villages and convenes a postwar tribunal. Lysander must testify; if the tribunal finds his demobilization or concealment violated his office, he accepts the lawful council's succession decision. Sigrun excludes marriage. Lysander suggests a delayed wedding to make the compact acceptable to Gorm, then understands that this converts no into later and removes it himself.

**Dialogue conflict / tactic changes:** Lysander wants aid without full exposure; Sigrun wants complete facts and enforceable terms. He shifts from summaries to defensiveness, then exact admissions. She shifts from questions to contradictory arithmetic, then offers a route. End with a viable unsigned compact and Lysander balking at its personal political cost.

### 4. The price that is his — about 500 words

The courier confirms Black Reed's wagons on the south road: departure must be before dawn. Sigrun presents the compact while Gorm still trembles under furs. Gorm tries to substitute another unnamed adult daughter; Lysander refuses to ask any daughter to purchase troops and ends the marriage promise with Sigrun's no. Gorm threatens to remove her, but duplicate reeve notices and captain marks show that coercion freezes depot release until a new selection.

Gorm offers 800 household troops, too few to hold Ninepin, for immediate marriage and permanent toll control. Lysander instead publicly corrects his “outnumbered” lie, signs away operational command and seven years of toll share, and accepts the tribunal and succession risk. He gives the wedding chain as first payment to the casualty fund, not a claim on Sigrun. The captains countersign; Gorm stamps the levy call and pretends the hard bargain was his.

**Dialogue conflict / tactic changes:** Gorm wants marriage or failure, Sigrun wants activation while his burst is spent, and Lysander must choose the aid's real owner and cost. Gorm shifts from removal threat to substitute bride, inadequate force, then face-saving assent. Sigrun shifts from procedure to deadline-backed ultimatum. Lysander shifts from mediation to explicit refusal and confession.

### 5. Five days of ordinary strength — about 450 words

The host leaves in staged columns. Depots supply grain, remounts, socks, runner grease, and traces; a broken sled costs hours but no miracle fixes it. Lysander rides with the route ledger, seeing authority renewed by keepers, drivers, reeves, and captains. He proposes a longer path around an unsafe river shelf; Sigrun verifies the mileage before adopting it.

Redwater's carts arrive on day four because Lysander ordered them in scene 1. They lack fodder, so he opens his household horse train and dismounts his escorts. The force proceeds without plunder and reaches Ninepin tired but fed on night five. Evacuation lamps show the villages leaving.

**Dialogue conflict / tactic changes:** Lysander wants shorter rests; Sigrun wants a force capable of fighting after arrival. He moves from deadline pressure to contributing remounts and local knowledge without demanding assent. She moves from refusal and figures to verified adoption of his detour. Cooperation remains practical, not romantic.

### 6. Ninepin holds — about 800 words

Ninepin is a raised road across marsh: capital north, Black Reed south, two culverts controlling the shoulders. Sigrun places wagons and spears on the firm center, bows on low mounds, pioneers at culverts, scouts forward, and Redwater watch behind the line. Thaw softens the marsh but threatens the northern return route. Lysander urges immediate flooding; Sigrun waits because scouts and civilian carts are not clear.

Black Reed's cavalry cannot deploy; its engineers push fascines and siege wagons along the center. Bows and spears delay rather than implausibly destroy five thousand. When watch confirms villagers and scouts clear, Sigrun orders staged culvert release. One gate is jammed. Lysander leads watch members through a known drainage cut and frees it with poles while pioneers hold the bank. He returns bruised and soaked; one unnamed watch member is missing, keeping consequence non-graphic but real.

Water undermines the shoulders and tips two siege wagons across the center. Sigrun advances only far enough to secure the blockage and rotates fed units. After two days of failed probes, unusable cavalry, threatened supply, and a blocked train, Black Reed retreats toward Greyglass. Lysander wants pursuit and the traitorous marshal; Sigrun invokes the border limit. He nearly reaches for rank, then sends riders to observe and aid returning villagers. The smaller host wins by arrival, terrain, supply, engineers, and restraint—not superiority or Gorm.

**Dialogue conflict / tactic changes:** Before contact, Lysander wants speed; Sigrun wants verified clearance. He shifts from argument to obtaining the missing report, and she shifts from refusal to staged flooding. After retreat, he wants pursuit while she wants the bounded objective; she cites the clause then acts, and he converts a royal demand into a relief order.

### 7. No wedding owed — about 400 words

Within twenty days the northern host begins returning. Redwater watch holds Ninepin; villages resettle; Greyglass remains unresolved, but the capital and immediate campaign are saved. Wages, injuries, repairs, and village losses already draw from the seven-year toll fund. Lysander's public tribunal is scheduled, with loss of succession real. Sigrun must answer to her reeves for the campaign and has openly broken with Gorm.

At a field table repaired with plain braces, Lysander signs the final tallies and confirms the wedding promise is void. Sigrun returns the cut clasp after the chain itself has been weighed into the casualty fund. She says that after the tribunal, if he writes his own letters instead of letting kings bargain with them, she may answer one: bounded permission, not betrothal or forgiveness.

**Dialogue conflict / tactic changes:** Lysander wants to renounce any claim while leaving room for honest contact; Sigrun tests whether he separates her refusal from the compact. He shifts from formal release language to one small personal question. She shifts from checking toll and tribunal details to the limited offer of a letter.

**Ending image:** The last supply sled starts north. Its runners leave two modest grooves among hundreds of boot tracks; Lysander and Sigrun walk beside them on separate footing, the unused clasp cold in his open palm.

## Stakes, reversals, setup, and ending

- **Stakes:** The capital, granaries, locks, three villages, and 1,840 northern personnel; Lysander's regency and integrity; Sigrun's office, refusal, command responsibility, and relationship with Gorm.
- **Reversals:** Three thousand are impossible; Sigrun refuses; Gorm's feat proves threat and limit; Lysander's concealment discredits him; aid costs his power rather than her body; Black Reed's numbers fail at prepared restrictive terrain.
- **Irreversible costs:** Deaths and injuries, seven years of toll revenue, tribunal and possible succession loss, Sigrun's rupture with Gorm, and no restored marriage bargain.
- **Setup/payoff:** Scene 1's carts feed the day-four march and its evacuation clears the culverts. The wedding chain becomes casualty money. Gorm's spent burst opens a same-day institutional counterstroke. The no-foraging rule forces Lysander to surrender remounts. Mapped culverts and no-pursuit terms govern the victory. Meaningful northern aid deploys, stops the siege route, and returns under its limits.

## Canon constraints

- Keep all polities, offices, routes, and conflict local to an unresolved era. No identification with Ersene, the Crowned Ancestors' kingdom, the former Sleepless Majesty polity, their sovereigns, dragons, or laws.
- Gorm is one original living human, not a dragon, collective, god, construct, or established person in disguise. Only living beings originate/use magic; scars, seals, ledgers, chains, furniture, and warm stones do not act.
- Do not universalize the scar-lattice to kings, bloodlines, men, northerners, breath, scars, pain, or vows. Pain is cost, not fuel; ten is a local rounded comparison.
- Marriage, refusal, signatures, titles, and names have no magical force. Sigrun's leverage is human commitment plus physical supply. Gorm's troop call cannot supply her consent, and other daughters are not fallback prices.
- Keep violence PG-13, coercion visible, and respect distinct from attraction. No prophecy, dream, hidden army, supernatural weather, coincidence, shortcut, or enemy irrationality repairs causality.
- Plans have `authority: none`. Preserve local qualifiers and the route, depot, cart, evacuation, culvert, command, and cost logic in later artifacts.

## Failure modes to watch

- Never let Lysander's willingness replace Sigrun's answer, soften her no into “later,” or make the alternate daughters available prices.
- Show the reeves, ledgers, depots, captains, and replacement delay so Sigrun's leverage is enforceable; give her command risk and accountability, not merely better opinions.
- Give Gorm one bounded feat, immediate cold/strain, and no second burst. His strength changes risk but cannot solve supply.
- Keep the six-day arithmetic, five-day march, day-four carts, night-five arrival, troop roles, command boundaries, terrain, culvert clearance, and reason for enemy withdrawal legible.
- The tribunal and toll share—not bruises, speech, chain, or marriage—are Lysander's separate cost. Do not grant automatic forgiveness or abdication.
- End with marriage void, aid accomplished, compact active, consequences underway, and future contact optional. Avoid policy dialogue and theme summaries; every conversation must turn because a tactic fails.
