## Verbatim writing prompt

> **[WP] Your kingdom is defenseless, and to secure an army, you, the prince, are promised in marriage to one of the daughters of the King of the North, a brutal warrior known for having the strength of 10 men.**

## Story controls

- Working title: The Strength of Ten
- Target length: 2,500–4,000 words
- POV: Close third person, centered on the prince.
- Tense: Past.
- Tone and genre: Character-led political fantasy; tense, wintry, and humane,
  with restrained wit and a hopeful but costly resolution.
- Audience/content rating: Broadly accessible / PG-13. Non-graphic martial
  threat and discussion of coerced marriage; no explicit sexual content.
- Required elements: A genuinely defenseless kingdom; an adult prince promised
  to one of the adult daughters of the King of the North; the northern king's
  brutality and reputed strength of ten men; a concrete need for the northern
  army; meaningful choice for the prospective bride; an alliance whose terms
  and cost are dramatized; a complete resolution.
- Prohibited elements: Sexualized minors; explicit sexual content; graphic
  violence; romanticizing coercion or treating a daughter as mere payment;
  arbitrary dream, prophecy, or timeline devices used to solve continuity.

## Assumptions

- The phrase "a brutal warrior known for having the strength of 10 men"
  describes the King of the North.
- The prince and every prospective bride are unambiguously adults.
- The second-person wording casts the reader as the prince but does not require
  grammatical second person; close third person will provide tighter control
  of political context and character interiority.
- The prompt does not specify which daughter, the invader, or the nature of the
  northern king's strength; those details may be selected during canon-grounded
  planning.
- The marriage promise creates the crisis but does not predetermine consent,
  affection, or the final terms of the alliance.

## Completion tests

- The result is a complete short story, not a synopsis or chapter fragment.
- The central promise of the prompt is dramatized and resolved.
- The prince's kingdom begins without a credible army, faces a specific threat,
  and secures meaningful northern military aid by the ending.
- The King of the North appears as a brutal warrior whose reputed tenfold
  strength materially affects the negotiation.
- At least one daughter acts as a political subject with meaningful leverage
  over the marriage and alliance.
- The final marriage decision, whether accepted, amended, or refused, follows
  from the prince's and prospective bride's choices rather than narrative fiat.
- The story passes canon and continuity review.
- Every character-facing name and alias is checked against `stories/NAMES.md`;
  any deliberate reuse has a documented narrative reason.
