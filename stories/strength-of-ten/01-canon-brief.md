## Hard constraints

- Fulfill the captured prompt contract: a genuinely defenseless kingdom; an adult prince promised in marriage to one of the adult daughters of the King of the North; a concrete threat requiring the northern army; a brutal northern king reputed to possess the strength of ten men; meaningful choice and political leverage for the prospective bride; dramatized alliance terms and costs; and a complete resolution. The prince’s kingdom must begin without a credible army and secure meaningful northern military aid by the ending. (`stories/strength-of-ten/00-prompt.md` — `## Verbatim writing prompt`, `## Story controls`, `## Assumptions`, `## Completion tests`)
- The King of the North is the prompt’s brutal warrior. His reputed tenfold strength must materially affect the negotiation rather than remain decorative description. The prompt does not require the reputation to be an exact measurement or require a magical explanation. (`stories/strength-of-ten/00-prompt.md` — `## Assumptions`, `## Completion tests`)
- The prince and every prospective bride must be unambiguously adult. Marriage may be accepted, amended, or refused, but the outcome must follow from the prince’s and prospective bride’s choices. Neither daughter may be treated as payment, property, a passive prize, or an interchangeable route to troops. (`stories/strength-of-ten/00-prompt.md` — `## Story controls`, `## Prohibited elements`, `## Completion tests`; `universe/style-guide.md` — `## Personhood and moral consequence`, `## Audience and content boundaries`)

A new pair of fantasy kingdoms may occupy an unnamed era and region, but they may not be described as a separate planet, plane, or disconnected fantasy cosmos. (`universe/premise.md` — `## The deep-time shared world`; `universe/locations.md` — `## The world across eras`)
- If the northern king’s strength or another capability is magical, only a living being may originate or use that magic. A crown, weapon, ring, throne, marriage contract, bloodline record, or other nonliving object may store, focus, transmit, or shape living-supplied power but may not independently choose or cast unless it is established as alive and treated as a person. (`universe/rules.md` — `## System categories`, `## Living agency and magic`)

## Conflicts and ambiguity

- **Contradiction:** None in the captured prompt. No user ruling is required.
- **Omission, not contradiction:** Canon does not identify the prince, either kingdom, the King of the North, his daughters, the army, the enemy, or the era.
- **Omission, not contradiction:** Canon does not explain why the prince’s kingdom is defenseless, how many northern daughters exist, how a prospective bride is selected, or what law governs royal marriage.
- **Omission, not contradiction:** Canon does not determine whether the king’s strength is literal, exaggerated, magical, biological, trained, inherited, or politically manufactured. Absence of a rule leaves local invention space; it is not evidence for a universal strength mechanism.
- **Omission, not contradiction:** Canon does not establish what makes the northern king brutal, what his daughters think of his rule, or whether the northern army’s loyalty belongs to him, the realm, individual commanders, or negotiated institutions.
- **Conditional contradiction:** Identifying the story as post-accession Ersene while presenting a living reigning king and an unrelated military-marriage alliance would conflict with Queen Olyra’s established accession and Ersene’s specific alliance structure.
- **Conditional contradiction:** Identifying the setting as the post-Charter Crown-era kingdom while restoring an ordinary reigning monarch would conflict with the transfer to elected human councils.
- **Conditional contradiction:** Identifying the northern ruler as the still-reigning Sleepless Majesty would conflict with that collective’s public renunciation of royal and religious authority.
- **Conditional contradiction:** A nonliving artifact independently granting, choosing, or exercising the king’s strength would contradict the locked living-agency rule.
- **Conditional contradiction:** Treating the marriage promise, military need, paternal command, or negotiated victory as the daughter’s consent would contradict the prompt contract and binding personhood constraints.
- **Collision risk:** A terrifying royal dragon, scale-mediated alliance, formal renewal, or “asked/answered/witnessed” structure would repeat Ersene too closely.
- **Collision risk:** A king revealed as one of a hidden dragon collective would repeat *A Crown of Quiet Hours* or *The Peace They Could Sleep Through*.
- **Collision risk:** A single absolute ruler using overwhelming strength to make every emergency choice risks repeating the central authority problem in *Daughter of the Sun*. The new story should make the alliance’s participants and costs concrete.

## Safe local invention space

- Create two original neighboring kingdoms in an unnamed fantasy era of the shared physical world. Keep both distinct from Ersene, the Crowned Ancestors’ kingdom, and the Sleepless Majesty’s polity.
- Make the prince’s kingdom defenseless for a specific, material reason visible in the opening—for example, its army was destroyed, deliberately disbanded under an expired peace, captured through betrayal, or rendered unusable by a recent institutional failure.
- Create a specific advancing threat with a credible deadline, objective, route, and force. Show why diplomacy without troops is insufficient and why northern deployment can arrive in time.
- Portray the King of the North as an original human or other living individual whose brutal reputation and exceptional strength affect the room. The strength may be mundane or a bounded local capability. If magical, identify him as the living user and define its limits without claiming a species-wide, royal, male, northern, or bloodline rule.
- Let the reputation be politically useful without making it wholly false: witnesses, scars, a controlled demonstration, or the negotiation’s physical environment may establish why people believe it. Avoid a hidden-dragon reveal unless the story intentionally accepts the strong collision burden.
- Give the prospective bride independent information, goals, and leverage. She may demand altered command terms, civilian protections, her own authority over deployment, a revocable betrothal, a delayed marriage, or an alternative compact.
- Treat the king’s offer and the daughter’s decision as separate acts. The king can offer troops or attempt coercion; he cannot supply her consent.
- Let the prince also make a costly choice beyond agreeing to marriage—for example surrendering unilateral command, accepting northern oversight, exposing his own court’s failure, committing resources, or yielding a political privilege. The daughter should not be the alliance’s only cost.
- Make the military agreement operational: troop numbers or units, departure time, supply, command, objective, limits, and what ends the commitment.
- The marriage may proceed, be conditionally accepted, be postponed pending freely renewed assent, or be refused in favor of another binding alliance. Any option is compatible if meaningful aid is secured and the prompt’s marriage crisis is resolved through choice.
- Keep the wider era, cosmology, and subsequent history unresolved unless the story needs them.
- Limit proposed canon additions to these kingdoms, people, events, and any observed local strength or political mechanism. Do not promote a universal northern culture, marriage law, royal-strength lineage, or army system.

## Concrete canon recommendation

Proceed with two new local kingdoms in an unnamed era. Open after a specific military collapse has left the prince’s realm unable to resist an advancing force. The prince reaches the northern court under an inherited or emergency marriage promise, expecting the brutal king to select a daughter and price an army.

Make the northern king’s tenfold strength real enough to shape negotiation—through an on-page feat, threat, or known battlefield consequence—but keep it personal and bounded. Let the prospective bride interrupt the transaction as an independent political actor. She should decide whether marriage serves her own goals, define conditions on troop command and civilian protection, and possess a credible refusal path.

A strong resolution would secure immediate northern deployment through a written reciprocal defense compact while making the marriage conditional on the prince’s and bride’s separately renewed assent after the crisis. The prince pays an additional political cost, and the bride gains enforceable authority rather than being exchanged for soldiers. This preserves the prompt’s marriage crisis, military causality, humane tone, and hopeful but costly ending without establishing unsupported shared lore.
