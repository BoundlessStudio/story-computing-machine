# Late Onset — provisional direction

The intended pleasure is a woman with an established, liked adult life becoming a spectacular beginner in a culture that thinks beginners are fourteen. The tone is wry, bodily, and increasingly exhilarating; the scale grows from an alarming accident to an airborne rescue over the city. The central relationship is between fifty-three-year-old Delia Moss and Joss Bell, a nineteen-year-old professional hero who knows powers and has little practice teaching someone who can refuse him. The anticipated payoff is Delia making a consequential rescue possible, then choosing an adventurous use of the same power for herself. She does not have to despise her ordinary life to want the next part.

**Explicit:** mutation-associated powers normally emerge during puberty; the protagonist thought that window had passed and discovers otherwise during menopause. **Interpretation:** the reversal should be more than a hot-flash joke. An older beginner has accumulated preferences, adult authority, and an existing life that complicate the superhero-origin machinery. No particular power, relationship, scale, tone, or ending was requested. General is a starting intensity target, not a restriction on the action.

Provisional form: first-person past tense, roughly 3,000–4,000 words. Delia's power transfers motion: by touching a moving object, she can stop it and store its momentum briefly, then release that movement through contact with something else. Mass matters to what she can safely hold; direction is a choice. Menopause is the unexpected activation window, not a condition she must cure or a reliable emotional switch. The fantastical mechanism need not explain real physiology.

- **A late first incident.** Returning from an appointment about menopausal symptoms, Delia stops a road accident with a hand against a bus. The bus stops; she travels no farther than the pavement, and something in her wants very badly to keep moving. She accidentally launches her handbag through a shopfront when she releases what she caught. Establish the dangerous, comic difference between possessing a power and knowing what has to happen next. Her positive mutation screening at thirteen had produced nothing; her relief was real because adolescent heroes were recruited into public lives before they could sensibly choose them.
- **Beginner, with terms.** The agency has useful expertise and induction materials aimed entirely at children. Joss, a flyer whose carrying capacity is sharply limited, teaches her in an empty practice hangar. Delia learns through failed attempts, asks for the actual risks, and declines the publicity visit. He treats her boundaries seriously after an awkward start; she takes his operational expertise seriously. Her longstanding plan for a first long solo trip, after years of work and a now-independent family, gives her something specific she refuses to surrender. She can absorb motion but cannot simply hold it indefinitely or let it vanish.
- **The experiment becomes necessary.** A wind-damaged aerial passenger car is stranded above the city during their session. Joss is called; Delia goes because her safe transport is still his responsibility, and sees that he can ferry people individually but cannot hold the whole descending car. The rescue has people and geography, not just a headline. His attempt buys time and leaves him exhausted outside the car. A conventional stop would leave Delia holding more movement than she can contain; dumping it into the nearest building would transfer the disaster.
- **Her solution has a destination.** Using what the practice established about choosing direction and dividing a release, Delia stops the car at its handrail, transfers successive portions of its fall into upward launches for Joss and the remaining passengers, and finally spends the last dangerous reserve getting herself clear. Their expertise is complementary: he can fly, reach people, and make landings; she can supply the force he cannot carry. She has to consent to the unpractised flight rather than remain the person who holds everyone else in place. Keep the physical sequence legible and let the power visibly change what can happen.
- **A next life she chooses.** After the immediate consequences, Delia's trip is still real. She negotiates further instruction rather than accepting a heroic life by default. End on an earned use of the power in open air with Joss, beyond the emergency: not a recruitment form or an inspirational speech, but Delia discovering she wants to go farther. The rescue does not retire her into permanent service; the ability that disrupted her plans also enlarges them.

Open craft risk: the transfer mechanics must survive a concrete rescue diagram, especially the fact that a release requires contact and Joss cannot suddenly carry the whole car. The action may discover a cleaner version than these beats. Protect Delia's pleasure and choices from being consumed by either symptom comedy or endless duty.
