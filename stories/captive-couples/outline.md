# Outline

## Story

- Proposed reader-facing title: *Held Together*.
- Premise and central promise: Married heroes Aveline Ormont / Tensile and Harlan Ives / Sideslip have spent six bitter weeks turning every patrol decision into a proxy argument about Harlan's failure to tell Aveline that his phasing has begun leaving parts of him numb. Married villains Sana Dacre / Latchkey and Orin Dacre / Parallax capture them during a private theft and imprison them in a purpose-built damping room. Expecting cruelty, the heroes instead see Sana and Orin disagree cleanly: they state needs, divide work by present capacity rather than pride, use a pause phrase before anger turns contemptuous, repair one hurt without cancelling the theft, and protect each other's freedom to refuse. The villains will not become therapists or models of goodness; their healthy marriage exposes the heroes' evasions while their crime keeps moral categories intact. Resolve the prompt when Aveline and Harlan stop weaponizing competence long enough to exchange the operational truth their powers need, escape together, prevent the theft, and make one concrete private agreement for addressing the marriage after the masks come off.
- Intended reader experience: Begin with taut superhero-capture banter in which practiced teamwork keeps misfiring. Let the comic surprise of considerate villains sharpen into uncomfortable recognition rather than a lecture: readers may admire Sana and Orin's marriage while still wanting the heroes to stop them. Build suspense through overheard domestic logistics, small humane boundaries, and a containment system that only honest coordination can beat. End with earned hope, not a repaired marriage by declaration.
- Generating force:
  - Mode: Plot-led intimate containment. Each failed escape reveals that Aveline and Harlan are acting on obsolete assumptions about one another; each villain-couple exchange demonstrates a usable relational behavior without supplying the heroes' answer. The physical escape and marital recognition become the same demand for current, unflattering information.
  - Focal attachment or contradiction: Aveline and Harlan still love, protect, and know one another deeply, but they have converted that history into entitlement to guess. Aveline wants partnership that does not make her manage Harlan's risks; Harlan wants to remain an equal rather than become a patient she monitors. Sana and Orin love each other without confusing loyalty with obedience: they cooperate in crime, preserve separate judgment, and refuse to turn stress into permission for contempt.
  - Plot-led wants and useful limit, if applicable: Aveline immediately wants to escape, secure the stolen phase-key, and prove she can finish the mission without depending on Harlan's unreliable power; she also wants Harlan to admit what he has hidden and choose their marriage over heroic pride. Her useful flaw is prosecutorial competence: once frightened, she converts questions into verdicts and withholds vulnerability until the other person confesses first. Harlan immediately wants to escape, keep Aveline safe, and conceal how badly phasing now impairs him; he also wants her trust and the right to decide what risks his own body takes. His useful limit is shame-driven minimization, which makes every reassurance less trustworthy. Sana wants the phase-key extracted and both captives kept alive without improvisational harm; Orin wants the theft completed but also wants Sana to postpone a cognitively taxing projection after she reports an aura warning. Their competing good is the job versus honoring the limit they agreed to respect.
- Counterforce or complication:
  - Source: Sana and Orin are real antagonists in the caper but not in the marriage. Their damping room translates directed force and phase changes into stored torsion, so brute effort tightens the restraints and destabilizes the stolen phase-key. A transfer window will open when their remote buyer's vehicle arrives before dawn, giving all four people a deadline without creating a public emergency.
  - Credible inside logic, if a person: The Dacres are stealing the phase-key for money and leverage; they avoid killing because murder is neither necessary nor acceptable to them, not because the heroes earn kindness. Sana protects the plan, her bodily warning signs, and the boundary that Orin may flag danger once but may not overrule her body. Orin protects her and the job, but after invoking their agreed pause phrase he accepts her refusal, asks what support she will take, and changes his own task. When challenged, both retain agency and criminal purpose. They will exploit distraction, lie about tactical matters, and recapture the heroes if able.
  - Pressure or change created: The heroes can neither overpower the cell separately nor rely on remembered timing. Escape requires Aveline to disclose the weakness she has been using anger to hide—her tensile field has developed a delayed recoil—and Harlan to give exact duration, numbness, and recovery facts rather than “I'm fine.” The villains' conduct removes the heroes' excuse that stress makes respectful conflict impossible, but only the heroes can decide what their marriage means.
- Narrative design:
  - POV, tense, distance, and limitation: Close third past through Aveline. Make intimate her rapid structural calculations and the accusatory interpretations she lays over Harlan's pauses; keep his symptoms and intentions exterior until he states them. Sana is concrete, procedural, and willing to name anger; Orin uses dry understatement, asks bounded questions, and becomes overprotective by taking tasks rather than raising his voice. Harlan jokes when ashamed and supplies totals instead of feelings. Aveline speaks in cross-examination when afraid, then must learn to make requests.
  - Time shape and compression: One contained night, roughly three hours from the botched intercept through the buyer window. Open after capture with the last seconds of the fight available only as fragmentary sensory recall. Alternate longer periods of enforced waiting and overheard work with three compact escape attempts; use the phase-key's rising whine, Sana's timed aura checks, and an old kitchen timer outside the cell as clocks.
  - Information and reveal strategy: Establish both marriages, masks, capture, and rough patch immediately. Initially let Aveline interpret the villains' courtesy as manipulation. Accumulate observable evidence that it continues when strategically inconvenient: Orin accepts Sana's no, Sana apologizes for a contemptuous jab without surrendering her position, and each follows a previously agreed pause protocol while still opposing the heroes. Reveal Harlan's numbness through a failed phase and his inability to feel Aveline's hand before he names its six-week progression. Reveal Aveline's recoil only when his proposed timing would seriously injure her. Explain only local power and containment limits needed for action; no universal superhero taxonomy or hidden authority.
  - Governing movement: Regain consciousness back-to-back; discover the damping room and stolen phase-key; blame the failed intercept; test force and make the restraints tighten; overhear the Dacres allocate guard, extraction, and food; mistake their check-in for a performance; watch a real dispute arise when Sana's aura warns against another projection; hear Orin invoke their pause phrase, accept her refusal, and offer options; receive water and a direct refusal to discuss the theft; attempt Harlan's old three-count phase and discover his numb hand; have Aveline accuse him of recklessness while concealing her own recoil; hear Sana make a cutting remark to Orin, stop, apologize specifically, and resume the disagreement; recognize that the villains' repair did not require agreement or innocence; let Harlan state the progression and fear beneath his secrecy; let Aveline name her recoil and ask for exact coordination; map the room through small, reversible tests; exploit the timer and the Dacres' task handoff, not their affection; escape into the adjacent workshop; confront the couple in a tight four-person fight; use updated limits to separate phase-key from buyer case; let the Dacres choose retreat together when the plan becomes untenable; secure the key; postpone marital promises until an honest unmasked conversation can occur.
- Speculative surplus: Parallax's repeated spatial projections leave harmless afterimages of recent gestures attached to nearby surfaces for several minutes: a hand continues measuring a gap on the wall; the outline of Sana's pacing crosses an empty doorway; an earlier kiss flickers beside an active disagreement. The residue complicates stealth and sometimes reveals where work occurred, but it cannot authenticate feeling, replay sound, expose lies, or solve the cell.
- Structural distinction: A pressure-room four-hander built from three failed/altered coordination attempts, with the supposed relationship lesson arriving sideways through captive observation. The decisive work occurs first in a private cell and then an adjacent cluttered workshop; the villains are neither converted nor publicly exposed, the heroes prove nothing to a crowd, no institution is rewritten, and no symbol certifies marital health. Unlike a threshold negotiation, branching puzzle, road loop, game-table reconvergence, or two-track body farce, the shape tightens around obsolete shared knowledge becoming dangerous.
- Decisive turn, deepening, or recognition: Harlan proposes their old maneuver—phase on three while Aveline loads the restraint with a counter-pull. She realizes his slowed recovery and her delayed recoil make that familiar choreography mutually unsafe. Instead of calling him careless, she says what her field now does and asks for his actual numbers. He admits he has twelve reliable seconds, cannot feel his left side afterward, and hid it because he feared becoming her responsibility instead of her partner. Aveline admits that her anger has been a way to demand reassurance without risking a request. They do not settle the marriage. They devise a new sequence with verbal checkpoints and either partner empowered to abort; when the first test slips, Harlan says stop and Aveline stops, proving the change before the successful attempt.
- Aftereffect or live uncertainty: The phase-key remains out of the buyer's hands, but Sana and Orin retreat through a prepared route after choosing each other's safety over one more attempt; they remain married, capable, affectionate criminals who may recur only if later canon establishes them. Aveline and Harlan are bruised, their powers still altered, and their underlying resentments unresolved. They agree to cancel the next patrol, disclose the symptoms to one another and an appropriate clinician/technician, and hold a conversation at home in which either can call a pause without ending the marriage. Whether they can rebuild trust is live; the story resolves their first honest turn toward it.
- Opening / ending relation: Open with Aveline and Harlan literally bound back-to-back, each insisting the other ruined the plan and trying to move without announcing it. End after the key is secured with them sitting back-to-back by choice while waiting for extraction—not as a polished emblem, but because their injuries make that position practical—quietly comparing what each can and cannot feel before deciding who will make the call home.

## Beats

1. **The wrong three-count:** Aveline wakes restrained back-to-back with Harlan in a reinforced room, hears the phase-key whining beyond the wall, and reconstructs their capture as a timing failure each blames on the other. Their unannounced first escape efforts feed the damping room and cinch the restraints.
2. **The other marriage at work:** Through the open upper vent, the heroes hear Sana and Orin divide extraction, guarding, food, and the buyer handoff. They disagree without scorekeeping. Aveline assumes rehearsed manipulation, while Harlan notes that no one performs this much logistics for unconscious captives.
3. **A no that remains a no:** Sana's projection aura warns her off another use. Orin tells her once that he thinks continuing is unsafe; she says he is sliding from concern into command. He uses their pause phrase, they take ninety seconds apart, then he asks whether she wants the job delayed, the physical extraction reassigned, or merely his trust. She chooses reassignment and keeps decision authority. The theft proceeds.
4. **Obsolete knowledge:** Harlan attempts the couple's established three-count phase. He emerges early, loses sensation in one hand, and cannot confirm Aveline's touch. She attacks the secrecy as recklessness; he fires back that she treats disclosure as surrender to her management. Neither reveals the full new limit. Their second attempt fails before it begins.
5. **Repair without absolution:** Sana brings water, confirms they will not be harmed if they stop fighting the room, and refuses to return the phase-key. Outside, pressure makes her insult Orin's competence. She catches the contempt, apologizes for the specific jab, and restates the tactical objection; he accepts the repair without pretending agreement. Aveline's expectation that healthy communication belongs only to good people, or only to calm moments, loses its shelter.
6. **Current facts:** The buyer vehicle's arrival signal shortens the deadline. Harlan finally states the twelve-second limit, numbness, and fear of being reduced to a liability. Aveline names her delayed recoil and fear that every hidden symptom means he values heroics over surviving with her. She changes a verdict into a request: exact counts, named sensations, and stop authority for both.
7. **A safe failure:** They map torsion with breath-sized tests. On the first coordinated sequence, Harlan calls stop when numbness spreads sooner than predicted; Aveline obeys despite the shrinking window. The restraint loosens rather than punishes them, establishing that the new process works because it allows refusal, not because trust has magically returned.
8. **Workshop escape:** They time a second attempt to the kitchen timer and the Dacres' task exchange. Harlan phases only the locking pin; Aveline absorbs the released torsion, waits through recoil, and redirects it into the door seam. They enter the workshop as Sana is fitting the key into the buyer case.
9. **Four-person confrontation:** The Dacres fight in coordinated retreat, still checking capacity and accepting aborts. The heroes use the same practice without copying their style: Harlan names dwindling seconds; Aveline revises instead of overriding. They separate the phase-key from the case. Orin proposes withdrawal; Sana weighs one last projection, accepts the lost job, and takes the prepared route with him rather than sacrificing either spouse for a win.
10. **The next private act:** With the threat contained and no audience present, Aveline and Harlan resist declaring themselves fixed. Sitting together because of their injuries, they inventory their present limits, cancel the next patrol, agree on clinical/technical help and a pause protocol for the home conversation, and decide together which of them will make the necessary call.

## Continuity

- Canon used: The story occupies an undated, unnamed modern-like locality in the one persistent shared world. It uses story-local powers and ordinary relationship choices under the binding system-category, living-agency, personhood, audience, and continuity rules. People, aliases, the capture site, buyer, and powers are all new and non-canon unless separately promoted.
- Boundaries and unknowns: Do not call either couple part of the Northbridge trio, Solstice's tradition, the era-local `ultrahuman` category, the unnamed hazard administration, or any universal hero/villain institution. The phase-key, damping room, aliases, phasing, tensile field, projections, costs, buyer, and locality must remain bounded to this story; neither hero nor villain status carries cosmological, legal, or moral taxonomy. The Dacres' healthy marriage must not excuse their capture or theft, and the heroes' escape must not imply that one honest exchange cures six weeks of relational damage. Keep violence non-graphic, all four spouses unambiguously adult, and no wider public proof or recurrence claim.
