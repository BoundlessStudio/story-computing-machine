# Opus review: Held Together

Independent audit for a later editing pass. It is not a `review.md` verdict,
does not change canon state, and does not authorize edits by itself.

## Verdict

- Dialogue: REVISE — all four speakers share one clipped, numeric, correction-driven register; the Kades repeat one routine; key numbers and times can't be recovered; the ending is two formal confessions.
- Prose: REVISE — narration keeps stating the theme and explaining lines just spoken.
- Continuity and prompt: REVISE — the shutter states contradict each other at capture and exit, and nothing in the text says the Kades are married.
- Edit scope: heavy-dialogue
- Existing review.md said: Verdict PASS, `Dialogue: PASS`; disagree — its notes check name collisions only and never test an exchange.

The design is good. The Kades name their exact limits and take correction. The heroes withhold numbers and give orders. The escape works only once Renata says "One" and Jonas stops commanding and starts reporting what he can do and routing her. The dialogue lets that design down on voice. Renata, Jonas, Livia and Xavier all speak in the same terse, article-dropping register of numbers, rulings and corrections. As a result, the contrast between a healthy marriage and a strained one sounds like two crews using the same radio procedure, and the story's deliberate echoes (Jonas's "All right", the matching route calls) get lost in it. The heroes fight about wording, and the narration then explains the fight. The Kades show their "healthy" marriage through four near-identical propose-correct-accept routines with no sign that they are married. The edit pass should keep every plot beat and the numbers motif, and give each speaker a different way to argue. Make every number readable, and end the hospital scene on choices that are asked and answered rather than on two affidavits.

## Edit guardrails

- Prompt: a married hero couple in a rough patch are captured by a married villain couple and are surprised at how healthy the villains' marriage is. It must be a complete story, broadly accessible, 2,500–4,000 words (now about 3,020, so there is room for the Kade and hospital changes). The active profile is `prospective-2026-08-21`, and the rewrite request asks for all dialogue to be revised to that standard.
- POV is close third on Renata. She can watch the Kades but can't know their motives (see D7 on L331).
- Keep the power limits as the story sets them. Nearside moves about five metres along a clear, visible line, three transits per six hours; since the concussion her edges double after the second transit and she loses depth after the third. Fixedpoint makes planes within six metres that last about thirty seconds, and concurrent planes numb his hands. Brownout drains active circuits within three metres into two finite cells. Downforce has three tags on sixty-second cycles and a harness that has to cool. Under LOCKED "Living agency and magic" (universe/rules.md), tags, harness, cells and app only store or shape supplied power and must not act on their own.
- Keep the timeline arithmetic: the ventilation stops at 2:13, the intake scene is at 3:28 (seventy-five minutes later), the reserve countdown starts at 45:00, the abort line is seventy-eight, and the escape comes at 8:41 remaining. Fixes for D4 and D5 must not break it.
- The villains stay villains. Their competence redeems nothing, Livia's "Don't." is for the cells, and the story ends with the heroes still married and the field partnership undecided.
- Keep the terse routing calls in the escape (L273–L327). That register is earned there. The heroes never discuss the Kades' marriage aloud; do not add a scene where they do.
- The story has no water infrastructure; do not add any.

## Dialogue findings

### D1 · Major · One register for four speakers

- Where: throughout; clearest at L79–L85, L109–L119, L151–L167, L173–L189
- Text:
> Xavier did not turn from the loading ramp. "Seated at sixteen. Checked at twenty."
> "Then answer one question. How many?"
> "Can you hold fifty percent?"
> "Not what I said."
- Problem: Identical Voices. Cover the tags and you can't tell Xavier's timestamps from Jonas's "Thirty seconds." (L239), or Livia's "Ceramic." / "Forty-six." from Renata's "Enough." / "One,". Both men say "Sixty seconds." (L65, L131). "Yes." and "All right." pass between the couples freely (L85, L201, L229, L251, L361). Everyone drops articles, answers with a number or a ruling, and closes on a corrective one-liner. The story relies on contrasts and echoes: Jonas borrowing Xavier's "All right", Jonas routing Renata the way Xavier routes Livia (L303), "One wall" answering "You don't renew the west shutter". Those only register if the four people otherwise sound different, and right now they don't.
- Fix: Use the voice map. Jonas says too much when he's afraid. Renata dodges through the task. Livia runs hot and quick. Xavier is slow and repeats himself. Keep the deliberate echoes and make everything around them less uniform. Keep numbers where a number is the point (the limits motif, the abort count) and let other lines be ordinary speech.

### D2 · Major · The hero fight is fought over wording, twice

- Where: L109–L125 (first count exchange through the relay-bay slots); L159–L169 (second, after the ankle twist)
- Text:
> "You don't need to authorize it."
> "It was the shape of it."
> "Stop giving me orders through a wall."
> "That isn't a number."
- Problem: The Ground holds. "You don't need to authorize it" is a readable accusation given the permission fight. But the marriage's rough patch comes out as a dispute about phrasing: correction ping-pong plus a Punchline Patch ("Not what I said." exists to set up "It was the shape of it."). L159–L167 is a Tennis Match of five equal one-liners with no beat, the "Stop X" / "Stop Y" pairing sounds written, and each exchange ends on a neat last word that the narration then explains (L125, L169; see D7). The second exchange repeats the first beat (he asks, she withholds, someone corrects) without adding anything, so it is inert. This couple has a secret veto, a concealed injury and years of history, and none of it comes out when they are trapped and scared.
- Fix: Keep what each exchange does (he needs the count, she withholds it) and cut "Not what I said." / "It was the shape of it." along with the L125 gloss. In the first exchange, let Jonas say too much: a contingency chain that shows his fear and his habit of planning around her. Let Renata answer with the task, the route, or a personal jab from their marriage. Make the second exchange turn rather than repeat. For example, he notices something specific (she keeps closing one eye) and she changes the subject, which plants the suspicion her later confession answers. The opening "You wrote the app." / "I changed one permission." / "Mine." can stay as the one compressed instance of this rhythm. Illustrative only: Jonas: "If it's two, I hold the baffle and you go on the cool-down. If it's one, you land half-blind and I need my hands. If it's none, I stop planning around you. Which is it?"

### D3 · Major · The Kades run one correction routine four times

- Where: L79–L85 (switch order); L137–L153 (burned glove, abort line); L171–L203 (bus sag, hand check); L297–L303 (sled or shutter)
- Text:
> "Return before auxiliary," he said.
> Xavier studied the switch labels, erased one arrow with his thumb, and drew it again beneath AUX. "After auxiliary."
> "Abort at seventy-eight," Xavier said.
> "Seventy-eight," she agreed.
> "You seated it wrong," she said.
- Problem: This is a negotiation/procedure routine. Every Kade exchange follows the same script: one proposes, the other corrects with a reading, and the first repeats the corrected term or says "Yes" / "All right" (L83–L85, L145, L153, L189, L201). These scenes are the prompt's showcase of a healthy marriage, but they play as two technicians sharing one voice. There is no irritation, humor or private shorthand, and no sign they know each other outside the job. The outline's own anti-default was "synchronized villain spouses", and it planned for Livia to snap and Xavier to repeat sequences when rattled. The prose does neither: "You seated it wrong" is flat, and Xavier answers with two timestamps. The best Kade beat (L191–L201, "Open." and the wrist) works because it pairs touch with a limit. The others are rulings.
- Fix: Keep the content of every beat: the switch-order correction, the burn refusal, the misread Livia owns, the hand check, the abort line. Vary the shape. Let Livia really snap at L173, and let owning it cost her a moment instead of the tidy "That was mine." Let Xavier repeat himself when accused. Let one exchange run mostly on action (at L145 she can simply fetch the ceramic clamp). Give them at least one line that isn't about the job (see C2). Do not add a speech about their relationship. Illustrative only: Xavier, not turning: "I seated it. I checked it. I seated it and I checked it, Liv."

### D4 · Major · "Fifty-three" and "west rail" have no referent

- Where: L103–L107 (after Renata's switchgear challenge); L297–L303 (the Kades regroup during the escape)
- Text:
> Livia looked at the scorched cabinet, then at the charging cell. "Xavier, temperature."
> "Fifty-three."
> "Xavier," Livia said. "West rail is seventy-two."
> "Sled or shutter?"
- Problem: Ground/referent. At L103–L105 the reader can't tell what fifty-three measures (switchgear? cell? the room, which read forty-one at L27?), or whether it answers Renata's switchgear challenge. The narration has to step in and say she "had heard Renata". At the climax, "West rail is seventy-two" names a part the story never shows. "West" already means the stairs and a shutter, and "rail" means the handrails Renata is groping for. Xavier's "Sled or shutter?" only follows if the reader links seventy-two to "Abort at seventy-eight" (L151), and that limit was set by a thermal strip on the cell housing (L145), not a rail. The line can only be decoded from the narration after it ("He stepped back at once..."). This is the Kades' one moment of coordination under attack, the place the prompt most needs to be readable.
- Fix: Name the measured object once in each place, using something the reader already has: the housing's thermal strip. Make fifty-three a clear answer to Renata (the switchgear is fine, or it's the cell and she ignores Renata). At L297, have Livia read the housing so that six degrees short of abort explains her choice of the sled. Illustrative only: "Xavier. Housing's at seventy-two."

### D5 · Major · Three clock systems in one format

- Where: L217 (Renata's count), L255–L271 (after the third transit), L371 (intake), L175 (Xavier)
- Text:
> "I used two after 1:31. One left in the six-hour window."
> Third transit. None left until after 7:31.
> The window started at 1:31.
- Problem: Ground/referent. The story writes three kinds of time the same way: the wall clock (2:13, 3:28), the reserve countdown (45:00, 31:18, 12:06, 8:41), and elapsed minutes ("Seated at sixteen. Checked at twenty."). "After 1:31" is a wall-clock time nothing has set up. The story opens at 2:13 and nothing happens at 1:31, so neither Jonas nor the reader knows what opened her window. If the window starts at her first transit, 1:31 implies a transit that her intake count ("Three") leaves out. Worse, L271's "None left until after 7:31" comes a few paragraphs after "At 8:41, the anchor tag went gray." On the countdown, 7:31 is seventy seconds away, so a reader can think her transit comes back mid-fight, which drains the escape's stakes. The red reserve clock (L69) is never explained either, so Livia's "five minutes to transfer the reserve after we're gone" (L99) can't be checked.
- Fix: Keep the countdown in digits and always mark it ("remaining", as L207 does). Write wall-clock times in words or keep them out of dialogue. Either say once what happened at 1:31 or have Renata count transits "tonight". At L271, give a duration instead of a time. Say once what the reserve clock counts down to. Illustrative only: "Two tonight. One left."

### D6 · Major · The intake scene ends in paired affidavits

- Where: L367–L381, hospital intake
- Text:
> I hid the change from medical and from my partner.
> On the phone, the supervisor asked, "Why did your route split?"
> I did it unilaterally, and I didn't disclose it to command. Renata rejected the route. We stopped sharing information before entry.
- Problem: The last meaningful dialogue opens with two complete, parallel statements in report register. Renata's L371 repeats L217 almost point for point ("the second transit doubles edges, and the third removes usable depth") and ends on a clause-perfect admission. Jonas's L379 is its mirror and ends on the thesis: "We stopped sharing information before entry." That is the Philosopher. The supervisor's question is plausible but exists mostly to cue it (explanatory chorus). Some formality is earned, since this is a physician and a supervisor on the record. The problem is the completeness and the symmetry: it reads as a reconciliation script, which the outline itself warned against ("Avoid full confession"). The beats that follow ("Remove the veto." through "Left.") already show the change.
- Fix: Give the physician only what she needs, and let the admission come out as an answer rather than a volunteered summary. Cut the repeat of the symptom list and either cut the "I hid..." sentence or let a question draw it out. Cut "I did it unilaterally" and "We stopped sharing information before entry". Jonas states the fact and stops, and his discomfort can stay in the warming pad. Keep everything from "Remove the veto," to the end. Illustrative only: physician: "Does medical know about the doubling?" / Renata: "No."

### D7 · Major · Narration explains the line just spoken

- Where: L107, L125, L169, L209, L283, L331, L381
- Text:
> She had heard Renata. She simply returned to the theft.
> There it was again. The shape and the substance.
> She knew. That was why she had chosen it.
> Not a command. An inventory.
> Silence, just long enough for him to look instead of assume.
> The plea was for the cells, not the reserve or the workers behind the door.
> He did not add a reason after the fact.
- Problem: Seven times, a sentence after a line of dialogue tells the reader what it meant, leaving nothing to infer. Three of them explain the story's central moves: Renata's evasion, Jonas moving from command to a list of limits, and Jonas not justifying himself. L331 also states Livia's motive, which Renata's close POV can't know.
- Fix: Cut these sentences. Where the rhythm needs a beat, use something observable (a hand, a sound, a pause). Keep L231 ("The words sounded unfinished in his mouth.") and L273's "His command voice caught on the first word, then changed." They report what Renata hears, not what it means.

### D8 · Minor · Two small knowledge slips

- Where: L63–L65 against L129–L131 (tag cycle); L305–L307 (before "One wall")
- Text:
> Jonas had seen it too. "Sixty seconds," he said.
> Renata saw his shoulders set for it, saw his numb fingers twitch.
- Problem: Ground/knowledge. Xavier says "Sixty seconds." aloud at L65 while Jonas can hear, so Jonas's line at L131 isn't a deduction, even though it's framed as something he "had seen". It also makes the two men say the identical line. At L305, after the third transit has left Renata seeing "two dark figures" and no distances, she still sees Jonas's numb fingers twitch, and her "One wall" depends on that.
- Fix: Cut "Sixty seconds." from Xavier's callout at L65 (keep "West shutter, relay shutter, anchor") so Jonas's line becomes his own deduction. At L305, give Renata something she could actually perceive, such as his breath catching or the edge of a second plane starting to grow.

## Continuity and prompt findings

### C1 · Blocking · Shutter states contradict at capture and exit

- Where: L51–L59 (capture); L135, L149, L195–L197 (west-shutter tag); L287, L305–L309 (Jonas's one plane); L349–L353 (the Kades' exit)
- Text:
> The shutter behind Renata dropped so hard its bottom edge starred the concrete.
> A pale plane grew under the descending shutter, rigid and translucent.
> The relay shutter lifted. Jonas ducked under it as Xavier renewed the tag, and the metal crashed onto the plane Jonas formed beneath it.
> He kept the shutter off them.
> He tapped the still-mounted west-shutter tag, increased the shutter's downward load,
> Jonas's plane expired. The shutter hit concrete between the heroes and the ramp.
- Problem: At L51 the west shutter slams down, and at L55 Jonas's plane grows "under the descending shutter", so the same shutter is down and then falling. After his first plane sinks under it (L59), nothing raises it. Yet at the exit Xavier weights the west shutter, and when "Jonas's plane expired", "The shutter hit concrete between the heroes and the ramp". At that point Jonas's only plane is under the relay shutter (L287). So either a shutter that is already down falls again, or Xavier weights the wrong shutter. "He kept the shutter off them." (L309) doesn't say which shutter or who "them" is. This beat is the villains' escape and the reason the heroes don't pursue.
- Fix: Decide which shutter Jonas's single plane holds after L287 and where that shutter stands relative to the ramp. Say it plainly at L309, and have Xavier weight that same shutter at L349. At the capture, either have the plane catch the west shutter as it falls and then fail under the added load, or drop "descending". Keep "He did not make another.", because not pursuing has to remain his choice.

### C2 · Major · The Kades are never shown to be married

- Where: throughout; L45 (Livia introduced), L75, L205
- Text:
> The Kades did not celebrate.
> She had expected vanity, cruelty, one of them performing dominance for the other.
- Problem: The prompt's premise is a married villain couple, but the text never says the Kades are married. There is no word for it, no ring, no private name, no shared history, no domestic reference. The only signal is the shared surname, which fits siblings or a family crew just as well. Renata's lens at L205 implies a couple without establishing one. The heroes' marriage is established (L121, L397). The prompt's central beat, the heroes' surprise at a healthy marriage, depends on the reader knowing it is one.
- Fix: Add one concrete marker the reader can't miss, preferably inside Kade dialogue (a private name, a reference to home, an old argument), or a detail Renata could know from the case files (L51) or see (a ring on the hand Livia turns over at L45). One is enough; do not add a speech.
