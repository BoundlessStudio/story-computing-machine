# Proposed canon delta

- Status: PENDING

This file proposes reusable shared-universe facts. It is not canon until the
user explicitly approves promotion and the facts are added to `universe/`.
Every proposal below is limited to an undated, Seattle-named locality in the
modern-like era; the story does not establish literal real-world Earth.

## New characters or character facts

- The unnamed adult first-person narrator owns
  and works at Hinge. They initially treat close observation of customers as
  harmless hospitality, permit Tess's receipt-paper relationship chart, and
  urge Nora to approach Rae. After recognizing the privacy and consent costs,
  they destroy the chart, stop disclosing or interpreting customer patterns,
  refuse to carry messages, and leave Nora and Claire's choices to them. The
  narrator remains intentionally unnamed and ungendered.
- **Nora (proposed):** A thirty-one-year-old museum-label designer and Sunday
  Hinge regular who favors cardamom lattes and quiet novels. Tess temporarily
  labels her `Green Coat` on a staff-only chart. Nora spends eleven Sundays
  idealizing Rae, accepts Rae's clear rejection, and gradually learns about
  Claire through consented conversation rather than observation or staff
  reports. In May she asks Claire for one intentional date before Claire's
  field assignment; Claire accepts on the condition that they proceed slowly
  without pretending no choice has been made.
- **Claire (proposed):** A twenty-nine-year-old illustrator of field guides and
  children's nonfiction who eventually chooses honey-ginger tea at Hinge.
  Tess temporarily labels her `Tea Girl`. Before meeting Nora, Claire makes
  repeated private pencil studies of her without permission. Claire identifies
  that conduct as a boundary violation, stops drawing Nora, apologizes without
  asking for absolution, and keeps the old pages closed. She later shares only
  current work by choice. She accepts Nora's invitation before leaving for a
  six-week coastal field assignment at the beginning of June.
- **Rae (proposed):** An adult woman in her thirties, a Hinge regular, and a
  confident crossword solver who favors black coffee. Tess temporarily labels
  her `Red Scarf`. Rae has not encouraged Nora's private fantasy; she rejects
  Nora's date request kindly and clearly because she is already seeing
  Highway, then continues an ordinary independent life without becoming an
  obstacle or antagonist.
- **Highway (proposed):** An adult man in his thirties and Rae's boyfriend.
  `Highway` is the only character-facing form he supplies; the story does not
  determine whether it is a surname, nickname, or mononym. He is friendly
  background rather than Nora's rival.
- **Tess (proposed):** A twenty-three-year-old Hinge barista who values correct
  cup names but initially converts customer patterns into a chart and emotional
  cup annotations. She helps destroy the chart, repairs Paul's annotated cup,
  stops romantic intervention, and learns to ask before adding decorative
  drawings.
- **Frank (proposed):** An older Sunday regular who orders steamed milk
  ostensibly for himself and allows Tess to draw Captain on his cup sleeve.
- **Captain (proposed):** Frank's gray terrier, not a human title.
- **Jonathan (proposed):** An adult Hinge customer whose cup helps expose the
  difference between recording a requested name and commenting on a customer.
  `Jomathan` is Tess's one-off misspelling of Jonathan, not another identity.
- **Paul (proposed):** An adult Hinge customer whose `PAUL?` cup Tess replaces
  with an unannotated `PAUL` cup before apologizing.

## New locations

- **Hinge café (proposed):** A neighborhood café at Broadway and Mercer in a
  Seattle-named modern-like city. Its name predates and is explicitly unrelated
  to the matchmaking service with the same name. It has fogged front windows,
  a delivery-side rosebush belonging to the neighboring building, a radiator,
  a crooked ferry print, mismatched tables, a communal crossword table, and a
  stable Sunday clientele.
- **Seattle-named neighborhood geography (proposed):** The local setting uses
  Capitol Hill, Broadway, Mercer, Volunteer Park, Lake Union, and Route 49 as
  ordinary place and transit names. These references establish only local
  modern-like geography. They do not import a nation, exact calendar,
  real-world historical sequence, or every fact about literal Seattle.

## New factions or cultural facts

- **Hinge's corrected customer practice (proposed, story-local):** Staff write
  requested names on cups, add decorative flourishes only after permission,
  and, after the chart incident, do not maintain romantic charts, swap cups,
  disclose visit histories, interpret customers to one another, or carry
  relationship messages. This is one café's learned practice, not a universal
  institution or cultural rule.

No new faction is proposed.

## New rules, capabilities, or costs

None.

Cup names, misspellings, chart labels, arrival times, sketches, the umbrella,
and the facing final cups have only ordinary social and symbolic force. They do
not compel attraction, reveal truth, bind identity, create fate, or establish a
local name-magic system. The costs and repairs in the story arise through
attention, consent, rejection, privacy, speech, and choice.

## Timeline events

- **Undated Hinge relationship sequence (proposed):** From early autumn through
  May in the modern-like era, Nora watches Rae for eleven Sundays, Claire
  watches and sketches Nora, staff observation turns the pattern into a chart,
  and the resulting boundary recognition ends staff intervention. During a
  storm Claire offers Nora an umbrella and discloses the sketches; Nora then
  asks Rae out and accepts her rejection. Over subsequent months Nora and
  Claire learn about one another through direct conversation, survive an honest
  test of Nora's motive, and agree in May to one date outside Hinge. Claire's
  six-week coastal field assignment is still scheduled to begin in June, and
  the relationship beyond the first date remains open.

No calendar year, nation, Galactic Cycle coordinate, or placement relative to
Ravel is established.

## New glossary terms or aliases

  aliases):** Tess's temporary receipt-chart labels for Nora, Rae, and Claire,

  chart is destroyed; they have no supernatural function.

  Jonathan on a cup, not a separate person.
