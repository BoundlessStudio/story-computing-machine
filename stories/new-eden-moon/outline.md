# An Unreasonable Moon

## Promised experience

The request gives New Eden’s night sky to a corporation that sells artificial sunsets, designer constellations, and moonlight to the highest bidders. A young woman street artist secretly hacks its atmospheric projection network, replaces advertisements with visions of the beautiful natural world humanity has forgotten, and paints a gigantic full moon over the city. “Street artist” and “secretly hacks” promise, to my reading, the pleasure of a dare carried off with an artist’s eye. “Sprawling metropolis” and “gigantic” put the payoff on the scale of a whole city. The reference’s weathered roof, two absorbed figures, and huge cratered disk suggest quiet wonder within that scale. Their relationship, the outcome of the hack, and its duration are open. The attachment direction governs how the reference is read.

## Takes considered

- **A live takeover.** Follow the artist from the first stolen ad panel through the company’s attempts to paint over it. The longest scene puts the whole sky briefly within her reach and makes her decide what to leave there.
- **A print made afterward.** Begin with the moon gone and the artist’s old lettering partner commissioned to reproduce it from the one image he took. Reconstruct the night through the parts of the picture that he and the artist remember differently, with their argument over the print as the hinge.
- **The evening auction.** Follow a bid spotter in the premium viewing pavilion as the artist takes over the previews. The central scene is a roomful of buyers trying to bid on a sky whose maker has quietly withdrawn it from sale.

Choose the live takeover. Each change in the projected sky comes from a choice the artist can make in front of us. The longest scene can begin in the pleasure of seeing what she has stolen and change, in the same span of attention, to the risk of taking the whole thing.

## Provisional direction

Nima has found a way into Daymark’s atmospheric projection network through the old proof relay on Vane House. She plans to borrow a few strips of sky without giving up the opening she’s spent months making. Emil, who used to letter their street pieces, knows about the local job and wants her to leave while she can still use the relay another night. She puts a white-flashing birch wood and a submerged forest of kelp over the ad strips. From the roof they can see the city catching sight of the images as Daymark restores its spots one by one.

The projectors hold their last picture when the feed drops. For the auction’s most expensive lot, all the city’s projectors are briefly taking one picture through the relay. Nima can withdraw while her intrusion still looks local, or use that short opening to paint across every district at once. She chooses the latter. She lays down a dark field and works up her old studies of the lunar face into one gigantic full moon, leaving the pocks and dark plains the moons for sale smooth away. Once it is overhead she cuts the feed to hold the picture. Daymark will come to this relay to restore service, and she loses her covert way back in. For now the full moon is visible even from the parts of town that usually get the ads.

## Craft plan

**Teller.** Try close third past with Nima’s irritable, delighted eye for a surface, and first person present with her drawing in real time and giving us her shifting idea of what the work needs. In either voice the view expands with the painting. Stay with the making and with the strange pleasure of seeing the work at a distance she has never had before.

**Nima.** A young adult who covets a wall big enough to be seen from across town, and still wants a stranger to recognize her touch on it. She talks with her hands and in bursts of exact looking, then lets out a longer, excited argument when someone mistakes what she’s showing them. The right figure’s choppy dark bob and dusty rose pullover suggest a wind-tossed, compact presence, with palms braced wide and a knee in work-blue denim. Let her clear a rain-smeared screen with her cuff, brace the tablet on that knee to draw through the train’s vibration, and give up pushing the hair out of her eyes once she can watch the moon directly. The wide, planted hands come back when she sits. Read that ease as a practiced familiarity with height.

**Emil.** A young adult who now letters shop signs. He wants Nima to keep the opening and wants to be there when her piece goes up. He fills tense waits with long stories about actual customers and neighbors, trying out how they’ll see the same thing. The left figure’s dark, untidy hair, blue-gray overshirt with gathered sleeves, and elbow over one high knee suggest someone comfortable staying put and watching a little from the side. He rolls a sleeve higher to keep wet cloth off her screen, pins their loose blackout fabric under his knee against the slipstream, and leans out to get the first look down the line. At the end he turns his shoulder square to the sky. The image shows both people from behind. Faces and histories are open, and their old partnership is invented.

**Place.** Vane House sits above the Needle Bend, where the night train swings out past the roofs. Late summer, just after rain, in the hour between a demonstration sunset and the auction’s big preview. People hang their blackout cloth to dry here and commute home just below. There is the sweet-hot breath of the compressor, the screech of the curve felt through a knee, chilled wet concrete, pink lamps reflected underfoot, and rust flaking from the old proof dish. The relay survives here because its roof once had the city’s widest sightline. Wind off the train catches the cloth and the parapet puts the approaching carriages almost at eye level. The Daymark pavilion would give the auction too much of the scene, and the covered Needle Bend platforms would give Nima little sky to work across.

## Scene briefs

1. **Proof, 25 percent.** On Vane House before the preview, Emil wants to get the cloth in and see what Nima has brought. She wants a witness to her first large surface. The fading showroom sunset gives way to an ad frozen in mid-fall when she drops her tiny test feed. She watches the hold and brings up the white undersides of leaves in the first live strip. Summarize the months of testing and the source of the paintings in a few strokes.
2. **Takeover, 60 percent, the long scene.** Same roof as the preview approaches. Let them follow the light through the city and talk while the kelp bends with the swell. Daymark’s ads advance through the art. Emil wants another night. Nima risks her way in, works the full moon into the sky as the shared feed opens, and cuts it once the picture reaches all the districts. The last glow on the tablet gives way to the whole, held, scarred face overhead. The auction room and the pursuit stay outside the scene.
3. **The hold, 15 percent.** At the coping just afterward, Nima and Emil sit with the picture. At the start the train carries people past the roof behind drawn shades and the block’s hanging cloths bar the ad glare. Now on the curve people raise the train shades window by window. The flats beyond answer with their own lifted cloths, across districts that have never had the same view. End with the opening windows and the same enormous moon above them, while the paint is still up.
