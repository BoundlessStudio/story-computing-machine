# An Unreasonable Moon

## Promised experience

The request gives New Eden’s night sky to a corporation that sells artificial sunsets, designer constellations, and moonlight to the highest bidders. A young woman street artist secretly hacks its atmospheric projection network, replaces advertisements with visions of the beautiful natural world humanity has forgotten, and paints a gigantic full moon over the city. “Street artist” and “secretly hacks” suggest the pleasure of a dare carried off with an artist’s eye. “Sprawling metropolis” and “gigantic” put the payoff on the scale of a whole city. The reference’s weathered roof and two absorbed figures suggest quiet wonder within that scale. Their relationship and the outcome are open. The attachment direction governs how the reference is read.

## Takes considered

- **A live takeover.** Follow the artist from the first stolen ad panel through the company’s attempts to paint over it. The longest scene puts the whole sky briefly within her reach and makes her decide what to leave there.
- **A print made afterward.** Begin with the moon gone and the artist’s old lettering partner commissioned to reproduce it from the one image he took. Reconstruct the night through the parts of the picture that he and the artist remember differently, with their argument over the print as the hinge.
- **The evening auction.** Follow a bid spotter in the premium viewing pavilion as the artist takes over the previews. The central scene is a roomful of buyers trying to bid on a sky whose maker has quietly withdrawn it from sale.

Choose the live takeover. It gives the artist the choices that change the sky, with room for her pleasure at seeing what she has stolen to turn into a desire for the whole thing.

## Provisional direction

Nima has spent a summer finding her way into Daymark’s atmospheric projection network through the old proof relay on Vane House. She plans to slip a different painting into a few ad strips every night she can get away with it. Emil, her former lettering partner, wants her to get out of the network while she still has somewhere to come back to. A wind-blown birch wood and a submerged forest of kelp take the place of ads. Emil knows kelp as a flavor. He follows the surge through the stems, expecting the forest to break the surface. The Vane relay is still live. Daymark sends its ads back through it strip by strip and cuts the forest in pieces.

The projectors hold their last picture when the feed drops. Daymark previews its smooth designer moon citywide for the length of its rise, with every district listening to the same line through Vane House. Then it switches the projection to the winning district alone and gives the other districts back to ads. As the shared feed opens, Nima takes it. She lays down a dark field and paints in the full disk from her study of the lunar face. The glare swallows her first sweep of gray. Emil takes the largest remaining dark spot for a fault in the relay. She brings the white down, enlarges the disk, and pushes that darkness out into a whole plain with a ragged edge. The bright rim and the marks around it come clear from the coping. She cuts the Vane relay while the projectors still share its feed. Her moon holds until Daymark can restore the line and send a new picture. The break brings Daymark to Vane House and ends the run of paintings she had planned.

## Craft plan

**Teller.** Try close third past with Nima’s irritable, delighted eye for a surface, and first person present with her drawing in real time and giving us her shifting idea of what the work needs. The view expands with the painting. Stay with Nima as she sees her own work at a distance she has never had before.

**Nima.** A young adult who covets a wall big enough to be seen from across town, and still wants a stranger to recognize her touch on it. She talks with her hands and in bursts of exact looking, then lets out a longer, excited argument when someone mistakes what she’s showing them. The right figure’s choppy dark bob and dusty rose pullover suggest a wind-tossed, compact presence, with palms braced wide and a knee in work-blue denim. Let her clear a rain-smeared screen with her cuff, brace the tablet on that knee to draw through the train’s vibration, and give up pushing the hair out of her eyes once she can watch the moon directly. The wide, planted hands come back when she sits. Read that ease as a practiced familiarity with height.

**Emil.** A young adult who now letters shop signs. He wants Nima to keep the opening and wants to be there when her piece goes up. He fills tense waits with long stories about actual customers and neighbors, trying out how they’ll see the same thing. The left figure’s dark, untidy hair, blue-gray overshirt with gathered sleeves, and elbow over one high knee suggest someone comfortable staying put and watching a little from the side. He rolls a sleeve higher to keep wet cloth off her screen, pins their loose blackout fabric under his knee against the slipstream, and leans out to get the first look down the line. At the end he turns his shoulder square to the sky. Both faces are out of view in the image. Their history is invented.

**Place.** Vane House sits above the Needle Bend, where the night train swings out past the roofs. Late summer, just after rain, in the hour between a demonstration sunset and the moon preview. People hang their blackout cloth to dry here and commute home just below. There is the sweet-hot breath of the compressor, the screech of the curve felt through a knee, chilled wet concrete, pink lamps reflected underfoot, and rust flaking from the proof dish. The relay survives here because its roof once had the city’s widest sightline. The parapet puts the approaching carriages almost at eye level. Across the curve is Daymark Tower, where the auction signs name the winning district. The pavilion would give the auction too much of the scene, and the covered platforms would give Nima little sky to work across.

## Scene briefs

1. **Proof, 25 percent.** On Vane House before the preview, Emil wants to get the cloth in and see what Nima has brought. A train passes with the shades drawn as the demonstration sunset ends. A sample constellation with the stars of a monogram takes its place. When Nima drops her test feed an ad freezes mid-fall. She watches the hold and puts the first live strip of white-flashing birch leaves overhead. Summarize the source of her nature studies.
2. **Takeover, 60 percent, the long scene.** On the roof, let them follow the light through town and talk while the kelp bends with the swell. Nima sees the tower send ads through the art. Emil asks her to save the opening. The preview gives her the city’s canvas. Give the painting time as the relay and the selling clock press on her. From the coping the disk looks blank as the company’s sample until she uses the crater field and dark plain to make the surface read. Nima cuts the feed on that choice. Leave the auction room and the recovery crew offstage.
3. **The hold, 15 percent.** At the coping just afterward, Nima sits back with her palms spread and lets her hair blow across her face. Emil shifts nearer and tries out the hollow’s ragged outline with a finger. As the next train comes around the bend, people raise its shades window by window. The flats beyond answer with their own lifted cloths.

## Ending

The train’s shut shades and the rooftop’s blackout fabric establish how people fend off the sold sky. The returning ads sever each district’s view from the next. End with the newly open windows and the same enormous moon above them, while the paint is still up.
