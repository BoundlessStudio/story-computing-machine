# Outline

## Story

- Premise and central promise: Six weeks after saving an unnamed kingdom, five companions find that sprawling Manydoor Hall offers ample room to avoid one another. Bored blue dragon Oruvane removes the interior doors and builds a library “dungeon”; recovering privacy forces the household through romance, grievance, and chosen absence.
- Focal pressure or attachment: Former leader Hedra Finne wants everyone together. Unable to call separate routines desertion, she turns affection into rosters and assignments that reproduce the command habits her friends retired from.
- Counterforce or complication: Ivena Mar and Torvik Auld want privacy without secession; Cavren Joll wants reliability treated as choice; Oruvane wants novelty without becoming transport or weapon. None wants the friendship ended, but his intrusive game exposes what each concealed.
- POV, distance, and information limit: Close third past through Hedra, who notices routes, attendance, and useful hands before emotion. Other motives emerge only through habits, speech, claimed thresholds, and refused burdens.
- Governing movement and time shape: A circular week: compress six evenings of separation and mischief; expand the seventh night’s door maze and predawn roof decision; return three days later to the library. Keep the climax private and relational.
- Speculative rule or ordinary-world constraint: Speaking, flying Oruvane can move doors, but fits comfortably only in the great library, broad passages, and roof court; human rooms exclude him. These are individual traits. No oath or magic compels anyone to stay or return.
- Dialogue promise: Warm ensemble comedy with bruised affection: overlapping domestic talk, accidentally excluding lovers’ shorthand, stale quest jokes, petty complaints, and a grievance eloquence cannot neutralize. Directness changes choices without total reconciliation.
- Dialogic medium: Speech, meal rosters, labels, claimed or carried doors, habitual touch, refused help, silence, spatial pranks, departure, and voluntary return.
- Dialogue engine: Recovering each door makes obsolete quest pairings negotiate weight, route, ownership, and privacy. Withheld plans resemble abandonment; disclosure changes who helps, leaves, and interprets a closed door.

## Voice

- Narrative texture: Follow Hedra through cluttered thresholds and layered household sound, warmly but unsentimentally. Find comedy in heroic competence misapplied to furniture and hurt; register firelight, blue windows, books, ladders, and shared space before interpretation.
- Conversational texture: Interruptions, unfinished practical remarks, frayed jokes, romantic shorthand, blunt requests, and talk overheard across alcoves; not every utterance needs wit or subtext.
- Rhetorical ownership: Hedra reasons in routes and mistakes authority for care. Cavren owns literal correction, dry understatement, and refusal to answer. Ivena plainly names comfort and embarrassment; Torvik evades with affectionate exaggeration. Oruvane makes grand quest-shaped misreadings and listens poorly to flattery.
- Pressure behavior: Hedra speeds up and delegates; Cavren contracts before becoming direct; Ivena loses courtesy; Torvik overperforms humor, then stops; Oruvane rearranges rooms instead of explaining.
- Relationship movement: Hedra wants belonging but cannot request reassurance. Cavren wants choice without granting absolution. The lovers want privacy without exile. Oruvane wants to be missed as a friend, not needed as a capability. Exchanges alter trust and permission, not old roles.
- Anti-default: Resist polished professionals solving housing. Keep sulking, bad jokes, embarrassment, territorial pettiness, mistaken motives, and friendship unfinished after the obstruction goes.

## Beats

1. Establish the book-crammed library: amber hearth and lamps against blue tall windows, shelves and ladders, intimate activities around Oruvane’s folded bulk. Hedra counts absences as the lovers withdraw, Cavren eats elsewhere, and Oruvane’s bids for play disrupt.
2. Across six evenings, Hedra adds compulsory togetherness while Oruvane escalates irritating inventions. She calls closed doors a scheduling defect and misses that Cavren never unpacked.
3. On night seven, thirteen doors form a crooked maze around Oruvane’s nest, old gear, and handwritten challenges. Comedy sharpens when the lovers lose privacy, Cavren’s packed chest appears, and Hedra’s roster becomes a prop.
4. Hedra assigns old marching pairs. Torvik jokes past Ivena’s anger, Cavren refuses assumed command, and Oruvane changes the route when ignored. Carrying forces new pairings through the hall.
5. Ivena makes Hedra understand that constant access, not romance, is separating them. Cavren reveals seasonal plans and his grievance: Hedra diverted promised relief while he held the final stair. He rejects tactical justification without an instant apology scene.
6. The last door is Oruvane’s roof gate. Packed for flight, he reveals the dungeon as a clumsy test: he wants his own journey but fears leaving proves the party existed only under threat. Each answers through choices about help and accompaniment; Hedra issues no order.
7. Oruvane leaves alone. Three days later he returns by choice to an unresolved card-game quarrel in the library. They make room without suspending it; he takes a side, restoring lively overlap rather than quest formation.

## Continuity

- Canon used: An independent fantasy-era entry point within the deep-time shared world; living agency and personhood remain consequential.
- Boundaries and unknowns: Kingdom, era, geography, victory, and dragon culture remain unconnected. Oruvane’s traits establish no species rule or recurring relationship.
