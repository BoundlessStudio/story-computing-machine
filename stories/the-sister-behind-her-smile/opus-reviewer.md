# Opus review: The Sister Behind Her Smile

Independent audit for a later editing pass. It is not a `review.md` verdict,
does not change canon state, and does not authorize edits by itself.

## Verdict

- Dialogue: REVISE — two dressing-room Ground failures (a knowledge leak and a line that misreports what Odrienne just said), and the key exchanges end on thesis cappers.
- Prose: REVISE — the narration explains the "my questions were commands" lesson four more times after the dialogue has already said it.
- Continuity and prompt: PASS — the prompt is delivered at 3,340 words; the remaining issues are Minor (Felisane's errand, what she knew about the wrist, a lamp nobody brought, "dictated" against "pen", canal references).
- Edit scope: targeted-dialogue
- Existing review.md said: PASS, "Dialogue: PASS"; I disagree. It missed both Ground failures and the repeated thesis lines at the climax and the ending.

The dialogue's best idea, the limited medium, works: Odrienne's stock agreements under the spell, the two-quick-one-slow tap code, the mirror colors that show what she can't say, and the musicians' uneven squabbling. The failures cluster where the story says out loud what that medium has already shown: Felisane relabels Camet's questions as "requests", Camet misreports Odrienne's words so Felisane can cap them, and the siblings' reckoning ends on "You asked constantly. That was the trouble." Felisane is also the default corrector and moral commentator, closing most exchanges she is in and never fraying after a night without sleep. The edit pass should fix the two Ground slips, show the lesson once, let Felisane be tired and plain, and give freed Odrienne the uneven, petty, emotional voice the narration promised, while leaving the signal scenes and the musicians alone.

## Edit guardrails

- Prompt requirements: first-person Camet; the sister is emotional, spoiled and conniving, Camet still loves her, she turns suddenly kinder, and something is off; a complete story that resolves this; broadly accessible tone. Stay within 2,500–4,000 words (now about 3,340). The active craft profile is prospective-2026-08-21 (from the rewrite constraints). There is no required verbatim line.
- Universe: LOCKED "Living agency and magic" (universe/rules.md): only life uses magic. Keep the silver thread, mirrors and music as channels for Odrienne's and the singers' force, and keep the "living anchor" as a stitch through a ribbon against her skin. LOCKED "Personhood and moral consequence" (style-guide.md): the spell must not become Odrienne's "real" self or erase her choices. Keep her cutting the stitch herself, and keep Camet not putting the knife in her hand.
- Internal facts the plot depends on: the chambermaid and ribbon six days earlier; kindness from the next morning; seven dawn cadences with this dawn the seventh; the four-note courtesy phrase and the grace clamping on the fourth note; color showing only in the reflection; two quick, one slow = "Follow"; Mother's harp; the blue guest room and six petitioners; the pastry cook fourteen months ago; Felisane refusing the clasp.
- What works and must not be flattened: Odrienne's stock agreements while graced, the tap code, the baseline mirror tests, the musicians' squabbling, Felisane's labor-based bluntness in practical moments, and the petty return of Odrienne's preferences at breakfast.

## Dialogue findings

### D1 · Blocking · Felisane knows how the seam tore

- Where: L143–L155, dressing room, as Felisane and Camet arrive
- Text:
> "It cannot. You tore it reaching behind the mirror."
> Odrienne went still.
- Problem: Ground/knowledge and Ground/referent. Felisane has been serving at supper and then in the corridor with Camet. She never saw Odrienne change or reach for anything, and the page shows no evidence she could read the cause from, such as a mirror pulled askew or a snag on the frame. She still states the cause flatly, and Odrienne's stillness confirms it as true. "Behind the mirror" is also never paid off: the workbasket and card are under the gown on the couch, and nothing is ever found behind a mirror. The reader is handed a plant that leads nowhere. "It can wait." / "It cannot." is also correction ping-pong.
- Fix: Either show the evidence Felisane reads (a mirror shifted from the wall, silver lint on the frame) and pay off what Odrienne was reaching for, or cut the "behind the mirror" clause. The simplest version is to let Felisane point only at the open seam. That seam then sets up L231 ("You were trying to take the gown apart") without claiming knowledge she lacks. Drop "It cannot." and let her simply keep hold of the sleeve.

### D2 · Blocking · "She didn't say anything" misreports what Odrienne said

- Where: L235–L251, dressing room, after Odrienne strikes Camet's wrist
- Text:
> She looked relieved before I finished. "If you think it best."
> Felisane put herself between us. "She said no plainly enough."
> "She didn't say anything."
> "She hit you."
- Problem: Ground/scene description plus Punchline Patch. Eight lines earlier Odrienne said "If you think it best," which is a spoken yes. Camet's "She didn't say anything" is simply false. It exists so that Felisane can land "She hit you." The real conflict here, a spoken yes against a physical no, is the story's consent point, and the line throws it away.
- Fix: Have Camet cite what she actually said, and deny Felisane the neat capper. Let her answer with a look at the red mark, or say nothing and keep her body between them. Illustrative only: "She said if I thought it best." Keep "She said no plainly enough." Keep Felisane stepping between them.

### D3 · Major · Word-game about "requests" instead of stopping visible harm

- Where: L183–L191, dressing room, after Camet's run of questions
- Text:
> Felisane caught my sleeve. "Stop making requests."
> "I'm asking questions."
> "To someone who agrees with them. Stop."
- Problem: Correction ping-pong over word choice at a moment of physical distress. The reader has just seen violet crowding into black, a mouth opening and a jaw forced shut. Felisane responds by relabelling his speech rather than to what she sees. Her theory ("someone who agrees") also doesn't fit these particular questions. A "yes" to "Are you angry with me?" or "Do you dislike the canal petitioners?" would be a complaint the spell forbids, so Odrienne isn't agreeing; she is choking on an answer she can't give. This is also the first of about six statements of the questions-as-commands idea (see P1 and D4).
- Fix: Felisane should stop him because of what is happening to Odrienne, in plain words. Cut the requests/questions exchange. Keep "Felisane caught my sleeve" and a flat "Stop." Illustrative only: "Stop. Look at her mouth."

### D4 · Major · The siblings' reckoning ends on a thesis punchline

- Where: L405–L419, mirror chamber, just after the stitch is cut
- Text:
> Anger arrived openly, uneven and alive.
> "I should have asked sooner."
> "You asked constantly. That was the trouble."
> There was too much truth in that for a graceful answer.
- Problem: Punchline Patch and Philosopher at the final meaningful sibling exchange. "Asked sooner" is vague (asked what, and when?). It exists so that Odrienne can flip "asked" into the story's lesson. Her first free anger comes out as a balanced aphorism, although the narration has just called it "uneven". The line after it then glosses the aphorism. The good part comes first: "You were pleased." / "At first." / "At first," she repeated, uglier. That is sharp and should stay.
- Fix: Keep L407–L413. Replace Camet's setup with something he would really say, or with an action, and give Odrienne a grievance that is specific, petty and hers. The harp she once bit him over is the natural target. Let it come out uneven (breaking off, repeating, a courtesy with a barb in it) rather than as a summary. Cut "There was too much truth in that for a graceful answer." and keep "Odrienne did not forgive me." Illustrative only: "You'd have let me send Mother's harp away."

### D5 · Major · Felisane as moral chorus and closing voice

- Where: L121–L129, corridor; L439–L447, breakfast; fatigue set up at L17
- Text:
> "Fewer people are frightened," she said when the door had shut. "That matters. It doesn't make this right."
> "That is why I'm refusing. Pay the people you owe. And someone besides you should hear complaints."
> "No. I have a laundress to help and would like to remain a maid instead of becoming your conscience."
> She had been on her feet since before sunrise
- Problem: Philosopher and epigram last words, plus uniform fluency under pressure. Felisane closes almost every exchange she is in, and at the two moments that frame the story she states its moral balance ("That matters. It doesn't make this right.") and its conclusion ("becoming your conscience"). Her exit also proposes an institutional fix ("someone besides you should hear complaints"), which the house style warns against as an ending default. By breakfast she has been awake a full day and night, yet her speech is as polished as at supper.
- Fix: At L125, cut "That matters. It doesn't make this right." Keep the evidence list at L121 and "I think she has stopped warning me when I stick her with pins." At L443–L447, keep the refusal and "Pay the people you owe", cut the complaints policy and the conscience line, and let her simply leave for the work she has put off since supper (this also pays off C1). Illustrative only: "No. The laundress has been waiting on me since supper." Somewhere in the mirror chamber, let her fatigue show once: a shorter, rougher instruction or a small mistake.

### D6 · Minor · "Follow after the music" points nowhere, then a tidy correction pair

- Where: L253–L261, dressing room, reading the second tap sequence
- Text:
> "Follow after the music," I said.
> "That's not a yes," Felisane said.
> "No. It's a guess."
- Problem: Ground/referent. "Follow after the music" can mean "wait until the music ends and then follow", but what happens is that everyone goes straight to the gallery while the musicians are playing. The next pair is correction-and-concession ping-pong that ends in a neat last word.
- Fix: Camet's reading should name the place her pointing indicated, so that the walk to the gallery follows from it. Keep his uncertainty, but let it show in his hesitation or in Felisane's silence rather than a matched pair of lines. Illustrative only: "The gallery. She wants us with the musicians."

### D7 · Minor · Camet set up to be corrected over the anchor

- Where: L345–L349, mirror chamber, looking for the anchor
- Text:
> "The card said a living anchor."
> "Cloth isn't living. Hold the lamp lower."
- Problem: The line is coherent but falls into the same correction rhythm: Camet quotes the card so that Felisane can answer with a neat reply. His objection would have more grounding if it came from what he saw, since her hand flew to the heart loop when the music stopped (L287).
- Fix: Base Camet's objection on that observation, and let Felisane's answer stay practical. Illustrative only: "She grabbed there when the music stopped."

### D8 · Minor · Attribution blurs in the musicians' squabble

- Where: L319–L323, gallery
- Text:
> "I can split the reed note," the player said. "Don't ask me to hold pitch while she drags the strings under it."
> "I wasn't going to."
> "You were looking at me."
- Problem: Attribution. "The player" could be any musician (a viola player was "the nearest player" at L41). Nothing tags "I wasn't going to", so it could be Felisane or the first violin, and it is unclear who "you" is. The pettiness is good, but the reader has to guess who is being petty.
- Fix: Write "the reed player" and tag or add a beat to "I wasn't going to" so the speaker is clear. Keep the bickering.

### D9 · Minor · Who is being thanked at breakfast

- Where: L455–L459, breakfast
- Text:
> He poured. She tasted it, frowned, and passed the pear preserve to me.
> "I hate this," she said. Then, after no music and no measured pause, she added, "Thank you."
- Problem: Ground/referent at the last spoken beat. "Thank you" could be for the footman, for Camet or for Felisane, and each would be a different emotional move given "Odrienne did not forgive me" at L419. "This" is split between the tea she frowned at and the preserve she passed. The gloss "after no music and no measured pause" explains the point instead of letting the reader notice it.
- Fix: Give "Thank you" an addressee through a glance or a gesture, and tie "I hate this" to one object. Trim the gloss to nothing, or to a plain beat.

## Prose findings

### P1 · Major · Narration re-explains the questions-as-commands lesson

- Where: L251, L343, L357, L419 (plus the dialogue versions in D3 and D4)
- Text:
> My wish to rescue Odrienne had been only another question with the answer supplied.
> I had done it again. Turned her action into my command, her struggle into an answer I wanted.
> Not permission. Not proof. A direction she had chosen before I understood it.
> There was too much truth in that for a graceful answer.
- Problem: Philosopher in narration. Each time the mirror and the action have already shown the point, and the narrator then states it. Counting the dialogue, the story delivers the idea about six times, which is the over-explanation style-guide.md warns against.
- Fix: Keep one narrated recognition at most. "I had done it again." alone after "Take the knife" is enough, since the violet storm shows the rest. Cut the second sentence of L251 and the "Not permission. Not proof." fragments, and cut the L419 gloss (see D4).

### P2 · Minor · Negation and fragment reversals as a tic

- Where: L33, L47, L195, L223, L357, L449, L451
- Text:
> Precision instead of humiliation.
> Perfectly timed. Perfectly reasonable.
> Color was evidence of pressure, not a vocabulary.
> No silver, no mirror-thread, no force in it at all.
- Problem: Repeated "not X, Y" reversals and fragment triplets. Taken alone each is fine, but together they make a noticeable pattern (L33's "No tears… No silk… No rivalries…" list is another).
- Fix: Sweep for variety. Keep "Odrienne winced. Not magically." (L449), which earns its place, and L33's list of concrete old tricks. Rework two or three of the rest into plain statements.

### P3 · Minor · Closing emblem restates the ending

- Where: L465, final line
- Text:
> The red stitch at her wrist held because it was only thread.
- Problem: A polished closing emblem that explains what the breakfast action already landed: preferences back, petty reach, three petitioners and a chamber pot.
- Fix: End on L461 or L463 (tea on Camet's knee while she butters her bread), or keep the stitch only as a plain image with no "because".

### P4 · Minor · Sentence missing its verb

- Where: L305, gallery
- Text:
> Then four even beats, broke the last with her knuckle, and did it again.
- Problem: Grammar: the sentence has no main verb to go with "four even beats".
- Fix: Add the verb: "Then she tapped four even beats, broke the last…"

## Continuity and prompt findings

### C1 · Minor · Felisane's laundry errand disappears overnight

- Where: L19–L27 and L113 (she will leave after the second setting); L447 and L461 (still going next morning)
- Text:
> "I'm leaving after second setting. Remember?"
> Felisane left for the laundry.
- Problem: She asks to leave, turns down "Go now", and reminds Camet she is leaving. She then stays all night without comment, and the next morning the laundress still needs her. Her choice to stay is the meaningful part, but the story never registers it.
- Fix: One beat is enough. Either Felisane decides aloud or by action not to go, or her exit line at breakfast names the delay (see D5).

### C2 · Minor · What Felisane knew about the wrist

- Where: L121 (her list of warning signs), L345–L351 (hunting for the anchor), L403 (she had told Odrienne twice)
- Text:
> "I did. Twice. You thanked me and put the cuff back on."
> Felisane crouched beside the chair and studied the gown. "The anchor isn't at the heart. That's a crossing."
- Problem: If Felisane twice removed the cuff and saw raw skin under a ribbon, the wrist should be on her corridor list of strange signs (she gives the lesser collar-pin scratch). She would also go straight to it instead of needing Camet's lamp to find the stitch.
- Fix: Either put the raw wrist into the L121 evidence list and let Felisane head for the wrist at L345, or change L403 so that she never saw under the cuff (for example, Odrienne never let her take it off).

### C3 · Minor · "Dictated" against her own pen

- Where: L423–L437, before breakfast
- Text:
> Before breakfast, Odrienne dictated three letters.
> Odrienne's pen stopped. "One."
- Problem: Dictating means someone else writes, yet the pen is Odrienne's, and she blots the page herself at L437.
- Fix: Change "dictated" to "wrote", or give the pen to a secretary and change the blotting beat.

### C4 · Minor · A lamp nobody brought

- Where: L349 and L377, mirror chamber
- Text:
> "Cloth isn't living. Hold the lamp lower."
> I did. Near Odrienne's inner wrist, beneath the cuff edge, one silver stitch disappeared through a narrow ribbon laid against her skin.
- Problem: Staging. The chamber is lit by moonlight (L327). No one has been shown carrying a lamp, yet Camet is suddenly holding one.
- Fix: Put a lamp in Camet's or Felisane's hand during the corridor walk (L271–L273) or on arrival at L327.

### C5 · Minor · Why a broken beat and not silence

- Where: L285–L311, gallery
- Text:
> Odrienne stumbled. Her hand flew to the silver loop above her heart. Then she straightened and pointed at the score.
> "Continue," she said. "The dawn service must be ready."
- Problem: The climax depends on the broken fourth beat opening a gap. A reader may still ask why silence, which stops the seventh cadence altogether, is abandoned. The stumble could read as relief or as pain, and "Continue" could be the spell or Odrienne.
- Fix: Add one concrete detail that makes silence visibly worse, such as the loop cinching or her smile locking with no beat to release it. The broken count then becomes the only way through. Keep her tapped instruction as the source of the plan.

### C6 · Minor · Water infrastructure as default texture

- Where: L63 and L183, supper and dressing room
- Text:
> I'm housing six petitioners from the canal quarter.
> Do you dislike the canal petitioners?
- Problem: Policy. "Canal quarter" is incidental water-infrastructure texture that the prompt does not call for (style-guide.md, "No default water infrastructure").
- Fix: Change both to a district that isn't hydraulic, for example a glovers' or chandlers' quarter. Nothing else depends on it.
