# Swan and Sword

The request explicitly promises a hero's mistaken expectation when he frees the swan who wants them to be together: her beauty, grace, and shyness survive the revelation that she is a muscular, battle-scarred giantess. I infer an affectionate fairy-tale romantic comedy, with pleasure in both his surprise and the unexpected combination of formidable physique and shy courtship. The prospective romance is explicit; its outcome, narrative scale, curse mechanics, and anyone's motives remain open. My chosen payoff is delighted attraction to the actual woman, carried beyond the initial joke into action and a romantic ending.

Provisional direction: Sir Roland and Halla have fallen for each other during his visits to the lake outside a dead enchanter's fortress. They have talked about her soldiering, but neither has discussed the shape of her human body; his familiar picture of a rescued maiden has supplied it. This is his untested assumption, without concealment by her or a lesson about his failure to listen. She asks him to break the curse so they can be together, and he agrees. Close third through Roland, warm and lightly comic. Both are adults.

- Begin with their last conversation as knight and swan: affectionate familiarity, a promised dance, and her practical knowledge of the dangerous fortress. He has obtained the enchanter's blade that can cut her enchanted collar. His imagined reunion includes a slender woman borrowing his cloak.
- He cuts the collar. Halla unfolds into a woman roughly nine feet tall, powerfully muscled, her arms, shoulders, and face marked by old battle scars. Roland loses his prepared gallant speech to the loveliest face he has ever seen; a scar crosses the cheek that dimples when she smiles. Beauty and her formidable body arrive together. She moves with the same exact grace and grows shy under his astonished attention. The cloak and his planned chivalrous gestures become inadequate; his attraction becomes harder to express, not weaker. Give them a substantial, compelling exchange here: he makes his continuing desire unmistakable before danger intervenes.
- Taking the blade has awakened the fortress's stone guardians, which arrive after the reunion has begun. In a brief encounter, Roland initially moves to shield her, then listens when she tells him where to stand. She retrieves her own enormous sword, long stored among the enchanter's trophies. He holds off one guardian while she defeats the others with decisive, graceful strength. Their old conversational trust becomes a fighting partnership; the woman's actual body and experience determine how they escape. This deepens admiration already expressed rather than winning his attraction. Her romantic shyness need not become hesitation in combat.
- Safe in the open lakeside clearing, she returns to the unfinished question of being together. He admits what he had pictured and asks her for the dance he promised, visibly enchanted by her as she is. She is confident enough to face stone soldiers yet blushes over taking his hand. They discover how to dance at their different heights, and she asks for a kiss. End inside their mutual pleasure, without a speech about appearances.

The added guardians and the dead enchanter are chosen adventure machinery, not prompt requirements. Their purpose is to make her revealed strength and their partnership consequential without postponing the romantic payoff behind a second quest. No further transformation or loss of her size, muscles, scars, grace, beauty, or shyness.
