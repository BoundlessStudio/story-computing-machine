# Editorial notes

## Request readings, 2026-11-14

- **Codex keeper.** The prompt explicitly links young immortals' risk-taking to physical invulnerability and older immortals' caution to the time that invulnerability can keep them trapped. The keeper inferred that the pleasure is “watching one fact make two opposite choices feel sensible.” “Rather sticky situations” suggests wryness, while “VERY long time” can carry dread and gives the story its likely scale. The expected payoff is a concrete predicament that makes an older immortal's caution feel earned. The cast, relationship, trap, duration, and possibility of escape stay open.

- **Claude.** Invulnerability, as opposed to agelessness alone, produces both the swagger and the predicament. “The joke and the horror are the same idea” because the expected end of the fall, sinking, or burial “just *continues*.” Claude hears a comic edge in “rather sticky situations” and expects a concrete ordeal, experienced or vividly conveyed, that makes the old fear legible. An illustrative personal scale and an old/young pair were suggestions. The prompt leaves the narrative register, relationship, and outcome open.

## First draft and cold readings

The writer used the first-person voice, the pop through the knee, and the warm white stone, then found the scree game, the spoon-beaked buckle and the fly. The first draft measures three scenes with roughly half the story inside the ice. The profile prompted a look at the long paragraphs and the texture of the escape.

- **Codex, independent.** The rivalry made sense once “The really maddening thing was that he wouldn't say it.” The reader kept listening with Marlis until “It took a long time to let my tongue down.” They also singled out “If I wanted to see it bigger I had to remember a different lamb.” The narration changes speed from the falling oval of snow to the wait after the last blow. Marlis recruits Delk into a joke, while “Come put your fingers in this first” asks her to attend to the place. His buckle feels lived and returns under the ice, as the stone set back in its wet seat anticipates his care. Warm hemp, the knee-pop and glacial retreat locate the story. The reader would take it with a repair to the escape. “If I could follow the rock there was room coming open beside it” was hard to place. “It took my weight” explains her decision after she has made it. “I had expected to be able to do anything as soon as I could move” felt more generic than the comic thumb beside it.
- **Claude, independent.** “It grew dark slowly, the way it does in a room when you have left a door open onto another room,” the lamb and the sound with “as little to do with a blow as the dusk had with somebody snuffing a candle” stood out. The reader tripped over the game, route and packer-yard digression, then over “Delk would have left the rope out if he knew I could see the sky.” Delk's one sustained speech and the spoon-beaked bird stayed alive. The turns into the ice and inside it felt close through the hemp, crunch, stone taste and glacial noise. At emergence the scenery felt cooler, especially “The oldest spruce below the mark the glacier had left were more than two hundred years old. I found that out later.” The reader hesitated to publish because Marlis scarcely reckons with Delk's absence after the last blow. They wanted the choice toward the water legible on first reading and wanted to keep the wait, buckle and last light the color of the rope.
- **Keeper.** An intact Marlis picking snow from her boot verifies the first half of the prompt. Her mental walks and the lamb show time becoming unmeasurable, and the cairn and spruce give its scale on release. “The high shelf was still in the light when I turned my face down to the water” makes for a credible gamble she declines herself. “I still wanted the ice” keeps that choice from becoming a loss of appetite for the climb.

- **Cold discussion.** Both readers needed Marlis to feel purchase on the rock before she gave up the climbable slit. The writer kept the upper shelf lit, made its slight give something she can judge and let her put part of her weight on rock while she can still reach it. Claude heard the chisel turning into a missing relationship at emergence. Codex already felt Delk in “the short scrape after each blow” and agreed that the ledge could take his absence further. Marlis can reckon with how long he might have come back without knowing his whereabouts. The keeper and Codex wanted concrete scale outside the ice because the first meltwater might still follow only years. Claude wanted the count to live in the scene. The writer tested physical evidence the valley can show her now.

The revised story lets the rock raise her shoulder while the shelf can still be reached, and lets her hear water dropping beyond her hand. She keeps hearing the scrape of Delk's chisel in memory. “I tried to put him there, with the light over the door and his rope loose in his hands, but I couldn't get him to go in” gives him a life outside the minute she holds in the dark. At his ledge she imagines the changing distance he would have seen. The fresh spruce section puts time on her route in the form of fifty rings at about a quarter of the radius, with younger trees growing in the old root plate. The evidence comes from ground she remembers crossing on ice.
