# Outline

## Promised experience

The prompt gives the same fact two consequences. “Nothing can hurt them” makes young immortals brash, and eventually makes older immortals cautious because they can stay stuck for “a VERY long time.” The reader should get to enjoy that bodily confidence, feel how little it buys once escape is impossible, and see what living through the interval does to someone's next decision. “Sticky situations” leaves room for a wry beginning even if the wait becomes frightening. No relationship, setting, length of confinement, or outcome is specified.

## Takes considered

- **The Rattlejaw.** A young immortal throws away the advantage of a rope to win a race across a glacier, then goes down-valley inside the ice. Play the fall, the confinement and the escape in their order, long enough for the prize she wanted to belong to a vanished world.
- **The Darning Scar.** An older immortal does the bargaining at a fair where the laws allow a debtor to be held until a particular tree bears fruit. She must bid on a young rival's time with her own while the gardeners openly discuss the blight. The auction would give other people too much of the power the glacier story lets the immortal exercise and lose.
- **Mothbank Head.** Start late, with a keeper writing directions for a light that goes dark once a century. On the night he intends to leave his post, a young immortal swims out to touch the exposed bell, and his furious account of the last swimmer gradually becomes a first-person history. The performance could be comic and eerie, but the listener would get the suspense and the confinement would reach us after the fact.

## Provisional direction

Marlis has caught up in miles with Delk, her maddeningly good climbing partner and an older immortal. She wants to make him acknowledge it. She wagers she can reach the far cairn before dusk, first across the ice. He uncoils enough rope to keep his feet on rock while she crosses the snow. She leaves it in his hands and goes straight across. She survives a fall without a mark. The glacier closes around her, and Delk's search passes out of earshot. She stays awake, able to feel the ice and unable to move it. It takes centuries for the flowing ice to carry her down to where it can melt her out.

Spend the central scene with Marlis inside. Her talent for finding the next hold finds ever smaller purchase, until it comes down to whether she can move a tooth against the tip of her tongue. When a thaw opens room to move again, she wants to rush into the first light she sees. She can see sky through the crack and has just enough room to risk it. The gap narrows, opens a little wider, then begins to narrow again. The other way is still dark, but she can feel bare grit in the melt running along her shoulder. She gives up the sight of the sky and lets the water make room to move toward the ground. She still wants to get across. From ground she can stand on, she chooses a route that costs her the daylight she has waited for.

## Craft plan

- **Teller.** Try a close third past with the momentum of a woman always mentally a step farther than her foot, and a first-person past Marlis can tell with the leisure to implicate herself. The longer the wait, the more narrowly she attends to the same inch of ice. At the first chance to move, let desire get ahead of judgment again and stay with the effort of resisting it.
- **Marlis.** She can look at a wall and see a line almost no one else can find. She wants Delk to see an equal, and a victory now would spare her having to ask. She speaks at a rush in invitations, boasts and unnecessary particulars, composing an anecdote in real time so that the other person is already a party to it. She won't take a second look while somebody is watching.
- **Delk.** He wants the pleasure of a good crossing with a partner he can keep. He once missed a whole summer a hand's width from a dropped belt buckle, caught behind a boulder on a route he thought he knew. He can tell when conditions have changed under a familiar surface and is proud enough to hate being hurried into guessing. He talks in things and distances, gives unglamorous reasons in full, and leaves a silence alone. No existing or supplied characters are used.
- **Place.** The Rattlejaw, a valley glacier with a shoulder of black rock above the tongue, gives this story moving confinement and a way out governed by the same ice. The Dryhooks, a broken limestone escarpment, could pin someone but would leave the release up to a searcher or an earthquake. A slip at Fleam's Head, a tidal headland, would offer the escape of a predictable tide. On the Rattlejaw, late-summer travelers and packers follow a ridge of dark debris that leads from the near rock flank toward a cairn on the far wall. Where the ridge bends, a snow bridge makes a tempting diagonal toward the cairn. Before dusk there is a grit crunch, the hard sweet smell of sun-warmed rope, melt ticking through a channel, slate suspended in blue ice, and a pop that comes up through the soles. A cleft opens and pinches where the ice crosses a rock step. After the intervening centuries, the old crossing is a bare stripe on the valley wall. ## Scenes and ending

1. **Late summer, toward dusk, at the debris line (25%).** Marlis wants Delk to take the wager and watch her win it. He wants her on the rope and points out where he can brace on rock while she tests the snow. She leaves without tying in and takes the diagonal across a roof of old snow. After the fall, she sees the rope lowering toward her as a blue seam closes between it and her hand.
2. **Inside the moving ice (55%, long scene).** At first she resents being unable to answer Delk. Let her invent the first thing she'll tell him as she tries to get a sound out. The remark gets funnier and more preposterous, and the last of his actual knocks comes in the middle of it. Stay for the first interval she can no longer count. At the lower tongue, give the two ways equal room. Through one, Marlis can glimpse a whole piece of sky when the ice shifts and can almost reach where she could begin the climb out. The narrowing gap makes that chance urgent. She turns her head away from it before it closes and listens to meltwater seeping around her shoulder. Stay as she waits out the thaw and inch by inch angles into the dirty hollow the water is making beside the rock. The long scene ends when she can choose where to put a whole hand.
3. **The bare flank below the old crossing, another late-summer evening (20%).** She finds the cairn by the split in the far wall, high above a long stripe of freshly exposed rock. A line across the lower tongue can still get her to the other flank before dark. She sees each move she would take and wants to take it. She puts her hand on the first of the exposed rock steps, a route long enough to put her across after dark. End on the last reflected light leaving the shortcut while she still has hours of climbing ahead. Load it with her earlier demand to win before dusk, the light she reached for inside the glacier and the first bare stone that held her weight.

Summarize the years in which nothing she can do changes her position. Leave the origin of immortality and the history of the valley outside the scenes.
