- Existing review.md said: Verdict PASS, Dialogue: PASS; I agree the dialogue is strong, but it should not pass while the L213 referent problem stands.

This story avoids most of the collection's usual dialogue problems. The two leads differ in what they notice. Cyril talks in hypotheticals, categories and self-defence ("People who knew what I could do would want it used"). Daybreak talks in rooms, head-counts, straps and conditions. Pressure changes both of them: Cyril's rehearsed fluency collapses into "I didn't do it.", and Daybreak drops to commands ("Stay there."). There is no correction ping-pong, no speech explaining the theme at the end, and the climax is an action (the looped passage) rather than an argument. The problems are local: lines that rely on the wrong antecedent, or on narration, to make sense. They cluster in the office aftermath (L207–L219), which sets up the decisive breach. The edit pass should make those few lines say plainly what the speaker means, in that speaker's register, and leave everything else alone.

## Dialogue findings

### D1 · Major · Verdict line points at the wrong people

- Where: L211–L213, office after the rescue, Daybreak's answer to "why I can't make a habit of it"
- Text:
> "You understand why I can't make a habit of it."
> "I understand why you're frightened." She rubbed a deep mark on her shoulder.
> "I don't understand leaving those people where they were. I'm glad you came."
- Problem: Ground/referent. The nearest "those people" are the eight he has just lifted out, so taken literally, "leaving those people where they were" is false: he did not leave them. Two other readings compete. One is the people queuing in his war story (L97, answered with "I left"). The other is earlier compound groups (L55). Only the counterfactual ("if I hadn't come, you'd have left them there") makes sense, and the words don't say it. This is the one line where Daybreak states her moral disagreement, and "Because I don't know what you'll do" (L233) builds on it. A reader should not have to stop and reconstruct it.
- Fix: Change only the middle sentence so that its referent and tense match what she means. The counterfactual about the eight is the most concrete choice and suits her voice. Keep "I understand why you're frightened" and "I'm glad you came" unchanged. Do not widen it into a statement about duty or heroism. Illustrative only: "I don't understand how you'd have left them in there if I hadn't come."

### D2 · Minor · "If you needed me again" is decoded only by clotted narration

- Where: L215–L219, office, just before "My name must stay out of this"
- Text:
> "If you needed me again, you would ask."
> There was no promise of reasonable hours, or reasonable things to want. Of course there wasn't.
> He had wanted her to make one without his having to hear himself ask.
- Problem: The line's meaning depends on the narration that follows. Spoken, the line can be an invitation (he wants to be needed, as at L177) or a check that she would ask rather than take. "Probably." fits either reading, which is fine. The narration then says he wanted her to promise limits, which the spoken exchange never carried, and it says so in a knotted phrase ("no promise of ... reasonable things to want"). This beat starts the promise thread that L225 ("Promise you won't tell anyone") pays off, so it needs to read cleanly.
- Fix: Keep the spoken pair as it is. Replace the first two sentences of L219 with one plain sentence about the limit he hoped she would volunteer. Keep "without his having to hear himself ask", or an equivalent, because it sets up the moment he does ask. Illustrative only: "He had hoped she would offer a limit of her own, so that he would not have to hear himself ask for one."

### D3 · Minor · Two referents propped up by position or narration

- Where: L23, start of the appointment (harness); L205–L207, office, right after the harness comes off
- Text:
> "Take that off if it hurts," he said, meaning the harness.
> She teased the strap free, breathing through her mouth.
> "I've been trying to work out how to do that for six days."
- Problem: Ground/referent. At L23 the last object named is the bloodied gloves, so "that" needs the gloss "meaning the harness" to resolve. The narration is doing the line's work. At L207, "how to do that" comes straight after she frees the strap, so for a moment it reads as six days spent trying to get the harness off. The intended referent, freeing the eight, is two scenes back.
- Fix: L23: name the object in the line and cut the gloss. Illustrative only: "Take the harness off if it hurts." L207: make "that" point at the rescue, either by putting "Thank you" before she works the strap or by naming the rescue ("how to get them out"). Keep "six days". It is compatible with "entered the compound yesterday" (L49).

### D4 · Minor · Doubled shout tag and stock heavy's lines

- Where: L179–L181, compound, the man in the coat after the captives are through
- Text:
> The man in the coat began shouting at Daybreak.
> "My employers will have this straightened out," he shouted toward Daybreak.
- Problem: Mechanics. The narration says he "began shouting at Daybreak" and the tag immediately says it again. His two lines are generic ("You have no authority to hold me here" follows). They exist to give Cyril the word "employer" for L185.
- Fix: Cut one of the two shouting statements, preferably the narration sentence, and keep the tag. Optionally, give him a line that fits his job (a researcher or administrator protesting), but keep an employer reference, because Cyril's "Somewhere no employer could fetch them" picks it up.

## Prose findings

### P1 · Minor · Word-level staging ambiguities

- Where: L27 (appointment), L61 (the ask), L143 (upper ward), L213 and L227 (office)
- Text:
> He drew the envelope toward him. She put a finger on it.
> a newspaper folded under his wastebasket
> keeping her light between its occupants and a guard
> The angry red marks remained when she lowered her hand.
- Problem:
  - L27: the only envelope named so far is the wrong one from L11 (cat photograph, sock receipt). The accounts are not given an envelope until L209.
  - L61: "under his wastebasket" is an odd place for a photograph she glances at.
  - L143: "its occupants" grammatically belongs to the bed, but it is the ward that held two people (L125).
  - L213 to L227: "a deep mark" becomes "The angry red marks". The number changes, and "angry red marks" is stock phrasing.
- Fix:
  - L27: say "the accounts", or give them their envelope at L21.
  - L61: "beside" or "in" the wastebasket.
  - L143: "the ward's occupants".
  - L227: one consistent, plain description of the strap marks.
