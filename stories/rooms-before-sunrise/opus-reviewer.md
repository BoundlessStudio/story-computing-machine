# Opus review: The Rooms the City Forgot

Independent audit for a later editing pass. It is not a `review.md` verdict,
does not change canon state, and does not authorize edits by itself.

## Verdict

- Dialogue: REVISE — one unreadable line (L155), an unattributed epigram at the beat where Mular breaks, comeback-and-rebuttal exchanges with Bordu, and the decisive fear exchange answered with a syllogism.
- Prose: REVISE — the narration explains the subtext of dialogue lines throughout; there are also minor reversal tics, a tidy closing roll call, and some muddled staging.
- Continuity and prompt: REVISE — the prompt is delivered, but the fevering man is evacuated twice and Bordu's eleven-minute debridement deadline runs out without anyone mentioning it.
- Edit scope: targeted-dialogue
- Existing review.md said: Verdict PASS; "Dialogue: PASS"; disagree — it missed a Blocking Ground line, a Blocking continuity contradiction, and several exchanges built on correction and comeback.

The dialogue is strongest when people are working. The clamp, root, feed and vent instructions in the comb-removal sequence are clear and correctly staged, they change what happens, and the pressure of the moment explains why they are clipped. It fails when people argue or react instead of work. Lirenn and Bordu trade matched one-liners and criteria as if they were equal debaters (L25–L39, L181–L193). Kevi and an unidentified speaker exchange an epigram at the moment that breaks Mular. When Mular voices the story's central fear, Lirenn answers with "It doesn't follow", and the narration then explains his echo. Eshun is given one distinguishing trait, a cosmetic habit of speaking "only with free hands". It recurs about a dozen times and makes her let go of a failing feed tube in order to talk. It also produces one line nobody can decode. The edit pass should keep the procedural spine and Eshun's plain domestic lines. It should cut or plainly ground the comebacks, let the arguments be lopsided and interrupted, and delete the glosses so the reader does the inferring.

## Dialogue findings

### D1 · Blocking · Eshun's "Use your mouth for one." cannot be decoded

- Where: L151–L157, Eshun proposes cutting through her own room and Mular refuses
- Text:
> “You have another route?” Eshun put both hands back and pumped.
> When the tube softened, she let it go for one beat. “Use your mouth for one.”
- Problem: Ground/referent. "One" has no recoverable object. The line could be a jab at Mular (use your mouth to name a route) or an instruction to someone to hold the tube by mouth for one beat; "let it go for one beat" in the same sentence invites the second reading. No one takes the line up, and Lirenn's next line moves to wall vibration, so the reader cannot check it against a reply. The setup also depends on the free-hands device (D2).
- Fix: Cut L155, including the "let it go for one beat" sentence. "You have another route?" already presses Mular, and his real objection at L161 ("There's a support rib behind it") answers it. If a second push is wanted, make it plain and let Eshun say it while pumping. Illustrative only: “Then say it. I can't hold this all night.”

### D2 · Major · Eshun's free-hands habit is cosmetic and breaks the staging

- Where: L77, L121, L145, L149, L153–L155, L165, L169, L175, L179, L247, L321, L363, L443–L445. It hurts most at L145–L153, during the air emergency on nineteen.
- Text:
> Eshun locked the tube between her knees and raised her empty hands. “Which stack?”
> “That's us.” She pinned the tube beneath her knee, took both hands away, and pointed through the curved wall.
> Without her pumping, fluid immediately darkened.
> The woman at the feed tube trapped it briefly under one knee, leaving both hands empty. “Eshun. Supplies.” Then the vessel kicked free
- Problem: Identical Voices, repaired with a quirk. The outline's "Eshun speaks only with free hands" is carried out as choreography before almost every line, but the story never motivates or registers it, so it reads as a tic. It also damages causal sense. At L149 Eshun explains that her feed pinches nineteen's air return when the chamber fills, and in the same paragraph she stops pumping to say so while fluid darkens. At L145 she has already locked the tube and raised her hands, and at L149 she does both again. The reader sees a competent woman repeatedly releasing a lifeline in a crisis, and nobody reacts.
- Fix: Cut the hands choreography from most instances. In crisis beats (L121, L145–L155, L165, L363), let her talk while pumping; strain in her voice or a short breath is enough. Keep the habit in at most two or three calm moments where it costs something, such as L321 before "Take mine first" or L443–L445 before "Salt broth". If it stays, let Lirenn notice it once in a clause. Never have her let the feed fail just to speak unless the scene registers the cost.

### D3 · Major · The opening Bordu exchange is built from comebacks

- Where: L25–L43, first radio exchange and deadline setting
- Text:
> “I can see the sea.”
> “Then you can see the light coming.”
> “Holding isn't opening.”
> “It is at this hour.”
- Problem: Two Punchline Patch pairs in the first exchange set the story's house rhythm. "I can see the sea" exists to set up Bordu's "Then you can see the light coming", and seeing the sea is not evidence of how long until dawn. "Open an emergency line" is never defined; it could be a medical standby, a legal hold or a comms channel. "Holding isn't opening." / "It is at this hour." is correction ping-pong whose meaning the reader cannot recover. Bordu's real answer, "Eleven minutes... Then I send the debridement team", does not respond to the emergency-line request, and the narration at L41 has to explain his motive.
- Fix: Let Lirenn answer the countdown plainly or not at all (she can keep working). Say concretely what she wants from the "emergency line", or cut it. Let Bordu's refusal be a plain statement of priorities that leads straight into the eleven-minute ultimatum. Keep "No third box." Illustrative only: Bordu, “I've got eighteen on hold with everything else tonight. Eleven minutes, then debridement.”

### D4 · Major · "Six floors if that tube fails" has no clear speaker and gets no uptake

- Where: L63–L71, first contact with the residents
- Text:
> A woman called from deeper in the growth, “Six floors if that tube fails.”
> The man glanced back. A partial answer, and he knew it.
- Problem: Ground/uptake and Attribution. The line reframes Lirenn's "How many?" into a precision correction of Mular's "Five". There are three women. "That tube" suggests the speaker is not Eshun, who is holding it, and the older woman is only placed "in the next alcove" later, so the speaker cannot be identified. The claim is large (six legal floors depend on a hidden tube), yet Lirenn, a municipal pruner, does not react and moves on to "Names. Work." The meaning is decoded only by the narration ("A partial answer, and he knew it"). The dependency is dramatized properly later by the air spike (L141–L149), which this line preempts. "Six floors" also sits awkwardly beside the one floor (nineteen, stack east six) that actually loses air.
- Fix: Preferably cut L67–L69 and let the air spike reveal the dependency. If the line stays, identify the speaker with a beat, make the number agree with the later event, and have Lirenn react before asking for names (a glance at the tube, a question).

### D5 · Major · The criteria argument with Bordu is a tennis match

- Where: L181–L195, Bordu after nineteen recovers
- Text:
> “Disease criterion.”
> “One criterion.”
> “And differentiated tissue accepted a relief path.”
> “At the cost of a room that shouldn't exist.”
- Problem: Tennis Match and correction ping-pong. Seven equal-weight clipped lines alternate a supervisor and a field worker as if both were arguing in court. Each line corrects the previous one's scope ("Disease criterion." / "One criterion."). Nothing about the relationship moves until the last line, which Eshun overhears. That consequence (Eshun's face going still; Lirenn turning the speaker down too late) is the real content of the exchange, and the ping-pong buries it.
- Fix: Cut to one Bordu challenge and at most one reply from Lirenn. Let Bordu run the call (what nineteen reads, what he is telling the night office) and not match her line for line. Keep "At the cost of a room that shouldn't exist.", but let him say it in passing, as a routine accounting remark rather than a riposte, and keep Eshun's reaction and the speaker being turned down.

### D6 · Major · An unattributed epigram triggers Mular's confession

- Where: L199–L207, Mular blocking the service sinus
- Text:
> “You said you knew the routes.”
> “I know when one closes.”
> Mular's prepared explanations had come apart.
- Problem: Attribution and Ground/referent at an important beat. L203 has no tag. It could be Mular reproaching Kevi, or Lirenn asking Kevi for a route around him, and the two readings mean different things. Kevi's reply is a last-word aphorism ("a route closes" standing for "our hiding is over") rather than an answer. Mular's collapse then has to be explained by narration ("prepared explanations had come apart") instead of by anything said to him.
- Fix: Tag L203 (almost certainly Mular) and make it a plain reproach. Replace "I know when one closes" with something Kevi would actually say to push him, in her blunt courier register. Cut the gloss sentence; Mular's broken speech at L207 already shows it. Illustrative only: Kevi, “She's already inside, Mular. Show her before the tube goes again.”

### D7 · Major · The decisive fear exchange is answered with a syllogism and then glossed

- Where: L305–L319, Lirenn proposes detaching the comb
- Text:
> “And if the rooms shed us when you take them?”
> “Lining, load, air. They have all three. The comb may have started them. It doesn't follow that it still has to hold them.”
> “Doesn't follow.” He repeated the uncertainty, not the hope.
- Problem: Ground/uptake, Philosopher, and uniform fluency under pressure. Mular asks what happens to them if the rooms reject them. Lirenn answers a different question, whether rejection is logically entailed, in a logician's register ("It doesn't follow that..."). The line also states the story's classification thesis before the test shows it. Mular, facing expulsion, calmly echoes her phrase, and the narration explains what the echo means. This is the story's central human stake, and the dialogue treats it as a debate.
- Fix: Keep L307–L313 ("The buds die off the feed." / "Maybe." / "They're our bindings."), which work. Make Lirenn answer the "if" Mular actually asked: admit she doesn't know, and say what she will do if it happens. Cut "It doesn't follow..." and Mular's echo with its gloss. His reaction can be physical (his hand on the comb), or Eshun's "Take mine first" can cut in. Illustrative only: “I don't know. If they do, the basket takes you out before the flush.”

### D8 · Minor · Stray comeback and correction pairs

- Where: L133–L137, Mular guarding the membrane; L231–L235, the comb discovered
- Text:
> “There isn't room.”
> “Then make it.”
> “You put lease buds on a common wick,” she said.
> “That isn't what I asked.”
- Problem: Punchline Patch and Ground/uptake. "There isn't room." exists to set up "Then make it." At L235 Lirenn objects that Mular answered the wrong question, but she asked no question; her line was a statement, and Mular reasonably heard an accusation of theft.
- Fix: Cut L135–L137 and let Mular's stance at L139 be his refusal; the air spike at L141 interrupts anyway. At L231, have Lirenn ask the question she means (why a wick, or since when), and drop "That isn't what I asked."

### D9 · Minor · A housemate calls her "the old woman"

- Where: L389, fifth root; also L71–L79, where only three of the five give names
- Text:
> “This is the old woman's. It's grown through two teeth.
- Problem: The narrator's label has leaked into Mular's mouth. He has lived with her for months and threaded her bud himself. In a story about people reduced to archived rows, two of the five residents are never named by anyone.
- Fix: Give the older woman a name and have Mular use it here (check the name against `stories/NAMES.md`). If a natural moment exists, have Eshun name the fevering man at L247. Do not introduce either name through narration.

### D10 · Minor · Small referent and unit slips

- Where: L179, L197, L329, L387, L409
- Text:
> “Then leave the rack.”
> “Bring your clamps,” she told Mular.
> “No. Last.”
> “Continue if it holds above eight.”
> “Perfusion in eighteen.”
- Problem: Ground/referent. At L179 nobody has offered to fetch the rack. At L197 Mular has never been shown with clamps, since the clamps are Lirenn's (L81, L171). At L329 it is unclear whether she means herself or Kevi. At L387, "eight" has no unit. At L409, "in eighteen" collides with "tier eighteen", which the story uses for the location.

## Prose findings

### P1 · Major · Narration explains dialogue subtext

- Where: L41, L69, L83, L123, L139, L207, L237, L265, L285, L319, L441
- Text:
> He protected totals. Six hundred sleeping tenants gave him reason to.
> The change was small and deliberate.
> Without the speech he had prepared, his words came singly.
> Conditional. It was not trust, but it was room to work.
> He nodded, once. That was all he had for her.
- Problem: This runs through the whole story. After a line lands, the narration states what it meant, what the speaker was protecting, or how the relationship shifted, leaving the reader nothing to infer. L207 and L237 say twice that Mular's prepared speech collapsed. L83 explains Bordu's pointed "occupants" swap, which is one of the best dialogue touches in the story. "Lirenn mistrusted anyone who made her anatomy wait upon a confession." (L139) is also awkward on its own terms.
- Fix: Delete most of these sentences outright; the lines around them already carry the meaning. Keep at most one or two where Lirenn's professional reading adds information the reader lacks (L123, the "please" and the failing feed, is the most defensible). Do this sweep after the D findings, since several glosses sit on lines those fixes change.

> not in a blind spasm but in time with the tower's carrying band

### P3 · Minor · The closing roll call is a tidy emblem

- Where: L447–L451, final paragraphs
- Text:
> Lirenn changed knives. Bordu called out the next pressure reading. Kevi held the vent.
> Lirenn set the blade against the dark edge and followed it toward the light.
- Problem: Each character gets one task sentence in turn, and the story closes on an image of walking toward the light. It is a polished back-to-work emblem. The outline intends the practical continuation, and that suits this story, but the symmetry and the "light" line lean toward summation. "Eshun counted three beats and caught the feed" also turns an emergency maneuver into routine.
- Fix: Trim the roll call to one or two concrete actions and end on the cut itself, without "toward the light". Keep the still-hungry fringe margin, which is a good unresolved cost.

### P4 · Minor · Hydraulic vocabulary for the tower's anatomy (policy)

- Where: L21, L25, L91, L93, L427
- Text:
> Behind his voice, the night office pumps beat their patient three-count.
> A warm return vessel pulsed toward the main night reservoir.
> squeezing night-cooled waste toward the lower reservoirs
- Problem: The prompt's living coral-and-muscle tower earns circulatory anatomy (feed, perfusion, vessels). But "pumps", "reservoir(s)" and "waste channels" read as water infrastructure used as texture, which `universe/style-guide.md` "No default water infrastructure" asks authorized revisions to avoid.
- Fix: Swap for organ-scale or non-hydraulic terms (a sac or bladder for the reservoirs; some other background sound for the night office). Leave the prompt-earned circulatory vocabulary alone.

### P5 · Minor · Staging muddles

- Where: L61, L167, L167–L179, L373–L385 with L413, L375, L379, L383
- Text:
> She took over. Only then did Eshun move.
> Lirenn helped lift the drying rack. They had no time for the shirts.
> Three instructions, three failures.
> The sleeping chamber's air gill reopened around the drying rack.
- Problem: "Another person" who lifts the clay cup at L61 is never identified. "She took over" (L167) gives the pronoun to the wrong woman. The rack and shirts are both lifted and abandoned, then the rack turns up for the vent. At the climax Kevi is assigned "the egress", yet the "vent" dragging across her shoulder turns out to be the sleeping chamber's air gill, so her position is unclear. "Three instructions, three failures" miscounts, since Kevi's line is a report. Eshun's glance at "the fevering man's empty place" (L379) has no readable reason. "jaw tight" (L383) is a stock beat.
- Fix: Assign the cup-lifter to Kevi or Eshun. Write "Kevi took over." Settle where the rack ends up. Give one term to the vent Kevi holds and place her plainly. Cut or reword "Three instructions, three failures" and the empty-place glance.

## Continuity and prompt findings

### C1 · Blocking · The fevering man is evacuated twice

- Where: L361–L363, L379 versus L431
- Text:
> “He's out,” she said, hands free for a moment. “Feed's slipping.”
> Medical hauled the fevering man into the external basket.
- Problem: A continuity contradiction. At L361 Eshun has already passed him into the hood, and the medic's line has taken his weight. L379 shows his "empty place". After classification, L431 has medical haul him into the basket again, as a current event.
- Fix: Delete the sentence at L431, or rewrite it as a consequence of the classification that does not repeat the evacuation (for example, what medical now does for the others, or where he is taken).

### C2 · Major · Bordu's debridement deadline runs out without mention

- Where: L43, L87, L253, L283, L301, L431
- Text:
> “Eleven minutes,” he said. “Then I send the debridement team.”
> “Seven minutes.”
> “Open it,” Bordu said. “Then I send medical. If the cut compromises lift, I send cutters with them.”
> The debridement crew became a fringe-pruning crew.
- Problem: Bordu starts an eleven-minute clock at about 50 minutes before intake and counts it down once ("Seven minutes"). By "Thirty-two minutes" (L301) it has expired with no consequence and no mention. At L283 the cutters still have not been sent. His central threat quietly disappears, which weakens the pressure on Lirenn through the middle of the story.
- Fix: At the air spike or the relief cut (L141–L195), give Bordu one line that extends or suspends the ultimatum, such as a legal floor losing air changing his priorities, or have him keep the debridement team at the ready from that point. Keep later references consistent with that.

- Problem: A sealed exit is later discussed as if it could be widened.
- Fix: Make it one or the other. For example, at L267 Lirenn asks about the sealed route and Kevi says why it can't be opened in time.

### C4 · Minor · Culture cups and repair documents slip

- Where: L331–L333; L237 versus L241

> The order stalled there, everybody watching the five empty culture cups.
