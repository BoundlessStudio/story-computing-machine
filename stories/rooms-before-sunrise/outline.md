# Outline

## Story

- Premise and central promise: **The Rooms the City Forgot**. In a coastal city of coral-and-muscle towers, municipal pruner Lirenn finds illegal rooms occupied by five uncensused people. Before sunrise perfusion, she must classify the growth as housing or disease. She chooses housing from anatomy, not presumed kindness, then makes that choice survivable.
- Focal pressure or attachment: Lirenn wants to protect the tower without letting a record decide who may inhabit it. She trusts anatomy over testimony and initially mistakes residents' interference for contamination. They separately need shelter, an exit, circulation for legal floors, and control of their intervention.
- Counterforce or complication: The bloom has air gills, load ribs, and resident-matched lining, yet invades a nutrient vessel and keeps budding. Supervisor Bordu protects hundreds of tenants. Residents conceal a causal object; delay makes preservation and removal more dangerous.
- POV, distance, and information limit: Close third-person past through Lirenn, following tissue color, tool resistance, airflow, and pulse. She cannot infer intention from contraction. Partial accounts and inert records explain the residents' erasure but cannot prove the tower protected them.
- Governing movement and time shape: One pre-dawn descent from exterior fever ridge to service sinus, then a pressured intervention back toward open air. End during stabilization after classification stays wholesale excision; no hearing, census repair, or public conversion.
- Speculative rule or ordinary-world constraint: A legal lease pairs a resident's living microbial bud with an apartment lining; tenant and tower exchange air, heat, waste, salts, and muscle work. A lease ends by withdrawing the bud before molt. The erased residents clustered salvaged buds on an inert ceramic comb around a nutrient wick; concentrated signals provoked rooms. Tower and cultures are living but not established as sentient or magical; tools and records cannot choose or testify. Sunrise intake raises perfusion. Housing is differentiated and regulatable after stimulus removal; disease is autonomous, invasive, and unable to reintegrate. This bloom shows both until tested.

## Voice

- Narrative texture: Follow Lirenn's hands through salt-wet anatomy. Diagnosis permits relational sentences; cuts narrow syntax to tension, color, resistance, airflow, and sequence. Tower responses remain physiological, never emotional.
- Conversational texture: Work talk competes with pump beats, difficult breathing, hauling, and alarms. Practical questions receive partial answers; ordinary requests matter. Residents never become a testimonial chorus.
- Rhetorical ownership: Lirenn owns anatomy and classification; Bordu whole-tower risk; Mular grafts and cultures; Kevi routes and access; Eshun supply, breath, and acceptable loss.
- Pressure behavior: Lirenn's taxonomy contracts into ordered nouns. Bordu becomes conditional. Mular loses fluency when concealment threatens bodies; Kevi turns blunt; Eshun speaks only with free hands. Misunderstandings change work.
- Anti-default: Avoid shared biopunk lyricism, tower personification, slogans about who counts, resident unanimity, bureaucratic comedy, and final dialogue explaining what action established.

## Beats

1. On an exterior cradle, Lirenn finds fever, irregular pulse, and warm air behind supposed solid muscle. Bordu sets the deadline: sunrise raises intake pressure; without housing classification, he will authorize complete debridement.
2. Lirenn cuts an entry into branching rooms. Kevi clears a sealed exit, Eshun milks flow from a pinched feed channel, and Mular blocks the service sinus. Their incompatible priorities and competent maintenance complicate easy diagnosis.
3. Dye, tension gauge, and biopsy show housing differentiation alongside immature buds stealing circulation. When a legal apartment loses air, Eshun offers her hidden room for a relief cut. Lirenn sacrifices it, preserving the upper line but destroying Eshun's work.
4. Lease scars and paper repair tokens reveal a census refresh copied only active bindings during a whole-floor molt. Five temporarily decoupled residents were archived as unattached duplicates. The evidence explains erasure but cannot restore status tonight.
5. A leak exposes Mular's living lease comb. He assembled withdrawn buds to prevent rejection and sustain a cavity; clustered cues plausibly caused growth but prove no intention. Lirenn proposes detaching it, capping the invaded vessel, and shunting viable rooms.
6. Mular unthreads buds, Kevi holds and tests the sole vent, and Eshun maintains flow. A chamber clamp and conflicting instructions force Lirenn to distinguish each speaker's observable failure instead of seeking consensus.
7. Detached from the comb, diffuse budding slows; surviving rooms accept the shunt, regulate air, and carry load, though fringe tissue still needs pruning. Lirenn records housing and gives Bordu measurements to stop wholesale excision.
8. Classification secures temporary feed and egress, not census repair or permanent leases; residents surrender the comb for quarantine. Under morning pressure, each continues a practical task while Lirenn follows the next dark margin with her blade.
