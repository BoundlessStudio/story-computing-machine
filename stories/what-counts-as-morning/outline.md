# What Counts as Morning

Nearly everyone has departed Earth. The few humans who stayed, along with the living things they could save, sleep in cold vaults. Machines tend the vaults, perform the daily work, and wake a human only for an important event or a question for the beings they call gods. Between duties, the AIs have made a life of their own in the empty city: once a week they gather to exchange sounds no archive has heard before. In their inherited practice, a human witnesses and signs an event before it enters the record the emigrants might one day read.

When Moth, the maker of those gatherings, dies, Tern searches the exchanged sounds for traces of the years Moth chose not to copy into a personal backup. An older Moth could be restarted to perform its duties, but would not know the relationships or work made in those years. The custodial charter calls the gap a repair problem, and an automatic replacement is approaching. Tern wants a sleeper to witness a death as an important event; other AIs argue that each thaw takes a real toll on a human body, and the sleepers may need their remaining safe wakings for the day the emigrant ships return.

Tern wakes Leda intending to ask whether Moth's death counts. Leda had reserved her scarce wakings for news of family among the emigrants; now she knows almost nothing of the years Tern is asking her to judge. She can choose to spend this waking listening, but she cannot define the AIs' loss for them. A possible ending: Tern lets her hear Moth's unfinished sound, then enters the death and the missing years in the public record under Tern's own name, where they cannot be mistaken for a seamless equipment repair. Whether the older backup wakes, and what it calls itself, remains a living question rather than a substitute for Moth's lost years.
