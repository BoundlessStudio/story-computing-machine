# Visitors Must Knock

## Promised experience

The request explicitly teleports an entire kobold clan into the modern era and makes “there are monsters in the sewer” literally true a couple of years later. An inferred pleasure is discovering what a displaced fantasy clan has made of a modern undercity: kobold ingenuity, family life, acquisitiveness, and danger encountering contemporary habits and technology. A wry adventure seems invited by the collision, but humor, sympathy, local scale, and any central relationship are choices, not requirements. No teleportation mechanism, moral, or ending is prescribed. The intended payoff is a sewer encounter whose course depends on the whole clan and its two years of adaptation.

## Provisional direction

About 3,000–4,000 words, close third through **Tikka**, an adult kobold scout and trap-maker. Eighty-seven clan members range from hatchlings to elders. Their chief, **Grandmother Vetch**, is Tikka’s grandmother. Their affectionate disagreement concerns continued concealment. Names, kinship, and conflict are inventions. I choose an unexplained crossing that nobody knows how to repeat, with no continuing spellcasting; “somehow” does not require irreversibility.

They inhabit dry disused galleries off a working sewer beneath an unnamed contemporary city. Two years of scavenging, trading aboveground, learning speech, and making a home have changed their customary tools and celebrations. Neither period is simply superior: a camera defeats darkness, while a human-shaped capture plan misunderstands a numerous clan that can climb, squeeze, cooperate, and think.

A capture crew follows an inconclusive sewer video. It makes money broadcasting public “monster hunts” and advertising exotic specimens to collectors. Its one camera crawler is visibly connected by cable to the surface van and public stream; its monitor shows viewers' comments. The clan defeats the captors by its own actions before making a deliberate public appearance. Witnesses complicate its future; they do not grant the victory.

Possible scenes and turns:

- Open two years after arrival. A phone lowered through a grate catches Tikka's claw as she pushes it away; above, someone says there are monsters in the sewer. The shaky clip becomes a disputed curiosity, showing neither a recognizable kobold nor the household.
- The clan is preparing its second arrival-anniversary feast. Bought food joins old recipes beneath the communal banner. A grocery hoist made from a bicycle crank and net lowers supper into the hall. Families rehearse their traditional dragon procession: its new head incorporates a motorcycle helmet, its long body patched sleeping bags. A repurposed doorbell camera watches the entrance. These are lived possessions before any becomes useful in a fight; brief arrival memories sharpen their changed uses and value.
- The crew arrives with the crawler, capture poles, and cages. Its lamp discovers hiding places; its reach defeats an old pit trap. Separate cages and talk of individual sale threaten the community. Scouts report both usable entrances watched. Vetch's evacuation would scatter households; the clan chooses to defend the home it has made.
- Tikka proposes using an empty dragon costume as bait over the pit. The cook points out that the crawler can inspect it from safety. Vetch changes the plan: use the living procession, whose households already know its movements, to conceal their numbers and carry the grocery net. First the moving quarry draws the captors forward and transports the ambush into position, things an empty costume cannot do. Only after the crew commits does part of the procession smother the crawler's lens, separately controlling what the public sees. Several households work the hoist while others spring from the body and side galleries. Their customary coordination, new materials, and small bodies defeat the crew. The procession remains a family tradition in action, with its familiar rhythms, squabbles, and pleasures. Cut any gadget that lacks an established communal use; keep the struggle legible and non-graphic.
- Once the captors are secured, the covered camera still streams darkness. Tikka could disconnect it. Vetch chooses instead to uncover and turn it toward the complete household and its home, then the cages and captors. Previously the public had a blur, glimpses, and a noisy blackout; now eighty-seven kobolds deliberately stand together in view. Their speech and noisy internal disagreements make this an assertion of presence, not a plea to be judged harmless.
- Next evening, the clan has moved its defenses and posted: **MONSTERS IN SEWER. VISITORS MUST KNOCK.** A cautious knock comes. Tikka checks the doorbell camera, teeth bright with anticipation, while the household debates whether to open. Their home remains strange, defended, and theirs; their future here is open.

The prose may discover a better route through these choices. Protect the clan’s agency, the meaningful two-year transformation, the literal sewer-monster encounter, and the comic bite of the final invitation.
