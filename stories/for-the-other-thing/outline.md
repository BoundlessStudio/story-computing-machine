# For the Other Thing

## Promised experience

A bride says she can't marry him, more than seven hundred guests are outside, and her companion proposes “some hard liquor and a pep talk.” The later direction specifies “just at their home (home weeding) between mother and daughter” and asks for fresh material after the earlier attempt. The wedding is at their home and these two make the consequential choices. I hear affectionate comedy in the jump from so many waiting people to something the mother can fetch from her own cupboard. The reader gets to stay with them for the answer to “Tell them to go home!” and feel what it costs once all those people are here. The bride's reason and the outcome are open.

## Takes considered

- **The pep talk for calling it off.** The bride asks for all seven hundred to be sent home. Her mother starts talking her through getting married and finds she has to work just as hard to talk her through stopping it. The scene to stay for is the bride asking her mother for help saying something her mother will hate to hear.
- **The witnesses.** The daughter wants to get married in the presence of a couple of people she can bear to look at. The mother has spent months asking everyone she knows to come. The scene to stay for is the mother being asked to stand as a witness after agreeing to send everybody else away.
- **What she told them.** Begin at dusk with the daughter asking what her mother said to make everybody leave. In their porch, they recover their exchange and find they have been protecting each other with two incompatible versions of it. The reckoning comes after the choice.

The first take keeps the live choice with the women and lets the rye and pep talk change what they do while everyone waits.

## Provisional direction

Renna and her mother Nita Corliss are used to being a good team with a houseful. Renna catches what needs doing before Nita asks, and Nita thinks she can tell when her daughter wants something badly enough to insist on it. Both signed off on inviting whole households from two large local families. Nita arrives to fetch the bride from the porch where they fitted the dress. This is the one place Renna can ask Nita for help before Nita's promise to walk her out comes due.

Nita reaches for the rye she intended to share with Renna after the wedding and starts with advice for getting to the end of the aisle. Renna asks for the pep talk for the other thing. In a short account she tells Nita that when she was writing her vows she kept changing her promises into thanks for what Callum had already done. He noticed her distress and offered to postpone or say the vows alone with witnesses. Picturing the two of them without an audience made the promise harder to say. The thing that relieved her was imagining telling him she couldn't make it. She told him she was fine.

Nita offers to tell everyone that Renna is feeling sick, a kindness Renna wants badly enough to consider. They share the rye. Nita uses the start of her original pep talk again. She makes room on the low bench where they fitted the dress, gets Renna to set her feet down and lift her eyes, and tells her to say one sentence to her mother with the waiting people left exactly where they are. “If you need a mother who looks disappointed, I can do that, and it won't require a bit of acting.” This time Nita gives Renna a chance to finish without supplying an answer. Renna tells her she is calling off the wedding and asks her to send the guests home, using her name. She also asks her mother to stay with her while they go. Nita sits down beside her, reads the excuse about Renna feeling sick that she had started on her phone, and erases it. She drafts the news as Renna's decision and asks the emcee in her own name to send everybody home.

Renna sends a brief text to give Callum word first. Nita has just left him in the field surrounded by family, the very thing that makes a private conversation now impossible. Renna asks him to come to the front door later so they can talk in person. Nita lets the emcee wait until Renna sees that Callum has read the private news. Then she sends the message about the guests. The story stays on the porch with the two women as seven hundred people start to leave.

## Craft plan

**Teller.** Trial Renna in close third past, her busy eye measuring exactly how much trouble the smallest answer will make, and Nita in first person past, wry and inclined to enumerate her good intentions until up against one that went awry.

**Renna.** Adult daughter who uses objects quickly and can usually guess Nita's requests. Her everyday talk is literal and willing to finish a sentence with the uncomfortable detail. Panic makes her stammer. She thinks Nita can relieve her of saying what she'd like her own mother to hear. She knows what Callum offered her and Nita has heard only her assurances.

**Nita.** Warm, proud, inventive about getting people fed and moving. She bids for time with conditional sentences, practical patter and extra options. She knows the head count, who is standing with Callum and who is waiting for her text. She wrongly takes Renna's assurances for consent to the marriage. Under the jokes she needs to be useful, and saying a thing faithfully turns out to be a way she can help. There are no supplied images or established characters.

**Place.** The Corlisses' enclosed back porch on Hennick Road, late June near five. Boots dry here and garden vegetables come in through the screen door. A chipped lino seam snags the train. Rye and the warmed rubber boot tray hang in the damp air. The screen door scrapes on its spring and the house can hear chairs moving on the mown field. Renna lifts the cold, damp edge of her hem off the sill. The door is due to open onto the head of the aisle. Nita reserved the house for the two of them after dressing and drew an old curtain. The guests can see the screen door move from their seats, and the women can see down the rows through the end window. The inside kitchen loses that direct pressure. The front bedroom strands Nita in preparations and away from her own promise to lead her daughter out.

**Conversation owed.** Can Nita help Renna stop the wedding, and can she carry the decision Renna makes without changing it for her? This happens on the bench where Nita expected to help with the dress.

## Scene briefs

1. **The door, minutes to five (20%).** Nita has given the emcee instructions to wait for her text to cue the procession. She wants Renna to take her arm and come. Renna wants her to take “Tell them to go home!” at face value. The quoted exchange comes at the spring of the screen door. Renna gets Nita to stop opening it.
2. **The long scene, on the porch (65%).** Renna wants this stopped and wants her mother beside her when it happens. Nita wants the wedding to go ahead and then, when that option goes, wants to spare Renna the full embarrassment with a decent excuse. The rye, the vow account, and a dance tune ending midway through their talk change who can be funny about what. Nita uses her coaching to make a space for Renna's own words and makes fun of how hard it will be for her to hear them. Renna tells Nita her decision first and asks her to give the actual words to the guests. The short notice to Callum follows their agreement.
3. **The word gets out, same porch (15%).** Nita uses the same channel that was to start the ceremony to send the people home. She stays on the bench as agreed. The change from everybody waiting on their one door to the shuffle and traffic as the field empties gives the announcement its size. Mother and daughter have time for the second sip in the very place Nita expected to toast the ceremony. The dress fitting, the waiting glass and Renna asking Nita to stay load that image. The proposal, the public announcement and the groom's reaction stay off the page.
