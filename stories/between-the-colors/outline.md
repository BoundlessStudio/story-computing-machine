# Outline

## Story

- Premise and central promise: **Between the Colors.** A woman whose green eye sees twenty minutes ahead and whose blue eye sees twenty minutes behind enters her family's little theatre and finds it already burning in one eye. Getting people out cannot prevent every consequence; evidence of her brother's conduct arrives only after she has acted on his account.
- Focal pressure or attachment: Iseth loves the brother who made childhood navigation into a private game and taught her to perform voices for shadow plays. She has moved out but still answers Berian's requests. Losing their struggling theatre matters; learning that his familiar directions can carry a lie matters more.
- Counterforce or complication: Berian has diverted earnings owed to Tesset, the theatre's adult assistant. A fire begins while he is alone with the accounts; its precise accidental or deliberate origin need not be established. He protects his money and his account of events even after another person's danger is apparent. Tesset has remained upstairs to collect her unpaid wages. Smoke, unfamiliar obstructions, and Iseth's visual displacement make every physical intervention uncertain.
- POV, distance, and information limit: First-person past through Iseth. Distinguish the eye and temporal offset whenever visual evidence matters. Present sound, heat, smell, contact, and proprioception remain available. Other minds remain inaccessible. Green images are facts; their meanings are Iseth's inferences.
- Governing movement and time shape: Approximately forty minutes around one fire, with brief childhood recollections. Start inside the theatre; expand the initial exchange, two separate rescue decisions, and the delayed visual discovery outside. The final consequence concerns a particular sibling relationship and truthful testimony. No institutional conversion or cure.
- Speculative rule or ordinary-world constraint: Each open eye shows the view along its present gaze twenty minutes before or after, including ordinary occlusion. Images move without displaced sound; closing an eye removes its image. No selectable dates, mind-reading, or present sight. Observed futures stay accurate: rescued occupants can coexist with a ruined theatre. No universal fate explanation. Cane, touch, familiar routes, and live voices aid movement without eliminating obstacles. Injuries arrive through sensation before sight.
- Dialogue promise: A sibling's childhood reliability makes a present lie persuasive. The assistant's interrupted calls contest his account before Iseth can inspect the relevant visual evidence.
- Dialogic medium: Present speech and calls, remembered navigation games, ordinary taps on surfaces, and silent past gestures seen through the blue eye. Neither temporal image supplies historical or future speech.
- Dialogue engine: Berian's descriptions decide which person Iseth helps first. Tesset's continuing voice makes her return. After the rescue, delayed sight makes Iseth distinguish what her brother actually did from his revised explanation; her final words give an arriving fire-watchman an accountable observation.

## Voice

- Narrative texture: Bodily, visually double, concrete. Let temporal images interrupt attention without making every paragraph a diagram. Childhood memory carries affection through practical games and theatre scraps. Close physical action narrows to touch, sound, weight, and incomplete decisions.
- Conversational texture: Familiar sibling shorthand initially; Berian fills uncertainty with plausible details. Tesset asks for specific help and corrects inaccurate descriptions. Ordinary requests, breaths, and incomplete replies have room alongside consequential statements.
- Rhetorical ownership: Iseth distinguishes observation from interpretation but makes an intimate exception for her brother. Berian owns performance, reassurance, face-saving explanations, and attempts at humor. Tesset owns her wages, bodily limits, and refusal to accept his version. No speaker delivers a theory of temporal ethics.
- Pressure behavior: Berian talks faster and revises particulars. Iseth stops arguing to locate sounds; later her questions narrow. Tesset repeats necessary facts as smoke defeats fluency.
- Relationship movement: Iseth wants the childhood brother to remain dependable. Berian wants her help and endorsement without disclosing his conduct. Tesset wants rescue and her work acknowledged. Saving both people does not settle whom Iseth will believe or protect afterward.
- Anti-default: Avoid a prophetic super-rescue, an argument proving everyone must trust one another, or a warm reunion that cancels the delayed discovery.

## Beats

1. Iseth arrives to help with the theatre's closure. Her future view reveals the coming fire; the past view contains an apparently ordinary room. A small navigation error immediately establishes the practical danger of lacking present sight. Berian reports a recently discovered accidental fire and insists the upstairs rooms are empty. His familiar taps carry her toward salvage and the exit.
2. Tesset calls from upstairs. Berian goes toward her while Iseth raises the alarm and searches for a usable way through the present obstructions. Establish the window, landing, and outside landing place before the rescue. A lost box and money become audible without revealing the complete interaction upstairs.
3. Berian returns injured and gives a reassuring account of Tesset's escape. Iseth helps him out. The assistant's live voice contradicts him. He substitutes an account in which he could not reach her. Iseth goes back; her decision needs neither a changed green image nor certainty of success.
4. Iseth reaches Tesset by voice and touch, learns what is trapping her, and helps her through the accessible window toward outside assistance. Berian eventually participates from below. Both survive; Iseth suffers a burned hand. The theatre still becomes the blackened structure seen at the opening.
5. Outside, Berian attempts to convert their joint rescue into a shared account that omits his earlier actions. Tesset is exhausted and being treated. Iseth initially believes the most forgiving explanation consistent with what she heard.
6. Twenty minutes after the upstairs encounter, the blue eye shows it through the same window from the street: Berian reached Tesset, took the money she was holding, and left her reaching after him. Respect that old window's sightline; show only what could be seen through it. Present questions expose the discrepancy. Iseth gives the watchman her exact observation, even as Berian asks her to preserve their old loyalty. End with the rupture enacted, keeping visual past and physical present distinct.
