# Opus review: Between the Colors

Independent audit for a later editing pass. It is not a `review.md` verdict,
does not change canon state, and does not authorize edits by itself.

## Verdict

- Dialogue: REVISE — nearly every line is grounded, but the decisive reveal hinges on a setup ("Now?") that only the narration can decode, and Berian talks in his sister's clipped register.
- Prose: PASS — concrete, bodily, well paced; only a small wording inconsistency in the replay.
- Continuity and prompt: PASS — the future/past/no-present premise is delivered and resolved; two small staging slips.
- Edit scope: targeted-dialogue
- Existing review.md said: Verdict PASS; Dialogue: PASS; I partly disagree. Ground holds, but the decisive beat and Berian's voice need targeted work.

This is one of the stronger dialogue tracks in the collection. Almost every line passes the Ground check. Berian's lies are legible and pile up against each other ("Only me", "I didn't know she'd come back", "She's out", "I couldn't reach her", "I couldn't get near her"). Tesset speaks in her own register, fragments about her body and her money. Iseth's clipped precision is earned by a narrator who always has to say how she knows what she knows. The problems are local. At the reveal, Berian asks a one-word question that a brother who "learned the distinction before he could read" would not ask, and the narration has to vouch for its meaning before Iseth delivers the premise as a neat last word. Berian is also written as an extravagant storyteller who "could make a whole stage out of a kitchen table", yet he makes his case in the same flat three-sentence declaratives as his sister, so his appeals in the street have little pull on her. Aim for the smallest fix to the decisive beat and a more performative, over-explaining Berian in the street. Leave Iseth, Tesset and the rest of the dialogue essentially as they are.

## Edit guardrails

- Prompt: the green eye sees twenty minutes ahead, the blue eye twenty minutes behind, and Iseth never sees the present. Images carry no sound (L303). Keep first-person past through Iseth, broadly accessible, moderate intensity, non-graphic injuries (burned palm, twisted ankle, trapped foot). Length band 2,500–4,000 words; the story is about 3,000 now, so small additions are safe.
- Reference-image detail already in place: "the white lock escaping from my red braid" (L233). Keep the eye colours matched to their functions.
- Timeline the edit must not break: arrival at T+0, roof falls at T+20 (L11, L231–L233). The window struggle happens while Iseth is back inside (L95–L97), and the blue eye replays it after the watchman arrives (L301). Street dialogue added before L301 is fine. Do not let Iseth learn what happened at the window before the replay.
- Knowledge chain: Iseth learns about the wages and "come back today" only from Tesset (L199–L203), and learns "He knocked it" (L179) without acting on it. Before the replay she chooses the forgiving reading (L279). Keep that order.
- Keep it ambiguous whether Tesset was taking her wages or saving the box. Do not settle it in narration.
- Keep "I've already sent for the watch" / "Who?" (L29, L67–L69) unanswered. Leave it as one more of Berian's legible, unconfirmed claims.
- No universe facts are at stake (non-canon, unnamed setting, a living faculty with no conflict in `universe/rules.md`). There is no water infrastructure now; do not add any.
- Iseth's short, exact speech is her voice and the story's spine. When you loosen Berian's lines, do not loosen hers.

## Dialogue findings

### D1 · Major · Decisive reveal leans on a glossed "Now?"

- Where: L329–L337, outside, Berian's hand on Iseth's sleeve just after "You reached her"
- Text:
> “What are you looking at?”
> “The window.”
> “Now?”
> He knew what he had asked. His grip loosened before I answered.
> “Twenty minutes ago.”
- Problem: Ground/knowledge plus Punchline Patch at the decisive exchange. Berian has known since childhood that Iseth never sees "now", so read literally the question is either redundant or impossible. The reader cannot tell whether he means "you're watching it right now?", "the window as it is now?", or "which time?" until the narration steps in with "He knew what he had asked". That sentence tells the reader what the question meant instead of letting the line carry it. The exchange then closes on a crisp restatement of the premise. Setup and delivery read as authored together, so the story's most important four lines feel built to land "Twenty minutes ago" rather than spoken by a frightened man.
- Fix: Give Berian the question he would really ask, knowing her eyes: he needs to know whether she is looking back or forward. Delete "He knew what he had asked." and keep "His grip loosened before I answered." so his body shows the realization. "Twenty minutes ago." can stay as her answer if it answers his real question. Illustrative only: replace “Now?” with “Which eye?”. A second option: cut Berian's third line entirely and let his loosening grip come before she volunteers the time.

### D2 · Major · Berian speaks in Iseth's register; the performer never appears

- Where: L273–L289, sitting in the street before the watchman arrives; the pattern starts at L155 and recurs at L351
- Text:
> “The rail was down. There wasn't room. I couldn't get near her.”
> “There were things to settle. I thought she'd done and gone. I was downstairs.”
> “There was smoke. I couldn't reach her. I called to her to use the window.”
> I remembered the way he could make a whole stage out of a kitchen table if a listener would let him.
- Problem: Identical Voices / house-voice convergence in a major exchange. The narration builds Berian as extravagant, a showman who promised "a kingdom, six elephants, the moon" and who talks fluently to the watchman (L299). On the page, though, every one of his defences to Iseth is three short flat sentences, the same shape as her accusation at L345 ("You took the box," / "The rail fell. You saw her fall."). Cover the tags on L273–L289 and you tell the speakers apart only by who is accusing and who is defending, not by how they talk. This is his one calm chance to win her back, and it exerts little pull, so her later testimony costs her less than it should. Terse is right at L155 (smoke, injury) and in the broken-off L351. It is not right in the street.
- Fix: Rewrite L277 and L289 (and optionally one line of the watchman account summarized at L299) so Berian sounds like the man the narration describes. When he lies he should run long, pile on plausible particulars, appeal to their history, reassure, and maybe try a small joke. Iseth's short questions should cut across that. Do not give him aphorisms or a speech about loyalty. Keep "I went back for her. You know I went back.", "Mother would have hated that." and "My sister knows me." as they are. Illustrative only (for L289): “There were things to settle. Her last week, the key, a cloak she wanted cheap. She went up to fold the good costumes, and when it went quiet I thought she'd let herself out the back, the way she always does. I was downstairs the whole time, Iseth, with your puppets.”

### D3 · Minor · "it" points to the wrong arm

- Where: L133, on the stairs after Berian falls
- Text:
> “Let go of my arm. Put it over my shoulders.”
- Problem: Ground/referent. Grammatically "it" is Iseth's arm, the only arm just named. The situation makes her meaning recoverable, but the line trips the reader in the middle of the action.
- Fix: Name his arm. Illustrative only: “Let go of my arm. Put yours over my shoulders.”

## Prose findings

### P1 · Minor · "old" used for past-time Berian

- Where: L309 and L313, the blue-eye replay of the window
- Text:
> I watched his old hands take hold of the little moneybox she carried.
> The old Berian looked toward the floor.
- Problem: Elsewhere the story marks past-time figures with "earlier" ("The earlier Iseth", L233; "his earlier face", L317). "His old hands" reads first as aged hands, which blurs the key image of the reveal.
- Fix: Use "earlier" (or "the Berian in the window") in both places for consistency.

## Continuity and prompt findings

### C1 · Minor · Two small staging slips

- Where: L109 (Berian coming down the stairs with the box) and L213 (upstairs, after Tesset is freed)
- Text:
> He was coughing, dragging something against the wall.
> I began to stand and hit my head on the rail again.
- Problem: At L109 the only thing Berian carries is "the little moneybox" (L309), which nobody drags, so the reader pictures some larger object that never appears. At L213 "again" refers to a head strike that never happened; Iseth only pressed her shoulder under the rail (L209).
- Fix: At L109 say what is scraping, e.g. his shoulder or the box against the wall as he feels his way down. At L213 drop "again", or make it plain that she hit the rail itself again.

## Recurring patterns

- Three-short-sentence speech units: L29, L155, L277, L289, L345, L351. They suit Iseth and the smoke-filled moments; they flatten Berian in the street (D2). Vary his, and leave hers.
- Narration decoding a line: L335 ("He knew what he had asked."). This is the only real instance; it is covered by D1.
- Rapid one-line volleys: L49–L61, L145–L155, L249–L259, L329–L337. The first three are earned: one speaker dominates, there are interruptions, and a beat or a silence closes each. Only the last needs work (D1).
- Past-time wording: "old" at L309 and L313 against "earlier" at L233 and L317 (P1).

## Preserve

- The opening: walking into a door the blue eye showed open while watching a flame in the green (L11–L13), and "Neither eye showed his hand on me." (L21).
- Berian's instant, practical "How much of it?" (L23), which shows his lifelong trust in her sight.
- The evasion at L57–L61: "Did you pay her?", then the tap moving farther off, then "I'm trying to save your puppets."
- Iseth catching the slip: "I thought she'd gone." / "You said she finished on Monday." / "She did. I didn't know she'd come back." (L77–L81).
- "I haven't pulled anything." and the laugh that turns into coughing (L127–L129). This is the one correction beat that earns its place: it is sibling affection under pain.
- "Can you see?" / "No." and the reflection that follows it (L189–L193).
- All of Tesset's lines, especially "He said come back today. He said I'd get all of it." and "I can't buy another pair." / "All right. Cut it."
- "Tap. Tap." and the ladder directions (L223–L225). This payoff of the childhood signal must stay warm.
- The bandaging woman misreading Iseth's reach (L239).
- "Give Tesset what you owe her." / "I will." / "Now." followed by his silence (L253–L259).
- "I went back for her," he said. "You know I went back." / "I heard you." (L273–L275). Her answer is exact, and it is a real answer.
- The spare-moon memory (L271). Leave it implicit; do not explain it.
- "You reached her," as the opening of the reveal (L319).
- The testimony and the ending: "He guided me down. After we got her free." / "Before that," I said, "he left her upstairs. I saw him leave." / "If I stayed here, in twenty minutes I could see the face he was making." / "Take him somewhere else," (L361–L371). Do not add a closing reflection.

## Suggested edit order

1. D1: fix the decisive beat first. It is the smallest edit with the largest payoff, and it sets the pitch for the lines around it.
2. D2: rework Berian's street defences (L277, L289, and optionally one watchman line) so his voice is his own before the reveal lands.
3. D3: fix the referent on the stairs.
4. C1: fix the two staging slips.
5. P1: change "old" to "earlier" in the replay.
