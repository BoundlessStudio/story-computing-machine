# The Last Beat on the Block

Kemi, twenty, wears Twenty-Third School black and red; Orli, twenty-one, wears Seventh School white and blue. Kemi's bare-bones **ring** can hold one moving thing for three breaths, but cannot send it elsewhere. Orli's bare-bones **angle** can turn a moving thing where it crosses her marked corner, but cannot stop it. The public, practiced spells of their schools do more, because a living caster's opening motion receives a closing reply from a charged mark painted on the block. Their rivalry has kept each from admitting she likes what the other can do.

The theft happens in front of them. While Kemi casts the market's awning-hold, Saye peels its glowing reply off the wall onto a strip; the awning drops. Kemi can still catch one falling spar with her small ring, but not the whole awning or Saye fleeing beneath it. Orli arrives in pursuit of the corner-turn stolen from her own block. She can turn Saye's bike but cannot halt it; her brother and the other skaters have already closed their blind-bend run because its protective turn no longer answers. Their separate failures reveal both the method and its limit: Saye must cut a reply while somebody is actively calling it. They team up to intercept his next theft, rather than merely inspect an aftermath.

They race through a drum-step block where a tailor's quick mending fails mid-seam and a painted-stair block where an elder's small following light goes out. Each place has its own use, people, dress, and beat; their interrupted casts give the pair an opening to interfere and a clue to the rooftop where Saye is winding the stolen replies into a reel. Saye painted parts of both schools' home walls and watched his own signature get covered as the schools competed for credit. He wants a midnight one-name spectacle: his living pulse can make the captured replies flare as beautiful echoes, though without their home casters' openings they do not do the useful work. He offers Kemi the awning mark immediately; her mother is still holding the damaged market stall beneath the roof. Taking that bargain would let him finish and keep the other blocks' spells.

Kemi and Orli get onto the roof to seize the reel intact. Their modest ring and angle techniques work because those do not depend on the missing public replies; earlier failures taught them exactly what each can and cannot save. Saye threatens to burn the reel rather than hand it over and, in the struggle, falls toward the street as the strip catches fire. Kemi has one ring and chooses the falling person. Orli can turn the burning strip at a painted corner but cannot reach every coil before it goes. They stop Saye tonight and save some marks, but many old replies are lost. The final movement returns to the actual blocks: a recovered reply can be fixed back only when a living neighbor calls to it, while a lost one must be made again over time. Kemi and Orli face the people whose work they could not preserve and use their smaller personal techniques to help with what still needs doing. No instant citywide reset, and no claim that Saye's hurt made his theft harmless.
