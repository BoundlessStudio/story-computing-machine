# The Last Beat on the Block

Kemi, twenty, wears her black-and-red Twenty-Third jersey over a school of **rings**: with a completed hand-circle she can hold one moving thing for a few breaths, but cannot change where it goes. Orli, twenty-one, wears Seventh School white and blue; her heel-drawn **angles** turn a moving thing as it crosses a marked corner, but cannot arrest it. They know each other as tournament rivals, not partners. Tonight Kemi reaches for the familiar ring that steadies the night market's awning and finds its painted home mark scraped blank. The vendor beneath it has to hold the frame up herself. Across the city, Orli finds a corner spell gone from the crossing where her younger brother normally guides skaters around a blind bend.

Someone is moving from block to block, peeling the charged closing marks out of public murals as each neighborhood uses them. The caster supplies the living magic; the marks hold a practiced reply that lets others learn and complete a local working. A stolen mark leaves a school able to remember its motions but unable to finish its familiar spell. Kemi and Orli follow the fresh absences through a drum-step block where a tailor's quick mending fails, and a painted-stair block where an elder's small light no longer follows her home. Each place has its own ordinary use for its magic, and the theft costs somebody more than a showpiece.

The thief is a living performer gathering the marks on a turning strip for a midnight spectacle of every neighborhood's spells under one name. Kemi and Orli reach the rooftop where the strip is being threaded through a spell-fed reel. He offers their own schools' marks back if they leave him the rest. Instead, the two use their incompatible limits in sequence: Kemi catches the moving strip for the few breaths Orli needs to lay a turning path, then releases it so Orli can send the marks back along the physical trails left by their removal. They have to risk losing their own marks with all the others, and the thief can still interfere. The ending should make the return visible in particular people's hands, and leave the two mages with a changed, earned relationship rather than a speech about the city.
