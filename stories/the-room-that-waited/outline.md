# Outline

## Story

- Premise and central promise: Generations after a non-nuclear collapse, Kilta and her thirteen-year-old daughter Zumi find a steel hatch while seeking a winter grain cache. The reader recognizes a nuclear launch complex through surviving evidence; neither explorer recovers that concept. Resolve what they do with dangerous uncertainty, without activation or full explanation.
- Focal pressure or attachment: Kilta needs a useful find and Zumi's obedience, yet promised Zumi first judgment on anything she discovered. Zumi wants her noticing treated as competence. Kilta teaches caution while forcing unknown things into confident names.
- Counterforce or complication: Falling lamp oil, stale air, repeating corridors, dead equipment, and a tiny inherited sign lexicon keep interpretation provisional. Zumi's play slows Kilta but catches relationships her categories erase. The facility offers no corrective voice.
- POV, distance, and information limit: Close second-person present through Kilta, the “you” who never realizes what she found. Stay inside her senses and concepts. Render inspected words, numerals, diagrams, and objects exactly enough for reader inference, but never provide technical narration or a pre-collapse viewpoint. Zumi may also be wrong.
- Governing movement and time shape: One late-afternoon descent and return, paced by oil. Each chamber invites a name that the next detail defeats. Reader recognition peaks in the control room; the human outcome comes at the hatch when they must guide later finders. Stop before public disclosure.
- Speculative rule or ordinary-world constraint: Ordinary archaeology: the hardened complex preserves geometry, print, paired controls, and warnings, not context. Power and communications are dead; nothing responds or establishes weapon operability. Their culture retains counting and chalk route marks but no missiles, nation-states, or nuclear war.
- Dialogue promise: A prickly parent-adolescent survey where corrections, teasing, embarrassment, and poor guesses carry affection. Immediate risk and naming remain easier to discuss than trust.
- Dialogic medium: Speech, hand and lamp signals, chalk marks, hazard pictograms, printed language sounded without comprehension, and a dead handset. Signs constrain choices without becoming a conscious message.
- Dialogue engine: Kilta's route sense and Zumi's pattern memory are both necessary. Each interpretation changes touch, direction, and authority; their entrance-mark dispute determines whether others read shelter, danger, or unresolved risk.

## Voice

- Narrative texture: Tactile second-person present, moving by lamp reach, breath, metal temperature, and acts of naming. Set recognizable evidence down without a narrator's wink.
- Conversational texture: Parent-child overlap: reminders, small mockery, half-answers, repeated objections, and busy or embarrassed silence. Allow single-purpose talk.
- Rhetorical ownership: Kilta owns terse categories, bodily analogy, dry affection, closing jokes, and social authority; demonstrations make her listen. Zumi owns counting, speculative comparisons, mischievous errors, revision, and suspicion of pretended certainty.
- Pressure behavior: Falling oil turns Kilta's explanations into commands and narrows listening. Zumi repeats, then drops humor for pointing, arranging, or refusal. Fear produces missed answers, not eloquence.
- Relationship movement: Kilta wants trust but cannot ask forgiveness for dismissal. Zumi wants adult standing but cannot request an admission of ignorance. Exchanges shift route authority and risk until both judgments remain visible.
- Anti-default: Avoid fluent experts decoding technology, debating nuclear ethics, averting launch, or extracting a clean lesson. Let reader recognition exceed consequential, sometimes silly character meanings.

## Beats

1. In a dry wash, Zumi finds cold air around a buried hatch while Kilta surveys for grain storage. Kilta tries to reclaim judgment despite her promise. Zumi makes the first chalk route mark; they set an oil limit.
2. Beyond an oversized door, duplicated hooks, chairs, and fittings make Zumi think the place expected pairs; Kilta calls them spares. Teasing establishes affection and Kilta's power to end inquiry by naming.
3. A yellow trefoil, protective garments, numbered doors, and washing diagrams defeat Kilta's cellar theory. Zumi discards one guess and remembers repeated EXIT forms. Kilta follows one while hiding her reliance.
4. Zumi play-acts a listener with a dead handset until silence embarrasses her. Kilta tries it after dismissing the game. Both had hoped for explanation; a wall diagram gives Zumi a return pattern, not history.
5. The control chamber supplies decisive reader evidence: target map, separated key stations, protected switches, status columns, and fragments including LAUNCH and MISSILE. Kilta calls it weather-counting. Zumi sees only a room built for two people acting together.
6. Kilta proposes testing the paired controls; Zumi refuses. Keep everything dead, with no countdown. Their argument exposes Kilta breaking her caution rule. She abandons the test and lets Zumi choose return turns from the diagram.
7. At the hatch, Kilta starts the safe-shelter mark; Zumi adds the incompatible danger stroke. Kilta leaves both and returns her chalk. End before they name the place or tell anyone.
