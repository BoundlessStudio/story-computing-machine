# Opus review: The Room That Waited

Independent audit for a later editing pass. It is not a `review.md` verdict,
does not change canon state, and does not authorize edits by itself.

## Verdict

- Dialogue: REVISE — one line that doesn't parse ("That is inherited from your father"), a volley of lines written only to set up Zumi's last word, and a decisive refusal played as an even-handed precedent debate.
- Prose: REVISE — the prose is strong, but the ending explains what the crossed chalk marks already show.
- Continuity and prompt: REVISE — the prompt is fully delivered, but the walk out passes the rooms in an order the walk in makes impossible.
- Edit scope: targeted-dialogue
- Existing review.md said: Verdict PASS; "Dialogue: PASS", Internal continuity PASS; disagree on both counts.

This is one of the better dialogue stories in the collection: Kilta names
things and Zumi challenges the names, with real play ("Rattle-ation", the dead
handset, "Don't lick the walls"), dramatic irony whenever Kilta says
"weather", and an admission Kilta has resisted all story. The failures cluster
where an exchange turns into a volley of clipped rebuttals scored for the last
word. The rules banter at the hatch ends on a line that doesn't parse, and the
chalk exchange exists for Zumi's topper. At the red cover, a tearful
thirteen-year-old wins every point of a debate over precedent in clean
sentences. The edit pass should fix the Ground failure, cut the patch lines,
let fear change the shape of the decisive exchange, and leave the playful
exchanges alone.

## Edit guardrails

- Prompt: second-person present, "you" = Kilta, who must never realize what the
  facility is. The reader recognises it only through surviving evidence
  (RADIATION, the trefoil, the suits, MISSILE, LAUNCH, TWO-PERSON, the target
  map, the empty key clips). No narration may supply the concept. Broadly
  accessible rating. Length band 2,500–4,000 words (currently about 3,050), so
  cuts are safe.
- Nothing activates or responds; the facility stays dead and details stay
  non-instructional (outline boundary; universe `premise.md` LOCKED deep-time
  world allows later cultures to misread old machines).
- Kilta is Zumi's mother and Zumi is thirteen (outline, review.md); the prose
  states the age but not yet the relationship. Kilta's name reaches the reader
  only through Zumi's handset play (L199); keep that.
- Motifs that other beats depend on: the "first judgment" promise (L29), which
  pays off at L323 and L341; pairs/spares (L93–L99), which pays off at
  L265 and L363–L365; chalk hooks and the two dots (L155), which pay off at
  L353–L355; the oil scratches; the glass mill (L305–L311), which pays off
  at L383.
- Do not flatten Zumi's play or Kilta's dry humour. The target is fewer scored
  volleys, not less wit.

## Dialogue findings

### D1 · Blocking · "That is inherited from your father" does not parse

- Where: L65–L77, setting the turn-back rules at the hatch
- Text:
> "Then tell me why quickly."
> "Because you enjoy a speech while falling down a hole."
> "That is inherited from your father."
- Problem: Ground/referent and uptake. Kilta asks for a quick reason. Zumi
  starts her answer with "Because", so it reads as the reason she would give
  for "up", not as the intended jab that Kilta would want an explanation even
  mid-fall. Kilta's reply then says "That" came from Zumi's father. The nearest
  referent is Kilta's own love of speeches, which Kilta cannot have inherited
  from Zumi's father. If "That" means Zumi's backchat, it points at the wrong
  line. This is also the only hint in the prose that Kilta is Zumi's mother, so
  the reader can't use the relationship to decode the joke. The narration then
  reports the joke landing ("Her smile arrives despite herself"), which tells
  the reader they missed something.
- Fix: Keep Zumi's reciprocal "If I say up?", L69, and Kilta's condition. Make
  Zumi's jab plainly about Kilta's habit of explaining. Make Kilta's comeback
  plainly about Zumi's mouth, so the father line parses and establishes the
  mother–daughter bond. Illustrative only: Zumi: "You'd want a reason on the
  way down a hole." Kilta: "You get that mouth from your father." Optional: the
  reciprocal "up" rule is never used again. At the red cover (D3), Zumi could
  invoke it ("Up.") before or instead of "No."

### D2 · Major · Chalk volley exists for Zumi's topper

- Where: L107–L115, first crossing, Kilta asks for smaller marks
- Text:
> "Neither are lost people."
> "Lost people use less chalk."
> "They do after."
- Problem: Punchline Patch / Ground. "Neither are lost people" is clear:
  losing someone costs more than chalk. Kilta's "Lost people use less chalk"
  has no plain paraphrase that serves her argument. Either it concedes Zumi's
  point or it's a joke with nothing to hit. It exists to set up "They do after",
  and the reader has to guess what "after" means (after getting lost? after
  dying?). The narration then scores the exchange for Zumi ("You have no answer
  that will not reward her"), so it reads as a contest the author arranged, not
  an argument about chalk.
- Fix: Cut "Lost people use less chalk." and "They do after." Go straight from
  "Neither are lost people." to the narration beat, adjusting it if needed. If
  Kilta must respond, use an action (she lets the thick mark stand), not
  another quip.

### D3 · Major · Decisive refusal plays as a precedent debate

- Where: L297–L323, control room, Zumi's palm over the red cover
- Text:
> "I said that about spring traps."
> "You said it after the glass mill."
> "Which had springs."
> "The hatch was a door."
> "After you opened it."
- Problem: Tennis Match, uniform fluency under pressure, and a Punchline
  Patch. This is the story's most frightening moment for Zumi: her mouth is
  pinched and her eyes are "wet, either from dust or fury" (L321). Yet it runs
  as eleven evenly alternating lines. First they argue over which trap Kilta's
  rule was about, then they trade precedents about the hatch. Zumi wins every
  volley in complete, well-formed sentences. "The hatch was a door." exists
  mainly to set up "After you opened it." The one concrete hit from shared
  history, the glass mill and the apprentice's fingertip, is what actually
  lands (L311 says so). The debate around it dilutes it and makes the
  relationship's crisis sound like two lawyers.
- Fix: Keep Zumi's "No.", her throwing Kilta's paired-catches rule back at
  her, the glass mill and apprentice, "The hatch hadn't.", and "You don't get
  first judgment on my hands." Cut the "spring traps" / "Which had springs"
  hair-splitting, naming the glass mill in Zumi's own line if needed. Cut "The
  hatch was a door." / "After you opened it." so that "The hatch hadn't." gets
  no ready answer from Kilta. Let pressure change the shape: Kilta talks more
  than Zumi, and Zumi repeats "No" or the apprentice instead of winning a new
  point.

### D4 · Minor · Epigram closers and a tagged correction

- Where: L55–L57 (hatch seal breaks), L83–L89 (great door), L161–L171 (cups room)
- Text:
> "Air," you correct.
> "Same door, depending where you stand."
> "That isn't a length."
> "It is when you have cleaned up after people."
- Problem: Epigram/last-word lines and correction ping-pong. Each exchange
  closes on a neat saying rather than an answer. "It is when you have cleaned
  up after people" is hard to paraphrase: it seems to mean that someone who has
  tidied up after others knows cup clips mean a long stay. The tag "you
  correct" names the correction the line already makes. Each case is small, but
  together they make Kilta's topper the default way an exchange ends.
- Fix: End one or both of the door and cups exchanges with a plain answer, a
  shrug, or no answer. L173 ("She rolls her eyes, but she studies the cups")
  already carries the cups beat. Change "you correct" to "you say" or drop the
  tag. Keep "Don't lick the walls." and "So are earwigs.": those are Kilta's
  earned jokes, and each one changes Zumi's mood.

## Prose findings

### P1 · Major · Ending explains the crossed marks

- Where: L385–L387, at the hatch, shelter mark struck through
- Text:
> Any other finder would curse the contradiction. They would stop. They would call someone over. They would argue before lifting the ring.
- Problem: Philosopher in narration / over-explained ending. The crossed
  shelter and danger marks already echo the TWO-PERSON stations: whoever finds
  them next has to stop and confer. These four sentences spell that out in a
  "They would… They would…" run, so the story's last beat states the point the
  image just made.
- Fix: Cut all four sentences, or keep at most one sentence about what the
  next finder will see, not what they will do or feel. "You look at the two
  meanings crossed on the dark metal." followed by "You leave both marks."
  carries it.

### P2 · Minor · Narration glosses the beat right after it lands

- Where: L241 (after the silent handset), L311 (glass mill), L331 (Kilta's admission), L345 (the way out)
- Text:
> That kindness is worse than teasing.
> The example lands badly in the sealed room.
> The words feel larger than they ought to, six common stones that will not stack neatly.
> That is not the whole thing she asked. It is enough for her to take.
- Problem: Each sentence tells the reader how the line or silence before it
  landed, at the moments where the scene has earned the reader's own
  inference. The second-person voice can comment, and L101 ("That ends it
  because you put the ending into your voice") is good character work worth
  keeping. But these four are stacked on the most loaded beats. At L331 the
  gloss also miscounts: "I don't know what this room does" is seven words, not
  six.
- Fix: Cut two or three of these. The silent handset (L239–L241) and the
  admission (L331) work without commentary. If the L331 image stays, fix the
  count.

### P3 · Minor · Water-infrastructure texture

- Where: L137 (suit-room pictograms); L255 (Kilta's weather guess)
- Text:
> Arrows herd the water toward a drain in the floor.
> "Storm counting. Flood paths."
- Problem: This is a policy nit under the style guide's "No default water
  infrastructure" entry. The prompt didn't ask for a floor drain; it is
  incidental texture. "Flood paths" is a natural-hazard guess, not
  infrastructure, so it is borderline and changing it is optional.
- Fix: End the pictogram at the figure raising its arms under falling water,
  cutting the drain sentence. Optional: Illustrative only: "Storm counting.
  Hail tracks."

### P4 · Minor · Mixed apostrophe styles

- Where: L237 onward (L237, L309–L331, L353, L359)
- Text:
> "We didn't know until it cut the mill apprentice."
- Problem: Up to L169 the story uses curly apostrophes (isn’t, doesn’t). From
  L237 on it uses straight ones (Zumi's, didn't, anybody's, hadn't, don't,
  we're, That's).
- Fix: Use curly apostrophes throughout.

## Continuity and prompt findings

### C1 · Blocking · Return route reverses the inbound room order

- Where: L349–L359, Zumi leads the way out from the control room
- Text:
> At the first crossing she chooses left, though your body insists on right.
> "That's the washing room. I put dots there."
> She takes you past 19, 18, 17.
- Problem: Continuity/staging. On the way in, the order is: great door and
  benches, first crossing (chalk), left passage, radiation signs, washing room
  (hook and two dots), doors 17, 18, 19, then EXIT closet with the map, then
  the centre (L101–L245). On the way out, Zumi reaches the washing room first
  and only afterwards passes 19, 18, 17 on the way to the benches. Retracing
  should pass 19, 18, 17 before the washing room. Three more slips add to the
  confusion. The EXIT that "points into darkness" ahead (L349) is unexplained;
  the only EXIT sign seen was past 19. The dotted mark is now "on a pipe behind
  you" (L355), but Zumi hooked it "around the door" (L155). Kilta's "Our marks
  are the other way" then contradicts marks that turn out to be right there.
  This is the payoff for Zumi's map memory and Kilta's backward first turn
  (L225), so the reader has to be able to follow it.
- Fix: Reorder the walk out so it retraces the walk in: centre, map/EXIT
  closet, 19, 18, 17, washing room, signs, first crossing, benches, great
  door. Put Kilta's wrong instinct and "Our marks are the other way" where the
  routes truly diverge. The first crossing works, since L225 says it is the
  turn Kilta carried backward. Keep "That's the washing room. I put dots there."
  and put the dotted mark where L155 put it. Cut the EXIT sign "ahead", or tie
  it to the one already seen.

### C2 · Minor · Small staging slips

- Where: L211 (EXIT closet), L321 (control room), L359 (great door)
- Text:
> She turns; her shadow climbs your chest and face.
> You can lift her by the waist, as you did at the crack
> At the great door, your first hooked line shines on the wall.
- Problem: (1) Kilta carries the lamp (L101, L129), so Zumi's shadow can't
  climb Kilta's face unless the lamp has been put down behind Zumi. The story
  never shows that. (2) At the crack Kilta pulled her back by the belt (L37);
  she didn't lift her by the waist. (3) Zumi drew the great-door mark (L101).
  "Your first hooked line" gives it to Kilta, which blurs Zumi's ownership of
  the route marks that the ending depends on.
- Fix: (1) Show the lamp set down, or change the shadow image. (2) Make L321
  and L37 match. (3) Change it to "her first hooked line".
