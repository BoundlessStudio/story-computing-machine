## Premise and promise

**One-sentence premise:** Desperate to buy a living heartseed for his dying
twelve-year-old sister, first-time Runner Cal Mercer crosses through a hidden
Queen Street portal into Deepmarket, refuses a bargain that would save Lena by
removing him from his family's memories, and steals the seed—only to become its
first blood host and the dangerous source of Lena's temporary reprieve.

**Central dramatic question:** Can Cal help Lena without treating his own
erasure, her memories, or anyone else's personhood as acceptable currency?

**Story promise:** Deliver the portal-wound, impossible sky of doors,
Deepmarket wonders, predatory child-shaped dealer, costly bargain, kinetic
heartseed theft, and double-heartbeat ending of the supplied premise while
making every cost and crossing causal. The emotional resolution comes before
the pursuit hook: Lena receives a meaningful but limited benefit, Cal chooses
continued accountable care over clean martyrdom, and Tuck and Voss cease to be
merely paid or procedural witnesses.

## Story controls

- **POV:** First person, tightly limited to Cal Mercer. Cal may report what he
  sees, remembers, is told, or reasonably infers; he cannot settle the Realms'
  ultimate ontology.
- **Tense:** Past tense throughout.
- **Target length:** 3,000–4,000 words; aim for approximately 3,600.
- **Tone and genre:** Street-level speculative fantasy with dry gallows humor,
  tactile wonder, predatory bargains, sibling tenderness, and a hard-won note
  of hope beneath the final threat.
- **Audience:** Teen. Brief non-graphic blood, a cut hand, constricting roots,
  bruising, and unsettling body imagery are allowed. No explicit gore, sexual
  content, or luxuriating in a child-shaped body's injury.
- **Scale:** One sibling rescue and one local crossing system. Do not explain a
  multiverse, declare literal present-day Earth, connect the portal to the
  Glass Gate, or turn Deepmarket's rules into universal cosmology.
- **Ending contract:** “Better” is the earned result, not “cured.” Resolve the
  bargain choice, the theft's cost, Lena's consent and bounded stabilization,
  and the three principal relationship changes before the dealer-pursuit and
  hungry-heart hook.

## Character engine

### Protagonist

**Cal Mercer** is an underfunded first-time Runner and Lena's older brother.

- **Concrete desire:** Return with a heartseed soon enough to keep Lena alive
  and give her a path beyond the transplant waitlist.
- **Deadline pressure:** Lena's heart is deteriorating now; the hospital's
  careful language has made waiting feel indistinguishable from surrender.
- **External stakes:** Failure may cost Lena the time needed to reach a donor;
  a failed crossing may strand or kill Cal; theft may bring the dealer through
  the same local doorway.
- **Relational stakes:** The dealer can weaponize the very bond Cal wants to
  protect. Cal also risks making a life-changing choice for Lena without her
  voice in it.
- **Blind spot:** Cal equates “I would pay anything” with pure love. He imagines
  self-erasure as a clean heroic sacrifice and treats his continuing place in
  other people's lives as selfish property rather than a relationship they
  also have a right to keep.
- **Internal tension:** He once felt a flash of relief at the prospect of the
  medical ordeal ending. Shame makes him overcorrect toward limitless
  sacrifice.
- **Movement:** He moves from absolute rescue fantasy to relational
  responsibility: no cure justifies stealing Lena's memories or rewriting her
  attachments, and loving her means remaining present for uncertain,
  consent-based care. His theft remains reckless, but the final choice is to
  own its consequences rather than romanticize disappearance.

### Key cast

- **Lena Mercer / Lena / Cal-Cal:** Lena is twelve, ill but not passive. Before
  entry, a short saved voice message gives Cal's goal a living person rather
  than a diagnosis and establishes “Cal-Cal” as her affectionate alias for
  him. After the monitored transfusion, Lena is alert enough to insist that he
  ask her before attempting another miracle. Her assent matters alongside
  their mother's medical consent.
- **Tuck:** The experienced guide conceals that Cal's cash pays only his fee,
  not the heartseed. His shame-door admission reveals that he once abandoned a
  friend for the friend's boots. During the theft he gets a clean chance to
  take his own token and leave, but returns for Cal instead, turning the old
  confession into a paid-off moral choice. He remains abrasive and culpable;
  one good act does not make him secretly noble.
- **M. Voss / Voss:** A licensed Exit Witness whose precision initially sounds
  like cynicism. She explains the portal and token rules, responds to the
  return as a professional rather than a mystic, arranges quarantine and
  monitored care, and stays when the open token creates a threat. Her arc is
  from registrar who tells Cal he is effectively alone to an accountable ally.
- **The unnamed dealer:** A living, child-shaped extension of the shop's root
  organism, not an ordinary human child and not evidence about all Realms
  beings. The dealer offers technically bounded but morally predatory terms,
  then uses the shop's living defenses. Keep `dealer`, `child`, and similar
  references lowercase descriptions rather than character names.
- **Priya:** A person from Cal's past whose first-kiss memory is taken as the
  gate toll. Cal retains the semantic fact that Priya existed and that the
  event mattered, but loses its sensory and emotional episode; he does not
  somehow narrate details after they have been extracted.
- **GutSaint72:** An old Runner-forum username attached to the salt advice.
  Cal cannot verify the poster's identity, fate, or authority.
- **Unnamed gatekeeper and unnamed family/medical staff:** Keep these as
  lowercase descriptive roles. They do not receive titles used as names.

## Plot-critical operational rules

These rules must appear economically through action or dialogue. They are
local mechanisms, not omniscient explanations of magic everywhere.

### Queen Street crossing and exit tokens

1. The portal wound is operated by a living seam organism in the tunnel wall;
   the strange rain is one visible sign of its metabolism. The metal disk is
   nonliving storage. Voss, as a living licensed witness, prompts the organism
   to mark a disk with the entrant's declared name, entry seam, and active
   return window.
2. Tuck already has his own current token. Show it briefly so his return never
   depends on Cal's disk.
3. A token remembers a route, not a rightful owner. If a Runner loses it before
   an exit opens, the Runner cannot deliberately return through that seam and
   “becomes local.” If the token opens the route and is then separated from the
   Runner, momentum can carry the Runner across, but the unstamped token retains
   one usable follow-trail for whoever holds it.
4. The dealer's living shop has a defensive ejection reflex: when a protected
   heartseed is stolen, its roots throw the thief and anyone they are gripping
   toward the most recently opened return route. This does not grant free
   travel to arbitrary doors.
5. During that ejection, Cal's disk opens the Queen Street route. A dealer-root
   tears it from his wrist after the threshold has formed but before it crosses
   and receives Voss's closure stamp. Cal completes the fall into the tunnel;
   the dealer keeps one active follow-trail. This sequence causes, rather than
   merely decorates, the final knock.

### Deepmarket costs, memory, and names

1. Deepmarket's living substrate senses expressed desire and uses it to weight
   prices. “The Realms run on want” is Tuck's imprecise Runner shorthand for
   this market, not a law of the universe or a reliable statement about every
   place beyond every door.
2. The smoke gatekeeper states the entry terms before contact: one emotionally
   weighted autobiographical memory, selected by the gatekeeper rather than the
   entrant. Cal knowingly crosses the collection line. The extraction removes
   the lived episode of his first kiss with Priya but leaves sparse semantic
   knowledge that there was a girl, an event, and a loss.
3. The entrance organism locally encodes Cal's declared name in his route
   token. Once he enters the dealer's shame-gated living shop, its roots can
   read that local mark. The dealer's knowledge of “Cal Mercer” is therefore
   not universal name omniscience.
4. The shame door opens only when its living root network registers a sincere
   admission. It is a local access test, not proof of universal truth magic.
5. The dealer's offered price is a relational-memory and attention binding,
   channeled through Cal's freely accepted contract to the named people linked
   to him. It would strip Lena and their mother of lived memories of Cal and
   bias them to rationalize the evidence he left behind. It would not alter
   history, birth records, hospital records, or photographs. Those traces
   would remain recoverable and painful. The dealer's description of Cal's
   room becoming storage is a predicted consequence; the room is not
   retroactively rewritten. Lena and their mother cannot consent to this
   bargain, which is part of why Cal finally refuses it.

### Heartseed and salt

1. A heartseed is a living symbiote kept dormant in a nonliving jar. The
   dealer's root network has tuned this seed toward Lena's damaged cardiac
   pattern using Cal's hospital bill, photograph, spoken desire, and living
   relational link; that is why its label depicts Lena.
2. Tuning determines what damage the seed's output tries to repair. It does
   not determine its host. When the jar breaks, the seed roots through the
   first fresh blood it contacts. Cal's knife-cut palm is that contact.
3. Once rooted, the organism cannot simply be transplanted without likely
   killing host and seed. It produces a limited surplus of repair-bearing
   blood factors tuned to Lena. A carefully screened transfusion can stabilize
   her heart and improve her numbers; it cannot restore all accumulated damage
   or guarantee a cure.
4. Donation drains Cal, and the seed responds to depletion with heat, a second
   rhythm, and hunger. Repeat transfusions require Lena's assent, guardian
   consent, medical monitoring, and limits protecting both siblings. Do not
   establish an exact long-term prognosis in this story.
5. Ordinary salt physically disrupts the dealer avatar and exposed shop roots
   by drawing moisture from their living conductive tissue. It is not a
   universal anti-magic substance. GutSaint72's old post gives Cal a plausible
   reason to try it without giving him certainty.

## Causal arc

### Beginning state

Cal has money for a guide, a cheap license, a hospital bill, and the belief
that willingness to lose everything makes him qualified to decide what Lena's
life is worth. Voss sees his inexperience; Tuck sees a paying client. Lena's
voice message makes clear that Cal is not an interchangeable sacrifice in her
story.

### Inciting disruption

Voss pairs Cal to a local route token, warns him that the disk is a transferable
route key, and lets him choose whether to cross. Cal enters despite learning
that the crossing itself is alive and indifferent to his reason.

### Escalating turns

1. The entry toll takes Cal's lived memory of Priya, proving that “anything”
   consists of specific pieces of a person rather than an abstract heroic sum.
2. Tuck reveals that cash bought guidance only. The market prices the emotional
   weight of desire, so Cal cannot solve the problem with the resource he
   brought.
3. Cal and Tuck open the dealer's shop with sincere shame. Cal's admission—his
   momentary relief at the thought that Lena's ordeal might end—exposes the
   guilt driving his martyr fantasy.
4. The dealer offers first twenty good years and then Cal's place in Lena's
   remembered life. Cal nearly accepts because the bargain resembles the clean
   sacrifice he rehearsed.

### Crisis and meaningful choice

Cal understands that the offer would not simply cost him. It would invade
Lena's and their mother's minds, force false explanations around surviving
evidence, and deny Lena the right to value her brother. He refuses. When the
dealer asks why he came if he will not pay, Cal chooses an imperfect, culpable
course—steal the heartseed and remain answerable for the consequences—over a
contract that makes violated relationships look noble.

### Climax

The living shop attacks. Salt buys seconds; Tuck breaks a decoy jar; Cal follows
the pictorial label to Lena's seed. A root gives Tuck a clean path toward his
own exit, but he returns to free Cal, reversing the choice confessed at the
shame door. Cal catches his knife by the blade, cuts himself, severs the root,
and takes the jar. The shop's ejection reflex hurls both men toward their marked
route. Cal's token opens the Queen Street threshold and is torn away after it
does so. The jar shatters on tunnel concrete, and the living seed encounters
Cal's cut palm first. It roots in him.

### Resonant aftermath

At the linked quarantine clinic, Cal learns that Lena and their mother agreed
to one monitored transfusion after receiving an honest account of the unknowns.
Lena's numbers are better, not normal. In a brief call she uses “Cal-Cal,”
demands a voice in whatever happens next, and confirms that the relationship
the dealer priced is alive and reciprocal. Cal agrees to monitoring and to no
repeat donation without shared consent; he chooses to be a brother who stays,
not a martyr who decides for her. Tuck returns the unearned portion of his fee
and commits to helping Voss close the route. Voss chooses to remain responsible
for the breach rather than merely log it. Only after those resolutions does
Voss discover that Cal's disk never crossed for closure. Three knocks, the
dealer's child voice, and the heartseed's hungry answering beat provide the
sequel hook without substituting for an ending.

## Proposed inventions for later canon-delta disposition

Every item below is a proposal, not established canon. The final editor must
record retained items in `06-canon-delta.md` and keep them scoped as stated.

1. **Queen Street seam organism:** A living portal organism below Queen Street
   produces black rain and can be prompted by a licensed living witness to mark
   local crossing routes.
2. **Exit Witness practice:** Local licensed witnesses register entries,
   maintain timed route windows, stamp returned tokens closed, and have access
   to a quarantine clinic. The wider institution and jurisdiction remain
   unknown.
3. **Exit tokens:** Nonliving metal disks store a living seam's declared-name
   and route mark. They are route-bound rather than owner-bound, normally
   support one return, and can retain one follow-trail if separated after route
   opening but before closure.
4. **Deepmarket location:** A market-city stands on a vast sleeping living
   being somewhere reachable by the Queen Street seam. Whether this is another
   region, era, constructed enclosure, or something else remains unknown.
5. **Deepmarket substrate:** The local living market senses expressed desire
   and weights prices; Runner claims about all “Realms” are fallible shorthand.
6. **Memory gate:** A living smoke gatekeeper charges one emotionally weighted
   episodic memory under stated terms while leaving sparse semantic knowledge
   and historical evidence intact.
7. **Shame-gated shop and dealer:** A living root shop opens on sincere shame,
   manifests a child-shaped dealer avatar, reads locally marked route names,
   and can defensively eject thieves toward their most recently opened route.
8. **Relational-memory contract:** The shop can, through an accepted bargain,
   strip linked people's memories of the payer and bias attention around
   surviving evidence, but cannot rewrite history or independent records.
9. **Local salt vulnerability:** Ordinary salt disrupts the exposed wet tissue
   of this dealer/root organism; no broader anti-magic property is proposed.
10. **Heartseeds:** Living symbiotes can be tuned toward a named patient's
    damage, root in the first fresh-blood host after release, and generate a
    bounded transfusible repair effect. Rooting, donation, depletion, hunger,
    and removal all carry serious unknown risks; no universal cure is implied.

## Failure modes to watch

- **Token contradiction:** Do not let Cal lose the disk before it opens the
  route, imply Tuck has no token, or let the dealer follow through an already
  closed token. Preserve the order: route opens, disk is torn away, Cal crosses,
  disk remains unstamped and usable once.
- **Unseeded heartseed behavior:** Show Cal's cut before the jar breaks and make
  first-blood rooting explicit. The Lena tuning controls repair output, not host
  selection.
- **Miracle cure:** Lena improves enough to buy time; she is not restored to
  perfect health, guaranteed adulthood, or removed from ordinary medical care.
- **Lena as an object:** Give her a voice before entry and meaningful assent
  afterward. The dealer's proposed invasion of her memory must be recognized
  as harm to her, not merely Cal's loss.
- **Heroic-erasure framing:** Cal may feel the selfish pull to remain known,
  but his decisive reason for refusal includes other people's agency and the
  value of reciprocal relationship. Do not imply that accepting the dealer's
  terms would have been morally pure.
- **Memory inconsistency:** After the toll, Cal cannot recall Priya's taste,
  face, or the kiss itself. He can know only sparse facts and feel the shape of
  absence.
- **Universalizing local rules:** Avoid narrator claims that every Realm, all
  names, all memories, all bargains, all salt, or all magic work this way.
  Tuck's slogans are fallible field shorthand.
- **Nonliving magical agency:** Doors, tokens, jars, records, maps, or artifacts
  cannot independently choose or cast. Attribute active effects to the seam,
  market, gatekeeper, shop, seed, or another living being.
- **Accidental world declaration:** Do not call the destinations parallel
  universes or assert that Queen Street is literal baseline Earth. Local
  contemporary details are enough.
- **Weak Tuck payoff:** Tuck must have a real chance to save himself before he
  turns back; otherwise his shame admission does not cause a meaningful change.
- **Premature hook:** Do not introduce the knock until Lena's bounded outcome,
  Cal's ongoing-care choice, and Tuck's and Voss's changed commitments are all
  on the page.
- **Name drift:** Use only the forms in the Name check. Do not invent Voss's
  first name, shorten GutSaint72, call Cal `Mercer`, or draft before the
  coordinator clears the two cross-story collisions and records a successful
  scoped check.
- **Lore tour or sequel sprawl:** Market wonders should be vivid but brief and
  should sharpen price, want, or danger. Do not add factions, portal history,
  dealer backstory, a broader chase, or a second rescue objective within this
  word budget.
