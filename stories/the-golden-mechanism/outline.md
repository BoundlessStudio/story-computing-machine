# Promised experience

An immense golden mechanism already turns in the divine capital, through fields the gods cannot account for. Its worship, wars, and competing religions have lasted “for eternity” while its purpose remains unknown. A young goddess mocked for having “no domain, no worshippers, and no meaningful power” publicly decides to try activating it “simply because no one has ever dared to try.” The pantheon threatens destruction out of fear of what it cannot understand. I read this as awe curdling into dread, with the pleasure of watching her get a hand on the very thing the gods keep at a distance. The later directions tie the increased intensity to their threat and explicitly require her death as the ending, brutal, messy, and on the page. I expect her attempt and the pantheon's response to drive that ending. The relationships, means of death, and discovery itself are open.

## Takes considered

- **The feast.** Follow the young goddess through a public ceremony in which the gods come within reach of the gold. Let one audience witness the declaration, the first attempt, and the killing. The confrontation on the approach to the mechanism puts her appetite to see it work in direct reach of divine force.
- **The killer's account.** The god who kills her gives a first-person account of the afternoon. Its heart is the moment he passes from trying to make the machine obey him to laying his power on the young goddess. His struggle over what to call the act would compete with her immediate discoveries.
- **The truce.** Begin in a resumed war for the capital's center. The young goddess walks between rival forces to try the one thing both sides forbid. Watching a feud end in unanimous violence gives the threat military scale, with the risk that the war's outcome claims the attention due her encounter with the mechanism.

Choose the feast. The same stone carries generations of devotion, her bodily approach to the unknown, and the gods' execution. She can see what her decision has brought into reach while everyone who threatened her has to see it too.

## Provisional direction

Two processions meet at Vessale's feast. Orsen presides over the worshippers who believe the machine holds the sky up. His rival celebrant leads those who believe it winds the sky in for destruction. Orsen takes the weight off the far end of the Near Step to hold it over the central drop. In the blessing he presses the worshippers gently to their knees, a customary assurance that he governs what falls. Edri, a young goddess the procession treats as an amusing extra, interrupts to say she intends to set the mechanism going because no one has ever dared try. They laugh until she walks toward the narrow gap where the metal comes within reach.

The rival celebrant breaks ranks with her own procession to stand with Orsen. He speaks the threat the pantheon backs. Step back now and live. Touch the machine and be destroyed. He wants Edri to take the offer. She wants him to tell her if anybody knows what effect her hand might have, then stay to see with her. His best argument is that the whole capital is under the spheres. He finally tells her how close he came to touching them before turning away in fear. Edri, scared and very much wanting to live, steps past the worn kneeling stones anyway. She puts her palm on the engraved band as it passes and gives it a push to see what it will do.

The concentric shells turn out of their old procession. Their fields spread above the roofs and open a view onto an upright black sea with lights moving in its depth. Salt rain comes through and falls toward that sea. Orsen makes a downward pull. The crowd's knees hit stone while the rain goes its own way. He lets up. The rival celebrant begins her hymn again and the people keep looking at the sea. She asks Orsen to give them their kneeling weight back. He does. Knees come down and faces stay turned up. Other gods steady worshippers at the sides of the wet Step, leaving the middle clear all the way back to the city.

Edri has come back from the gold to the kneeling stones. She beckons with the hand that touched it, wanting Orsen to see the lights. He keeps his eyes down and puts his power on her. He has her pinned, with the way back to the city open, and has time to lift his weight. She still tries to raise her face to the sea. He increases the weight until the body under his control breaks. The machine has stayed open through every choice he makes.

## Craft plan

**Teller and trial.** Stay with Edri through the killing. Try close third past, open to the bustle and mockery of the feast, against first-person present, impatient with the old ceremony and alive to what has changed overhead.

**Cast and speech.** Edri has watched the bands go past the rail for seasons. The senior gods call her a goddess in the city's terms and have given her no domain or standing, and she has no worshippers or meaningful power of her own. She rushes into concrete comparisons and doubles back when she cares how her question lands. She tends to assume anybody who's looked as long as Orsen must be just as eager to see what happens. Orsen likes having people safe under something he holds up and expects those same people to obey him. He talks in sweeping, hospitable periods and thinks the threat will persuade Edri to step back. He shifts weight, making the end of the Step light enough to hang out from the rock where it joins the city. The rival celebrant speaks in the short commands of a woman accustomed to an immediate sung response. She wants Orsen's demonstration of power to hold her congregation together when she can no longer secure their attention with a hymn. Keep her a public figure and give Edri and Orsen the length of the scene.

**Conversation owed.** On the Step, let Edri and Orsen talk through what he actually knows, why he is ready to kill her, and what she wants to find out. Give him room to tell her how he approached the gold when the walkway was new and came away without touching it. He hopes someone who spent years watching the machine will take that fear seriously. Edri has watched long enough to know when the band comes within an arm's length. The attempt answers a question she has no way to settle by watching.

**Reference.** The original shows the mechanism only. Use its incised gold, great nested forms linked by spars, and violet dark crossed by translucent geometric cages and strands of light. Let the familiar band give Edri a place to put her hand. The cages change shape as the spheres turn, and their light reaches the Step again during the killing.

**Place.** Pick the Near Step, a tongue of stone rising out of Vessale's rim and hanging over its central drop. The Nine-Apse Temple offers cover for the confrontation. The Riven Gate puts troops between Edri and the gold. The Step exposes both her approach and Orsen's choice, and the outer band actually comes within reach there. On the first dry autumn evening, sellers strike clay votive weights to advertise their tone, singers ride out the vibrations in the soles of their feet, and juniper smoke drifts against cold stone. The gentle drag into the feast's kneeling hollows is something a goddess as powerless as any pilgrim feels through the shins. Angular patches of light play over a rail made from captured spearheads. Past the end of the rail is a bare tip where the band passes an arm's length away.

## Scene briefs

1. **The feast, about 15%.** Bring the two worships together quickly on the hanging Step. Edri wants someone to take her proposal seriously. Orsen expects her to kneel with everyone else. She feels his power draw her down and makes her announcement as he releases it. Laughter travels out across the roofs until she walks up to the captured spearheads.
2. **The threat and attempt, about 40%.** At the limit of the rail, Orsen wants her alive under his terms. Edri wants him to admit how much he knows and to look with her. The rival celebrant backs the threat of death in front of both congregations. When Edri presses Orsen about knowing the danger, he gives her the account of the one approach he made. The passing gold interrupts the conversation. He offers a real reprieve before contact. Edri has time to feel what that offer means to her body before she takes the last step and pushes.
3. **The opening and the death, longest scene, about 45%.** Give Edri time to see what she's opened and feel the cold salt. Orsen's failed command makes his power over the crowd horribly plain. He grants the rival celebrant the old kneeling weight and the crowd keeps looking up. While the gods brace worshippers at the edges, Edri steps back toward the open way to the city and beckons him to see the underwater lights. He turns his attention down to stone, fixes his weight on her, and pauses. Let her try to breathe and lift herself before he chooses to press harder. Stay with the terrible bodily sequence as her limbs and chest give and she dies fighting for breath. Keep the rising blood after the reader has felt the injuries with her.

Keep the old wars to the evidence left on the Step and their brief living recollection. Leave the mechanism's builders and full function outside the story.

## Ending

Finish at Edri's irreversible death. The pressure she felt during the blessing returns at the kneeling hollows with enough force to destroy her body. At the edge of the light, blood from those injuries lifts toward the sea. The same hand she beckoned with stays on the stone while she makes her last effort to see the lights move through the dark. She dies while the machine continues to run.
