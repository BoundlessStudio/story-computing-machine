# Promised experience

An immense golden mechanism already turns in the divine capital, through fields the gods cannot account for. Its worship, wars, and competing religions have lasted “for eternity” while its purpose remains unknown. A young goddess mocked for having “no domain, no worshippers, and no meaningful power” publicly decides to try activating it “simply because no one has ever dared to try.” The pantheon threatens destruction out of fear of what it cannot understand. I read this as awe curdling into dread, with the pleasure of watching her get a hand on the very thing the gods keep at a distance. The later directions tie the increased intensity to their threat and explicitly require her death as the ending, brutal, messy, and on the page. I expect her attempt and the pantheon's response to drive that ending. The relationships, means of death, and discovery itself are open.

## Takes considered

- **The feast.** Follow the young goddess through a public ceremony in which the gods come within reach of the gold. Let one audience witness the declaration, the first attempt, and the killing. The confrontation on the approach to the mechanism puts her appetite to see it work in direct reach of divine force.
- **The killer's account.** The god who kills her gives a first-person account of the afternoon. Its heart is the moment he passes from trying to make the machine obey him to laying his power on the young goddess. His struggle over what to call the act would compete with her immediate discoveries.
- **The truce.** Begin in a resumed war for the capital's center. The young goddess walks between rival forces to try the one thing both sides forbid. Watching a feud end in unanimous violence gives the threat military scale, with the risk that the war's outcome claims the attention due her encounter with the mechanism.

Choose the feast. The same stone carries generations of devotion, her bodily approach to the unknown, and the gods' execution. She can see what her decision has brought into reach while everyone who threatened her has to see it too.

## Provisional direction

At Vessale's feast, there are rival processions. One treats the machine as the instrument that sets and holds the sky. Another believes it winds the sky in for destruction. Their gods and singers meet on the Near Step, built over the center of the capital. All the old combatants know what they stopped a war to possess. None can say what a person could make it do. Edri, a young goddess the procession treats as an amusing extra, interrupts the blessing to announce her intent to set it going. The congregations laugh until she walks to where the metal can be reached.

Orsen, god of weight and once a patient audience for her drawings, speaks the sentence the pantheon backs. Step back now and live. Touch the machine in defiance and be destroyed. He wants her to take the offer. Edri wants him to tell her if anybody knows what effect her hand might have, then stay to see with her. His best argument is that the entire capital is under the spheres. He eventually admits he has never had the nerve to touch them. Edri, scared and very much wanting to live, steps past the worn kneeling stones anyway. She pushes the engraved band that comes by within reach of the Step. The push has the plain uncertainty of trying something by hand.

The concentric shells turn out of their old procession. Their fields spread above the roofs and open a view onto an upright black sea with lights moving in its depth. Salt rain comes through and falls toward that sea. Orsen tries to force it down onto Vessale's pavement and finds that his power can still drive the crowd to its knees while the rain goes its own way. Edri has taken her hand away and is alive. He has the power to let her leave. With the pantheon still behind the sentence, he puts the weight on her instead. The assault destroys her body in plain view while the mechanism stays open.

## Craft plan

**Teller and trial.** Stay with Edri through the killing. Try close third past, open to the bustle and mockery of the feast, against first-person present, impatient with the old ceremony and alive to what has changed overhead.

**Cast and speech.** Edri has spent seasons drawing the bands, trying to catch the moving shape before it passes the rail. The senior gods call her a goddess in the city's terms and give her neither a domain nor standing, worshippers, or meaningful power. She likes finding a thing she can actually check, rushes into concrete comparisons, and doubles back when she cares how her question lands. She wants Orsen to share the sight with her, partly because she wrongly takes his past interest in her drawings for a willingness to go as far as she will. Orsen likes having people safe under something he holds up and expects the same people to obey him. He talks in sweeping, hospitable periods, offering terms even when speaking as a judge. He remembers the first consecration of the Step and how near he got to the gold. He thinks a death sentence spoken in time will make Edri submit. He can shift the weight of people and things in the capital, as everyone on the suspended Step sees daily. The pantheon has a collective presence through the rival rites, its public assent to the sentence, and its silence as he carries it out.

**Conversation owed.** On the Step, let Edri and Orsen talk through what he actually knows, why he is ready to kill her, and what she hopes will happen. Give him room to tell her how he once approached the gold when the walkway was new and came away without laying a hand on it. He tells it now because he hopes someone who spent years watching the machine will take his own fear seriously.

**Reference.** The original shows the mechanism only. Use its incised gold, great nested forms linked by spars, and violet dark crossed by translucent geometric cages and strands of light. Let the familiar band give Edri a place to put her hand. The cages change shape as the spheres turn, and their light reaches the Step again during the killing.

**Place.** Pick the Near Step, a narrow, unsupported tongue of stone above Vessale's central drop. The Nine-Apse Temple offers cover for the confrontation. The Riven Gate puts troops between Edri and the gold. The Step exposes both her approach and Orsen's choice, and the outer band actually comes within reach there. On the first dry autumn evening, sellers strike clay votive weights to advertise their tone, singers ride out the vibrations in the soles of their feet, and juniper smoke drifts against cold stone. The field's angular light moves over the gold and the blackened impressions of generations of knees. One section of the low rail is made from captured spearheads. The geography presses Edri past the kneeling line into open view and leaves the distance across to the band small enough to cross with a hand.

## Scene briefs

1. **The feast, about 20%.** The processions come together on the Step and reenact incompatible accounts of the turning. Edri wants someone to take her proposal seriously. Orsen regards her presence as something he once allowed and expects her to enjoy the indulgence. She interrupts the blessing to say what she means to do and why. Laughter travels out across the roofs until she walks up to the captured spearheads.
2. **The threat and attempt, long scene, about 45%.** At the limit of the rail, Orsen wants her alive under his terms. Edri wants him to admit how much he knows and to look with her. The other gods voice their assent to destruction, and Orsen has a brief story to tell of his own approach. The passing gold keeps interrupting. He offers a real reprieve before contact. She lingers at the afforded place to kneel, then steps beyond it and applies her hand.
3. **The opening and the death, about 35%.** The sea opens over the city and the first cold salt hits the Step. Edri has let go and beckons Orsen to see the underwater lights. He tries his command on the rain, feels his control over the Step still working, and obeys the sentence his peers have backed. Stay bodily close as the weight forces her against stone, her limbs and chest give, and she dies fighting for breath. The sequence makes the killing unmistakably his doing and gives her fear, pain, and desire to keep looking room on the page.

Keep the old wars to the evidence left on the Step and their brief living recollection. Leave the mechanism's builders and full function outside the story.

## Ending

Finish at Edri's irreversible death. The worn kneeling hollows have become the place where the god forces her down. The ordinary downward fall that he demonstrated in the feast gives way, at the edge of the light, to her blood lifting toward the sea. She has one last chance to see the lights move through the dark and dies while the machine continues to run.
