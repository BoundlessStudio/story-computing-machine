# Opus review: What Turned Eighteen

Independent audit for a later editing pass. It is not a `review.md` verdict,
does not change canon state, and does not authorize edits by itself.

## Verdict

- Dialogue: REVISE — the clue that finds the vent body exists only in dialogue, and the key spoken arguments turn into corrections, rulings and stated themes.
- Prose: PASS — the distributed-body sensation is vivid and concrete; only minor glosses and small ambiguities.
- Continuity and prompt: REVISE — the prompt and the nine-body arithmetic hold, but the climax runs on a rain gutter, downspout and rooftop water tank, which the water-infrastructure policy rules out for authorized revisions.
- Edit scope: targeted-dialogue
- Existing review.md said: Verdict PASS, "Dialogue: PASS"; disagree on dialogue and on policy compliance.

The best dialogue in this story is not spoken. Arlon's touch-count yes/no, involuntary colours, tones, failed formations and answers through vibration make a real dialogic medium, and Maela's counting is the story's emotional spine. The family bickering (the fan joke, the teeth, the forks, "Cake damp") is also good. The failures cluster wherever a speaking character has to explain something or win a point. Aluna gives the correct diagnosis every time in composed, finished sentences, even in the middle of a storm. The outline says she owns "testable questions, not infallibility", but the story makes her the one who is always right. In the roof argument, Maela rebuts an argument before Jaro has made it, and Jaro then states the consent theme and gets the neat last word. The edit pass should keep the medium and the counting, repair the one missing on-page event, and make the three speaking adults shorter, more specific and more fallible at the few exchanges where they now make speeches. Leave the lessons to action (Jaro's tapping, the split formation).

## Edit guardrails

- Exactly nine bodies. Every count and grouping must still add up after edits: L61 nine; recovery 5→6→7→8→9 (L95–L149); four plus five (L201); storm split 3/2/2/1/1 (L269); "Five here" (L277); 3+2+1 (L289); six→nine (L295–L333); two/two/two (L325); transport 2/2/2/3 (L337); nine drops, toothbrushes and mouths. The recorded rewrite authorized only this count change. Do not reopen it.
- Craft profile prospective-2026-08-23; broadly accessible; 2,500–4,000 words (currently about 3,650 of prose). The story must still fully resolve the true-form promise.
- First-person Arlon with a centerless "I": one person, not nine. No separate wills, telepathy or speech after the change. Arlon communicates only by touch (once yes, twice no), colour (often involuntary), tone, formation, contact and vibration.
- Naming carries plot. Jaro says "Arlo", while Maela and Aluna say "Arlon". The lid reads ARLO and the cake reads ARLON, and the closing green N depends on that. Aluna names Arlon and avoids pronouns for them; Jaro and Maela use "they/them". Keep that distinction.
- Personhood (LOCKED style-guide entry): the consent beats must survive. That covers Aluna asking before the bowl comes off (L137), "We'll ask before counting" (L231), Aluna reaching only as far as Arlon can see (L305), and Maela's "Count?" (L341).
- The prompt's cover note ("the fish make you think something else") concerns the cover image. The prose never calls the bodies fish; do not add fish vocabulary.
- Keep what works: Maela's recounting and "This time she did not start over", the fan joke, "Three what?", Jaro's silent tapping, and the plain final "More?" exchange.

## Dialogue findings

### D1 · Blocking · The vent clue refers to an event the story never shows

- Where: L117–L127, bedroom, searching for the ninth body
- Text:
> Jaro searched behind the radiator. Maela looked inside the wastebasket.
> "Do that again," she said.
> I did not know what she meant.
> "The low sound. When Maela touched the vent cover."
- Problem: Ground/uptake. Aluna's line supplies content that is absent from every prior beat. The page shows Maela at the wastebasket, not touching the vent cover, and no low sound is described. The narrator hears everything his nine bodies do, and at L69 his own hum was "so loud inside my new awareness", so it makes no sense that he missed a chord from eight of his bodies. The reader cannot picture when this happened. The rescue of the ninth body depends entirely on this retroactive event. "Do that again" is also addressed to Arlon, even though only Maela can repeat the action.
- Fix: Put the event on the page at L117–L119. While searching, Maela bumps or brushes the mixing bowl (for example, steadying herself on the chair), and the narrator registers a low hum from himself that he cannot place. Then point Aluna's request at Maela and cut "I did not know what she meant." Keep L127's tap-and-answer beat as is. Illustrative only: "Maela. Knock on the bowl again."

### D2 · Major · Aluna explains a mechanism she did not see

- Where: L129–L131, bedroom, the vent discovery
- Text:
> Maela went white. "I covered it."
> "There's a gap underneath," Aluna said. "Arlon went through before the tape settled."
> Another shot toward the uncovered gap beneath the mixing bowl.
- Problem: Ground/knowledge. Aluna states as fact when and how Arlon got in. If she had seen it, her silence through four failed counts makes no sense. If she didn't see it, she has no basis for the claim. "Before the tape settled" also conflicts with the staging: the tape was pressed down before the change (L15), and L67 describes an ordinary gap under the bowl, not tape that was still settling. This is the Aluna-supplies-the-answer pattern at a small scale.
- Fix: Keep Maela's "I covered it." It is good guilt. Cut "Arlon went through before the tape settled." Better still, let Maela find the gap herself when she climbs onto the chair at L141, so the discovery belongs to the person who taped it. If Aluna keeps any line here, make it an observation or question, not an explanation.

### D3 · Major · The first exchange after the change becomes a correction and a ruling

- Where: L79–L87, bedroom, just after Jaro steps toward Arlon
- Text:
> "Stop," Aluna said quietly. "Arlon moved away."
> "Or they're cold."
> "Both can be true. Stop moving."
> The fan spun at its lowest setting because the apartment held the day's heat.
- Problem: This is the first human exchange after Arlon comes apart, and it plays out as a hypothesis, a counter-hypothesis and an even-handed verdict (correction ping-pong plus an epigram). "Or they're cold" has no speaker tag and could be Jaro or Maela. It also contradicts the scene: the apartment holds the day's heat, the ceiling air "baked" Arlon (L61), and the fan is now off. Aluna's "Both can be true" endorses a guess with no basis just to sound fair. A shocked family would not argue about temperature here.
- Fix: Cut "Or they're cold" and "Both can be true." Keep Aluna's "Stop" and a plain instruction not to move. If Jaro reacts, make it hurt or bewildered and tag it. Keep "Jaro stopped." and the stillness that follows.

### D4 · Major · Maela rebuts an argument Jaro has not made

- Where: L179–L191, bedroom, after Arlon bumps the cake box
- Text:
> "Cake," Jaro said promptly.
> Maela looked at him. "You cannot translate them because you also turned eighteen once."
> "No. But I know Arlo."
> Jaro began, "When I changed—"
- Problem: Ground/uptake. Jaro has only said "Cake". Maela answers the claim "I understand because I changed too", which Jaro does not make until L189, where she cuts him off and rebuts it a second time. The order is backwards, and her first rebuttal makes the later, better one redundant. Read literally, her sentence says that turning eighteen is the reason he can't translate. It is also stiff and uncontracted. Jaro's "No. But…" is the house correction rhythm. At L187, "Maela repeated, 'No.'" repeats a word she has not said.
- Fix: Make Maela object to what Jaro actually did, which is answer for Arlon, and save the "your change was different" point for L191, where it works. Keep Jaro's gesture and "Upstairs?" addressed to Arlon. Change "repeated" at L187 to "said". Illustrative only: "Stop answering for them."

### D5 · Major · Jaro states the theme and gets the last word

- Where: L193–L199, bedroom, the cancelled-supper argument (the story's decisive spoken exchange)
- Text:
> "Yes. This is not the same. I was going to say everyone spent my first dinner deciding what I could handle without asking me."
> "You had words."
> "And nobody waited for them."
- Problem: This is the Philosopher plus a Punchline Patch. Jaro, the joker, switches into composed, uncontracted speech and names the story's consent theme in the abstract ("deciding what I could handle without asking me"). Maela's "You had words" exists only to set up "And nobody waited for them", a neat closing aphorism. The point is then acted out anyway through Aluna's palm questions (L207–L219) and everything that follows, so the speech explains ahead of time what the action will show.
- Fix: Keep Jaro's concession and his grievance, but make it one concrete memory from his own first dinner, said roughly and aimed at Maela. Cut the "You had words" / "And nobody waited for them" pair. Let Maela answer with the hand to her forehead that is already at L199, so "She was right about the roof. That made me furious with her." still lands. Illustrative only: "Yeah, not the same. But at my dinner you all decided I was done with swimming, and I was sitting right there."

### D6 · Major · Aluna lectures in the storm

- Where: L283–L291, roof, rain, bodies scattered
- Text:
> "Stop talking," Aluna told Jaro.
> "They need a direction."
> "You're giving nine of them one direction."
> "The light follows whatever Arlon notices. It isn't an answer."
> It took them, but this time I did not ask all six to do the same thing.
- Problem: This is uniform fluency under pressure plus the Philosopher. In driving rain, with bodies over the edge, Aluna delivers two finished analytic sentences. One turns Jaro's word "direction" into a neat correction. The other states a rule about the colours that she earlier admitted nobody knew ("We don't know", L101). "You're giving nine of them one direction" also states in advance the discovery Arlon makes alone at L325, so the climax gets explained before it happens.
- Fix: Keep "Stop talking" and Jaro's protest. Replace Aluna's reply with one short, shouted, practical observation about what she can actually see (his shouting makes Arlon flash), or with a hand on his arm and no words. Cut "You're giving nine of them one direction" completely so that L325 carries the discovery. Illustrative only: "Every time you yell, Arlon flashes. Quiet."

### D7 · Minor · Referent and count snags

- Where: L133–L135 (vent), L159 (yes/no protocol), L187 (supper argument), L291 (roof)
- Text:
> "Can they come back?" Jaro asked.
> The question was for me, but I had no yes.
> "Do you want the window closed?"
> Maela repeated, "No."
> "There are more behind it," Aluna said.
- Problem: Jaro's question is phrased in the third person, yet the narrator says it "was for me". "The window" has never been mentioned: the only opening shown is the balcony door, which Maela has already shut. Maela "repeated" a "No" she never said. "More behind it" suggests several bodies when only one remains behind the tank, which blurs the arithmetic the rewrite depends on.
- Fix: Have Jaro ask Arlon directly (for example, "Arlo, can you get out?") so "I had no yes" follows. Establish the window before L159, or ask about the balcony instead. Change "repeated" to "said". Change "more" to "another", or make Aluna unsure what is there.

### D8 · Minor · Jaro's nickname slips

- Where: L175 and L357 (compare L75, L183, L279)
- Text:
> "It might mean Arlon wants to be taller," Jaro said.
> "For Arlon's safety."
> "Arlo, come to the light!" Jaro called.
- Problem: Identical Voices, small scale. "Arlo" is Jaro's marker (and the ARLO lid), but twice he uses Maela and Aluna's "Arlon". At L175 this blurs him into Aluna's parallel line just above it.
- Fix: Change L175 to "Arlo". L357 can stay "Arlon" only if the editor wants it to read as mock-formal; otherwise change it to "Arlo".

### D9 · Minor · Stiff phrasing in the opening banter

- Where: L19 and L47, bedroom, before the change
- Text:
> "You are helping how?"
> "Still do. Entirely practical history."
- Problem: Both lines use the collection's clipped, legalistic register. "You are helping how?" drops the contraction that makes the idiom sound natural. "Entirely practical history" is an opaque self-defense that no one says aloud. The surrounding bickering (hormones, six eggs) works.
- Fix: Use the natural idiom ("And you're helping how?"). Cut "Entirely practical history." so that "Still do." leads into "See? I'm useful."

## Prose findings

### P1 · Minor · Narration explains what the action already showed

- Where: L295 (roof, count) and L323 (roof, Jaro tapping)
- Text:
> Then, because stress made her repeat, "Six here."
> He had finally learned that adding instructions would not help.
- Problem: Maela's repeating has been shown since L103, and Jaro's change is fully shown by the taps and "He did not call my name again." (L299). The glosses tell the reader what they have just inferred.
- Fix: Cut "because stress made her repeat" (keep the repeated line) and cut the L323 sentence, leaving "Jaro kept tapping."

### P2 · Minor · Jaro's and Aluna's relationship to Arlon is never stated

- Where: L13 and L27, first appearances
- Text:
> "That'll starve you of air," Jaro said from the doorway.
> Aluna came out of my closet holding the adjustable trousers she had brought me.
- Problem: Maela is identified as "My mother" (L15), but nothing says who Jaro or Aluna are. Maela knows Jaro's eating habits and school records, which suggests family, but the reader cannot tell whether the roof argument is a mother overruling her son or a guest. That changes how D4 and D5 read.
- Fix: Add one identifying clause for each at first mention (or at L37 for Jaro), consistent with the outline's "birthday companions" and with Maela's evident closeness to Jaro.

### P3 · Minor · Small ambiguities

- Where: L25 (opening) and L345 (final count)
- Text:
> The forecast said nine.
> Then I touched her back once.
- Problem: "Nine" what: o'clock, percent, millimetres? Next to a nine-body story it also reads like a leftover number. "Touched her back" reads first as the body part.
- Fix: "The forecast said rain by nine" (or whatever is meant). Change the last one to "Then I touched her once in return" or similar.

## Continuity and prompt findings

### C1 · Major · Water infrastructure as background and climax stakes

- Where: L37 (cousin), L235 and L261 (roof), L269–L327 (storm and gutter rescue)
- Text:
> My cousin had turned eighteen and acquired ears that could hear water moving inside walls, which had led to a plumbing apprenticeship
> Two blew behind the water tank.
> One of the two beyond the parapet struck a gutter and caught there, fins plastered by water.
> I felt cold water push it along the metal channel toward the downspout.
- Problem: Style guide, "No default water infrastructure" (CANON; applies to authorized revisions). The prompt never asks for it, yet it appears as a background profession (plumbing) and carries the climax: a rooftop water tank (its rim and bracket hide two bodies) and a rain gutter and downspout (the final body's peril). Rain itself is ordinary weather and stays.
- Fix: Change the cousin's gift and trade to something non-hydraulic (keep the "bad month of sleep"). Replace the water tank with another rooftop structure (stair housing, chimney stack, lift-motor hut) and keep its raised base and broken bracket. Replace the gutter and downspout with a non-drainage ledge (a cornice lip or sign bracket), with rain sheeting along it toward a broken end or corner. Keep every beat and count: pinned body, taps through the metal railing, the six-body formation, flank-to-flank climb, Maela's still palm. Also check "silver tanks" at L235.

### C2 · Minor · "Covered vent" after the bowl was removed

- Where: L201, bedroom, Arlon's four-and-five demonstration
- Text:
> I arranged four bodies over the tray and sent five toward the covered vent.
> When the bowl came free, the current from the duct breathed outward.
- Problem: The bowl came off at L145, and towels do not go into the vent gaps until L223, so the vent is open at L201. "Covered" also weakens Maela's "Is that a threat?", which only makes sense if the vent is open.
- Fix: Change "covered" to "open" or "bare".

## Preserve

- Maela's counting as the emotional spine, especially the final "Nine" she does not recount:
> This time she did not start over.
- The fan joke, the first moment the room loosens:
> I slapped Aluna's palm twice so hard that three bodies lost altitude.
- Maela's interruption, which is exactly how she should argue:
> "You had one pair of feet," Maela said.
> Arlon has already been inside a wall.
- Arlon's anger at being rightly overruled:
> She was right about the roof. That made me furious with her.
> "Is that a threat?" she asked.
- The palm protocol broken by play:
> I hesitated long enough for Jaro to say, "That's a strong maybe."
- Failed contact left as failed contact, and Aluna's self-correction toward personhood:
> "Three what?"
> "And if we lose sight of anyone—any part—we count anyway."
- Jaro's growth shown through action:
> He did not call my name again.
- Maela at the edge:

- The consent payoff and the plain final exchange (no thesis at the end; keep it that way):
> "Count?" she asked.
> "More?" he asked.
- The cake bickering at L349–L357 and the teeth exchange at L239–L245 are the right kind of ordinary play.
