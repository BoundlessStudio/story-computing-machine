# Outline

## Story

- Premise and central promise: At Arlon Vask's eighteenth birth minute, their expected one-body change becomes forty-three hand-sized luminous creatures that swim through air on translucent fins. Follow the wonder and danger of one distributed person as Arlon recovers every separated part, establishes usable communication, and reclaims the same day's birthday gathering as a participant rather than a spectacle.
- Focal pressure or attachment: Arlon wants their planned rooftop supper to continue. Pride makes them demonstrate control before understanding a body whose attention, pain, balance, and appetite arrive from forty-three locations; Maela wants the new body safely contained.
- Counterforce or complication: Drafts, vents, rain, and divided attention create danger without a villain. Maela counts, Aluna observes, and Jaro compares Arlon's change to his more familiar one. Each helps, but urgency makes them misread Arlon's unfamiliar chords and light.
- POV, distance, and information limit: Close first-person past through Arlon. Several simultaneous sensations compete, but whichever fragment is endangered seizes narration. Arlon cannot see their whole formation or know whether a signal reads correctly until someone responds.
- Governing movement and time shape: One afternoon and evening expands from bedroom to apartment to rooftop, contracts during a rain scattering, then settles into a redesigned indoor party. End with all forty-three parts accounted for and immediate accommodations working; lifelong adaptation remains open.
- Speculative rule or ordinary-world constraint: Everyone changes once at eighteen, usually within one humanoid body; height, face, mobility, voice, and sex changes are ordinary variations, not lesser forms or moral tests. Arlon's creatures form one person, never copies. Within an apartment's span, sensation and intention stay shared; walls, distance, panic, and competing noise cause lag. No creature speaks alone. Together they make fin-tones, bioluminescent patterns, visible formations, or tactile rhythms whose meaning stays contextual. Drafts scatter them, hot dry air exhausts them, and each part must drink and feed.

## Voice

- Narrative texture: Move among Arlon's competing balances, temperatures, and sightlines, then narrow when one endangered part steals attention. Keep the wonder domestic and tactile.
- Conversational texture: Mix safety questions with cake talk, peer jokes, mistaken signals, visible confirmation, and tactile counts. No mode becomes fluent immediately.
- Rhetorical ownership: Arlon thinks spatially; Maela owns counts and household sequences; Aluna uses patient observation; Jaro reasons by personal comparison and social momentum.
- Pressure behavior: Arlon's formation fragments; Maela repeats; Aluna grows quieter and exact; Jaro talks over uncertainty, then learns to wait.
- Anti-default: Avoid a polished identity debate, instant translation, public proof, or any claim that spectacular anatomy makes Arlon more authentic.

## Beats

1. Before the birth minute, Maela covers hazards while Aluna brings adjustable clothing. Jaro treats the sex change his own birthday brought as ordinary practical history amid talk of cake, weather, appetite, and rooftop seating.
2. Arlon becomes forty-three air-swimming rays. Simultaneous cold, ceiling heat, hunger, and many sightlines replace one body's orientation. A fan drives parts under furniture and toward a vent; Maela counts while Aluna builds slow barriers, recovering them without pretending to understand.
3. The group attempts ordinary conversation. Fin-tones cannot carry words; color and formation convey urgency only in context. Touch gives reliable counts and consent for immediate actions. Jaro's advice about thirst helps, while his one-body comparisons fail.
4. Arlon insists on the rooftop supper. They cover vents, remove candles, set water dishes, and erect cloth screens. When uncertain, others offer concrete choices and wait for touch or light. Let jokes, gifts, food, and irritation continue around the accommodation.
5. On the roof, Arlon enjoys circling friends, tasting diluted icing through many mouths, and feeling music across the shoal. They spread too far to demonstrate control. A rain front buckles the screen and drives parts over the roof edge, into a gutter, and behind a tank.
6. Distance makes sensation noisy. Maela presses for shelter; Jaro calls too many instructions; Aluna recognizes that Arlon's light shows attention, not a request. They stop guessing emotion. Maela maintains count and windbreak, Aluna follows orientation, and Jaro sends a slow railing vibration through the rain.
7. Arlon abandons the appearance of calm, concentrates movement a few parts at a time, and uses touch to accept routes around the tank and downspout. The last exhausted creature rejoins before the storm strengthens; imperfect signals and distinct attention resolve the danger.
8. Indoors, Arlon chooses fans off, one screened window, readable lighting, and no continuous counting unless requested. Maela confirms forty-three; Aluna resumes supper; Jaro returns to music and cake. End with Arlon participating through the body's new appetite and sound while the longer future remains open.
