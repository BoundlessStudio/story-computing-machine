# Outline

## Story

- Premise and central promise: In Palem, worshippers sometimes submit one prayer to both Elovar, god of chosen continuities and defended boundaries, and Orsava, goddess of endings and revision. Their opposing powers and authorities cannot rule on the same petition, so their adult head priests, Mezarin and Ankora, meet privately each quarter to return mixed prayers for a clearer mortal choice. Across five handoffs, professional argument becomes attention, anticipation, and love. When their unnecessary personal annotations are noticed by temple staff, resolve the promise without reconciling the gods or merging the faiths: Mezarin and Ankora name and reciprocate their love, refuse resignation as its price, and begin a bounded private relationship while remaining head priests who will still oppose each other in public matters.
- Intended reader experience: A mature, warm slow-burn fantasy romance with dry clerical comedy and genuine theological friction. Let the reader recognize Ankora's care slightly before Mezarin can, take pleasure in their increasingly unnecessary arguments, and feel why each vocation is beloved rather than merely an obstacle. The release should be an explicit mutual confession and an ordinary first plan, not a secret divine scheme, a grand truce, or proof that love makes their disagreements disappear.
- Generating force:
  - Mode: Relationship-led accumulation. Five quarterly transfers lengthen from a brisk document exchange to chosen meals and private questions; what moves the story is the growing amount of attention neither priest can justify as necessary to the work.
  - Focal attachment or contradiction: Mezarin falls for the precision with which Ankora asks whether an old commitment is still chosen; Ankora falls for the care with which Mezarin distinguishes endurance from mere inertia. Each wants the other exactly as a devoted rival, yet their offices make affection easy for either congregation to misread as capture, conversion, or a doctrinal concession.
  - Plot-led wants and useful limit, if applicable: Not principally plot-led. Mezarin initially wants clean classifications, short meetings, and no material a rival can use against her newly assumed office; her useful limit is that she treats ambiguity, including desire, as a boundary failure to correct. Ankora wants the mixed-prayer practice to remain honest and increasingly wants time with Mezarin that is not borrowed from petitioners; his humor keeps argument humane but also lets him retreat from direct vulnerability before she can answer.
- Counterforce or complication:
  - Source: Incompatible valuable duties, the public symbolism attached to two visible offices, and the lovers' own defensive habits. Elovar and Orsava are genuinely opposed peers: one exercises local authority to sustain deliberately chosen boundaries and commitments, the other to end or revise those that no longer carry present assent. Neither temple can let a head priest's private preference decide a mixed petition, and no official annotation stays reliably private once copied into temple records.
  - Credible inside logic, if a person: No one is an antagonist. Elovar and Orsava each protect a real good and continue to require honest opposition from their representatives; neither secretly engineers the courtship, converts, or grants a romantic exemption. Unnamed staff who notice the notes are doing ordinary archival work, not policing intimacy. Mezarin fears being treated as compromised; Ankora fears that stepping back will be read as Orsava's defeat, but he is tempted to volunteer that loss to protect her.
  - Pressure or change created: The pair can stop the personal notes, end the meetings, let one of them leave office, or accept that love will eventually become known and be interpreted by people they cannot control. Because resignation would turn one vocation into a courtship offering, a satisfactory choice must preserve both people's agency and allow conflict of duty to remain conflict.
- Narrative design:
  - POV, tense, distance, and limitation: Close third past through Mezarin, intimate with her bodily anticipation, adversarial vocabulary, and habit of sorting motives into permissible categories. She cannot read Ankora's thoughts or hear Orsava; Ankora's affection appears through remembered preferences, softened jokes, strategic lingering, and one withdrawal when exposure becomes possible. Give Mezarin the language of edges, weights, and what must be carried; give Ankora the language of changed conditions, present consent, and exits, with joking deflection as his failure mode.
  - Time shape and compression: Thirteen months in five scene-weighted quarterly handoffs. Render the first clash, the first shared food, the discovered annotations, and the final confession substantially; compress the intervening season through changed handwriting, longer margins, and the way Mezarin begins arranging other duties around the handoff dates. No flash-forward coda and no manufactured emergency.
  - Information and reveal strategy: Establish at once that both gods are living, worshipped powers with distinct local authority, not metaphors or uncertain machinery. Demonstrate their limited effects through prayer cords and consecrated seals, while making clear that petitioners retain the choice to restate, separate, or withdraw a request. Let readers infer Ankora's reciprocal interest before Mezarin accepts her own. Reveal the compromised privacy through a routine copied page, not spying. Do not reveal that the gods are one being, former lovers, secret allies, or authors of the romance; neither god can compel affection, read the rival's worshippers universally, or settle a mortal relationship by miracle.
  - Governing movement: Arrive early to avoid the rival; be made to stay by a petition that cannot honestly be divided; argue; notice care; return prepared; share unleavened food; extend the margins; ask one personal question and evade the answer; arrange life around the next meeting; find their joking note copied by ordinary staff; strip the exchange back to cold formality; feel the chosen absence; confront the apparent choices; refuse sacrificial resignation; name love without naming victory; hear it returned; set the smallest boundaries needed to begin; remain after the official work is over.
- Speculative surplus: Elovar's consecrated garments slow nearby yeast, while Orsava's make it race through its rise and collapse. When both head priests eat within arm's reach, bakers cannot predict which effect a dough will catch, so neutral meeting rooms serve griddle-cooked barley cakes and keep brewing starters elsewhere. The nuisance affects kitchens and temple hospitality but cannot reveal emotion, measure compatibility, alter consent, or resolve any petition.
- Structural distinction: A courtship built from five increasingly personal administrative handoffs, with elapsed seasons carried by marginalia and altered duration rather than a danger ratchet, chase, repeated escape, public ceremony, or single spectacular confrontation. The decisive venue is a bare rented counting room after the documents have been removed. There is no crowd conversion, technical proof, divine reconciliation, institutional rewrite, resignation spectacle, or polished emblem of unity.
- Decisive turn, deepening, or recognition: At the fifth handoff, Ankora proposes that deputies take over and admits he has considered resigning so Mezarin will not be accused of yielding to Orsava. Mezarin sees that accepting would make his cherished vocation the fee for her safety and would reproduce the very victory story they distrust. She says plainly that she loves the head priest who will keep disagreeing with her, not a converted or diminished version of him. Ankora drops the joke he prepared, reciprocates without qualification, and rejects resignation. They agree that mixed petitions will contain only necessary comments and that their first private supper will occur off temple time; these are relationship boundaries, not a new settlement between faiths.
- Aftereffect or live uncertainty: They begin in love and in office, with no blessing from the rival god, no promise of secrecy, and no control over how either congregation may react when the relationship becomes known. Future conflicts of interest will require case-by-case choices, and either person remains free to end the relationship without divine penalty. The immediate uncertainty is manageable and personal: where two conspicuous priests can have supper without turning it into an audience.
- Opening / ending relation: Open with Mezarin entering the counting room early because she intends to finish before Ankora arrives, only to discover that he has waited beside one inseparable petition. End in the same room after the official packet is gone: the handoff bell has rung, both are free to leave, and they stay long enough to disagree—now as acknowledged lovers—about the least ceremonial place in Palem to eat next week.

## Beats

1. At the first quarterly exchange, newly installed Mezarin expects to leave mixed prayers for Ankora without seeing him. He has waited with an unnamed craftworker's request to preserve a livelihood while ending an inherited obligation; their gods' authorities cannot safely be layered onto the same request, and both priests refuse to invent a choice for the petitioner. Their sharp argument ends in a jointly worded request for clarification, giving Mezarin her first unwanted respect for him.
2. At the next handoff, Ankora brings unleavened barley cakes because their consecrated clothes ruined a bowl of rising dough during the first meeting. He remembers how Mezarin takes her tea and denies that remembering is diplomacy. They resolve two petitions efficiently, then spend longer disputing a third than the text requires; Mezarin catches herself enjoying his distinctions and leaves before he can ask why she is smiling.
3. Compress a season through notes that shift from corrections to questions: each tests the other's reasoning, recommends an unnamed book or food, and learns when the other is tired. In the third full meeting, Mezarin explains that she sought Elovar's office because chosen continuities need active maintenance; Ankora explains that Orsava's service is not contempt for loyalty but protection against commitments surviving consent. Attraction becomes legible in their silences and in Mezarin's awareness of the next date.
4. At the fourth handoff, a copied page returns from ordinary temple processing with one of their private jokes preserved in the official margin. No threat accompanies it, but the loss of privacy is real. Ankora turns formal and proposes that all future notes be strictly necessary. Mezarin agrees too quickly, and the shortened meeting makes her recognize that the part she misses was never the paperwork.
5. In the following quarter, show Mezarin trying and failing to keep the anticipation out of her schedule and prayers. She does not ask Elovar to classify or remove the feeling: both gods have real authority, but neither owns unpledged affection. She arrives prepared either to speak or to end the private layer of their contact.
6. Ankora proposes replacing them with deputies and reveals that he considered resigning to contain the damage to Mezarin's reputation. His plan is protective and unacceptable; he jokes about Orsava approving an elegant departure until Mezarin makes him answer without doctrine.
7. Mezarin refuses to let love require his diminishment, names what she loves about his opposition, and admits she wants a relationship whose meaning they choose even if others later misread it. Ankora reciprocates and admits that withdrawal was easier than risking a direct refusal. Allow a first kiss or equally unequivocal consensual intimacy, kept non-explicit and grounded in the awkward furniture and cooling food.
8. They separate official practice from private courtship without pretending the separation will always be easy: clean petition margins, off-duty meetings, no use of either god's seal or authority to bind the relationship, and fresh consent when duty conflicts. The packet leaves with the courier; they remain in the counting room to choose an ordinary supper place, still disagreeing and no longer mistaking disagreement for absence of love.

## Continuity

- Canon used: The story occurs in the one deep-time shared world during an otherwise unspecified active-magic era. Elovar and Orsava satisfy the established godhood taxonomy through the continuing conjunction of worship, real power, and local authority. Both are living agents; consecrated cords, seals, and clothing only store or shape power supplied through living agency. `Magic`, `divinity`, `prayer`, and the gods' domains are Palem's cultural vocabulary for local real systems, not universal objective categories.
- Boundaries and unknowns: Palem, Mezarin, Ankora, Elovar, Orsava, their temples, powers, petitions, and rivalry are new and have no established connection to Earth, Ravel, the Glass-Sea era, the Founding Vigil, the Crowned Ancestors, the Keeper Below tradition, the Goddess of Time claimant, or any other existing deity, religion, polity, person, or place. The gods' local effects establish no universal prayer mechanism, vow or contract magic, truth compulsion, name or title power, divine hierarchy, power-transfer rule, or authority over love. Their opposition remains intact; the relationship does not merge worship, alter doctrine, prove a cosmological thesis, or enter canon unless separately promoted. The outline is advisory; the prompt's completed mutual fall into love is the drafting authority.
