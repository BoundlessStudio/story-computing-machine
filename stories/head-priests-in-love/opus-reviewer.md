# Opus review: Where Rival Gods Go Quiet

Independent audit for a later editing pass. It is not a `review.md` verdict,
does not change canon state, and does not authorize edits by itself.

## Verdict

- Dialogue: REVISE — Ambel's accusation after the fouled line is garbled, and the asylum exchange is a run of matched counters that ends on a reply that doesn't follow. The confession and declaration exchanges are strong.
- Prose: PASS — concrete, varied and tactile, with few em dashes; only the launch staging and one water-infrastructure policy note need attention.
- Continuity and prompt: PASS — the prompt is delivered in about 3,400 words; a few object slips (sandal/boot, tiller, bread) each need a one-word or one-clause fix.
- Edit scope: targeted-dialogue
- Existing review.md said: Verdict PASS; "Dialogue: PASS"; I partly disagree. It's right that the voices are distinct, but it missed two lines that fail the shared-reality check and the shared counter-and-correction rhythm in the first two private exchanges.

This story's dialogue is better than the collection default. Levara's clause-making is a flaw the story dramatizes: Ambel names it ("Stop making a clause."), she defends it ("It helps me not lie."), and it breaks at the right moments into plain speech ("I thought if you came to Holdfast, I would be permitted to want you." / "I love you."). The weakness is that in the asylum probe (L65–L101), the argument after the fouled line (L151–L163) and parts of the reef argument, both priests trade short counters and corrections of equal weight. In those stretches Ambel stops reasoning from particulars and people and sounds like a second Levara. Two lines fail the shared-reality check: "You let Holdfast catch me..." (L155) and "I meant you." (L101). A few more lines exist mainly to set up the next speaker's retort. The edit pass should cut those setup lines, fix the two broken lines, break the mirror-image symmetry of the two credos, and trim narration that explains lines the reader has already understood. It must not flatten Levara's legalism or Ambel's jokes, because those are character.

## Edit guardrails

- Prompt: the head priests of two opposing gods fall in love, and the story resolves it; 2,500–4,000 words (now about 3,400); broadly accessible; active craft profile `prospective-2026-08-21`. The kiss stays brief and non-explicit and keeps its asked-for consent ("May I?" / "Yes."). Both lovers are stated adults (38 and 41).
- Story mechanics other scenes rely on: Holdfast stills water only inside a properly tied mooring and speaks in salt letters on rope; Farwake moves a deliberately released craft outward and makes loose objects creep seaward; invoking both at once can roll a boat. Keep the seven-year partnership, the six-turn knot against the keeper's "Two turns" at the end, and the six days signalled by six fingers. Keep both salt commands and Farwake's "Bring her beyond every fastening" with its inducement. Neither priest converts, and the liturgy lines at L377–L387 stay verbatim.
- Universe: the gods satisfy Divinity (CANON: worship, power and authority) and nothing contradicts LOCKED entries. Stay inside the LOCKED audience and content boundaries.
- Keep what works: Levara's legalism and Ambel's gallows humor are the intended voices. Keep the unequal fluency at the declaration and the wordless beats (the boom he doesn't warn her about, the bread wrapping tucked under his thigh). The two late correction volleys (the bill, "We're east of the marker") are play and should stay.

## Dialogue findings

### D1 · Major · Garbled accusation after the fouled line

- Where: L151–L161, just after they clear the fouled home line at the harbor mouth
- Text:
> He peeled it back and wrung it out. "A persuasive demonstration."
> "Of what?"
> "You let Holdfast catch me and promise you'll loosen the line afterward."
> "I did not foul it deliberately."
- Problem: Ground/referent. This is where Ambel first says aloud that she is trying to recruit him, and it gives the near-capsize its meaning. But his answer to "Of what?" is a bare clause with the "what happens if" left out. It reads first as a statement that she has done something ("You let Holdfast catch me"), and the reader has to rebuild the analogy: Holdfast's line refused to let go, so a promise to loosen it later is worthless. Levara's reply takes "demonstration" to mean "you staged this". That misreading is legible and good, but it only works if the accusation underneath it is clear.
- Fix: Rewrite L155 as a complete, plain analogy in Ambel's rope idiom, and keep Levara's misreading and his "I know." Illustrative only: "Of what happens if I let Holdfast tie me up because you've promised to loosen the line later."

### D2 · Major · Asylum probe runs on matched counters and ends on a reply that doesn't follow

- Where: L65–L101, at the harbor rings as Levara raises the "fourth response"
- Text:
> "We are discussing the reach of consent under rival jurisdictions."
> "No. You're testing a knot before you put weight on it."
> "That is an image, not an answer."
> "It's the answer you're getting."
> Ambel caught the parcel at the bow. "Coward."
> "Careless."
> "I meant you."
- Problem: Tennis Match plus correction ping-pong, and one failure of uptake. From L65 to L101 there are 18 strictly alternating lines, nearly all one or two sentences, each correcting or countering the last. Ambel's "No." and the "image, not an answer" / "answer you're getting" pair are the collection's default house rhythm, not these two people. The closing volley doesn't hold up. Levara plainly took "Coward." as aimed at her, since she answers it with an insult of her own. Ambel's "I meant you." therefore answers a misunderstanding nobody had. "Careless." exists only to set up that last word (Punchline Patch), and readers can't tell whether it means "you're careless with your bread" or "I was careless, not cowardly".
- Fix: Keep the dodge through jurisdiction (it's hers), Ambel's "Are we discussing asylum?" and his knot image (it's his). Cut "That is an image, not an answer." and "It's the answer you're getting." Levara can pin the creeping line with her heel in silence and retreat to "The review is part of our duty." Keep "Coward." and cut "Careless." and "I meant you." so that the insult goes straight into him tearing the loaf and handing her the larger half. Insult followed by kindness does more than a third quip.

### D3 · Minor · Farwake's channel is an unintroduced referent

- Where: L185, at the first reef bell as Ambel reveals his own command
- Text:
> "Through the channel under the cradle. 'Bring her beyond every fastening.'
- Problem: Ground/referent. "The cradle" doesn't appear until L395, and "the channel" has so far meant the strip of water between the two wharves. Because the reader doesn't know how Farwake speaks (a voice in moving water?), the medium isn't legible next to Holdfast's salt letters.
- Fix: Either mention the boat's cradle at the launch (L29–L47) or have Ambel say plainly where and how he heard Farwake, in a few words. Keep the quoted command and the inducement.

### D4 · Minor · A distinction added only to cue "You always do that"

- Where: L253–L259, the reef argument after "Of course I do." / He flinched.
- Text:
> I imagined one means of obtaining it, and that means was wrong. Those statements are not equivalent."
> "You always do that."
> "Distinguish things?"
- Problem: Ground/referent plus setup line. After four statements, "Those statements" has no clear antecedent. The sentence mainly exists to cue Ambel's critique. That critique ("Answer the implication and leave the person waiting") is good and belongs to him. It works just as well if her speech ends on "that means was wrong."
- Fix: Cut "Those statements are not equivalent." and keep the rest of her speech and the exchange that follows unchanged.

### D5 · Minor · Mirror-image credos with an echoed "I hear you"

- Where: L279–L297, the reef argument after "I imagined it too"
- Text:
> I do not love him because I have failed to imagine leaving."
> "It is. You are staying because you mean it. I heard you."
> He doesn't call every departure a wound. I mean that too."
> "I hear you," she said.
- Problem: The Philosopher, plus a scripted reconciliation. Each priest gives a three-item defense of their god that ends on a thesis line, and each is answered with "I heard you" / "I hear you". The concrete details are strong (ferry families' winter sleep, the shrine that takes in the turned-out, lives that were killing people). The matched structure and the theme spelled out at the end of each credo are what make it feel authored. The outline itself warned against "paired speeches" and "symmetrical philosopher-priests".
- Fix: Cut Levara's closing thesis line; her list already proves it, and "All right." / "That is not an answer." still follow. Replace her verbatim echo at L297 with silence or an action. L295 already shows her declining to argue, so a small practical gesture is enough. Keep Ambel's "I heard you" and "You mistook devotion for fear." / "Yes."

### D6 · Minor · A quip-and-correction relay just before the declaration

- Where: L305–L313, returning along the reef, "What happens if neither of us converts?"
- Text:
> "That answer has furniture."
> "You asked what happens."
> "I asked what happens to us."
- Problem: Correction ping-pong at the most important exchange in the story. Levara's procedural dodge is set up and true to her ("preparing an answer for half the circuit"). The next two lines are a writerly image-quip ("has furniture" is only half recoverable) and a line that exists to set up Ambel's correction.
- Fix: Cut "That answer has furniture." and "You asked what happens." so that "I asked what happens to us." follows her procedural answer directly. If a beat is needed, make it non-verbal (he waits, or doesn't row). Keep everything from L315 on.

### D7 · Minor · Narration explains lines that already landed

- Where: L165, L209, L243, L269, L291 (also L385), through the post-foul and reef arguments
- Text:
> There it was: not anger, yet, but the withdrawal of an ease she had come to depend on without ever naming it.
> The answer struck because it was careless.
> The anecdotes and jokes were gone.
> He had done what he always demanded of disputants and rarely managed when the dispute touched him: corrected himself while the correction still cost something.
> The simple sentence left her with nowhere to press.
- Problem: At each emotional turn, the narration states the subtext the reader has just inferred: Levara standing up, Ambel's flat "No. It isn't.", "I heard you". L269 is the heaviest, because it tells the reader exactly why "No. It isn't." matters. The actions already carry these moments: the boom he doesn't warn her about, her standing so fast the boat rocks, his hand shaking on the halyard.
- Fix: Cut L209, L243 and the first sentence of L291. Cut L269, or reduce it to a short behavioral beat. At L165, keep the boom beat and cut or shorten the "There it was" sentence. L385's "Their old disagreement stood intact." is optional to cut.

## Prose findings

### P1 · Minor · Unclear boat position at the launch

- Where: L29–L47, the public launch
- Text:
> Across the channel, Ambel stepped into the circuit boat.
> Levara stepped aboard.
- Problem: From Levara's side, "Across the channel" puts the boat at Farwake's wharf, yet Levara steps aboard without crossing. Only L391–L395 show that the boat lies across the narrow channel, bow to Farwake's side and stern to Holdfast's.
- Fix: Add one clause near L29 placing the boat in the channel between the two wharves, stern to Holdfast's quay and bow to Farwake's.

### P2 · Minor · Water-infrastructure credential (policy note)

- Where: L283, Levara's defense of Holdfast
- Text:
> "Holdfast stilled the surge against the south quay when the piles split.
- Problem: Under `universe/style-guide.md` "No default water infrastructure", a god saving harbor works from a surge reads as flood control and structural repair used as texture. The harbor and tide-god setting is the story's speculative core and is not a violation. Passing mentions ("channel stones", "breakwater", pilings for a smokehouse) are harmless as long as they aren't expanded.
- Fix: When editing D5, replace this deed with a mercy to people, such as a crew brought home or a body returned to kin, rather than another public-works rescue. Leave the other two items in her list.

## Continuity and prompt findings

### C1 · Minor · Object and staging slips (footwear, steering gear, bread)

- Where: L43 against L129; L231 (steering gear is a steering oar at L133, L139, L319 and L365); L229 against L113
- Text:
> Her sandal strap had worked loose.
> Levara felt the loose line tug against her boot.
> Ambel's hand tightened on the tiller.
> The bread had gone to paste in her mouth.
- Problem: Levara wears sandals at the launch and a boot at the harbor mouth. The boat is steered with a steering oar everywhere else, and at L185 Ambel is sitting on the opposite bench while they're tied at the bell, so "tiller" jars. She took the bread at L113, before the near-capsize and two stations earlier, and is never shown eating it, so bread in her mouth now reads as a slip rather than a figure of speech.
- Fix: Make the footwear consistent (keep the buckle beat). Change "tiller" to a hand on the bench or the steering oar. Either show her eating at the bell or make L229 plainly about a dry mouth.
