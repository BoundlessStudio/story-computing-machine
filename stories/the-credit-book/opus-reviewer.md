# Opus review: The Credit Book

Independent audit for a later editing pass. It is not a `review.md` verdict,
does not change canon state, and does not authorize edits by itself.

## Verdict

- Dialogue: REVISE — voices are distinct and nearly every exchange works, but the line that turns Orlena at L225 can be read two ways, and three small exchanges need a touch.
- Prose: PASS — concrete, varied and unshowy; one pronoun wobble at an emotional beat.
- Continuity and prompt: PASS — the prompt is delivered, including the verbatim line; about 3,020 words; the three-day timeline and the money hold; one staging slip in the opening scene.
- Edit scope: targeted-dialogue
- Existing review.md said: Verdict PASS, Dialogue PASS; mostly agree, but it missed the ambiguous pivot line.

This is one of the collection's better dialogue stories, and the edit pass
should treat it that way. It does not have the house voice: Orlena speaks in
chores, refusals and grudges ("Put the table back", "After the washing-up").
Lutrel over-explains and apologises. Brisken acts before he has finished
listening. Fodric retreats into terms. Dorbett needles. Correction ping-pong
appears only in the pie squabble, where the two old friends' bickering is the
point. The one material failure is narrow. At the decisive turn, "I want this
room too" can mean either the old cookshop or Dorbett's bakehouse, because
the story has used "room" three times for the bakehouse. The edit should be
surgical: make that line unambiguous, add one clause of ground under the
spice-stall exchange, and break up one mirrored retort. Do not smooth,
lengthen or "sharpen" anything else.

## Edit guardrails

- Keep the prompt's required line exactly as it stands at L35: “Find these people,” she said. “Tell them I need a favour.”
- Keep close third person, past tense, on Lutrel. Keep the length at 2,500–4,000 words (currently about 3,020), the broadly accessible, non-graphic treatment, and a resolved central promise.
- Keep the timeline intact. The notice gives possession at sundown the day after tomorrow. Brisken's cart comes at noon the next day. The move and the handover of the keys happen on the third day, and supper follows that evening.
- Keep the money logic. Fodric prepays eight bowls a day for ten days at Orlena's usual price, and Lutrel carries covered pots up to his shop. The free meals survive. Dorbett's rent is a little below the old rent, due monthly and in advance, with one month required instead of a quarter. The spice-stall coins and the family's small reserve help cover the first month.
- Keep the quarrel's facts. It was a card game three years ago, after thirty years of rough friendship. Dorbett said Orlena had counted a trick twice, and Orlena told her to empty her sleeves. The exchange over the key (L247–L251) and the plums at the end depend on them.
- The wharf and the merchant guild come from the prompt. Do not add any other water infrastructure (drains, sluices, canals and so on) as texture in revisions. No universe entries bind this story. Kelvra and all the named people are new, with no collisions.
- What works and must not be flattened: Orlena's near-silence in the first scene; Brisken's boots, from purchase to bleeding feet; Fodric moving Lutrel into the back room away from his customers; Dorbett's rent terms given as jabs; the key tug; the open ending mid-story.

## Dialogue findings

### D1 · Major · Pivot line's "room" is ambiguous

- Where: L223–L229, behind the counter of the old cookshop after "Put the table back", the exchange that changes Orlena's mind
- Text:
> Orlena had never let anyone plane them out.
> “I want this room too,” Lutrel said.
> The room was smaller than the cookshop and smelled of cold flour.
> “Nowhere yet. Gran, there's a room—”
> The room's near enough for everyone.
- Problem: Ground/referent at the decisive beat. The height-mark setup and "this" point to the old cookshop: I don't want to leave either. But the story has used "room" three times for Dorbett's bakehouse (L177, L203, and L215, four lines earlier), and it set "the room" against "the cookshop" at L177. The verb "want" also suits acquiring a new place better than losing an old one. A reader can take the line as shared grief for the old room or as Lutrel staking her own claim on the new one. These are different emotional arguments, and Orlena's reply fits both, so nothing resolves the ambiguity. The line that ends the story's main standoff should not need to be decoded.
- Fix: Choose the meaning the counter-mark beat sets up (Lutrel admitting she is losing this place too) and say it plainly, without "room" and without turning it into a summary of the theme. Keep Orlena's wordless thumb on the counter and "We could look. After the washing-up." exactly as they are. Leave L215–L221 alone; "I didn't ask you to" is a good reveal. Illustrative only: “I don't want to leave here either, Gran.”

### D2 · Minor · Spice-stall exchange lacks its ground

- Where: L85–L87 (the afternoon of calls), with follow-ups at L185, L271 and L299
- Text:
> Lutrel tried to explain that she had come to ask after her mother.
> “I understood,” the woman said.
> the spice woman's coins and their own small reserve
> The spice seller's daughter had come with a pot.
> Across the room the spice seller's daughter was watching her.
- Problem: Ground/knowledge and referent. "I understood" is a good line, but only if the reader knows the mother is gone. That is currently implied only by a shuttered stall and "held the book for a while". "Ask after her mother" also misstates Lutrel's errand: she came to ask a favour, not to enquire after someone's health, and her point is that she is not collecting (compare "It's not a bill." at L53). The woman is then called "the spice woman" at L185 but "the spice seller's daughter" at L271 and L299, so a reader can take her for a new character and miss what Orlena's nod at L299 means.
- Fix: Give one clause at L85 that makes the mother's death or absence legible, for example the woman saying briefly that her mother is gone or how long ago she died, and have Lutrel explain that she did not come for money. Keep "I understood" unchanged. Use one label for the woman throughout (L85, L185, L271, L299) so the final nod lands. Illustrative only: Lutrel tried to explain that she hadn't come for money.

### D3 · Minor · Mirrored retort in the eviction scene

- Where: L21–L23, the guild's man in the cookshop
- Text:
> “They won't renew at any price,” he said. “The carts need to come straight from the boats.”
> “Our customers come straight from the boats,” Lutrel said.
- Problem: Near–Punchline Patch. Lutrel's protest copies the syntax of his reason word for word, so it reads as an authored rejoinder rather than an upset young woman's objection. This is the collection's neat-comeback shape. His reply, “I know.”, is good and should stay.
- Fix: Keep the guild man's reason and his “I know. I'm sorry.” Break the mirror so that Lutrel's line points at the people in the room rather than echoing his sentence. Illustrative only: “Half the men in here walked straight off those boats.”

## Prose findings

### P1 · Minor · Ambiguous "she" at the leaving beat

- Where: L263–L265, the last look at the old shop
- Text:
> Orlena stood in the doorway until she was ready.
- Problem: The previous paragraph is Lutrel's hand on the cooling wall, so "she" could be Lutrel (Orlena waiting for her granddaughter) or Orlena herself. The reader cannot tell who is taking leave of the room at this quiet emotional beat.
- Fix: Name the person. If Orlena is waiting for Lutrel, which the preceding paragraph suggests, write "until Lutrel was ready". Keep the paragraph's two-sentence brevity.

## Continuity and prompt findings

### C1 · Minor · Third portion already sent, still unserved

- Where: L17–L19, the notice scene
- Text:
> Lutrel put down the onions she was peeling.
> three carters were sharing two portions of beans, and Orlena had already sent Lutrel to give them a third.
- Problem: The pluperfect says Lutrel has already been sent with a third portion. But the carters are still sharing two, and Lutrel is at the onions, talking to the guild's man. The timing contradicts itself in the opening image.
- Fix: Make the third portion pending ("had been about to send Lutrel over with a third") or put the bowl in Lutrel's hands. Keep the image; it establishes feeding people whether or not they can pay.

## Recurring patterns

- Neat, mirrored rejoinder: one material instance, L23 (D3). The rejoinders at L81 ("Day after's too late to start."), L195 and L305 have a similar shape, but they are earned by character and scene function. Leave them.
- "She" shared between two women at a key beat: L265 (P1). L91 and L147 resolve from context; leave them.
- "Room" as a referent: the three uses for the bakehouse (L177, L203, L215) collide with L225 (D1). Do not reintroduce "room" for the old cookshop in any revision.
- "Ask after": L85 and L145. If D2 rewrites L85, the echo goes away; L145 ("She asks after her.") is fine as it stands.
- Correction ping-pong: only L283–L289 (the pie). It is earned; see Preserve.

## Preserve

- The boots scene: Brisken starting to pull the new boot off when the book opens, the pepper memory, “I can carry your tables.” answering a request he didn't take up, and his feet bleeding on moving day.
> Used to bring me the pepper and then stand there till I sneezed.
- Orlena's "Did you find Brisken?" / "Well, then." answer to "no one important", done with a stool pushed against the backs of Lutrel's knees.
- Fodric's embarrassment. He moves her into the back room, says "I would have paid her", and ups the order unasked.
> “Eight. The lads downstairs eat too.”
- The "Whose?" / "Dorbett's." / "Put the table back." run, and Brisken's “We'll need it at the other end.”
- Dorbett's "Nine suppers. I was stranded near a month." / "Perhaps you paid for the others." / "Perhaps." Keep its quiet implication that the book undercounts.
- The key handover: neither of them lets go, then the card-sleeve exchange.
> “Yes. I was scratching.”
- The pie squabble (L283–L289) and Brisken's ruse to seat Orlena.
> “Come here. I can't reach the bread.”
- The ending: Brisken asks what they fought over, the plums Lutrel never heard about, and Orlena reaching for more crust. It is not an emblem and not an explanation, so do not add a closing line.

## Suggested edit order

1. D1: make the pivot line unambiguous first. It is the story's decisive exchange and the only Major.
2. D2: add the spice-stall ground and unify the woman's label. The final-scene nod at L299 depends on it.
3. C1: fix the opening staging while you are in the first scene.
4. D3: break up the mirrored retort in the same scene.
5. P1: name who is ready at L265.
