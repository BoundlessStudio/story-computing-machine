# Outline

## Story

- Premise and central promise: **The Credit Book.** Forty years of unpaid meals become an unruly campaign of favours when a merchant guild evicts Orlena's cookshop. Her adult granddaughter Lutrel finds enough help to reopen nearby, with the old customers still welcome and the grandmother finally receiving hospitality.
- Focal pressure or attachment: Lutrel grew up behind this counter. She wants to defeat the eviction without making her grandmother beg, and initially values offers by their ability to recover the familiar room. Orlena wants her customers fed but hates being fussed over.
- Counterforce or complication: The guild needs its purchased premises for storage and refuses renewal. Former customers have limited money, vanished addresses, inconvenient availability, and grievances. Orlena's quarrel with a retired caravan driver obstructs the most useful offer.
- POV, distance, and information limit: Close third past through Lutrel. Other people's hunger, prosperity, embarrassment, and intentions emerge from encounters; the book contains meal entries and identifying scraps, not omniscient biographies.
- Governing movement and time shape: Three days, from notice to reopening. A citywide search gradually converges on one crowded move. Expand three substantial customer encounters and the grandmother's resistant response; compress further visits. Target 2,500–4,000 words.
- Speculative rule or ordinary-world constraint: No operative magic. Private ownership makes eviction real; goodwill cannot cancel it. Prepaid meals supply an initial rent payment, modest rent remains due, and voluntary help moves the business. The alternative premises have an existing usable hearth and public access outside guild property.
- Dialogue promise: Calling in generosity produces affection, embarrassment, competitive helpfulness, old jokes, and unreasonable objections alongside tangible solidarity.
- Dialogic medium: Face-to-face requests, the handwritten credit book, relayed directions, shouted moving arguments, and an overlapping supper conversation.
- Dialogue engine: Lutrel's requests change as each person offers something unexpected. People recruit others, arrive before arrangements are settled, and disagree about what help means. Speaking to the estranged driver unlocks a real possibility; accepting it requires action from Orlena.

## Voice

- Narrative texture: Follow Lutrel's hurried bodily passage through stacked streets: hot bowls, worn stair edges, parcels under elbows, river glimpses between houses. Let forty years enter through awkward particulars rather than retrospective tribute.
- Conversational texture: Uneven visits, interrupted sales, names remembered halfway through a sentence, irrelevant complaints, and people talking across tasks. Ordinary requests can remain ordinary.
- Rhetorical ownership: Orlena owns culinary judgments and personal grudges, not moral wisdom. Brisken boasts and acts prematurely. Fodric can sell cloth more comfortably than discuss dependence. Dorbett enjoys Orlena's discomfort but listens when Lutrel stops making promises for her.
- Pressure behavior: Lutrel overexplains, then asks plainer questions. Orlena redirects concern into criticism. Brisken grows louder when his generosity causes inconvenience; Dorbett refuses to hurry an answer.
- Relationship movement: Lutrel needs help she fears will humiliate Orlena. Customers want their old place and recognition without compulsory gratitude. Dorbett wants a tenant and her friend back but cannot comfortably ask for the latter. Orlena must entrust a meal and a move to other people.
- Anti-default: Avoid a procession of secretly powerful debtors, an articulate committee allocating expertise, or a closing chorus explaining generosity. Let useful help retain foolishness, friction, and pleasure.

## Beats

1. **A deadline interrupts lunch.** Establish forty years of feeding dockworkers, caravan drivers, and penniless traders regardless of payment. With three days before possession, Orlena hands Lutrel the credit book and makes the prompt's request. Seed an old entry for Dorbett and Orlena's conspicuous reluctance to discuss it.
2. **The first favour is too enthusiastic.** Lutrel finds Brisken, an aging dockworker, shopping for boots. He has no spare wages but immediately recruits relatives to move things before his next shift. Lutrel wanted money or influence; he hears an urgent need for hands. His group later arrives while the cookshop still has nowhere to go.
3. **Prosperity is awkward to approach.** Fodric, once a penniless trader, now sells cloth above the main crossing. Old meal entries embarrass him in front of buyers. His proposed loan assumes a paying-only dining room; Lutrel cannot accept that change. A smaller advance for his employees' lunches remains possible, and he supplies directions to Dorbett.
4. **An offer with a history.** Dorbett, a retired caravan driver, owns a small former bakehouse a few minutes from the wharf on the public lower approach. Its hearth works, carts reach its door, and workers need no guild gate or steep ascent. She needs ordinary rental income. She and Orlena stopped speaking after a ferocious card-game quarrel; she offers the room, not an apology. Lutrel must visit it and ask concrete questions before committing.
5. **The favours collide.** Back among prematurely lifted benches, Orlena rejects Dorbett's room and keeps serving. Lutrel admits that no one can win the wharf back. Fodric's advance and smaller cash gifts cover the first rent; the terms leave later rent payable from trade while preserving free meals. Orlena visits the bakehouse herself. Her decision costs the familiar room and her claim never to need Dorbett again; neither woman settles the old quarrel.
6. **Move the customers too.** Use promised hands, borrowed carts, and badly timed arrivals in a vigorous moving sequence. Brisken's party carries furniture; others carry pots and bowls. Lutrel gives regulars directions and brings the last lunch customers along. Let the guild representative take the old keys without conversion or spectacle. The new room must become usable through the help actually secured.
7. **Receive the favour.** Customers contribute the first supper from their own pots and provisions; Orlena's attempts to supervise run into cheerful resistance and genuine culinary disagreements. She sits because others have undertaken the meal. End amid competing recollections, renewed teasing, and actual eating: the cookshop has reopened, hungry newcomers can enter, and nobody has become easier company. Leave wording and the final gesture to the writer.
