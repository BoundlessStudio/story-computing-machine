# Outline

## Story

- Premise and central promise: In an unnamed fairytale kingdom, nothing gets done because the crown's living grace of later turns every future-tense undertaking into literal postponement: its first action cannot begin before dawn, and each dawn advances it again while people still promise it. An ordinary cobbler must save leather and finished shoes beneath a roof awaiting royal repair. She gets this one job done through costly action now; the kingdom remains unfixed.
- Focal pressure or attachment: The day's shoe payment buys food, lamp oil, and another week's rent, while water already marks her best hides. Proud of keeping commitments, she answers danger by securing more promises—the habit the magic traps. Waiting preserves her repair guarantee but ruins what it should protect.
- Counterforce or complication: The grace once protected commoners from rulers demanding impossible work at once. Its living fairy administrator will not revoke that protection because officials exploit courteous delay. The cobbler is no roofer, and available tradespeople have their own work and risks. Canceling her claim frees present action but forfeits reimbursement and makes a failed patch hers alone.
- POV, distance, and information limit: Close first-person past through the unnamed cobbler, attentive to prices, seams, nail angles, damp, grip, and work order. She knows daily symptoms and folk explanations, not the grace's origin or palace use. The fairy can explain its own administration, never universal magic or unseen deliberations.
- Governing movement and time shape: One stormward day narrows from appointments to smaller physical acts: whole repair, single step, freely chosen motion. Keep the turn in the attic and on the low shop roof with few helpers. End after rain tests the patch and a customer pays, before official response or reform.
- Speculative rule or ordinary-world constraint: A small living royal fairy supplies the local magic; blue thread, seals, calendars, and bells merely carry it. A commitment framed for later blocks the first necessary action until dawn, then rolls forward unless withdrawn. Unpromised present action remains possible. Withdrawal voids the grace's guarantee and attached claim. Payments demanded as due now are exempt, producing an unfair but bounded asymmetry.

## Voice

- Narrative texture: Practical first-person attention to costs, handholds, wet materials, tool order, and lost time. Treat fairytale strangeness as working conditions; tighten syntax with the rain without making every beat a quip.
- Conversational texture: Keep work talk brief and various: price, measure, availability, liability, and the next physical need. Courtesy can survive; danger does not invite banter.
- Rhetorical ownership: The cobbler reasons through materials and tradeoffs; officials through passive process and future eligibility; tradespeople through bodily capacity and competing jobs; the fairy alone through promise, protection, withdrawal, and consent categories.
- Pressure behavior: The cobbler drops explanations for concrete requests, then risks blunt withdrawal. Officials add conditions; neighbors shorten or refuse; the fairy stays fluent but answers exact questions. Listening narrows unevenly.
- Anti-default: Avoid whimsical bureaucracy jokes and polished allegorical speeches. Make delay hurt through blocked hands, soaked leather, spent coins, and unsafe choices; coordinated labor, not debate or slogan, carries the turn.

## Beats

1. At dawn, a leak stains a finished order and curls an expensive hide. Her months-old roof token advances when she repeats the promised repair. The collector can still demand rent by sunset because payment is due now. Show the asymmetry through material consequence.
2. A compact round of dependencies fails: the clerk adds a future inspection, the slater's promised trowel pulls away from work, and the rope-maker cannot start tomorrow's length. Liability, another dangerous roof, and scarce hemp distinguish their concerns. Each assurance adds blue thread as the storm nears.
3. An accident exposes the limit when the slater catches falling slate without first promising help. The cobbler tests a small present request successfully, then gathers only available materials: salvaged slate, her leather underlay, pitch, and borrowed rope already in hand.
4. The fairy arrives along the threads when she touches work covered by her claim. In a pressured transaction, it confirms that the grace protects refusal of immediate compulsion, cannot judge neglect, and rejects renamed promises. Full withdrawal would free the repair and void its guarantee.
5. She cancels the claim without offering a substitute, spends rent money on slate, and cuts saleable leather for the patch. She recruits only a few people for discrete present acts, paying or exchanging value now. Some refuse or leave; this is limited cooperation, not conversion.
6. A gust and runoff expose a real error in load, seal, or footing. The cobbler applies her knowledge of overlap, tension, and waterproof seams while the slater corrects what does not transfer. The fairy enforces consent but neither helps nor obstructs unpromised work.
7. The roof still leaks into a basin after the downpour, but the stock and shoes remain dry. She completes enough of the order for payment; lost leather and surrendered guarantee keep the victory costly. Stop on her next ordinary, immediate task, with the kingdom unresolved.

## Continuity

- Canon used: Independent eras and systems are permitted; only life uses magic. The fairy is the agent, while thread, seals, calendars, and bells are nonliving conduits. An ordinary life need not advertise wider cosmology.
- Boundaries and unknowns: This is not Ersene, the Crowned Ancestors' kingdom, or another established royal system. The grace is local, not universal promise, language, time, contract, or consent magic. Its origin, palace politics, and eventual fate stay unknown. No recurring nouns are proposed.
