# Outline

## Story

- Premise and central promise: In an unnamed fairytale kingdom, any spoken promise to do a task later attracts a living later-moth, which magically cocoons one idle, portable thing the speaker will need to finish it. Generations of soothing promises have filled rafters with tools, buckles, hinges, and half the kingdom's useful small parts. Della, a common rush-chair mender, needs the balance on one high-backed chair to pay her workshop rent by the final bell, but she has protected six commissions by promising all of them soon and has therefore made none finishable. Fulfill the comic frustration by letting her attempts to appear dependable worsen the infestation until she risks five future payments, names what she cannot do, recovers enough tools to finish one chair now, and keeps her shop without curing or converting the kingdom.
- Intended reader experience: A warm, wry squeeze in which each reasonable reassurance produces a fresh practical absurdity. Let laughter give way to recognition of the shame beneath overpromising, then to modest relief: Della does not become prosperous or perfectly disciplined, but does one honest day's work and earns room for the next.
- Generating force:
  - Mode: Plot-led recursive errand spiral. Each attempt to replace a cocooned prerequisite requires a favor, each promised repayment removes another prerequisite, and the spiral reverses only as Della contracts many claimed futures into one chosen present task.
  - Focal attachment or contradiction: Della's livelihood depends on customers believing she will finish their work; the reassuring future tense she uses to preserve that belief is precisely what prevents completion. She is attached both to the workshop as her one secure room and to being thought reliable by people whose coin she genuinely needs.
  - Plot-led wants and useful limit, if applicable: Della immediately wants to finish the apothecary's high-backed chair, collect its balance, and pay rent before the final bell. She also wants to retain five other commissions and their future income rather than admit that her capacity is smaller than her ledger. Her useful flaw is reflexive reassurance: when ashamed or cornered, she promises before counting time, materials, or moths.
- Counterforce or complication:
  - Source: The later-moths' bounded feeding and nesting behavior, Della's real poverty, and a lane full of other workers who cannot indefinitely lend tools or accept promises because their own incomplete jobs and bills depend on them. A rent collector will accept coin or completed useful work but cannot pass Della's assurance to the granary that employs him.
  - Credible inside logic, if a person: No antagonist drives the story. Customers need the objects they paid for; lenders need their tools back; the collector has an account of his own to close. Each may take unfinished work elsewhere or decline another delay without cruelty, and none is obliged to rescue Della from commitments she chose.
  - Pressure or change created: A later-moth can hear a sincere future commitment within a room and carry off one unattended portable prerequisite; it cannot take living beings, a tool already in active use, or anything too heavy to fly, and it releases its object when the promise is fulfilled or explicitly withdrawn. Della cannot seize a cocoon without the moth carrying it deeper into the colony. Every promise therefore buys social breathing room by making the practical bottleneck worse.
- Narrative design:
  - POV, tense, distance, and limitation: Close first-person past through Della, intimate with her tactile trade knowledge, quick arithmetic, hunger, and talent for making a delay sound small. She is reliable about what wood, rush, coin, and later-moths do but evasive about how long she has known the ledger is impossible. Keep the moths' inner experience unknowable; observed appetite and behavior do not make them moral judges.
  - Time shape and compression: One rent day from first light to the final bell. Render the first theft, the widening loan-and-promise circuit, the failed loft retrieval, Della's withdrawals, and the final chair work in scene; compress repeated errands through a tightening inventory of what each new promise has removed.
  - Information and reveal strategy: Demonstrate the moth rule on the first page and treat it as ordinary local knowledge, not a late cosmological explanation. Let Della initially frame one stolen binding knife as bad luck, then reveal the six jobs, their sealed deposits, and the extent of her knowingly impossible assurances as customers arrive. The turn is not discovering a loophole but accepting the familiar cost of withdrawal: lost work, embarrassment, and returned deposits.
  - Governing movement: Begin at one nearly finished chair; expand through a circuit of borrowed knife, cord, awl, and favors along the adjoining trade lane; return to a shop made less workable by every reassurance; climb into the crowded moth loft and fail to force an ending; come down to the ledger; retract or renegotiate five commitments one at a time; receive released tools amid customers' varied, unconverted responses; narrow attention to the one chair that can pay today's rent; work without promising an outcome; deliver, pay, eat, and assess the next object before naming a date.
- Speculative surplus: Empty later-moth cocoons smell of cedar and repel ordinary cloth moths, so dyers and wool sellers buy clean husks for cupboards while cursing the living colony. This minor household trade explains why the creatures are tolerated and gives the lane a practical fairy ecology, but it cannot release an object, satisfy a promise, or solve Della's rent.
- Structural distinction: A comic outward-and-homeward spiral in which accumulating absences, rather than escalating spectacle, create movement. The decisive action happens over a private ledger and then at a workbench, not in a court, public emergency, technical demonstration, crowd conversion, institutional rewrite, or ceremonial reform; the kingdom remains frustratingly itself.
- Decisive turn, deepening, or recognition: In the loft Della sees the newly cocooned rush bundle beside years of other people's indispensable last pieces and starts to promise the waiting collector that everything will be ready by noon. She stops mid-formula, climbs down, opens the cup holding unspent deposits, and tells the first waiting customer exactly what she cannot finish. She returns two deposits and offers the other three their unfinished work with no deadline attached. Anger, relief, and indifference replace the single disaster she imagined; as each promise is withdrawn, moths drop the corresponding tools. Della chooses the chair whose completion can meet today's need and begins before asking anyone to believe in it.
- Aftereffect or live uncertainty: The chair's balance covers the rent and a plain supper, not the income Della lost by releasing five jobs. Two customers leave, one takes work home, and two leave their pieces without promised dates. Moths still occupy the rafters, the kingdom still runs on deferral, and Della may relapse under the next lean week; what changes is her usable method for distinguishing present work from imaginary capacity.
- Opening / ending relation: Open with Della answering “later today” before inspecting what the first moth has taken. End after rent and supper, when a new customer asks when a broken stool can be ready; Della says she does not know, brings it to the bench, and starts by finding out what is broken. The repeated question moves from reassurance without attention to attention without a promise.

## Beats

1. At first light, the rent collector reminds Della that coin or completed work is due at the final bell. The apothecary's high-backed rush chair needs only binding and delivery, so Della promises it for later that day; a blue-grey later-moth immediately cocoons her binding knife under the eaves.
2. Della substitutes a kitchen knife, then crosses the trade lane seeking an edge, cord, and an awl. To secure each favor she promises a return, repair, or exchange “as soon as this chair is done”; moths successively remove the whetstone, cord shuttle, and awl haft she needs next. Other shops display the same almost-finished adaptations, making the kingdom's paralysis comic and materially ordinary.
3. Back at the workshop, five customers arrive for the commissions Della has kept visually near completion. She gives each a soothing future answer and a fresh moth joins the rafters. The reader sees the full ledger and the sealed deposits she has preserved but cannot honestly count as rent.
4. Della climbs into the loft with a broom and tries to knock down the cocoons. The living colony shifts the needed pieces deeper; she finds old hinges, spoons, cobbler's lasts, and her own newly stolen rush bundle, a domestic archaeology of promises rather than evidence of a conspiracy.
5. The collector returns before the bell and explains that he can take the finished chair at its agreed value but cannot carry another person's future claim into his grain account. Della nearly answers with a larger promise, hears the moths stir, and stops.
6. At the ledger, Della explicitly withdraws five deadlines. She returns two deposits, gives three customers the choice to remove their work or leave it without a date, and does not defend herself when two walk away. Released tools patter down from the rafters; nobody applauds or adopts her practice.
7. Della selects the apothecary's chair because its remaining work fits the hours and its balance fits the rent. She binds the rush, trims the underside, tests every joint, and delivers it without predicting success; the collector credits the completed object and closes today's account.
8. After paying rent and buying a plain supper, Della returns to fewer commissions and the same rustling loft. A customer brings a broken stool. Asked for a date, Della inspects the split first and leaves the answer open as she begins the next piece of present work.

## Continuity

- Canon used: The story is an independent entry point in the one deep-time physical world. Only living later-moths use the local magic; cocoons merely hold what the moths place in them. The mechanism is story-specific rather than universal word-, promise-, desire-, or cost-based magic, and the kingdom may understand it incompletely.
- Boundaries and unknowns: The fairytale kingdom, era, trade lane, and all supporting role characters remain unnamed. No connection is established to Ersene, the Crown-era unnamed kingdom, Sablewake's Covenant of Hours, the Fenwick watch, or any other royal or temporal system. The later-moths are non-person-like magical animals; their origin, wider range, life cycle, and capacity beyond the observed local behavior remain unresolved. Ordinary deposits, ledgers, rent, and spoken agreements retain ordinary social and economic force; they do not themselves think, enforce truth, or use magic.
