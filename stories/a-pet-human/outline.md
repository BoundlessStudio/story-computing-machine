# Outline

## Story

- Premise and central promise: **A Pet Human.** A stolen sacrifice lands in a demon child's birthday presents. His attempts to become an intolerable pet make him increasingly desirable, until he recruits his delighted keeper into arranging a spectacular return. Let pet care keep generating trouble after the opening reversal.
- Focal pressure or attachment: Caff, an adult fairground barker between engagements, wants his own bed, his confiscated boots, and the satisfaction of making the men who abducted him look ridiculous. His vanity and taste for being indulged complicate his sensible intention to flee immediately.
- Counterforce or complication: Nimmet wants an astonishing pet and considers almost everything Caff does an achievement. Her mother Mavrix accepted the offered human without asking him and finds his wishes inconvenient. The priest who supplied him expects a reward at the evening reopening; returning to that man's knife is not safety.
- POV, distance, and information limit: First-person past through Caff, close to immediate embarrassment and opportunism. His showman's salesmanship colors explanations; demonstrate the moments when his boasts exceed his nerve. Other people's intentions must become legible through behavior and speech.
- Governing movement and time shape: One birthday afternoon and evening. An opening fright becomes domestic comic escalation, then a conspiratorial outing through the same threshold. End shortly after his return, with the relationship's continuing nuisance materially evident. Target prose: 2,500–4,000 words.
- Speculative rule or ordinary-world constraint: Mavrix's living claws and breath open one doorway between her receiving hearth and the priest's prepared altar. Once closed, it can reopen when the priest renews his evening call; she must sustain it. Nimmet cannot make crossings. Ordinary ropes, bells, furniture, and written ritual instructions have no independent magic. Demons are physically formidable living people; this household's habits establish no species-wide law.
- Dialogue promise: An indignant adult must get through to an exuberant child whose pet vocabulary keeps turning his objections into compliments, while each discovers useful weaknesses in the other.
- Dialogic medium: Speech, hand signals invented during pet training, conspicuous disobedience, and the priest's audible calls through the opening.
- Dialogue engine: Misunderstanding has observable uptake and gets corrected when necessary. Requests produce unsuitable gifts; bad-pet demonstrations become prized tricks. Caff and Nimmet eventually cooperate through a showman's routine whose outward appearance misleads the priest, without requiring a lecture about ownership.

## Voice

- Narrative texture: Caff notices prices, edible things, clothes, and the exact dignity an indignity costs him. Vary expansive salesmanship with plain frightened perception; leave physical jokes space to arrive.
- Conversational texture: Child interruptions, literal follow-up, adult impatience, failed threats, ordinary feeding questions, and a few satisfying stage cues. Affection is intrusive before it is welcome.
- Rhetorical ownership: Caff owns patter and opportunistic reframing. Nimmet owns delighted insistence, odd priorities, and serious play. Mavrix owns domestic authority and dry dismissal. The priest owns rehearsed grandeur that becomes flustered bargaining.
- Pressure behavior: Caff talks too much when afraid but loses words near an actual knife. Nimmet repeats practical wants and becomes inventive when thwarted. Mavrix gets quieter; the priest abandons ceremony.
- Relationship movement: Caff wants release and cannot admit enjoying an audience. Nimmet wants a companion she can boast about and resists losing him. Their exchanges convert one-sided handling into mischievous cooperation without making either suddenly selfless. Mavrix must deal with the nuisance she acquired.
- Anti-default: Do not prove Caff deserves freedom by repairing a demon machine, saving the child, or delivering a model argument about personhood. Preserve his appetite for petty revenge.

## Beats

1. Bound and shoeless, Caff is offered alive by a priest who took his boots. Mavrix's enormous hand drags him through the altar opening before the knife can fall. Nimmet exclaims exactly: "A pet human! Thanks Mother!" Birthday wrappings and inappropriate pet equipment replace expected torture.
2. Caff protests clearly. Nimmet immediately understands that he talks but misjudges his needs, offering food, a sleeping basket, and a bell. Mavrix treats his request to go home as a tiresome return demand. Establish the evening opening and its priest-side danger early; there is a real way home, with somebody hostile waiting there.
3. Caff tries disobedience and expensive demands to get himself returned. Nimmet adores his insults, feeds him too much, and treats his refusal to fetch as sophistication. Give at least one genuine awkward pause or failed joke. Her physical handling is carelessly frightening; he objects to specific actions and she changes them rather than remaining conveniently deaf to clear speech.
4. Pet training reverses direction. Caff uses applause, treats, and a barker's gestures to get Nimmet to move, sit, and roar; she thinks they have invented an excellent double act. His attraction to the child's uncritical delight becomes visible through over-elaboration. They build a plan for his return using her wish to exhibit him and his need for protection from the priest.
5. At the evening call, the priest expects supernatural payment. Mavrix opens the doorway and Nimmet insists on a brief demonstration on the human side. Keep Mavrix sustaining the opening and observing throughout. Caff arrives apparently collared and obedient, letting his captor mistake his condition for helplessness.
6. The routine turns into Caff's revenge: he signals Nimmet's alarming tricks, uses her presence to reclaim his boots and the priest's ceremonial finery, and exposes the supposed recipient of a sacred sacrifice as an enthusiastic child. Threat remains non-graphic. Nobody reforms through a speech; the priest's immediate priorities change under pressure.
7. Resolve Caff's freedom explicitly before closing the doorway. Nimmet dislikes parting, but a housebound human has proved far less interesting than an accomplice with his own territory. Mavrix has practical reasons to stop the game before it grows. Allow an imperfect, concrete arrangement for a future visit, then end on its comic consequence rather than an explanatory moral or renewed performance.
