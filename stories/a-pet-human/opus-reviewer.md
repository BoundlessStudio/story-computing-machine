This story is a counterexample to the collection's usual dialogue problem. The
voices don't converge on the house register. Nimmet talks in exclamatory,
literal child logic ("He doesn't know it's my birthday", "Did he brush you too
hard?"). Mavrix is sparse and imperious, never uses Caff's name, and gives her
daughter one-word orders ("Nimmet. Slowly." / "Nimmet. My arms."). Caff mixes a
barker's courtesy ("Madam", "Splendid", "Ladies and gentlemen") with deadpan.
The priest never finishes a formula. Exchanges run on misreadings that the scene
registers (the brush offered for biting, "He'll be there" answered by a
wait), not on correction ping-pong. Each major exchange changes something:
Caff goes from pet to trainer to accomplice to a friend saying goodbye. His
fluency breaks where it should, at the knife (L369). The edit pass should only
fix two unclear referents in Mavrix's short replies, one attribution problem in
the roar scene, and two small wording slips. It should not polish the deadpan
lines or add explanations of what the action already shows.

## Dialogue findings

### D1 · Minor · Mavrix's first reply has no clear antecedent

- Where: L41–L43, just after arrival, Nimmet thanks her mother
- Text:
> “You didn't tell me,” the child said, jumping hard enough to make the chandelier shed ash.
> “I hadn't intended to. It was offered.”
- Problem: Ground/referent. The only verb phrase "intended to" can pick up is "tell me," so the line reads literally as "I hadn't intended to tell you." That makes the human a planned surprise, and "It was offered" says the opposite. The intended meaning is "this wasn't planned; the priest offered him," and the reader has to guess it. The line sets out Mavrix's motive for keeping Caff, so it should be clear.
- Fix: Make Mavrix's first sentence answer "You didn't tell me" directly, and keep "It was offered." Illustrative only: “I didn't plan it. It was offered.”

### D2 · Minor · "You're hurting" says the wrong thing

- Where: L63, Nimmet lifts Caff under the arms
- Text:
> “Put me down. You're hurting.”
- Problem: Read literally, "You're hurting" with no object means Nimmet is in pain. Context rescues it, since she drops him, but the wording is a slip.
- Fix: Add the object: “Put me down. You're hurting me.” Keep "She dropped me." and "Less quickly" exactly as they are.

### D3 · Minor · Roar aftermath: speaker and staging unclear

- Where: L249–L261, the first roar while Mavrix changes in the next room
- Text:
> I looked at Mavrix, who had gone to the adjoining room to change for the evening.
> “Nimmet.” “That was little!” “I heard it.”
> We stayed where we were until Mavrix's sleeve withdrew from the doorway.
- Problem: Attribution. "Nimmet." and "I heard it." have no tags. Caff spoke last, and he is also a natural candidate for "I heard it," so the reader only learns that both lines were Mavrix's from the sleeve at L261. That is after the joke, whose point is that she heard it from the next room. The staging line at L249 also has Caff looking "at" someone who has left the room.
- Fix: Put a tag or location on the first "Nimmet." so the voice clearly comes from the adjoining room. "I heard it." can then stay untagged. Change L249 to looking toward the doorway, where her sleeve shows. Illustrative only: “Nimmet,” said the voice beside the mirror.

### D4 · Minor · "What he told me" points to the wrong man

- Where: L311–L313, Nimmet asks her mother to take Caff home safely
- Text:
> “We have to take Caff home properly. The man there kills humans.”
> “I remember what he told me, Nimmet.”
- Problem: Ground/referent. The nearest "he" is "the man," the priest, who told Mavrix that Caff was willing. The intended "he" is Caff, who told her at L73 that he was nearly killed. Either reading ends at "I already know," so the reply still follows, but at this plan-making beat the reader has to stop and pick one.
- Fix: Name the source in Mavrix's own terms. She never uses Caff's name, so keep it that way. Illustrative only: “I heard the human, Nimmet.”

## Prose findings

### P1 · Minor · Cloth-and-shears wording slip

- Where: L191, eating from the cupboard
- Text:
> When I wiped my fingers, she handed me the cloth she had intended to shave me on.
- Problem: The order is backwards: he wipes his fingers first and gets the cloth afterward. "Shave" also doesn't match the props at L159, where the moustache was threatened with shears, not a razor.
- Fix: Put the reach for the cloth before the wipe, and use "trim" or tie the cloth to the shears. Illustrative only: When I looked for something to wipe my fingers on, she handed me the cloth she had spread for the shears.
