# Provisional outline

The explicit promise is the speaker's correction: they cannot raise the dead; they are a "Mech Romancer." I infer playful genre collision from "No, buddy" and "genres mixed up," and expect the alternative specialty to matter beyond the pun. Romance, however interpreted, should produce an actual romantic pleasure. No setting, scale, central relationship, ending, or particular definition of romancing is specified. My choice is a buoyant matchmaking adventure whose enormous machines have enormous, rather particular feelings.

## Chosen direction, open to discovery

First-person Bessa Nix is a professional matchmaker for sentient mechs. Sir Talven, a junior knight sent to hire reinforcements, needs an army of dead knights raised before an enemy colossus reaches his border town. Her correction delivers the prompt. His next proposal is to have her seduce the enemy weapon; she counters that she makes introductions, and that he has a perfectly eligible colossus of his own. Talven becomes her informed helper; his superior, the town's commander, is a separate character. No love spell, resurrection, or compulsory attraction: her tools are individual judgment, introductions, and brave practical arrangements. These limitations and relationships are writer choices, not requirements.

- At Talven's hurriedly prepared circle of skulls, Bessa unpacks a book of prospective matches instead of a grimoire. Magnificence, the town's long-retired parade champion and emergency guardian, objects to the knight's flattering account of his measurements. Bessa asks what he would actually enjoy with a companion his size. This vain, exacting giant wants to dance, rather than demonstrate marching turns to humans again. He has never met the approaching enemy mech.
- Bessa goes out under a white flag to investigate a possible match. Tenderness is a blunt siege chassis with broad stabilizing feet. Amid genuine preparations to attack, she is privately building a surprisingly delicate flower the size of a house from scrap. Bessa discovers that the sculptor would like company but assumes her formidable body makes her an embarrassing dance partner. The flower was intended for nobody; Bessa's questions create a new possibility. She offers each mech a truthful account of the other, and they separately accept an introduction.
- Bessa gets the town's commander and the invading commander to permit an encounter on the abandoned tournament ground between their armies. They assume she has arranged single combat to settle the town's fate; both mechs and Talven know it is a date. Clearing the lists and keeping troops outside them are practical conditions she insists upon. The commanders' expectation that one mech must be defeated remains a threat during the comic preparations. Talven helps clear the date ground instead of insisting on his original hire's worth.
- Tenderness brings the enormous flower, giving Magnificence something specific to admire beyond her military reputation. Their bodies make formal etiquette ridiculous. When she admits her knees cannot manage his precise dance steps, he asks her to lead. She turns around her planted stabilizers; he invents steps to follow her. They discover a shared, enjoyable movement neither could perform alone. This is the romantic turn, rather than Bessa explaining affection or forwarding an existing crush.
- The commanders attempt to stop the dance and resume the battle. The mechs choose to continue together. Using the coordination they have just discovered, they disarm the interruption without harming the troops; the invading force loses its heavy support and withdraws. The couple step over the armies toward a second date somewhere with more room. Matchmaking has changed this threatened attack, without a claim that romance abolishes all war.
- Bessa sends her invoice to both commanders; Talven may still want a necromancer for the bill. Then the last pleasure belongs to the couple: the huge flower passed hand to hand and the promise of the next dance.
