# Provisional outline

The explicit promise is the speaker's correction: they cannot raise the dead; they are a "Mech Romancer." I infer playful genre collision from "No, buddy" and "genres mixed up," and expect the alternative specialty to matter beyond the pun. Romance, however interpreted, should produce an actual romantic pleasure. No setting, scale, central relationship, ending, or particular definition of romancing is specified. My choice is a buoyant matchmaking adventure whose enormous machines have enormous, rather particular feelings.

## Chosen direction, open to discovery

First-person Bessa Nix is a professional matchmaker for sentient mechs. Sir Talven needs an army of dead knights raised before an enemy colossus reaches his border town. Her correction delivers the prompt. His next proposal is to have her seduce the enemy weapon; she counters that she makes introductions, and that he has a perfectly eligible colossus of his own. No love spell, resurrection, or compulsory attraction: her tools are knowledge, introductions, and brave practical arrangements. These limitations and relationships are writer choices, not requirements.

- At Talven's hurriedly prepared circle of skulls, Bessa unpacks a book of prospective matches instead of a grimoire. The town's mothballed guardian, Magnificence, immediately objects to her profile's flattering measurements. The knight's desperate wrong hire turns into a possible introduction.
- Bessa goes out under a white flag to meet the approaching enemy mech, Tenderness. Its apparent siege preparation is the construction of a flower the size of a house, intended for Magnificence. The mechs have noticed one another across past campaigns, but their commanders interpret every approach as combat. Each has an attraction and a particular wish for the meeting; Bessa must arrange a place and means for them to speak directly, beyond the commanders' shouted orders.
- On the broad abandoned tournament ground between the armies, Bessa stages their first date. Human-scale etiquette becomes joyful physical difficulty: a corsage requires Talven's entire horse, and a dance needs the lists cleared. The mechs' own awkward, funny exchange should supply the emotional turn, not Bessa explaining affection to them.
- Both commanders try to reclaim their weapons when the dance begins. The machines choose the date. Acting together, they disarm the attempted interruption without harming the troops, step over both armies, and leave for a second date somewhere with more room. Their coordinated exit is the romantic and action climax; matchmaking changes the threatened battle rather than decorating it. The border town survives this attack, without a claim that romance abolishes all war.
- Bessa sends her invoice to both commanders for introducing their most valuable assets. The last pleasure belongs to the couple: a huge flower passed hand to hand and the promise of the next dance. The knight may still want a necromancer for the bill.
