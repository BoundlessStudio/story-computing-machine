# What the Iron Takes

Provisional direction: close third person through Sivet, a veteran scavenger; roughly 3,500 words over one day. Her adult daughter Anka has bought passage on a trading caravan. Sivet is supposedly repairing Anka's handcart but has quietly removed its axle, obstructing departure with Anka's possessions without admitting she wants Anka to stay. Anka has plans and salvage skill of her own, and suspects the delay.

At dawn, while the two strip a buried wheel housing for a saleable bronze part, an inner engine begins turning. A concealed source of power remains unexplained, but its immediate action is visible: a great magnetic rotor gathers speed and pulls ferrous metal toward it. Bronze, wood and flesh are unaffected; iron objects tremble before they slide or fly. The pull comes in pulses, increasingly strong and close together. During the early lulls, iron drops within reach of the hulk. Their generation has made its ordinary life from this iron. The field drags the concealed axle out of Sivet's roof before it takes the roof ties, exposing her lie without a confession scene. It cannot distinguish a cherished object from scrap.

The engine belongs to a monumental wheeled machine still held down by an old cable running to an overturned wreck. Fresh trenches and repeated outward lurches against the cable show its wheels seeking a route away from the settlement; its future steering remains unknown. There is no awakened voice, readable enemy, or complete account of the war. Sivet correctly sees that keeping it tethered keeps their iron recoverable, and stopping the rotor would preserve an opened engine worth a lifetime of salvage. She uses a brake method that works on dead machinery. The powered rotor breaks it. Anka wants to release the cable's coupling immediately; Sivet stops her because it is still under lethal tension. Anka's urgency and Sivet's attachment each contain useful judgment and an error.

As the safe lulls shrink, Sivet gives up retrieval and helps Anka wedge a powered wheel during a pause, briefly unloading the coupling. Anka approaches without iron and drives out its keeper with a bronze drift. Releasing the cable lets the machine carry its dangerous field away, but surrenders both recoverable household iron and the extraordinary salvage they could otherwise keep. They act together without resolving their quarrel into instant forgiveness. The engine travels into the waste, hauling a ragged train of scrap.

Afterward, Anka still takes her paid passage on the caravan from the far ridge, carrying the reduced belongings she can manage without the cart. Sivet gives her a wooden tool that cannot be recalled. Their parting happens in the metal-stripped home, with the far-off engine still moving. The ending should hold the daughter's chosen departure and the mother's reduced, continuing life without a speech or a new communal maintenance ritual.
