# Provisional direction

A warm, lightly comic story told close to a customer whose subtitle work sends her to the bookstore/cafe at irregular hours. She first likes the apparent manager because they can disagree about a book without ending the conversation. Each visit seems to resume it; the shop's online voice, **rgbknights**, is the same friendly, opinionated presence. A small knight emblem and unobtrusive red, green, and blue details belong to the shop and its people rather than announcing a puzzle key.

Let the handoffs be pleasurable before they become suspect: a mug lands where she expects, her held book has moved from one side of the till to the other, and the manager returns from the stockroom just as an oven timer sounds. Then details fail to match. A nick on a finger disappears. A task changes hands. An apparently settled opinion about the book comes back with a different reservation. The customer's hours change for reasons already in her life; she notices during ordinary visits, without following anyone or setting traps. Customers with steadier routines continue to see a single indefatigable manager.

The curiosity becomes personal when she comes back to finish a conversation that mattered to her and receives a helpful answer that is almost right. The triplets' shared memory can carry orders and jokes; it cannot make every lived exchange belong equally to all three. She asks a direct question instead of producing a case against them. Their eventual invitation to meet all three comes from their own decision to let a regular know them. The secret began as a shared childhood knight identity and became a useful, enjoyable public voice, with a private joke about being the manager who never sleeps. Give the siblings overlapping tastes and disagreements, including one disagreement among themselves; do not assign each a single colour-coded temperament.

A possible ending: the unfinished book argument continues with the three siblings briefly together around one cafe table while the shop is quiet. The customer can finally address the person whose particular answer she wanted, then discover that another sibling has the next question. The shared rgbknights account and the seamless counter handoffs continue. The change is in whom she can recognize and speak to, not in a new shop sign or a public explanation. The last beat should be an ordinary, surprising contribution to the conversation rather than a summary of what three colours mean.

The generating attachment is pleasure in a familiar voice; the pressure is wanting a particular conversation with a particular person inside that voice. Protect the small details, competence, and affection in the premise. The first safe version to avoid is an observant customer catching fraud and teaching the triplets to be individuals. This outline is provisional; the prose may find a sharper reason for the unfinished conversation or a better final encounter.
