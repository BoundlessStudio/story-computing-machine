# Editorial memory

## 2026-10-03 — direction and independent outline readings

The prompt is preserved verbatim. The working direction, *The Second Door*, follows an amateur stage magician and a former performing partner; newly acquired powers complicate an existing shared life rather than cause DID. General is the starting intensity. The portrayal was checked against the [NHS description of dissociative disorders](https://www.nhs.uk/mental-health/conditions/dissociative-disorders/) and the [ISSTD public-resource index](https://www.isst-d.org/publications-resources/public-resources/trauma-dissociation-public-fact-sheets-support-sheets/): distinct identities and substantial everyday memory gaps belong to DID, while individual experiences differ. These references support restraint in portrayal, not the fictional powers or a universal account of experience. No supplied images or shared-world additions.

Before discussion, the independent Codex outline reader wrote: “The strongest possibility to protect is the concealed side exit,” but warned that selling the cabinet could “purchase the moral conclusion while the shared life remains narratively organised around Della’s career.” The reader also identified Amos’s garden wish as impossible under the proposed hand-on-handle rule and asked for a clear account of the long rehearsal return versus the short rebuilt route. Claude’s independent outline reading called the parallel between Sahar’s assistant status and the identities’ proposed backstage labour “the thing to protect.” Its principal objection was that Della’s surrender of money and equipment was “a concession rather than an act”; it also asked whether real magic and June remained consequential, whether Sahar already knew Amos and June, and whether stacked financial losses would become punitive. These are the readers’ first effects, before they saw one another’s findings.

The outline round-robin exchanged the readers’ actual accounts and the writer’s replies. Codex challenged a required performance cue for June (“an action with an effect, not necessarily a required performance cue”) and a desire-motivated switch that could imply control or blame Amos for the failed design. The writer agreed; Claude conceded both suggestions. June now applies for her own singing audition and refuses the trio billing. Della initiates renegotiation before performance; the cabinet sale repays one advance rather than stacking punishment. Amos’s inability to enter his garden is retained, with his own choice of contact changing the final event. Claude’s revised reading said the garden limit “may be the story’s most distinctive image.” Both readers tested the saved revised outline. Codex stressed that a power-independent apparatus cannot guarantee everyone wants or knows how to perform; Claude required an explicit order keeping Sahar clear of the garden threshold and a definite answer about cabinet ownership. The coordinator accepted the direction for drafting, with those staging and agency questions carried into scene execution. Sahar’s craft must remain her contribution; no extra feat is demanded from Della merely to make her sacrifice active. The package and branch were renamed for the chosen working title.
