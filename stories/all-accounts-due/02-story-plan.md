# Story plan — All Accounts Due

## Story promise and standalone mandate

On the first Open Day of 606 Common Tally, the world gathers for the Bearing Census, a twelve-year inventory of powers on which several civilizations have depended for centuries. Faris, a lifelong nonmagical route auditor, has spent eighteen years helping people through failures their magic could not conveniently solve. She has recorded every favor as an account she might one day collect. At dawn, every magical capacity in the present material world becomes zero forever.

In Drenovar, hub of the mundane Copper Relay, Faris must ask former practitioners, a communal engineer, a nonhuman water navigator, and a newly powerless former god to choose again. Their irreversible decision will determine whether remaining water, seed, pollinators, medical knowledge, physical routes, and public records are divided among many communities or enclosed for one city's survival. Magic never returns; no one saves the old world. They decide whether a first nonmagical world can begin.

This plan completely replaces both rejected versions. Other than Faris, nothing survives from their cast, locations, histories, institutions, systems, artifacts, imagery, relationships, rituals, or scene designs. The story is wholly standalone and uses no name, cameo, reference, altered name, distinctive mechanism, or indirect continuation from another repository story.

## Controls, chronology, and ontology

- **Target:** 4,500–6,000 words; scene budgets total about 5,400.
- **POV/tense:** close third person, past tense, fixed on Faris. One brief final prolepsis may widen narratorial distance without entering another mind.
- **Genre/tone:** epic and elegiac terminal-event fantasy; practical work carries planetary stakes. Teen ceiling: non-graphic deaths, ecological loss, panic, and displacement.
- **Private continuity placement:** a new terminal era after every admitted material-world history in which magic remains active. This is backstage only; prose names or alludes to no prior repository era.
- **Original calendar:** Common Tally years contain ten thirty-six-day spans and five Open Days outside the spans. The story begins on the first Open Day of 606. The Bearing Census has occurred every twelve years for 312 years; communities publicly state capacity, dependencies, and promised emergency output.
- **Story history:** kilnvoice has shaped Voln Plateau construction for 970 years; nerethi–pluun brining has sustained Aruq Saltlands settlements for at least 1,400 recorded years; Drenovar's wheelshare Turnworks have run for 412 years; Brameth Caum has held worship, power, and agricultural authority for 233 years; the nonmagical Copper Relay has linked distant continents for 112 years.
- **Terminal boundary:** every living material-world user loses every magical faculty permanently. Earlier acts, bodies, scars, relationships, and records remain real. The event makes no claim about earlier time, alternate histories, other worlds or planes, the universal afterlife, deceased continuing persons, or destinations beyond this material world.
- **Evidence and permanence:** the narrative frame authoritatively establishes worldwide finality. Characters receive independent reports through the crank-generated, copper-wire Copper Relay, signal towers, ships, and later couriers. Their evidence supports global present reach, not experimental proof of eternity or one common cause.
- **Maximum mana:** only Drenovar's administrative translation for a living user's presently available magical ceiling. Faris's card has always read zero. Other cultures report “no pitch,” “no pairing,” “no share,” or “no call”; simultaneous cessation does not imply a shared fuel, origin, or ontology.
- **Cause:** never identified. Faris is not dormant, severed, immune, chosen, divine, causal, or secretly capable.

## Original setting, communities, and institutions

- **Drenovar:** dense inland exchange city and primary setting. The Copper Relay, Provision Assembly, Exchange Hall, seed cellars, mechanical presses, and Drenovar Turnworks meet here. Its leaders can save more residents locally by enclosing reserves, creating the central moral pressure.
- **Voln Plateau:** kilnvoice-built highland towns whose ceramic aqueducts and load-bearing shells require skilled maintenance. When magic ends, completed fired structures remain, while active repairs freeze half-shaped and ordinary engineering becomes scarce.
- **Aruq Saltlands:** tidal settlements shared by humans, sapient nerethi, and non-sapient pluun partners. Magical brining supplied most fresh water; existing water remains, but new separation stops and nursery pools begin salinating.
- **Oltin Coast:** orchard ports whose staple crops depend on migratory pollinators guided by Brameth's bloomcall. The insects remain ordinary living animals; without guidance, colonies scatter and next season's harvest becomes uncertain.
- **Copper Relay:** wholly nonmagical global wire service powered by cranks, flywheels, batteries, type drums, and human operators. It carries the Census results and later the survival releases; cut cables and operator choice limit it.
- **Provision Assembly:** Drenovar's elected emergency body. Its legal reach is local despite its control of globally useful records and seed duplicates. It seeks temporary enclosure, not cartoon tyranny.
- **Drenovar Turnworks:** communal wheelshare utility of tread galleries, flywheels, air cisterns, cold rooms, and pneumatic freight tubes. Its surviving ordinary pressure is finite and becomes the climax's one-use material resource.

## Character engines, remaining skills, and arcs

- **Faris — nonmagical route auditor, late thirties:** Want: collect enough old accounts to make the world obey a survivable plan. Need: accept that help created relationships, not ownership. Pressure: each refusal costs irreplaceable hours; part of her enjoys watching former hierarchies meet her lifelong zero. Remaining skill: routes, inventories, mechanical communication, triage, asking the useful next question. Arc: private creditor -> coercive coordinator -> distributor who gives away access and permits others to revise her plan.
- **Malko Durev — Voln master kilnvoice and structural engineer:** Concrete loss: extraordinary voice and social rank. Present aim: spend hours attempting to recover kilnvoice and preserve one dangerous aqueduct rather than abandon a millennium of practice. Refusal: will not become Faris's “ordinary pair of hands” on command. Remaining skill: load calculation, ceramic fracture reading, rigging, drafting. Arc: capacity as identity -> grief and refusal -> authors a manual conversion bearing his own name rather than pretending magic never mattered.
- **Ishbel Rann — chief engineer of the Drenovar Turnworks:** Concrete loss: the shared multiplication she has coordinated for thirty years and the civic legitimacy attached to it. Present aim: retain finite air pressure and seed for Drenovar. Refusal: will not risk her crews or depressurize on Faris's ledger claim. Remaining skill: mechanical systems, crew management, cold-chain timing. Arc: local enclosure -> negotiated partial release -> makes the one irreversible pressure allocation and publishes its cost.
- **Ossun — nerethi water navigator and pluun nursery advocate:** Concrete loss: the paired sense that has shaped body, vocation, and kinship. Present aim: divert all portable water to Aruq nursery pools. Refusal: will not overdraw or transport pluun as equipment, even to save humans. Remaining skill: navigation, diving, salinity testing, water ration design. Arc: defensive triage -> reciprocal water plan that protects both species while accepting bounded loss.
- **Brameth Caum — newly former god and Oltin agricultural jurist:** Concrete loss: bloomcall, godhood, office's supernatural basis, and the intimate orientation of distant swarms. Present aim: prevent the Assembly from announcing spiritual authority void before frightened communities can respond. Refusal: will not bless Faris's plan or lend obsolete divinity to it. Remaining skill: entomology, orchard law, conflict mediation, migration maps. Arc: guardian of sacred continuity -> person who refuses a counterfeit miracle -> public witness whose ordinary expertise saves pollinators and seed.

## Story-specific style and dialogue profile

- **Voice:** Faris notices route margins, spoilage clocks, bodily fatigue, operator habits, and who gets omitted from totals. Her accounting metaphors reveal control and fear; the narration never turns trust into literal currency.
- **Distance/rhythm:** close enough for Faris's ungenerous flashes of vindication and shame. Longer sentences hold simultaneous continental reports; terse statements mark zero, refusal, and irreversible releases. A final controlled widening establishes lasting historical consequences.
- **Image sources:** punch holes and blank columns; flywheels coasting; voices reduced to breath; salt lines climbing nursery walls; pollinators settling without direction; seed warming in opened cellars; roads drawn where shortcuts used to be.
- **Dialogue pressure:** every exchange disputes pace, risk, category, or jurisdiction. Values appear through halted machinery, water not taken, records annotated, access destroyed, and limits accepted. Ration polished banter and thematic speeches.
- **Epic/elegiac register:** repeat Census responses in changing forms—capacity, dependency, loss, remaining skill—without liturgical borrowing. Let ages appear through worn civic objects and practiced assumptions, not lore recital.
- **Ending discipline:** no last spark, cosmic explanation, restoration hint, universal theory, or sentimental claim that cooperation is magic.

### Major-speaker dialogue profiles

- **Faris:** Wants actionable assent; defaults to exact remembered favors, clipped inventory phrases, and dry bargaining. Refuses to admit that “owed” feels safer than “please.” When debt language fails, she names the cost and exit. Would not naturally deliver an abstract speech about community.
- **Malko:** Wants his vanished mastery treated as real; uses load paths, material verbs, and formerly effortless imperatives. Keeps fear of ordinariness vague. Resistance makes him first grandiose, then rigorously technical. With Faris, old affection emerges as correction. Would not call magic a mere tool.
- **Ishbel:** Wants bounded risk and local survival; speaks in timed steps, crew counts, valve states, and blunt counteroffers. Hides grief behind calculations. When challenged publicly, she becomes more conditional rather than louder. Would not promise what a machine cannot do.
- **Ossun:** Wants both peoples in the water equation; uses salinity, current, distance, and plural kin terms, with considered pauses rather than exoticized grammar. Refuses the premise that pluun are supplies. When heard, shifts from refusal to precise allocation. Would not use “we” ambiguously when species interests differ.
- **Brameth:** Wants dignity without counterfeit holiness; measured legal syntax, agricultural examples, and long silences. Avoids saying that being needed felt like love. Under pressure, stops speaking as an institution and gives concrete entomological facts. Would not bless a plan after power is gone.

## Causal scene sequence

### 1. All zero — 700 words; dawn to minute twenty

- Open in Drenovar's Exchange Hall as the Bearing Census begins. Faris's brass capacity card shows its lifelong `maximum mana: 0`; Malko's normally immense value, Ishbel's wheelshare roster total, Ossun's paired response, and Brameth's bloomcall are being transmitted in their own terms.
- At dawn the magic ends everywhere. Malko's shaping tone is only cracked breath; the Turnworks lose multiplied force and flywheels begin coasting; Ossun and a pluun cannot sense or separate salt; pollinator clouds ignore Brameth's call. Nonliving gauges record effects but do not cause or explain them.
- The narrative frame states that every living material-world use has ceased forever. Faris knows only the present tests. She feels one shameful instant of vindication before the Turnworks alarms start.
- **Incompatible aims / tactic change:** Faris wants immediate dependency reports; Malko demands retests, Brameth wants ritual privacy, and Ishbel wants everyone out of the machinery. Faris stops declaring a common failure and asks each for one system-specific test; after independent failures, each names one physical deadline.
- **Turn:** zero is not a broken meter. Drenovar has hours before cold stores warm and air pressure vanishes.

### 2. The world's replies — 650 words; minute twenty to third hour

- The mundane Copper Relay delivers distinct evidence, not montage: Voln reports unfinished aqueduct joints separating; Aruq reports rising nursery salinity; Oltin reports pollinators landing instead of migrating; remote clinics and transport offices send their own failure codes. Cable breaks and missing stations remain visible gaps.
- Each report changes the survival table. Seed must leave refrigeration; water must be tested rather than assumed; physical roads replace magical transport; ordinary medical procedures must be copied; pollinator colonies must travel with compatible seed.
- The Provision Assembly orders the seed cellars and remaining pressure enclosed for Drenovar. Ishbel supports the temporary hold because an uncontrolled release could kill crews and waste both.
- Faris proposes a six-column open account for every relay station: people, potable water, viable seed/pollinators, ordinary skills and records, physical routes, and a named revocable custodian. Copies must propagate outward rather than report inward to one center.
- **Incompatible aims / tactic change:** Faris wants immediate global release; Ishbel and the Assembly want verified local control. Faris shifts from claiming past authority to showing spoilage and route deadlines. Ishbel grants a three-hour engineering audit, not permission.
- **Turn:** Faris has the communication spine but must earn four renewed choices before the finite pressure is gone.

### 3. The voice and the wheel — 750 words; third to seventh hour

- At the seed-cellar gate, Faris invokes Siltwinter as Malko's debt. He refuses to be reduced from master to laborer and nearly leaves to save a Voln aqueduct no one can reach in time.
- The red vise will not fuse the gate. Faris admits she needs Malko's retained fracture knowledge. He designs a manual clamp-and-wedge opening within a stated load, while Ishbel refuses to place crews beneath it until the Turnworks counterbar carries weight.
- A present physical scar on the vise and Malko's damaged tooth reveal the early service history through disagreement. The manual method costs ninety minutes and permanently ruins the shaped lock.
- **Incompatible aims / tactic changes:** Faris wants the gate sacrificed now; Malko wants his vanished craft honored; Ishbel wants crew safety and central custody. Faris changes from collection to request. Malko shifts from offended refusal to technical command; Ishbel supplies a counterbar but reserves the pressure decision.
- **Turn:** the cellar can open, making distribution possible and enclosure irreversible, but no plan works without a water allocation Aruq will accept.

### 4. Seven tests — 700 words; seventh to eleventh hour

- Ossun tests seven candidate cisterns. The first two are too saline; using them would poison drinking reserves and the pluun nursery. The finite case makes selection consequential.
- Faris asks Ossun to release nursery water, citing the Aruq Refusal. Ossun refuses: Faris once defended the right to say no and is now trying to purchase its reversal. A pluun remains an animal partner with needs and behavior, never cargo or a magical object.
- Faris yields allocation design to Ossun. The revised plan moves part of the nursery uphill with ordinary wet sledges, combines three safe cisterns, abandons two settlements' least viable stores, and pairs seed destinations with water chemistry.
- **Incompatible aims / tactic change:** Faris wants the largest human survival count; Ossun wants nursery survival and refuses aggregate arithmetic that erases species. Faris changes from a demanded quantity to a shared threshold; Ossun changes from departure to a mixed allocation and accepts that not every nursery pool can be saved.
- **Turn:** water and seed can now travel together, but the Assembly will not accept global release without political legitimacy—and Brameth refuses to supply divine endorsement.

### 5. No blessing remains — 650 words; eleventh to fourteenth hour

- Brameth faces worshipers and Assembly members who alternately demand a miracle, declare the god dead, or ask for the old authority's seal. Brameth demonstrates no hidden call and will not injure themself repeating it. Worship and memory persist; supernatural office does not.
- Faris invokes the orchard wagons saved during the Nine-Depot Fire. Brameth refuses the account and refuses to bless the plan. Faris finally asks what ordinary knowledge remains.
- Brameth maps which pollinator colonies can survive boxed travel, which seeds require them, and which roads avoid lethal heat. As an agricultural jurist, Brameth proposes a public rule: no emergency custodian may hold seed, water, or route records without publishing outgoing copies and a replacement process.
- **Incompatible aims / tactic change:** Faris wants borrowed legitimacy; Brameth wants the spiritual community protected from opportunistic abolition and false hope. Faris stops asking who Brameth used to be; Brameth shifts from ceremonial silence to expertise and public testimony.
- **Turn:** the plan has social legitimacy without divinity. Ishbel alone controls whether the last ordinary pressure serves enclosure or distribution.

### 6. Accounts due — 1,300 words; fourteenth to twenty-second hour

- The climax intercuts clear linked work in the Turnworks, seed cellar, relay floor, water yard, and departure gates. Faris coordinates; Malko controls the gate load, Ishbel the valves and crews, Ossun water testing and nursery transport, Brameth seed–pollinator pairing and custody language.
- First reversal: the counterwheel atlas shows three advertised relief routes depended on magic. Faris spends time recalculating six slower physical routes and publicly marks two regions unreachable before spoilage; grandeur includes acknowledged failure.
- Second reversal: wheelshare is gone, but ordinary air compressed before zero remains. Ishbel can spend it once to clear seed through pneumatic tubes and power the press brakes, or retain it for Drenovar's cooling. It cannot do both and will be ordinary pressure, fully vented by the end.
- Faris presents her eighteen-year ledger as authority. Malko, Ossun, and Brameth each refuse in a different way; Ishbel will not risk workers for debts they never incurred. Faris tears out no magically meaningful page and makes no ritual. She simply states that the ledger proves only what happened before, releases every claimed obligation, and asks what each will choose now.
- Each helper renews narrowly: Malko accepts the gate; Ossun the mixed allocation; Brameth the wagon and custody plan; Ishbel a staged pressure release with worker stop authority. Relay operators separately choose to transmit; receiving communities may reject or revise.
- **Artifact payoffs:** the atlas supplies ordinary routes and is sent away; the red vise holds the destroyed gate; all seven salinity reagents are spent; punch 9D authenticates the six-column release and its replacement rule, then loses its code tooth.
- The Provision Assembly votes to open the seed cellars, vent the air, and surrender exclusive custody. This shortens Drenovar's own reserve. Pressure drives one final material cascade—seed crates out, paper plates through presses, medical diagrams and route tables into dispatch tubes—then falls to ordinary atmospheric balance. No magic participates.
- The Copper Relay broadcasts the open account. Voln, Aruq, Oltin, and other stations answer differently: some accept, some reduce shares, some report nothing. The plan spreads by voluntary local copying, not command or instant rescue.
- **Incompatible aims / tactic changes:** Faris wants one coordinated yes; each participant protects a different non-negotiable limit. She moves from leverage to distributed vetoes. They move from refusal to bounded action only after ownership, safety, species needs, spiritual honesty, and accountability alter the plan.
- **Climactic turn:** water, seed, pollinators, ordinary knowledge, and route copies leave in different directions with publicly replaceable custodians. The decision cannot be recalled after pressure vents, stores divide, the punch breaks, and the atlas departs.

### 7. The first unmeasured day — 650 words; night to next dawn

- Consequences remain concrete: Turnworks cold rooms warm; clinics triage without magical care; kilnvoice buildings receive wooden braces; nerethi and humans haul water while pluun nurseries contract; former practitioners lose rank but not personhood; Brameth's worshipers keep vigil without supernatural answer.
- Historical truth remains: old shaped cities, prior rescues, scars, laws, and relationships are not exposed as errors. Inert tools become salvage or memorials. Faris's lifelong zero grants no new status in nature.
- A brief prolepsis follows physical effects of the six-column account: roads replace vanished transport, seed/pollinator exchanges prevent some famines, chemical water practice spreads, published custodians are removed or renewed, and multiple knowledge copies survive wars and weather. Other regions fail. The world is diminished, plural, and alive.
- Narration states without qualification that magic never returns—not through birth, artifact, god, hidden reserve, machine, future discovery, or changed name. The statement is about this material world's future, not other planes, worlds, timelines, afterlife, or the past.
- Faris begins a new page. It has no column for what anyone owes her, only what left, by which road, and who may be asked next. Outside, pollinator boxes and seed wagons take the long physical roads while the Turnworks' last flywheel stops.
- **Final dialogue pressure / tactic change:** Faris wants Ishbel to certify the account closed; Ishbel insists the first corrections are already due. Faris's joke about balances fails. She leaves the page open.
- **Ending image:** a stopped wheel, an unfinished line of punch holes, and ordinary insects waking inside travel boxes as the first fully nonmagical dawn reaches Drenovar.

## Stakes, reversals, and required consequence map

- **Immediate:** hours before seed warms, air vents, nursery water salinates, clinics fail, and physical routes clog.
- **World:** food, water, medicine, transport, construction, agriculture, spiritual authority, and governance lose magical foundations simultaneously.
- **Personal:** Faris risks becoming the coercive center she once opposed; the four former users must separate lost faculty and office from enduring personhood and skill.
- **Moral/political:** enclosure can save more of one city; distribution risks Drenovar to give many communities unequal chances and contestable authority.
- **Ecological:** specific pluun nurseries and migratory pollinators face bounded biological consequences; the story makes no species-wide survival rule beyond what it establishes.
- **Historical/material:** former magic's physical results and records endure; half-finished workings become hazards; audited artifacts retain only individual mundane properties.
- **Reversals:** global reports agree without explaining cause; the magical artifacts are inert; “owed” allies refuse; several routes prove magic-dependent; safe water is scarcer than inventory claimed; godhood cannot legitimize the plan; the last pressure cannot serve both city and world.
- **Resolution:** a distributed, correctable survival account preserves some people, water, seed, pollinators, knowledge, routes, and public accountability while permanently sacrificing central reserves, exceptional access, and all magic.

## Binding constraints and prohibited salvage

- The plan's original elements are local inventions under the new prompt, not current authority until promotion. They do not create a universal magic taxonomy or expand claims beyond the terminal present material world.
- Only life ever uses magic. Nonliving foci, gauges, presses, tools, wires, ducts, and artifacts do not choose or become replacement casters.
- Preserve personhood, prior choices, physical transformations, grief, scars, worship, and responsibility after faculties end.
- Prose must not name or indirectly evoke any prior repository character, place, era, artifact, institution, system, title, crisis, ritual, or relationship. No recognizable mechanism may be lightly renamed.
- All rejected crossover material is prohibited. `Tycho`, `Uma`, and `Zeke` remain abandoned prior-version reservations and must not appear. Rejected local names, settings, jobs, artifacts, and hydraulic plot machinery also remain excluded. Faris retains only her mononym and lifelong nonmagical premise; her entire identity and history here are new.
- Draft/final review must search every proper noun and distinctive mechanism against the repository and reject accidental reuse before release.
