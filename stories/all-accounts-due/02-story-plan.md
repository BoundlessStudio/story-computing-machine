# Story plan — All Accounts Due

## Story promise

Faris is an ordinary man who has never possessed magic. Over thirty-one years of being frightened, stubborn, useful, mistaken, rescued, and occasionally brave, he has come away from six mythic adventures with six impossible powers owing him choices and eight relics no sane collector would bring together. At the Ithrom Array, with every wonder of the material world still fully alive, he means to use those accounts once—not to rule magic, repair it, or reserve it, but to end it.

The six powers and eight relics are already assembled when the story opens. A magical opposition is closing around them. Exactly six nonchronological quest flashbacks reveal how Faris earned each renewed favor, acquired every indispensable relic, and learned one ordinary lesson that acts immediately in the terminal mechanism. The final continuous present action culminates in his physical release and witnessed declaration: `No More Magic`. Six unrelated systems then die in six different grammars. The goddess Seyathi realizes Faris obtained her contribution through a technically true, deliberately narrow promise, tries to punish or reverse him with her former faculty, and finds nothing answers. The story ends there.

## Controls, structure, and continuity boundary

- **Target:** about 5,800 words within 4,500–6,000.
- **POV/tense:** close third person, past tense, fixed on Faris; he/him throughout.
- **Rating:** Teen. Cosmic destruction, falls, magical-species deaths, betrayal, and non-graphic violence; consequence rather than gore.
- **Tone:** mythic adventure accumulated into tragic cosmic finality; awe remains tactile and person-centered.
- **Word architecture:** 170-word present frame; six 550-word dramatized flashbacks plus about 40 present-return words apiece (3,540); 1,950-word unbroken arming/declaration/extinction climax; 140-word goddess realization. Total about 5,800. Flashbacks themselves are 3,300 words, 56.9% of total.
- **Private chronology:** a new terminal era after every admitted material-world history in which magic remains active. Prose names, quotes, or alludes to no prior repository era or story.
- **Active magic:** magic is fully active at opening. Exactly six unrelated systems exhaust every active material-world magical system of this terminal era. Hybrids use combinations of the six and do not create a seventh source.
- **Terminal scope:** the wave ends living capacity, stored charge, fixed magical structures, material-world crossing anchors, active faculties, hybrids, reserves, and the six recoverable patterns forever. Earlier events and consequences remain real. Other worlds, planes, timelines, earlier eras, the universal afterlife, deceased continuations, and destinations beyond failed material anchors are unclaimed.
- **Faris constraint:** no magic, severance, immunity, prophecy, destiny, combat mastery, genius, universal key, or compensating super-skill. Any lifelong non-user is absent from the Array's magical binding registry and could physically occupy its unregistered control recess. Only Faris has this exact lawful/stolen custody, handling experience, six relationships, hard-won comparative knowledge, and willingness. Residual magic, weapons, heat, falls, and debris can injure him normally.
- **Declaration:** the words are witness, not spell, name-power, command, or hidden faculty. Six living authorities arm six terminal conditions; the relics align them; Faris performs an ordinary mechanical release while saying exactly `No More Magic`.
- **No restoration:** no relic, engine, offer, backup, seed, loophole, dormant user, or future quest can restore magic. The Ithrom Array synchronizes terminal conditions only.

## Present setting, opposition, and cosmic view

- **Ithrom Array:** nonliving planet-girdling assembly built by long-dead comparative engineers to synchronize six independently authorized endings if their systems were ever captured together. Six world-curves meet at a material hub hanging above the planet's equator. It supplies no magic, makes no judgment, and cannot arm or fire itself.
- **Unwritten Axis:** person-sized recess at the hub's center. Magical bindings seize any being ever registered by a system and route that being away from the manual release. A lifelong non-user has no registry address, so the recess does nothing to him. This is a design omission, not protection from magic generally.
- **Absolute Yoke:** coercive hybrid made from techniques of all six systems. It is not a seventh system. Once seated in the Array it can issue system-specific compulsions to every current user, god, hybrid, structure, and crossing. Its supporters promise orderly peace; its certain price is universal magical obedience.
- **Bound Ascendancy:** original coalition of frightened rulers, priests, users, and magical peoples trying to seize the Array and install the Absolute Yoke. They are morally credible: extinction will ground cities, end species-defining faculties, kill some magic-dependent beings, and strip cultures of sacred orders. They remain mostly an unnamed attacking host so the present cast stays manageable.
- **Visible world:** through six open world-curves Faris can directly see tessellated sanctuaries walking across deserts, black furnaces burning inward, dragon-drawn sky corridors, cities occupying several incompatible arrangements, Qeluun's living body encircling the upper air, and grafted peoples bearing borrowed anatomies. These are contemporary original wonders, not cameos or altered repository imagery.

## Character arcs and dialogue profiles

- **Faris (he/him):** Wants to make the Absolute Yoke—and finally magic itself—unable to own anyone. Needs to accept that ending magic is his morally compromising choice, not a clean debt collection or destiny. He uses patient questions, remembered practical details, opportunism, bad jokes when frightened, and direct admissions only after evasion fails. Sentences are plain and concrete; he is not eloquent at the decisive moment beyond the required declaration. He would never call himself the only possible savior.
- **Seyathi (she/her), goddess:** Wants the Absolute Yoke destroyed while preserving Grace, divinity, and those protected by it. Speaks in balanced spatial clauses and exact promises; changes from imperious interpretation to narrow bargaining. She refuses global extinction because Faris does not disclose it. Her final anger is justified. She would not mistake worship for informed agreement.
- **Hurzai (he/him), demon lord:** Wants demonic autonomy more than continued lordship but refuses sentimental claims on an old rescue. Dense, transactional phrasing drawn from appetite, heat, and metallurgy; under pressure he becomes brutally literal. He knowingly chooses mortality and would not call it purification.
- **Ekkorath (they/them), dragon:** Wants the sky routes free of the Yoke but grieves every city their extinction will drop. Expansive geographic syntax shortens to flight commands under attack. They refuse until Faris names the casualties; then they choose the last counter-flight. They do not treat grounded dragons as lesser persons.
- **King Malrec (he/him):** Wants his people saved and his authority remembered as more than magic. Formal conditional speech, dry insults with Faris, exact distinctions between theft and pardon. He first demands the Sole Chamber back, then knowingly renounces Manyroom after Faris refuses a private exemption.
- **Qeluun (they/them), cosmic person:** Wants their mind kept distinct from the inhabited masses they support. Speech arrives in slow concrete fragments about pressure, pain, and bodily direction. They refuse until Faris states that survival is uncertain; they knowingly consent to loss, not to being treated as landscape.
- **Jelveth Sorn (she/her), keeper and former adversary:** Wants grafted people protected from both the Yoke and Faris's simplification. Precise anatomical questions, clipped accusations, no banter. She refuses until the terminal rule preserves material bodies rather than “resetting” identities, then knowingly closes the reservoir and opens the Unwritten Axis.

## Exact goddess deception

At the first present arming, Seyathi asks whether Faris will fulfill the debt from Talarum. Faris says exactly:

> “Arm the Missing Tessera, and before this hour ends the Absolute Yoke will be powerless. I will not take its power, wear it, or pass it to anyone.”

Every clause is fulfilled. Faris knows she infers that only the Yoke will end. He does not correct her and moves the next relic into place before she asks a broader question. Narration registers his deliberate omission without excusing it. The Talarum flashback has already established that Seyathi taught him to promise only the boundary he could honestly keep, making her final recognition fair and personal.

## Story-specific style profile

- **Dominant voice:** Faris notices loose fasteners, wrong footing, breath, distance, hiding places, bargains' exact nouns, and when a powerful person is afraid. He is not a specialist; insights are limited, learned, sometimes late, and often supplied by companions.
- **Narrative distance:** very close during mistakes, shame, deception, and the final manual action; wider only when the Array exposes simultaneous world-scale extinction. Never enter another mind, including Seyathi's; her realization is shown through remembered wording, expression, and failed action.
- **Rhythm:** adventures use muscular spatial clarity and varied pace; present returns shorten as relics accumulate. The final 1,950 words lengthen into simultaneous spectacle, then contract sharply to the goddess's failed gesture.
- **Imagery:** tile angles, fire drawn inward, stretched horizons, rooms sharing one wall, the pressure of a body mistaken for sky, borrowed organs, and eight objects occupying six sockets. Avoid prior-version glass, ledger, road, seed, water-allocation, wheel, dawn, and restoration imagery.
- **Dialogue pressure:** powers dispute what their favor means, what Faris has disclosed, whose people bear the cost, and whether he can withdraw. Ethical content appears through refused arming, named casualties, excluded exemptions, and Seyathi's betrayal—not policy speeches.
- **Intentional mythic mode:** each flashback is a complete quest miniature with objective, danger, reversal, and acquisition, but no triumphant combat. Faris survives because others help and because ordinary actions sometimes matter inside magical spaces.

## Scene sequence and exact word budgets

### 1. Present frame: eight relics at the end of the world — 170 words

- Open at the Ithrom Array with Faris, all six powers, all eight relics, the Unwritten Axis, the Absolute Yoke descending through the world-curves, and the Bound Ascendancy attacking. Magic is spectacularly active; no “everyone wakes at zero” prelude survives this controlling version because Faris and allies intentionally cause zero at the climax.
- Relics already occupy carrying cradles around six empty sockets. Faris has a bruised hand and no protection except what the powers choose to provide. The opposition offers withdrawal: hand over the keystones, keep magic, accept universal order.
- Seyathi reaches for Missing Tessera. Its blank underside triggers the first flashback.
- **Present incompatible aims:** Faris needs immediate arming; Seyathi wants a narrow strike against the Yoke. He changes from invoking her old favor to the exact bounded promise.

### 2. Flashback I: Talarum Steps, nine years earlier — 550 flashback + 40 return

- **Objective/danger:** Free pilgrims trapped inside a Grace pavement that forces bodies to complete a coercive pattern. Faris cannot resist the pattern magically; he crawls along unprepared mortar while Seyathi fights her own corrupted geometry.
- **Mistake/help:** He assumes blank tiles are safe, steps on a prepared underside, and is folded painfully to his knees. A captive points out a mundane maintenance pin; Seyathi holds the pattern one breath while Faris knocks the pin loose with a sandal buckle.
- **Turn/acquisition/favor:** The pavement separates into ordinary slabs, freeing captives. Faris exposes the first Absolute Yoke trial. Seyathi gifts Missing Tessera and teaches him that an exact promise ends where its words end. She grants one future arming against the Yoke.
- **Return/payoff:** Faris uses her own lesson in the narrow promise. She freely arms the Tessera; its socket lights in flat geometric color. Reader can see he omitted worldwide scope.

### 3. Flashback II: Mavorn Palace, twenty-four years earlier — 550 + 40 return

- **Objective/danger:** Steal Sole Chamber to collapse nine overlapping prison wings. Faris enters as a laundry porter, following one scratched base wall; guards can attack from several arrangements and he cannot tell which door is physical.
- **Mistake/help:** He marks the wrong wall, loops back into a cell, and is freed by an unnamed prisoner who has tracked meal smells. He takes Sole Chamber but sees an assassin's blade sharing Malrec's private room.
- **Turn/acquisition/favor:** Faris chooses warning over escape. Malrec survives, the prison overlap collapses, and Faris is arrested. Malrec later pardons the theft, legalizes Faris's custody, and owes him one witnessed decision—not obedience.
- **Return/payoff:** Malrec refuses extinction and demands a capital exemption. Faris rejects it. Remembering the unprofitable warning, Malrec publicly renounces every room into the keystone.

### 4. Flashback III: Aphalion Belt, fourteen years earlier — 550 + 40 return

- **Objective/danger:** Return Qeluun's stolen Nearmost Vertebra and detach poacher rigs boring into their sensory tissue. Gravity shifts with Qeluun's pain; Faris cannot stand, navigate magically, or fight the poachers.
- **Mistake/help:** He cuts the wrong tether, sends himself outward, and Qeluun catches him by flexing their injured body. Guided by their slow knocks, he uses an ordinary pry bar to release the rig's physical clamps.
- **Turn/acquisition/favor:** Faris returns the bone rather than selling it. Qeluun entrusts it back because the portable segment can terminate a captured Worldcoil. They promise one bodily alignment if Faris never speaks of them as landscape.
- **Return/payoff:** Qeluun refuses Faris's euphemism. He states they may die. They knowingly align the Vertebra; gravity convulses and the third socket arms.

### 5. Flashback IV: Ghorvane Underforge, thirty-one years earlier — 550 + 40 return

- **Objective/danger:** Open air to demon young trapped during a root-furnace coup. Inward fire consumes heat toward Hurzai; Faris has no resistance and can only follow a draft along the floor.
- **Mistake/help:** He opens a slag hatch instead of the air shutter and releases a half-forged predator. Hurzai destroys it and is wounded; Faris abandons the artifact vault to crank the real shutter with both hands.
- **Turn/acquisition/favor:** The young survive; Faris's overheard details expose the coup. He bargains for Cold Maw: custody in exchange for never pretending a terminal use is painless. Hurzai owes one freely chosen root-furnace act.
- **Return/payoff:** Faris names mortal demons and probable deaths. Hurzai turns the jaws outward. Inward fire protects Faris for one crossing only, consumes a strip of Hurzai's body, and arms the fourth socket.

### 6. Flashback V: Keruvan Fold, four years earlier — 550 + 40 return

- **Objective/danger:** Steal Unhatched Form and Mortal Latch before patrons use forced grafts on captive peoples. Keeper Jelveth hunts Faris through a sanctuary where borrowed senses make every hiding place visible.
- **Mistake/help:** Faris breaks a support membrane while escaping, endangering residents. He gives up his exit and follows Jelveth's shouted instructions to brace it with ordinary poles while she stabilizes bodies.
- **Turn/acquisition/favor:** Jelveth learns the patrons deceived her. Under public bargain, she grants Faris lawful custody of both stolen relics if he repairs the sanctuary and promises never to call a fixed grafted body counterfeit. Former adversaries part without forgiveness.
- **Return/payoff:** Jelveth refuses until the Array reads “end function; preserve matter.” Faris sets that physical selector; she closes Unhatched Form, opens the Unwritten Axis, and arms the fifth socket.

### 7. Flashback VI: Skairrift, eighteen years earlier — 550 + 40 return

- **Objective/danger:** Rescue Ekkorath, pinned where a sky corridor has knotted around a falling ruin, and recover the Parity Astrolabe before both vanish into curved distance. Faris cannot fly.
- **Mistake/help:** His first ballast knot slips; Ekkorath catches him instead of freeing themself. Faris crawls a now-grounded beam, uses falling stones to find ordinary down, and hammers out the pin trapping their wing.
- **Turn/acquisition/favor:** Ekkorath gives Unflown Arc as rescue reward. Together they recover the Astrolabe from the ruin; the dragon consents to Faris's custody. Ekkorath promises one flight whose destination and casualties must be named first.
- **Return/payoff:** Faris names grounded dragons and falling cities. Ekkorath knowingly flies the terminal counter-circuit through the Array while under attack. Unflown Arc charges; the Astrolabe's six needles align. The sixth socket arms, ending all flashbacks.

### 8. Continuous present climax: No More Magic — 1,950 words

- No further flashback or time break. The Bound Ascendancy breaches the hub using six derivative attacks: commanding floor patterns, inward fire, shortened distance, overlapping soldiers, stolen weight, and forced grafts. The attack proves the Absolute Yoke already reaches all six systems and cannot be opposed magically without giving it another command path.
- **Spatial mechanics:** the six keystones ring the Unwritten Axis; Parity Astrolabe sits above them, connected by six passive indicator rods; Mortal Latch lies below with six insulated plungers and one iron cotter. Faris must cross three exposed body-lengths into the recess, seat the latch, turn its hand lever ninety degrees, and pull the cotter. Every bound magical being is rerouted before the cotter; Faris is not registered, but attacks and debris still reach him.
- **Opposition's credible temptation:** Withdraw the Mortal Latch and the Yoke will spare the six powers, keep cities aloft, preserve crossings and grafted faculties, and end war through enforced compliance. Faris wavers because extinction is not rescue. His choice rests on what the six quests taught him: the Yoke makes every future refusal impossible, while magic has repeatedly given rulers the means to make another person's “no” irrelevant. Ending it remains a tragic, culpable act.
- **Every favor acts:** Seyathi folds attacks away while believing only Yoke will end; Hurzai consumes incoming fire; Malrec collapses overlapping soldiers into one containable position; Qeluun holds the hub against a stolen-weight convulsion; Jelveth separates forced grafts without undoing bodies; Ekkorath keeps the final corridor open until Faris crosses. Each action costs the power something visible.
- **Every learned lesson acts:** Faris follows mortar rather than Grace; identifies the one scratched base surface; uses vibration to time Qeluun's safe interval; follows ordinary airflow through inward fire; preserves matter on the graft selector; drops ballast to find physical down inside curved space. No single lesson is genius and none substitutes for help.
- **Every relic locks:** Missing Tessera lies blank-side inward; Cold Maw points away from its furnace; Unflown Arc matches the dragon's counter-flight; Sole Chamber contains one room; Nearmost Vertebra beats opposite Qeluun; Unhatched Form is closed; Astrolabe reaches parity; Faris seats Mortal Latch. Removing any one leaves its system and the Yoke alive.
- Faris reaches the Unwritten Axis bruised and partly deaf. He inserts the ordinary iron cotter through the six aligned plungers, turns the hand lever ninety degrees, looks at the six rights-holders and the attacking world-curves, and pulls the cotter free while saying exactly: `No More Magic`.
- **Causality:** the words make no mechanism respond. His hand release lets six already armed terminal currents pass simultaneously through their own adapters. Astrolabe prevents one system from surviving long enough to capture another. Mortal Latch and the Ithrom adapter geometry destroy themselves in the discharge, removing the only synchronized terminal pattern.
- **Largest spectacle—six grammars, one witnessed beat:**
  1. Tessellate Grace flattens: sanctuaries cease walking, healing geometries become pigment, compelled bodies stand freely, the Absolute Yoke loses its divine command face, Seyathi's spatial radiance leaves her material body.
  2. Inward Fire pinches shut across black furnaces; impossible flame returns stolen heat as thunderous ordinary expansion, moving fortresses become slag, demon capacities and reserves read zero.
  3. Horizon Flight straightens every braided sky; crossing anchors close on the material-world side, suspended cities enter ordinary falls, dragon flight-curves and stored distance vanish.
  4. Manyroom sheds translucent architectures until each city occupies one material arrangement; parallel streets and reserves cease without duplicating or erasing the people who remain.
  5. Worldcoil auroras drain into weather as Qeluun's vast body loses impossible leverage; orbiting masses take physical paths, their capacity becomes zero, and their personal consciousness remains visibly responsive.
  6. Kindred Graft draws possible anatomies out of the air; completed material bodies stay themselves while impossible faculties stop, storage organs empty, and keeper senses go dark.
- Hybrids fail system by system as each contributing capacity ends; none reveals a seventh source. Gods everywhere lose power and therefore godhood while worship and personhood may remain.
