# Opus review: All Accounts Due

Independent audit for a later editing pass. It is not a `review.md` verdict,
does not change canon state, and does not authorize edits by itself.

## Verdict

- Dialogue: REVISE — The deception at the centre breaks, because the five favor-holders say the hidden scope aloud next to Seyathi. The Talarum tile exchange describes the wrong tile and an injury that has not happened yet. The present-day renewals run as one repeated confirm-the-cost ritual.
- Prose: REVISE — The opening is a block of unexplained coinages. Mechanism inventories and exact relic ages recur throughout. The extinction wave turns from spectacle into an audit list, and the narrator often explains or moralizes after the action.
- Continuity and prompt: REVISE — The prompt's explicit requirements are met (structure proportions, length, declaration, hard ending). But Seyathi's hearing contradicts her LOCKED entry (see D2), and there are three minor staging, pronoun and motive slips.
- Edit scope: targeted-dialogue
- Existing review.md said: 04-review.md pass 14 gave Verdict PASS with zero findings. It has no separate Dialogue line, but it certified "dialogue distinction" and "Seyathi's credible uninformed choice" as intact. I disagree on both counts.

The dialogue here has better raw material than the house default. The plan gave each power its own idiom, and several survive on the page: Qeluun's pressure-fragments, Ekkorath's geographic figures, Jelveth's anatomical orders, Malrec's dry courtly barbs. The failures cluster where the story's architecture of debts and exact terms takes over the talk. In the present, five favor-holders run the same ritual: a closing question, a one-word answer from Faris, a formal acceptance. They do it within earshot of the one person the plot and LOCKED canon say must not hear the scope. In the flashbacks, the favor grants converge on one clause template, and two exchanges hinge on lines that misdescribe what just happened (the Talarum tile, the Ghorvane choice). Twice, Faris wins an exchange with a riposte. The edit pass should restage who hears what at the Array, let the renewals differ in length, medium and pressure, and repair the broken flashback exchanges. It should leave the distinct voices and the user-approved ending alone.

## Dialogue findings

### D1 · Blocking · Talarum tile exchange misdescribes the tile and an unshown injury

- Where: L39–L47, Talarum Steps, after Faris touches the blank tile
- Text:
> The prepared underside turned him over and folded him to both knees.
> "Blank side inward," she said, voice tight. "You were meant to notice."
> "I noticed when my ankle turned."
> The tile corrected him by twisting his ankle until his heel fitted the design.
- Problem: Ground/referent and timing.
  - Seyathi's line misdescribes the tile. The narration has just shown a tile whose blank face is up and whose working pattern is on the underside, so the blank side faces out, not in. "Blank side inward" therefore describes the opposite of what happened, and a listener could not map it onto anything. "You were meant to notice" has no recoverable sense either: meant by whom, and to notice what?
  - Faris's reply cites an ankle turn that has not happened. The shown injuries so far are his knees, shoulder and teeth; the tile twists his ankle only at L47, two lines later.
  - The pair exists to stage a setup and comeback (Punchline Patch), and the comeback refers to a future event.
  - The scene also plants "blank-side inward" for the Tessera at L263, so the muddle spreads to the arming beat.
- Fix:
  - Give Seyathi a line that names what actually happened: plain face up, working pattern underneath. Ideally she also admits she should have warned him. Illustrative only: "The plain ones carry the pattern underneath. I should have told you."
  - Cut "You were meant to notice."
  - Cut Faris's ankle quip, or move a pained, self-directed reaction after the ankle twist at L47.
  - Leave L263's "blank-side inward" as the Tessera's working orientation; do not try to make L43 echo it.

### D2 · Blocking · The withheld scope is spoken in Seyathi's hearing

- Where: L261–L351, the present arming sequence and the Ascendancy's offer (Seyathi is at the first socket of the ring, then beside Faris in the battle)
- Text:
> The other five had heard the whole request before they came, knew Seyathi had not, and had agreed not to supply the missing scope.
> "If my capital's crossings remain," he said, "I will renounce every foreign claim and submit the record to any witness you choose."
> "No exemption."
> "You have named flesh, furnace, and crown. I accept the burn."
> "And the people beneath your answer?"
> "I have found no way to spare them."
> "The cities remain aloft. The crossings remain open. Chosen bodies keep their gifts. Gods keep their care.
> "The Yoke," she said. "Finish the Yoke."
- Problem: Ground/knowledge at the story's central twist.
  - L265 says the five agreed not to supply the scope, and then they supply it aloud, one after another, around a ring Seyathi is standing in:
    - Malrec bargains to keep his capital's crossings, and Faris refuses.
    - Hurzai lists his crown among the costs.
    - Ekkorath asks about the people under the falling cities, and Faris answers that they cannot be spared.
    - Qeluun asks "END US?" and Faris answers "anyway".
    - Then the Ascendancy says outright that the Latch will drop cities, close crossings and end gods' care.
  - Nothing shows Seyathi failing to hear or choosing to disbelieve any of it. Yet she urges "Finish the Yoke" and only understands at L411.
  - As written, she either heard and ignored obvious statements, which makes her foolish (the plan forbids that), or the story's premise fails. It also contradicts her LOCKED entry in `universe/characters.md`: the five "renew their choices without stating that scope in her hearing". It undermines the prompt's demand that the deception be "fair in retrospect".
- Fix: Smallest route — once Seyathi has armed the Tessera (after L263), take her out of earshot with one staging sentence.
  - She came through the tessellated wall at L23, so she can go back to hold it or the ring's edge against the Ascendancy's prepared color while the five renew. The five exchanges can then keep their spoken costs (reworked per D3). She can still fold the pattern away from Faris at L331 from a distance or on her return.
  - For the Ascendancy's offer (L347), make her non-uptake legible. Either she is too far off or losing her spatial sense (L351 already has this), or she hears it and rejects it as the Yoke's lie. The rejection sharpens the betrayal, but it must be an action or a short urgent line, not an explanation.
  - The alternative route: keep her present and make the five renewals non-verbal or scope-free. That costs Malrec's and Ekkorath's best lines, so prefer the staging fix.
  - Do not touch L257 or L263.

### D3 · Major · The five renewals run as one confirmation routine

- Where: L267–L325, Malrec, Qeluun, Hurzai, Jelveth and Ekkorath at sockets two to six
- Text:
> "Then I ask nothing further."
> Let the record show that necessity has defeated negotiation.
> Hurzai reopened it. "And the cost you named?"
> "Unchanged," Faris said.
> "And you offer no further assurance?"
> Ekkorath spread their wounded wings. "I will fly it once."
- Problem: Negotiation/procedure routine, Tennis Match, Identical Voices, and an inert exchange at the story's major dialogue sequence.
  - Malrec, Hurzai, Jelveth and Ekkorath each open with a condition or a confirming question. Faris answers Malrec, Hurzai and Jelveth in single words ("No exemption." / "Unchanged." / "Nothing." / "None."). The holders then accept in formal terms ("Then I ask nothing further." / "I accept the burn." / "I will fly it once.").
  - Each exchange is propped by a "before they came" backfill (L273, L283, L299, L309, L319), so the real choices are all offstage and the on-page talk mostly re-confirms them.
  - With the tags covered, Hurzai's "And the cost you named?", Jelveth's "And you offer no further assurance?" and Ekkorath's "And the people beneath your answer?" are interchangeable in shape. These are five beings losing their worlds, and they sound like one clerk.
  - Malrec's "Let the record show that necessity has defeated negotiation." states the scene's point (Philosopher).
  - Two exchanges do change something and should anchor the rework. Qeluun's fear gets a direct, costly answer (L281–L285). Hurzai demands proof and Faris gives a demonstration (L289–L293).
- Fix: Rework the sequence under D2's staging.
  - State once, before the renewals, that Faris told the five everything before they came; this is LOCKED. Drop the four later per-exchange backfills.
  - Malrec: keep the exemption bargain, and let it come from looking at his people rather than from a clause. Cut "Let the record show that necessity has defeated negotiation." Keep "Witness this: I release every claim in the Sole Chamber, including my own." (it pays off the public-decision term).
  - Qeluun: keep as is.
  - Hurzai: keep the challenge and the demonstration. Trim Faris's L295 explanation to what Hurzai needs, since the band jumping into Grace already shows it. Cut or replace "And the cost you named?"/"Unchanged". If Hurzai speaks the cost, make it his own brutally literal statement.
  - Jelveth: cut "And you offer no further assurance?"/"None." Let her check the selector in her own anatomical way and act; at most one precise question about a specific body.
  - Ekkorath: keep the gaze on the cities and "I have found no way to spare them."
  - Let at least one renewal be done almost wordlessly, and let one be cut into by the attack (see C1). Do not end each exchange on a formal acceptance line.

### D4 · Major · Ghorvane choice has no legible stakes and is then undone

- Where: L157–L177, Ghorvane Underforge, after the slag hatch
- Text:
> He held a strip of cloth near each seam and watched which way the loose end bent.
> "You opened waste," Hurzai said. "Air is above. Your nose has cheated you."
> "I can consume a wall or carry you. Not both."
> Faris looked at the half-closed passage, then at the ladder rising through heat toward the shutters. "The wall."
> Instead, the demon lord lifted Faris clear of the buckling gear train and showed him the real shutter chain.
- Problem: Ground/uptake and staging.
  - Hurzai offers a choice whose options the reader cannot map: which wall, where carrying leads, and what each costs the trapped young. So Faris's "The wall." shows no reasoning or character. The narration then supplies a meaning ("knowing rescue had already placed him in debt").
  - Two paragraphs later Hurzai lifts Faris anyway, which breaks the "Not both" that gave the choice its stakes.
  - "Your nose has cheated you" names the wrong sense; the story has just shown Faris reading the draft with a cloth strip. His reply, "Then I read the draft wrong.", then works as a quiet correction of Hurzai.
  - "Review while moving." (L171) is managerial and belongs to no one in particular.
- Fix:
  - Make the two options concrete, and make them options about the young. For example, Hurzai burns a way through toward the air shutters at the cost of his own flesh, or carries Faris out through the breach and leaves the furnace sealed. Faris's one-word answer then means something.
  - Remove the later lift, or show Hurzai breaking his own limit and paying for it.
  - Point Hurzai's rebuke at the cloth or the draft, not the nose.
  - Replace "Review while moving." with something in his heat-and-appetite idiom, or cut it.

### D6 · Major · Favor grants share one contract template

- Where: L103 (Malrec, narrated), L147 (Qeluun, narrated), L187 (Hurzai), L223 (Jelveth, narrated), L253 (Ekkorath; see D5), the closing beat of each flashback
- Text:

> granted one decision over the Chamber if that decision was made publicly and applied to the palace as well as its enemies.

> In return, she would perform one bounded root act after reading its selector herself.
- Problem: Identical Voices / negotiation routine.
  - Every flashback closes on the same unit: one act, under a condition, stated as a clause schedule. A demon lord just out of a coup, a king, a being the size of a sky and an unforgiving keeper all grant favors in Seyathi's exact-promise register.
  - The prompt asks for a mix of bargains, rescues and freely given gifts. The uniform clause makes every relationship read as the same contract.
  - The spoken case is Hurzai's L187: three clipped clauses, the same shape as Ekkorath's "One flight. If you state...".
- Fix:
  - Keep each condition that pays off later: Malrec's public decision, Hurzai's "name the meat cost", Jelveth reading the selector herself, Ekkorath's named destinations and fallers. Change how the conditions arrive.
  - Hurzai should demand it the way a furnace lord would, bluntly and bodily, and not as a clause list.
  - Qeluun's grant can stay wordless or in their fragments.
  - Jelveth's can be grudging and short.
  - Leave exact-promise phrasing to Seyathi (L61), which is her voice and LOCKED.

- Where: L9, L21, L255–L329, present frame through the start of the assault

### C3 · Minor · Keruvan theft and bargain lack a reason
