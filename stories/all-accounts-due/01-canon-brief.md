# Canon Brief — All Accounts Due

## Authority basis and feasibility

The controlling prompt requires a wholly standalone, world-ending culmination built from six major quest flashbacks. Faris is a lifelong nonmagical man who has accumulated at least eight wondrous relics and at least six freely renewed favors from entirely original powers, including a goddess, demon lord, dragon, and king. Magic remains fully active until Faris performs a mundane final action while saying exactly `No More Magic`. The resulting synchronized event permanently ends every form and reserve of magic in the present material world. (`stories/all-accounts-due/00-prompt.md` — `## Verbatim quest-build clarification`; `## Story controls`; `## Adventure-build requirements`; `## Assumptions`; `## Completion tests`)

The latest direct user clarification for active guard `7d3fee2087114cdc85be8baaeeba2528` additionally establishes:

- Faris uses he/him pronouns.
- He obtained a necessary contribution from the original goddess through a bounded deception.
- After the declaration and extinction wave, the goddess realizes what he did, wants to act, discovers that her power is gone, and the story cuts to black.
- There is no dawn, reconstruction, or aftermath coda after that realization.
- Worldwide zero and permanence must therefore be established during the extinction wave before the final goddess beat.

This remains canon-compatible. Magic comprises multiple real systems without a required common origin, and nonliving artifacts may route or store power supplied through living agency without becoming autonomous users. (`universe/rules.md` — `## System categories`; `## Living agency and magic`)

No retcon or user ruling is required.

## Hard constraints

1. Place the story privately in a new terminal era after every admitted material-world history in which magic remains active. Earlier stories remain true in their own eras; the permanent ending affects the world from this terminal event forward. (`universe/premise.md` — `## The deep-time shared world`; `universe/timeline.md` — `## Established era order`)

2. Keep the prose wholly original. Do not name, cameo, inherit, quote, or adapt any repository character, location, artifact, institution, title, magical system, crisis, relationship, or plot history. Recognizable renamed analogues are also prohibited.

3. Faris is a man and uses he/him pronouns. He has never possessed magic, undergone magical severance, carried a dormant gift, fulfilled a prophecy, or belonged to a chosen lineage.

4. Faris has no compensating super-skill. He survives through ordinary nerve, persistence, empathy, bargaining, observation, preparation, opportunism, accumulated practical knowledge, and help from others.

5. Use six substantial quest/adventure flashbacks comprising approximately 55–65% of the prose. The continuously returning present, extended climax, extinction wave, and final goddess beat comprise approximately 35–45%.

6. At least eight individually bounded wondrous relics must converge causally in the climax. They may have been gathered, recovered, stolen, bargained for, received as rewards, or freely given.

7. At least six materially distinct favors must be freely renewed in present time. Their holders must include an entirely original goddess, demon lord, dragon, king, and at least two other original powers or authorities.

8. The goddess, demon lord, dragon, king, and other powers must be new identities rather than crossovers, reused characters, descendants imported for recognition, or renamed analogues.

9. Magic remains active and spectacular until Faris says exactly:

   > No More Magic

10. The words are not a spell, true-name effect, universal command, or concealed faculty. They coincide with a clearly mundane physical action that releases an already armed terminal sequence. (`universe/rules.md` — `## Names and identity binding`)

11. The event simultaneously ends living magical capacity, stored charge, magical structures, material-world crossings, active magical faculties, and every reserve in the present material world.

12. No magical recovery, restoration, surviving reserve, backup, seed, loophole, dormant user, reusable terminal pattern, or future restoration quest may remain.

13. The event does not retroactively erase earlier history and does not establish extinction in the afterlife, other planes, alternate timelines, or destinations beyond destroyed material-world crossing anchors. (`universe/rules.md` — `## Afterlife and continuation`; `## Portals and crossing systems`)

14. Faris intentionally and materially deceives the goddess. The deception must be understandable in retrospect, ethically consequential, and achieved through ordinary conduct rather than mind control, magical compulsion, altered memory, or a metaphysical consent loophole.

15. The goddess’s gift and renewed favor may be freely chosen while based on incomplete understanding. Her free action must not be misrepresented as informed consent to world extinction.

16. The goddess remains a person after losing her power unless her independently established biology makes survival impossible. Her identity, relationships, memory, anger, betrayal, and moral claim against Faris are not erased by the end of her godhood. (`universe/style-guide.md` — `## Personhood and moral consequence`; `universe/rules.md` — `## Divinity`)

17. The extinction wave itself must establish worldwide zero and permanence. Nothing after the goddess’s failed response may explain, verify, soften, or administer the outcome.

18. Maintain the Teen content ceiling: dangerous, tragic, and morally severe consequences may be shown, but violence remains non-graphic and non-endorsing. (`universe/style-guide.md` — `## Audience and content boundaries`)

## Private chronology and scope

The modern-like low-magic era precedes later eras in which gods and active magic rise again, including the Glass-Sea era. Other admitted stories also establish active magic in otherwise unresolved periods. A permanent ending cannot precede those events without contradiction. (`universe/timeline.md` — `## Established era order`; `stories/daughter-of-the-sun/05-story.md` — `# Daughter of the Sun`; `stories/a-crown-of-quiet-hours/05-story.md` — `# A Crown of Quiet Hours`)

Therefore:

- Privately place this story after all currently admitted material-world magic-active histories.
- Do not state a date or identify earlier eras in the prose.
- Do not turn the story into a crossover or survey of prior canon.
- Let the same physical world continue nonmagically afterward.
- Limit “all magic” to the exhaustive magical systems active in the present material world.

“Cosmic” controls spectacle and consequence, not unrestricted ontology. The event may transform skies, seas, world-spanning structures, magical ecologies, and material-world crossings without claiming to erase the universal afterlife or unrelated realities.

## Six-system terminal model

Use exactly six unrelated active magical systems in the terminal era, one anchored to each major quest. Hybrids may combine those systems but must not constitute a hidden seventh source.

For each system, establish:

- an independent origin or explicitly unresolved origin;
- a living source of magical use;
- an ordinary operating mechanism;
- system-specific limits and costs;
- a legitimate living authority capable of arming its terminal condition;
- one terminal keystone;
- how its users, structures, stored charge, and hybrids are reached;
- a distinct visual grammar for extinction;
- why ending that system alone would leave the other five active.

The systems may interact at the climax without sharing one universal fuel, language, or origin. (`universe/rules.md` — `## System categories`; `## Costs, desire, and local mechanisms`)

A nonliving convergence assembly may synchronize the six endings, but it cannot generate magic, choose the outcome, judge consent, or act as a magical user. The six living participants supply and aim the active workings; the assembly only holds, routes, and times them. (`universe/rules.md` — `## Living agency and magic`)

Synchronization is necessary because partial extinction would allow surviving systems to preserve hybrids, sustain crossings, shelter reserves, interrupt another terminal sequence, or absorb abandoned structures.

## Faris’s bounded access

Faris’s lack of magic may permit him to occupy the final control position only through a specific engineered condition:

- the armed assembly seizes or routes every active magical binding entering its interior;
- a magical user attempting Faris’s task would become an uncontrolled conduit;
- Faris has no binding for it to seize;
- any lifelong non-user could satisfy that condition.

This is not immunity or destiny. Active magic, magical beings, heat, force, falling structures, and collapsing crossings can injure or kill him. His survival still requires artifacts and help.

His decisive advantages remain ordinary:

- remembering six incompatible handling procedures;
- noticing physical details others dismiss;
- preparing mundane contingencies;
- bargaining without supernatural leverage;
- accepting rescue or instruction;
- understanding each favor-holder well enough to ask at the decisive moment;
- physically completing the final connection.

At least two flashbacks should show another person rescuing or materially enabling Faris. At least one should show a costly mistake he made. At least one relic should be freely gifted because of help he gave rather than expertise or destiny.

## Six major quest flashbacks

Target approximately 5,500–5,800 words:

- Opening present setup: 200–300 words.
- Six flashbacks: approximately 3,100–3,500 words total.
- Continuous present climax and extinction: approximately 1,900–2,300 words.
- Final goddess realization: approximately 100–180 words within the climax allocation.
- No later coda.

Each flashback must be a dramatized adventure rather than a résumé entry. It needs:

- a concrete objective;
- a distinct original mythic setting;
- danger Faris cannot overpower;
- an ordinary tactic, compromise, or moral choice;
- one or two relic acquisitions;
- the origin of one present favor;
- a specific lesson or handling detail needed during the climax;
- a present trigger and immediate present payoff.

Recommended quest functions:

1. **The goddess:** Faris helps resolve a threat or coercive bond, earns her trust, receives her terminal keystone, and establishes the ambiguous promise that later enables his deception.
2. **The demon lord:** Faris bargains inside an original infernal or hostile jurisdiction and obtains a relic or terminal right without supernatural enforcement.
3. **The dragon:** Faris survives through observation, empathy, opportunism, and another person’s help; the dragon’s freely renewed debt unlocks a distinct extinction step.
4. **The king:** Faris confronts political power, law, or succession without becoming a master courtier; he gains a royal relic or authority through service, theft, or a morally mixed bargain.
5. **Another power:** Faris aids an original nonhuman, cosmic, ecological, or constructed living person whose system supplies a necessary terminal keystone.
6. **Final keeper or former adversary:** Faris obtains the last knowledge, stolen component, or relationship needed to understand synchronization and physically survive the terminal site.

The flashbacks need not be chronological, but their order should progressively reframe Faris’s lifetime plan and the moral cost of the present act.

## Eight relics

Use eight major relics as the clearest exhaustive inventory:

1. Six system-specific terminal keystones, one from each quest and system.
2. One synchronization instrument that reads only the six established terminal intervals.
3. One final junction that holds the six armed outputs until Faris performs the mundane release.

The acquisition history should include at minimum:

- two freely given gifts;
- one bargain;
- one theft with continuing consequences;
- one recovered or collected relic;
- one rescue reward.

Every relic requires an audit covering:

1. creator or provenance;
2. age;
3. system;
4. material construction;
5. owner or custodian;
6. method of acquisition;
7. living-agency dependency;
8. stored state or charge;
9. exact capability;
10. exact limitation;
11. required mundane handling;
12. flashback function;
13. climax function;
14. terminal destruction, consumption, or permanent inertness.

The synchronization instrument supplies timing, not universal truth. The final junction originates no power and has no agency. Every relic must be consumed, destroyed, or rendered permanently ordinary by the extinction wave.

Remove or merge any relic whose climax function could be deleted or exchanged without changing the causal chain.

## Six freely renewed favors

Each favor-holder must:

- have one precise earlier relationship with Faris;
- retain a credible present reason to refuse;
- make a fresh voluntary choice;
- contribute an irreplaceable action while magic remains active;
- impose a boundary Faris must answer or violate;
- suffer a specific personal loss when extinction succeeds.

Favors remain social obligations, not magical fuel or compulsions. Consent has no universal metaphysical behavior, and the ethical consequences of deception remain real. (`universe/rules.md` — `## Costs, desire, and local mechanisms`)

Five favors should be renewed with materially informed understanding of the extinction plan. The goddess’s favor is the intentional exception: she freely acts, but Faris has withheld the full purpose and reach.

Faris is not a universal sovereign. He coordinates six authorities and exploits one authority’s incomplete understanding. The story must portray that distinction rather than declaring universal consent.

## Required goddess deception

Use a bounded technically honored promise combined with incomplete disclosure:

- During the goddess quest, Faris promises to use her keystone once to make one specifically named magical chain, bond, weapon, or coercive structure powerless.
- He does not promise that this will be the only thing made powerless.
- He knows the keystone can become that system’s terminal adapter when connected to the other seven relics.
- He allows the goddess to understand the intended use as narrow and local.
- She freely gives him the keystone because the stated use is real and because his earlier help earned trust.
- At the climax, she freely renews the favor by arming the keystone against the named target.
- Faris does not reveal that the junction will propagate her system’s terminal condition worldwide while synchronizing the other five extinctions.
- The named target is genuinely made powerless, so the literal promise is fulfilled, but the omission is deliberate and morally compromising.

This must be fair in retrospect. The flashback should preserve Faris’s exact narrow wording, and the climax should show the keystone’s physical placement or routing clearly enough that the goddess’s final recognition feels earned.

Do not excuse the deception as:

- mind control;
- magical compulsion;
- prophecy;
- altered memory;
- inability to understand;
- a secret clause enforced by magic;
- proof that the goddess deserved no choice;
- universal consent produced by technical wording.

Her gift and renewed act are voluntary. Her assent to global extinction is not informed. The ethical breach is intentional and user-authorized.

## Declaration and final action

Faris must say exactly:

> No More Magic

The declaration coincides with one mundane action, such as removing an ordinary restraint, closing a mechanical bridge, driving out an insulating pin, or turning a physical selector after all six keystones have been armed.

The causal order must be explicit:

1. Six living authorities arm their system-specific keystones.
2. The synchronization instrument identifies the one compatible terminal interval.
3. The final junction holds the six outputs apart.
4. Faris completes the ordinary physical connection.
5. He says `No More Magic`.
6. The six terminal conditions propagate simultaneously.
7. The relics and assembly consume their own magical patterns.
8. Worldwide zero and permanence become directly evident.
9. The goddess realizes how her gift and favor were used.
10. She tries to act through her former power and cannot.
11. Cut to black.

The words signify Faris’s witnessed choice. They do not generate power. (`universe/rules.md` — `## Names and identity binding`)

## Spectacular extinction wave

The wave must be the story’s largest continuous spectacle and must prove exhaustive reach before returning to the goddess.

Show the six systems ending in distinct sensory and physical grammars. The terminal site may provide direct access through original world-girdling architecture, linked horizons, crossing mouths, atmospheric structures, living cosmic bodies, or other bounded means established before activation.

The wave must establish:

- every living magical capacity reaches zero;
- active workings fail;
- stored charges discharge, dissipate, or collapse into ordinary effects;
- magical structures become inert matter, ruin, heat, weather, motion, or darkness;
- material-world crossing anchors close and are destroyed locally;
- original magical beings lose their active faculties according to their established biology;
- all six keystones, the synchronizer, and the final junction are consumed or permanently inert;
- every hybrid fails because its constituent systems have ended;
- no reserve, hidden focus, dormant user, autonomous substitute, or reproducible magical pattern remains;
- Faris remains exactly as nonmagical as before;
- permanence follows mechanically because both capacity and every means of magical storage, transmission, or restart have ended.

A single interface may display zero, but `mana` and `maximum mana` must not become mandatory universal vocabulary. Other cultures and beings should register extinction through their own original practices and bodies.

The original waking image may be echoed inside the wave only if it occurs before the goddess’s final realization. There is no dawn scene or verification sequence afterward.

## Final goddess beat

After worldwide extinction and permanence are already established, narrow the narrative completely to the goddess and Faris.

The goddess must:

- recognize the terminal use of the keystone she gave him;
- connect it to his earlier technically accurate but incomplete promise;
- understand that her renewed favor armed more than the narrow outcome she intended;
- experience the betrayal as a person rather than as a failed system component;
- want to stop, reverse, punish, seize, or otherwise act;
- attempt one specific faculty that was previously unmistakably hers;
- discover that nothing answers because her power is gone.

If her body survives, she may retain speech, movement, memory, worshippers, legal authority, and relationships, but the power component of godhood has ended. She is therefore no longer objectively a god under the shared taxonomy even if others continue calling or worshipping her as one. (`universe/rules.md` — `## Divinity`)

Her failed magical response must not be played as a joke or uncomplicated triumph. Faris’s deception has denied her informed choice about an irreversible loss. Her anger and moral claim remain valid even if the story also supports Faris’s reasons.

End immediately on her powerless realization or the failed gesture that proves it. Do not add:

- Faris’s explanation;
- forgiveness or reconciliation;
- a victory speech;
- dawn;
- reconstruction;
- political administration;
- ordinary-life reassurance;
- an epilogue;
- a hint of surviving magic.

## Personhood and ontology boundaries

Loss of magic does not erase identity, consent, grief, wrongdoing, memory, relationships, or prior consequences. (`universe/style-guide.md` — `## Personhood and moral consequence`)

For every original magical or cosmic person, the plan must specify:

- whether their body survives without magic;
- whether they become ordinary, fixed in one form, disabled, mortal, or die;
- which nonmagical abilities remain;
- what they knowingly agreed to lose;
- what Faris withheld from them;
- what relationships and moral claims survive.

The goddess requires special precision because she did not knowingly consent to the event’s full reach.

The universal afterlife remains outside the event’s reach. If anyone dies during extinction, do not claim erasure from all continuation. (`universe/rules.md` — `## Afterlife and continuation`)
