# Canon Brief — All Accounts Due

## Authority basis

The newest prompt contract requires a wholly standalone culmination with an original cast, setting, history, cultures, magical systems, kinds of life, artifacts, titles, institutions, and relationships. The story prose may not name, cameo, or directly reference any character, location, artifact, institution, title, magical system, crisis, or plot history from another repository story. (`stories/all-accounts-due/00-prompt.md` — `## Verbatim crossover clarification`; `## Story controls`; `## Assumptions`)

The user explicitly authorizes the permanent end of magic throughout the story’s present material world. This authority does not erase earlier magic from history or extend automatically to other worlds, planes, timelines, afterlives, or eras. The *House of M* reference controls cumulative scale and emotional finality only; it imports no Marvel content or plot.

Current `LOCKED`/`CANON` universe entries and the stories inventoried by `stories/all-accounts-due/authority.json` constrain the work only behind the scenes. They are not a pool of named material for the prose. Sources, old briefs, plans, drafts, reviews, and proposed deltas remain non-canon. (`universe/README.md` — `## Authority order`; `AGENTS.md` — `## Authority and records`)

## Chronology

Place this story in a new terminal era after every currently admitted material-world history in which magic remains active.

This is necessary because the established modern-like low-magic era is followed by later magic-active ages. A permanent ending placed earlier would contradict those later histories. Nothing in canon requires magic to return after the latest currently established age, and the physical world may continue through deep time without it. (`universe/timeline.md` — `## Established era order`; `universe/premise.md` — `## The deep-time shared world`)

The chronology is a backstage constraint only:

- The prose must not identify or reference any prior named era.
- The new culture need not know that earlier repository histories existed.
- Do not use archaeology, legend, inherited titles, recognizable motifs, or altered names as indirect cameos.
- The plan may record that this is the latest terminal era for continuity purposes, while the story uses only its own original calendar and historical reference points.
- Earlier magical acts and consequences remain true in their own times; the terminal event is not retroactive.

No retcon or separate user ruling is required.

## Hard constraints

1. Every living user in the present material world loses all capacity to use magic, permanently.

2. All usable magic must be gone by the ending. The result cannot be temporary suppression, exhaustion, blocked access, faulty measurement, hidden survival, delayed recovery, or an unresolved question.

3. `Maximum mana` may be one original culture’s capacity term or measurement. It must not become proof that all magical systems share one substance, origin, fuel, interface, or objective taxonomy. Magic, technology, and divinity are cultural labels for underlying systems, and multiple unrelated systems may exist. (`universe/rules.md` — `## System categories`; `universe/glossary.md` — `## Magic, technology, and divinity`)

4. Only life can use magic. A nonliving object may store, focus, transmit, or shape power supplied through living agency, but cannot decide, cast, become a replacement user, or rescue anyone autonomously. (`universe/rules.md` — `## Living agency and magic`)

5. Faris or another chosen protagonist must have been genuinely nonmagical for their entire life. Do not make them dormant, severed, immune, chosen, secretly divine, or the hidden cause of the ending.

6. Favors remain social relationships, not magical contracts or literal power. Every helper must retain the ability to refuse, narrow, renegotiate, or freely renew assistance. Consent, debt, sacrifice, desire, and cost have no universal magical behavior. (`universe/rules.md` — `## Costs, desire, and local mechanisms`)

7. The original systems must remain mechanically and culturally distinct. Shared cessation does not prove shared ancestry or cause.

8. Ending a faculty does not erase personhood, relationships, prior choices, grief, scars, memories, or moral responsibility. Magical beings and constructed or transformed people remain morally protected regardless of what functions survive. (`universe/style-guide.md` — `## Personhood and moral consequence`)

9. Keep the Teen ceiling: large-scale death, ecological collapse, political upheaval, and spiritual grief may be acknowledged, but treatment remains non-graphic and character-centered. (`universe/style-guide.md` — `## Audience and content boundaries`)

## Standalone invention boundary

Planning may create, entirely for this story:

- a new late-era world culture or compact;
- original cities, regions, roads, seas, habitats, and political borders;
- an original civil calendar and decades or centuries of local history;
- original magical traditions with different users, mechanisms, costs, and social meanings;
- original nonhuman or constructed living peoples;
- original religions, gods, professions, offices, factions, institutions, and titles;
- original material and magical infrastructure;
- original crises that shaped the protagonist’s service history;
- original artifacts with individually specified mechanisms and provenance.

Every named element must be original. Do not adapt a canon element by changing its proper noun while preserving its distinctive mechanism, relationship structure, visual signature, plot function, or historical crisis.

## Building cumulative historical weight inside one story

The story does not need cross-story history to feel like a culmination. Establish its own long history through a compressed but causal service record.

Recommended time depth:

- Give the protagonist twelve to twenty years of practical service.
- Give the principal culture at least several generations of dependence on magic.
- Give one or more institutions a century-scale expectation that magic will always remain available.
- Use an original annual observance, civil reckoning, or public ritual to make the terminal day historically charged, without echoing a named canon ceremony.

Seed three service eras rather than listing dozens of favors:

1. **Early service:** the protagonist helps one future ally before possessing influence, earning trust through inconvenient ordinary work.
2. **Middle service:** the protagonist protects a group’s refusal, evidence, property, or vulnerable members when exploiting the situation would have been easier.
3. **Recent service:** the protagonist coordinates an original major crisis, acquires an artifact or access privilege, and learns a mistaken lesson about being owed.

Each past incident should be revealed through a present object, scar, disagreement, institutional rule, or changed relationship. Avoid a detached lore summary. Every remembered event must alter a present request or refusal.

The protagonist’s emotional arc should move from treating old help as accounts collectible in emergency to recognizing that the accumulated value lies in people choosing again. The climax should require at least three materially different relationships:

- practical labor, transport, engineering, medicine, or shelter;
- information, testimony, memory, or access;
- political, spiritual, or communal legitimacy.

No helper should answer solely because a ledger says they owe the protagonist.

## Original magical-system diversity

Create at least three and preferably four original systems. They must differ in more than visuals.

For each system, the plan must define:

- what kind of living being uses it;
- what action constitutes use;
- whether a nonliving focus is involved;
- what the system can and cannot do;
- its local cost or limit, if any;
- its relationship to consent and authority;
- its cultural interpretation;
- what physically happens when living capacity becomes zero;
- which mundane consequences and records remain.

A useful diversity matrix is:

- **Individual embodied practice:** power originates in one living body without necessary apparatus.
- **Living symbiosis:** two distinct living kinds cooperate, with neither reduced to equipment.
- **Communal infrastructure:** many living contributors sustain a public function through original procedures and nonliving conduits.
- **Spiritual or sovereign practice:** an original being’s power, worship, and authority have political and religious consequences when power ends.

These are structural categories, not systems to copy literally. The architect must invent original capabilities, costs, vocabulary, organisms, tools, rituals, and history. Do not borrow recognizable mechanics such as branch solar fire, dusk thresholds, transformation tokens, living portal seams, bloodline timepieces, prophecy schedules, dragon alliance scales, memory-cost severance, magical waterwork contracts, or any renamed equivalent.

The world-wide event may strike all systems simultaneously without explaining them as one system. One original culture can call its observed capacity `maximum mana`; other original cultures should experience cessation through their own language and tests.

## Establishing world-wide reach

The event is world-wide fact, not a conclusion drawn from one town’s broken meter.

Use two complementary methods:

- The narrative frame authoritatively establishes that living magic has ended everywhere in the present material world and will never return.
- Characters receive bounded corroboration through original, preferably mundane communication: physical signal stations, couriers, mechanical radio, printed reports, optical relays, ships, or another newly invented nonmagical network.

Show a small number of specific consequences from distinct original communities rather than a generic montage. The central plot should remain in one primary location with a scene-manageable cast, while three or four sharply different reports or brief viewpoint widenings demonstrate global reach.

Do not claim that the characters scientifically prove all future time. Permanence is authorized by the narrative, while characters may understand only that every independent test and report agrees.

## Present-material-world ontology boundary

The terminal event reaches:

- every living material-world user of magic;
- original gods or magical beings physically present in that world insofar as they use magic;
- material-world magical infrastructure that requires living agency;
- material-world endpoints of original crossing systems;
- stored magical effects according to their individually defined terminal behavior.

It does not automatically reach:

- earlier eras or overwrite prior events;
- alternate histories or timelines;
- other worlds or planes;
- the universal afterlife;
- deceased continuing persons;
- destinations beyond a failed material-world crossing;
- memories, records, scars, relationships, or physical changes created earlier.

If an original god loses all power, the worship-power-authority conjunction required for godhood no longer holds, although worship, legal office, cultural memory, and the underlying person may persist. (`universe/rules.md` — `## Divinity`)

If an original magical species loses its faculty, survival must follow the biology established for that species. Do not make a universal rule that magical beings all die, all become mundane, or all remain healthy.

If an original constructed or transformed person loses magical function, the story must not reduce that person to broken equipment or invalidate their identity.

## Original artifact rules

At least three original artifacts must pay off causally. Audit them separately; never apply one rule to all artifacts.

For each artifact, the plan must state:

1. creator and date or historical period;
2. current owner or custodian;
3. why the protagonist possesses or can access it;
4. whether it is living or nonliving;
5. ordinary material components;
6. former magical function;
7. exact living-agency requirement;
8. whether it stores charge;
9. its state when living capacity reaches zero;
10. remaining nonmagical function;
11. causal payoff;
12. final disposition.

Safe artifact patterns include:

- a formerly magical instrument whose physical geometry, engraved record, container, lens, key teeth, leverage, insulation, or structural material remains useful;
- an always-mundane redundancy received for past service;
- a finite precharged object whose last residue has a predetermined bounded release and no decision-making capacity.

Stored charge is not a refuge for continuing magic. If an artifact contains residue when living users first reach zero, its mechanism must be explicit. It may be released only through its already defined non-agentic behavior and a living person’s ordinary physical choice, or may dissipate into ordinary heat, light, motion, or inert matter. Every residue must be spent, dissipated, or rendered permanently inaccessible by the ending.

Artifacts cannot:

- speak or choose unless independently established as living original characters;
- awaken because the protagonist is secretly special;
- rebuild magic;
- contain a hidden surviving user;
- copy another story’s distinctive object with a new name;
- turn favors, names, promises, grief, or sacrifice into universal fuel.

## World-scale consequences to dramatize

The story must show consequences across at least five domains:

- **Civilizational:** magical medicine, transport, food production, communication, defense, construction, climate control, or water access fails in original ways.
- **Ecological:** original magic-dependent species or habitats face bounded physical consequences.
- **Political:** offices and hierarchies justified by magical capacity lose leverage; ordinary institutions must decide whether authority survives power.
- **Spiritual:** worship, ritual, grief, and community identity persist even though supernatural response is gone.
- **Personal:** practitioners lose vocation, bodily capability, social rank, or a defining relationship without losing personhood.
- **Material:** artifacts and infrastructure become hazards, salvage, memorials, or mundane tools.
- **Historical:** records of earlier magic remain true and become vital to deciding what can survive without it.

The protagonist cannot save everyone. The world-scale victory should be a choice about distributed survival rather than one enlarged local rescue—for example, preserving original seed stocks, routes, water access, medical knowledge, records, living populations, and an accountable coordination method across multiple regions.

Magic remains permanently absent. What survives does so through bodies, tools, knowledge, trust, ordinary physics, and freely renewed cooperation.

## Scene-manageable cast guidance

Use approximately five major speakers:

- the lifelong nonmagical protagonist;
- one former high-capacity practitioner;
- one representative of a communal or infrastructural tradition;
- one original nonhuman, symbiotic, constructed, or transformed person;
- one civic, political, medical, or spiritual counterforce.

Additional communities should appear through messengers, brief reports, crowds with specific choices, or tightly bounded interludes. Do not build grandeur by importing established characters or by creating thin renamed analogues of them.

Every major speaker needs:

- a distinct prior relationship to the protagonist;
- an incompatible present aim;
- a meaningful ability to refuse;
- a concrete loss when magic ends;
- an ordinary skill or responsibility that remains;
- a causal contribution to the climax.

None.

### Omissions, not contradictions

Canon does not establish:

- the terminal event’s cause;
- a single universal mana substance;
- the terminal era’s calendar or geography;
- its cultures, systems, species, governments, or religions;
- the protagonist’s final identity, occupation, or relationships;
- how each new artifact handles stored charge;
- how every new magical species survives loss;
- a universal experimental proof of permanence.

These omissions are safe invention space under the revised prompt. They do not require a user ruling.
