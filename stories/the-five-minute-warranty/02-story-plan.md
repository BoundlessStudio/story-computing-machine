# Story plan

## Premise and promise

When a blast shears the only escape lift serving a burning quarry terrace, goblin emergency engineer Kezli is offered the civic contract she has wanted on the condition that she perform an impossible permanent repair. She refuses the false promise. Instead, she and loadwright Olbit build a nonmagical, deliberately sacrificial transmission that can make the mismatched surviving parts cooperate for exactly five minutes at the declared evacuation load. Its five colored friction leaves make the remaining service life visible, and its terminal break is designed rather than accidental.

The story promises to demonstrate that this local goblin crew is not merely clever at improvising: they diagnose the original failure, calculate the load and ratios, state the limits, negotiate the use case, build safeguards, operate the rig through predicted degradation, and leave an honest failure record. The five-minute window buys human lift director Nalda Vick a real choice. She can obey a council order to spend one of four available lift loads retrieving the civic strongbox, or she can leave the strongbox below and evacuate all forty people. She chooses the people in a signed, witnessed order whose financial and professional consequences survive the machine.

The prompt's “anything” appears as local sales-room hyperbole and hostile shorthand—people in this city say Kezli's workshop can “make anything work”—not as a universal fact about goblins. Kezli's precise correction is the story's operating ethic: her crew can make *these parts* agree, under *this load*, for five minutes.

## Story controls

- **Target:** Approximately 3,300 words, within the required 2,500–4,000-word range.
- **Viewpoint:** Close third person on Kezli, the goblin lead engineer. Remain inside her observations, calculations, professional anxieties, and changing definition of a successful repair.
- **Tense:** Past.
- **Tone/genre:** Warm, high-pressure comic fantasy with sincere civic stakes. Humor comes from Kezli's severe warranty language, Olbit's dry load accounting, clients mistaking predicted noise for catastrophe, and goblin confidence grounded in measurements. Do not make goblin bodies, intelligence, or safety disposable punchlines.
- **Rating:** Teen. Smoke, heat, panic, a bruised lift attendant, and the threat of a terrace fire are acceptable. Keep injury non-graphic; show evacuation discipline and accountability.
- **System choice:** Entirely mundane machinery. The lift, temporary transmission, governor, indicators, and safety release are nonliving and nonmagical. Coal combustion supplies the energy. No living being supplies magic, and no machine speaks, chooses, suffers, or behaves ambiguously like a person.
- **Era and place:** An otherwise unplaced fantasy-industrial era in one unnamed quarry city in the single physical world. Do not connect it to any established era, city, culture, artifact, or mechanism.

## Character engine

### Kezli — close-third protagonist and goblin emergency engineer

- **Want:** Win a full civic maintenance contract and force the lift office to recognize her workshop as more than a provider of embarrassing temporary patches.
- **Need:** Stop measuring her worth by permanence or by skeptical human approval. Practice the deeper professional truth she already knows: an engineer owes people an honest capability and boundary, then must let the responsible human choose what the purchased interval is for.
- **Pressure:** Forty people are cut off below; smoke is moving toward them; the lift's permanent drive cannot be rebuilt in time; Nalda previously dismissed Kezli's bid as “short-term work”; council property competes with passenger capacity; and Kezli knows that one unsafe extra minute could turn a successful rescue into a fall.
- **Arc:** She begins tempted to make the temporary rig look more permanent than it is because she wants respect. Diagnosis makes concealment impossible. She publicly stamps a five-minute warranty, refuses Nalda's request for an optimistic sixth minute, and refuses to decide whether property or people ride. During operation she trusts her calculations instead of appeasing frightened spectators. At terminal cutoff she declines an unnecessary restart for the strongbox. She ends understanding that exact, spent usefulness can earn trust without pretending to be permanence.

### Olbit — goblin loadwright and Kezli's operating partner

- He independently checks tooth counts, passenger weights, collar clearance, and safety-brake engagement; his competence prevents Kezli from becoming a lone legendary fixer.
- His dry humor keeps the tone warm: he treats wildly improvised materials as ordinary line items but treats an unstated passenger weight as an outrage.
- He challenges Kezli when her desire for the contract nudges her toward promising six minutes. His role is not merely comic support: he designs the flexure brace, monitors the load, and performs the planned pressure adjustment at the third warning stage.

### Director Nalda Vick — human director of the North Rise lift

- **Want:** Save everyone, preserve the civic strongbox, keep the lift office solvent, and avoid admitting that postponed foundation work helped turn a repairable break into a total drive failure.
- **Need/choice:** Accept that responsibility is not the same as preserving every asset. She must decide what the finite interval is for, put the decision in writing, and bear its aftermath rather than asking Kezli to absorb the moral and legal risk.
- **Arc:** Nalda initially repeats the city's condescending distinction between “proper engineering” and goblin emergency work. Kezli's transparent diagnosis forces her to recognize both the crew's expertise and her own agency. When the council orders the strongbox retrieved, Nalda signs “persons before property,” leaving it behind. After the rescue she does not hide behind the goblins or the emergency; she records her maintenance failure and commissions a permanent rebuild led and inspected by the people whose temporary work behaved exactly as declared.

### Relationship movement

Kezli and Nalda begin as frustrated bidder and skeptical gatekeeper. Kezli's refusal to lie initially feels to Nalda like another limitation; Nalda's signed priority decision initially feels to Kezli like a client finally doing her own job. They become professional allies by respecting the boundary between engineering fact and human value choice. No romance is planned.

## Causal arc

1. A lower-terrace kiln-shed blast jolts the North Rise funicular, shears its worn crown gear, and shifts the drive pedestal. The stair road is buried, so the stalled lift becomes the only possible evacuation route for forty people ahead of the spreading fire.
2. The ordinary lift mechanics correctly conclude that a permanent gear repair will take days. Nalda calls Kezli's nearby workshop because of its local emergency reputation while still demanding a permanent result.
3. Kezli and Olbit prove that the boiler, cable drums, cages, and landing brakes remain usable, but prove just as firmly that a replacement toothed gear cannot survive the misalignment. Kezli notices that the smooth remnants of the two incompatible shafts can accept a compliant friction bridge.
4. The goblins calculate a governed five-minute operating envelope at four ten-person loads. They create a flexing roller train and a five-leaf sacrificial sleeve, with a designed safe release after the fifth leaf. Because the timetable has no spare passenger cycle, Nalda must declare the purpose before startup.
5. A council message orders Nalda to use one load for guards and the civic strongbox. Kezli supplies consequences, not a moral command: retrieving it will leave ten people below. Nalda signs a contrary order, assigning all four loads to evacuation and accepting liability.
6. The rig runs. Each consumed leaf visibly advances the deadline. Apparent failures—smoke, a kicked brace, showers of harmless scale—are anticipated transitions that the goblins manage. Four passenger loads rise while the strongbox remains below.
7. At exactly five minutes, the fifth leaf strips, the shear link parts, steam vents, and the original landing brake catches the final cage as designed. The lower station is then lost to the fire and rockfall, making Nalda's choice irreversible.
8. All forty evacuees survive. The temporary drive is spent and cannot disguise the need for a permanent rebuild. Nalda records her responsibility, gives Kezli's crew paid authority on that rebuild, and preserves the completed warranty as evidence of competent limitation rather than failure.

### Scene 1 — The answer is not a repair

- Open on the violent, unmistakable mechanical failure: the lift shudders, crown-gear teeth punch through the inspection cover, and the landing brake catches. Kezli identifies the sound before the human mechanics open the housing, establishing expertise without boasting.
- Use signal flags/speaking wire to establish forty living evacuees at the lower terrace, grouped into four loads of ten. Smoke has crossed the kiln yard; the buried stair road will not reopen in time. One lift attendant has a bruised shoulder but can organize loading.
- Nalda's earlier rejection of Kezli's maintenance bid surfaces. Her bias is institutional and personal, not narrated as truth: she equated short warranties with poor work and deferred repairs to the drive pedestal. Kezli feels the temptation to promise the impossible merely to defeat that judgment.
- End when Kezli says the permanent answer is no, then points to the intact boiler shaft and cable drum. The parts cannot mesh, but they can touch.

### Scene 2 — Five leaves fit

- Kezli and Olbit inspect independently and compare results: boiler pressure is stable; cable and cages pass a rapid visual/load check; both landing brakes engage; the crown gear is fragmented; the pedestal has shifted by more than toothed gearing can tolerate.
- Show why common alternatives fail: welding the brittle crown fragments would crack under the first load; a leather belt would walk off the misaligned rims; recasting and cutting a gear plus resetting the pedestal takes days; direct coupling would spin the drum too fast and remove braking control.
- Kezli proposes a spring-mounted contact roller carrying a compliant sleeve between the engine flywheel and hoist drum. Olbit adds a second roller and weighted flexure brace so the train can follow the shifted axis. A salvaged ore-sorter flywheel smooths the boiler pistons; a ball governor made from scale weights holds the tested speed. These are deliberate functions, not a pile of lucky junk.
- They press a small sample against the powered shaft. Wear rate, contact temperature, and the measured clearance show that five standard leaves of resin-bound oakcloth fit. A sixth prevents engagement; making all six thinner would expose iron before four loads complete. Kezli announces five minutes under declared load, with no rounding.

### Scene 3 — The warranty is part of the machine

- Construction uses mundane salvage with assigned purposes: cart springs become the flexure; straight ore-rail becomes the brace; the sorter flywheel buffers pulses; five differently dyed oakcloth leaves form the sacrificial sleeve; copper tabs strike a clapper when each layer clears; a notched bronze shear link isolates the final drive; the existing ratchet brake remains the last stop.
- Olbit makes Nalda supply a load sheet. The exchange should be funny because the improvised apparatus looks outrageous while the goblins' paperwork is exact. Kezli refuses passengers described as “roughly adult.” Ten load marks per cage are established using people and ballast at the upper landing.
- Kezli writes/stamps the operating ticket: four passenger loads; governed speed; five minutes from clutch engagement; blue, yellow, orange, red, then bare iron; expected smoke; expected clatter; mandatory clearance at red; terminal shear, steam dump, and brake engagement. This is disclosure, not a magical contract.
- Kezli briefly considers writing six minutes to satisfy Nalda. Olbit asks whether the extra minute will be made of ink. Kezli corrects the ticket to the measured five and signs.

### Scene 4 — What the interval is for

- Before engagement, a council instruction arrives over the speaking wire: send guards down on the first empty cage, then use the returning cage for the civic strongbox. Nalda argues that the money funds rebuilding and wages, so the conflict is not cartoon greed.
- Kezli lays out the fixed consequence. Four complete passenger loads fit inside the rig's travel and loading budget. A strongbox cycle means one group of ten remains when the red leaf ends. Reducing speed worsens heat dwell; increasing speed overruns the landing brake. There is no hidden fifth trip.
- Nalda asks Kezli to decide, implicitly trying to transfer blame. Kezli says the machine can price the choices in minutes and loads; it cannot decide whose future counts. This is the pivot in Kezli's need.
- Nalda writes, signs, and has the unnamed council courier witness her override: all loads are for people; the strongbox stays. She transmits it to the lower attendant, making the decision operational before startup and durable afterward.

### Scene 5 — Five-minute operation

- **5:00 to 4:00 — white/outer leaf:** Kezli engages the clutch. The improbable roller train grips, the empty cage descends, and the loaded counter-cage begins to rise. The lift visibly works. Kezli calls speed; Olbit calls pressure; Nalda calls the passenger manifest. At the first minute the outer leaf tears away in smoking ribbons and its copper tab rings once. Onlookers flinch; Kezli identifies the expected blue layer and keeps the machine running.
- **4:00 to 3:00 — blue leaf:** First ten evacuees arrive; loading swaps below. The council courier again raises the strongbox order. Nalda publicly repeats her override and sends the empty cage back. The blue surface darkens exactly along Kezli's chalked wear line. This pairs the visible mechanical countdown with the cost of Nalda's choice.
- **3:00 to 2:00 — yellow leaf:** The second load rises. Heat expansion pushes the contact roller toward the edge of its allowed travel and the brace kicks hard, an anticipated danger named on the ticket. Olbit inserts the premeasured second fulcrum pin and shifts the spring weight; the roller recenters. The correction demonstrates planned adaptability rather than luck. Yellow peels, the second/third-stage clapper sounds, and orange appears.
- **2:00 to 1:00 — orange leaf:** The third load rises, including the bruised attendant after another worker takes over below. Resin smoke and scale make the rig look doomed, but the governor remains on its painted line and the cable drum remains steady. Nalda helps clear arrivals instead of hovering over Kezli, showing earned trust. The orange tab drops; Kezli orders everyone but the operating trio behind the shield line as red appears.
- **1:00 to 0:00 — red/final leaf:** The fourth and final group rises. The lower signal confirms no people remain; only the strongbox does. Olbit reads the final wear mark. Kezli is tempted by the quiet hope that the red leaf might exceed its warranty, but she orders the boiler tender to cut fuel and prepares the dump. The final cage reaches the upper landing with seconds remaining.
- **Terminal behavior at 0:00:** The red leaf strips to bare iron; loss of compliant grip spikes the preset shear link; the link parts; the spring brace snaps the roller away; a mechanical latch opens the steam dump; and the original ratchet brake catches the cable drum at the landing. Noise, sparks, and a burst of harmless steam look catastrophic but match the ticket in order. No explosion or uncontrolled drop occurs. Kezli refuses the courier's request to re-pack the sleeve for a strongbox trip because the shaft is heat-scored, the brace has spent its spring travel, and the lower station is already being overtaken.

### Scene 6 — What remains

- A secondary rockfall closes the lower station after the people are clear, burying the strongbox and preventing a convenient later recovery. Its loss and Nalda's written responsibility persist.
- Inventory the spent mechanism: five stripped leaves, a cleanly parted shear link, a scored but stationary shaft, a caught brake, and a cracked pedestal still requiring reconstruction. The temporary success does not become a secret permanent fix.
- Nalda dictates two records: her own maintenance deferral and priority override, and an emergency-performance report stating that Kezli's rig met its declared duration, load, warnings, and shutdown. She faces the coming council inquiry without blaming the crew.
- Nalda pays the emergency invoice and commissions the permanent pedestal/gear rebuild with Kezli's workshop as paid lead and the ordinary lift mechanics as independent inspectors. This resolves the professional stake through demonstrated competence and transparent accountability, not a claim that goblin work is universally superior.

## Stakes and reversals

- **Life:** Forty workers and family members, including one non-graphically injured attendant, will be overtaken if the lift cannot make four loads.
- **Mechanical:** An unsafe extension can overheat the shaft, defeat the brake, and turn a controlled temporary drive into a cage fall. Kezli's refusal to exceed the declared terminal state matters as much as making it start.
- **Civic/material:** The strongbox funds public obligations, and its burial creates a real recovery cost. Nalda also risks her job and must expose postponed maintenance.
- **Professional:** Kezli risks having an intentionally spent device misread as proof that goblin engineering is shoddy. The exact match between warranty and performance reverses that interpretation.
- **Reversal 1:** “Impossible to repair” becomes “possible to operate,” but only after Kezli narrows the claim and makes limitation part of the design.
- **Reversal 2:** Nalda's desired sixth minute is not an engineering challenge to conquer; it is an attempt to avoid choosing. Kezli returns ownership of the choice.
- **Reversal 3:** The council's strongbox order turns a simple rescue into a voluntary, costly human decision rather than letting the machine dictate the ending.
- **Reversal 4:** Each alarming sign of apparent failure proves the goblins' competence because it arrives in the disclosed order and triggers a planned response.
- **Reversal 5:** The terminal self-destruction is the machine succeeding at its final requirement. The true failure exposed afterward is deferred maintenance and an institution that treated honest limits as inferiority.

## Setup and payoff

- Kezli hears the damaged crown gear before anyone opens the cover; during the run she identifies each later sound against the warranty, paying off her diagnostic ear without giving her supernatural perception.
- Nalda's rejected maintenance bid establishes both the shifted pedestal and Kezli's hunger for legitimacy; Nalda later records the deferral and hires the workshop for the permanent rebuild.
- Olbit's insistence on exact passenger mass becomes the four-load plan and prevents a last-minute chest substitution.
- The test coupon, five-leaf clearance, chalk lines, colors, tabs, shear link, steam dump, and brake test all appear before startup and pay off in the countdown/terminal sequence.
- The phrase “anything work” begins as a condescending joke. Kezli's scoped correction governs the successful mechanism and can be repeated in the performance record without universalizing goblins.
- Nalda's blank priority line on the operating ticket becomes the physical place where she writes the durable choice. The ticket survives after the rig does not.

## Ending and final image

The rescue, machine, human choice, and professional relationship all resolve on the same morning. The lift is safely dead, not mysteriously healed. The lower station and strongbox are gone; forty people live; Nalda owns her signed override and maintenance failure; Kezli and Olbit are paid and given accountable work on a permanent rebuild.

**Ending image:** On the silent lift housing, Nalda pins the red-stamped warranty beside the new rebuild order. Five colored boxes are punched through, exactly as promised; below them, forty soot-marked fingerprints crowd the line reserved for witnesses. Kezli looks past the spent leaves to the cracked pedestal they will repair properly next, content that the five-minute machine does not need to last for its consequence to do so.

## Canon constraints

- Treat every goblin, workshop practice, reputation, warranty custom, and engineering method as local to Kezli, Olbit, their workshop, and this unnamed city. Do not state or imply biological engineering talent, universal goblin behavior, a shared global civilization, or a fixed five-minute limit for goblin work.
- “Anything” remains controlled professional hyperbole. The plan demonstrates extraordinary recombination within measured physical constraints, not omniscience, reality alteration, or automatic mastery.
- Keep the setting in one unnamed fantasy-industrial era of the single physical world. Do not identify it with Ravel, the Glass-Sea era, the modern-like trough, Qarsis, Ersene, Sablewake, the Crown-era kingdom, or any other established location/era.
- Keep the North Rise and every repair component nonliving, nonmagical, and non-agentic. Mechanical automatic actions—governing, latching, venting, braking—must never be described as choice, desire, pain, refusal, or spellcasting.
- Use “engineering,” “craft,” and any skeptical use of “magic” only as viewpoint-cultural labels. The mechanism itself is described through materials, force, heat, speed, clearance, and combustion.
- Do not borrow Hadrik's lone-smith shape, plot-armor harness, consent ring, or three-use redesign; Qarsis, waterworks, sunwheel, bimetal regulator, brine exchange, or two-stroke contract; the eleven-foundation emergency topology; or Cinder Annex's living constructs/visual relay.
- Make goblin competence causal and collaborative. Kezli and Olbit both diagnose, calculate, construct, test, operate, disclose risk, and inspect aftermath. Humor cannot rely on stupidity, disposability, biological recklessness, or random survival.
- The limit, warnings, and terminal behavior must be introduced before operation and occur in the promised order. Do not add a secret permanent fix, surprise magical reserve, random extra minute, or improvised restart.
- The human choice must remain Nalda's. The ticket is ordinary evidence, not a magical oath; Kezli explains consequences but neither coerces nor decides for her.
- Keep all reusable setting/character/mechanism material proposed only. This plan establishes no canon, and later delta work must preserve local qualifiers.

## Failure modes to watch

- Do not let the build become a montage of unexplained junk. Every salvaged item must have the function listed in the engineering audit, and both goblins must make visible decisions.
- Do not call the five-minute life a goblin trait. It follows from clearance, material thickness, governed speed, declared load, heat, and the chosen safety threshold for this rig.
- Do not make Nalda's skepticism the narrator's view. Her deferred maintenance and Kezli's accurate disclosure should expose the local prejudice without turning every human into a caricature.
- Do not hide the time limit for a final joke. State it, write it, test it, display it, and let readers track every color transition.
- Do not reward boundary-breaking with a miraculous sixth minute. The final passenger load must arrive inside the calculated window, and the drive must become unusable at zero.
- Do not let the strongbox choice vanish in the aftermath. Its burial, Nalda's signed liability, and the city's recovery cost are the durable price of choosing all forty lives.
- Do not anthropomorphize the rig beyond casual metaphor. If a sentence makes it seem to want, decide, complain, suffer, or use magic, replace it with concrete mechanical behavior.
- Do not imply the emergency patch removes the need for ordinary engineering. The cracked pedestal and crown gear remain, and the ending explicitly begins a paid, inspected permanent rebuild.
- Do not broaden canon through a named era, cross-story echo, universal mechanic, or authoritative goblin lore. All inventions remain candidate-local until separately promoted.
