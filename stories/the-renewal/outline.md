# Promised experience

“Marriage is a contract” and “a yearly term” still make the renewal negotiation an actual choice for both spouses. The later request, “i have read the story and make it man and women and give it some twist...”, calls for a husband and wife and asks for a surprise after the reader has met this story. I read that as a reason to let the reader recognize the old bargain before one spouse overturns what the other can offer. The ghost is already familiar. I want the pleasure of finding out what a spouse has been doing out of the other's sight and feeling that discovery put a different year of marriage within reach. Tone, setting and which spouse is alive remain open.

## Takes considered

- **The other ballroom.** The living husband proposes bringing his dead wife onto the dance floor he runs. She offers him a ballroom of her own among the dead. The renewal can put him in her world for the year, if she can offer him a place in it as her husband.
- **The audience bought the anniversary.** The wife learns halfway through the negotiation that the husband has sold tickets to a live renewal. He has to choose between making good on what he sold and accepting the conditions under which she is willing to sign.
- **The lease is gone.** Two people open the annual negotiation expecting the choice between the band's room and the ballroom. The husband has already sold the hall to get out of the work that took him away from her. She bargains for a room he has never been willing to call home.

The other ballroom gives the wife a life and a bid the husband has to reckon with, then gives him a choice she has to earn. Its last dance can show their roles reversed as a consequence of what they signed.

## Provisional direction

Ellis Galt runs the Lark Assembly Rooms. His wife, Rilla Toller, died last December, and their current marriage gives her entry to the little band room where he comes upstairs to see her. The contract names one room, on either side, for a year. It gives the spouse on the other side the choice of stepping through a doorway to the named room and returning the same way. Anyone in the room can see and touch that spouse. Ellis has seen Rilla go out the band room door and leave the living landing empty. Their term ends at midnight, when they lose contact unless they both sign again.

Ellis brings up the familiar late-set bill with her name on it and offers the Lark's main ballroom. He thinks he knows what he can offer her there. Rilla lays a folded programme from her coat beside his proof. Her name is on it as leader of the orchestra at the Plover Rooms. She has drawn a roomful of regulars among the dead and wants Ellis to come there. He knew she played occasionally. He has been looking for ways to get her back to a life she has in fact gone on making.

He offers the band room upstairs again, where he can scrape together a visit at the end of his shift. Rilla refuses. He resists coming to the Plover to be another person waiting for her to finish. They tell each other what happened the anniversary when she played every request and he took his shoes up to the landing to hear the dance she'd promised him. Rilla will leave the contract unsigned if the band room is the only room he will put down. Ellis asks for the first Saturday late set of every month as time together on her side. He will pay a relief cashier to take his own busiest set. Rilla will hand her late set and its fee to her second pianist on those nights. She can still play the rest of her season. This is the first Saturday, and both late sets begin after the midnight renewal. They write the room and the protected hours, and both sign. Ellis gives up having Rilla appear at the Lark for this term. Rilla gives up the private room where she has been able to hear the old band and keep Ellis to herself.

## Craft plan

- **Teller.** Stay close to Ellis as he comes upstairs. His livelihood is in notices, tickets and small promises made in a loud room. A trial can test his first-person defensiveness against close third, in which his hands might give his arithmetic away. Keeping the offer and programme a surprise to him lets the reader find out alongside him.
- **Ellis.** A good host with a crooked thumbnail from opening the post and an instinct to ask for a ticket when somebody asks something of him. He brings a particular offer and wants Rilla to accept in time for the last dance. He assumes her other engagements must be occasional. The proof flat under his arm is his care and his presumption.
- **Rilla.** A pianist who writes two extra beats for a bad knee and likes a room to wait for the first note. She can tell him in order how a few tunes with whoever could play became a band, a paid set, and regulars who know she has a husband. She wants him to see that room and take his share of a year in it. She assumes he will be as glad to come as she was to hear him ask for her.
- **The conversation owed.** Will you marry me for another year, and which of us will put down our work and come to the other? The old anniversary account cuts into the new terms. Rilla remembers how much she liked knowing he would wait. Ellis can name how long he waited and where he went when she kept playing. He can ask for a set with her on the floor, and she can give the bench and its fee to someone else while he is there.
- **Place.** The Lark's band room keeps the rainy November night, the warm chair and iron radiator, scorched dust, the brass grille carrying a request, and a brass saddle that leads to a landing Ellis can touch. The Fellcross registry would put its own business between the spouses. Their flat would leave the music and other people's claims outside. The Plover Rooms occupy a former covered market in the town where Rilla now lives. The room has amber stall lamps, the smell of hot lemon peel from the tea counter, squares of ridged matting on the stone, shoes hitting the boards laid across the middle, and the old market bell that still marks the end of a set. The columns push the dancers close to the piano steps. Rilla has to come past those steps to reach Ellis on the crowded floor.

## Scene briefs

1. **Before midnight, 20 percent.** Ellis leaves the Lark's till with the attendant who covered it when Rilla was ill. He is expected back to take the late admissions. In the band room, a hand on the brass knob recalls seeing Rilla go through that door while he stood on the empty landing. He wants to take her downstairs under a new term. She wants him to hear her own proposal. The cup and the broad proof make a reader of the earlier story expect her objection to the billing.
2. **The negotiation, 65 percent.** Rilla answers with her own folded programme, carried in the coat she arrived in, and asks for the room named at its top. Give her one short account of how the band came to be, then let Ellis try again for the old room. He refuses to come to the Plover to sit through her set. A request through the grille brings the anniversary back. Let both tell it at length, including what Rilla liked about keeping Ellis waiting and what he had to do with his shoes. The accounts give him the terms he wants and her a reason to meet them. Before signing, he asks how he could get back to the Lark if he's needed. She tells him which door to use and how to come back the way she's done all year. She signs after he has written in the time he will be off work. He signs after she names the pianist who will take her place and what she will pay.
3. **The new room, 15 percent.** Rilla leaves the band room as she has all year. Ellis gives the attendant the cashbox key and pays her to take the late set. After midnight he puts his hand back on the band room's brass knob, opens the door with the Plover in mind and steps into the place she put on the paper. He can step back onto his own landing the same way. At the Plover, Rilla hands her set to her second pianist and passes the steps. A regular knows Ellis's name and shifts a chair. Rilla comes far enough through the crowd to ask him for the dance herself.

## Ending

Ellis stays on her side of the threshold and takes Rilla's hand. Her band starts the late set with somebody else at the piano. Load the final turn with the feet that waited on the Lark landing, the two rooms each could keep by refusing the other, and Rilla leaving the first beat to someone else.
