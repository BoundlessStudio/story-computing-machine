# Promised experience

“Marriage is a contract” makes the bargain literal, and “Yours has a yearly term” puts a spouse inside a marriage that can change or end at this anniversary. “It’s time for your renewal negotiation” promises the encounter itself. I read the pleasure as hearing two people with a shared history make offers about their future, and seeing what each will give up or ask outright once another year ceases to be a certainty. The prompt sets the couple at the center and leaves the setting, tone, scale, and outcome open. In this take the bargaining is funny until it puts something they genuinely love at risk, and the result can be felt in the room they choose.

## Takes considered

- **The dressing room.** Two married stage comics have been licensing their domestic stories to each other by the year. On the evening of a live broadcast, one presses for the right to use the best story they have and the other names the one exchange that could make her give it away.
- **The sky above a city.** A marriage between two rulers draws their cities together for one year at a time. At the renewal each asks to make the other city their common home, for incompatible reasons, and they bargain in sight of the streets about to separate.
- **The ballroom.** A living dance-hall proprietor and her dead wife have shared the old band room upstairs for a year. The negotiation can move their marriage into the ballroom below, if they can agree on whose company they will be there. This gives both wives a real veto and puts the consequences of each offer within earshot of the table. The final change is something they can do together the night they sign.

## Provisional direction

Alis Galt and Rilla Dacre have had years of annual contracts. Rilla was still alive when they put the band room into the current one. In Fellcross a marriage between a living person and a dead one lets them see and touch each other in one room they name for the term. Rilla comes there from her own life among the dead by choice. At midnight they need both signatures for another year together.

Alis asks for the ballroom when she brings Rilla a proof of the new season's handbill, still selling Saturdays under Rilla's name. She wants to take her wife on the floor in sight of the regulars who have spent the year commiserating with her. Rilla spent her working life on that bandstand. She wants a room where Alis will come to see her and where she can choose when to play. She offers another year upstairs with guests allowed. Alis refuses and, when Rilla pulls the renewal toward herself, says she might let it expire. Upstairs has begun to feel like an obligation she carries out at the edge of her working night.

Rilla counters with the ballroom on terms of her own. Alis must drop her name from the bills and turn one Saturday dance a month into a free public dance. Rilla will go if nobody there has bought a ticket under the impression they've paid to see her. Alis bargains over the cost and asks to reserve that date for the two of them, with neither on duty. Rilla agrees and gets time with Alis alone after the hall shuts on the other Saturdays. Alis trades a familiar draw and a night's receipts each month for an appearance Rilla promises to make as her wife. Rilla gives up a private room where she can count on being unobserved. Each signs. When the new term begins, Rilla comes to the floor and asks Alis to dance the last number.

## Craft plan

- **Teller.** Try Alis in first person that night, quick with a justification for everything she has put in print, against close third on Rilla, whose ear is downstairs before Alis arrives. The first can let the proprietor's self-defense turn funny and then costly. The second can follow a musician's appetite for a room she has reason to avoid.
- **Alis.** Lavish about a roomful of strangers and stingy about what her own hours cost. She can tell by the coats how long a night will pay. She tends to talk in figures and anticipates objections out loud, then slips in a request while the listener is waiting for the sum. She knows the numbers on the bill. She assumes Rilla will be glad to find that her name still brings the regulars back.
- **Rilla.** A bandleader who will make a loud room wait and who remembers which shy dancers kept time in their chairs. She misses the floor and loves that people remember her playing. She has let herself believe Alis wants to hire her back along with her name. She gives directions by ear, lets requests sit through a bar of music, and tells a story at length when she can make it do some work for her. Both women are original characters.
- **The conversation owed.** Will you marry me for another year, and what will you promise me if I sign? It belongs in the band room. Rilla tells the story of their last anniversary dance, when she let one request after another keep her at the piano while Alis waited. Alis knows that she was left waiting. She has never heard how Rilla watched her from the bandstand and liked being able to make her wait. Rilla tells it now because another year on those terms would cost her the thing she wants to keep.
- **Place.** Lark Assembly Rooms at the Fellcross tram turnaround. A municipal registry would put paperwork between them and the disputed room. Their flat would give Alis too easy a retreat into privacy. Here the old band room is one flight above a working ballroom. On a rainy November Saturday around midnight, the house band changes sets while Alis's staff check coats and sell admissions. The sprung maple moves under a crowd, the brass vent between floors rattles on low notes, the upstairs radiator scorches dust, wet wool steams on the banquettes, and the old penciled band lists show through whitewash. A stuck louver lets through even speaking voices on a lull. They hear a request for Rilla's old number during the bargain. At the end the floor offers her a place beside Alis among the people she's spent the year hearing through the vent. ## Scene briefs

1. **The set before midnight, 20 percent.** In the ballroom a regular offers Alis his wife's arm for the last dance, as he does on every Saturday when Alis would have danced with Rilla. Upstairs she wants Rilla to come downstairs at midnight as her wife. Rilla, who has heard the dedication from the bandstand, wants to know what else Alis has promised. Alis lays the printed proof beside the renewal, and Rilla sets her cup on her own name. The audience Alis wants to dance with carries expectations Alis has helped create.
2. **The negotiation, 65 percent.** In the band room, Alis wants her invitation accepted and Rilla wants a credible alternative to working the room. Let them bargain through renewal upstairs with guests, letting the year lapse, and the cost and hours of a dance in the ballroom together. Rilla's account makes room for her pride as a performer and her certainty that Alis would wait. Sound from the floor interrupts them where they are most tempted to settle for an old understanding. Alis commits to changing the bill before she knows Rilla will take her terms. Rilla takes the pen only after Alis has put hours for the two of them in writing. The important marks on the paper are in different hands.
3. **The last number, 15 percent.** Just after midnight, Alis has to hand over the floor to the band without making a dedication, and Rilla wants a dance before either woman can retreat to work. Rilla steps past her old station at the piano. She takes the lead and has to steer them around the very couple who offered Alis a partner. Alis feels the floor give under their shared weight. Summarize how the women chose the room last year. Leave the nature of Rilla's days among the dead outside the story.

## Ending

Rilla leads Alis into the room on the first night of the new term. The piano that kept her at work at their previous anniversary, the courtesy Alis was offered as a widow, and the time they bargained to spend as spouses load that first turn among the dancers.
