# Outline

## Story

- Premise and central promise: Ten thousand years after the last pharaoh was entombed, genetics fulfills the burial promise: clones of Cleopatra VII and other ancient rulers awaken. Across sixty years Cleopatra knowingly claims the name, rebuilds Egypt, and makes it a black-gold technological super-empire governing most of the world—without being genetic programming.
- Focal pressure or attachment: Cleopatra wants the return to be true enough that she is not an exhibit. She loves Egypt as something she can make but cannot remember, and needs Hatshepsut—the peer who understands inherited sovereignty—to recognize her freely.
- Counterforce or complication: Hatshepsut will build but refuses possession; Ramesses wants conquest and equal precedence. Regions joining for water, energy, medicine, and orbital protection become difficult to leave. Cleopatra converts need into allegiance while making resurrection resemble annexation. No participant is a simple villain.
- POV, distance, and information limit: Close third past through an ambitious, vain, aging Cleopatra. She has no ancient memories or access to another clone's mind. Incomplete archives mediate history; filtered petitions and changing court matter reveal imperial scale.
- Governing movement and time shape: Four throne returns across sixty years—exhibit, Nile sovereign, continental emperor, world ruler—compress campaigns through altered surroundings and relationships. The last leaves celebration offstage and contracts into the Giza Renewal Vault as worldwide accession meets Hatshepsut's refusal.
- Speculative rule or ordinary-world constraint: Ancient DNA and accelerated maturation create a near-identical adult; tutoring supplies languages, history, and skills. It cannot restore memory, prove continuity, install loyalty, or dictate temperament. Each clone may reject the assigned identity and ages normally. Solar wings, foundries, genomic medicine, orbitals, and turquoise cooling are technology, never magic.
- Dialogue promise: Reconstructed courtliness collides with present bluntness through titles, omissions, teasing, temper, and sudden plainness. Cleopatra and Hatshepsut share competitive affection; Ramesses brings wounded grandeur.
- Dialogic medium: Speech, formal address, petitions, dispatches, throne-interface maps, gesture, and silence; interfaces only transmit.
- Dialogue engine: Cleopatra needs the clones' voluntary participation; they need her resources without becoming branded ancestors. Every name, endorsement, command, or departure changes leverage and imperial credibility.

## Voice

- Narrative texture: Majestic compression anchored in aging hands, black stone, gold conductors, turquoise coolant, map-light, and fewer unfiltered visitors. Race across decades; slow for misread looks and private choices. Keep splendor visibly manufactured.
- Conversational texture: Halting reconstructed honorifics grow into interruption, dry teasing, petty arguments without old memories, and uneven modern bluntness. Petitions remain local, never a chorus praising competence.
- Rhetorical ownership: Cleopatra owns seductive reframing, theatrical timing, vanity, and selective listening. Hatshepsut uses abrasive humor, corrective affection, practical analogy, and moral authority. Ramesses uses martial simplification, touchy misreading, and embarrassing precedence claims.
- Pressure behavior: Cleopatra becomes publicly fluent and privately evasive. Hatshepsut stops joking, then refuses directly. Humiliation makes Ramesses repeat titles and lose his thread. Listening fails before improving.
- Relationship movement: Cleopatra wants Hatshepsut's recognition but cannot ask her to stay; Hatshepsut wants freedom without denying affection. Separation costs ceremonial leverage but restores trust. Ramesses wants reassurance; he moves from rivalry to conditional allegiance.
- Anti-default: Avoid resurrected executives optimizing infrastructure. Preserve absent memories, vanity, crossed etiquette, failed jokes, jealousy, and asymmetry; empire arises from Cleopatra's appetite and improvisation, not automatic moral triumph.

## Beats

1. Cleopatra wakes in the Giza Renewal Vault knowing languages and biographies but no ancient life. Hatshepsut withholds the word resurrection until Cleopatra chooses her name. Cleopatra chooses name and throne over the planned exhibit.
2. Her court fuses winged solar-falcon geometry, black stone, gold conductors, and turquoise cooling. Cleopatra joins fractured Nile cities to one energy-water spine; Hatshepsut repairs an early overreach without accepting subordination.
3. Twelve years later Alexandria prospers. Ramesses urges conquest; Hatshepsut disputes calling dependence consent. Cleopatra mixes limited force, orbital protection, medicine, spectacle, and treaties. A blocked withdrawal establishes the empire's cost.
4. Compress three decades through throne returns: aging hands, altered regalia, foreign petitions, worldwide solar wings, and clones who serve or leave. Selected local consequences replace campaign catalogue. Cleopatra grows magnificent and insulated as Egypt becomes a superpower.
5. Once most of the world belongs to the New Egyptian Empire, Cleopatra plans an image of one timeless royal house. Ramesses bargains over rank; Hatshepsut rejects the image and permanent office, comparing court to laboratory captivity.
6. In the Vault, the final major accession completes the promise. Cleopatra releases Hatshepsut and will not counterfeit endorsement, accepting dynastic fracture without surrendering empire. End as Ramesses seeks private audience and Cleopatra reduces the distance between their seats; no speech or policy coda.

## Continuity

- Canon used: Deep-time Earth permits technological refoundings; this era occupies the Long Dark's material zero. Memory versus myth limits history. Personhood protection applies to every clone.
- Boundaries and unknowns: Cloning proves no soul, continuity, memory, destiny, afterlife, or canonical resurrection link. Do not connect Egypt elsewhere. Borders and intervening history remain local. Use the reference's long black hair, poised queen, winged solar-falcon throne, black/gold/turquoise palette, warm light, and technological-Egyptian monumentality—not pseudo-glyphs or exact jewelry.
