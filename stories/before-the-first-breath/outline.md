# Provisional outline

The explicit promise is the first humans waking together without memories of their creation, surrounded by the lake, green valleys, mountains, sunsets, paths, ridge monuments, and enormous submerged remains of an older civilization. I infer an expansive, uncanny origin mystery: the pleasure of discovering that this apparently virgin paradise already has a history, and that genuinely first humans need not be the world's first people. The supplied image suggests luminous beauty and immense landscape rather than a ruined wasteland. No central relationship, explanation, tone beyond that contrast, or particular ending is specified. Awe, unease, adventure, and a moving discovery are my proposed treatment, not additional requirements.

Working title: **The World Before Us**. Close third through Neral, one of twenty-nine adults who wake together beneath the island's tree. They possess language and bodily skills, but no personal past. Her companion Orr is quick to try things; she wants to understand them. Their developing friendship supports the expedition; its resolution is not the main payoff.

- The lake is perfectly still when they wake; a pebble makes ordinary ripples. Beneath the clear water, Neral sees an immense avenue with openings unlike anything their bodies need. A pale submerged line reaches a shore path. The group crosses to investigate the world together rather than leaving twenty-seven people waiting for its explanation.
- The path alternates walkable stretches with six-lobed footholds across sheer rock. The ridge monuments are worn, many-limbed figures. Their first hypothesis, that earlier humans walked here, fails as the body impressions, tools, and architecture accumulate. A route over the mountain appears to end in an impassable climb. The others establish a camp near the ridge while Neral and Orr investigate a passage descending toward the lake; their companions' exploration and questions remain part of the narrative.
- Inside the submerged city, routes designed for climbing, six-handed bodies require attention and cooperation. There is no separate broken-bridge escape climax. Physical evidence deepens the question instead of answering it: enormous domestic spaces, human-sized growth chambers, and a model of their island before it bore a tree. Were humans inhabitants, captives, or something being made? They find an image-bearing cylinder with the same arrangement as the ridge monument's aperture. Its minute layered images are unreadable here. Taking this record back is part of investigating their origin, rather than a fetch quest after that origin is already explained.
- At the next sunset the whole group places the record in the surviving display. This is the first full revelation, not a larger repeat: immense recorded figures work, play, endure their world's decline, and deliberately make the first human bodies. An image of a builder's six hands tending a human establishes the relation that the cold growth chambers could not. The first humans were grown unconscious beneath the island and awakened only after the abandoned world became habitable. No former human lives or creation memories are restored. The records cannot explain every cause of the decline or the builders' own beginnings.
- The final sequence shows the builders altering the mountain route for the smaller people who will come after them. The six-handed route which seemed impassable has a second, much lower set of grips and a human-sized passage, now obscured by growth. It leads beyond this nursery valley into the builders' larger world. This changes what the humans can do with a concrete place they have already encountered. Neral recognizes the lower marks; the group chooses to leave the nursery rather than mistake its bounds for the world's. The ending is their first crossing through that passage, out onto a mountainside overlooking further green valleys and older works. The builders' disappearance remains inseparable from this beautiful new beginning: the humans are first, but they are entering someone else's history.

The actual dramatic center is discovery of humanity's place in an older inhabited world. The city supplies material evidence and unsettling possibilities; the sunset supplies the creation revelation and the changed use of a previously impassable route. Protect the builders' bodily difference, the real age of the landscape, and the distinction between recorded images and living people. The main remaining risk is overexplaining the builders through the final display; leave their earlier origins and much of their decline unrecovered.
