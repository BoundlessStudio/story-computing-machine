# Opus review: The Gentlest Terror

Independent audit for a later editing pass. It is not a `review.md` verdict,
does not change canon state, and does not authorize edits by itself.

## Verdict

- Dialogue: REVISE — four key exchanges fail. The sluice plan skips Olyra's decision. At the brace and in the closing speech, lines state the theme instead of answering the other person. In public, Olyra talks in Osric's legal register.
- Prose: REVISE — the narration explains the moral after most major beats, and the aftermath stacks five lesson sentences before the closing speech.
- Continuity and prompt: PASS — the story delivers every prompt requirement at 3,512 words. What remains is minor: Mossback waits too long, the swallow sits in a lit mouth, and there is a water-infrastructure policy note that is bound by CANON entries.
- Edit scope: targeted-dialogue
- Existing review.md said: `04-review.md` PASS (pass 5, zero findings), with no Dialogue line at all, because none of its five passes assessed dialogue. I agree on continuity and prompt and disagree on dialogue and prose.

The dialogue is better than the collection's usual failure. Most speakers talk from their own wants. Yselle uses euphemism and leverage ("We discussed an adjustment", "Renew, and I will call you to the rescue"). Mossback keeps coming back to "I warned". Osric says "we require". Ruvik and Larkspur bicker about three ungreased gates. The best exchange does the prompt's work in one move: Nyrzath looms over Mossback, then asks, "Where is the clutch?" The failures cluster where the story reaches for its theme. At the brace and at the proclamation, lines point at the moral (appearance against substance, fear against loyalty) instead of answering the person present. In public, Olyra trades citations and ripostes with Osric and never sounds like an heir three weeks bereaved with her claim at risk. The climax plan also has everyone look at Olyra, then skips her choice to go into the passage. The edit pass should mostly cut: theme lines, glosses and ripostes. It should add only the few plain lines that make Olyra's public uncertainty and her sluice decision visible. Leave the banter, the gate bickering and the Flint confrontation alone.

## Edit guardrails

- Prompt (`00-prompt.md`, no craft-profile line): close third on Olyra, past tense. Warm court fantasy with political tension and dry humor, ending in earned hope. Teen / PG-13. Stay within 2,500–4,000 words (currently about 3,510). Nyrzath stays the largest and most frightening dragon and a genuine sweetheart through consequential choices. At least two non-royal alliances (Cress/Larkspur and Flint/Mossback) must materially affect the plot. Olyra and Nyrzath each make a costly, causal choice. Dragons stay allies, never pets or weapons.
- CANON universe entries name this story as their source: `characters.md` (Olyra, Nyrzath, Osric, Yselle, Mossback, Ruvik, Larkspur, Ambric); `factions.md` (Ersene noble-dragon alliance law, House Flint, House Cress, Dread Passage tradition); `glossary.md`; `locations.md` (convocation court, Flint valley and quarry, Cress lands); `rules.md` (Ersene alliance focuses and calls); `timeline.md` ("Undated Ersene accession and Flint flood"). `story.json` says `canon: false` but also records `promotionDate: 2026-08-01`, so confirm editability under AGENTS.md before editing. Edits must preserve these facts:
  - Nyrzath renews only "on the condition that she remember witnessing was not commanding" (L317).
  - Olyra publicly rejects Ambric's "gentle friend / monster's face" lesson after her accession (L583–589).
  - Mossback uses the still-active focus twice before his refusal is witnessed. After that he guides Olyra by voice and claws only.
  - Olyra's call makes Nyrzath shift his brace, which worsens his left-wing injury.
  - Larkspur empties the Cress upper cisterns.
  - Mossback renews his refusal but watches the valley by choice.
  - The proclamation ends the Dread Passage and restores the impartial-witness role.
  - The formula stays "Asked first. / Answered free. / Witnessed whole."
- Focus mechanics must stay consistent. A focus needs two living partners, spoken assent, and a scale warmed by breath. Calls carry direction and urgency, cost heat from both partners, and never compel. Death or a witnessed refusal makes a focus inert. No new line may have a call or a vow command anyone.
- What works and must not be flattened: the swallow thread and its three callbacks, the Cress gate bickering and its payoff, the Nyrzath–Mossback–Yselle confrontation, Mossback's terse practical guidance in the passage, and Osric as a credible traditionalist rather than a villain.

## Dialogue findings

### D1 · Major · Olyra's sluice decision happens off the page

- Where: L383–L441, quarry, planning the diversion through to Olyra entering the passage
- Text:
> "Neglected," Mossback said. "Not sealed. The gate is inside a service passage. Human-width."
> They all looked at Olyra.
> He looked toward the exposed nesting seam, then toward the villages.
> "Go."
> Olyra entered.
- Problem: Ground/referent and staging at the climax. Right after "Human-width", the look at Olyra could mean two things. She may have to authorize sending the flood onto Cress land, which is what the next paragraph talks about. Or she may be the human expected to crawl in. Neither is answered. Ruvik settles the first on his own ("Three gates?"). Nyrzath's one-word "Go." settles the second. So the newly confirmed queen goes into a collapsing passage without anyone, herself included, choosing it on the page. Nothing says why her rather than Yselle, who knows the quarry, or Ruvik. The prompt requires the protagonist to make a costly, causal choice. Here the story skips that choice.
- Fix: Give Olyra one plain line after "Human-width" that claims the passage. If possible, give a reason in a clause (she fits, Ruvik is needed for the call, Yselle won't go). Then "They all looked at Olyra" becomes the setup for her answer, or it can be cut. Nyrzath's later "Go." then reads as his agreement to her plan, not an order. Keep Ruvik's gate exchange as is, and don't add more channel or gate detail. Illustrative only: "Then I'm going in."

### D2 · Major · Closing speech states the thesis

- Where: L583–L589, resumed convocation, after the proclamation is read
- Text:
> "King Ambric used to say a crown sometimes needs a gentle friend to wear a monster's face," she said.
> "He was wrong. A crown that needs a friend to lie about his nature has mistaken fear for loyalty."
- Problem: The Philosopher, in the final meaningful exchange. The proclamation (L580–581) has just enacted the change, and the rescue has already shown it. The aphorism explains the story to the court and the reader. "Lie about his nature" also recasts Nyrzath's years of performance as lying, which the story doesn't need. The speech is aimed at the court, so the relationship the story is about gets no line at its close. The plan's own failure-mode list says "Do not let Olyra solve the story by making a speech."
- Fix: Keep the quotation and a public rejection, which is CANON. Cut the second sentence. The smallest edit ends her speech at "He was wrong." If anything follows, make it concrete and addressed to Nyrzath (the wing, today), not a maxim. Keep "Nyrzath's gold eye rested on her." and the swallow return that follows.

### D3 · Major · Olyra answers Osric in Osric's register

- Where: L168–L179, convocation, just after Mossback's "No"; also L299–L301, before the accession
- Text:
> "The founding order provides for refusal."
> It provides for private petition and repair. Not spectacle.
> "They may have them already."
> "Then do not teach them that refusal is easy."
> "Will begin where every alliance was meant to begin."
- Problem: Identical Voices, correction ping-pong, and uniform fluency under pressure. Olyra is three weeks bereaved, and a refusal has just blocked her renewal. She answers with a legal citation. He corrects it ("Not spectacle"). She parries, and he closes on a neat riposte: five alternating lines of equal weight. Cover the tags and "The founding order provides for refusal" could be Osric's line. The narration later says she fears the fracture (L275) and her voice cracks (L280), but her lines never show it. L301 repeats the move mid-flood-alarm: a riddle that only her next action decodes.
- Fix: Let Osric keep the legal argument. Turn Olyra's citation into a question he can answer. That still seeds the formula she uses at L280 and gives her uncertainty. Cut "They may have them already." or make it a worried aside he talks over, so Osric's line lands as pressure, not as the winning point in a debate. Replace L301 with a plain answer or no answer: she can simply unclasp the old scale. Illustrative only: "Doesn't the founding order let him refuse?"

### D4 · Major · Theme quip instead of an answer at the brace

- Where: L409–L425, quarry, Nyrzath decides to brace the wall with his wing under the ledge
- Text:
> "It may crush the joint," she said.
> "Nyr—"
> "The wall will not be persuaded by my appearance."
- Problem: Ground/uptake plus Punchline Patch at the sacrifice beat. Olyra's broken-off "Nyr—" protests the risk to his wing. His reply answers the story's theme, appearance against substance, instead of her fear. Nobody suggested intimidating the wall, so the line answers something no one said. It also tells the reader what his bracing is about to show. His plain "Yes." two lines earlier is the stronger answer.
- Fix: Cut the line. "Nyr—", then the look toward the seam and the villages, then "Go." already carries his refusal to be talked out of it. If a line is wanted, it should answer her (about her, the eggs or the time left) and not mention how he looks.

### D5 · Minor · Larkspur's gate complaint reads as Nyrzath's

- Where: L123–L133, Cress renewal
- Text:
> "You missed three."
> "Three. The little eastern gate makes a noise like Lord Osric considering a new form."
- Problem: Attribution. Nyrzath has asked both ritual questions, so the reader gives "You missed three." to him. The story means Larkspur, which is confirmed only at L395–397 ("Three gates?" / "Three," Larkspur said.). The payoff depends on the reader knowing whose complaint it was.
- Fix: Tag Larkspur on "You missed three." with a speech tag or a small physical beat. Change nothing else. This is one of the story's best ordinary exchanges.

### D6 · Minor · Mossback's mirrored comeback and borrowed vow template

- Where: L223–L225, Flint confrontation; L553–L554, aftermath
- Text:
> "You watch our lands. You owe us protection."
> "And you owed the nest protection from your work."
> "I will watch the valley's people because I choose to," he told Duchess Yselle.
> "I will not renew with you."
- Problem: Mossback's voice is otherwise his own. Twice it resolves into a tidy last word. The owe/owed mirror closes the argument like a debating point. The aftermath refusal uses Nyrzath's rehearsal template ("I will enter… I will not force", L88) and states its own principle ("because I choose to"), so the two dragons converge.
- Fix: Make the L225 rebuttal specific instead of mirrored: point at the ledge, the eggs, or the tremor he reported. In the aftermath, keep the refusal (CANON) in Mossback's shorter, grievance-driven voice, and drop "because I choose to", since his actions already show it.

## Prose findings

### P1 · Major · Narration explains the moral after most beats

- Where: L227–L228, L275–L276, L296–L297, L330, L378–L379, L407, L511–L512, L557–L558, L562–L564, L570–L574. Heaviest in the aftermath (L556–L574).
- Text:
> He had entered in horror. He had made truth possible by refusing to use it.
> His softness had kept a map no military survey possessed.
> Neither command nor scale moved her. She moved herself.
> An alliance, she had learned, was easiest to praise while asking the dragon to bleed for it.
> House Flint's vacant place did not break the law; it proved the law meant something.
- Problem: After nearly every consequential action, a sentence spells out its meaning, often as a "not X but Y" reversal or a two-beat aphorism. The final section stacks five of these before the closing speech (D2) adds a sixth. The actions have already landed: the question about the clutch, the refused blast, the brace, the empty Flint place. "He had entered in horror" also reads as "he was horrified".
- Fix: Cut most of these sentences outright rather than rewording them. Keep facts (title suspended, ordinary judgment, splints, renewals one pair at a time) and drop the lessons. Worth keeping because they add perception or voice rather than moral: L193–194 ("This was what the crown had kept polished…") and L268 ("It was not a foolish argument. That made it worse."). If L227 needs anything, make it literal: he had come in as the threat and used only a question.

### P2 · Minor · "Four words" is five

- Problem: The italic line has five words.

### P3 · Minor · Final swallow relay is staged, not motivated

- Where: L591–L604, after the proclamation
- Text:
> Then he turned immediately toward Olyra.
> Nyrzath bent toward her.
- Problem: Two consecutive sentences make the same move. The relay (Ruvik puts the bird on Nyrzath's tongue so Nyrzath can carry it a few yards to Olyra) has no stated reason, so the gag looks arranged by the author.
- Fix: Cut one of the two sentences. Add one clause of motive: for example, the bird won't settle in anyone else's hands, or Nyrzath chooses to return it himself in front of the court now that the Passage is gone. Keep the scattering workers and Osric studying the margin.

### P4 · Minor · Close-third POV reaches past Olyra in the flood montage

- Where: L501–L512, Olyra lying in the quarry mud
- Text:
> Far off, three cistern gates opened in succession.
> Each chose the cost again because each knew what the other was asking.
- Problem: The POV is close third on Olyra, who is in the quarry. She can't plausibly hear one distant gate shriek "exactly like Lord Osric" through a storm and a flood, or watch Larkspur over the channels and read both partners' motives.
- Fix: Anchor the montage in what Olyra can perceive. Ruvik is at the quarry, and she can see him flinch and frost with each call. The roar of the water changes as it drains away. Or mark the passage as what she learned afterwards. Keep the eastern-gate callback if it can be heard or reported. Drop the motive sentence (see P1).

## Continuity and prompt findings

### C1 · Minor · Mossback waits through the accession while his clutch floods

- Where: L258, L294–L342, L352–L353, from the courier's report to the arrival at the quarry
- Text:
> Mossback's gaze did not leave Olyra. "I am already answering the people."
> Mossback swept down beside him, smaller by half in every measure
- Problem: The courier says water is entering the nesting cavern. Mossback says he is "already answering". Then he stays through Olyra's ruling, her renewal with Nyrzath, the ritual and the swallow banter, and reaches the quarry together with Nyrzath. A dragon whose eggs are flooding would not wait out someone else's accession. CANON needs him present only until his refusal is witnessed (timeline item 3).
- Fix: Once the ruling frees him (L294–297), have Mossback leave immediately, in one sentence. At the quarry, have him already there, working at the spoil or the nest seam, when Nyrzath lands. Adjust "swept down beside him" to match. Leave Larkspur and Ruvik's arrival unchanged.

### C2 · Minor · The swallow sits in a lit furnace mouth

- Where: L522–L544, collapse and aftermath at the quarry
- Text:
> Even half buried, Nyrzath snapped his jaws through the falling rubble.
> Nyrzath banked the furnace-light behind his teeth to darkness and opened his jaws.
- Problem: The glow in Nyrzath's mouth is a standing trait (L21, and "Furnace-light kindled behind Nyrzath's tusks" at L363). He catches the swallow and holds it through the collapse, and banks the fire only after the stone is cleared. The ending has him quench the glow before taking the bird (L593), which draws attention to the earlier gap. The climactic catch reads as careless, which works against the prompt.
- Fix: At the catch, add a clause saying the red light went dark as his jaws closed. Then simplify L541–542 so he only opens his mouth.

### C3 · Minor · Water infrastructure is the plot engine (policy note; CANON-bound)

- Where: L118, L125–L126, L236, L383–L407, L449–L514, L532–L533, L561
- Text:
> "Do you offer watch over the Cress orchards and storm channels?" Nyrzath asked.
> "The old sluice," Lord Ruvik said. "Could it divert the water east?"
- Problem: The prompt did not ask for water infrastructure, yet cisterns, storm and orchard channels, greased gates, a flood channel, a sluice and an outflow carry the climax. Under `universe/style-guide.md` "No default water infrastructure", this would not be written today. However, the story predates that policy (2026-07-28 against 2026-09-09). CANON entries also record these facts: `locations.md` "Flint valley and quarry" and "Cress lands", `factions.md` "House Cress", and `timeline.md`. The policy "does not … alter established universe facts."
- Fix: Remove nothing. In any edit, including the D1 line, don't add or elaborate water-infrastructure detail. If the owner wants the story to comply, that is a separate ruling touching CANON entries and is outside this pass.
