# Story plan

## Premise and promise

On the day Princess Olyra must renew the royal alliance that legitimizes her
claim, a dragon refuses an abusive noble house and forces Olyra to choose
between preserving the crown through fear or risking it to prove that every
dragon's vow—including the terrifying royal dragon's—must be freely given.

Central dramatic question: Will Olyra command Nyrzath to frighten a dissenting
dragon into submission, as generations of royal pageantry demand, or stake her
throne on the gentleness she has helped him hide?

Story promise: The royal dragon is unmistakably the largest and most
nightmarish creature present, yet his active tenderness, restraint, memory, and
freely chosen loyalty become the practical means by which the kingdom survives
its crisis.

## Story controls

- POV: Close third person through Princess Olyra.
- Tense: Past.
- Tone and genre: Warm court fantasy with political pressure, dry humor, and
  an earned hopeful ending.
- Length budget: Approximately 3,100 words, acceptable range 2,500–4,000.
- Audience/content boundaries: Teen / PG-13. A dangerous flood, structural
  collapse, and a dragon's non-graphic injury are permitted; no graphic
  violence, sexual content, or cruelty for spectacle.
- Scope: A standalone story in the locally invented kingdom of Ersene, in an
  unnamed era of the one shared physical world.

## Character engine

- **Princess Olyra** — The crown heir, hours from accession after her father's
  recent death. She wants an orderly renewal that will secure the throne before
  rival houses can contest it. Her pressure is immediate: every noble alliance
  is traditionally renewed at the royal succession, and a failed royal renewal
  permits the assembled houses to choose another heir. Her blind spot is
  believing Nyrzath's gentleness is a beloved private truth but a political
  liability the crown must conceal. She moves from protecting him through
  secrecy to protecting the freedom that makes his loyalty meaningful.
- **Nyrzath, the Grief-Clad** — The royal family's ancient dragon ally and
  official witness to noble renewals. He is the largest dragon in Ersene: black
  plated scales, a crown of hooked horns, tusks, torn-looking wing edges, a
  milky scarred eye, and a voice like masonry failing. He is also patient,
  attentive, fond of small living things, and constitutionally unable to
  ignore fear or pain. He wants Olyra to inherit, but not at the price of
  coercing another dragon. His costly choice is to use his enormous body as a
  living brace beneath a collapsing quarry wall rather than destroy it and the
  nests below, accepting severe wing injury while trusting smaller dragons to
  lead the rescue.
- **Lord Osric Fane** — Senior court minister and keeper of succession
  procedure. He is not secretly treasonous; he genuinely believes the kingdom
  stays coherent because lesser houses fear the royal dragon. He pressures
  Olyra to perform the inherited "Dread Passage" and treats voluntary refusal
  as a contagious political threat.
- **Duchess Yselle Flint** — Head of House Flint. To increase stone revenue,
  she has let quarrying cross the protected boundary around her allied
  dragon's nesting caverns and concealed the resulting instability. She
  assumes the crown will value unanimity over the dragon's grievance.
- **Mossback** — House Flint's slate-green dragon ally, broad and lichen-coated.
  Mossback watches the Flint valley and cares for the people there but refuses
  to renew with Yselle after repeated breaches of the house's duties.
- **Lord Ruvik Cress and Larkspur** — A healthy non-royal alliance used as
  contrast and as practical rescue support. Larkspur patrols the Cress orchards
  and storm channels; Ruvik's household maintains her hillside cisterns and
  protects her molting grounds. They disagree freely and answer each other's
  emergency calls without command.
- **The late King Ambric** — Olyra's father, present through memory and one
  quoted instruction. He loved Nyrzath yet taught Olyra that a crown sometimes
  needs a gentle friend to wear a monster's face, embodying the belief she must
  outgrow.

### Local alliance mechanism

- A noble house exists in law only as a paired public service: a human family
  and a freely consenting dragon ally. The dragon watches the house's lands and
  offers emergency aid; the humans maintain agreed roost or nesting territory,
  provide material care when the dragon is injured or molting, and advocate
  for the dragon in human courts. Dragons are neither owned nor required to
  serve as mounts or weapons.
- At each human succession, both parties renew before the royal dragon as
  witness. A voluntarily shed scale is warmed by the dragon's living breath
  while the heir holds it and both speak assent. The focus then carries a
  directional alarm and urgency between those two living allies. It is inert
  unless one partner deliberately calls; repeated calls drain body heat and
  stamina from both.
- Either party may refuse renewal or petition the royal witness for release.
  There is no magical punishment or compulsion. A refusal suspends the human
  family's noble title until it repairs the breach and the same dragon freely
  returns, or another dragon independently chooses the partnership.
- The royal dragon is the impartial witness and first model of consent, not the
  commander of other dragons. The "Dread Passage" is a later court tradition
  that turned Nyrzath's entrance and roar into an implied threat; it has social
  force but no legitimate power to compel a vow.

## Causal arc

- **Beginning state:** Olyra rehearses the Dread Passage while privately
  soothing a frightened swallow tangled among Nyrzath's horns. She accepts the
  contradiction: the dragon she loves must look like the crown's threat.
- **Inciting disruption:** At the succession renewal, Mossback publicly refuses
  Duchess Yselle. Because the royal accession requires the crown to witness
  every house, Osric insists that Olyra order Nyrzath to terrify Mossback into
  completing the vow.
- **Escalating turn one:** Nyrzath advances in his full horrific splendor but
  asks Mossback, gently and audibly, where the nesting clutch is. Mossback
  reveals the Flint quarry breach. Olyra realizes coercion would ratify a
  human house's abuse, but acknowledging the refusal can suspend the
  convocation and expose her own claim to challenge.
- **Escalating turn two:** A storm-triggered watch-call arrives from the Flint
  valley: the illegal quarry wall is failing, floodwater is entering the
  nesting caverns, and villages lie below. Yselle demands that Mossback renew
  first so she can command a rescue; Osric urges Olyra to finish the Dread
  Passage before panic dissolves the assembly. The crisis proves exactly why
  coerced alliance would be expedient.
- **Crisis:** Olyra must choose whether to preserve her automatic accession by
  treating dragons as instruments or invoke the founding procedure that lets
  every ally—including Nyrzath—refuse. She stops the ceremony, recognizes
  Mossback's exit, suspends House Flint's title, and places the royal renewal
  first. Until Nyrzath answers freely, she is only one possible heir.
- **Climax:** Nyrzath freely renews with Olyra, then rejects a destructive
  blast that would kill Mossback's clutch. He braces the cracking wall with his
  body. Mossback guides Olyra through a human-width sluice passage; Olyra
  opens the neglected flood gate despite the collapse risk. Larkspur answers
  Ruvik's voluntary call and empties the Cress hillside cisterns to receive the
  diverted surge. Nyrzath's attention to the nest locations, willingness to
  suffer, and trust in the others—not intimidation—make the rescue possible.
- **Aftermath:** The flood is diverted, the clutch and valley survive, and
  Nyrzath is injured but alive. The houses renew one pair at a time without the
  Dread Passage. Their witnessed assent confirms Olyra after she has accepted
  the possibility of losing the crown. Yselle faces ordinary legal judgment;
  Mossback continues watching the valley's people by choice while remaining
  unallied to House Flint. Olyra's first proclamation restores the royal
  dragon's true role as witness. The closing image lets Nyrzath remain
  terrifying—court workers flee his approaching jaws—before he delicately
  returns the rescued swallow to Olyra's hands.

## Setup and payoff

- The swallow tangled in Nyrzath's horn crown establishes the scale contrast,
  his patience, and Olyra's private knowledge; he rescues the same bird during
  the climax and returns it in public at the end.
- Olyra knows Nyrzath keeps a precise mental map of every dragon nest because
  he sends hatch-day gifts. That apparently soft habit lets him reject the
  fatal blast plan and identify where he can safely brace the quarry wall.
- The Cress hillside cisterns are mentioned during their healthy renewal as
  Ruvik's fulfilled duty to Larkspur. Emptying them later gives the diverted
  flood somewhere to go.
- The alliance focus makes Olyra's hands cold during a rehearsal call, seeding
  the cost before multiple emergency calls visibly exhaust the rescue pairs.
- King Ambric's maxim about a friend wearing a monster's face initially
  supports Olyra's secrecy. She answers it in the aftermath: a crown that needs
  a friend to lie about his nature has mistaken fear for loyalty.
- The founding formula—"asked first, answered free, witnessed whole"—appears
  as ceremonial language early. Olyra invokes its literal order at the crisis
  instead of discovering an unseeded legal loophole.

## Canon constraints

- Keep Ersene in an unnamed era within the one shared physical world. Do not
  imply a separate plane or link it to Earth, Ravel, the Glass-Sea era, or any
  candidate-story kingdom.
- Treat "magic" as Ersene's cultural label. The alliance focus is one local
  living system, not the source or universal form of magic.
- The shed scale stores and channels a connection created by living assent; it
  never initiates a call or makes a decision independently.
- Consent, refusal, cost, and exit behave only as defined in this plan. They
  are legal, relational, and locally magical facts, not universal metaphysics.
- Dragons remain living persons with goals and the power to refuse. Do not
  write possessive language that makes them property, pets, heraldry, mounts,
  or weapons.
- Do not make names inherently powerful, call Nyrzath a literal god, or imply
  that size and fear establish divinity.
- Historical custom may be distorted, but the resolution must rely on the
  already seeded founding formula and present choices rather than myth being
  automatically true.
- Maintain the Teen / PG-13 ceiling and non-graphic treatment of injury.

## Proposed inventions

- The kingdom of Ersene, its royal succession convocation, and the legal
  principle that nobility is a paired human-dragon public service.
- The reciprocal alliance duties, voluntary renewal and exit procedure,
  suspension of title after dragon refusal, and royal dragon's original role
  as impartial witness.
- The living watch-call mechanism using a voluntarily shed scale, dragon
  breath, mutual assent, directional alarm, and shared heat/stamina cost.
- The later Dread Passage tradition and the court's distortion of witness into
  intimidation.
- Princess Olyra, Nyrzath, Lord Osric, Duchess Yselle, Mossback, Lord Ruvik,
  Larkspur, King Ambric, Houses Flint and Cress, the Flint valley and quarry,
  and the Cress storm cisterns.
- Nyrzath's physical form, disposition, nest-memory, private nickname, and
  relationship with Olyra.
- The succession-day quarry collapse, rescue, voluntary renewals, House Flint's
  suspension, and Olyra's accession.

## Failure modes to watch

- Do not let the premise collapse into a cute reveal. Nyrzath's gentleness must
  determine his tactical restraint, willingness to suffer, memory of the
  clutch, trust in other allies, and the final political model.
- Make Nyrzath obviously the largest and most frightening dragon in the
  kingdom through comparison with Mossback and Larkspur, not merely an
  unsupported adjective.
- Do not let Olyra solve the story by making a speech. Her legal choice must
  risk her claim, and opening the sluice must be physically necessary to the
  rescue.
- Preserve Mossback's refusal after the rescue. Protecting valley residents
  does not equal forgiving Yselle or silently renewing the broken alliance.
- Do not make Osric a cartoon villain or secret saboteur. His fear-based
  argument should be plausible and defeated by events and choices.
- Do not introduce an unseeded dragon power, legal clause, secret archive, or
  outside army to solve the climax.
- Keep alliance rules consistent: no remote compulsion, no object acting
  without a living caller, and visible cold/exhaustion from repeated calls.
- Do not imply that every kingdom or every dragon in history follows Ersene's
  model.
- Keep the side cast functional. Yselle embodies breach, Mossback refusal,
  Ruvik/Larkspur reciprocity, and Osric institutional pressure; none needs a
  subplot.
- Register every planned name before drafting, then reconcile against the final
  prose, including `Nyr`, `the Grief-Clad`, and Olyra's title change.
