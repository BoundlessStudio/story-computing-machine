# Canon brief

## Hard constraints

- The story occurs somewhere in the one physical world that persists through
  many civilizations and eras. It may introduce an otherwise unknown kingdom,
  but it must not treat that kingdom as a separate plane or planet merely to
  avoid continuity. No connection to Earth, Ravel, or the Glass-Sea era is
  required. (`universe/premise.md`, **The deep-time shared world**;
  `universe/locations.md`, **The world across eras**)
- If the story calls a phenomenon magical, that is the viewpoint culture's
  label for a real underlying system rather than an objective cosmological
  category. A local dragon-alliance system may exist, but it must not be
  presented as the single source or universal form of magic.
  (`universe/rules.md`, **System categories**; `universe/glossary.md`,
  **Magic, technology, and divinity**)
- Only life can use magic. Dragons may use magic if the story establishes them
  as living beings; a nonliving crown, treaty tablet, heraldic image, or other
  device may store, focus, transmit, or shape magic supplied through living
  agency, but may not independently act as a magic user unless the story
  establishes that it is alive. (`universe/rules.md`, **Living agency and
  magic**)
- Any supernatural force attached to an alliance must follow a defined local
  mechanism. Cost and desire are not universal fuels, and consent has no
  universal metaphysical behavior; if consent, oath-breaking, inherited duty,
  sacrifice, or desire affects the bond, that behavior must be explicit,
  causal, ethically considered, and internally consistent.
  (`universe/rules.md`, **Costs, desire, and local mechanisms**)
- Names are not universally magical. Any true-name, bloodline-name, title, or
  oath-name effect must be a rare local technological, magical, legal, or
  cultural binding rather than a general truth of the universe.
  (`universe/rules.md`, **Names and identity binding**)
- Dragons must retain meaningful personhood and moral agency. A magical or
  political explanation for the alliances may complicate identity, duty, or
  consent but may not erase those stakes. This reinforces the prompt contract's
  requirement that dragons are reciprocal allies rather than property, pets,
  ornaments, or weapons. (`universe/style-guide.md`, **Personhood and moral
  consequence**; `stories/the-gentlest-terror/00-prompt.md`, **Story controls**)
- If any dragon is literally described as a god, canon requires the continuing
  conjunction of worship, power, and authority. Fear, enormous size,
  immortality, or great magic alone does not establish godhood.
  (`universe/rules.md`, **Divinity**)
- Provenance, chronology, and causal consequence outrank a convenient
  explanation. Shared imagery, titles, dragon motifs, artifacts, or folklore
  must not be used to imply a connection to another era or system without
  specific evidence established in this story or authoritative canon.
  (`universe/style-guide.md`, **Continuity and evidence discipline**;
  `universe/rules.md`, **Artifacts, motifs, and folklore**)
- The treatment must remain broadly accessible and non-explicit. Violence and
  peril may occur, but they must remain non-graphic and emphasize character
  and consequence, consistent with the prompt contract's Teen / PG-13 ceiling.
  (`universe/style-guide.md`, **Audience and content boundaries**;
  `stories/the-gentlest-terror/00-prompt.md`, **Story controls**)

## Useful established context

- The shared setting explicitly allows cultures to hold incomplete or
  incorrect models of how the world works. The court may therefore
  misunderstand dragons, the alliance custom, or the royal dragon's character
  without the narration endorsing that misunderstanding as cosmological truth.
  (`universe/premise.md`, **Knowledge and connection**)
- The universe supports a wide range of genres, tones, scales, and
  independently readable entry points. Warm court fantasy with political
  tension and dry humor fits the established range and need not advertise the
  deep-time cosmology. (`universe/style-guide.md`, **Tonal range, scale, and
  connection**; `universe/premise.md`, **Memory, myth, and recoverable truth**)
- Civilizations commonly inherit distorted stories, rituals, records, and
  artifacts whose original purposes may be forgotten. If useful to the plot,
  the court's public story about why the royal dragon must appear terrifying
  can differ from evidence about what the alliance was actually for, provided
  the story supplies a concrete, causal trail rather than treating myth as
  automatic proof. (`universe/premise.md`, **Memory, myth, and recoverable
  truth**; `universe/rules.md`, **Records, falsehood, and historical traces**)
- Many genuinely different systems may interact or imitate one another. The
  alliances of different noble houses may therefore have local variations,
  but any differences used in the plot must be explained and remain coherent.
  (`universe/rules.md`, **System categories**)
- The project favors precise, concrete prose and exposition that earns its
  place through story action. The alliance system should be demonstrated
  through consequences, behavior, and specific court practice rather than a
  detached lore inventory. (`universe/style-guide.md`, **Prose defaults**)
- `stories/INDEX.md` currently marks no story canon `yes`. Consequently, no
  existing story prose supplies binding facts about dragons, monarchies, noble
  families, or this kingdom. Candidate and abandoned dragon stories are
  production history only. (`stories/INDEX.md`, **Story index**)

## Conflicts or ambiguity

- No authoritative source conflicts with the prompt. There is no canon entry
  for dragons, noble-dragon alliances, a relevant kingdom, or a royal family,
  so the prompt can be realized as a local invention.
- The prompt's “every single noble family in your kingdom” could be misread as
  a universal rule for aristocracy or dragons. The prompt contract resolves
  the intended scope to one particular kingdom; the story should preserve that
  scope and avoid claims about every kingdom, era, or dragon culture.
  (`stories/the-gentlest-terror/00-prompt.md`, **Assumptions**)
- The kingdom's era is unspecified. An explicit placement in the modern-like
  era would need to reconcile an openly magical dragon aristocracy with that
  era being a low-magic trough in which ordinary life treats magic as myth or
  legend. Placement in Ravel would also import only the few canon facts in the
  glossary; unpromoted legacy Ravel lore would remain nonbinding.
  (`universe/timeline.md`, **Established era order**;
  `universe/glossary.md`, **Modern-like era** and **Ravel**)
- `stories/NAMES.md` contains dragon and royal identities from abandoned,
  candidate, and legacy stories, but the registry explicitly does not make
  them canon. Those entries constrain production naming, not the setting's
  history.
- No conflict requires a user ruling. Canon-research status: **READY**.

## Unknowns

- **Unknown:** Whether dragons exist elsewhere in the world, in which eras they
  exist, whether they form one species or culture, and whether the word
  “dragon” has a consistent meaning across eras. The story may define these
  matters locally but should not universalize them.
- **Unknown:** Dragon biology, lifespan, reproduction, diet, speech, breath
  weapons, magic, flight, senses, size limits, and death. Appearance and
  capabilities needed for this cast are safe local inventions when kept
  consistent.
- **Unknown:** The kingdom's name, geography, era, calendar, language, dynasty,
  succession system, noble ranks, law, economy, faith, and relationship to
  other polities.
- **Unknown:** How each noble family and dragon chooses, inherits, renews,
  leaves, or enforces an alliance; whether the alliance is magical, legal,
  ceremonial, emotional, or some combination; and what reciprocal obligations
  “watches over” entails.
- **Unknown:** Whether dragons and human families entered the system as equals,
  whether either side can refuse heirs, how abuse or breach is remedied, and
  what prevents an alliance from becoming ownership. The prompt requires
  present-day reciprocal agency but does not supply this history or mechanism.
- **Unknown:** Why the royal dragon is the largest, whether size has political
  meaning, and whether terrifying appearance is natural, cultivated, or
  propagandized.
- **Unknown:** Whether the court knows the royal dragon is gentle, who benefits
  from its frightening reputation, and what immediate political danger tests
  that contrast.
- **Unknown:** The kingdom's position in Galactic Cycles. A local calendar is
  permitted; an exact universal coordinate is not required because the master
  timeline's epoch and known-era coordinates remain unresolved.
  (`universe/timeline.md`, **Universal chronology**)
- Empty authoritative topic files establish no recurring characters or
  factions for this story to use. Their silence is an omission, not evidence
  that such people or institutions cannot exist. (`universe/characters.md`,
  **Recurring characters**; `universe/factions.md`, **Factions and cultures**)

## Safe invention space

- Create one locally bounded kingdom in an otherwise unnamed era, including its
  royal house, court offices, noble families, succession practices, and
  immediate neighbors. Avoid claiming that its institutions govern or typify
  the whole world.
- Create individual dragons as distinct living persons with their own goals,
  temperaments, relationships, homes, and reasons for maintaining alliances.
  The royal dragon's frightening body and genuine sweetness can be defined
  without establishing universal dragon psychology or biology.
- Define a reciprocal alliance custom at kingdom scale: how partnerships begin,
  what each side promises, what “watching over” means, whether either side may
  refuse or end the alliance, and how breaches are judged. A ceremonial or
  magical bond is safe if its living agency, consent behavior, limitations, and
  costs are coherent.
- Invent a local founding event, treaty, ritual, council, archive, succession
  crisis, or propaganda tradition that explains the court's dependence on the
  royal dragon's terrifying image. Keep the evidence story-scale and do not
  connect it to an established era, portal, deity, or artifact without cause.
- Invent at least two non-royal family-dragon alliances with materially
  different personalities, duties, or political interests. Differences should
  be local social arrangements, not evidence of unrelated universal magic
  systems unless the story explicitly establishes that distinction.
- Give the royal dragon a concrete form of active gentleness—restraint,
  attentiveness, protection, mediation, care, or mercy—that can impose a cost
  and causally resolve the crisis without making the dragon childish,
  incompetent, secretly cruel, or owned.
- Use a local calendar and culturally appropriate language for magic and
  dragons. The story can remain an independent entry point with no crossover,
  archaeology, portal, or cosmological exposition.

## Name constraints

- `stories/NAMES.md` is production memory, not canon. No character-facing names
  have yet been proposed in the prompt contract, so there is no current
  collision to adjudicate. Every eventual personal name, surname used alone,
  mononym, nickname, alias, epithet, title used as a name, and named dragon must
  be checked and registered before drafting.
- Avoid reusing the most directly relevant reserved royal and dragon forms:
  `Kharost`, `Queen Orvessa IV`, `Queen Orvessa`, `Orvessa`, `King Othran`,
  `Queen Aurelia`, `Aurelia`, `Sun Queen`, `Burning Queen`, `Sleepless
  Majesty`, `Majesty`, `Your Majesty`, and `Varkesh`. These belong to distinct
  abandoned, legacy, or candidate identities; none is established canon, but
  all remain reserved production names.
- Avoid `Crowned Ancestors`, `First Monarch`, and `Court Ghost` as
  character-facing epithets. They are also reserved. Common-noun uses of
  “ancestor,” “monarch,” “court,” or “ghost” are not automatically names, but a
  capitalized or consistently substitutive label must be validated.
- Noble-family naming should avoid the already reserved family forms
  `Bellweather`, `Morrow`, `Mercer`, `Venn`, and `Vale`, along with all other
  surnames in the registry, unless reuse is intentional, meaningful, and
  documented as the same identity, an earned connection, or another
  reader-clear device. Convenience or genre familiarity is not sufficient.
- Do not add another use of the unresolved forms `Lena`, `Mara`, `Nisha`,
  `Pell`, or `Voss`. Each already labels distinct identities whose collision
  remains unresolved in production memory.
- Take particular care with stand-alone court or military titles. `Captain` is
  reserved as a named dog, while `Majesty` and `Your Majesty` are reserved for
  the candidate Sleepless Majesty identity. Ordinary role descriptions can
  remain common nouns, but any title consistently used as a person's or
  dragon's name must be treated as a character-facing form and checked.
- New dragon names should also be readily distinguishable in spelling and
  cadence from the existing dragon-associated `Kharost` and `Varkesh`, not
  merely non-identical.

## Required checks after drafting

- Confirm that every dragon who affects the plot acts as an ally with
  intelligible agency rather than as property, pet, heraldic ornament, mount,
  or weapon, and that human and dragon obligations are meaningfully
  reciprocal.
- Confirm that “every noble family” remains true within the defined kingdom.
  Search for overlooked houses, cadet branches, disputed ennoblements, extinct
  lines, or office-holders that accidentally create exceptions, and ensure at
  least two non-royal alliances materially demonstrate the system.
- Confirm that the royal dragon is visibly the kingdom's largest and most
  frightening dragon, remains genuinely gentle throughout, and makes a costly
  causal choice that helps resolve both the immediate danger and the
  protagonist's conflict over the crown's use of its reputation.
- Trace the alliance rules scene by scene: selection or inheritance, consent,
  duties, range of “watching,” breach, enforcement, cost, and exit. Repair any
  moment where the plot requires the same bond rule to behave incompatibly.
- Check every magical act for living agency. If a crown, contract, seal,
  portrait, fortress, or other object appears to act independently, either
  establish the relevant living user or revise the mechanism.
- Search for universal claims about dragons, magic, monarchy, names, desire,
  sacrifice, or consent. Narrow unsupported claims to this kingdom, this
  alliance system, this dragon culture, or an explicitly fallible character's
  belief.
- Check chronology and setting labels. If the story names the modern-like era,
  Ravel, or the Glass-Sea era, verify every associated canon constraint; do not
  import details from candidate or legacy stories. If the kingdom remains in
  an unnamed era, ensure no accidental contemporary or established-era
  reference fixes it elsewhere.
- If worship or the word “god” is applied literally to a dragon, verify all
  three canon requirements for godhood—worship, power, and authority—or make
  the language clearly figurative or culturally mistaken.
- Verify that any archive, myth, portrait, treaty, or relic supplies
  source-specific evidence rather than automatic truth, and that its
  provenance matters to the conclusion.
- Search the full prose and canon delta for every character-facing name and
  alias, reconcile them against `stories/NAMES.md`, document any deliberate
  reuse, and run the scoped story-name validation script.
- Check the final story against the non-graphic Teen / PG-13 ceiling, the
  warm-court-fantasy tone, the prompt's prohibited reversals, and the required
  earned hopeful ending.
