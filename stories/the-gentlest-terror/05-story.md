# The Gentlest Terror

The swallow had chosen the worst possible crown in Ersene.

Princess Olyra stood on a scaffold beside Nyrzath's head, one hand hooked
around a black horn, while the other worked at the gold thread wound around the
bird's leg. Beneath her boots, the royal dragon kept so still that dust had
settled along the hooked ridges of his brow.

Stillness did not make him reassuring.

Nyrzath filled the convocation court from dais to doors. Black plates overlapped
down his neck like shields laid for a siege. Tusks curved past his lower jaw.
One eye was gold; the other, scarred milky-white. The ragged edges of his folded
wings rose above the galleries, and heat leaked between his teeth in a red glow
that made the ceremonial banners stir. The masons had rebuilt the west arch
after his last Dread Passage. Lord Osric Fane called that an unfortunate
expense. Privately, he had also requested a wider arch.

"If you twitch," Olyra murmured, "it will think the end of the world has
arrived."

"I have that effect on small creatures," Nyrzath said. His whisper sounded
like a cart overturning in a cellar.

The swallow trembled against the horn. Olyra teased free the last bright
strand. It fluttered once, failed to gain height, and tucked itself into the
hollow behind Nyrzath's forward horn.

"Stunned," she said. "Keep your head level."

"I had intended to nod gravely throughout your accession."

"You may nod gravely tomorrow."

"Excellent. I have been practicing for centuries."

Lord Osric's shoes clicked into the court. Olyra did not need to look down to
recognize the pace: brisk enough to rebuke everyone else for wasting time,
measured enough to suggest he never did.

"The noble pairs are assembling," he said. "Princess Olyra, we require one
complete rehearsal."

Olyra descended. On the lowest rung, the scale beneath her collarbone shifted,
cold and smooth against her skin. Nyrzath had shed it freely for King Ambric's
alliance and warmed it with his breath while her father held it. With her
father's death, it had become inert: a keepsake that could point nowhere and
draw heat from no living hand. A working focus required two living partners,
their spoken assent, and a new scale freely offered and warmed by dragon
breath. Today Nyrzath would have to choose her before any connection opened.

Lord Osric noticed her hand at her collar. "The old focus?"

"Only a memory now," Olyra said.

"Then we shall require the new one to function immediately."

"After both of us agree," Nyrzath said.

Lord Osric inclined his head, accepting the correction without enjoying it.
"And the Dread Passage?"

Nyrzath unfolded his wings.

The court vanished under shadow. His horn crown scraped stone. He drew one
breath; furnace-light brightened behind his tusks, and the damp on the flagstones
turned to mist. This was the face Ersene painted over gates and stamped into
wax: Nyrzath the Grief-Clad, largest of the kingdom's dragons, ruin wearing
scales.

Olyra heard King Ambric as clearly as if her father stood beside her: *A crown
sometimes needs a gentle friend to wear a monster's face.*

Three weeks dead, and he could still give terrible advice at exactly the useful
moment.

"Enter as rehearsed," Olyra told Nyrzath.

The red light dimmed.

"I will enter," he said. "I will witness. I will not force another dragon's
vow."

Lord Osric's mouth tightened. "No one anticipates that necessity."

They all looked toward the waiting doors.

No one believed him.

* * *

Every noble family in Ersene entered the convocation court beside its dragon
ally. There were long-legged dragons from the reed estates, copper-scaled
dragons whose human partners tended cliff roosts, and one elderly white dragon
who accepted a heated stone from her family before settling onto the tiles.
Each alliance represented a paired public service: watch and rescue from the
dragon; protected nesting ground, injury care, and a human voice in court in
return.

None of the dragons came on a chain. None bore a saddle.

House Cress renewed first.

Larkspur was considered large anywhere Nyrzath was not present. Beside him,
the green-and-violet dragon looked almost delicate, though one foreclaw could
have covered Lord Ruvik Cress from polished boot to thinning hair.

She plucked a loose scale from her shoulder and dropped it into Lord Ruvik's
waiting hands.

"Do you offer watch over the Cress orchards and storm channels?" Nyrzath
asked.

"I do," Larkspur said. Her voice had the crackle of dry leaves.

"Do you offer the care you agreed?"

Lord Ruvik bowed to her before he answered Nyrzath. "We cleared her molting
ground, rebuilt the upper cisterns, and greased every gate."

"You missed three."

"Two."

"Three. The little eastern gate makes a noise like Lord Osric considering a
new form."

The court laughed. Even Lord Osric almost did.

Larkspur breathed over the scale. It warmed in Lord Ruvik's palms, the heat
coming from a living will rather than the bit of scale itself.

"Asked first," Nyrzath said.

"Answered free," said Larkspur.

"Witnessed whole," Lord Ruvik finished.

He and Larkspur exchanged a look that made it clear the eastern gate would be
discussed again.

Then House Flint approached.

Mossback was slate-green, broad through the chest, and furred with lichen along
his spine. He watched the Flint valley from nesting caverns above the villages.
Olyra had seen him carry lost quarry workers home through sleet, walking so
slowly that no injured limb jostled.

Duchess Yselle Flint held out both hands.

Mossback did not shed a scale.

Silence moved across the court more quickly than flame.

Duchess Yselle kept her hands extended. "We are waiting."

"No," Mossback said.

The word was quiet. It struck harder than Nyrzath's roar.

Lord Osric stepped near Olyra. "The convocation cannot proceed past an
unwitnessed house. Your royal renewal cannot be confirmed."

"The founding order provides for refusal."

"It provides for private petition and repair. Not spectacle. If one house may
humiliate the crown before every alliance in Ersene, others will discover
grievances before sunset."

"They may have them already."

"Then do not teach them that refusal is easy."

Duchess Yselle's hands fell. "Mossback is agitated. The Grief-Clad can remind
him what this gathering requires."

Olyra looked up at Nyrzath.

He rose.

Larkspur backed one pace despite herself. Mossback did not retreat, but the
lichen along his spine flattened. Nyrzath advanced until his shadow swallowed
House Flint. His hooked horns nearly touched the ceiling. Heat rolled from his
mouth, and every person in the first gallery leaned away as one.

This was what the crown had kept polished: not Nyrzath, but the instant before
people obeyed him.

Olyra could finish the performance with four words. *Make him renew the vow.*

Nyrzath lowered his tusked face until his scarred eye was level with
Mossback's.

"Where is the clutch?" he asked gently.

Duchess Yselle went pale.

Mossback's claws scored the floor. "In the north nesting cavern. Where it has
always been."

Nyrzath remembered every nest in Ersene. He sent hatch-day gifts: bright river
glass, smoothed roof tiles, once an entire wagon of pears that had been less
popular with the hatchlings than with their human neighbors.

"The quarry boundary lies south of that cavern," he said.

"It did," Mossback replied. "House Flint cut past it. I warned them after the
first tremor. They shored the visible face and dug beneath my nesting ledge."

Voices broke across the galleries.

Duchess Yselle turned on him. "We discussed an adjustment."

"You ordered me to stop frightening the stone crews with talk of collapse."

"You watch our lands. You owe us protection."

"And you owed the nest protection from your work."

Nyrzath looked to Olyra. He had entered in horror. He had made truth possible
by refusing to use it.

Olyra felt her throne shift beneath feet that had not yet reached it.

* * *

Duchess Yselle's old alliance scale began to smoke.

She cried out and nearly dropped it. Frost whitened her knuckles as Mossback's
deliberate call seized both their attention and pointed north. Olyra felt
nothing through her own focus, but she saw the cost in Mossback: his breath
shortened, and a rim of ice formed where his claws touched stone.

"Water," he said. "The quarry wall is moving."

The court's high windows flashed white with the storm. A heartbeat later,
thunder shook the banners.

Mossback called again. Duchess Yselle staggered.

"The cut has opened into the flood channel," he said. "Water is entering the
north cavern. If the wall fails outward, it reaches the villages."

"Then renew," Duchess Yselle said. She thrust the smoking scale toward him as
if it were a bridle. "Renew, and I will call you to the rescue."

Mossback's gaze did not leave Olyra. "I am already answering the people."

"Without a witnessed alliance, there is no lawful duty—"

"There are lives."

Lord Osric seized Olyra's elbow and lowered his voice. "This is precisely why
the vow must be secured now. Complete the Dread Passage. Establish command,
then settle the breach after the valley is safe."

It was not a foolish argument. That made it worse.

Around them, noble families clustered close to their dragons. If the royal
renewal failed, the assembled houses could choose another heir. If the
convocation dissolved in panic, every disputed obligation in Ersene might go
with it. King Ambric had feared that fracture. Lord Osric feared it now.

Olyra feared it enough that she understood how easily love became a mask one
made another person wear.

She stepped onto the witness stone.

"Mossback was asked," she said. Her voice cracked once, then carried. "He
answered free. Nyrzath witnessed him whole."

"He refused," Duchess Yselle said.

"Yes."

Olyra faced the galleries. "House Flint's noble title is suspended. It may be
restored if its breach is repaired and Mossback freely returns, or if another
dragon independently chooses the partnership. Until then, Mossback owes House
Flint no alliance."

Duchess Yselle made a small sound of disbelief.

The frost melted from her knuckles. Her old focus stopped smoking and dulled
in her palm. With Mossback's refusal witnessed whole, it could no longer carry
either person's call or draw heat from either body. His exit was not a
courtesy granted in words and denied in flesh.

"And your accession?" Lord Osric asked.

"Will begin where every alliance was meant to begin."

Olyra unclasped the old scale from her neck and set it on the witness stone.
Then she held out empty hands to the largest, most frightening creature in the
kingdom.

"Nyrzath. Before crown or court, will you choose an alliance with me?"

He could refuse. Until he answered, she was only one possible heir standing
before people trained to prefer certainty.

Nyrzath studied her with his clear gold eye. Then he lifted one foreclaw and
worked loose a black scale no larger than Olyra's palm.

It rang on the stone.

"I will," he said, "if you will remember that witnessing is not commanding."

"I will remember."

She picked up the scale. Nyrzath breathed over it, carefully banking the heat
that could turn rain to steam. Warmth spread into her hands.

"Asked first," Olyra said.

"Answered free," said Nyrzath.

Together, they said, "Witnessed whole."

The scale became only a focus, not a will. The choice remained theirs.

Nyrzath crouched and opened one foreclaw before Olyra. She stepped into it.

"The swallow," she warned.

"Still behind my horn."

"Then keep your head level."

The roof groaned as he spread his wings.

"I had intended," he said, and launched them into the storm.

* * *

The Flint quarry had become a brown lake standing on end.

Water pressed behind a cracked wall of cut stone and rubble. Below it, the
valley villages huddled beneath rain. Above it, the northern nesting ledge had
split, exposing a dark seam into the cavern where Mossback's clutch lay.

Nyrzath landed hard enough to sink ankle-deep in mud. Mossback swept down
beside him, smaller by half in every measure, and Larkspur landed beyond them
with Lord Ruvik clutched in the careful curl of one forefoot.

The dragons had spent several minutes tracing the cracks by the time Duchess
Yselle's quarry cart completed its slower journey from court. She arrived
white-faced and soaked and pointed at the wall.

"Burn a channel through the center. Let the water out before it takes the
whole face."

Furnace-light kindled behind Nyrzath's tusks.

Olyra imagined the blast: wet stone bursting, water punching through, the
wall's middle falling inward.

Nyrzath paced three enormous steps left. He placed one claw against the rock,
then shook his head.

"No."

"You can melt siege iron," Duchess Yselle said.

"The clutch lies behind the center cut."

He knew the distance because he remembered where Mossback had nested, where
each egg had lain when he sent six blue roof tiles as gifts. His softness had
kept a map no military survey possessed.

Another crack ran down the wall.

"The old sluice," Lord Ruvik said. "Could it divert the water east?"

Duchess Yselle stared at him. "It was sealed when we deepened the quarry."

"Neglected," Mossback said. "Not sealed. The gate is inside a service
passage. Human-width."

They all looked at Olyra.

The eastern channel led toward the Cress hills. Toward the cisterns Larkspur's
alliance maintained.

Lord Ruvik pulled Larkspur's new scale from inside his coat. "Three gates?"

"Three," Larkspur said.

"I apologize."

"Do it after we live."

He called through the focus. Heat vanished from both of them; Lord Ruvik
doubled over, breath smoking, while Larkspur's wings shuddered. She answered
by launching toward the hills. There, she could release the stored water down
the orchard channels, emptying the cisterns to receive the diverted flood.
Neither command nor scale moved her. She moved herself.

Nyrzath examined the cracking wall. "If I brace here, the center may hold
until the sluice opens."

The torn-looking edge of his left wing would lie beneath the failing ledge.
Olyra saw it. So did he.

"It may crush the joint," she said.

"Yes."

"Nyr—"

"The wall will not be persuaded by my appearance."

He looked toward the exposed nesting seam, then toward the villages.

"Go."

Nyrzath drove his shoulder beneath the overhang.

Stone boomed against black plates. His wings spread along the wall, not in
threat but as a living brace. The whole quarry seemed to lean upon him. He
grunted, masonry-failure voice reduced to one rough breath, and the left wing
buckled without breaking.

Mossback led Olyra to a narrow opening half hidden by spoil. He could not fit
past the first bend. He thrust his head into the gap until lichen scraped from
his brow.

"The passage turns right, then drops," he said. "I will follow above. Listen
for my claws."

Olyra entered.

The dark smelled of rust and flooded earth. Behind her, Nyrzath took another
impact. Dust leapt from the ceiling. Ahead, a faint scrape sounded through the
stone: Mossback pacing above her.

"Right," his muffled voice called.

She found the turn by touch and slid down a wet incline. Water already ran
around her boots. At the bottom stood the sluice wheel, red with rust and
twisted slightly off its axle.

Olyra pulled.

Nothing moved.

Above, claws scraped twice, urgent, then struck three hard blows that shook
grit from the ceiling. Mossback's voice came thinly through the rock.

"The ledge is going."

Olyra braced one boot against the wall and hauled until her shoulders burned.
The wheel shifted the width of a fingernail.

The passage ceiling cracked.

She grabbed the new black scale and called.

Cold drove to her elbow. Direction and urgency struck through Nyrzath's
attention: *here; failing; hold.*

Outside, he answered.

The wall lifted a fraction as he changed the set of his shoulder. That fraction
kept the ceiling off Olyra. It put the weight onto his left wing.

His roar shook grit into her eyes.

Olyra pulled again. The wheel turned half a spoke, then another. Muddy water
foamed through the gate and struck her knees. She did not have enough strength
for the final turn.

A slate-green claw punched through the passage roof behind her.

Mossback tore away loose stone, widening the gap only enough to hook one claw
tip around the far side of the wheel.

"Together," he said.

Olyra pushed as Mossback pulled.

The gate opened.

Water took the passage.

It flung Olyra off her feet and dragged her toward the dark eastern channel.
Mossback's claw closed around the back of her coat and hauled her through the
gap he had made. They landed in the quarry mud as the diverted flood burst from
the old outflow and raced toward the Cress hills.

Far off, three cistern gates opened in succession. The first clean surge
flashed silver through the orchard channels. The second made space. The third,
the little eastern gate, shrieked exactly like Lord Osric considering a new
form.

Then the brown flood reached them.

The emptied cisterns swallowed the first violent rush. Larkspur flew low over
the channels, wrenching debris free with her claws, answering Lord Ruvik's
repeated calls until frost dulled her green-and-violet scales and he could no
longer stand without help. Each chose the cost again because each knew what
the other was asking.

At the quarry, pressure came off the wall.

It did not come off quickly enough.

The northern ledge sheared downward. Nyrzath twisted to keep it from the
nesting seam. His damaged wing folded under the stone with a sound Olyra felt
in her teeth.

Something small tumbled from behind his horn.

Even half buried, Nyrzath snapped his jaws through the falling rubble.

For one sick instant Olyra thought the swallow was gone.

Then Nyrzath closed his mouth without biting and settled his tusked chin into
the mud. The wall held. Behind his shoulder, Mossback reached the nesting seam
and called into the dark. Small answering chirps rose from the cavern.

The clutch lived. The villages stood. In the Cress hills, the flood spread
through stone basins built and maintained by both halves of an alliance.

Nyrzath's gold eye found Olyra.

He could not speak with his mouth occupied, so he nodded gravely.

It was, she thought, very nearly tomorrow.

Once the fallen stone had been levered clear, Nyrzath banked the furnace-light
behind his teeth to darkness and opened his jaws. Olyra lifted out the shaken
but living swallow, wrapped it in her scarf, and sent it to a warm quarry shed
under Lord Ruvik's careful eye while Nyrzath's wing was freed.

* * *

Mossback refused House Flint a second time before sunset.

He did it while standing between the villages and the injured royal dragon,
his scales streaked with quarry mud.

"I will watch the valley's people because I choose to," he told Duchess
Yselle. "I will not renew with you."

House Flint's title remained suspended. Duchess Yselle went before ordinary
judgment for the illegal cut and concealed instability. No dragon's roar
replaced evidence, repair, or law.

Nyrzath's wing required splints the length of roof beams. Olyra saw to the work
with the same seriousness House Cress gave Larkspur's cisterns and molting
grounds. An alliance, she had learned, was easiest to praise while asking the
dragon to bleed for it. Its truth showed afterward, when care was owed in the
other direction.

The convocation resumed around his bandaged wing.

Without the Dread Passage, the court seemed at first too quiet. Then every
human heir and every dragon ally came forward one pair at a time. They asked.
They answered freely. Nyrzath witnessed them whole. House Flint's vacant place
did not break the law; it proved the law meant something.

Their assent confirmed Queen Olyra only after she had accepted that it might
not.

Lord Osric presented her first proclamation with the expression of a man who
had survived the collapse of civilization and found it required only modest
changes to the filing system.

Olyra read it aloud. The royal dragon would enter future renewals as impartial
witness, without threat or Dread Passage.

"King Ambric used to say a crown sometimes needs a gentle friend to wear a
monster's face," she said.

Nyrzath's gold eye rested on her.

"He was wrong. A crown that needs a friend to lie about his nature has
mistaken fear for loyalty."

As her last words faded, Lord Ruvik emerged behind Nyrzath with Olyra's scarf
cupped in his hands. Sheltered from the court by the bandaged wing, Nyrzath
quenched the glow in his mouth and accepted the swallow onto his tongue. Then
he turned immediately toward Olyra.

Nyrzath bent toward her.

Court workers scattered from the path of his approaching jaws. Lord Osric
stood his ground, though he became extremely interested in the proclamation's
lower margin.

Nyrzath opened his mouth.

The damp, furious swallow hopped off his tongue into Olyra's hands.

It pecked her thumb, launched itself toward the rafters, and vanished into the
light.

"Terrifying," Olyra said.

"I have that effect on small creatures," Nyrzath replied.
