# Story plan

## Premise and promise

At the ceremony where supposedly giftless Adrian Bellweather expects to be
quietly sidelined by his great-mage family, his estranged father returns with a
coerced magical beast and reveals that Adrian can permanently sever any mage
from magic—and that the act erases Adrian's memory of doing it. The story
promises a concrete answer to the family's fear, sibling-scale elemental
spectacle, and a climax in which Adrian must decide whether mercy means making
dangerous people harmless or preserving their power to choose.

Central dramatic question: when Adrian can end the crisis by stripping magic
from the people he loves, can he use the feared gift without becoming the
threat everyone assumed he was?

Meaningful change: Adrian moves from seeking belonging by appearing harmless to
claiming a family role through informed, bounded action; his siblings move from
protective concealment and fear to honest consent.

## Story controls

- POV: First person, tightly limited to Adrian Bellweather
- Tense: Past
- Tone: Wry family mystery that turns urgent and morally serious without losing
  warmth
- Genre: Character-driven high fantasy
- Target: About 3,400 words within the 2,500–4,000-word contract
- Content boundary: Teen / PG-13; magical destruction, coercion, and
  non-graphic injury, with consequences foregrounded
- Scope: One evening at Bellweather Hall and its living orchard; an unnamed
  high-fantasy era independent of established named eras and legacy material

## Character engine

- **Adrian Bellweather, nineteen, protagonist/narrator:** The third of four
  siblings. He believes he is the family's only powerless child. He wants his
  family to stop flinching and to give him an honest role at Bellweather Hall.
  His blind spot is equating safety with harmlessness and reading silence as
  pity rather than fear. His latent *severance* lets him perceive the living
  desire sustaining a spell or magical faculty and cut a chosen magical
  connection. A complete cut can permanently strip a mage's gift; a narrow cut
  can undo one imposed command. The mirrored local cost is that Adrian loses
  his memory of the specific severing once it is complete.
- **Evelyn Bellweather, twenty-six, eldest sister:** Keeper of ashfire, capable
  of reducing forests to ash. Controlled and protective, she loves Adrian but
  has trained herself never to touch him when angry. She burns a firebreak
  through the orchard to save people, then must trust Adrian not to erase the
  power that defines her.
- **Marcus Bellweather, twenty-three, older brother:** Wields abyssal cold and
  has frozen an ocean reach solid enough for an army to cross. He uses jokes to
  manage fear. His ice contains the threat but also traps the family with it.
  He is the first sibling to admit their silence was cowardice.
- **Phoebe Bellweather, sixteen, younger sibling:** Empathically bonds with and
  guides magical beasts through felt intention rather than domination. Phoebe
  wants Adrian included. In the climax, Phoebe supplies the decisive
  distinction: the stormhart's terror and storm are its own, but the order
  driving it is not.
- **Lady Rowena Bellweather, mother and head of the lineage:** A ward-maker who
  plans to name Adrian the house's nonmagical steward, hoping a public role
  will protect him while keeping his gift secret. She called concealment mercy
  but also feared what informed Adrian might do to the family's power.
- **Garran Bellweather, estranged father and antagonist:** Once master of
  command-bonds. Ten years ago he tried to force Phoebe's emerging beast
  affinity into a dynastic weapon. Nine-year-old Adrian reflexively severed
  Garran from his whole magical faculty. Garran's grievance about that
  irreversible, nonconsensual loss is real, but he now endangers the family
  with a stormhart he bound before losing his gift and demands restoration.
- **The stormhart:** An unnamed, antlered magical beast that generates wind and
  lightning through its living body. Garran's old command-bond compels it
  through a scar. Its magic is its own; the foreign command is a separate
  connection Adrian can cut.

Opposition is layered: Garran's coercive demand creates the external crisis;
Rowena's concealment prevents informed action; the siblings' fear tempts Adrian
to prove safety through passivity; and severance's memory cost threatens to
take the truth away again.

Stakes include immediate deaths, the destruction of the living orchard,
Phoebe's mind being overwhelmed through forced beast contact, permanent loss
of the siblings' defining gifts, Adrian's memory of the truth, and whether the
family can remain a family once its lie is exposed.

## Causal arc

- **Beginning state:** At the Bellweather succession supper, Adrian prepares to
  accept appointment as nonmagical steward. He thinks this will finally make
  his usefulness legible. Tiny family precautions establish the unexplained
  fear.
- **Inciting disruption:** Garran arrives with a stormhart carrying his
  decade-old command-bond. He demands that Adrian “put back what you took.”
  Adrian has no idea what he means. Rowena attempts to expel Garran, triggering
  the command to attack.
- **First turn:** Phoebe feels two intentions in the beast: its panic and a
  foreign imperative. Garran tightens the bond. Evelyn's firebreak and Marcus's
  freeze save guests but drive the stormhart toward the Hall and seal the
  exits, making Adrian's passive safety impossible.
- **Escalating revelation:** Garran identifies Adrian's ability. Evelyn and
  Marcus confess that Adrian severed Garran's magic after Garran tried to
  enslave Phoebe's gift. Adrian remembers the before and aftermath but not the
  act because severance took that memory as its mirrored price. Rowena admits
  the family concealed the truth partly to protect him and partly from fear.
- **Crisis:** The stormhart's command pulls Phoebe into its pain. Adrian senses
  Evelyn's ashfire, Marcus's cold, Phoebe's beast bond, and Garran's coercive
  scar-command as vulnerable connections. A broad reflex could silence them
  all. Garran urges him to make everyone powerless; Rowena orders him not to
  act.
- **Climactic choice:** Adrian rejects both commands. He asks Phoebe whether
  the stormhart wants its own storm. She says yes, but not the order to kill.
  Adrian targets only the foreign connection. Evelyn and Marcus voluntarily
  lower their attacks at his request. The stormhart keeps its magic, Phoebe
  guides it out, and Garran loses his last means of control.
- **Price and safeguard:** Adrian knowingly pays with his memory of the present
  severing. Before completing it, he asks Marcus to begin an unenchanted,
  eyewitness record and speaks his consent and purpose aloud. The memory
  leaves; evidence and changed relationships remain.
- **Aftermath:** The next morning, Adrian expects the old lie. Instead, every
  sibling tells the same uncomfortable truth and gives him the signed account.
  He accepts stewardship only under new rules: mundane records after any
  severance, informed consent except to stop immediate coercion, and no more
  hiding his history. The family is not suddenly unafraid, but stops calling
  secrecy mercy.

## Setup and payoff

- Adrian's conspicuously nonmagical place setting first looks like an
  accommodation; it later proves the family fears he can cut living wards.
- Marcus's jokes and careful hands mask the remembered sight of Garran becoming
  magicless. In the climax, Marcus lowers his ice when Adrian asks.
- Evelyn's scorched glove shows that celebrated power damages living places and
  its wielder. Her voluntary restraint makes Adrian's precision possible.
- Phoebe first distinguishes the stormhart's own fear from its foreign command;
  this becomes Adrian's moral and technical target.
- Adrian's unexplained one-day childhood gap is seeded early and revealed as
  severance's mirrored cost.
- Rowena dismisses mundane ink and servant ledgers as beneath great mages.
  Adrian later relies on signed paper because it preserves evidence without a
  living spell he might cut.
- “Harmless” and “safe” recur as false synonyms. The ending replaces universal
  powerlessness with boundaries, evidence, and chosen restraint.
- Rowena calls concealment mercy; Garran calls stripping the house mercy;
  Adrian gives mercy a narrower shape by ending coercion without erasing the
  capacity to choose.

## Canon constraints

- The story stays in an unnamed high-fantasy era on the shared physical world
  and does not invoke Earth, Ravel, the Glass-Sea world, or the Glass Gate.
- Bellweather inheritance, first-desire manifestations, command-bonds, and
  severance are one household's local system, not universal laws.
- All autonomous magic use comes from living people or the living stormhart.
  The beast's scar and Garran's bracelet only focus a command created through
  his former living agency.
- Severance targets magical connections, not universal memory substance,
  souls, names, time, mundane skills, or identity. Its local mirrored cost
  affects Adrian's memory of the completed act, not responsibility or evidence.
- Consent has moral and practical weight but is not a universal magical rule.
- Fire, ice, beast empathy, storm, wards, and severance may interact without
  sharing one origin.
- Burned trees, ice damage, injury, coerced animal pain, family betrayal, and
  Garran's loss receive consequence-centered, non-graphic treatment.
- No existing character, faction, location, artifact, or legacy motif is
  treated as connected.

## Proposed inventions

- House Bellweather, a locally renowned hereditary mage household in an
  unnamed high-fantasy era, and its six named family members.
- The Bellweather “first desire” tradition: a child's magic tends to manifest
  through an early dominant desire. This is local belief, not a universal law.
- Evelyn's ashfire, Marcus's abyssal cold, Phoebe's consensual beast empathy,
  Rowena's wards, Garran's command-bonds, and Adrian's severance.
- Severance's mirrored cost: after cutting a magical connection, Adrian loses
  his autobiographical memory of that specific act; mundane evidence and other
  people's memories persist.
- Bellweather Hall and its living orchard as local locations.
- Stormharts, living antlered beasts whose bodies generate wind and lightning.
- The childhood incident: Garran tried to force Phoebe's affinity into a
  weapon; Adrian stripped Garran's magic; the family hid the truth from Adrian.
- Post-crisis safeguards concerning informed consent, emergency severance of
  active coercion, and mundane records.

## Failure modes to watch

- Do not let Garran's speech alone establish the truth. Sibling testimony,
  Rowena's admission, Phoebe's sensory evidence, Adrian's childhood gap, and
  present severance must corroborate it.
- Adrian must act: inspect connections, solicit Phoebe's knowledge, reject both
  parents' commands, select the narrow target, and knowingly pay the cost.
- Seed precision before the climax through Adrian's differentiated perception
  and Phoebe's distinction between the beast's desire and imposed command.
- Do not imply Adrian can erase souls, names, mundane skills, whole people, or
  arbitrary memories.
- Do not let Garran's grievance excuse coercion or the family's fear excuse a
  decade of deception.
- Evelyn's fire and Marcus's ice must save people, alter the battlefield,
  worsen another problem, and become powers they voluntarily lower.
- Phoebe's taming is empathic, not domination; the stormhart retains its own
  dangerous magic.
- Mention the siblings' forest/ocean scale credibly but confine on-page damage
  to Hall and orchard.
- Do not capitalize ordinary “my mother,” “my father,” “my sister,” or “my
  brother” as substitute proper names.
- Keep all Bellweather rules local and all inventions proposed.
- End after the morning-after terms; do not open a war, political subplot, or
  sequel hook.
