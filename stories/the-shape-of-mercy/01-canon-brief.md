# Canon brief

**Disposition: PLANNING UNBLOCKED.** No authoritative canon conflict prevents
this story. The prompt's mage lineage, four siblings, spectacular powers, and
central fear mystery are omissions in current canon rather than contradictions.
They may be developed as a bounded, story-local system. The prompt contract is
binding for this production, but its assumptions do not establish
shared-universe canon.

## Hard constraints

- Earth, Ravel, and the Glass-Sea world are era-bound names for the same
  physical world, not separate planets or planes. If the story invokes one of
  those names, it must preserve that relationship and the established era
  limits rather than treating the fantasy milieu as a disconnected world
  (`universe/premise.md` — `## The deep-time shared world`;
  `universe/locations.md` — `## The world across eras`).
- “Mage” and “magic” may be the viewpoint culture's correct vocabulary, but
  they are cultural labels rather than proof of an objective, universal
  category. The lineage's abilities must be one locally defined system; the
  story must not claim that all magic, technology, divinity, elemental power,
  or supernatural ability has the same origin or rules
  (`universe/rules.md` — `## System categories`;
  `universe/glossary.md` — `## Magic, technology, and divinity`).
- Only life can use magic. A nonliving focus, weapon, seal, collar, archive, or
  other device may store, transmit, focus, or shape power supplied through
  living agency, but cannot independently be a magic user unless the story
  establishes that it is alive (`universe/rules.md` —
  `## Living agency and magic`).
- No universal cost, desire, inheritance, bloodline, or consent rule exists.
  Any hereditary transmission, exhaustion, emotional trigger, sacrifice,
  command bond, or other limit introduced for this family must be stated or
  dramatized as a rule of this local system and applied consistently. Consent
  need not have a universal magical effect, but the story must take its ethical
  consequences seriously (`universe/rules.md` —
  `## Costs, desire, and local mechanisms`).
- If the protagonist's sincere ignorance involves erased, altered, copied,
  suppressed, transferred, or reconstructed memory, the story must define the
  particular mechanism and cannot imply a single universal “memory substance.”
  The explanation must preserve the protagonist's personhood, relationships,
  consent, grief, and moral choice rather than dismissing them as a magical or
  technical error (`universe/rules.md` —
  `## Memory, transformation, copies, and branches`;
  `universe/style-guide.md` — `## Personhood and moral consequence`).
- Names are not universally magical. Any true-name effect, identity binding,
  or name-triggered forgetting would need to be a rare, explicitly local
  mechanism rather than a general law of the setting
  (`universe/rules.md` — `## Names and identity binding`).
- The story must retain causal and evidentiary integrity. A dream, prophecy,
  myth, alternate timeline, resemblance, or convenient contradiction cannot
  substitute for the concrete revelation and causal explanation required by
  the prompt (`universe/style-guide.md` —
  `## Continuity and evidence discipline`;
  `universe/rules.md` — `## Records, falsehood, and historical traces`).
- The planned teen/PG-13 treatment fits the universe ceiling. Peril, injury,
  coercion, family harm, and death may be explored when relevant, but should
  remain non-graphic and consequence-centered rather than spectacular for its
  own sake (`universe/style-guide.md` —
  `## Audience and content boundaries`).

## Useful established context

- The universe explicitly permits many distinct real systems that cultures may
  call magic. Different systems can interact or imitate one another without
  sharing an origin, so one lineage can credibly contain related but
  non-identical fire, ice, beast-taming, and feared protagonist abilities
  provided the story establishes their local relationship rather than
  universalizing it (`universe/rules.md` — `## System categories`).
- A high-fantasy era is compatible with the deep-time setting. Civilizations
  and systems rise, interact, collapse, and become archaeology over spans that
  can reach millions of years, while different supernatural systems need not
  be active in every era (`universe/premise.md` —
  `## The deep-time shared world`; `universe/rules.md` —
  `## System categories`).
- The central universe theme is memory versus myth: evidence can degrade, be
  controlled, or be distorted, while partial recovery still leaves people
  responsible for consequential choices. That theme can enrich the family's
  conflicting accounts and the protagonist's revelation, but it does not
  supply a magic mechanism by itself (`universe/premise.md` —
  `## Memory, myth, and recoverable truth`).
- Characters and cultures may hold incomplete or incorrect models even though
  reality is coherent backstage. The protagonist's sincere ignorance and the
  family's compromised interpretation can therefore coexist with a real,
  causally knowable capability (`universe/premise.md` —
  `## Knowledge and connection`).
- The universe does not require every story to advertise its deep cosmology or
  connect to existing eras, artifacts, or characters. An independent
  high-fantasy entry point is supported (`universe/premise.md` —
  `## Memory, myth, and recoverable truth`;
  `universe/style-guide.md` — `## Tonal range, scale, and connection`).
- Precise, concrete prose and a dramatized revelation are aligned with the
  production style; exposition that exists only to display lore is discouraged
  (`universe/style-guide.md` — `## Prose defaults`).

## Conflicts or ambiguity

- **No authoritative contradiction found.** No `LOCKED` or `CANON` entry
  forbids hereditary mages, elemental abilities, magical beasts, a feared
  sibling, or a locally caused knowledge gap. No canon story is currently
  available to add constraints: every row in `stories/INDEX.md` has canon
  `no` (`stories/INDEX.md` — `# Story index`).
- **Era ambiguity, not conflict:** the prompt does not place the family in the
  modern-like era, Ravel, the Glass-Sea era, or another period. The
  modern-like Nell-and-Bash era is an established low-magic trough whose
  ordinary life treats magic as myth or legend; placing a renowned, publicly
  spectacular mage lineage there would require a coherent secrecy, isolation,
  or anomaly explanation. Ravel's chronological position and detailed legacy
  lore are unresolved, and the Glass-Sea era's detailed geography and cultures
  are unresolved (`universe/timeline.md` — `## Established era order`;
  `universe/glossary.md` — `## Modern-like era`, `## Ravel`, and
  `## Glass-Sea world`).
- **Scale ambiguity, not conflict:** “turn forests into ash” and “freeze oceans
  over” establish the siblings' promised capability or credible reputation,
  but canon supplies no numerical power ceiling, affected area, duration,
  recovery rate, or ecological consequence. The story must establish whatever
  scale it needs locally without presenting that scale as a universal rule.
- **Vocabulary ambiguity, not conflict:** the family may sincerely use
  “mage,” “lineage,” “tamer,” and “magical beast,” but those terms do not yet
  specify the underlying mechanisms or prove the four abilities share a single
  origin (`universe/glossary.md` —
  `## Magic, technology, and divinity`).
- **Provisional guidance:** no relevant `PROVISIONAL` universe entry was found.
  The prompt's assumptions and completion tests are story-production
  requirements, not established lore. They may guide planning but cannot be
  cited later as shared-universe fact (`universe/README.md` —
  `## Authority order`; `stories/the-shape-of-mercy/00-prompt.md` —
  `## Assumptions` and `## Completion tests`).

## Unknowns

- **Unknown:** the era, region, realm name, calendar, geography, government,
  culture, religion, and public standing of the lineage. No recurring faction
  or culture is established (`universe/factions.md` —
  `# Factions and cultures`).
- **Unknown:** whether magical ability is actually hereditary, how the four
  siblings acquired or express it, whether their powers share a source, and
  what rules, limits, costs, counters, training, or failure modes apply. A
  local hereditary system appears safe to invent, but heredity must not be
  stated as a universal property of magic.
- **Unknown:** the literal extent and history of the eldest sister's fire and
  older brother's ice, including whether a forest or ocean has already been
  affected. Canon establishes no relevant named forest, ocean, catastrophe, or
  ecological aftermath.
- **Unknown:** what “taming” magical beasts means, whether the relationship is
  consensual, trained, empathic, coerced, contractual, or biological, and
  whether any beast independently uses magic. A story-local answer appears
  safe if living agency and ethical consequence remain intact.
- **Unknown:** the protagonist's capability, why it provokes greater fear, who
  knows the truth, how that knowledge is evidenced, and why the protagonist
  does not know. Canon does not establish a suppression, forgetting,
  self-alteration, prophecy, or family-conspiracy mechanism.
- **Unknown:** whether the protagonist's feared power affects bodies, minds,
  souls, magic, identity, records, time, or relationships. If the answer
  touches afterlife, godhood, portals, name-binding, or personal copying, the
  corresponding established system rules must be checked rather than assumed
  (`universe/rules.md` — `## Afterlife and continuation`, `## Divinity`,
  `## Portals and crossing systems`, `## Names and identity binding`, and
  `## Memory, transformation, copies, and branches`).
- **Unknown:** all character-facing personal names, lineage names, titles,
  aliases, named beasts, constructs, and institutions. The prompt supplies
  roles only.
- **Unknown:** whether the title phrase “mercy” is an in-world term, a thematic
  description, or a property of the protagonist's choice. It has no current
  canonical definition.

## Safe invention space

- Create one bounded mage household or lineage, its immediate dependents and
  rivals, and a local high-fantasy community in an otherwise-unplaced era of
  the shared physical world. Keep its political reach and historical claims
  proportionate to what this story can establish.
- Define a local inheritance or training model that explains why one family
  contains four spectacular capabilities. Give each ability stable limits,
  triggers, counters, and consequences, while avoiding claims about all magic
  or all bloodlines.
- Invent the concrete feared capability and the causal reason for the
  protagonist's sincere ignorance. The mechanism may be intimate and
  family-scale; it should produce observable evidence in the climax and leave
  the protagonist with a morally meaningful choice. If memory or identity is
  altered, specify actor, trigger, scope, persistence, and consent consequences.
- Invent locally named forests, shores, seas, estates, schools, beasts, rituals,
  household customs, witnesses, and prior incidents. Their histories should
  remain local and should not silently redefine established named eras or the
  world's global chronology.
- Invent a beast-taming practice and one or more living magical species,
  provided that any creature using magic is alive and any nonliving equipment
  remains a focus or conduit rather than an independent magic user.
- Keep the family independent of Ravel, the Glass-Sea era, the Glass Gate, and
  legacy characters unless a later production decision supplies specific
  evidence and accepts the added continuity burden. Shared fire, ice, beasts,
  glass, sun, moon, or other imagery alone does not establish a connection
  (`universe/rules.md` — `## Artifacts, motifs, and folklore`;
  `universe/style-guide.md` — `## Continuity and evidence discipline`).
- Do not invent a new universal law of memory, souls, names, consent, costs,
  portals, godhood, or elemental magic for this story. Any reusable fact should
  be recorded later as a proposed canon delta rather than treated as already
  established.
