# Outline

## Story

- Proposed reader-facing title: *Before She Gets Tired*.
- Premise and central promise: After another weekly game night, Omar corners his close friend Kian with the prompt exchange and insists that Yasmin likes him just as the previous four women did before Kian waited them out. Kian follows Yasmin to an ordinary bus shelter intending to perform one brave ask and secure a yes. His first attempt merely renames their existing ambiguous outings, and Yasmin refuses to keep translating hints into commitments for him. Resolve the prompt when Kian stops treating Omar's confidence, past signals, and Yasmin's patience as guarantees; asks Yasmin plainly for one specific date; clarifies rather than retreats when she cannot make the proposed night; and receives a freely given yes to another time. The romantic success is real, but it rests on accepting that a clear question also permits a clear no.
- Intended reader experience: Begin with fast, affectionate ribbing and the guilty recognition that Omar's ridiculous certainty has evidence behind it. Let Kian's walk downstairs turn comic bravado into intimate discomfort, then make the bus-shelter exchange warm, exacting, and slightly embarrassing rather than punitive. Readers should root for the yes while understanding why Yasmin is tired of the arrangement, and feel the deeper relief when Kian listens through an apparent rejection instead of fleeing from it.
- Generating force:
  - Mode: Plot-led romantic approach whose forward movement comes from removing buffers. Omar's prediction gets Kian moving; Kian's rehearsed wording fails; Yasmin states what the ambiguity has cost; Kian must finally ask without outsourcing either the risk or the answer.
  - Focal attachment or contradiction: Kian genuinely values Yasmin's friendship and does not want to presume on her warmth, but he has made caution serve a second purpose: preserving indefinite closeness without ever giving her the information needed to choose what it is. Yasmin likes him and has repeatedly initiated time together, yet no longer wants to manage both his feelings and his fear. Omar loves Kian enough to interrupt the pattern, but mistakes being right about attraction for being entitled to script the relationship.
  - Plot-led wants and useful limit, if applicable: Kian immediately wants to ask Yasmin out and hear yes; he also wants to protect their friendship, avoid embarrassing her, and behave as though the answer belongs to her. His useful flaw is certainty-hoarding: he waits for proof strong enough to eliminate rejection, then adds so many qualifiers that the other person cannot tell what he is offering. Yasmin wants a real date with Kian but also wants the ambiguous run of almost-dates to end tonight, even if ending it costs the romance. Omar wants Kian to act before another connection expires; his limit is framing courage as masculinity and other people's feelings as calls he can get right.
- Counterforce or complication:
  - Source: No antagonist. The complication is Kian's practiced retreat into plausible deniability meeting Yasmin's finite patience. The approaching bus supplies a natural end to the conversation, not a magical or do-or-die deadline; either person can continue later, but neither can keep this particular exchange suspended forever.
  - Credible inside logic, if a person: Yasmin is not withholding a yes to teach Kian a lesson. She protects her ability to enjoy the friend group, stop scheduling one-on-one outings that leave her guessing, and decline whatever he actually proposes. She admits her attraction directly, corrects his assumption that liking him means unlimited patience, proposes an alternate evening when his first date does not work, and retains agency after challenging him. Omar's push is rooted in four observed missed connections, but he remains fallible about what Yasmin will decide.
  - Pressure or change created: Kian's vague invitation would restart the exact arrangement Yasmin intends to leave. To get an answer, he must risk naming a date; to deserve the answer, he must not treat her attraction, Omar's record, or his own effort as a debt. When Yasmin says she cannot do Friday, his old habit reads total rejection into a scheduling fact, creating the smallest possible test of whether he has changed his way of listening.
- Narrative design:
  - POV, tense, distance, and limitation: Close third past through Kian, intimate with the defensive classifications by which he turns flirtation into neutral data and every possible ask into a risk assessment. Keep Omar and Yasmin exterior. Omar speaks in blunt verdicts, competitive metaphors, and affectionate insults; Yasmin uses concrete examples, refuses euphemisms, and goes quiet rather than filling pauses Kian creates.
  - Time shape and compression: One continuous late evening, roughly forty minutes from Yasmin leaving Omar's apartment to her bus departing. Use Omar's rapid, one-detail summaries of the previous four women rather than flashback scenes; allow only brief sensory recollections when Kian recognizes the same delay in his history with Yasmin. The present conversation must carry the emotional weight.
  - Information and reveal strategy: Give the prompt exchange in the opening movement. Let Omar's four compact examples establish that Kian has a pattern without making Omar an omniscient romance detector. Show enough of Yasmin's recent invitations to make hope credible, but withhold confirmation of her attraction until she states it herself. The main uncertainty is not whether Omar correctly decoded her; it is whether Kian can ask in a form she can actually answer. Treat her scheduling conflict first as Kian experiences it—an apparent no—then let one clarifying question separate the person from the proposed night.
  - Governing movement: Clear the game-night dishes; watch Yasmin leave; receive Omar's push; dispute the previous four cases; hear one undeniable detail from each; say “I am going for it”; descend while rehearsing borrowed lines; find Yasmin waiting at the shelter; open with a vague offer to “do something” they already do; hear her refuse another unnamed almost-date; admit Omar sent him and worsen the exchange by citing her signals; let Yasmin distinguish liking him from waiting indefinitely; learn she had decided to stop initiating after tonight; abandon the case for certainty; ask plainly for dinner Friday as a date; hear that Friday is impossible; resist retreat; ask whether the no is to Friday or to him; accept her Sunday counteroffer; decline to press for a kiss or escort; let the arriving bus end the encounter; leave the next move shared rather than guaranteed.
- Speculative surplus: None. This is an entirely ordinary modern-like relationship story; phones, transit, social cues, memory, and timing have only ordinary force.
- Structural distinction: A linear subtraction rather than a loop, chase, threshold negotiation, two-track farce, or branching puzzle. Each short movement removes a proxy—Omar's certainty, the previous four cases, Kian's rehearsed script, and Yasmin's presumed patience—until two adults make one modest plan at an unnamed bus shelter. There is no public confession, audience reaction, collective turn, technical proof, institution, montage of dates, or symbolic seal on the outcome.
- Decisive turn, deepening, or recognition: Kian finally asks, “Dinner Friday—with me, as a date?” Yasmin answers that she cannot Friday. He begins the familiar retreat, ready to assure her he meant nothing by it, and realizes that erasing the question would again force her to infer him. He instead asks, “Is that no to Friday, or no to going out with me?” Yasmin says Friday is the problem, offers Sunday, and then confirms in equally plain language that Sunday would be a date. The decisive act is not boldness that wins her; it is staying present long enough to hear the answer she actually gives.
- Aftereffect or live uncertainty: Yasmin accepts Sunday dinner but does not promise a relationship or reward the months of delay. She declines Kian's offer to walk her home because she wants the bus and an ordinary end to the evening; he accepts that no without translating it into a reversal of her yes. Omar will almost certainly claim vindication, the first date may still be awkward, and Kian's habit is not cured by one question, but the indefinite almost-relationship has ended through a plan both people named.
- Opening / ending relation: Open with Omar defining “being a man” as acting before a woman gets tired and Kian agreeing to “go for it” as though courage were a single charge. End with Kian letting Yasmin board after she declines company, holding the yes to Sunday and the no to walking home without making either cancel the other. The opening's borrowed performance becomes a quieter capacity to ask, distinguish answers, and stop when asked.

## Beats

1. In Omar's kitchen after game night, Yasmin leaves for her bus. Omar delivers the prompt exchange nearly verbatim. Kian disputes the “last four”; Omar answers with one unmistakable invitation from each and identifies Kian's six-month strategy of waiting until the choice disappears. Kian says he is going for it.
2. On the stairs and sidewalk, Kian rehearses confident lines that all presume Yasmin's answer. Concrete memories undercut each one: her repeated invitations, his habit of calling them group plans even when nobody else came, and her increasing refusal to rescue the silences he created.
3. Kian finds Yasmin at the shelter and asks whether she wants to “hang out properly sometime.” She points out that they already hang out and asks what “properly” means. His attempt to soften the question turns into another non-question.
4. Yasmin says she likes him, but that is not permission to keep her in suspense. She had decided tonight would be the last time she initiated one-on-one plans; she will remain part of the group, but she is done turning coffee, errands, and gallery visits into hidden tests of whether he will ever name what he wants.
5. Kian confesses that Omar pushed him and starts presenting the case of her signals. Yasmin refuses to let Omar's record speak for her: Kian may ask her, and she may answer, but nobody gets to announce the result beforehand. Kian recognizes that his demand for certainty and Omar's certainty both erase the same choice from opposite directions.
6. Kian asks clearly for dinner Friday as a date. Yasmin cannot Friday. He nearly withdraws the whole question, then asks whether she means no to Friday or no to him. She says Friday, offers Sunday, and confirms that she wants Sunday to be a date.
7. The bus arrives before either can inflate the yes into a future. Kian offers to walk her home; Yasmin says no, she wants the bus. He accepts it. They confirm only a time to choose a restaurant the next day, and she leaves with the date real but the relationship undecided.
