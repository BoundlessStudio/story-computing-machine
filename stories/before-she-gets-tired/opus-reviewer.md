# Opus review: Not Friday

Independent audit for a later editing pass. It is not a `review.md` verdict,
does not change canon state, and does not authorize edits by itself.

## Verdict

- Dialogue: REVISE — at the decisive exchange Beatriz rebuts a claim Wesley never made, and through Wednesday Wesley and Jamar trade the same clipped courtroom deadpan.
- Prose: PASS — concrete, brisk and funny. Minor fixes: two narrated thesis lines, a repeated verdict-shaped closer, and some opening staging.
- Continuity and prompt: REVISE — the prompt is fulfilled and the length is in band, but the quintet's running order contradicts itself. Two smaller logistics details also wobble.
- Edit scope: targeted-dialogue
- Existing review.md said: Verdict PASS; "Dialogue: PASS", which called Jamar's mock-legal pressure, Wesley's corrective precision and Beatriz's directness "distinct". I disagree: the decisive reply answers words that were never spoken, and the two men share one register.

The Friday work talk is the best dialogue in the story. Beatriz gives orders, Wesley reports counts and reads back instructions, and nobody performs. The trouble is on either side of it. On Wednesday the prompt's excitable "Bro, just trust me" friend becomes a dry court clerk ("Objection.", "Sustained as to four months.", "Motion carries."). Wesley answers him in the same two-to-four-word register ("Nobody invited you to court.", "Fire code.", "Thirty feet."), so their volleys become matched rulings, and Jamar's worry for his friend exists only in narration. At the decisive exchange Beatriz answers an assumption Wesley never voiced, and her yes turns into a short lesson on friendliness and promises. Just before that, Wesley proposes the very Friday he had ruled out a page earlier. The edit should loosen Jamar into a hype-man who talks too much and plainly cares, and let Wesley hedge and over-specify instead of landing comebacks. It should rebuild the invitation so each reply answers the words actually spoken, and leave the Friday work scenes and the Tuesday ending nearly alone.

The sound fails at 6:58. Facilities hard close is 10:00. The quintet is cut to eight minutes with no encore. The four prior women stay unnamed with distinct outcomes: the coffee question left unanswered for seven months, dinner plans for four months before she moved for a job, friendship, and someone else. ## Dialogue findings

### D1 · Blocking · Beatriz rebuts a claim Wesley never made

- Where: L129–L139, lobby doors on Wednesday, the invitation
- Text:
> "I am interested," she said. "But don't backfill every friendly thing I did into a promise. I hadn't made one."
> "I know. Or I was trying to know that."
> "Those are different."
- Problem: Ground/uptake plus Philosopher at the decisive exchange. Beatriz has just heard a plain invitation: dinner, "Just the two of us", an easy out, a promise not to make Friday weird. Wesley said nothing about her friendliness, dumplings, rides or signals. The backfilling exists only in Wesley's private reasoning at L71 and in Jamar's case, which Beatriz is never shown hearing. So her correction answers the author's theme, not his words. Wesley then accepts a charge he was never openly given, and the exchange turns into correction ping-pong ("trying to know" / "Those are different." / "Yes."). The answer to a date invitation becomes a lesson on the difference between friendliness and promises. The outline's anti-default warned against exactly this "consent seminar", and the story has already made the point in narration.
- Fix: Cut "But don't backfill every friendly thing I did into a promise. I hadn't made one." and L135–L139. Let "I am interested," run into the bus beat (L141) and her day-and-place terms (L143). Her agency already shows in her refusing Friday, choosing Tuesday and the restaurant, and reconfirming it herself on Friday night. If the editor wants a boundary beat, ground it in something she has seen. Plant at L113 that she catches Jamar's eyebrow campaign, then let her ask one plain question. Illustrative only: "Is this you asking, or Jamar?" Wesley's answer should be short and true, not a correction.

### D2 · Major · Wesley proposes the Friday he just ruled out

- Where: L101, L115, L129, L143; setup hour and the invitation
- Text:
> My first paid call is Saturday morning, assuming Friday doesn't bury me under a silent auction basket.
> Friday belonged to the showcase. It also stood between Beatriz and a new job she had spent months trying to get.
> "Would you like to have dinner with me after the showcase on Friday?" he said.
- Problem: Causality and knowledge at the key beat. Wesley knows she starts her new job Saturday morning because he asked and she told him (L101). Fourteen lines before the invitation, the narration has him conclude that Friday belongs to the showcase and to her job. He then proposes Friday night, and nothing registers it as a slip. Beatriz's "And not Friday… Saturday is my first contract call" (L143) then tells him what he already knows. The title needs him to name Friday, but as written the reader sees a contradiction, not a character mistake.
- Fix: Keep Friday in the invitation and make it a legible slip. His rehearsed openings all assumed Friday, and under pressure the rehearsed day comes out. Add one clause at L131 in which Wesley hears, too late, which day he has named. Leave L115 as it is. With the slip marked, Beatriz's reminder at L143 reads as fair; trim its "Friday is the showcase." since both of them know it.

### D3 · Major · Jamar and Wesley trade one clipped courtroom deadpan

- Where: L21–L69, Wednesday chair setup (the "court" volley, the tape measure, the exhibits)
- Text:
> "Nobody invited you to court."
> "The court convened when you made the same mistake four times."
> "Flight from evidence."
> "Sustained as to four months."
- Problem: Identical Voices and Tennis Match, with epigram closers. After L19, every Jamar line is a short ruling or label. Wesley answers each one with a matched two-to-four-word comeback: "Nobody invited you to court.", "Fire code.", "Not each time." Each run closes on a last word: "The court convened…", "Fire code.", "Four situations. One delay." Cover the tags and the rhythm is identical, with only the legal vocabulary to tell them apart. "Sustained as to four months" is lawyerly precision, not a friend who opened with "Bro, just trust me." The outline gave Jamar interruption and selective listening, and gave Wesley caveats and restarts; neither appears aloud. Jamar never holds the floor after L19, never loses the thread, and never says anything unguarded, so the concern described at L33 is never heard.
- Fix: Rebuild the Wednesday volleys so that Jamar dominates and Wesley doesn't match him. (1) Cut L27–L31. After "Not each time." let Jamar talk over him in a longer, looser burst. He can get details slightly wrong and not care, and one plain phrase should let his worry show. Once it does, L33's second sentence can go. (2) Wesley's real move is evasion into measurement (L37). Keep it, and drop the "Flight from evidence." / "Fire code." topper pair, or let Jamar's jab go unanswered while Wesley measures. (3) Replace "Sustained as to four months." with a concession in Jamar's own voice that keeps pushing. Illustrative only: "Okay, fine, four. She still moved to another city while you were comparing restaurants." Keep "Exhibit one" and "Exhibit two", since he is enjoying the performance. Keep "Four situations. One delay." as his one earned aphorism, because L73 tests it.

### D4 · Major · The commitment turns into a terms negotiation

- Where: L75–L89, right after "Okay. I'm going for it."
- Text:
> "You are not coming within twenty feet of the conversation."
> "Unreasonable restriction."
> "With excellent hearing."
- Problem: A negotiation/procedure routine and a Tennis Match at the prompt's climax. Wesley finally agrees, which is what Jamar has wanted for four relationships, and the friendship's payoff becomes six alternating clauses: restriction, objection, counteroffer, concession, thanks, topper. Jamar's reaction to the moment is a ruling ("Motion carries.") and then a bargaining position. It stays comic but inert: nothing between them changes except the distance clause.
- Fix: Keep the chair slap (L77); it is a good beat. Keep Wesley's condition (L79) and Jamar's plain "I'll be in another room." (L85). Cut L81, L83, L87 and L89 so the terms become one request and one concession. Let Jamar's first response to the agreement be excitement in his own voice rather than "Motion carries." (optional, but the scene needs one unguarded Jamar reaction).

### D5 · Minor · Narration misdescribes Jamar's uptake

- Where: L21–L23, opening volley
- Text:
> "Six-plus months," Jamar repeated, having selected the useful portion of Wesley's sentence. "Each time. Pattern established."
- Problem: Ground. Wesley's last sentence was "Or she isn't waiting." It contains no "six-plus months". That phrase is Jamar's own, from L19, so he is repeating himself, not selecting part of Wesley's sentence. The reader stops to look for the portion. "Pattern established." is another ruling-closer (see D3).
- Fix: Make the clause describe what Jamar actually does: he ignores the objection and repeats his own number. Cut "Pattern established."

### D6 · Minor · "Ask on behalf of all your distinctions" has no recoverable sense

- Where: L69, end of the exhibits
- Text:
> "Great. Excellent distinctions." Jamar shoved the next chair into line. "Ask on behalf of all your distinctions."
- Problem: The sarcasm is legible, but "on behalf of" gives no usable image; you can't ask on behalf of distinctions. The line exists to close the volley on a last word.
- Fix: Cut the second sentence. "Great. Excellent distinctions." and the shoved chair carry the sarcasm.

### D7 · Minor · "Send me your order" doesn't match what he sends

- Where: L149–L151, end of the invitation scene
- Text:
> "Good. Send me your order if the food vendor texts you back."
> Then he went inside and sent her the vendor's revised count.
- Problem: Ground/referent. Straight after a dinner date is fixed, "your order" first reads as his noodle order. The vendor clause then points to Friday's caterer, but the story never showed Wesley dealing with the vendor, and the narration has him send a "revised count", not an order. Her pivot back to work is right; the referent isn't.

### D10 · Minor · The prompt's lines lose their energy

- Where: L13–L19, opening speech
- Text:
> They left because you waited six-plus months to find out anything. Now ask Beatriz before she gets tired of waiting for you to ask.
- Problem: The prompt's exchange is a burst of exclamations ("Bro just trust me!", "shut up!", "……ok i am going for it!"); the story has none. "To find out anything" is a limp substitute for the prompt's challenge. "Ask Beatriz" drops "out" and then repeats "ask", which dulls the slug line "before she gets tired".

## Prose findings

### P1 · Minor · Narration states the lesson at the turn

- Where: L71–L73, Wesley's reasoning before "Okay. I'm going for it."
- Text:
> Friendly was not a coded contract.
> Waiting protected Wesley from answers while he called the protection consideration.
- Problem: The dumpling, apostrophe and rides evidence already makes the point, and the first line then labels it. The second is a neat maxim at the story's hinge. Together with Beatriz's L133 (D1) the theme is stated twice in about sixty lines.
- Fix: Cut "Friendly was not a coded contract." L73 can stay, since it is Wesley's own analytic idiom and it motivates his agreement. Once D1 is fixed it will be the story's only explicit statement of the point.

### P2 · Minor · Repeated verdict-shaped paragraph closers

- Where: L115, L151, L251, L261
- Text:
> Adding his uncertainty to her load would not make the question more romantic.
> None of those sentences changed the clock.
> None of it would help her empty the room by ten.
- Problem: Paragraphs keep closing on the same verdict template: "would not make X more Y" at L115 and L151, and "None of…" twice within ten lines at L251 and L261. L151 ("would not make the answer more answered") is the funny one. The others shrink its effect and explain actions the scene already shows.
- Fix: Keep L151. Cut or vary L115's closer, and cut L261's last sentence, since the action before it already shows Wesley staying at his post.

### P3 · Minor · Opening staging slips

- Where: L11–L19, L53–L61, L91; chair setup and Beatriz's entrance
- Text:
> On Wednesday afternoon he carried four, which left Jamar Ellis two free hands for opinions.
> Wesley set down his load before Jamar could hit one.
> Jamar planted both palms on the chair backs as if calling a witness.
> Jamar presented that history with two fingers raised, then four
> Beatriz came through hip-first, a cardboard file box balanced against one hip
- Problem: Small logic and staging snags. Wesley carrying four chairs doesn't explain why Jamar's hands are free. It is unclear what "hit one" refers to or why setting down the chairs prevents it. Planting palms is closing-argument body language, not calling a witness. Jamar raises "a finger" for exhibit two, then "two fingers… then four" for the fourth woman. "Hip-first… against one hip" repeats the word.
- Fix: Make each clause say what happens: Jamar carries nothing; Wesley sets his chairs down so that Jamar, walking backward, doesn't walk into them. Pick a simile that fits the gesture, make the finger count consistent, and change one "hip".

## Continuity and prompt findings

### C1 · Blocking · The quintet's running order contradicts itself

- Where: L97 (Wednesday) against L237–L263 (Friday)
- Text:
> I moved the student quintet ahead of the auction
> the auction had started late, and the closing quintet still needed a twelve-minute changeover
- Problem: On Wednesday Beatriz says she moved the quintet ahead of the auction. On Friday it is the "closing quintet". It plays after the gallery closes at 9:15, and bus riders take aisle seats "when they come out of the auction". Friday's decision to cut her favourite act only works if it closes the show.
- Fix: One phrase at L97, for example "moved the student quintet to close the show" or "after the auction". Leave Friday as it is.

### C2 · Minor · Beatriz gives rides but takes the bus

- Where: L71 against L119 and L151
- Text:
> She had given both of them rides when the buses stopped running.
> At six-twenty, Beatriz checked the bus app and began packing her papers.
- Problem: Giving rides after the buses stop implies she drives. Yet she takes the bus home from the same building, carrying a file box.
- Fix: Change L71 to a kindness that doesn't need a car, or add a word explaining the car's absence. The L71 change is the smaller edit.

### C3 · Minor · Venue name and seat count drift

- Where: L15, L195, L241
- Text:
> forty-eight black seats waited to become eight straight rows in the rehearsal hall
> every speaker in the black-box theater made a low electrical thump and fell silent
> "One hundred twelve inside. Eight have left.
- Problem: Wednesday sets up forty-eight seats in "the rehearsal hall" (the room has a stage); Friday's show is in "the black-box theater", with 112 people inside and "a hundred conversations". The reader can't tell whether these are one room or two, and forty-eight seats for more than a hundred guests goes unexplained.
- Fix: Use one name for the room, and either raise the Wednesday count (keeping rows of six, which "stacks of six" at L267 echoes) or make "inside" clearly mean the whole building.
