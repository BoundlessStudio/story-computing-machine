# Collection art direction

This is visual guidance for new, authorized character and location art. Artwork interprets a story; it does not establish shared-universe facts or change a locked story package.

The collection direction is Canadian Group of Seven inspired oil painting: broad, rhythmic colour and value shapes; varied directional opaque strokes; quieter connecting passages; selective substantial impasto; simplified figures and architecture; and story-specific natural or otherwise motivated light. Keep shadows connected and allow darkness and lost edges. Avoid glossy faces or lenses, uniform bevelled strokes, theatrical rim light, and automatic orange-blue contrast.

When this collection style is requested, use actual paintings such as [Apple Orchard Hillside](../stories/a-beginner-of-considerable-age/art/landscapes/01-apple-orchard-hillside.png) and [The Stream After Ash](../stories/friends-of-the-night/art/landscapes/05-the-stream-after-ash.png) as visual references. The finished story and any supplied references determine identity, clothing, setting, and light.

For a new `[WP]` story, make character and location references after the prose is ready and before making the cover, unless the user requested prose only. Choose the principal characters and every distinct location important to the finished story from the prose and supplied originals, including places that will not appear on the cover. Stories with multiple important settings need multiple location paintings, at least two distinct places. Different views or corrections of one location do not satisfy that coverage. A story confined to one setting needs only that location; do not invent another. An image must not silently add canon.

Each character sheet should let a later artist recognize the same character across multiple views or poses, expressions, and story-relevant clothing or props. Label it with the character and story, and check that its panels agree with each other and any supplied originals. An ensemble sheet must give each principal character those studies and clear labels. Existing sheets such as [Melicent](../stories/a-beginner-of-considerable-age/art/characters/melicent.webp) and [Tavik](../stories/friends-of-the-night/art/characters/tavik.webp) show the format; a scene portrait alone does not satisfy it. Each location painting should establish the place's story-relevant geography, architecture, layout, and light independently of any cover composition.

Finish and inspect the full reference set first, then use the relevant accepted character and location references as visual inputs for the cover. Check the cover against them for identity and setting consistency. The reference subjects come from the story; the cover does not define the reference set.

Keep accepted final art in its story package. Preserve supplied originals under `stories/<slug>/art/references/` with their recorded names. Save accepted character art in `art/characters/` and location paintings in `art/landscapes/` or `art/interiors/`, with descriptive character or location names. Do not create paired “Original” and “Selected” location images or a new `art/selection-notes.json`. Corrections replace candidates; keep drafts, superseded candidates, and rejected images under the worktree's `tmp/`, with revisions to previously committed art preserved in Git history. Existing story assets and legacy selection files remain historical work; this workflow change does not authorize modifying locked packages. There is no required gallery inventory.
