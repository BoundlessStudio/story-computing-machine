# Prompt-scout taste profile

- Updated: 2026-07-22
- Evidence state: Seed inference only; no explicit prompt feedback yet
- Confidence: Low to moderate

## Inferred positive signals

- An impossible premise grounded in a specific person's relationship, grief,
  duty, consent, identity, or moral choice.
- Hidden rules that create consequences rather than serving as decoration.
- Memory, names, mistaken history, recoverable truth, and the gap between myth
  and evidence.
- Copies, reflections, transformations, inherited identities, and other
  personhood questions that preserve human stakes.
- Portals, layered worlds, deep time, buried civilizations, gods with material
  consequences, and ordinary lives brushing against a much larger reality.
- Time loops, bargains, curses, or rituals that force a costly choice.
- A premise with room for escalation and an ending, not only a punchline or a
  single reversal.
- Broadly accessible treatment even when the subject is dark.

## Neutral until feedback exists

- Comedy, romance, horror, fantasy, science fiction, and ordinary realism are
  all viable; no genre is currently presumed unwanted.
- Second-person framing is common on r/WritingPrompts and is not itself a
  positive or negative signal.
- Familiar tropes may rank well when the prompt adds a strong causal or moral
  complication.

## Weak negative signals

These are ranking cautions, not established dislikes:

- Pure power fantasy without vulnerability, consequence, or meaningful choice.
- Premises that prescribe the entire plot and leave the writer little room.
- A joke setup whose only available ending is the joke already stated.
- Graphic or exploitative treatment incompatible with the repository's content
  ceiling.
